About this text

The Recitation of the Names of the Tathāgatas: A Garland of Ornaments of the Fortunate Eon, in Sanskrit Tathāgatanāmasaṅgītikālpikabhadrālaṃkaramāla and in Tibetan De bzhin gshegs pa'i mtshan brjod bskal bzang rgyan gyi phreng ba, is a devotional work in verse honoring the thousand buddhas who appear in our present world age, the Fortunate Eon. It opens by recalling how, in a distant eon, the thousand sons of the universal monarch Dhṛtarāṣṭra were prophesied to become the buddhas of this eon, and then recites their names stanza by stanza, each stanza closing with the refrain "to them I pay homage." Its closing verses praise the benefit of hearing these names with reverence, which is said to burn away misdeeds, free one from the suffering of the lower realms, and lead in the end to nirvāṇa, and its colophon names the Kashmiri paṇḍita Śākyaśrī as its author, who translated it together with the Tibetan translator Jampé Pal, with a later revision by the Sakya Lotsāwa. It is Toh 1169 in the Tengyur, where it belongs to the section of praises (stotra).

The names of the thousand buddhas who appear in this Fortunate Eon:
Whoever hears them and is reverent burns away all misdeeds,
Sees all the Victors, leaves behind the suffering of the lower realms,
And, endowed with patience, through a succession of the highest happiness, attains nirvāṇa.

Well urged by the kindness of the bodhisattva King Supreme Joy,
The great paṇḍita of Kashmir composed this work,
Which surpasses others in five respects.
Even so, I confess every fault and misdeed, one by one.

This virtue that I have made with great effort and a devoted mind,
And all virtue without limit,

The names of the buddhas are translated by their meaning, except for those widely known by their Sanskrit names and those formed from the names of gods; the Indian forms of most of them cannot be identified with confidence from the Tibetan alone. The names of the eon, the realm, and the buddha who gave the prophecy in the opening verses are likewise translated by meaning. Because the verse runs the names together, the division into separate names is uncertain in a few places, and a few rare names, such as Long Pennant and Abiding There, are rendered tentatively. The name given as Free of Strife takes an unusual spelling as the word for strife.
