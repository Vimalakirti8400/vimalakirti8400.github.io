About this text

The King of the Array of the Three Pledges, known in Sanskrit as the Trisamayavyūharāja Tantra and in Tibetan as Damtsik Sum Köpé Gyalpo, is a Buddhist tantra preserved as Toh 502 in the Kangyur, where it belongs to the Tantra section among the scriptures of action tantra (kriyā tantra). Set in the great vajra palace of the dharmadhātu, where the tathāgatas and the great vajra holders gather, it presents a large body of mantras, mudrās and rites, setting out the pledges and conduct of a bodhisattva who practices through the gateway of mantra and the stages by which a practitioner becomes a vidyādhara, a holder of knowledge. Much of the work consists of mantras assigned to particular deities, among them the wrathful Acala, together with instructions for recitation, maṇḍalas and the sacred commitments that must be kept. It closes with a common rite and was translated into Tibetan by the Indian preceptor Kṛṣṇa Paṇḍita and the monk-translator Tsultrim Gyalwa.

Translation

In the language of India: trisamaya vyūha rājā nāma tantra. In the language of Tibet: The Tantra Called the King of the Array of the Three Pledges.

Homage to Mañjuśrī, the Youthful.

Homage to all the buddhas, the bodhisattvas, and the noble Saṅgha who dwell throughout the infinite world systems of the ten directions.

Measureless mountains of qualities, without center or limit,
Born supreme in great offering at boundless stūpas,
Great objects of offering, lords of the great noble ones among the noble:
To the hosts of sugatas who arise in the three times, I pay homage.

In the environs of the great vajra palace of the dharmadhātu, blessed by the Blessed One, the Tathāgata, the place where the great vajra holders (Vajradharas) assemble, where the tathāgatas sport in exceeding delight, within the supreme great palace of the king of jewels, fashioned from the substances of the king of jewels in all their variety, upon the lofty summit of the great vajra that has neither center nor limit, the Blessed One was seated upon a lion throne, surrounded by a host of great bodhisattvas.

Then the Blessed One spoke the Tathāgata Heart, made vast through the blessing of great miraculous power, the domain of all the tathāgatas, unobstructed and unfailingly accomplished:

namastraiyadhvikānāṃ tathāgatānāṃ sarvatrāpratihatāvāpti dharmatābalināṃ aṃ a samasamasamantato anantāvāpti śāsani hara hara smara smaraṇi vigatarāga buddhadharmate sara sara samabala hasa hasa traya traya gaganamahāvaraṇalakṣaṇe jvalajvalana sāgare svāhā

Son of noble family, this Tathāgata Heart is blessed by all the buddhas. Whatever deeds a bodhisattva endowed with skillful means and wisdom, who practices the conduct of a bodhisattva through the gateway of mantra, comes to perform by means of it, the Tathāgata could not finish teaching even in a hundred eons; elsewhere they have been taught only in summary.

Through it, that holy being, by limitless gateways, follows after all sentient beings and enters the various gateways of liberation, such as the gateway of liberation that focuses especially on all the tathāgatas. He sports in the meditative absorption that abides in the various kinds of application for training sentient beings. He attains the dhāraṇī of correctly applying the rite of recollecting all actions, and he manifests various forms in accordance with the elements and inclinations of sentient beings.

If, with devotion that recollects the Buddha, one recites this heart, the gateway that brings forth the power of the blessing of all the buddhas and bodhisattvas and powers of every kind, one will see various forms. In brief, one will even see the various world systems in which buddhas appear. The maṇḍala of the wheel of the Tathāgata's speech will also resound. Rains of gold, jewels, pearls, and the like will also pour down. In all the realms of sentient beings, food, nourishment, and drink will also rain down in abundance for the hungry ghosts. For the hell beings it produces warmth against the cold. It also pacifies the suffering of animals devouring one another. It purifies the realms of sentient beings whose domains are the various gateways of beings' inclinations.

It blesses them, it fills them completely, it brings them to full maturity, it trains them, it thoroughly purifies them, it fully engages them, it makes them flourish, it gladdens them, it establishes each one, it liberates them, and it makes them ascend ever higher in the vehicle of awakening. It also teaches the instructions on the grounds and the perfections by which one ascends swiftly. In brief, it makes them incline step by step toward awakening and establishes each one there. It brings about steadfastness in the practice of virtue. It exhausts suffering and the afflictions, crushes them, dispels them, leads beings on, and vanquishes. In brief, through the power of one's own aspirations, the power of merit and wisdom, and the power of the Tathāgata's blessing, one will even perform the deeds of a tathāgata throughout all the realms of sentient beings.

Here I shall also explain the rite by which the welfare of suffering beings is never allowed to decline. First of all, the mantra holder should have faith in himself as precisely this: a son or daughter of noble family who has generated the mind set on awakening, who has passed beyond the level of foolish ordinary beings, and who has received the blessing of the Tathāgata.

At the very beginning of the morning, the practitioner, the son or daughter of noble family, should offer himself to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas together with their retinues, saying: "I, named so-and-so, offer myself to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas, together with their retinues, who belong to the dharmadhātu. May those endowed with great compassion bless me and take me into their care in every way and at all times. Grant me accomplishment and the supreme!"

Then one should confess one's misdeeds, saying: "I confess all misdeeds born of attachment, aversion, and delusion." One should confess with that same generation of mind with which the buddhas and bodhisattvas, generating their resolve, made confession. Just as the buddhas, the blessed ones, know how, so too should one confess, to the full extent of one's ability.

Then one should rejoice in merit. Whatever accumulations of merit belong to the buddhas, bodhisattvas, śrāvakas, pratyekabuddhas, and all sentient beings of the three times, which fully engage the realms of sentient beings and wholly protect the realms of sentient beings, worldly and transcendent, in all of them one should rejoice with unsurpassed rejoicing. Just as the buddhas and bodhisattvas know how, so too should one rejoice, to the full extent of one's ability.

Then one should perform the act of offering. Whatever rites of offering there are in the realms of sentient beings that belong to no one and are claimed by no one, all of them one should offer by way of the offering activity of the buddhas and bodhisattvas.

Then, by the power of the blessing of all the buddhas and bodhisattvas, the power of one's own merit, and the power of the secret mantra of knowledge, one should imagine or declare that clouds of offerings rain down upon the maṇḍala of the assemblies of all the buddhas and bodhisattvas, supremely vast offerings brought forth even beyond the highest reaches of the great and supreme conduct of the bodhisattva Samantabhadra; and one should recite eight times this great queen of knowledge (vidyā), which brings about great offerings:

namaḥ sarvabuddhabodhisatvānāṃ sarvathā uṅgate spharaṇahaṃ maṃ gaganakhaṃ svāhā

Then, just as one has dedicated them to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas, so will they certainly come about. And those offerings are known to the buddhas and bodhisattvas.

Then, uttering in words or imagining an ocean-vast expanse of clouds of songs in praise of the qualities and the manifold renown of all the buddhas and bodhisattvas together with their retinues, one should recite eight times this queen of knowledge, which brings about incomparable praise of this kind:

namaḥ sarvabuddhaboddhisatvānāṃ sarvatra saṃkusumita abhijñārāśini namostute svāhā

Then one should likewise have faith that, in the same way, an ocean of clouds uttering praises of the qualities of the buddhas and bodhisattvas pours forth.

After that, one should keep in mind or utter aloud intentions of benefit toward all sentient beings, such as: "May the clouds of the emanated bodies of all the buddhas and bodhisattvas, arising from their focus on all sentient beings, pacify all the sufferings of all the realms of sentient beings, and swiftly grant every happiness!" or else: "May the practice that gives rise to the undefiled virtue of all sentient beings truly arise, and may they attain the supreme accomplishment of the tathāgatas!" And one should recite eight times this great king of knowledge, which is the cause for bringing forth the clouds of emanations of all the buddhas, purifies all the realms of sentient beings, and fully pacifies the suffering of all sentient beings:

namaḥ sarvabuddhabodhisatvānāṃ namostute mahābarja sarvasatvāhitaṃkara tiṣtha sarvatra sarvveṣāṃ dharmmāṇāṃ adhiṣthāya svāhā

Merely by reciting this king of knowledge, through the power of the blessing of all the buddhas and bodhisattvas, vast clouds of their emanations are brought forth by one's devotion, and, in brief, one will even perform the deeds of all the buddhas and bodhisattvas in the realms of sentient beings. A follower of the bodhisattva vehicle should have genuine faith in these things. And they are known only to the buddhas and bodhisattvas.

Then one should dedicate all of it, the unsurpassed accumulations of merit and wisdom, the rejoicing, and the rest, entirely to unsurpassed, perfect, and complete awakening.

After that, one should say: "To me, who am suffering, may all the buddhas and bodhisattvas grant their blessing in every way, and grant me the supreme accomplishment!"

Then, paying homage to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas, with visualization, with recitation, with earnest application, with rejoicing in every accomplishment, such as the attainment of the unsurpassed awakening of all the buddhas and bodhisattvas, and with a mind well trained in the visualizations, one should recite this unsurpassed heart for as long as one does not grow weary.

After that, longing for the buddha-awakening of all the tathāgatas, for turning the wheel of Dharma by sounding the unsurpassed lion's roar, for accomplishing the unsurpassed welfare of sentient beings, and for perfectly attaining the body of the Tathāgata Samantabhadra, one should bow to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas and then rise.

Then one should worship and read the sūtras of the vast way of knowledge that teach entry into the conduct of a bodhisattva, such as the noble Gaṇḍavyūha, or at the very least bow to them, and then one should eat one's meal. First one should consecrate the food eight times with this mantra, offer the first morsel to the deity, and only after that eat the food. The mantra for this is as follows:

namaḥ sarvabudhabodhisatvānāṃ oṃ baliṃdāte tejomālīni svāhā

Once the fatigue has been relieved by the meal, whatever is to be done in the morning is all to be done in the afternoon as well.

When going to sleep, one should first bow with all one's limbs to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas and praise them; then, seated on one's bed, one should also make this supplication: "May all the buddhas and bodhisattvas bless me and grant me the supreme, unsurpassed accomplishment! Please pacify all harm!" In the first and last watches of the night, practicing wakefulness, one should recite the sacred Dharma and so forth.

In the same way, every day that practitioner, the son or daughter of noble family, should first bow to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas; every day offer himself; likewise confess his misdeeds; rejoice in the accumulations of merit and wisdom of all the buddhas and bodhisattvas, past, future, and presently living; share his own merit with all sentient beings; bow to the volumes of the sacred Dharma and keep them; read them or meditate on them, or at the very least make offerings to them; and then with faith recite this heart. In a place such as a mountain, a temple, a house, or a private chamber, reciting with special focus on all the buddhas and bodhisattvas, with a mind intent on wholly protecting all sentient beings, seeking and yearning for the ground of the knowledge of the ten powers, one should recite it a hundred thousand times.

Then one will see the tathāgatas and bodhisattvas, and one will attain meditative absorptions, dhāraṇīs, and the like, up to entering the irreversible ground. This is the recitation rite for those of the bodhisattva vehicle who are skilled in skillful means and wisdom.

If one wishes, "May I become a holder of knowledge (vidyādhara)," then, keeping this in mind, one should complete the hundred thousand recitations. Then, on the fifteenth, the thirteenth, or the fourteenth day, one should keep the poṣadha observance for a day and a night. Having made whatever offerings one can afford, one should place a stūpa, a cast image, or a volume of the sacred Dharma before a maṇḍala strewn with a carpet of flowers, and sit on a mat of kuśa grass or on kuśa grass spread out, or sit close upon leaves. Requesting the supreme from all the buddhas and bodhisattvas, one should bow to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas; and with visualization and recitation, holding an iron wheel in one's hand, one should recite until it blazes. When one recites in this way, it will blaze, and the great earth will quake. All the holders of knowledge will gather, and they will confer empowerment. Among the family of bodhisattva holders of knowledge one will find a place that accords with one's merit and with one's own fortune. In that very state one will attain the ground of practicing the conduct of an irreversible bodhisattva, and there one's body will not decline. Through one's very skill in means, even while enjoying the five qualities of sense pleasure, one will have incomparable splendor.

Following the same procedure as for the wheel, one should also perform the accomplishment with a vajra, a spear, a sword, a single-pointed spike, a pestle, a staff, an axe, a khaṭvāṅga staff, a curved knife, a dagger, and a hook.

One who is endowed with skillful means, whose mind is set on protecting sentient beings, who has no regard for body or life, and whose attention extends to all, should take an object made of iron, gold, silver, the three metals, sandalwood, stone, or even clear wood, or one made from the substance of palm leaves; with the collection of items mentioned before, sitting alone in solitude, if one accomplishes it with a hundred thousand recitations, one will become a holder of knowledge as above.

One may perform the accomplishment on realgar, eye ointment, ochre, bezoar, and the like, placed between leaves, between pieces of cloth, or within iron vessels joined mouth to mouth; or on garments, banners, jewels, garlands of pearls, and other ornaments; or on special flowers such as the lotus and the blue lotus; or on requisites taken in the hand, such as a belt, an alms bowl, Dharma robes, or a monk's ringed staff; or on a leaf, a cloth, or a noose. If one accomplishes them, they will blaze, and one will become a holder of knowledge. One will fly and travel together with as many companions as one wishes.

This heart, the Unobstructed and Unfailingly Accomplished, will be accomplished whether by one who has merit or by one who lacks merit. Whether one keeps moral discipline or not, it will be accomplished even by one who lacks moral discipline. It will be accomplished whether by one who has skillful means or by one who lacks them. Whether one is learned or unlearned, it will certainly be effective. Otherwise, however, it is not accomplished by those who lack faith and who have not generated the mind set on awakening.

Whether there is no painted image or one worships one, whether there are offerings or none, whether there is bathing and the like or none, whether there are outer practices such as cleanliness or none, and even if fish, meat, and the like have not been entirely given up, it will be accomplished. It is accomplished by a mind that cultivates its focus on all the buddhas and bodhisattvas.

If one wishes to perform the accomplishment with a painted image, one should paint, directly in front, the Blessed One seated upon a jewel lotus on a mountain of jewels. To the left of the Blessed One one should paint the noble Vajradhara, kneeling, seated on a lotus, with palms joined and holding a vajra, the color of a blue lotus petal or deep blue like the priyaṅgu flower, or else golden in color. To the right of the Blessed One one should paint the vajra holder called Mahāvajradhara, golden in color, with matted locks and a diadem, wearing yellow garments, and holding a hook with his left hand. Below him one should paint an attendant named Ananta, whose heart is ablaze, wrathful and agitated, adorned with a jewel topknot, the color of a red aśoka flower, holding a curved knife and a noose in his hands. Below Vajradhara, on the left, one should paint a messenger called Anantā: a maiden with a single topknot, deep blue-black, playful, gazing at the retinue with delight, wearing blue garments, and holding a garland of flowers in her hand.

Here too, with the painted image, one should perform those same actions and those same accomplishments as elsewhere, or apply the procedures of the rites of all the ritual manuals, or the rite that one desires.

If one recites a hundred thousand times in a charnel ground, Vajradhara will come, take one by the hand, and lead one to Alakāvatī. One will thereby attain the state of a youth of the vajra family. One who has done the approach practice on the substances of the ground of a holder of knowledge, such as the sword, will become a holder of knowledge.

If one enters the ocean and recites a hundred thousand times, one will reach the abode of the nāga king Sāgara and will see the nāga king Sāgara. One will fully attain the body of an irreversible bodhisattva. One will become a holder of knowledge of the wish-fulfilling jewel, the vajra, the sword, the wheel, the jewel, and the like, and will be able to go to and come from many buddha fields. All the families of nāgas will become well disposed. Great rains of many kinds will pour down.

If one recites a hundred thousand times at any nāga pond, one will enter that nāga abode and will find there whatever one wishes.

If one enters the Ganges and recites, and having completed the hundred thousand recitations builds a stūpa on that very spot and performs a fire offering with honey, milk, and white mustard seed, all the nāgas will arrive in fear and will do whatever one says.

If one performs a fire offering with white mustard seed in a charnel ground, the yakṣas will act as above.

If one performs a fire offering of sesame oil and sandalwood for the gods of the mountains, or a fire offering of honey and long pepper for the holders of knowledge in a forest dwelling, they will all gather, and at all times, merely by being recalled to mind, they will carry out one's commands.

A tree whose shadow does not fall on any other tree is a solitary tree. At the foot of a solitary tree, recollecting the buddhas, one should make a stūpa and recite a hundred thousand times. One should make a trident of aśvattha wood and set it before one, and keep a fast for a day and a night. On the full-moon day one should anoint it with perfume and make whatever offerings one can afford, and then perform the accomplishment throughout the night until dawn, until it blazes. When one does so, that practitioner of the bodhisattva conduct who tames those hard to tame will become lord over the divine abundance of the four continents.

If one recites a hundred thousand times at a stūpa that contains relics, and on the full-moon day takes up a wheel emblem, a vajra emblem, or a banner, and recites until it flies, one will become a holder of knowledge who goes everywhere.

Having entered a river and recited a hundred thousand times, at the foot of a stūpa made of earth, on the full-moon day or another such day, at the place described, following the rite as before, one should take a jewel in one's hand and recite until it blazes; one will then become a holder of knowledge who bears the wish-fulfilling jewel. Moreover, if one takes that same jewel in one's hand and recites, it will become a wish-fulfilling jewel. If one performs the accomplishment on a copper vase by this same rite, the vase will become full.

Living on alms, one should take an iron vajra in one's hand and recite until it reveals what is to be told of past, future, and present; by reciting, it will reveal whatever Dharma one wishes to hear. If one performs the accomplishment further upon it, it will blaze. When it blazes, one will enter the vajra family of Vajrapāṇi. Just as with the vajra, by taking a sword, a wheel, a hook, or a book in one's hand and performing the accomplishment, one will enter the vajra family and become a holder of knowledge unobstructed everywhere.

A boy or a girl should be well bathed and adorned with ornaments. One should first do the approach practice, make whatever offerings one can afford, and, fasting for a day and a night, sit on a carpet of kuśa grass spread before a stūpa, a cast image, a volume, a painted image, or something else. Joining one's palms toward the buddhas and bodhisattvas, one should make supplication, and then perform a fire offering of sandalwood, camphor, guggulu, or turuṣka incense on smokeless embers. If these are not available, one should sit cross-legged, hold the children with one's hands, and recite until the boy and the girl blaze. By reciting, they will blaze intensely and will fly. If one takes hold of them with one's hand, one will become a holder of knowledge together with them.

Over a woman who has been well bathed and adorned with ornaments one should recite until she becomes a man.

In a charnel ground, if one recites over a lion that is painted or made of wood or clay until it walks, one will mount it and go wherever one wishes. If one recites every day without fail, whatever region one goes to will be protected. If one performs the accomplishment day and night, then within five, six, or eight days, or within the remainder of half a month, it will rise up, and one will even be victorious over the gods. Mounted on it, one will go and come wherever one wishes. In the same way, if one recites over a horse, an elephant, a bull, a buffalo, a goat, a sheep, or a deer, all of them will go and come wherever one wishes, for as long as one wishes.

In a maṇḍala well strewn with flowers, if one kindles a fire of charnel-ground wood and performs a fire offering of ghee and white mustard seed, within half a month one will certainly fly in the sky. As in the charnel ground, so too on a mountain or the like one should sit alone in solitude and perform the accomplishment; or, having cultivated faith and the like, one should perform the accomplishment; or by this same rite one should also, as one's mind desires, accomplish a wooden seat, sandals, and the like, according to the rite.

Holding on to a branch of an aśoka tree, one should recite until one goes into the sky and becomes invisible. In the same way, climbing an aśvattha tree, a nyagrodha tree, a nāgapuṣpa tree, a tilaka tree, a mango tree, or a charnel-ground tree, one should recite until one goes invisible. Having become invisible in this way, one will live for a hundred thousand years and will travel without touching the ground, a cubit above it.

Living on alms, one should recite in a charnel ground until one becomes invisible.

Having stayed five days in a charnel ground reciting, one should afterward build a stūpa of earth on that very spot and make a maṇḍala of flowers there. Placing ashes between one's cupped hands, one should recite until smoke rises; then, by making three finger marks with it, one will become invisible.

Having ground the dried root of the śamatāla into powder, one should make a maṇḍala strewn with flowers in a charnel ground, place the powder between cupped hands or between two bowls joined mouth to mouth, and recite until smoke rises. If one anoints one's eyes with it, one will not be seen.

Likewise, for going about: the roots of puṣpa, atipalā, and indravāruṇī, or śudag, aparācita, aparatinī, the root of a lotus stalk, daṇḍotpala, the blue lotus flower, human flesh, peacock flesh, the three hot spices, rajapata, white incense, or other things, ground into powder and placed between two bowls joined mouth to mouth or between cupped hands, one should recite until smoke rises. If one anoints one's eyes with it, one will become invisible.

In a charnel ground one should consecrate honey a thousand times; if one anoints one's eyes with it, one will see all that is to be seen.

In a wilderness one should paint a chariot on the side of a stūpa and recite day and night until, within half a month, it moves. Mounted on it, one will go everywhere.

Taking ochre, one should recite at a secluded stūpa, in a wilderness, on a mountain, or the like, until the three accomplishments are attained. In the same way one should perform the accomplishment with bezoar, eye ointment, and the like.

A monk should always recite while holding his alms bowl, his Dharma robes, his ringed staff, and the like, and when he sets them down, he should set them down after making a protection over them.

When one carries out such tasks as these, if one performs the accomplishment by the rite of one's own heart's desire, or performs it by the rite of the ritual manuals of all the tathāgatas, this heart of the buddhas, the blessed ones, will bring one's wishes to perfect fulfillment. One should perform the accomplishment as one wishes, by the unobstructed and effective rite of one's heart's desire.

Having thus acknowledged all the tathāgatas together with the bodhisattvas as one's lords, one should, through homage, visualization, and recitation, recollect the buddhas together with the bodhisattvas and make recitation a habit.

If, without breaking one's cross-legged posture, one sits alone at night in solitude in a fragrant, secluded place such as a mountain and recites, then at the time of sunrise, even without having done the approach practice, one will certainly attain the state of a holder of knowledge in a single night. If one does it sitting in a squatting posture, without doubt within half a month it will completely transform one's body.

Having strewn a maṇḍala with flowers and the like, facing a stūpa or facing a cast image, offering oneself to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas, if one recites eight thousand times, it removes the fault of having rejected the sacred Dharma and the obscurations of karma from beginningless saṃsāra.

If one builds a stūpa on the bank of a river, enters that same river until the water reaches one's neck, and recites, one's karmic obscurations will be exhausted. Likewise, if one circumambulates a stūpa and recites eight thousand times, one will be freed from misdeeds.

If in solitude, with eyes closed, focusing on all the buddhas and bodhisattvas, one recites until, with those very eyes closed, one sees the Tathāgata with one's mind, one will be fully freed from the obscurations of negative karma.

If one recites eight thousand times while keeping silence, one will gain entry into all maṇḍalas. Through this one will also come to know all the pledges.

Against harm, one should perform a fire offering of ghee and white mustard seed. For the sake of the sick, one should perform a fire offering of ghee and honey. For the sake of those who are angry, honey. To draw a king to oneself, one should perform a fire offering of white mustard seed soaked in sesame oil. When going into battle, bamboo thorns; or, against death, madana thorns, arka flowers, or dudurga flowers soaked in mustard oil: if one performs a fire offering with these, death will be completely averted.

If one hides a peg of mucukunda wood in a maṇḍala of the mātṛkās, the mudrās and mantras of all the ḍākinīs will be pinned down by the peg. If one draws all the kings of the holders of knowledge and strikes them with an iron peg, all the holders of knowledge, the faithless, and those who do harm will be pinned down by the peg. If one digs in an aśoka peg at a crossroads of a highway, it will paralyze great grahas.

For all fevers, such as the one-day fever, if one wraps the head with a thread or with fragrant incense and fumigates the person, it will drive them out, and, made known by the deity, the fever will speak. In the same way, any fire offering will also do away with nāgas, yakṣas, and the like.

At the time of a lunar eclipse, one should recite over ghee placed in a vessel of copper, iron, gold, or silver until the three accomplishments are attained; if one drinks it, one will become one who retains all one hears, or a holder of knowledge.

At the time of a solar eclipse, one should place ochre on a leaf of the aśoka tree, make a maṇḍala strewn with flowers at the foot of a stūpa in a wilderness or a stūpa on a mountain, and recite until the three accomplishments are attained; it will blaze, and one will become a holder of knowledge. As with ochre, so too one should perform the accomplishment with ligbu mik, realgar, and the like at the time of a lunar eclipse or a solar eclipse.

One who wishes to enter the domain of activity of the buddhas and bodhisattvas, or who wishes to transfer to another support, or who wishes to cross over, or who wishes to protect sentient beings and suffering people from saṃsāra, or who wishes in this very life to apply himself to the deeds of a buddha, or who wishes to engage others correctly in what clearly appears, or who wishes swiftly to obtain the proclamation in the form of "Excellent!" from the buddhas, the blessed ones, or who wishes to find birth upon the irreversible ground, should train in this conduct of a bodhisattva, the gateway of the conduct of mantra.

This must also be embraced by skill in means: one should make vast aspirations, confess one's misdeeds, and rejoice in the accumulations of merit and wisdom, worldly and transcendent, that arise in the three times; and one should dedicate in this way: "May this rejoicing, too, be fully applied as the very cause of buddhahood for all the realms of sentient beings."

Focusing on all the buddhas and bodhisattvas, one should first say aloud, or afterward reflect: "May the welfare of sentient beings come about!" Or, focusing on them, one should first say aloud or contemplate: "By the power of my merit and the power of my aspirations, may all the buddhas and bodhisattvas bless me, and may this welfare of sentient beings come about!" And then one should recite.

In a secluded place, beginning on the first day of the month and continuing for one month, one should read, reflect upon, and worship the exceedingly vast sūtras, such as the noble Gaṇḍavyūha, that thoroughly enter into the causes of the conduct of a bodhisattva. Then, on the full-moon day, if one makes offerings and recites throughout the whole night, the tathāgatas will come, stroke one's head with their hands, and say, "Excellent, excellent, holy being!" One will obtain the dhāraṇī of non-forgetting and others, and, following the conduct of a bodhisattva, one will attain the five higher perceptions.

Contemplating, "May this become the cause for sentient beings to awaken to buddhahood," one should recite. Likewise one should recite thus: "May the illnesses of all sentient beings be pacified, and may all who are tormented by suffering find complete peace!" One should recite: "May warmth arise in the cold hells, and coolness itself arise in the hot hells!"

One should recite: "May the animals cease devouring one another!" "May all suffering and unhappiness cease throughout all the realms of sentient beings! May all happiness and joy arise! May food and drink appear for the hungry ghosts!" Thus: "May enjoyments arise for all sentient beings who lack material goods! May the impetus arise for the virtue of all sentient beings to increase greatly! May that happiness and joy arise for them! May the impetus of virtue arise, such as faith, joy, supreme joy and the like, and rejoicing and the like! May all the accumulations of merit and wisdom be completely perfected! May sentient beings each come to abide on the ground of one who knows the ten powers!" With these and other such words, focusing on what will be a cause of benefit for sentient beings, one should recite the mantra. In brief, one should recite the mantra thinking, "May the afflictions, sufferings and so forth that belong to saṃsāra be completely abandoned, and may the accumulations of a bodhisattva be completely perfected!"

By reciting in this way, through the blessing of the tathāgatas, the power of aspiration, and the power of merit and wisdom, there will arise in due order, in the realm of sentient beings, the lesser, middling and great forms of benefit for sentient beings. These too the Tathāgata will know. One should also have faith in that child of good family who, with vast devotion and with faith, longs for awakening.

An illustration of this is as follows. In the desire realm there is one called Īśvara, the Lord. By the vidyā called Producer of Desire, he overpowers with desire the sons of the gods who move in the desire realm, and he also displays the full enjoyment of the pleasures of desire. He provides many flowers of various kinds, places where there are jewels and gems, requisites and enjoyments. Likewise, there is a son of the gods called Īśvara who, by the vidyā called Swift as Mind, carries out the tasks of the great trichiliocosm, and, emanating requisites and enjoyments, provides them even to the sons of the gods of the Pure Abodes. Likewise, by the mantra called Vidyādhara, one emanates garments, drinks, celestial palaces, pleasure groves, perfumes, flowers, powders, garlands and so forth, enjoys them fully, and gives them to others. Likewise, the one called the conjurer of manifold illusions displays illusions of many kinds. Likewise, the asuras produce illusions by mantra. Likewise, physicians by mantra dispel the fevers of many sentient beings, and dispel pretas, poisons and diseases. Likewise, by mantra one takes away the heat of fire, and the cold too becomes warm. Likewise, those who do harm seize beings with fever. Likewise, by the rites of the mātṛkās they kill and injure many sentient beings. Again, against the wicked, by wicked mantras they bind them to wickedness. Again, by the mantra that stirs the minds of yakṣas, they bring about delight of mind, happiness and the like, and other things as well. Such displays as these should be regarded as the power of mantra.

The power of secret mantra does not arise from the secret mantra itself, nor does it enter from sentient beings, and no applying of it is observed either; yet the power of the power of secret mantra should be fully understood to come from the prior rite of approach. It does not go beyond what arises from the nature of reality of blessing. And this nature of reality of all phenomena neither goes nor comes; it is not an object of perception, not something that makes go, not an agent, and not an act. It is true and undeceiving. Therefore, with faith, one should accomplish the aims of all sentient beings by means of this mantra.

Those who wish for the qualities of the nature of reality seen in this very life, who wish to enter the conduct of a bodhisattva, who wish to perfect the accumulations of merit and wisdom, and who wish to obtain the precious qualities one by one should practice it. If one practices it by this application of recitation for the sake of sentient beings, one will very swiftly accomplish the state of a vidyādhara and the like, just as one wishes. Furthermore, if one contemplates whatever state of accomplishment, as it has been described, will become a cause of benefit for sentient beings, and performs a hundred thousand recitations, one will certainly see the face of the Tathāgata, who will stroke one's head and swiftly bestow the accomplishments one wishes. One may apply it to whatever work of the mind one wishes; one should apply it with other ritual procedures, or with the procedures of all the ritual manuals for this mantra.

From the rites taught in the King of the Three Samayas, the chapter on the tathāgatagarbha is complete.

Then at that time the Blessed One entered the meditative absorption called Supreme of All, King of Absorptions. As soon as he had entered it, from all the limbs of his body there came forth the king of vidyās called Supreme of All the Domains of the Tathāgatas:

namaḥ sarva buddha bodhi satvā nāṃ oṃ amala vikrāṃ ta tejīni araje svāhā

Vajrapāṇi, this is the supreme vidyā of the tathāgatas whose domain of Dharma has neither end nor middle, the unobstructed great vidyā-mantra accomplished without fail, the vast, wide and boundless application of the rite. By it the bodhisattva who has entered the gateway of mantra and practices the conduct of a bodhisattva carries out the rites. But a bodhisattva who has first generated the mind of awakening with a careless mind will go mad, unless blessed by the tathāgatas. In brief, by merely reciting it one will perform the deeds of a buddha.

Having attained the conduct of secret mantra, one will truly display to all the realms of sentient beings the bodies of tathāgatas and the bodies of bodhisattvas in manifold forms. Suited to beings of various inclinations, in order to turn them back from saṃsāra, one goes everywhere like a conjurer, unobstructed toward all. Thinking, "By this great secret mantra, which has arisen from the accumulations of merit and wisdom of all the buddhas and bodhisattvas, through the power of the blessing of the tathāgatas, which has neither end nor middle, and blessed by the movement of a mind enriched by virtue, may the wheel of speech of the tathāgatas, and other things as well, arise from the realms of sentient beings and be heard!", one should focus on protecting all sentient beings, recite the mantra, and have faith in the force of the power of the blessing of the tathāgatas. Therefore a follower of the Bodhisattva Vehicle should apply it even if only to holding it in memory.

In this ritual manual, accomplishment is blessed by the meaningful and unobstructed power of aspiration and the power of blessing of the buddhas, the blessed ones. Of this, I shall here explain just a portion of the means of attaining the state of a vidyādhara.

Here, a follower of the Bodhisattva Vehicle who is endowed with faith should first, as before, offer his own body to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas. Then he should confess the misdeeds of all times; he should confess, as well as he is able, those that have arisen from the afflictions and secondary afflictions of body, speech and mind. Then he should rejoice in all the accumulations of merit and wisdom of all the buddhas and bodhisattvas of the past, the future and the present, saying: "I rejoice in their protecting and benefiting all sentient beings. I rejoice also in the immeasurable tathāgatas' working for the benefit of sentient beings, and in the bodhisattvas' very working for the benefit of sentient beings." Then he should also reflect thus: "If, having rejoiced, thinking that the accumulation of merit and wisdom of even a single tathāgata is immeasurable, one dedicates it fully for the sake of all sentient beings, a vast ripened result without end or middle will arise; what need, then, to speak of rejoicing after one has comprehended the buddhas and bodhisattvas who are infinite, whose limit cannot be seen, and who are immeasurable?" Or else he should say, "I rejoice in whatever way the buddhas, the blessed ones, know."

Then he should dedicate, as an offering to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas, whatever has truly arisen in all the world systems that is not owned and not appropriated by anyone, such as things growing in the open plains and mountains of jewels. Then, firmly thinking, "Through the power of my merit, may manifold, sublime and vast clouds of offerings, emanated from the sphere of reality, arise in the maṇḍalas of the retinues of all the tathāgatas, through the blessing of the tathāgatas and the power of my aspiration!", he should recite the following up to eight times:

namaḥ sarva buddha bodhi satvā nāṃ sarvathā uṅgate spharaṇa hīmāṃ gaganakaṃ samantāta svāhā

Right after that, the offerings will arise just so, not otherwise, just as the tathāgatas know them. Then, thinking, "May infinite, vast oceans of praises of the qualities of all the tathāgatas and all the bodhisattvas arise, through the power of my aspiration, the power of my merit, and the blessing of the tathāgatas!", he should recite this mantra up to eight times:

namaḥ sarva buddha bodhi satvā nāṃ sarvatrāṃ saṃkusumitā abhijñāna rāśini namostute svāhā

Then oceans of true praise will arise, just as the tathāgatas know them. Then he should say aloud: "May all the buddhas and bodhisattvas bless me, who am suffering in every way! Please grant me accomplishment and the supreme!" Then he also says: "May all sentient beings fully obtain the stainless accumulation of roots of virtue that truly arises from yoga!" Likewise, keeping in mind the bestowal of benefit and happiness on all sentient beings without end or middle, he should recite up to eight times the mantra of the tathāgatagarbha taught in the application that brings forth such clouds of emanation.

Then, paying homage to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas and focusing on them, he should make the recitation habitual; with a mind focused on rejoicing in the buddhas and bodhisattvas who are accomplishing unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening and who have accomplished all the qualities of a buddha, he should make it habitual and recite, for as long as he does not grow weary. Then, with devotion to the tathāgatas who, by turning the wheel of Dharma, roar the unsurpassed lion's roar and work the unsurpassed benefit of sentient beings, and to the Tathāgata Samantabhadra's perfect body of wisdom, he should pay homage to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas and rise. Then he should spend the time until the morning period reading the vast and extensive sūtras, such as the noble Gaṇḍavyūha, or else anything else, as he has in mind. Then, having worshipped the volumes of scripture and so forth, or at the very least having paid homage to a stūpa, he should eat his meal. When eating, he should consecrate the food by reciting this mantra over it eight times, offer the first handful as an oblation to the deities, and then eat. The mantra for this is:

namaḥ sarva buddha bodhi satvā nāṃ oṃ baliṃtāte tejomālini svāhā

Having rested a little from the tiredness brought on by the food, in the afternoon period too he should perform this same rite. When going to sleep, first he should prostrate with all his limbs to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas and offer himself; after that he may sleep on his bed as he likes, but he should not sleep during the first and last parts of the night. The rite for this is as follows.

Beginning from the first day of the month, one should do this day after day until one month is complete. If one fails to perform some part of the rite, or, being busy with other affairs, lapses from the practice for one, two, three or more days, one should not on that account abandon one's diligence. On the full-moon day of that same month, for one day and night, in front of the Blessed One, or in front of a stūpa, an image, or a volume of the sublime Dharma, or in front of something else, one should make whatever offerings one can afford, sit upon strewn kuśa grass, and perform the rite of protection. Then one should take in one's hand whichever one wishes of the implements taught in the chapter on the tathāgatagarbha: a wheel, a vajra, a trident, a spear or a khaṭvāṅga, whether made of iron or of clay. If one performs the practice with it, at midnight or at sunrise it will certainly blaze. With that very implement one becomes a vidyādhara: in that instant all the families of vidyādharas gather around and confer empowerment upon one.

From then on one travels through the sky in all the world systems, follows into the maṇḍalas of the retinues of all the tathāgatas, and, having entered upon the conduct of a bodhisattva who makes great offerings, while remaining endowed with bliss, one becomes a great king of vidyā-mantra holders, worshipped by all the gods, nāgas and yakṣas and delighting all the vidyādharas. Even though one fully enjoys an infinite retinue of women and the like, one does not degenerate, and without abandoning that very body one follows on in bliss. By this very means one will gradually attain even the wisdom body of the buddhas and bodhisattvas.

Just as with the implements such as the wheel, one may also accomplish with substances such as realgar, eye ointment, orpiment, bezoar, cloth, kuśa grass, a jewel, a peacock feather, a vessel, sandals, a parasol, a yak-tail whisk, a victory banner, a pennant, a lotus, a blue lotus, and a sword, a curved knife, a knife, a khaṭvāṅga and so on made from palm leaves, or with any others. Whatever one has in mind, one may also apply it to vidyā-mantras, secret mantras and other ritual procedures; and if one performs the practice with the substances taught in the tathāgatagarbha chapter, with the rite taught in the tathāgatagarbha chapter, or with whatever rite one has in mind, by that very means one becomes a vidyādhara.

This is the rite for receiving accomplishment in the presence of all the buddhas and bodhisattvas. In a secluded spot as described, in front of a stūpa, an image, a volume of the sublime Dharma, or anything else, one makes a maṇḍala of scattered flowers; whether or not one makes offerings of perfume, flowers, garlands and so on as one can afford, one sits close by on a bundle of kuśa grass, takes a substance such as realgar in the hand or elsewhere, places it between leaves or within cloth, or in a vessel of iron, silver or gold, and recites until it blazes. In that way, in accordance with one's merit, one becomes a vidyādhara. It is called "Not to Be Taken Away by Others." This is the rite of followers of the Bodhisattva Vehicle who are skilled in means.

By this same rite one should practice in seclusion: on a secluded and empty mountain, by a river, by a small lake, in a pleasant grove, at a solitary tree, at a monastic dwelling, at the foot of a stūpa containing relics, where an image is enshrined, or else in a cell of a temple and the like, free from bustle. Or, having made oneself familiar with the recollection of the Buddha, endowed with a mind of benefit toward all sentient beings, endowed with diligence, endowed with faith, without avarice, with a mind free of doubt, one should be unconcerned even for one's body and life.

Further, the rite common to all is this. In secluded and empty places, such as a mountain, a riverbank or the side of a stream, one dwells with a volume of the sublime Dharma as one's only companion. At the three times one confesses misdeeds and rejoices in the accumulations of merit and wisdom, and then, supplicating in the presence of the buddhas and bodhisattvas, one recites until a sign appears. On the full moon of the waxing fortnight, following the previous rite, one keeps a fast for one day and night, takes any of the substances described, and recites until it blazes; by that very means one becomes a vidyādhara.

This supreme and meaningful vidyā is accomplished by its very nature; unobstructed, it brings about the fulfillment of the mind's hopes. In whatever way one applies it, in that way it will be accomplished. Since it is accomplished by its nature and succeeds through being recited, by mere recitation it does everything one desires. One should apply it in accordance with the ritual procedure of the meaningful manual, or practice it however one wishes.

Or else, if one wishes to practice with a painted cloth, the Blessed One should be painted seated cross-legged on a lotus seat. To the right of the Blessed One one should paint the noble Vajrapāṇi, surrounded by blazing light, being empowered by rays of light issuing from the Blessed One's crown ornament. To the left of the Blessed One is the queen called Supreme Vidyā, golden in color, with matted locks, wearing clean garments, seated on a lotus seat, joining her palms toward the Blessed One, adorned with a garland of light. Below the Blessed Lady one should paint the attendant called Ananta, holding a sword and a noose in his hands, dark blue in color, wrathful and agitated, wearing a pair of red and yellow garments, frowning in anger, the subduer of all obstructors, a servant, seated on a rock of jewels. Below the noble Vajrapāṇi one should paint the practitioner with palms joined, seated on a lotus stalk on the jewel rock, surrounded on all sides by flowering creepers. In front of such a painted cloth one should apply this rite as one wishes, or the procedures of other ritual manuals.

Another painted cloth of the Supreme Vidyā should be painted absorbed in meditative absorption, seated on a red lotus. Below her one should paint the servant Ananta, holding a sword and a noose in his hands, just as before. With this painted cloth too one should perform all those rites.

Another rite is this. In a secluded spot, such as a mountain, utterly solitary, one sits cross-legged, focuses on the Tathāgata together with the bodhisattvas, and recites throughout the whole night without breaking one's cross-legged posture. Then, even without having first done the approach, one will become a vidyādhara at dawn or at midnight. Endowed with unequaled might, one will be worshipped by the irreversible vidyādharas, and worshipped by all sentient beings. Whoever obtains this Supreme Vidyā in hand will be blessed by all the buddhas, and will be truly protected by the whole vajra family. By applications such as the fire offering one should summon gods, nāgas and the like, and accomplish whatever else one has in mind according to the procedures of other ritual manuals. One should apply, doubled, the best of whatever belongs to the rite of the tathāgatagarbha.

The Supreme Vidyā called the Arising of the Tathāgatas is complete.

Then the bodhisattva great being called Cakravajra entered the meditative absorption called Gazing upon the Domain of All the Buddhas. Then, through the blessing of all the tathāgatas, there arose what is called the Syllable of Vajra Force, and from it there arose these vajra words of the Supreme Vidyā:

namaḥ sarva buddha bodhi satvā nāṃ oṃ piraji viraji mahāviraji satva satva sārate sārate trāyi trāyi vidha mahātī sabtañjani tarmati siddhā agre traṃ svāhā

For this Supreme Vidyā called Cakravajra too, whatever is applied doubled, first the ritual procedure of the tathāgatagarbha manual within the ritual procedure of the first Supreme Vidyā, equal to the unequaled, is likewise applied doubled. The distinctive feature of its painted cloth is this. The Blessed One is seated cross-legged on a great jewel lotus growing from a rock of jewels, his hand granting the supreme. On the Blessed One's left one should paint the Supreme Vidyā, holding a wheel in her hand, adorned with all ornaments, seated on a rock of jewels. On the Blessed One's right is the vajra holder called Cakravajra, holding a wheel in his hand, slightly dark blue, seated on a rock of jewels, encircled on all sides by the rays of light of the Tathāgata. Below him one paints the practitioner, paying homage. Below the goddess one should paint that same attendant called Ananta, the subduer of obstructors, holding a sword and a noose in his hands. The second painted cloth has the goddess alone, holding a wheel in her hand. Below her, likewise, is Ananta, holding a sword and a noose in his hands, a wrathful one in the form of a youth, with a quivering wrathful frown. Here too the rite is performed with the previous one. It may also be performed however one wishes, or with the procedures of other ritual manuals. Here the distinctive feature is this: by merely reciting it one comes to know all the samayas and enters all the maṇḍalas.

The Supreme Vidyā called Cakravajra is complete.

Then the vajra holder called Amṛtavajra, through the power of the blessing of all the buddhas and bodhisattvas, entered the meditative absorption called King of Samayas. From it there manifestly arose the vajra sound of the wheel of words of truth called Infinite Domain of Play, and from that this Supreme Vidyā called Amṛtavajra was heard:

namaḥ sarva buddha bodhi satvā nāṃ amṛta amṛta śikhi śikhi vimala vimala vīrājīnī vīrājīnī hara hara vidāṃ mahāsamaya vajra agre svāhā

The rite of this Amṛtavajra, too, is that of the three Supreme Vidyās taught before. Here the distinctive feature of its painted cloth is this. One should paint the Blessed One in the same way, seated on a great jewel lotus atop a jewel mountain, his hand granting the supreme. On the Blessed One's left one should paint the goddess, dark blue like a priyaṅgu flower, adorned with all ornaments, holding in her hand a banner of the wish-fulfilling jewel. On the Blessed One's right one should paint the vajra holder called Amṛtavajra, absorbed in meditative absorption, seated on a jewel lotus, two jewel banners raised on either side, encircled on all sides by the rays of light of the Tathāgata. Below him one paints the practitioner as before. Below the goddess one should paint the servant Ananta, holding a sword and a noose in his hands, in the guise of a youth, the subduer of vināyakas. The second painted cloth is painted in the same way with the goddess holding the wish-fulfilling jewel in her hand, and the attendant Ananta is also as before. Likewise, free of doubt, one should apply this ritual manual. Here too one should apply, doubled, the ritual procedure of the tathāgatagarbha manual.

The Supreme Vidyā called Amṛtavajra is complete.

Then the bodhisattva called King of Heroes entered the meditative absorption called the Power of the Equality of the Three. Then he spoke this Supreme Vidyā called the Power of the Three Vajras, taught by all the tathāgatas of the three times:

namaḥ sarva buddha bodhi satvā nāṃ aha ha ha vīṃ vīṃ bhrāmaṇi tripalini svāhā

By this heart of the Supreme Vidyā called the Power of the Three Vajras, whatever was not accomplished in those others will be accomplished. Its rite is those very same rites. The distinctive feature of its painted cloth is this. In the place of the Supreme Vidyā one should paint a maiden, Alakāvatī, adorned with all ornaments, seated on a lotus seat, joining her palms toward the Blessed One. On the Blessed One's right, in the place of the vajra holder, one should likewise paint the bodhisattva called King of Heroes, golden in color, seated cross-legged on a lotus in the equipoise of meditative absorption, likewise encircled by a garland of light. Below him is the practitioner.

Below the goddess, likewise, is Ananta. For the second painted cloth one should paint, in the same way, the goddess Alakāvatī seated on a jewel lotus, together with her attendants. With this painted cloth too, joining it with the tathāgatagarbha manual, one should likewise perform all the rites doubled.

The Supreme Vidyā called the Power of the Three Vajras is complete.

Then the bodhisattva called Vajra Abiding in Equality entered the meditative absorption called Vajra, Supreme Domain of Great Power. Having entered it, he spoke this eight-syllable Supreme Vidyā, meaningful and not to be taken away, which in all things does not depend on the blessing of the tathāgatas:

namaḥ sarva buddha bodhi satvā nāṃ asahānāmāsama hūṃ

This is the unsurpassed pinnacle, the king, the supreme of great vidyās. There is no accomplishment whatsoever that it does not accomplish, and it is not blameworthy. One should apply it likewise with the ritual procedures taught before; it is to be done with the tathāgatagarbha rite tripled. For the painted cloth one should paint the Tathāgata seated on a lotus. On the Blessed One's right one should paint the bodhisattva seated on a lotus, in the equipoise of the absorption of abiding in equality. On the Blessed One's left one should paint the maiden Alakāvatī, adorned with all ornaments, seated cross-legged on a lotus, encircled by a blazing garland, in meditative equipoise. Below the Supreme Vidyā one should paint the one called Ananta, holding a vajra in his hand, dark blue and wrathful, encircled by a blazing garland, frowning in anger, the servant who subdues obstructors. Below the bodhisattva is the practitioner. The second painted cloth is that same maiden with Ananta below her. Here too one should apply the rites, as before, with whatever ritual procedure one has in mind or with the procedures of other ritual manuals.

The Supreme Vidyā called Unforgetting Vajra is complete.

Then the vajra holder called Samaya Vajra Heap spoke this mantra:

namaḥ sarva buddha bodhi satvā nāṃ hāṃ hāṃ asamacinta mahāvajra viśvadhara ananta vikrānta jāsāṃ hūṃ phaṭ

Its rite is this: one should apply the ritual procedures taught before.

The Supreme Vidyā called Supreme Samaya is complete.

The vajra holder called Vajrakīlikīla likewise entered meditative absorption and spoke this Supreme Vidyā called the Beautiful Maiden of the Vajra Family:

namaḥ sarva buddha bodhi satvā nāṃ acala sambara viṣaye hūṃ mahāvajre gateje hī hī asama mahāvidho uddhame ha ha siddhi siddhi vara aṃga deva rāga he sphara jvala nāma svāhā

This is the Supreme Vidyā called the Beautiful Lady of the Vajra Family.

The bodhisattva entered the meditative absorption called All-Encompassing Vajra and spoke this Supreme Vidyā:

namaḥ sarva buddha bodhi satvā nāṃ avajra mahāvajra mahābala sara vīrājāte sama sama khuru khuru viṣmo dhari dhari sīri sīri vare svāhā

This is the Supreme Vidyā called Supreme Hero.

The bodhisattva entered the meditative absorption called Acalavajra and spoke this Supreme Vidyā:

namaḥ sarva buddha bodhi satvā nāṃ asamanta vātri acala vara aṃ vilasinī khele vara uttama hāsini harāte ghana maṇa deni haṃ

This is the Supreme Vidyā called Body of Supreme Immovability.

The bodhisattva entered the meditative absorption called Compassion Vajra and spoke this mantra:

namaḥ sarva buddha bodhi satvā nāṃ amṛte amṛte śikhine śikhine samate samate asame asame gagane gagne tiṣtha tiṣtha asahe hūṃ jvāla jvāla svāhā

This is the Supreme Vidyā called Pinnacle of Nectar.

The youth called Vajra of Utter Play entered meditative absorption and spoke this Supreme Vidyā called Unequaled Beauty:

namaḥ sarva buddha bodhi satvā nāṃ āti āti vara vara piri piri samaliṣṭiṇi tiri tiri hāsa hāsa ārīṇi bandhaya mahābhogini tahāta mahāvajra guṇoṅgate vajrale svāhā

This is the Supreme Vidyā called All-Pervading.

The bodhisattva called Vajra of the Tathāgata's Light Rays spoke this eight-syllable Supreme Vidyā called the Power of the Tathāgata:

namaḥ sarva buddha bodho satvā nāṃ ananta satvā maṃga hūṃ

The bodhisattva called Power of the Wheel entered the meditative absorption called Gateway of the Power of the Pinnacle of the King of All Powers, and spoke this Supreme Vidyā called Blessing the Domain of All the Tathāgatas:

namaḥ sarva buddha bodhi satvā nāṃ asama mahāvaradayagi titi titi amū amū khaṃ khaṃ ṇā asamaguṇā akāra titi titi agra vālo adhaye taṃ svāhā

The bodhisattva called All-Encompassing Wheel Vajra entered the meditative absorption called Gazing upon the Supreme Domain and spoke this Supreme Vidyā:

namaḥ sarva buddha bodhi satvā nāṃ ati ati sama sama kīli kila bar bar samaye hasa hasa asate trayi trayi gane kuru kuru ajite svāhā

This is the Supreme Vidyā called Most Supreme Vajra.

Then the bodhisattva called Vajra of Blessing entered the meditative absorption called Radiating the Gateways of Wisdom in the Ten Directions. As soon as he had entered it, in the vast oceans of world systems there appeared many and various signs, great, sublime, vast and wondrous. Then this ten-syllable mantra, spoken by all the tathāgatas of the ten directions, arose and was heard:

namaḥ sarva buddha bodhi satvā nāṃ mahāvajrā vikrānta agrasa hūṃ

This is the Supreme Vidyā called Light of the Ten Directions.

The bodhisattva called Vajra of the Tathāgata's Light Rays spoke this Supreme Vidyā:

namaḥ sarva buddha bodhi satvā nāṃ ārya agra te jvāla jvāla svāhā

This is the Supreme Vidyā called Three Eyes, whose domain is the unsurpassed Summit.

Then the Blessed One brought forth the five-syllable Supreme Vidyā called Proclaimed Everywhere:

namaḥ sarva buddha bodhi satvā nāṃ mahā bala hūṃ

Then Vajradhara, his own vajra having been blessed by all the buddhas, spoke his own Supreme Vidyā:

namaḥ sarva buddha bodhi satvā nāṃ oṃ vajra netra jvāla jvāla kuru vara kuru vara vira vira mahā bala ati ho mahā samaya teja lan hūṃ

Then Vajrapāṇi in turn blessed that very vajra:

namaḥ sarva buddha bodhi satvā nāṃ mahā candra hūṃ

This is the Supreme Vidyā called Supreme Secret.

The noble Vajrasena spoke his own Supreme Vidyā:

namaḥ sarva buddha bodhi satvā nāṃ a vajra hūṃ

This four-syllable Supreme Vidyā is called Four-Faced.

The vajra holder called Krodhavajra entered the meditative absorption called Power of Splendor and spoke this Supreme Vidyā:

namaḥ sarva buddha bodhi sa tvā nāṃ agra te jo bala hūṃ rakṣa rakṣa dama dama jāla na mahā hara hara viṣaṇa tara tara nada nada kara kara haṃ haṃ amogha apratihata amṛta bala jvāla jvāla svāhā

This is the Supreme Vidyā called Power of Splendor. It is fierce; it protects, upholds, slays and accomplishes; it is unobstructed and unfailing. By the procedure taught earlier it accomplishes limitless rites. The procedure of the Tathāgatagarbha should be applied threefold.

These Supreme Vidyās have arisen from the domain of all the buddhas. They are inconceivable, limitless and endless, and they purify the realm of sentient beings. They should be applied to the sequence of procedures taught earlier.

Those who wish to obtain immeasurable life in this very birth, who wish to enjoy immeasurable happiness, who wish to enter the noble ground of non-regression, who wish to make offerings to the buddhas and bodhisattvas, and who wish to see them, will succeed by means of this irresistible and unfailing vidyā, once they have abandoned doubt and are endowed with faith and discernment.

It will bring success whether or not there is a painted image, whether or not the preliminary practice has been done, and even without offerings. It will bring success even without bathing and the other observances of purity, even if meat and fish have not been given up, and whether or not one has merit. Whether one has moral discipline or lacks it, whether one is lowly or not, learned or not, possessed of skillful means or lacking them, one will certainly succeed, with the exception of those who have no faith and those who have not generated the mind set on awakening.

Further, the procedure for this Supreme Vidyā is shared with the procedures of the ritual manuals taught earlier. I shall state in brief just a portion of its special procedure.

This Summit, the Supreme Vidyā, king of great vidyās, is accomplished by its very nature and accomplished by being read; by mere recitation it accomplishes the perfect fulfillment of one's desired aims. If one recites it preceded by the visualization of purifying the realms of sentient beings, it should be applied directly, through the special power of accomplishing an endless range of rites: protection, the maṇḍala, binding, striking, bringing down a spirit into a medium, paralyzing, expelling, summoning, subduing the malevolent, burning, boiling, displaying power, summoning gods, nāgas and others, bringing under control, accomplishing aims, summoning and winning over kings and princesses, increasing the approach, dispelling illness, bringing nāgas, yakṣas and others under control, and so forth.

Whatever the rite, and whatever the fire offering, whatever the means by which each is accomplished, by that very means one should perform it according to the procedure for that rite.

Generating diligence as best one can, and reproaching the human body as hard to tame, one should go into a mountain retreat. Confessing one's misdeeds at the three times of day, rejoicing in merit, and making dedication, one should recite, according to one's capacity, until a sign appears.

Then, on the day of the full moon, take a wheel or a sword, made of gold, silver or copper, or made of iron or precious substances, or even made of palm leaf. Having made whatever offerings one can afford, or having made protection with any mantra whatever, one should recite until it blazes. Then whoever is struck with that weapon, he too, together with his retinue, will become a vidyādhara. As with the wheel and the rest, so it is with all weapons. By the common procedure one should accomplish this with all substances, such as orpiment, realgar, eye ointment and the like.

Here, application is of two kinds: with skillful means and without skillful means. In this regard, by whatever procedure and according to one's ability, if one recites while bringing to mind, "May whatever I do become a cause for benefit and happiness to arise for the realms of sentient beings," one will quickly obtain accomplishment. Likewise, one should recite while rightly thinking, "May this become a cause for the accomplishment of all sentient beings." Likewise, having made applications that benefit sentient beings, such as "May the realms of sentient beings enter into virtue," and having aroused devotion, one should accomplish it. Focusing on all the buddhas and bodhisattvas and paying homage to them, one should recite.

Focusing with effort on aspiring to the grounds of all the buddhas and bodhisattvas, one recites and confesses one's misdeeds; one recites for the sake of aspiring to perfect completely all the accumulations of merit and wisdom; one recites while focusing on aspiring to perfect the maṇḍala of aspirations of the conduct of the bodhisattva Samantabhadra; one recites while focusing on aspiring to be pervaded by the wisdom of the ten powers; and having rejoiced in all of these, if one then accomplishes it with any of the Supreme Vidyās whatever, one will quickly obtain accomplishment.

If one performs a hundred thousand recitations in a mountain recess, one will see a bodhisattva, whichever it may be, such as the noble Vajrapāṇi. With two hundred thousand, one will see the blessed buddhas. With three, one will converse with the bodhisattvas. With four, one will hear the speech of the tathāgatas. With five, one will obtain the dhāraṇīs. With six, one will travel through the sky. With seven, one will fully obtain the five higher perceptions. With eight, one will make offerings to all the buddhas. With nine, one brings about nirvāṇa. With ten, one will roam through the world systems. These are the procedures of recitation for those skilled in means, and they become the cause for obtaining limitless results.

Even those not skilled in means, whether their diligence is great, middling or slight, if they apply themselves thoroughly in accordance with the approach they have performed, will become vidyādharas in a single night, as before.

In some solitary and empty place, such as a mountain, a cave or a cavelike chamber, strewn with loose fragrant flowers and perfumed with fragrant incense, one should sit cross-legged at the time of sunset. Focusing on all the buddhas and bodhisattvas, without breaking the cross-legged posture, one recites any one of the Supreme Vidyās until midnight or until sunrise. Then one's body will blaze and become the color of gold, and one will become a vidyādhara who travels through the sky and goes everywhere. If one does not succeed, one should rest for one night in between and do the same again on the third night. And on the fifth night, again with one night in between, even one who has rejected the sublime Dharma will certainly succeed.

Having performed the rite of protection and recited a hundred thousand times, whatever substance one accomplishes it upon will be accomplished. Holding in the hand a wheel made of iron, or a vajra, a sword or the like, one should recite until it blazes; by that very means one will become a vidyādhara. If it does not blaze, one should put it inside cow dung, make protection with any one of the Supreme Vidyās, and set it aside.

Staying in a charnel ground, one should recite until one becomes a yakṣa. Then, sitting at the foot of an aśoka tree, one should recite until a yakṣiṇī comes. When she has come and says, "What shall I do?", if one tells her whatever one wishes, she will do it all, and embracing one, she will enter one's own house.

At the foot of a plakṣa tree, on a maṇḍala strewn with loose flowers, one should draw the mount of a god; if one recites until it blazes, one will become a vidyādhara. In a mountain recess one should draw a lion or a horse and, staying right there, recite until it blazes. Likewise, having drawn an elephant, a buffalo, a sheep, a goat, a goose, a peacock or the like, and having made a maṇḍala adorned with loose flowers, one should recite through the night until daybreak. If one does not succeed that night, one should practice again.

While recollecting the Buddha, one should enter a temple of the Great God (Śiva) and recite until the Great God comes in his own form. Then, if one sleeps right there, he will come and do whatever one tells him. Holding in one's hand an image of Nārāyaṇa, if one recites until Nārāyaṇa comes, he will come and do whatever one tells him. Whatever god it may be in whose temple one recites, that god will certainly come within half a month, or else he will wither.

On the eighth or the fourteenth day of the waxing fortnight, taking orpiment, one should sit to the north of the Great God, close to a bundle of kuśa grass, and recite until it blazes; then one will become a vidyādhara or become invisible.

Having drawn Vaiśravaṇa, one should recite until he comes before one and grants a boon. At a pond that is an abode of nāgas, one should recite until the nāgas rise up. If they do not rise, one should fill an iron or copper vessel with sesame oil over a fire of husks and recite until it boils. If one then smears a nāga twig with salt and sour gruel and burns it in it, they will certainly rise up, or else they will become afflicted with impurity.

Gazing at an image of the noble Vajrapāṇi, if one recites until it speaks, moves and blazes, it will then grant a boon. Having made a maṇḍala adorned with flowers in front of a painted cloth, if one recites until the noble Vajrapāṇi arrives, he will come and touch one's head. By that touch alone, one will dwell in Alakāvatī and take on the form of a yakṣa. Taking the vajra of the noble Vajrapāṇi, one should recite until one becomes invisible. Through a hundred thousand recitations one will perform all rites.

Holding the big toe of the foot of an image of the noble Avalokiteśvara, if one recites until it blazes, radiates light and shows wrath, it will then do every task, and it will also speak. Gazing at the noble Mañjuśrī, if one recites until it blazes, one will become invisible or become a vidyādhara. Gazing at the noble Samantabhadra, one should recite until one becomes insatiable for virtue, until a descent takes place, and until one rises up into the air.

One who has entered upon the conduct of mantra in this tantra should, when the moon is eclipsed, place ghee in a copper vessel, make a maṇḍala of fragrant flowers, perfume it with fragrant incense, and recite until it blazes. If one then drinks it, one will retain whatever one hears.

When the moon or the sun is eclipsed, one should enter a temple of the Great God, take realgar, eye medicine or orpiment, place it between the leaves of an aśvattha tree, and recite until it blazes; by that one will become a vidyādhara or become invisible.

Placing one's hand upon the Great God, one should recite until one becomes invisible. Dwelling in a charnel ground, one should recite until one becomes invisible. Having made a maṇḍala in a charnel ground, one should fill two unfired clay bowls, joined mouth to mouth, with ashes, and recite until smoke appears. Having then put it into water three times, one becomes invisible.

Summoning bodhisattvas and summoning gods and nāgas will be accomplished in a single night. In brief, these Supreme Vidyās bring about the perfect fulfillment of one's wishes. The wise, having examined this and understood that whatever one practices by the rite one has in mind will be accomplished just so, should, as followers of the bodhisattva vehicle, have no doubt about it.

Next, the painting common to all of these will be taught. The Blessed One is seated on a jeweled lotus. On his right is the noble Vajrapāṇi. One should paint the goddess, adorned with every ornament, with matted locks and golden in color, who is called All-Supreme Vidyā, or else called Stainless Splendor That Overpowers; one should paint such a Blessed Lady with a garland of light, seated on a lotus stalk that rises from a rock. Below the goddess, likewise, one should paint her attendant called Ananta, who destroys all obstructors. Below Vajrapāṇi one should paint the practitioner, paying homage. Or else one may paint the painting however one wishes, because the form bodies of the blessed buddhas and of the bodhisattvas are limitless. Those endowed with discernment and skilled in means will succeed even without the procedure of the painting, or without offerings.

This concludes the vast chapter, the compendium of the king of Supreme Vidyās of bodhisattva conduct practiced through the gateway of secret mantra.

Then the Lord of Secrets, Vajrapāṇi, so that the teaching might give rise to unfailing accomplishment, requested from the Blessed One the Summit, the queen of vidyās called Eye of Space, an ocean of the domain of all the tathāgatas. Then the Blessed One entered the meditative absorption called Following All the Tathāgatas as One, and from his entire body he spoke this supreme Summit, the queen of vidyās called Eye of Space:

namaḥ straiyadhvikānāṃ sarvatathāgatānāṃ oṃ gaganalocane gaganasama sarvathā oṃ gatātisarasaṃbha vajralā namo amoghānāṃ svāhā

"Vajrapāṇi, this great Summit, queen of vidyās, has arisen from the domain of all the tathāgatas. She is the mother who gives birth to all bodhisattvas. She is accomplished as limitless, unobstructed and unfailing, and she abides in thoroughly engaging the bodhisattvas of mantra conduct in the deeds of a buddha. The son of good family who holds her will become irreversible. He will be blessed by all the tathāgatas. Through recitation itself he will obtain limitless and unequaled qualities. If he practices her, he will be blessed by all the tathāgatas. He will obtain entry into the ground of bodhisattva conduct. By this irresistible and unfailing one he will obtain, with little hardship, the accomplishments such as that of a vidyādhara, just as he wishes."

Next, a portion of the ritual procedure that becomes the cause for attaining the state of a vidyādhara will be set forth. The son of good family, a bodhisattva who has generated the mind of awakening, beginning on the first day of the month, should first of all offer himself to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas. He should say these words of offering: "I, named so-and-so, offer myself to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas together with their retinues. May all of you who possess great compassion, at all times, please bless me who am suffering, please take me under your care, please protect me completely."

Then one should confess one's misdeeds, saying, "With that same generation of the mind of confession with which the buddhas and bodhisattvas set their minds, I too confess all the misdeeds of all times, just as the blessed buddhas know them"; or else one should confess as one is able.

One rejoices in the accumulations of merit and wisdom of the buddhas and bodhisattvas who have gone in the three times, which become the cause of benefit and happiness for all sentient beings and purify the realms of all sentient beings, saying, "I rejoice, just as the blessed buddhas know"; or else one should rejoice as one is able.

Then, having made oneself familiar with the method of offering things that belong to all the world systems, which have no owner and are not appropriated by anyone, such as mountains of jewels born from the plains and from the waters, the jewels of the ocean and the like, one should offer them and dedicate them to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas together with their retinues.

Then one should say, "Into all the maṇḍalas of the retinues of the buddhas and bodhisattvas, may infinite clouds of vast and excellent offerings arise, each one distinctly, through my aspiration, through the power of my merit, and through the blessing of all the buddhas and bodhisattvas." And one should also recite up to eight times this great queen of vidyās, which brings about such great offerings:

namaḥ sarva buddha bodhi satvā nāṃ sarvathā uṅga te spharṇa hi maṃ gagana khaṃ samantā ta svāhā

Then the offerings will truly arise in just that way, and not otherwise.

Then one should think, "Through infinite melodies proclaiming an ocean of praises of the qualities of all the buddhas and bodhisattvas, may their fame enter all the realms of sentient beings and the expanse of phenomena. May it follow the wheel of Dharma of all the tathāgatas. May it proclaim the way of the sūtras." And one should also recite up to eight times this great queen of vidyās, which accomplishes praise and eulogy and rains down clouds of all the dharmas.

namaḥ sarva buddha bodhi satvā nāṃ sarvatra saṃkusumitā abhijñāna rāśinī namo stute svāhā

Then, in just that way and not otherwise, the melodies of praise and of the teaching of the Dharma will certainly remain, just as the buddhas and bodhisattvas know them.

Then one should say aloud, or think: "Pacifying all the sufferings of all beings, displaying, through every visualization devoted to the benefit of all sentient beings and to the concentration of happiness, modes of conduct marked by the signs of various meditative absorptions, may clouds of emanations of various kinds arise; spreading through the expanse of phenomena, may they remain turned toward all sentient beings, until they have established sentient beings on the ground of the wisdom of the ten powers." And one should recite up to eight times this great queen of vidyās, which brings about such great emanations:

namaḥ sarva buddha bodhi satvā nāṃ namo stute mahā vajra sarva sādha hitaṃ kara tiṣtha sarvātra sarveṣāṃ dharmāṇāṃ adhiṣthāya svāhā

Then whatever has been brought about will remain just as it is.

If one does as much recitation of all these vidyās above as one wishes, the buddhas and bodhisattvas will see, just as they are, those who have truly accomplished the conduct of secret mantra. Thus having made the aims and needs of sentient beings his own, he rejoices in the way the buddhas and bodhisattvas, from the time they first set their minds on awakening up to unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening, have accomplished the aims of all sentient beings. Having made himself familiar with that visualization, he pays homage to all the tathāgatas, or simply makes himself familiar with the visualization, and here too he should recite the Eye of Space.

Or else one should first bring to mind, "Through the power of the blessing of the tathāgatas and through the power of my own merit, may the eyes of the tathāgatas, blessed by the Tathāgata, arise for sentient beings as is fitting," and then recite. Or else one should say, "May they obtain the accomplishment, praised by the Tathāgata, that arises for the sake of all sentient beings." Or else one should say, "May sentient beings be endowed with the eye of the Tathāgata's wisdom." Or else one should say, "May they strive in virtue." Or else one should say, "May the cause of the Tathāgata's wisdom arise."

Or else one should say, "Through the complete perfection of the ten powers, may all sentient beings each obtain the complete perfection of the accumulations of merit and wisdom, or the complete perfection of the wisdom body of the Tathāgata, or the complete perfection of the body endowed with the major and minor marks, or the complete perfection of the body that turns the great wheel of Dharma." Or else one should say, "May there arise for all sentient beings true companionship with all the buddhas and bodhisattvas; the true accomplishment of the conduct of all the buddhas and bodhisattvas; for the sake of perfecting completely the perfections of all bodhisattvas and of gradually mastering the grounds of all the buddhas and bodhisattvas, entry into all the maṇḍalas of the conduct of the bodhisattva Samantabhadra; the individual attainment of all the dhāraṇīs, meditative absorptions and liberations; the emanation that brings forth clouds of great offerings; the firm establishment of the stream of rain of the Dharma falling from the clouds of all the tathāgatas; the unbroken lineage of the Three Jewels; the attainment of the glory of a vidyādhara; and the full attainment of the state of a vajra holder."

Or else one should recite according to one's intention and according to one's inclination, or with the intention and the superior intention that benefit and happiness may arise for all the realms of sentient beings. Or else one should recite after thinking, "Through the power of my aspiration and the blessing of the tathāgatas, may all the unsurpassed accomplishments arise for all sentient beings," or after reflecting in one's mind in whatever way is fitting.

If one recites in this way until it becomes habitual, one will obtain accomplishment. If one turns toward and enters the conduct of a bodhisattva in harmony with one's aspiration and in harmony with one's diligence, one will certainly succeed. These are the procedures to be performed always. Or else, with faith free of doubt, one should perform whatever procedure one is able.

Then one should recite a volume of the Dharma such as the noble Gaṇḍavyūha, or a sūtra that expounds the vast way of the bodhisattva's conduct, and make offerings to it, or at the very least pay homage to it.

When one has become thoroughly weary and eats food, one should recite this mantra over the food eight times:

namaḥ sarva buddha bodhi satvā nāṃ oṃ baliṃ dade tejo mālini svāhā

Then, having dispelled one's fatigue with the food, one should perform in the afternoon this very procedure of the morning. If one becomes disheartened, one should recite the mantra until one is no longer disheartened. Then, having made aspirations, one should rise. Having risen, one should do other things, such as reading the profound and vast sūtras.

At the time of going to sleep, too, bowing with one's whole body, one should pay homage from the depths of one's heart to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas, and afterward one may sleep as one wishes. The wise should always say, "May all the buddhas and bodhisattvas please bless me. Please grant me accomplishment and the supreme. Please completely pacify all harm."

One should perform such a practice day after day for one month. Then, on the full moon, one should observe a fast for one day and night and make offerings with whatever one can afford. In a solitary and empty place, before a stūpa, an image or a painting, one makes a maṇḍala strewn with loose flowers, places a volume of the sublime Dharma in front, perfumes it with fragrant incense, and sits cross-legged. Taking an iron wheel, a vajra or a sword, and sitting on spread kuśa grass, one should recite until it blazes. At midnight or at sunrise it will blaze.

When it blazes, great portents will occur: the earth will quake, and other great portents will occur like those at the appearance of a buddha. In that instant, through the blessing of all the buddhas and bodhisattvas, a light ray called Binding will shine forth. Then all the vidyādharas with their retinues, the vidyādhara maidens with their companions, and the great kings of the vidyādharas with their retinues will empower him. Thereby he becomes unobstructed everywhere and is honored by all the gods. In accordance with the merit he has gathered, he travels to the buddha fields, and he fully obtains the ability to make offerings to the buddhas and bodhisattvas and to bring sentient beings to maturity. Without abandoning that very body, he fully attains the following of the bodhisattva's conduct. According to his strength and according to his capacity, he will master the bodhisattva grounds in their proper order, and by that very means he will attain up to perfect and complete buddhahood. These are the procedures of skillful means for those of the bodhisattva vehicle who are skilled in means, chief among whom are those who have entered upon the conduct through the gateway of secret mantra.

One should practice before a painting in an empty and solitary place: on a mountain, on the bank of a river, on an island in a lake, at the foot of a solitary tree, in a park, in a hermitage, in the precincts of a monastery, before a stūpa containing relics or before an image, or at the very least in a temple, a house, or any place one makes use of. If one is able, one should give up busyness and, in empty and solitary places such as mountains, or at the very least in a house, a chamber, a place one makes use of, or the inner room of a storehouse, recite until the sign appears. Then, as before, one should accomplish a wheel, a vajra or the like, according to one's wish. Having recited a hundred thousand times, one should accomplish all the substances that confer the state of a vidyādhara, such as realgar or eye medicine.

This vidyā, by the reading of which all rites such as bringing under control are accomplished: if one enters a mountain recess and recites it three hundred thousand times, one will see the Tathāgata. Having seen him, one will fully obtain the dhāraṇīs and the like. Sitting cross-legged on a solitary and empty mountain or the like, if one recites the whole night through, focusing on the Tathāgata without breaking the cross-legged posture, then at midnight or at sunrise one's body will blaze. When it has blazed, one's body will be transformed and one will become a vidyādhara. If one draws the mount of a god, sits on it and recites until daybreak, the mount of the god will become made of jewels, and one will travel through the sky wherever one wishes.

Since this vidyā is accomplished by its very nature, even one who has not previously performed the approach should practice according to the procedure. One may also practice with the procedure of the Supreme Vidyā as taught; or else, one who lacks skillful means should accomplish it through rites, as one wishes or according to one's inclination. With the procedure of this ritual manual one should apply whatever one has in mind, as is fitting.

Or else, if one wishes to accomplish it with a painting, one should paint on the cloth an image of the Tathāgata seated on a lotus seat. On his right one should paint the noble Vajrapāṇi seated on a lotus seat, with palms joined and surrounded by light. To the left of the Blessed One one should paint the Eye of Space, golden in color, adorned with every ornament, with matted locks, her palms joined, seated on a lotus seat, with a garland of light. Below her one should paint the young maiden called Great Terror, wrathful, dark green, with a single lock of matted hair, holding and brandishing a vajra in her right hand, wearing a garland of jewels, seated on a rock, with a garland of flames, clad in saffron robes, her eyes slightly red, destroying obstructors. Below the noble Vajrapāṇi one should paint the practitioner, paying homage. With this painting one should perform the procedures of this ritual manual, or else do as one wishes.

The second painting shows the terrifying goddess with her companions, her matted locks hanging down, her body wreathed with a garland of light. With such a painting, too, one should apply here the procedures given earlier.

Whether one has skillful means or not, this Eye of Space is unfailing. Applying it broadly, as in the words "whether one has merit or has no merit," anyone at all will certainly succeed. The wise should know that it brings about the perfect fulfillment of one's desired aims.

This concludes the ritual manual called Eye of Space, Mother of the Bodhisattvas.

Then again the Blessed One entered the meditative absorption that radiates forth the domain of the buddhas and bodhisattvas, and in order to protect sentient beings who suffer, he spoke this mantra:

namaḥ sarva buddha bodhi satvā nāṃ ārya āgāje ananhā viśāyanī araje viraje hūṃ trā kara svāhā

When all the buddhas without exception
Spoke this secret mantra,

Next I shall explain how those who have the power of the strength of the unsurpassed, unobstructed, unfailing dharmas and of all the buddhas and bodhisattvas enter into the common rite.

Those skilled in means who wish to carry out the deeds of all the buddhas should, as before, beginning from the first day of the lunar month, offer their very own being to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas, and, as before, confess their misdeeds. As far as they are able, they rejoice, with heartfelt rejoicing, in the accumulations of merit and wisdom of all the buddhas and bodhisattvas who have come in the three times, by which they bring all sentient beings to maturity. Likewise one should dedicate, saying, "Through my merit, may it come to pass that the Tathāgata together with his retinue comes forth to protect all sentient beings," and one should say, "Please grant your blessing." Then, in secluded and empty places, one should recite this mantra and become habituated to it. If the mind grows weary, one should read the sublime Dharma.

Having given up busyness, focusing on all the buddhas and bodhisattvas and bowing to them, one should recite, habituated to the recitation; or else, as far as one is able, one should recite beginning from the first day of the month for the month, and then, at the full moon, having made a maṇḍala strewn with fragrant flowers, if one recites throughout the whole night, the Tathāgata will come, touch one's head with his hand, and speak of the aim one desires. By that touch alone the five superknowledges will arise. One will also fully attain entry into the conduct of mantra. If one sees an image of the Tathāgata, then within one further month one will attain the five superknowledges.

Likewise, one should accomplish it with the rite of the chapter on the Essence of the Tathāgatas, or that of the Supreme Vidyā, or the rite of another ritual manual, or whatever rite of accomplishment the mind desires. If one recites thinking, "By the power of the blessing of the tathāgatas and by the blessing of the nature of reality of Unobstructed Unfailing Accomplishment, may this become a cause of benefit for sentient beings here," then, without fail, it will be truly established through the power of the blessing of the tathāgatas, and benefit for those beings will also arise; one should have faith in this as well. With this Unfailing one, too, lesser, middling, and great accomplishments will certainly arise, and the tathāgatas will know them perfectly.

In accordance with one's merit, one should accomplish it by the means through which one becomes a vidyādhara, fully attains the nectar of infinite qualities, suchness itself, and goes forth with companions who can never be robbed of any of their enjoyments, or else by whatever rite one desires.

In brief, one becomes a vidyādhara even in a single night. Without breaking the cross-legged posture, recollecting the Tathāgata, if one recites throughout the whole night before a volume of the sublime Dharma placed within a maṇḍala strewn with fragrant flowers, then certainly, most certainly, at sunrise one will become a vidyādhara.

Other actions will also come about. Summoning gods is done with white mustard seed and ghee. Summoning nāgas is done with ghee mixed with honey, and with black pepper. Summoning yakṣas is done with honey-laden flowers anointed with ghee, and with the substance called maru, anointed with sesame oil and empowered by mantra. For vidyādharas, it is done with white mustard seed anointed with sesame oil. For summoning a king, it is done with a fire offering filled with ghee: one should burn it together with flowers. For bringing men under one's control, it is done with a maṇḍala of ghee. For bringing women under one's control, one should burn priyaṅgu flowers anointed with honey. Free of doubt, one should certainly accomplish whatever one desires. This is the common application.

The painting rite for this is as follows. One should draw the Blessed One, golden in color, seated cross-legged, with a disc of garlands of light rays above the rays of light that issue from the crown of his head. On his right is the noble Vajrapāṇi, his palms joined toward the Blessed One, seated on a lotus, with a garland of light. On the Blessed One's left is a goddess the color of a white water lily, with red lips, red hands and feet, with matted locks, without ornaments, her palms joined, seated on a red lotus seat.

Below her one should draw the maiden called Tamer of the Great Fierce One, wrathful, adorned with ornaments of snakes, black and green, with a single matted lock, her eyes slightly red. Below the noble Vajrapāṇi one should draw the practitioner, prostrating. Here too the rite is exactly the same; whatever the mind desires is also fine. In the second painting, the goddess stands on a lotus with her matted locks let down. The messenger, the Great Fierce One, should be drawn as a youth with his hair standing on end.

If, free of miserliness, one becomes accustomed here to the pledge from which accomplishment arises, and then, endowed with radiance, enters here into the pledge of accomplishment, the accomplishment praised by the tathāgatas will quickly arise. If, thinking, "May it arise for all sentient beings," one binds the mudrā and recites the mantra, the eight accomplishments will arise. One should do the recitation after paying homage with one's attention on all the buddhas and bodhisattvas.

Here one should not train in two things: one should not pay homage to a single tathāgata alone, and one should not work for the aim of a single sentient being alone. Rather, one should become accustomed to the greatness of generating a mind that extends to all without exception. In every circumstance these two should be abandoned: a regretful mind and a discouraged mind. Further, these two are to be done: confessing all misdeeds, and rejoicing without interruption in the accumulations of merit and wisdom. Here these two should be abandoned: not applying oneself to the training, and not practicing. There are two things through which there will be no accomplishment: lack of faith, and not having generated the mind set on awakening. There are two pledges to be guarded: against doing things at the wrong time, and against lack of examination. The qualities that accord with the conduct of mantra are two: generating recollection of the Buddha, and being intent in every respect on perfect accomplishment.

Then, in this way, the ritual procedure for the heart mantras of all the vajra holders and for the mudrās and mantras of all the deities of the maṇḍala will come forth. By means of any protective maṇḍala whatever, all the rites to be accomplished, such as binding, squeezing, striking, beating, killing, cutting, casting down, purifying, accomplishing, summoning, and bringing under control, will be performed by this very means, which is accomplished simply by reciting it. All the rites, such as accomplishing the supreme, which are likewise to be applied by the Extensive Rite arranged in the stages of the Tathāgata Heart, or by the rites of the Supreme Vidyā and so on, should also be performed by this, which accomplishes without obstruction and without fail.

One should draw any one of the Anantas, the servants who slay obstructors: black and looming, a youth with matted locks, eyes somewhat red, wrathful, seated on a jewel rock. Below is the practitioner.

Here too one should accomplish it with the heart mantra of any vajra holder whatever. Likewise, according to the rite taught earlier, or the mantras of the tathāgatas, or the rite of the Tathāgata Heart, one should accomplish the vajra holder too by the rite of the tathāgatas. Once accomplishment is reached, one becomes a vajra holder. The heart mantra of the bodhisattvas, the unsurpassed ocean of jewels, which accomplishes without obstruction and without fail, should likewise be applied. It is applied by the rite of all the ritual procedures, or by the rite of whatever the mind desires. One should likewise accomplish through the power of the heart mantra, the incomparable Hook of the Tathāgatas, which arose from the treasury of the tathāgatas' qualities.

Here no obstacles arise,
there is no delusion and no attachment.
Here the wealth of all the buddhas
abides, constantly blessed.

The accomplishment of the common mudrās will now be given. Thinking, "Through the blessing of all the tathāgatas and the power of my aspiration, may vast expanses of clouds of manifold, extensive, supreme offerings arise in the maṇḍalas of the retinues of all the tathāgatas," one should recite this mantra. Then one will abide truly and not otherwise, and will be beheld by all the buddhas and bodhisattvas.

na ma strai ya dhvi kā nāṃ ta thā ga tā nāṃ sarba tra saṃ ku su mi tī rā śī ni na mo stu te svā hā

With this too, thinking, "Through the blessing of all the tathāgatas and the power of my aspiration, may vast oceans of the praises praised by all the tathāgatas arise in all the maṇḍalas of the retinues of the buddhas and bodhisattvas," one should recite the mantra, focusing on the tathāgatas. Then the melodies of praise will be firmly established, and not otherwise.

Then, having made aspiration prayers, and having rejoiced in all the accomplishments of all the buddhas and bodhisattvas, such as the attainment of perfect awakening in every aspect, one should pay homage facing all the tathāgatas, and recite the mantra until one grows weary. Then, keeping in mind conformity with all the sūtras that enter the vast way, reciting the heart of the Buddha, and speaking the pledge called "Gods and all others, listen!", one should reflect and meditate.

Having done thus in the morning session, when eating food one should recite this mantra, which blesses it into great strength and into the great king of medicines:

na ma strai ya dhvi kā nāṃ ta thā ga tā nāṃ oṃ bhu ja li te svā hā

Then, having rested, one should afterwards read the scriptures and so forth, and in the afternoon session too, as before, having performed the activities, one should recite. At dusk, having made aspiration prayers and paid homage to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas by bowing with all the limbs, one should first lie down on a spread of grass and go to sleep.

Beginning from the first day of the month, one should perform this activity every day for one month. On the night of the full moon, having placed a volume of the holy Dharma upon a maṇḍala of scent, or before a stūpa containing relics, or a stūpa that is a dwelling place, or an image, or else elsewhere in solitude, one should sit cross-legged and, without breaking the cross-legged posture, recite while focusing on the Tathāgata. One should do so until light rays issue forth and strike the body; then the body will blaze. After that, this body is transformed, and the classes of vidyādharas draw near. Being empowered by them, one becomes, according to one's merit, a bodhisattva of the vidyādhara class, and according to one's fortune attains one's own abode. There, too, endowed with wisdom and skillful means, one will also attain full application to the perfections. One will also follow along in manifold displays. One will also make offerings to the tathāgatas. One will never be separated from the Saṅgha of bodhisattvas. Being skilled in means, even though one fully enjoys sense pleasures, one will not fall away. With that very body one will tread the grounds, and, according to one's merit, likewise one will approach the maṇḍalas of the retinues of the tathāgatas, and will gradually gather and individually attain the conduct that follows them. Even if one does not attain the state of a vidyādhara, one will see the body of the Tathāgata, or be illumined by his light, or hear his speech, and a pure intention will arise in one. One will also fully attain the dhāraṇīs.

Others too should practice it. In such secluded places as mountains, rivers, lakes, islands, inner chambers and temples, before a stūpa containing relics, or an image, or at the very least a painted cloth, in a secluded temple, having given up bustle and holding the holy Dharma as supreme, one should oneself take up a special implement such as a wheel, a vajra or a sword, and for one night until dawn, without breaking the cross-legged posture, or else squatting, sitting upon a spread of grass, one should recite until it blazes. In the same way one should practice by placing substances such as realgar on a maṇḍala of scent or in a vessel of leaves. This is the method of accomplishment, wholly free of conceptual reference, of bodhisattvas who are skilled in means and endowed with yogic application.

In solitude, before a stūpa or the like, having habituated oneself to the recollection of the Tathāgata, one should recite until a sign arises. On the full moon one should recite until dawn, seated cross-legged. If, having given up sleep for one day and night, one practices in the same way, holding the substances such as the wheel, one will become an irreversible vidyādhara. Such a one will become unequaled in splendor by anyone. Even one who lacks merit will certainly succeed in this very life. Even without a painted cloth, and even without offerings, one will certainly succeed.

Then, if one wishes to practice with a painted cloth: on the painted cloth is the form of the Tathāgata seated on a lotus. On his right is the noble Vajra Holder. On his left is Gaganalocanā, radiating manifold light rays and seated with palms joined. Of these, Vajra Holder is the color of a blue utpala lotus, and Gaganalocanā is pale blue. Below, the practitioner should be shown facing the goddess. With this painted cloth too, one should practice by the former rite for one month, until the full moon. By a fire offering of blue aparājitā flowers one should summon a king; if one combines the whole collection of rites, one will succeed in everything.

On the second painted cloth one should draw a lone goddess, golden in color, blazing with light like a sesame flower, firmly seated on a lotus, and the practitioner with matted locks. For these too one should proceed by the former rite. One should practice upon a sword, realgar and so forth, as one wishes. Everywhere, free of unhappiness, one should read a volume of the holy Dharma, and if, with a focus of attention and without breaking the cross-legged posture, one recites on the full moon, it will be accomplished even in a single night. Through delighting in solitude, reading the volumes of the holy Dharma and not engaging in bustle, the rites will certainly become fruitful.

The noble Samantabhadra, recollecting the Tathāgata, spoke this secret mantra:

na ma strai ya dhvi kā nāṃ ta thā ga tā nāṃ oṃ a sta ba ra bho gi svā hā

By reciting this, one fully attains entry into the quality of being blessed so as never to turn back from the conduct of the noble Samantabhadra. With this mantra too, which is among those blessed by the Tathāgata, the rite will certainly succeed through the very rite of Gaganalocanā.

The distinctive features of the painted cloth are these. To the right of the Blessed One is the noble Samantabhadra. To the left is the goddess called She Who Has the Might of Paramāśva, green like priyaṅgu, with palms joined, and the noble Vajra Holder with palms joined. Below the noble Samantabhadra one should draw the practitioner, facing the goddess.

The second painted cloth shows the noble Samantabhadra seated on a jewel lotus, radiating manifold light rays, his matted locks coiled downward, holding a sword, golden in color, together with the practitioner. The rite for this painted cloth is also the same as those, and one becomes irreversible from the state of a vidyādhara, and unequaled. Through the conduct of Samantabhadra and the unequaled power of the king of vidyās, with that very body, by gradually accumulating roots of virtue, one will see the noble Samantabhadra, and will never again fall away.

Another mantra:

na ma strai ya dhvi kā nāṃ ta thā ga tā nāṃ sa manta gā ma nā na u jva lā ya svā hā

Through this the noble Samantabhadra will turn to face one. It also accomplishes the vision of the illusion-like samādhi. It purifies. It brings to definite completion. By this very mantra one will gradually become a vidyādhara as well. Its rite, too, is the same as the preceding ones.

Further, the vidyā for emanating offerings is this:

namaḥ sarba ta thā ga tā nāṃ sarba thā udga te spha ra ṇa hi maṃ ga ga na khaṃ svā hā

Having visualized the offerings, if one recites it straight away until one grows weary, one will certainly succeed in this very life. This Unfailing Pinnacle of the Kings of Vidyās should be combined with all the procedures of the ritual manual. Likewise, if one practices this, one will not turn back from the stream of virtue. For a bodhisattva who wishes to go on to attain great illumination in this very life, and who wishes to accomplish an ocean of great aspirations, focusing on the Tathāgata together with the buddhas and bodhisattvas and without breaking the cross-legged posture, it will be fruitfully accomplished in a single night.

The noble Mañjuśrī, having made supplication to the Tathāgata, spoke this mantra:

na ma strai ya dhvi kā nāṃ ta thā ga tā nāṃ ho he raṃ he raṃ su ga tā su ga tā bajra śi kha ra na mo stu te ku mā ra sma ra sma ra svā hā

Its rite, too, is the same as those. Even without a painted cloth, and even without an image of the deity, it will succeed. The distinctive features of the painted cloth are these: the noble Mañjuśrī, just slightly past youth, blazing with every ornament. To the right of the Blessed One one should draw Vajra Holder on a lotus, kneeling with one knee set down. The practitioner has palms joined. On the second painted cloth, if one draws Mañjuśrī alone, seated on a lotus, as though gazing at the practitioner who stands before him, it accomplishes whatever one wishes.

The noble Avalokiteśvara spoke this mantra:

na ma strai ya dhvi kā nāṃ ta thā ga tā nāṃ oṃ sau mya ra bajra jva la sambha rā jā ya namaḥ svā hā

The painted cloth shows the noble Samantabhadra seated in a divine palace. One should draw Vajrakumāra, dark and green, seated on a lotus, encircled by the light of the Tathāgata. In the place of the noble Avalokiteśvara and Samantabhadra is Vajra Holder, who should be drawn above the youth Vajrakumāra. These are the distinctive features of the painted cloth.

The noble Vajra Holder, having surveyed the whole vajra family, spoke this mantra:

na ma strai ya dhvi kā nāṃ ta thā ga tā nāṃ sarba bajra dhva rā ṇāṃ oṃ caṇḍa lā caṇḍa lā ca la ca la bajra bajra śānta nā śānta nā pha la na pha la na ta ra ta ra mā ra mā ra bajra da la spho ṭa lā spho ṭa lā li śe kha ras manta bajra ṇī jvā la ne jvā la ne sam stha a gre a gra śa sa ka nāṃ ra ṇā ra ṇa hūṃ phaṭ bajra utta me svā hā

Merely by reciting this, one will not be harmed by gods, asuras, yakṣas and the like, and vināyakas and the like will not come near. Its rite, too, is the same as those. Through the rites of the whole vajra family as well, by means of unfailing activities, one becomes a vidyādhara. In that place of the vajra family too, endowed with the splendor of the Tathāgata, one should stay there and, giving up sleep, recite until dawn; after that, signs of accomplishment will appear. Then one should take up whatever one wishes, realgar and the like, or a vajra, a sword and the like, and practice. In the first watch of the full moon it will certainly succeed. One will not fall from the state of a vidyādhara. One will also enter the conduct of a bodhisattva and will not fall away. If one practices this by whatever rite one's mind desires, it will certainly succeed swiftly. Even without the work of a painted cloth, even without bathing, even without offerings, by recitation alone wonders will occur, such as seeing the body of the Tathāgata.

This division of the stages of the vidyādhara was taught in order that one might accomplish unfailingly, and in order to demonstrate truly the power of the teaching. In order to arouse infinite faith and enthusiasm in beings, one should put it to the test even for a single night. Those who recite these secret mantras are blessed by all the buddhas. They are fully protected by Vajra Holder. All of these should be practiced by the rite of Gaganalocanā. If one is able, one should, with diligence, seated cross-legged until dawn, practice by whatever rite one's mind desires, together with this one. One should protect oneself with this mantra. For the rest, this is mostly done with the Uṣṇīṣa, the Great Peahen, the Supreme Vajra and the like.

na ma strai ya dhvi kā nāṃ ta thā ga tā nāṃ sarba trā pra ti ha ta bā pti dharma tā ba li nāṃ aṃ a sa ma sa ma sa manta to a nanta to bā pti śā sa ni ha ra ha ra sma ra sma ra ṇa bi ga ta rā ga buddha dharma te sa ra sa ra sa ma ba lā ha sa ha sa tra ya tra ya ga ga na ma hā ba ra lakṣa ṇi jvā la jvā la na sā ga re svā hā

This is the heart of all the tathāgatas. This was the division of the stages of the vidyādhara.

Next I shall teach all the detailed procedures of the ritual manual that are common to all the divisions of the Extensive Rite.

Here, the rite of practicing the king of bodhisattva conduct through the gateway of secret mantra is of two kinds: with skillful means and without skillful means.

The preliminary approach is of two kinds: familiarity gained in former times; and seeing the deity, or hearing a voice, or light rays striking the body, or seeing the body of the Tathāgata, through reciting a hundred thousand times without interruption for a month or so.

The places are of two kinds: mountains and the like that are naturally secluded, and the chambers of temples and the like that are naturally empty and free of bustle.

The preliminaries are of two kinds: confessing misdeeds of the three times, and rejoicing in merit and wisdom.

What is to be cultivated is of two kinds: applying oneself to what is good, and cultivating meditations such as the recollection of the buddhas and bodhisattvas.

Purity is of two kinds: truly showing benefit to faithful beings out of an excellent superior intention, and the purity of never committing misdeeds through taking no delight in the things of saṃsāra.

The things that bring swift accomplishment are two: undertaking to free the whole realm of beings without remainder from saṃsāra, and generating the awakening mind and the like; through these one attains accomplishment. Having reflected thus correctly, one should recite, or else one should recite focusing on all the buddhas and bodhisattvas.

For two, accomplishment will be far off: for those without faith, and for those who have not generated the mind set on awakening.

Through two powers the welfare of beings of inferior, middling and superior capacity comes about: the power of one's own aspiration, merit and wisdom, and the power of the blessing of the buddhas and bodhisattvas.

What is to be accomplished is of two kinds: one's own aim and the aim of others.

Accomplishment is of two kinds: attaining the state of a vidyādhara and the like, and following the conduct.

Application here is of two kinds: the application of generating immeasurable minds, and application without discouragement.

What is to be done is twofold: entering the gateway of skillful means and wisdom.

What is not to be done is twofold: doubt, and acts that harm beings.

The qualities of this body are two: swift and fruitful accomplishment, and freedom from obstructors.

What is to be abandoned here is twofold: rejecting the secret mantra and the like, and disrespect.

What is to be cultivated is twofold: the application of recollecting all the buddhas and bodhisattvas, and working for the welfare of beings.

The wonders here are two: just as the mantras are practiced, so do they come into accord; and whatever rite one applies to whatever purpose, if one practices it, it succeeds.

The qualities of the dharma not yet seen are two: wherever one is born, keeping company with bodhisattvas and being free of defilement, and entering what is vast.

If one recites, one becomes unshakable in two respects: in what arises from the nature of reality, and in not falling away from the blessing of the Tathāgata.

The painted cloths are of two kinds: one with the Tathāgata, and one with the vidyā alone.

The pledges of what is not to be practiced here are two: intoxicating drinks, and lying on a raised bed.

What is not to be done here is twofold: one should not be miserly with the secret mantra, and one should not perform acts of little purpose.

What will not arise here is twofold: all acts that become faults, and desire for what is meaningless.

What is certain here is twofold: the arising of wondrous qualities, and entry into the family of the Tathāgata.

Blessings arise here in two ways: leaving nothing whatever undone, and the wish to keep company with all the buddhas and bodhisattvas.

For those who have entered the conduct of mantra, many such means as these will arise.

Next I shall explain the rite of preliminary approach common to all practitioners. First of all, one should take any mantra whatever, and through the blessing of truth adopt the one toward which one has the greatest devotion. In the morning session, having placed a token before an image, or before a painted cloth, or before a volume of the holy Dharma, one should pay homage with all the limbs to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas and say these words: "I, named so-and-so, offer myself to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas. May all of you take me fully into your care. May all the buddhas and bodhisattvas possessed of great compassion bless me." One should say this up to three times.

Then one should confess the misdeeds one has done: "Here I confess all misdeeds without exception that have arisen from desire, aversion and delusion. With that generation of the mind with which the buddhas and bodhisattvas confessed, I too confess, just as the buddhas, the blessed ones, know them." Or else one should confess with a confession according to one's ability. Then one should also say this:

"I rejoice in the immeasurable accumulations of merit and wisdom that have truly arisen in all the buddhas and bodhisattvas of the three times, in their sameness. I rejoice with a rejoicing that follows the realm of phenomena, which has neither end nor middle. I rejoice in the complete protection of all, everywhere, and in the virtue of all beings of the three times who act well with body, speech and mind. I rejoice with the supreme rejoicing, just as the buddhas, the blessed ones, know it. Just as the buddhas, the blessed ones, rejoice through their knowing, so do I rejoice." One should say this, and rejoice according to one's ability.

Then, all the articles needed for offering that there are, namely those produced from the furnace, the jewel mountains and the like produced from water, the jewels of the ocean and the like, all those delightful things without remainder that have no owner and are claimed by no one, and those that exist in the realms of beings, one should dedicate at all times to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas. Having dedicated them, one should say, "By the power of my merit and the blessing of the tathāgatas, may vast expanses of clouds of offerings, supremely extensive, amply filling the realm of phenomena, arise in the maṇḍalas of the retinues of all the tathāgatas." And one should also recite this queen of vidyās, which causes such clouds of offerings to arise:

namaḥ sarbabuddhabodhisatvānāṃ sarba ta thā ga te adhi ṣṭhi te ga ga na khaṃ bho spha ra sa mante na svā hā

One should recite it up to eight times. Beyond that, one should recite it as many times as one wishes. Then the offerings will be truly established for all the buddhas and bodhisattvas just as they are, and they will bless them and know them.

Then one should say, "By the power of my merit, and through the blessing of all the tathāgatas, may oceans of praise of the qualities of all the buddhas and bodhisattvas arise, vast expanses of supreme and extensive song." And one should recite up to eight times the queen of vidyās that causes to arise melodies praising the qualities of all the buddhas and bodhisattvas and proclaiming the Dharma:

namaḥ sarbabuddhabodhisatvānāṃ sarba tra saṃ ku su mi ta a bhi jñā ta rā śi ni na mo stu te svā hā

If one practices thus, having gained conviction in this unfailing one, it will arise here in the same way.

Then one should dedicate all those unsurpassed accumulations of merit and wisdom to perfect awakening. Then, generating the mind with the superior intention of any one of the immeasurable minds, one should recite the mantra and say, "First of all, by my generation of the mind, and by the power of my aspiration, merit and wisdom, may all the realms of beings here accomplish the generation of the mind. May the blessing of the Tathāgata arise as well." Or else one says, "May entry into the conduct of a bodhisattva be accomplished. May it arise." Or else one says, "May an ocean of accumulations of unsurpassed wisdom arise for all beings." Or else, thinking, "Whatever accomplishment the buddhas, the blessed ones, know, by the power of my merit and wisdom and through the blessing of the tathāgatas, may the accomplishments of all beings arise," one should recite the king of mantras up to eight times:

namaḥ sarbabuddhabodhisatvānāṃ na mo stu te ma hā bajra sarba stā hi taṃ ka ra tiṣṭha sarba tra sarba ṣa ṭa dha rmā ṇā ma dhiṣṭhā ya svā hā

After that, here in the realms of beings, every kind of benefit for beings will arise, just as one intended.

Then, focusing on all the buddhas and bodhisattvas and paying homage to them, and focusing on rejoicing in all the accomplishments of all the buddhas and bodhisattvas, such as perfect awakening, one should recite until one grows weary. Then, having read the scriptures, one should take one's meal, and if one is tired from the food, rest; then by that same morning rite one should also perform the afternoon session. When lying down, one should first pay homage with all the limbs to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas, and then lie down on something that is not a raised bed. With this very rite, according to one's ability and according to one's strength, one should do this every day for one month, until the full moon of the waxing fortnight.

Then the rite without skillful means should also be practiced as it has been taught, according to one's ability. One should not become discouraged, thinking, "I am not capable." Rather, thinking, "As is the action, so is the fruit; therefore the fruit of accomplishment will be achieved," one should perform it according to this rite in a thoroughly secluded place such as a mountain, or an empty building such as a temple, or without a roof, having wholly abandoned bustle and without harming beings. Even without bathing and the like, even without a painted cloth, even if one's moral discipline is broken, even if one is lowly, even if one lacks merit, one should in any case accustom oneself to practicing a great deal.

One who wishes to enter into light out of great darkness, who wishes to set those fallen into saṃsāra upon the vehicle of the bodhisattvas, and who wishes, with great effort, to take fully into care those who have fallen into wrong ways and to attain the power of illuminating them, should perform it according to the rite every day for one month. Finally, on the fifteenth day of the waxing fortnight, having made offerings according to one's means, one should make a maṇḍala strewn with loose flowers before a stūpa, an image, a painted cloth, a volume of the holy Dharma, or whatever is at hand, sit on a spread of leaves, contemplate the rite of protection and the rest as before, and recite the mantra three times. Then one should place an iron wheel, or a vajra, or a sword, or a spear, or a hook, or a knife, inside cow dung for half a month, then take it out and hold it in one's hand, and, beginning from the first part of the evening until dawn, having paid homage to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas, one should practice. Or else one may use one made of vajra that has been placed in goat's milk.

Or else one may use one made of silver, copper, or gold that has been placed in water for half a month, or placed in a vase of the milk of a tawny cow, or one that has been fired; or, holding it in the hand set between realgar, orpiment, or gorocana, or, just as it naturally is, between leaves or cloth, one should practice. Or else one holds in the hand a jewel, or a garland, or a washed alms bowl, or a robe, or a dwelling, or a parasol, or a drawing; or, if one practices until dawn with a sword made of palm leaf and the other substances for attaining the state of a vidyādhara, they blaze up at midnight or at sunrise. If one succeeds, great wonders suddenly occur: a rain of flowers falls from the sky, the buddha-field quakes violently, a great light suddenly appears, and many other great wonders of various kinds occur. All the families of vidyādharas gather around, empower him with an extraordinary power of mantra in accordance with his merit, and take him into their care.

From then on he will move among the assemblies as a king of vidyādharas, a chief of the host of vidyādharas. He enters in accordance with his own fortune, and without suffering he attains the very kingship of the class of bodhisattva vidyādharas. Remaining on that very ground, he becomes a universal monarch called Great Chief of the Vajra Holders, one who has entered, never to be deprived of it, the sphere of the noble Dharma of the infinite Extensive Rite, beautiful with infinite displays of play. Having become a vidyādhara of the Tathāgata, he attains a state from which there is no falling back, so that even at the time of the eon of burning he goes to another world system. He does not fall away from the nectar of unsurpassed bliss, nor from the enjoyment of the five qualities of sense pleasure, and he is surrounded by a host of many women. He obtains both wisdom and skillful means, comes to possess great power, and does no harm to sentient beings. Compared with his many enjoyments of the nectar of bliss, even the kings of the worldly vidyādharas are as though impoverished and inferior; so he becomes supreme among those worldly vidyādharas, and he regards them with compassion. Compared with the speed at which he travels, even the vidyādharas of the world of the gods seem like those who go on foot. The difference between one who has attained the state of a worldly vidyādhara and this one is like the difference between a blade of grass and Mount Meru.

This is the rite endowed with such skillful means. For one not endowed with skillful means, according to his inclination, there is the rite of the Extensive Rite, the detailed rite of the tathāgatagarbha set out in stages. One should accomplish it by means of all the rites of the earlier ritual manual, and apply all the rites of the earlier ritual manual.

The means of accomplishing the five superknowledges are of two kinds: in complete solitude, constantly repeating the meditative focus on the buddhas, the bodhisattvas, and so on; and accomplishing the five superknowledges by pleasing the deity. Having constantly familiarized oneself through those ritual means, the five superknowledges will arise. By two kinds of activity one attains the bases of miraculous power: familiarizing oneself with, and repeating, lightness of body and lightness of mind; and dwelling in solitude and familiarizing oneself with mantra recitation.

The rites of invisibility are three: accomplishing it in one day, or accomplishing it by a practice of one day and night, and the following. In a charnel ground, before a stūpa one has made of earth, one makes a maṇḍala strewn with loose flowers and sits upon it. Placing the object on orpiment, or gorocana, or leaves of the aśvattha, or of another tree of awakening, or of an auspicious tree such as the aśoka, the practitioner should practice the whole day, from dawn until evening. If smoke comes from it, one becomes invisible. If it is not accomplished on that day, one should practice for up to three days. If even then it is not accomplished, on the fourth day one should walk about in the charnel ground the whole day while practicing, and it will certainly be accomplished: one becomes invisible, takes on whatever form one wishes, and travels as far as the slopes of Mount Meru.

Or else, in that same place before the stūpa, on the thirteenth day of the month, having made offerings from sunset until sunrise, one sits on tree leaves, places the object between leaves of orpiment or gorocana, and, beginning at sunset, recites through the whole night. When smoke appears, if one draws it in three times, one becomes invisible. Or else, in that same charnel ground, one makes a tilaka mark with blood and sits holding a sword in the hand, and if one recites through the whole night, one becomes invisible at sunrise. Pressing down a corpse with the foot and holding a sword in the hand, one should recite until one becomes invisible. Holding a sword in the hand, on any day whatever, on the ashes of a cremated corpse, if one performs through the whole night a fire offering of white mustard seed steeped in blood, one becomes invisible at dawn. In short, whatever rite of any of the ritual manuals it may be, one should perform and apply it in a charnel ground.

Or else, for a whole day, in a place such as a mountain, or an empty house, a temple, a solitary tree, a stūpa in the wilderness, a place with a single landmark, or a charnel ground, one makes a maṇḍala strewn with loose flowers. Before a drawing of a chariot, a mount of the gods, a lion, a horse, an elephant, a buffalo, an ox, a deer, a goat, a sheep, or even a rooster, if one performs through the whole night a fire offering of butter, honey, and powdered human bone, at midnight or at daybreak it will blaze. Then one goes wherever one wishes and also becomes invisible. If one wishes, one comes back again and goes forth again.

For this, one should prepare pills by the following method. Take the root of atipala, the root of trivarṇa, which is also called godpari, the root of mahāvege, which is also called trailavarṇe, and the root and bark of the great tree. Grind these in equal parts into a fine powder, make three pills with one's own blood, and wrap them in copper. Another pill: take aśoka flowers, the root of mātuluṅga, the root of tarabarata, and the husk of flaxseed, combine them in equal parts, grind them into a fine powder, make with one's own blood three pills the size of a pea, and wrap them in copper.

Another pill: take sesame root, trapuṣa seed, malata root, and tamāla leaves, combine them in equal parts, make three pills from the fine powder with one's own blood, and wrap them in copper. Another pill: take myrobalan, the stalk of the bibhītaka fruit, nāgakesara, spangma, and jāti flowers; grind them in equal parts into a fine powder, mix it with one's own blood and sesame oil, make three pills, and wrap them in copper.

Next comes the method of accomplishing those pills. Having recited any mantra a thousand times, one makes a maṇḍala in a charnel ground and places a stūpa or a volume of the holy Dharma in its center. On the thirteenth day of the month, for the whole day and night, one sits fasting on a spread of leaves, holds a sword in the right hand and any one of the pills in the left, and, beginning in the evening, practices until smoke appears and it trembles violently. Then, if one puts one pill in the mouth, one becomes like the light of the sun, and one will not touch the ground, staying about a cubit above it. If it is not accomplished that night, one should go again to the charnel ground and, beginning in the evening, walk about and recite until sunrise. Or else, in a shrine where Maheśvara (Śiva) was accomplished, one places it upon the liṅga and sits facing north, holding a sword in the hand; if one practices from evening until sunrise, putting it in the mouth in the same way, one becomes the color of the sun. Or else one sits close to a stūpa in the wilderness, or at the foot of any one of the seven trees of awakening, holds any three of the pills in the hand, and does as before. Or else, in solitude, one places it in the hand of the Noble One, makes offerings, and practices. Or else, in the places that have been taught, one should practice when the moon or the sun is seized by Rāhu, as before.

Next comes the eye ointment. Take spangma, azurite, and copper powder in equal parts, make pills with one's own blood and honey, and place them in unfired clay bowls joined mouth to mouth. In places such as a charnel ground, holding a sword in the hand in the same way, one should practice as with the pills. Likewise, one places orpiment between leaves of the tree of awakening, holds a sword in the hand, and practices as before. At a stūpa in the wilderness, or at the foot of any tree of awakening, or at a shrine of Maheśvara, one places it in the hand of the noble Vajra Holder and practices while keeping a fast for a day and night on the thirteenth day of the month; or one should practice beginning in the evening when the moon or the sun is seized by Rāhu. If it is accomplished, by making a tilaka mark with it one becomes invisible. If it is not accomplished that night, one should practice in the same way in that very place, walking about, from sunrise until evening. When it has been accomplished, one lives for an intermediate eon, becomes golden in color, and takes on whatever form one wishes. Just as with orpiment, so too with gorocana, realgar, and the rest, one should practice diligently as before.

Next come the practices with a youth and a maiden, by which a woman's sex is changed into another and one's former body is changed into another. Then one has a maiden bathed well and adorned with ornaments, and offers her to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas. There one will attain one's own place in the family of the noble Vajrapāṇi. Having come, he takes by the hand even one who has committed great evil and certainly leads him to Alakāvatī. When one sleeps, one should protect that vajra and place it under one's pillow. When one eats, one should eat while holding it in the hand. If one holds a vajra made of red sandalwood in the hand, constantly goes for alms, climbs a mountain and recites, within five months one will attain a place among the gods of the realm of the Four Great Kings, in accordance with one's fortune. Living on alms on a mountain, never setting the sword down, one recites constantly; when one eats, one holds it and recites. When one sleeps, one protects it, puts it in its sheath, and places it under one's pillow; then within three months one becomes a vidyādhara. For the wheel, too, the rite is this very one.

One makes a hook of iron or of clay, makes in a charnel ground a maṇḍala strewn with loose flowers, lights a fire of charnel-ground wood in the center of the maṇḍala, and, holding the hook in the right hand, performs a fire offering of mustard seed. One should do this until those who dwell permanently in the charnel ground come. If, having come, they say, "Please command what I am to do," one says, "Do as I command." From then on, at the mere thought of them, they will carry out one's commands. Likewise, if one holds it at the foot of a great tree and recites while living on alms, a yakṣa comes, says, "What shall I do?", and accomplishes whatever aim one pursues. If, living on alms at the foot of an aśoka tree, one holds that same hook and recites, a yakṣiṇī comes and stays near one, just as one desires. If one climbs a mountain, holds that hook, and recites, and does the same with whatever substance one wishes that brings about the attainment of a vidyādhara, one becomes a vidyādhara of those.

If one recites with that hook over the dwelling of a nāga, the nāga will then rise up and do whatever one says. As with the hook, so too one should apply the practice with a sword, a wheel, or a vajra made of iron.

In a secluded and delightful place such as a pleasure garden, one uses offering substances of flowers, but one should offer them to the current of the wind.

To a stūpa, an image, a painted cloth, a volume of the holy Dharma, or anything else, one should offer flowers and the like for the sake of the substances. The fire offering to be performed for illness is as follows. Likewise, if one does this before a stūpa or the like for up to seven days, as much as one can, one will certainly become free of illness and also bring others under control.

Bhūtas, piśācas, rākṣasas, and the like flee when one draws with ash and the like, and there is no need to strike, cut, or bind them. To protect oneself, one recites it three times. To protect others, one does it five times, and the protection will hold. By clapping the hands, by tying a knot, or even by wishing, one brings them under control and summons them. With a kīla, a mantra knot, or water one also accomplishes everything. In short, if one recites with something held in mind, it makes everything effective.

When one causes a deity to descend upon a person in a normal state, it is done on a maṇḍala strewn with loose flowers, with incense of sandalwood, aguru, and the like. When one causes a deity to descend upon a person seized by a graha, it is done with incense of uśīra and butter. All the incenses should be mixed with butter. One should also apply this to glorious pisthaka and the like. Mixed with butter, it pacifies quartan fevers and the like. This is done with all the incenses, such as kunduru, mixed with sesame oil.

With a kīla made of iron one pins down malevolent rākṣasas, asuras, and the like. For yakṣas, it is done with a kīla of a milky tree. For nāgas, it is done with one made of nimba wood. For vidyādharas, it is done with one made of śrīkhaṇḍa. One should also pin down with the kīla beings who are hostile to the teaching and those who are hostile to sentient beings. With the glorious kīla the army of a malevolent king will be pinned down. To pin down a king, the king will be pinned down with one made of campaka wood. With a kīla of human bone one pins down obstructors. Kīlas made of bone, of iron, of khadira wood, or of bamboo should be applied against all malevolent beings. Even if one acts as though striking into empty space, the target will be pinned down by the very nature of the vajra.

namastraiyadhvikānāṃ tathāgatānāṃ amalābega sambhaba adhiṣṭhāna vajra bala bega samantāgami bhramaṃ bhramaṃ bidharaṇa bidharaṇa namostute jvālānalā bimala locana sarbata prasaraṇa bisaraṇa bara prabala dru bara śaraṇa hūṃ svāhā.

Here, this is Vajrarāja, the protector of the Tathāgata's teaching. If one accomplishes the vajra by the rite of Tathāgatalocanā, one becomes a vidyādhara. By merely reciting it, the vajra in one's hand here becomes invisible. Dwelling on a mountain, one should do mantra recitation by the rite taught before until a sign appears. Then, on the full moon, if one sits cross-legged and recites through the night until dawn, the vajra will blaze, all the world systems will quake, and one will become Vajradhara, king of knowledge.

All these secret mantras were requested and asked of all the tathāgatas by Vajradhara through the rite of Gaganalocanā. This supreme king of knowledge has power without equal. It is blessed with the blessing of all the tathāgatas. Praised by all the bodhisattvas, its practice is bound by no fixed requirements. This classification of the vidyādharas, which gathers the essence of all mantras, mudrās and conduct, takes gods, nāgas, yakṣas and the rest into its care, and fully reveals the power of the tathāgatas. One who wishes to see wondrous power in this very life should take up any one of these mantras and practice it.

For this it does not matter if there is no painted scroll, if the preliminary practice has not been done, if there are no offerings, if there are no ablutions, or if meat and the like have not been given up. In this practice of the classification of the vidyādharas, one who has generated deep faith in all the buddhas and bodhisattvas and is held by the awakening mind will certainly attain accomplishment.

Here, its sacred commitment is this: having generated compassion for all sentient beings and taken up the awakening mind, one should sit cross-legged near a stūpa of the tathāgatagarbha, recite one hundred and eight times, and at all times offer oneself to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas. Laying all one's limbs on the ground, one should also speak these words: "May all the buddhas and bodhisattvas, endowed with great compassion, take me, who am suffering, into their care. Please bless me."

Then one should bind the mudrā of the palms joined above the crown of the head: namaḥ sarba tathāgatānāṃ gagane gagane. Through these mantras and mudrās one comes to abide in the sacred commitment of the vidyādharas. One should practice any one of these sacred commitments. Through these rites one becomes a vidyādhara, and through this very rite one comes to see all the maṇḍalas.

Here, these are to be avoided: harming those who train sentient beings; doing deeds that incur fault; working to harm the teaching; rejecting the sacred Dharma; performing fierce rites against householders or renunciants who keep moral discipline; and forming the mudrās with a silent application. And one should make the supplication not to apply them to any other end that is not meritorious.

In this classification of the vidyādharas, these sacred commitments will be properly guarded; and by the power of the blessing of all the tathāgatas and through the application of mantra, entry into the gateway of the bodhisattva's conduct is unfailingly accomplished. It leads along the path beyond the world. What Vajradhara requested becomes the essence of the ritual manual of the vajra family. One who has faith and diligence will certainly attain accomplishment.

Those who wish to attain distinction in this very life, who wish to attain the body of a bodhisattva, and who wish to accomplish with certainty the body of a bodhisattva should, in a secluded and empty place, perform the practice of focusing on the buddhas and bodhisattvas, and then recite any mantra whatever. In solitude one should recite the sacred Dharma, or else contemplate it, and also make prostrations as far as one is able. Then, before a stūpa or an image, at mountains, rivers, lakes, islands, parks, nearby groves and the like, or at the site of a temple, a place that holds relics, or a crossroads, or even in a cell of a monastery, or before a volume of the sacred Dharma, one should focus on the Buddha and give up all bustle. If, free of laziness and holding the sacred Dharma as supreme, one does the practice, one will become a vidyādhara in a single night.

On the fifteenth day, while one keeps the cross-legged posture unbroken, they praise one and also say, "Excellent!" Some even grant a vision of themselves. Some cause immeasurable roots of virtue to increase, and also make them descend. One will be blessed; they will bestow their blessing; and one will also be firmly established in immeasurable qualities. Likewise, through the boundless and unseen dharmas of all the buddhas and bodhisattvas, one will also fully attain their qualities. One will also fully attain great power. Thus, this is said to be the last of his worldly states.

So it is that the conduct of mantra is boundless and vast, without end or middle. Because the tathāgatas are immeasurable and boundless, this king of the lords of the sūtra collections, which knows no satiety, is called the Vajra of the Three Great Pledges. It is called the Great King of Ritual Manuals that gathers the essence of the very gateway of the Vajra of the Three Pledges, the maṇḍala of the secret of the Tathāgata's mind. The secret essence that the Lord of Secrets (Vajrapāṇi) truly received from Vajradhara, this essence of the treasury of all the buddhas and bodhisattvas, is for the sake of fully purifying the realm of sentient beings without remainder and for the sake of protecting the teaching. These two carry out the command of all the buddhas and bodhisattvas toward those who practice mantra and mudrā.

It is called Carrying Out the Command, the Supreme Offering of the Ocean of the Tathāgata's Great Jewel. It is also called The Conduct of Mantra and Mudrā. It is also called The Unmoving Jewel. It is also called Infinite Qualities. It is also called The Guardian of the Sacred Commitments. It is also called The Holder of Inconceivable Aspirations. It is also called The Protector of the Teaching. It is also called Great Power.

Free of doubt about this unfailing one, one should apply oneself earnestly and with faith to accomplishing the inconceivable accomplishment. Here, by chanting that wondrous praise which I sang before, one will then attain the accomplishment that accords with it:
