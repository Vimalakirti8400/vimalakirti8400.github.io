About this text

Toh 366 in the Kangyur is a Vajrayāna tantric scripture known in Sanskrit as the Śrī Sarvabuddha Samayoga Ḍākinījāla Saṃvara Uttara Tantra and in Tibetan as the Dpal sangs rgyas thams cad dang mnyam par sbyor ba mkha' 'gro ma sgyu ma bde ba'i mchog, rendered in English as the Subsequent Tantra of the Glorious Union with All Buddhas, the Illusory Net of the Ḍākinī, the Supreme Bliss. It belongs to the class of Highest Yoga Tantras and is presented as a "subsequent" or continuation tantra, meaning it elaborates on and supplements a root tantra of the same cycle devoted to the union of all buddhas. The text centers on Vajrasattva as the embodiment of all buddhas, on the figure of the ḍākinī as the supreme illusory expression of non-dual bliss, and on the esoteric principle that the illusory net of feminine energy is the highest vehicle transcending both attachment and renunciation. As a canonical Vajrayāna tantra, it is placed among the Anuttarayoga tantric scriptures of the Kangyur, the collection of translated words of the Buddha in the Tibetan Buddhist canon.

In the language of India: Śrī Sarvabuddha Samayoga Ḍākinījāla Saṃvara Nāma Uttara Tantra. In the language of Tibet: the Subsequent Tantra of the Glorious Union with All Buddhas, the Illusory Net of the Ḍākinī, the Supreme Bliss.

Homage to the glorious Vajrasattva.

The bodhisattva, the very substance of all buddhas,
Vajrasattva, the Supreme Bliss,
abides forever as the self of all
in the delight of the supreme secret.

This is the self-arisen Blessed One,
the god uniquely and supremely distinguished,
the union of all buddhas,
the illusory ḍākinī of supreme bliss.

Neither devoid of attachment nor bound to it,
nor yet is the Middle Way apprehended.
The illusory net of all women
is this supreme vehicle of non-duality.

Among all illusions whatsoever,
the illusion of woman is pre-eminent.
Through the power of essential nature,
it is accomplished through intrinsic nature itself.

By this woman, in all three worlds,
those who pay reverence are transformed.
Through gain and pleasure and every joy,
even base conduct is accomplished.

When one thoroughly transforms one's own nature,
it is accomplished through the illusion of all women.
This wondrous illusory mudrā (Skt. mudrā)
is taught under the designation ḍākinī.

The syllable ḍā is explained as "moving in the sky,"
discerned directly from the element.
She who accomplishes movement throughout all space
is renowned as ḍākinī.

Through the mudrā of all without remainder,
through the supreme bliss of all without remainder,
she who is the union of all buddhas
is renowned as ḍākinī.

She is called "moving in the sky,"
discerned thoroughly from the buddha-element.
She who is accomplished as pervasive throughout all
is the ḍākinī who is the self of all buddhas.

The syllable śaṃ is explained as bliss,
the great bliss of all buddhas.
She who perfectly unites all illusions,
supremely blissful, is the Supreme Bliss.

Entirely and from every side, in all things,
as the self forever, in all aspects,
she has become the very substance
of all things — buddhas and all else, stable and moving.

Mind, the samādhi of the bodhisattva,
and the manifold conduct of awakening,
likewise the māras and the conqueror of māras —
that is awakening, that is a buddha.

Vajra and vajra-holder alike,
likewise lotus and lotus-bearer,
jewel and jewra-bearer alike —
these too are the families thereof.

That very reality is attachment and freedom from attachment,
it is desire and it is liberation.
The three times, the three existences, the three supreme ones,
the three worlds, the three realms,
all that abides in its various, distinct forms —
all of that is suchness itself.

All beings without remainder
in every respect become this.
All that is supreme and boundless, beginning with that —
the dharmadhātu, equal and unequal,
all things that are the limitless expanse of the sky —
all that is precisely that.

At every moment throughout all space, the glorious
Vajrasattva, the Tathāgata,
the union of all buddhas,
the illusory ḍākinī of supreme bliss.

That which all buddhas have not attained
throughout innumerable tens of millions of aeons —
through whichever mudrā rituals
it shall be attained in this very life —
that I shall now investigate.

The Lord of all buddhas, the Supreme Bliss,
attained through thoroughly accomplishing one's own nature:
he is the Lord of all buddhas, the Supreme Bliss.

Yogīs whose minds are not authentic,
who meditate upon a deity by fixing the mind upon it —
yoga arises as the reflection's own nature,
in traditions such as the imitation and the like.

Yogīs of supremely authentic mind
who meditate upon a deity by fixing the mind upon it,
who unite themselves with their own samaya (Skt. samaya) —
they will accomplish the immutable, the holy.

In practices such as the reflection tradition and the like,
yoga will not arise.
But if one strives with diligence in the awakening mind,
from that, the yogī becomes divine.

The awakening mind is the vajra,
it is the very nature of all buddhas.
Therefore, by uniting oneself with all through it —

one will attain all buddhas.
I myself am all buddhas,
and all heroes (vīras) am I.
Therefore, by uniting oneself with the divine,
one should accomplish oneself completely.
By this, all buddhas
and all heroes,
and all vajra-holders —
in this very life they will be accomplished.

Thus spoke the Blessed One, the glorious Vajrasattva, on that ground.

This is the first reflection of the wisdom seal (jñānamudrā), which enters into the sacred domain of the luminous suchness of the supreme yoga of the union of all buddhas, the illusory ḍākinī of supreme bliss.

This is the yoga of the Blessed One,
Vajrasattva the Tathāgata,
the union of all buddhas,
the illusory ḍākinī, the supreme bliss.

By uniting oneself with Vajrasattva,
all that is to be accomplished is perfectly achieved.

The union of all buddhas,
the lord of yoga, the great bliss —
by that very bliss
all the bliss of buddhas is obtained.

The union of all buddhas,
the lord of yoga, the great bliss —
in this, bathing and vows are not needed,
nor is the practice of severe austerities required.

Without austerities, without vows,
through bliss and through joy it is accomplished.

Therefore, conduct oneself as one desires,
eat all things, likewise act in all ways.
Do whatever one wishes to do,
move about as one pleases.
Whether rising or sitting,
walking or remaining in whatever position —
whether laughing or speaking at length,
wherever one is, however one conducts oneself —

even one who has not entered a maṇḍala,
or one who possesses every obscuration:
if one unites oneself with one's own deity,
even with little merit, accomplishment will come about.

Indeed, through this yoga,
which accomplishes all things,
by all manner of inferior food and inferior conduct,
no faults whatsoever will arise.

All the holy buddhas
whom one could not attain in a billion aeons —
if accomplished through the rites of the seal,
they will all be attained completely.

This, which is the supreme means of accomplishing oneself,
is what I must examine.

The union of all buddhas,
the illusory ḍākinī, the supreme bliss —
by uniting oneself with Vajrasattva,
through transforming one's dress and appearance accordingly,
one becomes the lord of all the various seals.
Even with little merit, accomplishment will come about.

Simply by accomplishing this seal,
all buddhas bestow their blessing.
When one is utterly blessed,
all buddhas will assemble.

By uniting equally with all buddhas,
one becomes equal to all buddhas.
Because one is equal to all buddhas,
all buddhas will be accomplished.

Whoever sees the one who possesses this
and venerates them in every way —
by that, all buddhas too
will be seen and venerated.

Through seeing and touching
both this one and all buddhas,
even one who has not entered the maṇḍalas
will come to see the truths.

Through seeing and touching,
through hearing and through recollecting,
one is freed from every sin,
and all the accomplishments (siddhis) of the buddhas are achieved.

Through the full yoga-union with this one
and with all buddhas,
even women will be liberated
and will touch the awakening of the buddhas.

Everywhere, from all sides, in all things,
in every aspect of oneself,
one will come to see one's own nature
as the actual accomplishment of all buddhas.

Because this abides as the very self of all,
through vast and boundless masses of offering clouds
arranged in infinite assemblies,
the Tathāgatas are offered worship.

The splendour of union-worship as one's own self,
the supreme Vajrasattva,
is the intrinsic nature of all buddhas —
the supreme bliss will be obtained.

By the full union of all yogīs —

splendour becomes the ground, and accomplishment is achieved.
All yogīs are the Blessed One,
Vajrasattva, the Tathāgata.
His union encompasses
all three realms without exception.

Through the enjoyments of all yogīs,
resting in bliss just as one wishes,
and through the yoga of one's own deity,
one makes oneself utterly supreme.

Through sequential union one performs worship,
and if one continuously savours
all the bliss of every yogī as one's own self,
through most refined union it will be accomplished.

By this rasāyana bliss of the essential nature of all buddhas,
the splendour, life, and youth of Vajrasattva,
freedom from illness, and supreme bliss are accomplished.

Everywhere, from all sides, in all things,
the union of all buddhas —
the supreme bliss of the ḍākinī illusion —
will always be accomplished.

Thus did the Blessed One, the glorious Vajrasattva, declare.

This is the second chapter, the tantra of total union, from the Glorious Union of All Buddhas, the Supreme Bliss of the Illusory Ḍākinī.

This is the yogī, the Blessed One,
Vajrasattva, the Tathāgata,
the union of all buddhas,
the supreme bliss of the illusory ḍākinī.

The yogī of Vajrasattva,
glorious, resting in one's own sacred pledge,
the great bliss of the yoga-sovereign
of the union of all buddhas —

Through the glorious union of Vajrasattva,
by that very power, throughout all things,
all the great-bliss mudrās
will each individually arise of themselves.

Through the glorious union of Vajrasattva,
by that very power, throughout all things,
all the blissful intoxications of yoga
will each individually arise of themselves.

Through the glorious union of Vajrasattva,
by that very power, throughout all things,
all the accomplishments of blissful intoxication
will individually arise of themselves.

Through the glorious union of Vajrasattva,
by that very power, throughout all things,
all the sovereigns of blissful intoxication
will individually arise of themselves.

Through the glorious union of Vajrasattva,
by that very power, throughout all things,
the boundless expanse of space
will give rise to the supreme women.

Arising from that sacred pledge,
the beloved sacred pledge follows me.
Refined union and great union —
the supreme sacred pledge of all as oneself.

Acting as one with one another,
delightfully following through shared activity —
the union of all buddhas,
the supreme illusory ḍākinī blazes forth.

All the mudrās of complete joy
are the great bliss.
The supreme worship of the ever-perfect goddess-queen
is the highest of all worships.

Through Vajrasattva's union
and the gathering of all goddesses,
every auspicious fortune without exception
is accomplished individually through bliss.

By the seal impressed through this mudrā,
Vajrasattva, the great deity,
and all the great-bliss goddesses
of the boundless expanse of space —

All are intoxicated with the bliss of yoga.
By the force of faultlessness, openly manifest,
they delight one another with great joy
and rejoice in the manner of heroic beings.

Through what arises from all the senses,
through the array of all vast offering clouds
spread in infinite assemblies,
one performs worship as the nature of all.

The empowerment of supreme great bliss
arising from all the bliss of yoga —
by all the assembled jewels,
empowerment is conferred in utter rapture.

The enjoyments of all the goddesses
are to be enjoyed just as one wishes.
By uniting myself with the great deity,
I myself offer worship to myself.

Through the sequential union of all the bliss of yoga,
worship is performed,
and when one continually savours it,
through most refined union it is perfectly accomplished.

By that rasāyana bliss of the essential nature of all buddhas,
the splendour and life of Vajrasattva —

youth, freedom from illness, and supreme bliss are accomplished. The great body of all buddhas, the melodious speech of all buddhas, the great mind of all buddhas, the great worship of all buddhas, the great sovereignty of all buddhas, the lord of all vajra-holders, the foremost among all lords of the world, the lord of all jewels — when one revels in joy with these, one moves, comes and goes, and soars according to one's wish. All the goddesses accomplish the great siddhi. The wheel-turning sovereign is perfectly accomplished. Equal to the dharmadhātu and yet unequal to it, boundlessly extending to the limits of the expanse of space — intoxicated by the bliss of all siddhis, there is no other supreme vastness beyond that. The union of all buddhas, by that great bliss of the sovereign of yoga, in this very lifetime, without anything whatsoever remaining unaccomplished in any respect — intoxicated by the bliss of all siddhis, equal to the dharmadhātu and yet unequal to it, boundlessly extending to the limits of the expanse of space, intoxicated by the bliss of all sovereigns — there is nothing whatsoever that is a higher vastness. The union of all buddhas: by that supreme bliss of the ḍākinī illusion, in this very lifetime, there is nothing that remains unaccomplished in any respect.

Thus did the Blessed One, the glorious Vajrasattva, declare.

This is the third chapter, called "The Auspicious Vajra Samaya Cognition of Supreme Auspicious Fortune of the Union with All Goddesses," from "The Supreme Bliss of the Ḍākinī Illusion, the Union with All Buddhas."

This is the Blessed One of yoga, Vajrasattva the Tathāgata, the union of all buddhas, the supreme bliss of the ḍākinī illusion, the union with all goddesses — the wish of supreme auspicious fortune, the one who savours the bliss of genuine union, deep and unwavering in attachment. The great form of all buddhas, and melody, fragrance, taste, and the supreme tactile bliss of all buddhas — these are fully enjoyed as one's own experience. The perfections of all buddhas accomplished, and the dhāraṇīs and grounds likewise — one who delights in these: what need is there to speak of the auspicious fortune of gods, humans, and women? The joy of divine siddhi, the great bliss of the awakening of all buddhas — by savouring auspicious fortune and bliss, each one will be spontaneously accomplished. All that arises from siddhi, the splendour of all Tathāgatas, all the enjoyments of auspicious fortune — these are enjoyed as one's own experience. The supreme accomplishment of the vajra-holder, the splendour of the first lord of the world, the supreme bliss in which buddhas rejoice — having seized it, one makes full use of it. All the great buddha-sovereignties of the entire three realms without remainder — by the force of splendid power, having seized them openly, one makes full use of them. The great siddhi of all buddhas, the supreme and sacred lords of excellence — if one seizes all of these and enjoys them, what need is there to speak of other enjoyments? The auspicious stability that arises from mighty power, joy from what is seized through recollection, and the delight of the siddhi — and further, having seized them by the force of splendid power, one bestows them upon the faithful. The entire sovereignty of the three worlds without remainder, together with all lords of the world — having seized them by the force of splendid power, one bestows them upon the faithful. Brahma's entrance-dispelling great lord, the supreme and sacred lords of excellence — if all are seized and swiftly bestowed, what need is there to speak of other enjoyments? There is nothing he does not bestow. There is nothing whatsoever to be done for the sake of the buddhas that is not done. It is also generosity and the act of giving. Buddhas and bodhisattvas too — if one seizes them and bestows them, what need is there to speak of other supreme deeds, vast merit, and other siddhis? The supreme siddhi is certain. The vanquisher of all hosts, the great siddhi of all buddhas —

The perfection of the wheel-turning sovereign, the union with all buddhas — through the supreme bliss of the illusory ḍākinī, the great miraculous power of all buddhas fully enters into wondrous manifestation. In some cases, there is the arising of the great awakening mind; in some cases, conduct proceeds harmoniously; in some cases, there is the supreme transference from among the Tuṣita gods; in some cases, birth is utterly pure; in some cases, the definitive going-forth (pravrajyā) is clearly accomplished; in some cases, one proceeds toward great awakening; in some cases, māra is thoroughly vanquished; in some cases, awakening is directly and fully realized; in some cases, the wheel of Dharma is set in motion; in some cases, the tīrthikas of the opposing shores are brought to account in accordance with the Dharma; in some cases, one is victorious over the three worlds — the lord of all attainments. In some cases, the supreme of all conceptions, the unsurpassed lord of siddhi. And so on, and on, limitlessly and supremely — through the supreme bliss that is the master of all buddhas, the definitive dance of skillful means for taming beings is enacted with delight in wondrous emanation. The supreme of all great siddhis, the master of the great lord of stability — form, long life, complexion, and strength — like the glorious Vajrasattva, are to be served by beings, and are venerated by all Tathāgatas. The supreme and glorious one among all goddesses is perfectly accomplished in Vajrasattva. All the many realms of sentient beings, without exception, are sealed with the mudrā. The hardships of the gods and of awakening are firmly ripened within sentient beings. Through the supreme bliss that is the master of all buddhas, the welfare of all sentient beings is accomplished, and, without remainder and without exception, one abides throughout all aeons in existence.

Thus did the Blessed One Vajrasattva declare these words.

This is called the supreme siddhi of the highest excellent fortune of all the various kinds, from the Supreme Bliss of the Illusory ḍākinī, the Union with All Buddhas — the fourth chapter: the Reflection on the Glorious Samaya.

This Blessed One of yoga, Vajrasattva the Tathāgata, the Union with All Buddhas, the supreme bliss of the illusory ḍākinī — he alone is perfectly and truly accomplished. Applied just as one desires, in all the ritual procedures of reflection, he is the supreme of the supreme, the highest siddhi. Through the engagement in the various activities of karma and through rites of many kinds, in order to tame sentient beings according to their wishes and to accomplish all their happiness — this very Blessed One of yoga, emanating as all the buddhas, when he manifests emanations for the welfare of all sentient beings, swiftly and perfectly accomplishes everything. Whether day by day, month by month, or year by year, having bestowed blessings accordingly, the dance of Buddha Supreme Bliss is performed.

From all the essences of the elements, and likewise arising from the vital breath and the channels, one performs the sealing mudrā of accomplishment in the guise of one's own deity. Whatever maṇḍala one relies upon, through offerings of the various mudrās, one makes offerings to each and every assembly of disciples there, and through that accomplishes the aim. Beyond that as well, the emanations of all buddhas throughout all reflective rituals — having made whatever emanations are fitting — their accounts are to be fully expounded.

The bodhisattva, the very reality of all buddhas, Vajrasattva of supreme bliss — when he delights in the supreme secret, all dwell permanently as that very nature. This Blessed One of yoga is the true, supreme, stable, and enduring one — ever arising, as though in the present moment — whose essential nature is difficult to transcend. For all those who desire the various rites, through the various engagements of activity, this has been done for the sake of taming such as Buddha Vajradhara and the rest. All beings, from buddhas onward, whether animate or inanimate — everything becomes this very reality. The supreme among all the various forms,
the supreme aims of all sentient beings —
these are accomplished through taming, beginning with the buddhas.
This is the relief breathed forth by glorious Vajrasattva.

In a past time, what arose before —
the permanence of the Great Self —
within the noble speech of the Tathāgata,
the way of beings, what arose before:
at that time all sentient beings,
striving ardently in profound emptiness,
engaging with the nature of reality that is cessation,
ceased and passed utterly into nirvāṇa.

That very Blessed One of yoga,
Vajrasattva, the Tathāgata —
for the complete purification of all who are liberated,

Neither empty nor non-empty,
nor is the Middle Way itself found as an object.
Engaging in the Perfection of Wisdom,
skillful means and compassion are the very self of all —
and therefore that is the method of compassion,
made luminously clear as the Perfection of Wisdom.

Among phenomena free of conceptual thought,
having thoroughly examined for the sake of sentient beings,
and having committed oneself to non-conceptuality —
all things are to be examined through examination.

Like the sound of an echo from a cliff,
that becomes the words of the Dharma.
And from that, by the song
of the true and expansive qualities of the Great Vehicle,
the three commitments of the three times
praise all the Tathāgatas.

Peerless, unmoving, possessed of the nature of reality that is the supreme without equal —
whose very nature is compassion, dispelling the suffering of beings.
The bestower of limitless qualities and all attainments,
stainless, unmoving, possessed of the supreme Dharma of the incomparable and holy.

Even with respect to the most subtle fraction of qualities, there is no measure —
nor can sky itself serve as an analogy to be compared.
Those limitless attainments, peerless and beyond example —
bestow assuredly the supreme attainments upon the realm of sentient beings.

Always arising from the power of stainless compassion,
possessed of the nature of reality in which fulfilled aspirations are unceasing —
accomplishing the welfare of beings with utterly boundless endeavor,
the great compassion itself, its very nature, ever appears in all its forms.

Immeasurable, and transcending measure — supremely and perfectly complete —
even having attained the noble state of the Sugata (the Well-Gone One),
the supreme attainments are bestowed constantly upon all three realms.
The activity of compassion is immovable and will never cease.

How wondrous — endowed with the supreme nature of reality of the utterly good!
Having attained the highest rank among all those who bestow supreme gifts,
the supreme attainments are bestowed constantly upon all three realms.
The Protector who knows the three times without obscuration —
grant to me the supreme and holy attainments of the three supreme commitments.

This is the vast and authentic quality of all buddhas.
By uttering it even once,
all buddhas will be accomplished.

By engaging with the identity of all buddhas,
through this very king of praises,
all the Victors of the three times
will be accomplished through the three commitments.

The form (kāya) that the eyes can never tire of beholding —
having revealed the identity of all things,
saying "Excellent!" (sādhu), the supreme attainments,
all of them, are fully ripened.

The joy of accomplishing the identity of all things,
and all aspirations are completely fulfilled.
All the supreme accomplishments of awakening,
and true yoga as well, are bestowed.

Then, through that accomplishment,
and the utterly stable attainments —
whoever attains truly stable attainments
in this very existence, it is said,
should embrace all buddhas
with the vajra-interlocking gesture (vajra-añjali).

Having placed the palms together well,
clap the joined palms three times.
Whatever faculty becomes a path,
make that into that very identity.

Through utterly equalized meditative engagement,
enter into union with all buddhas.
According to whatever inclination and attachment one has,
with the hexasyllable (ṣaḍakṣara) mantra and others —
so that there is absolutely no weariness at any point —
always recite without measure.

Entirely, on all sides, all things —

oneself always in every aspect,
accomplishing attainments through all buddhas,
supremely beholding and achieving the highest —

through this, all buddhas,
likewise all the heroes (vīras),
and all the vajra-holders (vajradhāras):
in this very lifetime they will be accomplished.

Singing just once the king of praises — the vast eulogy extolling the qualities of all buddhas —
one attains the supreme and inexhaustible bliss-attainment,
the permanent supreme yoga of universal pervasion.

By this king of praises,
the supreme sovereign of all, oneself,
taming all through manifold means —
all Tathāgatas are accomplished.
Therefore, through the three pledges (samaya) and so forth,
and through all supreme unconceptualized contemplations,
the realm of sentient beings as vast as the boundless expanse of the sky
is fully liberated.
This was breathed forth by the glorious Vairocana.

In the past, what arose long ago —
the great self, the eternal —
according to what arose before, in the manner of transmigrating beings,
within the transmitted word of the Tathāgata:

at that time, all sentient beings
become exceedingly fierce and cruel.
Through being extremely cruel and fierce,
they will fall into shrieking and crying.

Then, having transmigrated, those sentient beings,
through the linking of habitual tendencies,
become supremely and utterly ferocious.
The mind, blessed by māra,
becomes epidemic, poison, weapon-poison and disease,
female demons (piśācīs), yakṣa-spirits,
māra itself and the perverse misleading spirits —
all become supremely and utterly ferocious.
They terrify all three abodes
and devour even accomplished practitioners.
They cause gods and others to transmigrate,
and they destroy the teachings as well.

The red lotus of the buddhas,
the varied Vajrapāṇi,
the white color of the lord of the world,
the gold of glorious Vajrasūrya —
all colors and whatever is available:
the parasol, the victory banner, the ornaments —
all these accomplish the glory
of the supreme horse endowed with the vajra.

Whatever the place and retinue are like,
whatever the arrangement and the beings present are like,
whatever the arrangement of the retinue is like —
with a cakra supreme in just that fashion one acts.

From the direct vision of the cakras of the deities of Vajrasattva,
whatever the examination of merit is like —
what is written later is likewise so.

One who is wise draws the maṇḍala with cords,
accomplishes the equality of all directions,
and by drawing four cords in the eastern, western, and northern directions,
one has eight maṇḍalas.

Who can ever perfectly fix
precisely what a correct maṇḍala is?
Through the intrinsic nature of the deity by oneself,
the examination of the maṇḍala will be accomplished.

By joining oneself to the deity in one's own nature,
one accomplishes all siddhis.
Even if the rite is excessive or incomplete,
there is no fault — it will be accomplished.

Everywhere varied — the excellent fortune.
Everywhere varied — the one possessing the wheel.
Everywhere varied — for the sake of accomplishment.
The maṇḍala is everywhere varied.

The round maṇḍala of the buddha,
like the crescent of the vajra-bearing one,
the lotus form, by the lordship of the lotus,
the jewel form, by the supremacy of the jewel.

However one wishes, whatever is available,
whatever the occasion, whatever is comfortable —
the maṇḍala dwells glorious in all forms.
It is that of the supreme horse, the mudrā, the vajra.

One who has not entered the maṇḍala,
who has abandoned the sacred pledges (samaya),
and who does not truly know the secret —
even if one strives, nothing will be accomplished.

Do not kill living beings.
Do not abandon the Three Jewels.
Do not give up the awakening mind (bodhicitta).
Do not abandon the auspicious guru.

By accomplishing through oneself with all-various good fortune,
one's fortune is excellent.
Possessing the selfless nature of reality —
one who is hostile to the sacred pledges has ill fortune.

Through having thoroughly accomplished existence,
the excellently fortunate one becomes a Blessed One (Bhagavān), a buddha.
The ill-fortuned one becomes a hearer (śrāvaka) in this world,
passing completely into nirvāṇa.

For the sake of accomplishing all conceptions,
the glorious Vajrapāṇi is of excellent fortune.
Through ardent application to emptiness,
the pratyekabuddhas are of ill fortune.

Through the complete purification of all attachment,
the lord Avalokiteśvara is of excellent fortune.
Through holding wrong views,
non-Buddhists (tīrthikas) in the world are of ill fortune.

For the sake of gathering the supreme holy riches,
the glorious Vajrasūrya is of excellent fortune.
Through not being attached even to one's own child,
all those who practice austerities are of ill fortune.

Through gathering all supreme sovereign dominion,
the glorious supreme horse is of excellent fortune.
Through clinging to what has no self and yet holding onto it,
all the sages (munis) are of ill fortune.

For the sake of accomplishing all happiness for oneself,
the guru of all is of excellent fortune.
All disciples who hold wrong views and are hostile to the sacred pledges
are of ill fortune.

By one who knows clearly
the true meaning of the secret,
in accordance with the word of all buddhas,
one accomplishes buddhahood completely.

When, abiding in the guru of all,
one proceeds to enter the wheel,
to the disciple who desires the maṇḍala —
the supreme infinite true secret:

At the very outset one speaks the secret,
asking: "You there — what do you delight in?"
"I delight in excellent fortune," the one entering
should also declare.

If one knows this limb of the secret,
one should be introduced according to one's wish.
If one does not truly know the secret,
one should be introduced by the right hand.

Having thus entered the maṇḍala,
one then offers gifts most generously.
By knowing the rite of the secret itself,
the supreme immutable accomplishment is achieved.

To the all-various guru,
one must certainly offer the supreme gifts.
Whatever is most difficult to give away —
that, it is said, is the highest offering.

Whatever is one's most beloved and precious,
or whatever is exceptionally distinguished —
that very thing, to the guru,
one offers with inexhaustible desire.

Or if it is something one desires and may lose,
or if it is something the mind truly needs —
give that, or render the needed service.
Even if small, that is supreme.

If, however, one possesses excellent things
and offers a paltry gift,
one despises the guru of all,
and will always obtain suffering.

A richly adorned young woman,
or likewise a beloved wife —
offering her and redeeming her with a price
is the supreme offering for buddhahood and awakening.

Great mothers, offered
and then redeemed again with a price —
this is the supreme offering
for the union that is the glorious Vajradhara.

Fine pearls, jewels,
a pair of very white cloth,
and likewise white horses —
these are the supreme offerings for the lotus-bearing one.

Royal dominion, wealth, and treasures,
assemblies of garments and ornaments,
and likewise all manner of worship —
these are the offerings for Vajrasūrya.

Supremely adorned horses,
and supremely adorned elephants,
and chariots filled with precious things —
these are the offerings for the king of horses.

All the intrinsic natures of the elements,
and likewise those of the vital force and the root —
the precious empowerment of buddha:
the wise one examines it in all its various aspects.

On the crown of the head, the forehead,
the two cheeks, and the back of the neck —
of all the precious buddhas,
this is the supreme immutable crown ornament.

All the intrinsic natures of the elements,
and likewise those of the vital force and the root —
whether a precious skull crown ornament
or the wish-fulfilling gems at the crest of buddhas:
in every case, variously and supremely,
it is to be applied to all teachers and masters.

All realms of sovereignty, utterly pure,
are accomplished without remainder, without exception.
Made from a single wondrous jewel
and adorned with varied ornaments in the same way,
by the empowerment of the lotus-buddha,
all disciples receive empowerment.

The precious buddha acts as vajra,
and is the vajra for the vajra family.
The body of the buddha of Avalokiteśvara
is the jewel of the jewel-holder.
The precious lotus-buddha of the buddhas,
and the vajra-jewel likewise —
for the supreme horse (Hayagrīva), all supreme things
are gathered into his own empowerment.

Whether cast, crafted,
beaten, or painted —
the symbolic mudrās of the various excellent marks
are to be examined carefully.

In every case, the various ones of auspicious fortune —
the symbolic seal of the self-arisen —
the goddesses of the limitless expanse of space,
the supreme bliss:

bow and arrow likewise,
the auspicious fortune of the fortunate one in the same way,
skull-cup and khaṭvāṅga,
and the vajra sword likewise.

The lotus sword of Amitāyus,
accomplishing the life of all buddhas —
the assembly of all gatherings of goddesses
accomplishing assemblies of symbolic mudrās:

arrow, and likewise the head of Brahmā,
and likewise the mudrā of the wheel,
the supreme mudrā of the vajra-trident,
the wish-fulfilling tree and the staff,
the lotus, Hayagrīva of great stature,
and the green Hayagrīva likewise,
the accomplished boar's tusk,
the moon and sun likewise —

in a great vessel of various precious things,
the mercury of the buddha's form:
all are experienced in oneself, auspicious,
in accordance with the assembly of symbolic mudrās.

Flute and all manner of vīṇā,
kumuṇḍa and murajadrums,
flowers, incense, lamps,
and the various fragrances likewise —

these and such boundless supreme things,
equal and unequal with the dharmadhātu (basic space of phenomena),
the assembly of sacred symbolic mudrās
of the limitless expanse of space:

throughout the expanse of all space, in glory,
dwelling in vajrasattva itself —
by the lord of accomplishment, supremely accomplished,
all become accomplished in themselves.

Dharma robes and the alms bowl,
and likewise the ringing staff,
and books of sacred Dharma —
these are another assembly of symbolic mudrās.

Likewise the mudrā of the pinnacle,
cymbals and drums,
the vajra and the precious vessel —
these are another assembly of symbolic mudrās.

Likewise images of buddhas,
mirrors and fans,
vajra hail together with clouds,
and likewise the mudrā of the eye,
the blazing vajra and khaṭvāṅga,
the skull-cup bowl likewise,

great crimson and compassion,
and great flesh likewise,
sharp arrows and likewise hooks,
beer and water likewise,
a staff moved by wind,
cloth and likewise fire,
the glory of the wrathful vajra-king,
the devourer of all that is harmful —
the assembly of accomplished symbolic mudrās
supremely accomplishing the yogic attainments (siddhis).

Various lotuses: red,
green, likewise white,
blue, golden, and the old stalk —
the seven aspects of the supreme lotus mark,
the lotus stalk, the seal of women,
and likewise the wheel of Dharma —

radiant and glory-bestowing, the vajra
and the further assembly of lotus marks —
these are gathered together now.

The bow, the arrow, and the sword,
and likewise blazing fire,
empowerment treasure and victory banners —
these are the gathered marks of offering.

Likewise the various lotuses
and the vajra cross in the same way:
through the accomplished mudrās of the marks,
all dominions are overcome.
All the buddhas and so forth,
all things moving and unmoving without remainder —
the seal of the marks of all self-arising beings
extends to the boundless reaches of the expanse of space.

Thus did the Blessed One, the Tathāgata,
Glorious Vajrasattva declare.

This is the seventh contemplation: the mudrā of all maṇḍalas
from the union of all buddhas — the Supreme Bliss of the Illusory Ḍākinī.

Now the exposition of further matters:

The glorious deity Vajrasattva,
supreme in all his varied forms,
is the emanation of all offerings.

With both right and left hands,
turn the index fingers and little fingers into a beautiful gesture.
The glorious left hand uses the swaying method;
the right is made into a triple banner.

On the supreme seat adorned with fringe,
oneself as glorious Vajrasattva
gazes with smiling eyes —
this is the mudrā that stirs the mind.

The buddha-vajra holds the lotus.
If the vajra sun of the three realms (tridhātu)
makes even a single gesture of homage,
how much more need be said of those who are already full of it.

The wrathful one, the lord, together with the proximate lords,
the sun, the moon, Yama (the Lord of Death), and Brahmā —
bowing, they pay supreme reverence
and will perform all actions.

When homage is paid with the glorious vajra's palms joined,
with a mind that is stirred,
and the offering to the buddhas is well made,
all without remainder will be accomplished without remainder.

By lifting the left eyebrow
and filling it with the sound "ru lu ru lu,"
Brahmā, the proximate lords, and the wrathful ones
of the three realms will come to pay homage.

With the palms joined like a lotus in bud,
bound at one's own heart with the seal of union,
the thumbs and little fingers spread like petals —
one also makes the prostration of full unfolding.

All the limbs of the body
are laid upon the ground in prostration;
at the heart, the mouth, and the crown of the head
one joins the palms in homage.

When homage is performed with the glorious vajra swaying method
and with smiling eyes,
even the three realms will pay homage —
what need then to speak of those of lesser power?

This is known as the mudrā of prostration.

All the streams of dance, however many,
and every manner of performing actions —
these are the seals of Vajrasattva
that accomplish one's own purposes.

Having performed the swaying method,
one dances in whatever style befits,
and with the various circling dances
all offerings are to be presented.

With both the right and left hands,
the index fingers and little fingers are turned into a beautiful gesture;
the left index finger is clenched into a fist,
and the right is formed into a triple banner.

When this mudrā is sealed,
one comes to abide with all the buddhas,
and the supreme great sovereign mastery
of all the great siddhis is achieved.

This is the mudrā called "Auspicious Companionship with All Buddhas."

Placed with great pride,
one lifts that very identity through the union,
turns it fully toward the direction of the side,
and places it at the crown of the head.

Performing the dance of causing all to descend,
having danced and then brought it down,
the body called "Auspicious HŪṂ"
is made to present all offerings.

This is the mudrā called "Union with All Buddhas — Auspicious Great Bliss."

With both the right and left hands,
the index fingers and little fingers are turned into a beautiful gesture,
the vajra palms are joined together,
and in the same way it is turned to the left.

Through the union, that is lifted
and placed at the crown of the head.

With the song called "Auspicious,"
all offerings are to be presented.

This is the Auspicious Vajra Joined Palms — the offering to the buddhas.

With both the right and left hands,
the index fingers and little fingers are turned into a beautiful gesture,
and the vajra palms are released upward.
This is the manner of the offering torma for all.

Performing the dance in charnel-ground attire,
one dances with this mudrā,
and Auspicious Vajra is set forth in fierce array —
all offerings are to be presented.

This is the Skull-Cup Joined Palms of Glorious Vajra Heruka — the rite of all offering tormas.

With both the right and left hands,
the index fingers and little fingers are turned into a beautiful gesture,
and the right and left palms are clapped together —
all offerings are to be presented.

This is the Joined Palms of Glorious Padma, Lord of Dance.

With both the right and left hands,
the index fingers and little fingers are turned into a beautiful gesture,
and the vajra and jewel palms are joined together —
all offerings are to be presented.

This is the mudrā called "Glorious Vajra Jewel" — the fulfiller of all wishes.

Scattering upward and bringing down,
circling and in the same way laying flat,
flowers, incense, supreme lamps,
and the offering of fragrance arise.

Whether full or empty,
or with whatever is at hand,
by accomplishing the samaya in an even, balanced way,
all offerings will be accomplished.

By binding these mudrās,
all buddhas will be offered to.
Even the lord endowed with siddhis,
the very self of all, will be offered to.

Of all the offerings of the self-arisen ones
in the space of the dharmadhātu — equal and unequal,
boundless as the expanse of the sky —
there is no supremely noble great wave
that has not been made by this
and has not been caused to multiply.

There is nothing whatsoever that is not accomplished
by the union of all buddhas,
by the lord of union with supreme great bliss,
by siddhis, actions, and bliss.

The limitless accomplished offerings,
without remainder, without exception, are all accomplished.

Thus spoke the Blessed One Vajrasattva.

This is the eighth chapter of "Glorious Auspicious Samaya Ritual: Union with All Offerings" — drawn from the supreme illusory-bliss ḍākinī chapter of Union with All Buddhas.

Then, moreover, it is further explained:

All the supreme, manifold, and universal mudrās
of the glorious deity Vajrasattva
are emanations.

The bodhisattva, the very essence of all buddhas,
Vajrasattva, supreme in bliss —
in the delight of the highest secret,
the self of all ever abides.

In order to be attached to all bliss
and to purify all siddhis,
Vajrasattva, supreme in bliss,
has a body red like a fish in moonlight.

Because he is attached to all attachment,
the Blessed One of great passion —
this mudrā of Vajrasattva
blazes with red and manifold light.

In the three realms there is no other jewel,
nothing like the buddha-jewel.
The great jewel of all buddhas,
crowned with a diadem, is supreme in bliss.
It is not another's wealth, and this cannot be stolen.
It is purified by this divine water,
and by power, essential nature,
and inherent character, that is accomplished.

With a crown adorned with a garland of skulls,
bearing the wish-fulfilling gem at the crown,
the lord of a world entirely afflicted —
Abhirati and so forth — is accomplished thereby.

Glorious Vajrasattva in union,
gathered together with all the goddesses,
with all the manifold and auspicious —
all ḍākinīs are accomplished.

The buddha Vajra-Lotus Bearer,
Vajra Sun of the three realms,
aroused by the seal of bewilderment,
is engaged in all activities.

Sealed with this mudrā,
one goes, comes, and soars as desired.
All the supreme glorious goddesses —
Vajrasattva is supremely accomplished.

This is called the supreme mudrā "Glorious Vajrasattva, the Supreme Play of Bliss in Auspicious Fortune, Fully Engaged in Supreme Joy."

With the union of right and left,
dancing in the manner of bliss,
seated in the bodhisattva's cross-legged posture,
one utterly vanquishes all armies.

This is the cross-legged posture of glorious Vajrasattva.

A face smiling like the color of the moon,
purifying all accomplishments,
and purifying the desire of the lotus —
between the thighs, red and white,
the supreme hand like a blue leaf.

Likewise, with the lord of bliss
and the lord who touches bliss,
bearing thighs white and golden,
with divine substances of variegated supreme colors,
with radiance of many kinds gathered in brilliance,
abiding upon manifold lotuses,
all abide in perfect purity.

Supreme among buddhas such as Padma (Amitābha) and the rest,
adorned with jewels of many kinds, clothed in robes —
it is the gathering of all buddhas,
adorned as with Abhirati and the rest.

Activities, reversals,
and seizures — perform these supremely.
With the embrace-mudrā,
the seizure of all limbs is accomplished.

Sealed with this mudrā,
one goes, comes, and soars as desired.
Glorious sovereign over all desires —
Vajrasattva is supremely accomplished.

This is the mudrā called "Glorious Vajra-Arrow, the Supreme Play of Bliss in Auspicious Fortune, Fully Engaged in Supreme Joy."

With attachments of manifold samaya,
dancing in the manner that brings down bliss,
the supreme embrace-mudrā
of the great supreme deity of the center —
with this mudrā, the yogin
goes, comes, and soars as desired.
Glorious, endowed with the sovereignty of great bliss —
Vajrasattva is supremely accomplished.

This is the mudrā called "The Samaya of Glorious Vajrasattva, the Supreme Play of Bliss in Auspicious Fortune, Fully Engaged in Supreme Joy."

In the manner of displaying the fangs,
grasping the vajra-khaṭvāṅga,
the mudrā of embrace —
the supreme vajra-cross together with the lotus.

Sealed with this mudrā,
one goes, comes, and soars as desired.
Sovereign and victorious over all yogins —
Vajrasattva is supremely accomplished.

This is the mudrā called "Glorious Vajra-Yoga, the Supreme Play of Bliss in Auspicious Fortune, Fully Engaged in Supreme Joy."

Accomplishing the skull-cup of the fully awakened buddha,
with the embrace-mudrā,
glorious Vajrasattva in union —
the great supreme deity of the center.

With this mudrā, the yogin
goes, comes, and soars as desired.
The glorious accomplishment of life and youth —
Vajrasattva is supremely accomplished.

This is the mudrā called "Glorious Vajra-Life, the Supreme Play of Bliss in Auspicious Fortune, Fully Engaged in Supreme Joy."

And so on, supreme and boundless —
the dharmadhātu, equal and unequal,
the self-arisen manifold mudrās
of the limitless expanse of space:
in every expanse of sky, gloriously
they abide in Vajrasattva,
and through the supreme accomplishment of the lord of attainments,
all descend, are accomplished, and are fulfilled in one's own being.

This is the assembly of root mudrās of the illusory supreme bliss of the ḍākinīs, united with all buddhas.

With the great dances of glorious completion,
befitting every tradition of sacred learning,
when the four arrows are fully released,
one becomes victorious over the four māras.

This is the mudrā called "Glorious Vajra-Arrow, the Crown of Great Brahmā."

With the manner of lifting and lowering the feet,
and the steps of sliding and leaping,
praised with the mudrā of the wheel,
one leaps beyond the three realms.

This is the mudrā called "Glorious Vajra of Great Power."

Activities, reversals,
and seizures — in their distinctive particulars —
dancing with manifold mudrās,

one gathers in the three realms.

This is the mudrā called "Glorious Vajra, Lord of the Dance."

Taking hold of any consort whatsoever
with the mudrā of embracing her to oneself,
and sporting with all women —
this accomplishes everything always, like the wish-fulfilling tree.

This is the mudrā called "Glorious Vajra Goddess of Desire."

Acting in the manner of tasting and drinking,
one dries up the three worlds together with the fierce ones, the lords, the attendant lords,
the sun and moon, Yama (the lord of death), and Brahmā.

This is the mudrā called "Glorious Vajra Horse-Face."

Through the inhalation of the fragrance breathed in by the nose,
together with the fierce ones, the lords, the attendant lords,
the sun and moon, Yama, and Brahmā —
one gathers in the three realms as well.

This is the mudrā called "Glorious Vajra Sow."

Through gazing with fixed and longing eyes,
together with the fierce ones, the lords, the attendant lords,
the sun and moon, Yama, and Brahmā —
one devours the three worlds as well.

This is the mudrā called "Glorious Vajra, Sun-and-Moon Eyes."

Through tasting all of it oneself,
together with the fierce ones, the lords, the attendant lords,
the sun and moon, Yama, and Brahmā —
even those reduced to ashes are restored to life.

This is the mudrā called "Glorious Vajra, Essence of All, Reviving the Three Realms."

The yogin who displays the manner of playing the flute
and dances however it arises —
together with Brahmā, Indra (Śakra), and the fierce ones —
brings the three destinies under his power.

This is the mudrā called "Glorious Vajra Flute, the Wielder of Power."

The yogin who displays the manner of playing the lute
and dances however it arises —
all activities are perfectly accomplished throughout boundless space,
and every siddhi is swiftly attained.

This is the mudrā called "Glorious Vajra Lute, Bewilderer with Bliss."

The yogin who displays the manner of the small cymbals
and dances however it arises —
all suffering arising from epidemics, demons, and poisonous attachment
is swiftly cleared away.

This is the mudrā called "Glorious Vajra Small Cymbals, Clearing Away All Suffering."

The yogin who displays the manner of the clay drum
and dances however it arises —
all three dwelling-places without exception
are brought down in a single instant.

This is the mudrā called "Glorious Vajra Clay Drum, the Intoxicator."

through the offerings of mudrā-dance,

are accomplished entirely, without remainder.

they abide in Vajrasattva.
Through the sovereign perfection of siddhi,
they all descend as one's own nature.

of the union of all buddhas,
of the illusory ḍākinīs, the supreme bliss —

the song of the glorious supreme vajra,
accomplishes all activities completely
and perfects existence to the utmost.

the Victor's vajra and precious jewels —

with the right thumb and ring finger,

and the turning of the wheel of Dharma —

the supremely and permanently abiding buddha.

Whoever sees this, being endowed with it,

seeing and making offering.

the wheel of Dharma is beautifully turned.

and conquering all with the left limb,
