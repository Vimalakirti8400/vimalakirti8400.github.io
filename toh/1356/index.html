About this text

The Four-Limbed Sādhana of Glorious Kālacakra, known in Sanskrit as the Śrī Kālacakra Caturaṅga Sādhanopāyikā and in Tibetan as Dpal dus kyi 'khor lo'i sgrub pa'i thabs yan lag bzhi pa, is a short verse manual of tantric practice centered on Kālacakra, the "Wheel of Time." It opens with homage to Mañjuśrī and Avalokiteśvara and hymns of praise to the deity, then sets out the meditation through the four limbs of approach, close approach, accomplishment, and great accomplishment. Along the way it treats the blessing of the body, the inner maṇḍala of channels, winds, and drops, the six-branch yoga of the Kālacakra completion stage, mantra recitation, the torma offering, and a coded reading of the precepts and perfections in terms of inner yoga. Its colophon credits it to the great Kālapāda and says it was translated into Tibetan by the paṇḍita Viśvaśrī and the translator Gyijo Dawé Özer. It is Toh 1356 in the Tengyur, where it belongs to the tantra commentaries, among the works on the Kālacakra cycle.

Translation

In the language of India: śrī kālacakra caturaṃga sāṅhanopikā nāma. In the language of Tibet: The Four-Limbed Sādhana of Glorious Kālacakra.

Homage to noble Mañjuśrī. Homage to noble Avalokiteśvara.

Through your supreme compassion,
to draw others out of the unbearable suffering of saṃsāra,
you, the Teacher, yourself abide
on the path of wisdom.

Though old, you are a youth,
the son of all the victors without exception,
the first of the First Buddha;
united with a woman, you live in pure conduct.

Through your supreme compassion,
friend of the world, foe of Yama,
you alone are the very vajra
that dispels the fears of existence.
You are forever the māra of māras;
you are wholly liberated from existence;
for the sake of ripening beings,
you are Yama who enters the world.

To whose family the sages bow,
sun of the brahmin family, lord,
at whose lotus feet all bow down,
who, for the liberation of sentient beings,
made the whole of Kālacakra
clear upon the earth:
you are of the lineage of the degenerate age,
honored by the lords of the earth,
renowned in glory as the fifth Kalkī.
At your lotus feet,
having joined the palms of my hands
and placed them upon my highest limb,
I bow down with the crown of my head,
and shall explain the sādhana,
complete, with nothing lacking.

A pleasure grove, mountain crags,
a place of the supreme victors,
an empty house, a temple of the gods,
a place of the siddhas, a charnel ground,
a lakeside, a hidden spot:
wherever the mind delights,
there the practice
is to be done, O lord of men!

One whose mind is profound and vast,
who delights in the guru's assurance,
who has a generous mind and knows good qualities,
who longs for liberation and has faith in the tantra,
whose mind does not waver,
who has found reality and keeps it secret,
who is free from the company of the malicious,
and who has received empowerment, in due order,
in the maṇḍala of glorious Kālacakra:
such a one should sit on a soft bed
or on a comfortable seat.

Having beautifully smeared the maṇḍala,
one sets out the offerings endowed with the ten substances.
One should call to mind the threefold purity
disclosed by the excellent mudrā.

On the ground of the three vajras,
first of all, torma
should be given to the protectors of the Dharma.
Then, with the four vajras,
one surely burns the hosts of māras
that have gone into the directions and intermediate directions.
The hosts of māras
dwelling on the ground of the glorious vajra
are surely burned, without remainder.

Then, on a wheel of ten spokes,
one should visualize the host of wrathful ones
going into the directions and intermediate directions.
Reaching to the very limit of the earth's boundaries,
as far into the distance as can be seen,
three fences of triple vajras
go round the circuit of the earth,
reaching to the underworld and to the sky.

In the vajra mansion of many stories,
in the center is a lotus of twelve cubits,
its hub and seat
measuring four and two cubits.
Upon it, oneself,
entirely free of stains,
in the center of the wheel, the lord of wrathful ones,
with twenty-six arms
and four faces, Vajravega:
thus the yoginī should meditate.

First, in the center of the moon at the heart,
one meditates on the variegated rays of light
of that very syllable, going out into the ten directions.
Having purified the mouth and the rest,
again, by sending out rays of light
into the sky, one invites the victors.
Having made various offerings,
one should confess
the many misdeeds amassed in existence.
Going for refuge to the Three,
with body, speech and mind made pure,
is what the practitioner is to do.

In all the many kinds of virtue
that the perfect buddhas, the bodhisattvas
and the noble Saṅgha have done,
in all of it without exception, I rejoice.
I follow the conduct of awakening,
free from afflictions.
In the Buddha, the Dharma and the Saṅgha,
which dispel the fears of existence,
I take refuge until awakening.
"May I become a perfect buddha!"
Making this aspiration
is for the sake of sentient beings.

All these beings, without exception,
have the nature of the very essence of things;
free of things, they are empty.
Therefore there is no buddha and no awakening.
With compassion that benefits others,
one has made the pledge in a signless way.
Having understood all of this in this way,
one should meditate on the bodhisattva
who is the vajra of body, speech and mind,
endowed with immeasurable qualities,
the lord of the maṇḍala in the maṇḍala.

First, the one who strives in the vows
brings about, in the center of the body,
the destruction of fire by water.
Then earth turns into water,
entering into the water like salt.
Wind dries up the water,
and it swiftly vanishes
into space. Then
mind and fire, at the end of darkness,
wholly free of objects,
are to be set in the center.

Space, wind, fire and water,
earth, ocean, mountain, lotus,
moon, sun and fire,
and the mansion of many stories, from every side
radiating stainless rays of light,
a tent born of vajras;
within it, on the vajra ground,
like the light of various jewels,
spreading out over the maṇḍala,
born from the wisdom of oṃ:
on the lotus of the supreme victors,
seats of moon and sun.
Within are sixteen pillars,
having the nature of the lords of the families.
The wheel of the victors' enjoyment,
at its four royal doors,
is adorned with archways of the nature of jewels and gold,
and with charnel grounds.

a and the rest, ka and the rest, sun and moon,
together with the lords of the families,
and the mirror-like wisdom and the rest,
radiating stainless light,
sending forth five rays of light:
one should meditate on Kālacakra.

The body endowed with vajra ornaments,
twenty-four lotus hands,
twelve upper arms, four faces,
three necks, twelve eyes,
mouths gaping and fangs bared:
the glorious one born from oṃ.

On the eight petals of a lotus,
upon moon, sun and fire,
with feet sporting greatly
upon the hearts of the two, Rudra and the god of desire,
the lord of victors stands with the right leg extended.
Beneath the supreme feet,
the red Māra is under the right,
and the white Rudra under the left.

The necks, center, left and right,
and the upper arms, are of the colors of sun and moon.
Likewise, of the twenty-four,
on the right and on the left,
the shoulders are blue and red
and white like the moon.
On the right and on the left are the two legs;
the upper arms are dark,
sun-colored and moon-colored.
Likewise, the twenty-four
hands, in their order,
are together with the weapons;
on the fingers are three joints each.
From the lotus hands, moonlike,
five colors radiate.
In the palms of the most excellent hands,
black, white and red,
the array of weapons rightly rests:
vajra, sword and trident,
the curved knife that terrifies the three realms of existence,
and the arrow of fire;
then the vajra hook,
the sounding damaru,
the hammer and the wheel,
the spear, the staff and the axe:
these are in the twelve right lotus hands
of the vajra-bearer.
Bell, shield and khaṭvāṅga,
the skull cup with mouth wide open
and filled with blood,
bow, noose and jewel,
lotus and the dweller in water, the conch,
mirror and iron chain,
the head of Brahmā with four faces,
and the lotus:
these the Victor's left hands hold.
Pressed beneath the feet,
made to bow their faces low,
are Māra and Rudra with their own goddesses.

With four faces, like gold,
embracing with eight lotus hands,
is the consort Viśvamātā.
In the right are the curved knife, the hook,
the rattling damaru
and the rosary, in their order;
in the left, mother-of-pearl and the noose,
the lotus of a hundred petals,
and likewise an excellent jewel.
With left leg extended, with twelve eyes,
crowned with the lord of victors,
she is sealed with the mudrās.

On the lotus petals of the directions are the four buddhas,
with nine eyes indeed,
their eyes as said, and six hands.
In the intermediate directions are Tārā and the other goddesses.
Further, again, in the eight
intermediate spaces between them are eight vases.
Black, red and yellow,
and white like the moon,
are the colors of the gods and goddesses.
Black, red and moon-white,
and further, going off into the quarters,
red, yellow and sun-colored:
on the walls, in the directions and intermediate directions,
are Ākāśagarbha and the others,
and Sparśavajrā and the others.
To the right and left of the east,
and on the lotuses of the other quarters,
are Samantabhadra, likewise
glorious Vajrapāṇi,
Śabdavajrā,
and Dharmadhātu, in their order.
Those called the Mute One, the Paralyzer, the Victorious One
and the Supremely Mighty One
are the door guardians of the east and the other quarters.

On moon lotuses,
the gods and goddesses
arisen from wisdom and means,
the two embracing one another,
their own lotus hands
holding each their own emblems.
Whatever emblem each holds on the right
is in the first hand;
for that reason it is free of a mudrā.
Through the wheel of wisdom and means
they dwell in supreme bliss,
on seats of lotus, vajra and the rest.

The black ones hold the sword,
the curved knife that terrifies the realms,
and the trident in their right hands;
in the left, shield and skull cup,
and the white khaṭvāṅga,
are held in their palms.
Likewise, the red ones
hold the arrow, the vajra hook
and the sounding damaru
in their right hands, in order;
in the left, bow and noose,
and the stainless radiant jewel.
The yellow ones hold the wheel, the staff
and the terrifying vajra
in their right hands, in order;
in the left, the glorious chain,
together with the ringing of the bell.
The moon-white ones hold in the right
the glorious hammer,
the spear and the trident;
in the left, the white lotus
with its eight petals,
the mirror and the rosary.
The green ones hold in the right
vajra, curved knife and axe,
in their order;
in the left, bell and skull cup,
and likewise the utpala,
treasury of all good qualities.
For the blue ones, the emblems are to be known
according to their respective qualities.
The goddesses likewise
are black, red and white,
and like refined gold:
they are the goddesses of the gods of the east and the other quarters.

Thus the vajra-bearer
has declared the three realms of existence, without exception,
to have the form of the maṇḍala.
In the outer, in the body and in the other,
which have no nature of elaboration,
the arising of things rightly abides;
in that, always, is the space vajra.
Beings gone into the three realms of existence
are impermanent.
The host of beings is not permanent;
they are just like illusions.
Viewing things as permanent and impermanent,
that too is for the sake of
purifying the minds of those of dull intellect:
it is for them that the sādhana is set forth.

What only goes from the mouth to the heart
has no power to accomplish.
Whatever sādhana of the vajra-bearer
the practitioner is to accomplish,
all of it, without exception, is delusion.
Therefore, O king, one's own mind,
free from afflictions,
should be accomplished as the maṇḍala itself.

The syllable ho, at beginning, end and middle,
bestowing the fruit of perfect bliss:
the vajra of body, speech and mind
melts through passion for Wisdom.
Seeing the vajra-bearer too
melt, like the moon,
the goddesses sing a song:
"Blessed One, vajra-bearer,
lord of the Thirty and of humans,
you who bring happiness to all sentient beings,
please protect us,
who long for the object of desire!"

When that song reached the vajra-bearer,
seeing the three realms of existence, without exception,
as being like an illusion,
the vajra-bearer arose once more,
radiating stainless rays of light,
and sent forth the deities with their emblems.
The vajra-bearer is endowed with ornaments,
crowned with the lord of victors,
embraced by Wisdom.
O king, through wisdom and means,
once again the deities of the maṇḍala,
all without exception, are to be brought forth.

Radiating blue light, terrifying in form,
with faces laughing loudly,
fangs bared like half-moons,
roaring aloud, with eyes of fire,
with twenty-six arms,
four faces and two legs,
with limbs of wisdom and means,
fully marked by the weapons:
Vajravega is to bring them in,
from the chariot of the eight-legged one,
terrifying the enemies of the victors,
for the sake of the wheel of wisdom.

Seeing that wisdom maṇḍala before one,
one should make the tenfold offerings,
recite all the mantras, and offer praise.
Having caught it at the navel with the hook,
the very lord of the wheel of wisdom,
radiating stainless light,
is firmly bound with the noose
of the one whose faces are called the lords of the families.
Terrifying it with the weapons
and bringing it wholly under control,
then turning back once more
and circling one's own wheel,
drawing in, binding, delighting,
and making of one taste
are done by the Summoner and the others.

The generation of the yoga of mind itself
is on the sun of the inner lotus,
together with space.
In the east are formations and earth;
in the south, feeling and water;
in the north, perception becomes fire;
in the west, form and the remaining one.
In Agni's quarter, the southeast, they become wind and form;
in Nairṛti's quarter, fire and perception, the two;
in Īśāna's quarter, water and feeling, the two;
in Vāyu's quarter, earth together with formations.
In the spaces between the buddhas and the goddesses,
in the intermediate quarters, is nectar:
eight vases full of flavors,
and again the order is no different.
Above, they become nose and smell;
in the south, one places eye and form;
on the left, they become tongue and taste;
in the west, body and the tangible
arise in the same way.
To the left of the southern door,
below, they become ear and sound,
according to their families.
To the north of the eastern door,
in space, they become mind, the two,
and the element of phenomena.
First, the means, of six kinds,
the special deities of the maṇḍala,
arise according to their families.
Then wisdom and the rest, six,
on their own lotus seats,
become manifest.

At the eastern door is Mahābala,
most fierce, holding a sword;
his wisdom consort is the Paralyzing Woman.
In the south, the Mute One becomes paired with the Proud Woman;
in Kubera's quarter
are the Pride-Maker and the Stupefying Woman.
The Paralyzer, of immeasurable diligence,
dwells in Varuṇa's quarter.
In the middle of the doors they dwell on lotuses;
these deities stand with the left leg extended.

The vajra-bearer's is short a;
the sword-bearer's is short e;
the one with the jewel mudrā becomes ṛ;
Amitābha becomes short ū;
and again, the letter ḹ
is Vairocana's.
By the distinction of the long ones as their substance,
the whole set of vowels
follows the order of the six families.
That of the glorious consort is called the imperishable.
From a is born the element of space;
from ī ṝ ū ḹ, in order,
wind, fire, water and earth:
all of them become the elements.

For the sake of the bliss of the lord of the supreme victors
and of Viśvamātā,
their bodies are joined with one another.
Akṣobhya is the element of emptiness;
the sword-bearer, with lotus hand,
is the substance of the Locanās.
The jewel lady is Māmakī;
further, the lotus-holder
and Pāṇḍaravāsinī are the substance of the body;
likewise, the wheel-bearer and Tārā,
according to their natures and qualities.

With the short and long vowels:
aṃ becomes Samantabhadra,
by way of the body;
Vajrapāṇi's is a;
Ākāśagarbha becomes short e;
and further, likewise,
for Kṣitigarbha it is ar;
oṃ is Lokanātha (Avalokiteśvara);
likewise it becomes a la,

a likewise surely becomes
Dharmadhātu.
Born from a is Śabdavajrā;
born from e is Sparśavajrā;
the family lady of taste
is born from a through the division of the body;

Glorious and excellent Dharmadhātu;
the lady who is chief of the family of sound
with the form of Vajrapāṇi;
Ākāśagarbha with Gandhavajrā;
the supreme lady, chief of the family of taste,

Kṣitigarbha with Rūpavajrā;

Akṣobhya and Vajrapāṇi,

In the inner center of the maṇḍala,
the Teacher, the goddesses and the others, and the vases,
together, are the body of the Victor.
The buddhas and the rest are the Dharma body;
Śabdavajrā and the rest, and the bodhisattvas,
are the enjoyment body;
the wrathful ones are the emanation body.
They arise according to their qualities.
Dwelling in the maṇḍala of mind
and acting for the benefit of all sentient beings,
the vajra of body becomes a pair
together with the body of the Victor,
as the cause of the perfection of the bodies.
The vajra of speech and the Dharma body
also become a pair.
Mind, which teaches the dharmas,
and the enjoyment body
also become a pair,
acting for the benefit of all sentient beings.

Gnosis and the emanation body,
which grants liberation to living creatures,
also become a pair.
The actual limbs of wisdom and method,
the families of the concordant and the discordant,
sun and moon, a, ka and the rest,
pervading the three kinds of existence:
this distinction between gnosis and consciousness
the yogin should understand.

The three vajras, together with svāhā,
at the crown, the throat, the heart,
the navel, the secret place, and the crown of the head:
with these one blesses the body.
The supreme lord of the Victors
is to be bathed by the goddesses.
The vajra, crossed with a vajra,
becomes the diadem.
Sometimes, by the five buddhas,
above the form is Akṣobhya;
on the right, marked with a lotus,
it becomes Vairocana himself.
The jewel; the one holding the lotus of the tongue;
the one who makes the supreme jewel
is the head ornament of Amoghasiddhi.
The six make diadems of their matted locks;
this comes about by virtue of their qualities.
The remainder are the other wheels.

Emptiness and the Dharmadhātu;
by the three families, the pledge,
the gnosis offering, devotion,
and going for refuge to the three:
that which has this as its very nature
the practitioner should worship.
Smoke and the rest, and the vases,
are purified by the eight channels
of the Dharma wheel at the heart.
The consorts and the buddhas
are the innate one of the crown,
renowned as having sixteen spokes.
Of the enjoyment wheel at the throat,
the twice lords of men
are the purity of the bodhisattvas and the rest.
Of the emanation wheel at the navel,
the jewels multiplied by the hooded ones
are the purity of the Terrifying Ones and the rest.
The joints of the hands and feet,
numbering empty, six, and fire,
are the purity of the deities of the action wheel.
The sixteen of the nāgas and the fierce goddesses,
the junctures of the finger joints,
become the purity of the wheel of activity.
The vajra-bearer, as the purity of glorious Time,
becomes the lord of men
through the divisions of year, month, and so forth.
Mind, appearing and not appearing,
by the power of the individual days,
is the purity of the manifold objects.
Smoke and the rest are the purity of the winds;
the lotus of one's own heart
abides in the wheel of channels.
Trampling Rudra together with his wife
is the conquest of the Māra of the afflictions.
With the soles of the feet of the All-Pervading Lord,
trampling Māra is the conquest of the hosts of Māra.
The conch, the gaṇḍī gong, the jewel,
and likewise what is called the tree,
are like the vajra of body and the rest.
The eight vases are the body, the eight nectars;
the Victor's vase of complete victory
shows bodhicitta and rajas.
Formations are Amoghasiddhi;
feeling is Ratnasambhava;
perception is Amitābha;
form is Vairocana.
Semen, marrow, and blood,
urine and excrement, the purified
six elements, wholly pure, are the six goddesses.
The purity of objects and subjects
is the bodhisattvas together with their consorts.
The five wrathful ones are the five powers;
the others are the five faculties.
Carcikā and the rest, the eight, with their retinue,
who are placed on the lotus petals,
are the purity of the watches of the solar day.
Nairṛti and the rest, the twelve months,
who are placed on the lotus petals,
are the measure of the number of breaths in a watch.
The nāgas and the fierce goddesses at the secret place
are twice the lords of men:
they are the purity of the channels.
Likewise Locanā and the rest,
by virtue of their natural qualities,
are the purity of the body's activities.
The hairs of the head are all the siddhas;
the body hairs are the hosts of spirits,
those who dwell upon the earth.
The principles are the weapons of the chief;
by virtue of their natural qualities
they are the seals of the purified elements.
The vajras are the inner seal;
in the heart of the chief
they rest perfectly upon a moon.
The vajra-bearer and glorious Viśvamātā
pervade the three kinds of existence
through union with the gnosis of the three times.

From the glorious purity comes the Dharma body;
from the Dharma body it becomes the enjoyment body;
from the enjoyment body it becomes the emanation body,
and so becomes the lord of the Victors
for the benefit of sentient beings.
There is the fourth state, and sleep,
and again the other two;
through the divisions of the body there is waking.
Thus, through the divisions of the body,
mind abides in four modes

Translator's notes

The text counts with the conventional number words of Kālacakra literature, which are rendered here as numbers: "sun" for twelve (the lotus of twelve cubits, the twelve upper arms, the twelve eyes), "victors" for twenty-four (Vajravega's arms, "victors plus two," and Kālacakra's twenty-four hands), "time" for four (the faces, and the hub and seat of "time and hand," four and two cubits), "Vedas" for four (the doors), and "wealth" (vasu) for eight (the lotus petals). The value of "time" could also be six; four is chosen because Vajravega and Kālacakra are four-faced. "Renowned in glory as the fifth Kalkī" reads ka lnga pa as "the fifth Ka[lkī]"; this is uncertain. In "at its four royal doors," sgom ("meditation") is read as sgo ("door"). In "rgyal ba'i longs spyod 'khor lo" and "rgyal po," the exact sense is unclear. "Thus the yoginī should meditate" follows rnal 'byor ma yis as written, though rnal 'byor pa, "the yogin," may be intended. "Sun" in "upon moon, sun and fire" reads nyid ma as nyi ma. "The mouth and the rest" (kha la sogs pa) might instead refer to syllables or to space. "Set in the center" renders the obscure dbus kyis ru. Number words of Kālacakra usage are rendered as numbers: "eight-three" for twenty-four, "sun" for twelve (right hands, eyes), "Vedas" for four (the faces of Viśvamātā and of Brahmā's head), "wealth" (vasu) for eight (Viśvamātā's arms), "victors plus two" for twenty-six (Vajravega's arms), and "time" for six (the hands of the four buddhas, who have three faces and so nine eyes). "Blue" reads sdo as sngo. "Glorious hammer" reads thob as tho ba. "Four faces and two legs" reads rig byed rgyal la as rig byed zhal la. ནོར་བུ་བཟང་པོ here is clearly Viśvamātā's emblem, "an excellent jewel," not the yakṣa Maṇibhadra. "Mother-of-pearl" renders nya phyis; descriptions of Viśvamātā usually give a skull cup here. "The dweller in water" (chu la spyod) is taken as the conch, which this list of emblems normally includes. The names of the four door guardians are translated from the Tibetan, since their Sanskrit forms could not be identified with confidence; "the Paralyzer" may stand for Stambhaka and "the Supremely Mighty One" for Atibala. The colors given for the wall deities ("going off into the quarters," phyogs bral du song ba) and the line on the emblem "free of a mudrā" are obscure, and the renderings are tentative. This section maps the deities onto the aggregates, elements, senses and vowel-syllables in Kālacakra fashion, and several lines are compressed or obscure; the renderings follow the Tibetan closely. "In the intermediate quarters" reads mtshan gyi phyogs as mtshams kyi phyogs, and "full of flavors" reads ro yis ba'i as ro yis gang ba'i. "Kṣitigarbha" in "rightly, Kṣitigarbha" reads yang dag par nas snying po as yang dag par sa snying po. "Mahābala" renders stobs po che (and stobs); in Kālacakra sources this door guardian may appear as Atibala, possibly the same figure as "the Supremely Mighty One" of the previous passage. "Sumbha" and "Sumbhā" render gnod mdzes and gnod mdzes ma, Tibetan for Sumbha; the identification of the female is tentative. The other door guardians and their consorts (the Mute One, the Paralyzer, the Pride-Maker, the Stupefying, Muting, Proud and Conceit-Making Women, the Diligent One, the Handless One, the Harming Woman) are translated from the Tibetan, since their Sanskrit forms in this text could not be fixed with confidence. Several lines use the Indian convention of number-words. "Lord of men" (nṛpa) stands for sixteen, so "twice the lords of men" gives the thirty-two channels of the throat and of the secret place; "jewels multiplied by the hooded ones" (vasu, eight, times nāga, eight) gives the sixty-four channels of the navel; "empty, six, and fire," read in reverse order, gives three hundred sixty, the joints of the body. "Serpent" renders a Tibetan word for bhujaga, which may likewise be a number-word here. The verse on the head ornaments of the five buddhas is terse and obscure; it is rendered literally. The crown is listed twice among the places blessed; the first probably stands for the forehead. "Fierce goddesses" renders gtum mo where it is paired with the nāgas as a class of maṇḍala deities; where it is called the wheel of the navel it is the inner fire, Caṇḍālī. In "nyon mongs la yi," "la" is read as the evident slip for "pa." "The outcaste woman" renders g.yung mo, which here appears to name a figure associated with recollection; the identification is uncertain. "When one grows weary of the recitation" reads skyo ba for the written skye ba, following the parallel phrase just before the mantras ("if one grows weary of meditating"). In this section the six-branch yoga is mapped onto the deities: withdrawal onto the lord of the Victors, meditative concentration onto Akṣobhya, breath control onto the sword-bearer, retention onto the jewel-seal holder, recollection onto the lotus-holder, and samādhi onto the wheel-bearer. The verse on the bliss that "falls along the path of earth" is obscure and is rendered literally. The garland mantra continues into the next piece. The closing section reads the root precepts and the six perfections in the coded language of the completion stage: "destroying life" is cutting off the life wind, "speaking falsehood" is the sound present in the heart apart from the mouth, "another man's woman" is the drop of bliss that does not fall, and "drinking liquor" is the melting of the moon by the fire of breath control. "Go, ku, and the rest" are the code syllables for the five meats. The line "that too is taught for the yogin" seems to stand for the precept on taking what is not given, but the Tibetan does not name it. "Trickling" renders the unclear "chang nge" in the line on Māmakī, and is tentative. "Kālacakrapāda" in the closing verse plays on the author's name; the colophon calls him "the great Kālapāda."
