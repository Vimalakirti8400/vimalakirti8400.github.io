About this text

The Noble Great Queen of Vidyā-Mantras Called Jayavatī, known in Sanskrit as Āryajayavatīnāmamahāvidyārājā and in Tibetan as Phakma Rikngak kyi Gyalmo Chenmo Gyalwa Chen, is a short scripture centered on a protective incantation personified as the great queen of spells, Jayavatī, "the Victorious One." It opens with the Buddha dwelling on Mount Videha at a time when faithless gods, nāgas, yakṣas, rākṣasas, and many other spirits have grown agitated and are harming monks, nuns, and lay followers devoted to the Three Jewels. Moved by great compassion, the Buddha surveys the four directions, sees lightning, hail, torrential rain, and falling meteors bearing down, and responds by teaching a powerful vidyā-mantra to pacify these threats and protect his followers. It is Toh 1003 in the Kangyur, where it belongs to the class of dhāraṇī texts, the collection of incantations and protective formulas valued for warding off harm and calamity.

Translation

In the language of India: ārya jayavatī nāma mahāvidyārājā
In the language of Tibet: The Noble Great Queen of Vidyā-Mantras Called Jayavatī.

Homage to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas.

Thus did I hear at one time. The Blessed One was dwelling on Mount Videha. At that time the bhūtas became agitated. Gods without faith, nāgas, yakṣas, rākṣasas, asuras, garuḍas, kinnaras, mahoragas, pūtanas, kaṭapūtanas, and kumbhāṇḍas were agitated and enraged, and they harmed those monks, nuns, laymen, and laywomen who had firm faith in the Buddha, the Dharma, and the Saṅgha. At that time a great uproar arose.

Then the Blessed One, wholly seized by the force of great compassion, gazed out in the four directions. He saw lightning, hail, great torrents of rain, and falling meteors coming from the four directions. Having seen this, he gave a sign to Vajradhara.

Then Vajradhara, fierce and wrathful, rose from his seat, emanated a thousand blazing vajras in the eastern direction, and spoke these words: "Hosts of bhūtas, all of you, listen! In the presence of all the buddhas, the blessed ones, I shall make this blessing of truth. For the sake of those fierce bhūtas who now and in the future arise to harm humans and to spoil the crops, I shall utter the bases of the secret mantras of knowledge:

tadyathā gari giri vajra hala hala vajra jvala jvala vajra hana hana vajra daha daha vajra paca paca vajra sarva bhūtānāṃ bhrāmaya bhrāmaya bho bho vajradhara vajreṇa pātaya vātaya śīghraṃ śīghraṃ matha matha pramatha pramatha hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ phaṭ phaṭ jālaya nāgabhavānāṃ bandha bandha tiṣṭha tiṣṭha

I shall bind the eastern direction. I shall consecrate this vajra heart. Whoever transgresses these bases of secret mantra, his head will split, his heart will split, his heart will be crushed.

vajrāya svāhā mahāvajrāya svāhā vajrabhairavāya svāhā apratihahabalaparākramo vajradhara ājñāpayati svāhā"

Then Vajrakrodha rose from his seat, looked toward the southern direction, and in wrath spoke these words to Vajrapāṇi, the Lord of Secrets: "Lord of Secrets, I myself shall destroy all the nāgas of the south, reducing them to ash. I shall set ablaze the abodes of all, as far as the kaṭapūtanas. I shall throw into turmoil the abodes of all the nāgas."

And again he said: "Lord of Secrets, this vajra of mine, the vajra that stupefies the asuras, all from the nāgas down to the kaṭapūtanas, and all down to humans and nonhumans, the vajra that paralyzes, the vajra that bewilders, the vajra that makes tongues hang out, the vajra that crushes heads, the vajra that splits hearts, shall be set in the southern direction, and now and in the future the nāgas who intend harm will be subdued:

tadyathā sara sara visara visara āvarttāya saṃgocala hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ dhona dhona dhona dhuna dhuna dhuna bandha bandha bandha sarvapāśena mohaya jambhaya stambhaya hasa hasa hasa santrāsaya sarvaduṣṭānāṃ vajradhara ājhāpayati svāhā"

Then Vajradūtī spoke these words to Vajrapāṇi: "Lord of Secrets, if any, now or in the future, transgress these bases of secret mantra, I too shall scatter the abodes of all in the west, from the nāgas down to the kaṭapūtanas. I shall crush their hearts:

tadyathā kili kili mili mili traṭa traṭa bhaṭa bhaṭa sloṭaya sphoṭaya jralaya jralaya dūtidayaṃ śoṣaya

Whoever transgresses these bases of secret mantra, ho! this blazing trident will set flames blazing in his heart.

hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ vajradhari vajreṇa bhañja bhañja bandha bandha tiṣṭha tiṣṭha vajradūti rājñāpayati svāhā"

Then the Great Goddess Vajraśṛṅkhalā, together with a retinue of twenty thousand attendants, rose from her seat, looked toward the northern direction, and spoke these words to Vajrapāṇi, the Lord of Secrets: "Lord of Secrets, I too, together with my twenty thousand attendants, shall turn back all those in the north, from the nāgas who intend harm down to the kaṭapūtanas who intend harm. For the sake of those who now and in the future harm sentient beings and spoil the crops, I too shall utter the terrible bases of secret mantra:

tadyathā hili hili mili mili cili cili dhaka dhaka dhūma dhūma dhūna dhūna matha matha hana hana daha daha paca paca naśāya nāśaya ha ha ha ha ha vajradūti mohaya jambhaya stambhaya sarvanāgānāṃ sarva ahiteṣaṇo vajraṇi buddhamaitri dharmasatyena indrasatyena indravālen lokapāla praṣhāvena jambhayāmi sarvanāgānā stambhayāmi sarva ihateṣiṇo varunapāpaśena bandhayāmi sarvanāgānā indracilenāṃ sarva ahiteṣiṇachagalikaromi kara kara kiri kiri kuru kuru vajraśaṃkale svāhā"

Having smeared a maṇḍala with cow dung, scatter flowers, fumigate it with incense of aloeswood and sandalwood, face toward the east, and perform eight thousand recitations. In this way it will be accomplished, and after that one should perform whatever rite one wishes.

With a black thread one dispels epidemic disease. With a twig of karavīra wood one turns back clouds. If one is in water, one should call it to mind. If one is among fierce wild beasts, tie a knot in the hem of one's garment. If the belly is swollen, one soothes it with salt water. For toothache, one soothes it with a thread. Scatter eight hundred jasmine flowers, one flower at a time, at the feet of the Blessed One. In seven days one will see the Tathāgata.

namo ratnatrayāya nama ścaṇḍavajrapāṇaye mahāyakṣasenāpataye apratihatapalaparāgramaye namaḥ sarvavajrakulavidyā

Then the Lord of Secrets, Vajrapāṇi, surrounded by five hundred of the vajra family and leading them, went to where the Blessed One was. He bowed his head at the Blessed One's feet and said this to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, out of compassion for all sentient beings, for the benefit of all sentient beings, and for the benefit and happiness of the kings of men, may the Tathāgata please proclaim this queen of spells called Jayavatī."

Then the Blessed One, out of compassion for the Lord of Secrets Vajrapāṇi, proclaimed this queen of spells Jayavatī: "Vajrapāṇi, this great queen of spells Jayavatī has been spoken and blessed by many hundreds of thousands of millions of billions of buddhas. Now I too, out of compassion, shall speak it for the welfare, benefit and happiness of all sentient beings. You must hold it!

tadyathā jaye jaye mahājaye jayavāhanīye buddha adhiṣṭhite dharma avabhāsane buddha adhigame jñāna avabhāse svāhā

"Vajrapāṇi, these are the mantra bases of the Tathāgata's crown empowerment. Vajrapāṇi, whoever has these mantra bases of the crown empowerment conferred by the Tathāgata on his body or in a book, adversaries will be unable to harm his body within a hundred yojanas. Weapons, poison, venom and sorcery will not overcome him. Into the land of that lord of men, that ruler, enemy armies will not come. That realm will not be overrun by hostile forces.

"Vajrapāṇi, any son of good family who writes out this great queen of spells Jayavatī and carries it, for as long as he lives his body will not be overcome by poison, weapons, water, venom, or even other poisons made from compounds. Fear of enemies will not arise. Harm and abuse will not befall him. Fear of kings and fear of robbers and thieves will not arise. Even if he enters the battlefront, weapons will not strike his body. Everywhere he will proclaim victory. Through the splendor, the power and the blessing of this great queen of spells Jayavatī, he will be victorious, unobstructed, unharmed and unhurt.

tadyathā jaye jaye jaye mahājaye jayottame dharma avabhāsani buddha buddha dhara dhara bodhani bodhani tathāgata adhiṣṭhite avarohaṇi avarohaṇi avarovyani jaya kusume svare svare vati dṛḍhe dṛḍhe dṛḍhe dṛḍha pīśani daṣte mohani mohani jambhani jambhani stambhani stambhani jambhini jambhini mohani sarvā hite ṣinan namo bhagavato buddhasya mahā kāruṇa vihārine siddhyantya me mantra pādāni svāhā"

Then the Lord of Secrets Vajrapāṇi said this to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, through the power of the Buddha and the blessing of the Buddha, I too shall recite the mantra bases of the dhāraṇī of the great queen of spells Jayavatī.

tadyathā jaya jaya jaya paṭa paṭa paṭa jvala jvala jvala vajriṇi prahara prahara dhara dhara dhara hara hara hara bhara bhara bhara bhara tara tara jaya kusume vala vala cakra cakra pīḍaya pīḍaya akrama akrama vikrame acale macale khagacāriṇi nīla bhasani vajra praharaṇi vajra sambhave vajra kili buddha adhiṣṭhite buddha raśmi mahā prabhave ucchaskandhe mahā tejavati alepacale dhuna dhuna dhuna kaṅka kaṅkare kasini samahete dharma avabhāsani cara cara cara ghughute vajra mālādhari sara sara sara kālācāraṇi svāhā

"Blessed One, these mantra bases of the dhāraṇī have been spoken by ninety-nine koṭis of buddhas. All those who act against this great queen of spells Jayavatī are at odds with the buddhas, the blessed ones. That is to say, whether they be gods, nāgas, yakṣas, rākṣasas, pretas, piśācas, kumbhāṇḍas, shadow spirits, speaking spirits, killing spirits, sorcery spirits, vetālas, inauspicious ones, mātṛkā grahas, other grahas of any kind, Skanda grahas, or grahas of Yama, may all of them become dazed, paralyzed and stupefied!

tadyathā vajre vajre vajre hana vajre nihana vajre khāda khāda vajreṇa sphāṭaya sphāṭaya vajreṇa idaṃ grahana hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ phaṭ phaṭ grasa grasa grasa pātaya pātaya pātaya dhame dhadhame dhudhume

"From all fears and harms, from battles, quarrels, fights, combat, disputes and all conflicts, protect me, protect me! O you who make victorious, make me victorious!

tara tara tara mahāvataraṇe kuṭe kuṭe mukuṭe vajra kuṭe svāhā"

Then the Blessed One gave his "Excellent!" to the great yakṣa general Vajrapāṇi: "Excellent, excellent, Vajrapāṇi! You have set out for the welfare, benefit and happiness of all sentient beings and out of compassion, and in speaking this great queen of spells Jayavatī, great being Vajrapāṇi, you too have done well. Vajrapāṇi, wherever this great queen of spells Jayavatī is found, whether in someone's hand, on the body or in a book, or in a village, a town, a market town, a district, a royal palace, a monastery, a dwelling, a walking path or a hermitage, there the great king Vaiśravaṇa, together with his retinue of hosts of attendants, will protect that village, that town and that land.

No hostile armies will come to that land. No famine will come. No enemies will arise. In that region the Great King Vaiśravaṇa, together with his retinue of attendants, will protect the village, the town and the land. No hostile armies will come to that land. No enemies will arise. In that region the Great King Vaiśravaṇa will roam together with many hundreds of thousands of millions of billions of yakṣas. There they will provide protection, shelter, upholding and guardianship. They will bring peace and well-being. They will ward off punishment and ward off weapons. They will neutralize poison, kill poison and destroy poison. They will turn back enemies, seal the boundaries and bind the ground.

tadyathā tapane tapane tapane pratapane pratapane pratapane dhurume dhurume dhurume mahādhurume buddha addhiṣṭhite mukhe mukhe sumukhe svalabrati samādhi samādhigarbhe mānasi phuru phuru dhuru dhuru buddha mānasike svāhā

Vajrapāṇi, these have been empowered at the crown of the head by the Tathāgata. They are a secret abode. They are the great secret of all the tathāgatas. They have been blessed by all the tathāgatas.

Vajrapāṇi, suppose some king of men writes this great queen of vidyā-mantras called Jayavatī carefully in a book and keeps it, or keeps it on his body. Suppose he honors it with incense, garlands, ointments and ornaments of various flowers, holds it as supreme, reveres it and worships it. Then the Great King Vaiśravaṇa, together with many hundreds of thousands of millions of billions of yakṣas, will follow close behind that king of men and protect him from all adversaries. Enemies and hostile armies will find no opportunity against that lord of men, that sovereign. Hostile forces will not enter that land. Even if they do enter, the Great King Vaiśravaṇa will create obstacles for them: he will create hundreds of various obstacles and thousands of various obstacles.

Wherever those kings of men go, this great queen of vidyā-mantras called Jayavatī should be tied to the top of a banner and placed in front of them. Wherever one fights a battle or does combat with a great enemy, if one ties this great queen of vidyā-mantras called Jayavatī at one's neck and enters the battlefront, there the Great King Vaiśravaṇa will strike that hostile army with this harm, and it will be defeated. Whoever wears this great queen of vidyā-mantras called Jayavatī at the neck will be victorious.

Vajrapāṇi, in whatever village, town, market town, district, royal palace or monastery various epidemics and great, terrible famine arise, there one should spread a canopy of various flowers for this great queen of vidyā-mantras Jayavatī. For the torma rite, one should fill four basins with various tasty foods and place them in the four directions. If one then recites this great queen of vidyā-mantras Jayavatī, the Great King Vaiśravaṇa with his retinue of attendants, together with many hundreds of thousands of millions of billions of yakṣas, will completely pacify the epidemic and the famine there.

Then Vajrapāṇi, the Lord of Secrets, said to the Blessed One, "Blessed One, there is no vidyā-mantra more powerful than this, or more splendid, or of greater miraculous power, or of greater might. This great queen of vidyā-mantras Jayavatī is supreme among all vidyā-mantras. In whatever lands it is practiced, in those places it will perform the deeds of a buddha.

tadyathā vajre vajre amitavajre jvalitavajre hutaśani bhañja bhañja bhañja bhañja jambhaya jambhaya stambhaya stambhaya sarva duṣṭa grahānāṃ hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ phaṭ phaṭ grasa grasa grasa paṭa paṭa paṭa taṭa taṭa taṭa muru muru muru dhama dhama dhama jvala jvala jvala khukhure khukhure khukhure khukhume khukhume khukhume ghori ghori ghori vajra mānasi vajra huti phuru phuru phuru grasa grasa grasa sarva ahiteśinī surume mumule vajra pratipati sarva bale phu svāhā alubalani cucure svāhā jaye jaye jaya sambhave svāhā jayottame svāhā jalavarde svāhā jalandhare svāhā jayamani svāhā jaya vajrāya svāhā ajita vajrāya svāhā mahāvajra hutaśanāya svāhā amogha vajra hastāya svāhā"

Then the Great King Vaiśravaṇa went to where the Blessed One was, bowed his head at the Blessed One's feet, and said to the Blessed One, "Blessed One, I too shall grant protection to this Dharma discourse. I shall shelter it, uphold it and guard it. I shall bring peace and well-being, ward off punishment and ward off weapons, and neutralize poison. I shall turn back enemies, prevent every untimely death, and prevent fights, quarrels, disputes, conflicts, battles, all tumults and all fears. I shall seal the boundaries.

Blessed One, these bases of the secret mantras of the dhāraṇī have been spoken and blessed by nine hundred and ninety million buddhas."

Whoever transgresses this great queen of vidyā-mantras, Jayavatī, his head will split into seven pieces, like a siddhaka.

If anyone holds this great queen of vidyā-mantras called Jayavatī, reads it, masters it, worships it, writes it down, or keeps it as a book in a village, a town, a market town, a region, a royal palace, or a monastery, then there, together with my host of attendants and with many hundreds of thousands of koṭis of niyutas of yakṣas, I will follow behind that holder of the vidyā-mantra. "Vajrapāṇi, in later times, in the final five hundred years to come, when the true Dharma is perishing, there will arise yakṣas and rākṣasas who steal vitality, fierce of mind and utterly terrible, and kings who practice what is contrary to the Dharma, short-lived, ruling unlawfully. The Blessed One said: "Vajrapāṇi, then you should remember this Dharma discourse as the Tathāgatagarbha and the Seal. Remember it also as the Great Queen of Vidyā-Mantras, Jayavatī, Who Destroys All the Maṇḍalas of the Māras."

When the Blessed One had spoken thus, the Lord of Secrets, Vajrapāṇi, the Great King Vaiśravaṇa, that entire assembly, and the world with its gods, humans, asuras and gandharvas rejoiced and praised what the Blessed One had said.

Translator's notes

The location ལུས་འཕགས་རི is rendered "Mount Videha" on the basis of ལུས་འཕགས་པོ = Videha; the identification of this mountain is uncertain. In the speech of Vajrakrodha, ལུས་སྤྲུལ་པོ is read as ལུས་སྲུལ་པོ (kaṭapūtana), as in the surrounding lists. The speaker called རྡོ་རྗེ་ཁྲོ་བོ ("Vajra Wrathful One") is given as Vajrakrodha. རྡོ་རྗེ་ཕོ་ཉ་མོ is given as Vajradūtī, consistent with the vocative "vajradūti" in her mantra. རྡོ་རྗེ་ལུ་གུ་རྒྱུད is Vajraśṛṅkhalā, matching "vajraśaṃkale" in her mantra. The spirit classes rendered "speaking spirits" and "killing spirits" are literal translations of Tibetan terms whose Sanskrit originals could not be identified with confidence. "Shadow spirits" likewise renders a term probably reflecting Skt. chāyā. The Tibetan term rendered "Skanda grahas" is the usual Tibetan rendering of Skanda, the child-seizing spirit. In the lists of protections, ཆད་པ is rendered "punishment" (royal penalties) rather than the glossary's doctrinal sense "annihilation," which does not fit this apotropaic context. The yakṣa count བྱེ་བ་ཁྲག་ཁྲིག་འབུམ་ཕྲག་དུ་མ combines several large Indian numerals and is rendered loosely as "many hundreds of thousands of millions of billions." The number of buddhas, "ninety-nine koṭis," is given as nine hundred and ninety million. The speaker of the passage from "Whoever transgresses" to "I will turn them back" is not named in the Tibetan. Its humble verbs and the Buddha's reply praising Vaiśravaṇa show it is Vaiśravaṇa addressing the Buddha, so it is rendered in the first person. "Siddhaka" is kept as written. It is evidently a plant whose blossom or head splits into pieces, a simile that other texts usually make with the arjaka.
