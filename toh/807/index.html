About this text

The Chapter on the Gradual Methods of Practice from the Great Tantra of Excellent Accomplishment, known in Sanskrit as Susiddhikara-mahātantra-sādhanopāyika-paṭala and in Tibetan as Legs par grub par byed pa'i rgyud chen po las sgrub pa'i thabs rim par phye ba, is a tantric scripture devoted to the methods by which practitioners achieve the many accomplishments of mantra. It takes the form of a dialogue in which a great wrathful being, after paying homage to Vajradhara, asks how those who recite mantras can swiftly attain accomplishment, and what the characteristics of a secret mantra are. The text belongs to the early, or kriyā-class, tantric literature and sets out the stages of practice in a graded way. It is Toh 807 in the Kangyur, where it is classed among the tantras.

Translation

In the language of India: Susiddhikara-mahātantra-sādhanopāyika-paṭala. In the Tibetan language: The Chapter on the Gradual Methods of Practice from the Great Tantra of Excellent Accomplishment.

Homage to the glorious Vajrapāṇi. Homage to the Blessed One, Mañjughoṣa, whose body is wisdom.

Then the great and mighty wrathful one bowed his head at the feet of Vajradhara as well, and with palms joined spoke these words:

Blessed One, I have heard of the maṇḍala of the king of awareness-mantras, of its power, of its subjugation of hostile forces, and of this gathering of the retinue. Blessed One, how will those who recite come swiftly to attain the many accomplishments, so variously distinguished through the many secret mantras? What are the characteristics of a secret mantra? What are the characteristics of a master? What kind of person is the practitioner? Likewise, please speak of the mantra attendants. What kind of place is approved? Please tell me which locations are proper. What are the disciplines and restraints for one who recites secret mantras? Which flowers are auspicious, and which fragrances are praised? What is the rite of the offering cake? What are incense and lamps like? What are the varieties of action, whether pacifying, enriching, or wrathful? What are the superior, middling, and lesser accomplishments?

Please teach at length the rite of invitation, the fixed rules of offering, the application of protective actions, and the manner of recitation. Please teach how to satisfy the deities, how to give empowerment, how to prepare the secret mantra, how to maintain it, how to take it up fully, how to make restitution, and how to generate radiance. Please teach the rite of the burnt offering, the fixed requisites of the practice, the full characteristics of the substances, and how the substances are to be gathered. Please teach the rite of purification, the fixed measures, and the rites for accomplishing all these, proclaimed without omission.

Out of compassion for me and for those who recite, the Blessed One has taught all of this. Please teach me chiefly the fixed rules concerning the guarding of accomplishment-substances, how to give instruction, and how to put them into use. Please explain at length how to summon back what has been stolen, how to protect, how to repel, and the omens that foretell obstructing spirits. Please explain at length the empowerment for the activities of accomplishment, the maṇḍalas to be established, and all the other great secrets. Please explain the mudrā and the secret mantra that are required in this rite, the signs of successful accomplishment, and the dreams of those who recite. Out of compassion for all beings, quickly tell me these things.

Then the glorious Vajradhara, praised by all the holders of awareness-mantras, spoke in his great radiance to the greatly diligent, great wrathful one:

Since you have asked me this, great wrathful one, well done, excellent! Therefore listen with one-pointed mind to all of the excellent, supreme rites.

This is called the king of awareness-mantras, greatly diligent, never to be harmed even by the fierce, the Excellent Accomplisher. It encompasses all that belongs to secret mantra. This mantra tantra accomplishes and likewise teaches everything without omission. Those who rely on it with the secret mantras will swiftly succeed. This is the powerful king of awareness-mantras of all the families. The skilled apply it to all actions, such as protection and repelling, offering and satisfying the deities. It teaches, and it urges others on, and it brings about the accomplishment of all secret mantras. Or else its essence, joined with three Hūṃs, is applied by the wise to protection and the other actions, in accordance with the rites taught in the tantras. Having first examined the tantras, the practitioner then attains the accomplishments as well.

The distinctions among the secret mantras of the supreme, middling, and lowest accomplishments should be understood. For the three families are all gathered together in this maṇḍala. Therefore I shall explain all the rites of the mantras taught there.

The Sugata (the Well-Gone One) is to pacify. Those born of the lotus family are to increase. Those born of the vajra family, with their secret mantras, are to perform the actions of wrathful, fierce conduct.

What arises from the upper part of the body is supreme; what arises from the navel downward is the lowest. The accomplishment of mantras arising from the middle of the body is taught to be middling.

Supreme, middling, and lowest as well all come from the power of conceptual construction. The skilled teach the rites of secret mantra as threefold in respect of the accomplishments.

The skilled should perform the actions in three modes even within each of the three families, through the distinctions of supreme and the rest as taught, and of pacifying and the rest. For the supreme accomplishment, the awareness-mantra is taught to be the master. For the very lowest, the skilled should know commissioning and compelling and the like.

Pacifying and increasing, and whatever is of wrathful, fierce conduct, in all three families as well, all of these should be known by the distinctions of secret mantra.

Increasing is done with the king of awareness-mantras. Fierce conduct is practiced with the wrathful ones. Pacifying is done with Locanā and the others. These are the rites of the three natures.

Even what is not explicitly mentioned should be known through the words and syllables of the secret mantras. A mantra that urges on a given action should be understood as accomplishing that action.

By the pacific, pacifying actions are done. By those endowed with power, the actions of increase are likewise performed with secret mantras. By the fierce, fierce conduct is to be practiced.

Above all, as for pacifying action, since the Victor has spoken of it, one should pacify. The actions of increase taught in the glorious Saṃvara tantra are auspicious to perform. And by the wrathful ones of fierce conduct, through teachings such as those, the action is proclaimed as threefold.

Since most secret mantras contain words of urging, the actions of this and that kind are to be accomplished thoroughly by those very mantras themselves.

The king of awareness-mantras, the Blessed One, called the Perfect Accomplisher, is the Great Glorious One, who in particular performs all actions. He is honored by all the holders of awareness-mantras. The glorious Vajradhara teaches all the awareness-mantra tantras and likewise brings about their accomplishment.

His messengers, great and small, the wrathful ones, with their secret mantras and hand-emblems, and his retinue in manifold forms, are renowned for subduing others.

Whatever many secret mantras of this god among gods are taught are of great splendor and especially noble; all of them perform all actions. In the Victor family and the lotus family as well, because he is appointed king here, he should be known as supreme. There is none equal to him.

Secret mantras of few syllables, and those endowed with svāhā and oṃ, quickly bring about all the pacifying actions.

Those with hūṃ and phaṭ, and those with many syllables rā and kan, are the secret mantras that the skilled should apply throughout to fierce conduct.

Mantras other than these, with meaning and words that are peaceful, the skilled should apply well to all actions of increase.

For the action of bringing down spirits and the like, the skilled should act through messengers and the like. Whatever secret mantras perform that action are to be applied to this and that purpose.

In the detailed and extensive teaching of the rites of the secret mantras that perform all actions, the action mentioned first is shown to be the principal one. Whatever secret mantras are taught on the occasion of a given introductory setting, that very action is shown as principal for them. The remainder should be understood in other contexts.

Having considered family, intrinsic nature, powers, and the action as well, one then acts in accord with one's own mind.

Here the preparatory service of the secret mantras is begun. This is the chapter distinguishing, in order, the characteristics of the secret mantras.

Next, from other treatises as well, I shall explain, just as it has been taught, the characteristics of the master, who is held to be the very root of the rite.

He is complete in every limb and endowed with the marks. He is skilled in the glorious temples. He delights single-mindedly in the Dharma, and he abides in his own training. To keep company with such a one brings happiness. His very nature is compassion, and he is loving. He is of noble family and venerable, and free of conceit. He is intelligent, eloquent, and greatly patient. He has entered into the meaning of the Great Vehicle and has a special devotion to the profound meaning. He regards even the smallest transgression with dread and restrains himself well. He is especially diligent in reading the sūtras, the dhāraṇīs and the like. He is diligent in the practice of the secret mantras, and he teaches well the instructions concerning temples. His rites succeed, and he is devoted to recitation. He is skilled in the rites of the maṇḍalas. He is pleasing and teaches well. He aspires to what is great and is free of discouragement. In the great maṇḍalas his own master has properly conferred empowerment upon him, and from his master he has received the scriptural transmission. Such a one is declared to be a master.

He himself, according to the rite, draws the maṇḍalas, the pledges, and so on. Whatever the rites of the secret mantras may be, it is he who is fit to teach them. All the secret mantras taught by him will be accomplished without doubt. Otherwise, even if one performs the preparatory service, all the recitation is without fruit.

Just as one regards the Three Jewels and the sons of the Victor, so, by repeated practice, one should regard the master of great power in the same way, and honor him. He urges all beings on and bestows refuge and the other commitments. He is the cause of all that is virtuous and of every higher rebirth. With the master as support, accomplishments, even exceptional ones, arise swiftly. Right up to the fruit of awakening, he is declared to be the cause. When, through service and attendance, he has at some time been pleased, then the secret mantras and the knowledge-mantras will be accomplished without doubt.

This is the chapter distinguishing, in order, the characteristics of the master.

Next I shall explain further the characteristics of the reciter of secret mantras, the one devoted to the rites of practice, by which accomplishment comes swiftly.

He is pure, disciplined, and steady. He is not discouraged, and he is intelligent and very conscientious. He is eloquent and fearless, and he has spiritual friends. He is patient and honest, without deceit or trickery. He is free of illness, and speaks the truth. He is skilled and adept, and he does not waver. Such an intelligent one, complete in all these limbs, will be auspiciously successful.

Whoever has faith in the Three Jewels and also meditates on the Great Vehicle, and is diligent in the side of virtue, will swiftly accomplish the knowledge-mantras.

Whoever is a bodhisattva, reveres the secret mantras, and holds in high regard service and attendance, and has compassion for all sentient beings, will attain the fruit of the knowledge-mantras. One who delights in solitude and speaks the truth, who is very clean, peaceful, and with an undistracted mind, will attain the accomplishments of the knowledge-mantras.

For whoever has even heard of the great power of the Vajra-holder, and, turning back, delights in him, in that person's hand all accomplishments stand ready, quickly. One who has few tasks and small desires regarding food, seats, and bedding, who is diligent and does not know weariness, will certainly accomplish.

When the rite is read out at length, whoever's mind becomes joyful, or whoever's hair stands on end, is a vessel for the fruit of the knowledge-mantras. Whoever, as each is explained, becomes of the very nature of that, and gives up attachment, attains accomplishments even in dream. In such a one, too, accomplishment is present.

Toward the master who holds the knowledge-mantras and the pledges, regard him as the Teacher himself. Whoever has a reverent mind and wisdom, in that person's hand accomplishment abides. Even if one has begun and no fruit appears, whoever does not abandon the knowledge-mantras...

...whoever is wise, and whose mind does not grow weary, obtains accomplishment. This is the chapter distinguishing, in due order, the characteristics of the practitioner.

Now, furthermore, for the practitioner's ritual undertakings, I shall explain the characteristics of those mantra-assistants who are truly auspicious.

One of noble family, who practices the Dharma and has faith, strong of mind and bold in effort, truthful in speech and greatly reverent, whose limbs are whole and not despised, neither too tall nor too stout, not gaunt and not dwarfish, not too uniform in complexion, free of illness: such a mantra-assistant is truly auspicious.

One who endures all hardships and is steady, who is diligent in all tasks, who is patient and capable of austerity, who is agreeable and speaks pleasantly, who is wise, upright, and free of pride, who has abandoned mutual rivalry, who is learned and loving toward his companions, who is mindful and also bold in giving up, who knows the distinction between secret mantras and knowledge-mantras, who recites the secret mantras and has keen discernment, whom the skilled take as the mantra's assistant for the rites of protection and the like; who is well restrained and knows the pledges, who delights in the teaching and knows the signs of those who perform recitation, and who has such faith: such a one is desired as a companion.

One whose intention is good and who does not grow discouraged, who does not give up until success is achieved, if the reciter relies on a companion of great strength of mind, he will attain accomplishment.

One who is without attachment even to great possessions, who is reverent and delights in being of benefit, such a one is proclaimed a true friend. Even if others tempt him with the substances of accomplishment, he does not give up so much as a drop of water; by this one knows him to be a capable friend.

One who, until accomplishment is reached, works toward it without doubt, and who also acts with loving-kindness, such a one is a capable assistant. One who is not parted from you for a long time, and who, though troubled by various kinds of harm, does not grow weary, such a one alone is the true assistant.

The qualities of the supreme companion of the practitioner should be understood in just this way. In particular, such a person is skilled in the tantras and maṇḍalas. A wise one who surpasses the practitioner in any quality can by himself perform the remaining rites of accomplishment: the empowerment ritual, the fire offering, the ritual killing of Dri and the like, and whatever rites of protection and so on there are. These the wise one should perform.

By him, too, all the cherished practices, outer and inner, should be brought to perfect completion through the rituals taught in the tantras. If any lapse has occurred in the earlier rites of accomplishment, then, keeping in mind the task, he should engage in it so as to repair it.

In other rites involving substances as well, so that no obstacle arises, either he should praise himself, or the reciter, pure in conduct and eagerly pursuing the substances, even at times when he has set aside his recitation, should always strive diligently to recite that mantra.

The companion should not speak during the meditation, and should not undertake any vow of observance. Because that restraint is pure, one should therefore engage in it fully. Whatever food belongs to him, the disciple should eat it after him.

The characteristics of the master and of the practitioner, and the characteristics of the mantra-assistants, whatever has been taught of these: those very qualities are the qualities of the supreme companion of the practitioner. This is the chapter distinguishing, in due order, the characteristics of the mantra-assistant.

Now, furthermore, I shall explain the characteristics of the place: in which regions the practitioner should dwell so that accomplishment is quickly attained.

Where, having subdued the four māras, the Blessed One became fully awakened and attained a wondrous state, the supreme of all the many accomplishments, on the bank of the river Nairañjanā, a place that is pleasing and free of harm: of the accomplishment of that supreme field, the Blessed One himself has spoken.

There, even many māras are unable to bring harm. Therefore, in such a place, the practitioner should count on success and begin all his rites.

Wherever the Dharma was taught, wherever the Sage passed into peace, and wherever, for the sake of ripening beings, his birth was shown: in those places, accomplishment is proclaimed to be of all three kinds. It is stable, and obstacles are swiftly pacified.

Wherever the buddhas and their disciples, those of great fortune, have dwelt, and in whatever place of the meritorious, there too accomplishment is proclaimed.

A mountain endowed with many kinds of springs, adorned with many sorts of trees, abundant in flowers and fruit, is proclaimed for the sake of accomplishment.

In whatever region of the wilderness there are flowers and fruit and abundant water, and where the mind too becomes delighted, in those places accomplishment is taught.

A grassy place where herds of deer roam in auspicious joy, free of venomous snakes, is beneficial for those who recite.

Where living beings are free of harm from such things as epidemics, and where one becomes deeply glad, that place is taught for the sake of accomplishment.

Hillocks, ridges, and mountain peaks, mountain meadows, and slopes of yak pastures with gently flowing streams: a mountain endowed with such distinctive features is taught as especially suited to accomplishment.

Where there are many delightful forest groves, abundant flowers, and trees of aromatic wood, that place is proclaimed for the sake of accomplishment.

A stūpa containing relics, mountains, the shores of lakes, mountains of many kinds, forests of unbroken growth, a charnel ground with smoke rising, waterfalls and pools, a delightful spot on a riverbank, a cow pen, fresh green grass, a tree with a rounded crown all about, a lone tree, a crossroads, a pond, and a place with a spring: these are proclaimed for the sake of accomplishment.

Whatever lands have been frequented by the lord of men, since it is said that accomplishment arises there especially, accomplishment will surely come about there. Whatever places honor the teachings of the buddhas, those lands are taught for the sake of accomplishment; others are said to have lesser merit.

Wherever there are many people who are virtuous and noble, the practitioner who desires accomplishment should dwell in that very place.

Just as the sites of maṇḍalas are proclaimed to be distinguished according to the differences of family and their qualities, so too is it with the sites for accomplishments. Wherever the mind of the practitioner who is to accomplish becomes joyful, there, by the graded stages of purification, he should begin his rites.

This is the chapter distinguishing, in due order, the characteristics of the place.

Now, furthermore, I shall explain the discipline of reciting the secret mantra: by dwelling in accordance with it, those who recite will swiftly attain accomplishment.

The wise practitioner should feel no hostility toward any secret mantras, toward any of the deities, or toward a great-souled reciter. The wise should not invent their own mantras or mantra rites. Nor should one show contempt for people of bad character.

Even if the guru who shows the maṇḍala should behave badly, one should never disparage him, whether by speech or in thought.

The wise should never, in anger, use against the knowledge-mantras the acts of suppressing, binding, cutting, or punishing, nor any such fierce conduct.

One should not take up a secret mantra without the permission of another. A knower of the rites should not give a mantra to one who has not offered service and respect.

One learned in the tantras should not give secret mantras, mudrā, detailed rites, the explanation of the tantras and maṇḍalas, to one who has not entered the maṇḍala. Nor should one give any kind of weapon...

Likewise, one should not eat the likeness of living beings, nor the likeness of the various mudrās, and one should not trample upon them. The practitioner who has understanding should not slight those of lesser standing, should not touch what is unclean, and should not trample anything even with the foot.

One should not quarrel with followers of the Great Vehicle, nor with those who possess wisdom. Having heard of the great and mighty bodhisattvas, one should not turn away in rejection. One should never compete with fellow practitioners of mantra. For small faults one should not, in anger, resort to fierce conduct.

One who abides in the rites of recitation should not sing, dance, or play, and should not wear garlands or bright colors for the sake of adornment. Trampling, leaping, running, whispering, and taking up a bad posture arising from the body, all of these should be entirely abandoned. One who abides in the Dharma of truth should also abandon false speech, harsh words, words that sow discord, and idle talk. One should not dwell together with non-Buddhists, nor engage in disputes with them. One should not speak the recitation of secret mantras to outcastes and those of low birth.

When the wise one is reciting, except for a single attendant of the mantra, one should not speak with anyone at all, there being no need to be together with others.

One should not anoint the body with oil or butter, and one should not eat sesame. Radish, garlic, salt, the various kinds of sour spirits, ground cakes, bean dumplings, likewise lentil broth, and sesame cakes: one who desires accomplishment should avoid all of these. One should avoid all foods of the corrupting spirits, all food offerings of the torma, as well as kṛsarā and milk gruel, while reciting secret mantras.

One should not ride in chariots and the like, nor crush lotuses and the like. One should not touch with the foot the leftover or discarded food. All rites of coquettish display, wearing sandals, carrying parasols, and all bodily activities of that sort should be avoided while reciting secret mantras. One should not rub foot against foot, nor rub hand against hand. One should not cast excrement or urine into water, nor near it.

One should not eat from the cupped hands alone, nor eat from a broken vessel. Nor should one eat upon the back of a leaf. The wise should not eat food to which they are attached. On couches and small beds, the practitioner who has understanding should not sleep together with another who is unclean, nor lying on the back or face down.

One should not eat again and again, nor eat too little or too much. One should not refrain from eating altogether, and one should not eat what one doubts. One should not delight in marvels, nor look upon spectacles of any kind. Toward all women one should not be attached with a clinging mind that is infatuated with their wanton ways.

One who abides in the rites of mantra recitation should never be unrestrained in body, speech, or mind, in eating or likewise in conduct. A learned householder reciting mantras should not wear clothes of altered color. Likewise one should never wear a single garment that is old or dirty. One should not belittle oneself, nor be displeased with oneself, nor become despondent, nor trample others forcibly.

Except when one has been given a reply, one should not on one's own initiative send forth a secret mantra. One should not scorn it with a deluded mind, nor practice the visualization laxly. Unless the mind has strayed elsewhere, and unless it is filled with many thoughts, or with unclean attachment, the reciter should recite the secret mantra.

One should not perform the rites of possession by spirits, nor the rites of protection and of turning back. Whether by one's own mantra or another's, one should not by recitation perform the work of poison. Except for what is needed in practice, one should not use secret mantras for any other purpose. Nor should one apply secret mantras for the sake of rivalry or of provoking others.

The wise should recite at three times of the day, and bathe at the three times. Taking water from a lake or the like, one should bless it with the mantra and wash. Oṃ hana vajri vajrena hā. Recited three times over the ground, this is for anointing the upper body and the like.

Having completed all the actions of the body, the practitioner should bathe. Oṃ vajra-tāra hūṃ. The intelligent one, in every undertaking, should first cast this mantra upon the water, and so clear away obstacles at the outset; only then should the work be begun. Namo vajrāya. Hūṃ hana dhuna mathavi dhānsaya utsāraya phaṭ.

Then the wise one should stir the water with the hand and recite over it, and until the mind has become pure should also speak this secret mantra. Namo ratnatrayāya. Namaś caṇḍavajrapāṇaye mahāyakṣasenāpataye. Namo vajrakrodhāya mahādaṃṣṭrotkaṭabhairavāya. Tadyathā: Oṃ amṛtakuṇḍali, khakha khāhi khāhi, tiṣṭha tiṣṭha, bandha bandha, hana hana, daha daha, garja garja, visphoṭaya visphoṭaya, sarva vighna vināyakaṃ mahāgaṇapati jīvitāntakarāya svāhā.

With the water over which the secret mantra has been recited, one may then bathe as one wishes. While bathing, one should recite this mantra again and again in the mind. Oṃ amṛte hūṃ phaṭ.

When the bathing is finished, one should recite this same mantra over the water once more and pour three handfuls upon the crown of the head. While bathing, the practitioner should keep silent. Then, repeating this secret mantra, one should also bind up the topknot at the crown. Oṃ susiddhikari svāhā.

Then, having also done the purifying contact, one should guard oneself with great care by the secret mantra that accomplishes all actions, and recite one's own vidyā-mantra a hundred times. Oṃ kīli kīlaya vajra hūṃ phaṭ.

Having recited as much as one wishes, the wise one goes to the place of the deity. Until then, one should not be angry, nor become possessed of desire. With a mind devoted to the deity, one should go without distraction. One should take up the vows and guard the pledges with great care. Skilled in the rites of recitation, one should be diligent in recitation, constantly make maṇḍalas, and constantly read the sacred books. The wise should constantly make stūpas, and always do what is wholesome.

The practitioner of discernment should take refuge at the three times of the day, confess misdeeds at the three times, and generate the awakening mind at the three times. One who knows the rites as seen in the tantras should make aspirations at the three times. Whatever conduct the rites prescribe, one should do as far as one is able, according to one's wish.

One should be constantly diligent in giving, free of weariness and endowed with compassion, always patient and endowed with loving-kindness, and always possessed of diligence. One should also constantly meditate, with firm intention, on the six recollections. Having heard the Dharma in its many aspects, one should practice with devotion. One should read the secret mantra rites closely and honor the mantras and mudrās. As set forth in the rites, the one possessed of intelligence should draw the maṇḍala.

With right view made certain and the awakening mind made firm, the wise one should always keep close the fourfold assembly, monks and the rest. Having bound the pledge with the mudrā, one should teach one's own secret mantras and tantras. The wise should fully expand all the scriptural transmissions of secret mantra.

The practitioner should make offerings especially on the eighth, the fourteenth, and the fifteenth days, and in the half-month of miracles. Recitation, fire offering, and worship, and likewise the taking up of vows, and especially merit: toward these one should be reverent and strive earnestly.

By means of this very mantra, praised a hundred or a thousand times, the wise one always holds the vajra of action. Oṃ dhuna vajra ha. Draw down the meteoric iron. With wood of the pāla tree, or neem, or a charred piece of wood from a charnel ground, or sandalwood, or whatever else is suitable, one should make a three-pronged vajra.

That which is called the "perfect accomplisher" is held by the wise one in the left hand, and it is to be taken up for the rites of recitation, fire offering, and the like. By holding this vajra, obstructors and misleading spirits, and all those who cause harm, are struck with terror and become fastened in place.

With that very same secret mantra the wise one should always make offerings. Having anointed it with red sandalwood, one sets it down and recites the mantra before it. This is called the vajra of action, and it performs even wondrous deeds. It is proclaimed to be uniquely precious for practitioners.

When engaging in the practice of recitation, one should always wear on the hand cords perfumed with incense and recited over a hundred times with this vidyā mantra: Oṃ kulandhari bandha bandha hūṃ phaṭ.

This great mudrā-mantra is renowned as Māmakī. It accomplishes all vidyā mantras, and likewise causes blessing and increase to flourish. By it one may perform all rites such as protection. It is the mother of all vidyā mantras and of the vajra-holders.

Pacifying, enriching, and likewise the wrathful practice: in brief, the rites are proclaimed in all secret mantras as of three kinds. The rites by which the attainments of all three kinds come swiftly and are certain to be accomplished are explained here.

Having established the mindfulness of loving-kindness, one should be wholly devoted to the Three Jewels. Becoming peaceful, one should perform peaceful recitation or the fire offering. From the first day of the waxing moon, one should properly begin the rites, and especially in the evening, for that is the time of pacifying rites. Since the gods of the Pure Abodes look upon it everywhere then, the rites will swiftly be accomplished. Therefore one should begin everything then.

Wearing white clothes, seated on a seat of kuśa grass, facing north, pure and peaceful, one should perform the pacifying rite as taught. At the three times of day, one should smear the place with fresh cow dung, and if one scatters white flowers, it is most auspicious. Then barley, milk gruel, and curds, or rice, or sesame, or dūrvā grass or green kuśa grass, or priyaṅgu flowers, or sāli rice-grains or saffron, or white flowers, or kindling of śamī wood, or else fragrant water, or the wood of a tree growing at a shrine are suitable.

Let the fuel be moist sticks, leafy, with milky sap, cut from the tips of the sacrificial branches. Honey or else butter is also suitable. The rite taught in the Kalpa, this and that, one should employ. One who is intent on the pacifying rite should take a fire made from a milk-bearing tree, kept burning continuously, from a person who maintains it. Having made the fire in accordance with the rite, if one offers the burnt oblation for the pacifying action, it will bestow accomplishment abundantly.

On a fire of milk-bearing sacrificial wood, with sticks a span in length, one should mix milk, honey, and butter and make the burnt offering for the pacifying rite. Dung of cattle from the forest, and likewise good wood of the śamī, or else the fuel of fragrant trees, is auspicious for the pacifying rite.

Let the fire pit be dug in white earth, round on all sides, and make it a cubit in measure, twelve finger-widths deep. Above that, set a rim of four finger-widths, perfectly even. At its foot place a wheel, one finger-width in height. Seated in the lotus posture, practice meditation with the mind, make offerings and so on properly, and then perform the pacifying rite.

First make offerings to the Teacher, the Great Sage, to the Perfection of Wisdom, to the hearers free from attachment, and to the bodhisattvas. Make offerings to Maitreyī, to the Tathāgata's Tuft of Hair, to the Invincible One of other knowledge-mantras, to the two nāga kings named Nanda and Upananda, and to the gods of the Pure Abodes. Let these be entrusted with the work, and offer to them constantly in pacifying. Then, having set out flowers, perfumes, and so on properly, perform the rites. Because it pacifies untimely death, sicknesses, epidemic diseases, harms, and likewise obstructing spirits and plague, it is called pacifying.

Place your own mind in equipoise and generate joy in the mind. Perform recitation or burnt offering with offerings of increase, that is, the rite of enriching. Begin on the full moon day and continue until the full moon of the following month. As the rite of enriching is taught, so long should the rite be performed. For the rites of enriching, the early morning is especially auspicious. The gods who accomplish that rite then bestow their blessing. Since all the gods without exception also move about at that time, every action to be done is most auspicious at that very time. If the gods are pleased at the time of the second watch, they grant the supreme gift. Therefore the wise should begin especially at that time.

Wearing a pair of white garments, seated in an auspicious cross-legged posture, facing east and with joy, one should carry out the rite of enriching with concentration. One should smear the ground with fragrant perfume three times a day, and it is taught that it is greatly adorned there with various scattered flowers.

Balls of the finest food, possessing the three sweets, or honey, butter, and curds mixed, or black sesame, or butter and curds, śatapuṣpā, bilva, sāli rice-grains, nāgakesara, lotus, karṇikāra with butter, parched grain and all the grains, and likewise offering fuel for the burnt oblation: with these and similar substances, perform the burnt offering of enriching in the proper manner.

A fire taken from the house of a warrior-class family, or from the house of a king, or a forest fire, or a great fire, is auspicious for the rites of enriching. Sacrificial sticks thick as a handful, at most a cubit in length, mixed with curds, butter, and milk, should be burned in the rites of enriching. The fuel for the fire should be taken from just those woods taught for the burnt-offering sticks. Or else trees bearing flowers and fruits are most auspicious as firewood.

The fire pit, in red earth, should be about two cubits in breadth, square, with a rim, and a cubit deep. For a span's height of the rim, it should also be smeared with red coloring. The lotus seal should be a handspan, and should be four finger-widths in height. As for what is needed in the matter,

Fixing the mind upon that very practice, one should first perform the rites that were taught at the beginning, and then undertake the rites of enriching. One should make offerings to Lotus-Handed Vajrapāṇi (Padmapāṇi) of great power, and also to Amoghasiddhi. One should make offerings to the holders of awareness-mantras and to the yakṣas, to all the goddesses of glory, to Brahmā, to the bodhisattvas, to the great-powered queens of awareness, and to the Plunderers, the great awareness-mantras. For the rites of enriching, such offerings should be made.

The wise one who has made offerings in this way, according to the rite, should himself receive empowerment directly, and then begin the rites of enriching. Whatever increases life and youth, wealth and power and strength, and whatever accomplishes good qualities and the things one desires, that is called enriching.

Then, abiding in the supreme and the wrathful, with firm resolve, one subdues enemies by reciting the fierce secret mantras or by performing the burnt-offering. For this there is no need to consider the lunar days or the lunar mansions, nor to observe a fast. Having made one's wrath firm, one should begin the fierce rites at once. Especially in the dark fortnight, or in the dark seasons, at midday or at midnight, one should begin the fierce rites. For at that time, hungry ghosts, flesh-eaters, spirits, and hosts of rākṣasas, being fierce, bless the rite, and therefore the rites are accomplished. At that time, the fierce rite will succeed, and so it is most auspicious. Or else, whenever one has become wrathful, at that very time one should begin the rite. For the fierce deities of the secret mantras bless the practitioner just when he is wrathful. Therefore, having generated wrath, one should perform the fierce rite.

For the most fierce rites, one should wear red clothing, or blue clothing, or clothing moistened with water or stained with blood, as has been taught. One should glare wide-eyed, frown in wrath, and bare the teeth, clenching them tightly. Pressing one foot upon the other, one should sit facing south. With one's own body, speech, and mind, firmly resolved, one should visualize the secret mantra as if directly present, and then perform the fierce rite.

In that rite, at the three times of day, one should smear the ground with black earth, or with the dung of donkeys, she-donkeys, and the like, or with ashes from a charnel ground. One should spread out fragrant flower petals, or else unscented ones, or spread blue or red flowers, or grain, or husks. Mustard seed, pungent, or one's own blood, or salt, or newly sprouted neem leaves, or earth from a footprint, or ashes, or the dung of bulls and the like, or cut hair, or crow dung, or peacock and other feathers, or fat, or urine, or thorns, or pieces of poison, or powdered bone, likewise the flesh of the great animals and the like, or kapitha cloth, or neem, or a charcoal fragment from the charnel ground, or thorny trees, or sticks of the khadira tree: with these the wise one, according to the rite, should perform the burnt-offering for the sake of the fierce rite.

Calling out the enemy's name, one should prepare all poisonous things and all kinds of salt, mixed together with blood, and perform the fierce burnt-offering. Fire blazing in a charnel ground, or fire taken from the house of an outcaste, or fire drawn from bones or from stones, is auspicious for fierce rites.

The sticks for the burnt-offering of wrathful confession, sharpened to a point at both ends and smeared with poison, blood, and mustard oil, should be made ten finger-widths long. The firewood should be bitter, hot, and foul-smelling, or dry wood; or for fierce rites anything taken from the charnel ground will do.

The fire pit, on black earth, should be triangular, facing away, twenty finger-widths in measure and half a cubit deep, as taught. Its rim should be three finger-widths, and should be smeared with ashes. At its base there should be a vajra eight finger-widths long, and a height of one finger-width should be set. For the materials of the burnt-offering, ashes, charcoal, husks and the like, or salt, newly sprouted shoots and the like, one should fashion an image of the enemies.

Pronouncing the name of that image as one's own, one should cut it and cut it again, and perform the burnt-offering. Or else, at the heart of that image, one should begin the burnt-offering rites from below. Just as in the burnt-offering, so too in the recitation, one should perform it while pressing down upon the image of the fierce one. Or else, it is taught, one should accomplish it according to the ritual procedure set forth in the Kalpa.

Whatever the matter held in mind, one should begin only after pointing it out to the deity. Having performed everything in accord with the rite, one carries out the fierce practice.

Vajrapāṇi the wrathful, and whichever wrathful ones one wishes, together with all the attendants, the Fierce and Radiant Noble One, Vidyā-vajra Aṅkuśā, the wrathful Vajra Thunderer, the Deathless One of Life, Hundred-Eyed Indra, the nāga, Kumbhīra, the rākṣasa, and others of this kind: for works of fierce practice, a wise person should first make offerings to them according to the rite, and having made protection, should begin.

Whatever rite destroys limbs, or drives someone away, or sows division, or sends disease, or brings ruin, or torments, or turns people into enemies, or else any rite of killing: all these are taught as fierce practice. Thus one skilled in such rites should generate wrath for the benefit of those who slander the words of the Sugata, those of evil nature and hatred, those who harm the Three Jewels, the holders of wrong views, the sinful, those of broken discipline, the quarrelsome, and those who deceive their teachers.

With the procedures embodied in the many Kalpas, the wrathful ones, messengers, and envoys, with fierce assemblies one performs fierce practice. For beings in misery one performs pacifying; for those living in comfort, enriching. Whatever works of fierce practice there are, these are to be applied against enemies.

The wise should perform fierce practice by means of the secret mantras of the earth-dwellers, devising the detailed rites, and apply it to those who are hateful and deceitful. One must accept thinking: "I myself must surely experience the many results of karma." In this very life, one should bring to ripeness all the wrongs that person has done. Thus the deities of the secret mantra, out of love for those of evil conduct, deliver them from the lower realms by such methods.

Harm to an enemy must also be fitting to the offense. For a small offense, one should not undertake what would cause death. Even if the heart is satisfied, having acted under the sway of anger, one should, in order to pacify it, perform the pacifying rite with a pacifying mantra. Or else, at that very fire, having fully recited the exhortation of that very mantra, one should make a pacifying burnt-offering with milk. The wise should recall the Sugata's nature of reality and the awakening mind; for the arrogant and the sinful, one should pacify immediately. Pacifying is done at a stūpa with relics; enriching, at a lotus pond; and the practitioner in particular should perform fierce practice at charnel grounds.

Whatever works go upward, all of these should be fully understood. Those that are somewhat less apparent are taught as the middling rites. Whatever works are of the earth below, all of these are taught as the lowest. Now, therefore, let me distinguish the divisions of the works of the highest, middling, and lowest.

From the division of accomplishments into highest, middling, and lowest, the abodes of the sky, the intermediate space, and the earth are taught as threefold. These are: holding the vidyā-mantra and traveling in the sky; the accomplishment of manifold forms; likewise clairvoyance and seeing; a peaceful mind and the attainment of wisdom; mastery of all treatises and a clear intellect; eloquence and retention of what is heard; the accomplishment of raising corpses and of directing attention toward a goal; the accomplishment of yakṣiṇīs and the like; and earthly kingship, the wish-fulfilling jewel, and great treasures. These are the second; and the teachings of these and the like follow.

The accomplishment of the vidyā-mantra is the highest. Invisibility, strength, and diligence, and the accomplishment of the cave, taking the essence; the accomplishment of directing and commissioning the asuras, of flesh-eaters, and of the earth-dwelling bhañjikas; the attainment of knowledge through much learning, and the accomplishment of swift feet: these and the like are taught as belonging to the middling kind.

Delighting all beings, empowering the people of the world, and the rites of fierce and wrathful conduct, such as expulsion and all the like, together with whatever else is minor and ordinary, the wise should know as the lowest.

From a blazing substance, the supreme accomplishment arises. From smoke itself, the middling. From mere warmth, the lowest accomplishment. Thus there are three kinds of accomplishment. The great power of the secret mantras taught by the noble ones is renowned for achieving the supreme accomplishment. The mantras of the gods accomplish the middling. The earth-mantras are said to accomplish the lowest.

By the mantras of the vidyā of the Tathāgata, the great accomplishment is attained. By the secret mantras of the Lotus-Holder, the middling accomplishment is said to be attained. What is spoken by the Vajra-Holder is proclaimed to bring about the lowest accomplishment. The lowest of the supreme is not equal to the supreme of the lowest. The remaining two kinds of accomplishment follow that very same sequence of rites.

Among the three great divisions that teach the qualities of the result, the distinctions of the highest, the middling, and the lowest are fully proclaimed. Whatever secret mantras were spoken by the buddhas and bodhisattvas, the supreme accomplishments that arise from them come to be found even among the lowest.

Among the secret mantras taught in full detail, some accomplish only a single activity. Even for these, that activity is said to be highest, middling, or lowest. Some, without such gradation, give rise to accomplishments, as a lotus is born from mud, and as fire comes from sandalwood.

Among the highest, middling, and lowest, the accomplishment of the body is the highest. What is accomplished through substances is the middling. The accomplishment of enjoyments is the lowest.

If someone recites a mantra of the lowest class and does the full approach practice, then, having taken accomplishment from others, it will come to bestow even the supreme. Even for the highest, if someone does the approach practice without having been consecrated, that mantra gives only the lowest accomplishment. Therefore the approach practice is held most dear.

Among the gods too there are the poor; among hungry ghosts too there are the greatly powerful. In the same way, accomplishments also come in various kinds. Each of the highest, middling, and lowest is again divided into three kinds of accomplishment. Knowing the power of the secret mantras, begin the pursuit of accomplishment through the approach practice.

This is the gradual division of the defining marks that distinguish the accomplishments.

Recite as follows. First, enter the temple and settle in concentration. Sit facing the deity, join the palms and prostrate. Then arrange suitable flowers and clean fragrant water as offering water in due order. Fumigate well with incense, and after reciting seven times over that offering water, invite the deities. Having invited them with the secret mantra, make the offering after that.

The vessels may be of gold, silver, copper, stone, or wood, or of clay, conch, fish-skin, or even a leaf, cleansed. For the activities and accomplishments, whether pacifying, enriching, or wrathful, and whether highest, middling, or lowest, fitting vessels should be matched to each. From the descriptions of color and qualities, and in other ways as is suitable, whatever vessels one wishes should be used according to the desired enjoyment.

The pacifying offering water is accompanied by grain. For enriching, it is accompanied by sesame, since it is opposed to all else. For wrathful conduct, it is accompanied by the kotra plant. For the pacifying offering water, milk is used; for enriching, curd; and for the wrathful, cow urine, or else blood.

For the sake of wrathful and fierce conduct, it is auspicious. For all rites, whether highest, middling, or lowest, rice is auspicious. Incense, flowers, kuśa grass, and sesame are good, and waters of every kind, without distinction, are excellent. For the arghya offering, a copper vessel is supremely auspicious for all rites.

If one cannot gather all of this, then I ask that you be patient with me, and I shall do whatever I am able to gather. Whatever is taught in the mantra tantras, the Vidyārāja, the king of awareness mantras, has explained in due order the rite of invitation.

Or again, whichever mantra tantra it may be, whichever lord one follows, and whichever vidyā queen it may be, if one recites with both of these together, one then praises and invokes accordingly. In the same way, for the three families, one should invite all of them.

For those who are taught in their own tantras, that same procedure is explained. One declares the command of the lord, saying "Depart!" and, having repeated it, proclaimed it well, one sets out. And the moment the invitation is made, the secret mantra, delighted, will come.

In some cases, whatever the rite may be, those who teach the invitation and the return, having performed the rite of supplication, in great delight do just so. With the vidyā queen one performs the rite of invitation of the vidyā mantras; with the vidyārāja, the rite of invitation of the secret mantras; for the others, with both.

One recites once, or three times, or else praises and invokes seven times. Knowing the particular secret mantras, one may even recite twenty-one times. Having recited over the arghya according to the rite, one fumigates well with incense smoke. Respectfully placing both knees on the ground, one offers with hands, together with the implements of the rite, saying with faith and with samaya: "Come here, come here, Blessed One! Accept this arghya of mine, and be pleased with my offering."

For those secret mantras that are fierce and wrathful, and which subdue others, one should perform the rite of invitation with their own mantra alone. And for those which are among the common ones, and those which have become attendants, the rite of invitation is taught with their own mantra.

For the vidyārāja and the secret mantras, in brief, either with each one's own secret mantra, or for all of them with the essence mantra, the invitation is taught to be supreme. In short, the wise one, with the arghya and with the essence mantra of the general family, invites with the words "Come here." This is declared to be a secret.

One bows with deep faith, makes the supplication again and again, and the wise one respectfully invites the secret mantras and the vidyā mantras. Having recited, one joins both hands in equipoise, and if one offers the arghya, placed upon the crown of the head, to the supreme secret mantra, then indeed the invitation is accomplished.

For those of middling rank, one offers from the level of the chest; for the common ones, from the level of the navel, reciting the arghya offering according to the proper manner. In this way one should know the rite of invitation.

By reciting the arghya mantra, with regard to a drawn design or a figure, whichever secret mantra appears standing, one invites it standing; if it appears seated, one invites it seated; if it appears reclining, one invites it reclining. For the sake of the invitation, one should offer the arghya according to the rite.

For the abodes of some secret mantras, in whatever direction they lie, one first looks toward that direction, recites over the arghya, and faces that way. Having looked toward the direction of that rite, one then offers the arghya. This is another rite of invitation, explained in the supreme secret.

Whether with hands joined, with an arghya offering, with flowers, or with the mind, or even with water, it is acceptable. The wise one performs the rite of invitation in front of oneself, wherever it may be.

In this rite of all the secret mantras, what is taught as the rite of invitation, endowed with mantra and mudrā, is the general rule for all. The practitioner, having first completed the preliminary approach and the rites such as offering, and in performing the rites of the action, should perform the invitation with the mind, in accordance with whatever procedure is fitting.

The rite of invitation is taught for the sake of accomplishing every purpose. For minor matters, one may perform it in an ordinary way. Having invited the deity with recitation and pleased him according to the rite, the gods of the secret mantras, too, will do all that is beneficial and the rest. This is the division of the rite of invitation in sequence.

In all actions, the practitioner should first invite the deities, and then, in accordance with the sequential division of the secret mantras, begin the offering, knowing the rite. Say: "O Sugata, Blessed One, come here; please be seated here. Having accepted my offerings, please look upon me with your mind." And "I am endowed with reverence." Having entreated the deity again and again in this way, the practitioner of secret mantra should make offerings with this rite at the three times of day.

According to the scriptures of the earlier mantra tantras, one should first offer fragrances. Then come flowers, as is fitting. Then proclaim the incense aloud. Next, the food offering to the deity, as is fitting, and finally the lamps, properly arranged. The substances are to be purified by the King of the Wrathful Ones, and should be clean, not inferior, suitable, and of good quality. One who has understanding should offer them with reverence. With the individual secret mantra of each deity, one praises each one directly, and then offers the limbs of offering as one wishes. To each one separately, one presents the arghya water, saying, "Please accept this," taking the substance's name and presenting it. If the substances cannot be gathered, one may make images of them and present the arghya.

Through reverence and the offering of arghya, all the rites become complete. Joined palms, arghya, the offering with the mind, the very limbs of the rite, and its basis: all of these should be applied to every offering rite as is appropriate. One who is skilled in every aspect may always do whatever he is able to do. Since the Great Sage declared, "The mind is the forerunner of all phenomena," the mental offering is especially excellent, and one should begin it well. This is the sequential division of offerings.

Having settled the mind in deep equipoise, the remaining rites are said to be performed. First, the practitioner should drive away obstructers by means of the King of the Wrathful Ones. Or else one may use the awareness-mantra of each deity, the one that accomplishes all actions:

Namo ratnatrayāya. Namaścaṇḍavajrapāṇaye mahāyakṣasenāpataye. Namaścaṇḍavajrakrodhāya. Oṃ hulu hulu, tiṣṭha tiṣṭha, bandha bandha, hana hana, amṛte hūṃ phaṭ svāhā. Oṃ traṃ bandha svāhā. Namo mahāśriyai. Oṃ śame samaye saume siddhi siddha sādhaya śive śivaṃkari, āvaha sarvārtha sādhani svāhā.

This is the secret mantra that performs all actions of the three families.

With the wrathful secret mantra, together with its mudrā, one purifies the flowers and the other offerings. First, with water held in the left hand, one sprinkles for purification, doing so with the mantra of the lord, or else with the heart mantra of the family. In short, all secret mantras are to be used for purification together with their mudrās.

To purify oneself, one pours scented water into the palm, looks at it, recites its heart mantra, and sprinkles it on one's own crown. To purify the seat as well, one sprinkles scented water, using either the secret mantra that performs all actions or that of the King of the Wrathful Ones. With water over which the mantra has been recited seven times, one sprinkles in all directions of the ground. Purification and cleansing are accomplished by this secret mantra:

Oṃ kīli kīla, vajrī vajrī bhūr bandha bandha hūṃ phaṭ.

Then the wise practitioner, reciting this King of Awareness-mantras above, should purify the deities with incense, binding and cleansing them.

Oṃ susiddhikara jvalahi anantamūrtaye, jvala jvala, bandha bandha, hana hana hūṃ phaṭ. This is common to all.

Oṃ jvala hūṃ. This is for the Tathāgata family.

Oṃ padma-ni bhagavati mohaya jagan mohani svāhā. This is for the lotus family.

Then the wise practitioner thoroughly recites the heart mantra of the family.

With scented water, all around the enclosing circle, the directions are to be bound everywhere. By the heart of the King of Awareness-mantras, or by one's own King of Awareness-mantras, or by one's own secret mantra, with command and employment, the binding of the directions is to be done with reverence.

Bhrūm. Oṃ amṛtodbhava hūṃ phaṭ. Namo ratnatrayāya. Namaś caṇḍa-vajrapāṇaye. Mahā-yakṣa-senāpataye. Oṃ sumbhani sumbha hūṃ. Gṛhṇa gṛhṇa hūṃ. Gṛhṇāpaya gṛhṇāpaya hūṃ. Ānaya ho bhagavan vidyārāja hūṃ phaṭ svāhā. This is the mantra of the King of Awareness for the three families.

With the very hearts of these same mantras, the fence-posts too are to be properly applied. With the awareness-mantras of the families, the deities are to be protected on every side.

In all rites, everywhere, to dispel obstructors and the like, by the procedure of visualization, the nectar and the ash are to be properly applied. Around the outside of the house, the net, the fence, and the stakes, along with Kīli-kīla and Amṛta, the two wrathful ones, are always to be applied.

Whatever different protective secret mantras are taught in the ritual, those very ones are to be applied here. Or else, whichever one has faith in: the Blessed One's Eye, or the White-Robed Lady, or whichever awareness-mantra of Māmakī, these are to be recited first. By this, oneself and the deities too are protected, all sins are purified, obstructors are thrown down, even crushed, and the fruit of one's intention becomes manifest.

The awareness-mantra of Māmakī alone, even for the family of the Victor and the lotus family, the wise one regards as his own dhāraṇī, and it is to be applied to all. With the secret mantras taught in the ritual, the protective rites may also be undertaken. Or else, by a single hero, everything is to be done according to the ritual.

Thus all secret mantras are to be used as the ritual taught in the maṇḍala prescribes. At the time of preliminary recitation, too, the reciter should understand them from the complete instructions. Whether it be the secret mantra of the family lord, or the heart of the family, or the king of all secret mantras, or the king of the mantra tantras, the secret mantra that accomplishes all rites is, for all three families, properly to be recited by the reciter through these, with distinctions according to the rites of one's own family.

Protection, invitation, purification, binding, repelling, suppressing, fulfilling, pacifying, subduing, and urging, and all rites of that kind, for all three families, and for the accomplishments, are to be performed in every case by the practitioner with these mantras.

One's own secret mantra protects oneself also, through those very hearts. Taking the topknot, the garment, and the cleansing vessel, tie knots and recite three or seven times. Placing the tip of the thumb at the heart, recite for the sake of protecting oneself. Or else ash or white mustard seed with water may be used for one's own protection.

In wrathful rites, protection is to be made with the mantra of the lord. Even in pacifying rites, the practitioner should apply the wrathful secret mantra with firmness. The blessing of the deities will also come about through the lord's mantra. If one fears the lord's mantra, it is taught that protection is to be made by the lord.

The wise practitioner, in both cases, in all rites, should perform the procedures for protecting himself especially well, with purity.

Outside the temple of the deities, when one goes about alone at will, in order to protect the deities fully, one should act in all things as one would for oneself. Outside, in places of attachment and the like, and whenever there is impurity, protection is taught to be made at that time by the secret mantra of Krodha Acala.

When bathing, protection from the enemies, the obstructors, is always to be made. When eating, protect with the lord's mantra. When lying down, protect with the awareness-mantra of the family. For all three families, constantly, all obstructors are on the watch for an opening.

By the awareness-mantra called "the King," protection is taught to be made. This is the division of the stages of protection.

The seat is of blue kuśa grass, four finger-widths in thickness, level, one cubit in width, and sixteen finger-widths in length. At the time of the preliminary approach recitation, all of these are taught to be done. If these are not available, kuśa grass, or else green reed grass, will also do. According to the particular activity, a seat made of wood is also suitable; in that measure, a well-made wooden stool is also most auspicious. Clean leaves of this or that kind, especially for all activities, by the instruction taught, a seat of leaves is likewise explained. Then, of the substances of accomplishment, all the characteristics that have been taught, I shall explain in brief, for the sake of the accomplishments of one who recites mantra.

The wish-fulfilling jewel, the excellent vase, the rain of wealth, the great gold, the wheel, the lapis lazuli, and the sword: these are the seven accomplishments. Mostly they are common, yet the special ones are supreme; the great ones are the best of the best. By them one gains a great heap of merit, and even kingship will be attained.

Furthermore, the supreme accomplishments are the three of the Uṣṇīṣa, the Lotus, and the Vajra, according to the distinctions among the families; these are mostly the ones proclaimed. By any secret mantra whatever, any accomplishment whatever may be gained; yet the higher cognitions and the knowledge of what lies beyond are proclaimed to be chief.

Of pure gold and silver and various precious things, in a circuit of five measures' distance, wealth is said to fall like rain. The great inexhaustible treasure is that filled with gold and the like; though one gives and enjoys and so on, and draws from it, it does not run out.

The wheel of iron from a dead man's tomb should be one handspan across, six-spoked, finely shaped, with a sharp edge; it is quickly accomplished and becomes great. Like the form of the rising sun, the gold is like refined gold, and in color like the karavīra flower; the lapis lazuli is said to be supreme.

The sword should have a small hilt, be tall, with a straight blade and no flaw, four finger-widths in breadth, sharp, like a leaf of the ciṣa; it has the nature of vengeance for the sake of accomplishment.

This teaching on the maṇḍala ritual was spoken by the family of the Tathāgata. By means of it the substances are made ready for accomplishment, and success comes swiftly to the one who recites. Merely by entering there, obstructors are made to vanish, and the deities, in a special way, will bestow their blessing.

This is the detailed ordering of the maṇḍala ritual for accomplishing the substances of the Tathāgata family.

With the five colored powders, as before, draw the entire maṇḍala. Having drawn a lotus in the center, first, in the eastern direction, arrange the one who fulfills wishes. Then the six kings of secret mantra; below them, the White-Robed One; the seven Tārās and the seven heroines; the queen of awareness, the Renowned One. At the door, arrange the single-topknot vidyā mantra and also Hayagrīva. Place at the door the vase that accomplishes all actions. In the corners of the door draw vajras. Outside are Brahmā, the Eye of the Gods, the Great Lord, the Son of the Gods, the Stainless Prince, the one adorned with garlands of light, the nāga king Ananta, Mighty and Beautiful, and Joy; the king of mantra-holders, Great Diligence, and the Conch-Bearer. As before, arrange the guardians of the directions, and here too place the deities of awareness. As in the rite of the earlier maṇḍala, everything here too is said to be done. One who relies on this exposition will be hard for any obstructors to overcome.

This is the detailed ordering of the maṇḍala ritual for accomplishing the substances of the Lotus family.

Having struck the lines for a square, first draw the maṇḍala. Having drawn a vajra in the center, in the east first arrange the king of vidyā mantras, who brings perfect success. Then, on either side of him, draw the two queens of vidyā mantras. Place the jewel vajra and the meteoric iron vajra, and on the left the vajra Khucura. On either side of these arrange Kīlī and Gnodmdzes. Fully arrange the Vajra, the Vajra Staff, the Hammer, and Luguryud; the Jewel Vajra, the Iron Hook, and Māmakī. Then the king, the minister, and the other messengers, Vajramati and the rest, the Great Lord together with his consort, and the lord of wealth together with the yakṣas, all outside the vase that accomplishes all actions. Outside again are the vidyā mantras of the Vajrapāṇi family who wish to be born, spirits, rākṣasas, flesh-eaters, siddhas, and the great-bellied yakṣa. Arrange all the deities who guard the directions in their proper order.

Then perform the rite of offering and all the protective rites and so forth, together with the fire offering, as taught, and carry out whatever actions have been taught. The reciter should perform the invitation of the deities into the maṇḍala and the other actions, either with the heart mantra of the king of awareness or by distinguishing them in detail. All the filling of vases follows the ritual spoken for accomplishment, and the ritual taught for the accomplishment maṇḍala is said to be the same.

All beings of great power and renown do not transgress their pledge. So even if the protective rite has not been performed, one may accomplish in the maṇḍalas. The deities of the vidyā mantras, through their pledge, will bestow an exceptional blessing within the maṇḍala, and special signs will arise. By this ritual, which is to be honored always with devotion, they will be pleased.

The mantras bring about the bestowal of all the accomplishments that the practitioner desires. Here the essence of the family, the mother of the family, the king, and the general protective mantra as well, all bestow accomplishments swiftly. These three maṇḍalas are proclaimed to be great secrets.

This concludes the sequential exposition of the ritual of the maṇḍala for accomplishing substances, of the vajra family of messengers.

Now, furthermore, this maṇḍala, which has become the great secret essence, is to be drawn well, in due order, marked with vajras. In the center, having drawn the emblem of the family, one should place the awareness mantra upon it. Or else one may set down the filled vase, with the auspicious items as explained before. Upon it, place the bowl and the other vessels, and within the joined vessels place the substance as well.

In the east is the Buddha, the Blessed One. In the north is Avalokiteśvara, the Sovereign. In the south is the Blessed Vajradhara. In the west is the wrathful Lord of Wealth. In the manner of the kings of awareness, all are to be set upon their mudrās. To the right is the awareness mantra of the family, and to the left, the one who accomplishes all actions. On the two sides of the western direction, place the white-robed goddess and Śrī.

In the two eastern corners, draw the alms bowl and the monastic robe. In the northern direction, draw the staff and the water vessel of Padmapāṇi. In the southern direction, draw the vajra and the hammer of the Vajra-holder. In the two western corners, draw the three-pointed trident and the vase of wealth. At the gate, place the Invincible Goddess; in the east, place the Snatcher; in the south, the Mountain-Fortress Goddess; and in the north, draw the One with a Single Braid.

The placing of the outer deities is, in short, as one wishes. All the rites of offering the mantras are proclaimed just as before. It is said that when this exceedingly secret maṇḍala is being accomplished there are no obstructing spirits. All the substances and all the rites succeed as one wishes through one's own mantra. Even the mere act of entering and emerging cannot be harmed by obstructors.

Then begin the offering well, with perfumes, flowers, fragrant incense, bright lamps, and food offerings of many kinds. The recitation is as taught in the ritual, and whatever was shown for the maṇḍalas, likewise perform the offering ritual in a hall or in the maṇḍala. For the king of awareness mantras, the god-offering of food, the various essences, and whatever diverse offerings there are, it is taught that they should be done according to the ritual. Offer bright butter lamps, and offer the essences clearly. For the sake of leading beings onward, present the water offering to one's own awareness mantra.

Then, having praised the white mustard seeds with the king of awareness mantras, one should perform the secret protection against all diseases and epidemics. Either the awareness mantra that accomplishes all actions, or any other protective mantra will do. The white mustard seeds, thoroughly recited over, should be placed near the substances. By scattering them everywhere, one will be protected from obstructors.

On the left, one may set one's own mudrā, or else a sword that has been properly praised. Having set guardians of the directions in their directions, one should keep a fast and guard at that very time. A man of great mental strength, who is clean and wears clothing of the appropriate color, endowed with a strong body, vigor, youth, and good looks, holds a sword recited over with the mantra of Śrī. A garland hangs from his neck. He pours the white mustard seeds into the water. He is skilled at seeing obstructors. He is able to hurl the garland and the mustard seeds at the obstructors. Whether the obstructors appear from all sides or there is great danger, he drives them off fearlessly with the sword. If they come near and strike, he scatters mustard seeds and garlands and incense, and the obstructors will be completely pacified. Or else, in his own place...

Let him act with a sword that is firmly held in place. If the places around the circle are abandoned, an opening and a opportunity will be found, and obstructors will enter there. Therefore, generating earnest diligence, let him guard them. This is the work of protection, summarized from the rites for accomplishing those substances.

If the directional guardians, endowed with the qualities spoken of in the tantra, cannot be found, then recite over iron arrows, or over those who bear arrows, and set them toward the directions. A mantra practitioner who recites and practices austerities, who upholds the tantra and has knowledge, is the supreme companion in mantra practice. Let him recite and be stationed at the door. By him obstructors are turned back, and he should be engaged in all the rites.

Whatever seal of one's own is taught in the various tantras, whatever protection cannot be established by any means, having recited and made offerings to those, one should hold them oneself.

Against great and fierce obstructors, either recite those mantras, or else use the praised white mustard seeds together with whatever mantra is desired. Outside the deities' maṇḍala, make a torma offering in whatever way is fitting, either to the serene and excellent ones, or else to all spirits.

Banishing, binding below, and binding above as well, and all around, let him seal the boundary and bind the directions, and make a vajra net and a fence. By the tantras and the teacher's instructions, let him protect the substances, himself, and the deities. By secret mantras, let him turn back all obstructors, whether outer or inner.

All these rites are taught to be performed in the afternoon. When the sun has set, let him begin the accomplishment of the substances. The wise practitioner, having gone outside, touches himself again and again, and drinks a handful of scented water over which the nectar rite has been recited. He drinks a little butter recited over with the heart mantra of the lord of the maṇḍala. All harm, such as fatigue and drowsiness, will be pacified. Resin, honey, and long pepper, recited over with the Buddha-eye mantra, or smeared on with the former mantra, quickly dispel the sleep caused by obstructors.

Restrained, he sits facing east. Having paid homage to the deities and made a mental request to all, let him then engage in the practice.

Having been seized by the three good signs through his recitation, with a mind greatly joyful, let him then engage in the practice. Just as the signs are, so the accomplishments are said to be. Then, through the signs of repetition, one should wish to be shown the accomplishment.

Thinking of the intention of the king of awareness called the One Who Brings to Perfect Accomplishment, he sets the vase at the door, and shows reverence to those in the circle as well. Having entered the deities' abode, he makes obeisance beneath them, and performs the offering of arghya to all, in order to proceed.

Whether with the king of awareness mantras, or the queen, or the general essence mantra, or with each one's own mantra as is fitting, let him perform the rite of invitation. As done in the maṇḍalas, let him display one's own mudrā well. When this is done, the awareness mantras and the great power of the secret mantras confer their blessing.

Some of the substances, once examined, are placed in the hollow of the hand, some are placed in an offering vessel, some on top of the vase, in an anthill or a clay bowl, or else placed on spread leaves. The one to be accomplished is placed on a stand. A vessel smeared with gorocana is set in place. Having placed it at the site of recitation, he protects it directly with white mustard seeds. The wise one who knows the rite makes offerings with tamāla flowers.

Stabilizing, protecting, and empowering, as taught, should be done to the substances in whatever way is suitable. It is proper that they not be left empty. Between the deity and the substances, nothing at all should be placed. On the right and left of the substances...

Place the protective seal and the white mustard seeds. Set out incense, offerings, water for the guests, the torma, curds, lamps, and the rest, and the substance to be accomplished as well. Then establish a place from which the maṇḍala can be seen. Look upon the substances with the eyes, or else scatter them with the hand. With the wavering mind brought to equipoise, let the wise one recite with the breath held within, as in a vase. Here too the rites of blessing and generation are to be performed at the appropriate moments, as before. Throughout the night until dawn, in the same way, recite without interruption. All the rites, the offerings and the rest, are taught to be performed at the three times of day. For those who need to scatter and drink, the assistant is placed for that occasion. When the practitioner is practicing, the assistant should always remain close at hand. Whatever remains of the rite, just as the practitioner would do it, the supreme companion in practice should perform. For turning back the great obstructors, the assistants should act. If they are unable, the practitioner himself should do it by reciting over white mustard seeds and the like.

From the east a great rain pours down, and a fearful thunder resounds. In the direction of fire, suddenly, the quarters light up as though the sky had risen. From the direction of Yama there comes a terrifying form of the Lord of Death, dreadful to behold, brandishing sword and club, uttering a mighty roar. He wears a garland of severed noses and leaves, and a human skull, and drinks human blood. His hair stands on end and blazes, and he rains down streams of urine; he destroys the works of protection. From the direction of Nirṛti come many beings of terrifying and varied forms. From the direction of the water god come the afflictions of the gods, thundering with clouds. From the direction of the wind a great tumult arises. From the north come yakṣas, male and female. From the direction of Īśāna comes a host of forms. From above the mighty gods appear, and below the earth is made to tremble by the earth-dwellers.

For the great accomplishment through substances, such obstructors come at midnight. For the great accomplishments, the obstructors too become great. For ordinary accomplishments, obstructors of every kind that accord with them appear.

The accomplishments of the three times are taught as lesser, middling, and supreme. If it occurs at the proper time it is most auspicious; repetition of it is inauspicious. Warmth, and rising smoke and blazing, are taught as signs of success. Of blazing, three kinds are taught; of smoke, two in sequence; from warmth there is only a single accomplishment, and that is taught to be the lowest.

Some, through the power of the secret mantras, find it swiftly descending. Or if, at the very beginning of the practice, light appears from certain accomplishments, then one should begin to suppress the accomplishment with the mantra of one's own awareness, the awareness-mantra. With the awareness-mantra of the Victor, the essence mantra, or with the syllable bā, if one scatters it by hand, the accomplishment is suppressed and becomes stable. One may also recite over butter, flowers, or other kinds of substances. Thus, if the instructions or the substances are used, qualities are obtained.

If it is accomplished at the first period, first establish it, and then, in the remainder of the night, the wise one may recite as much as he wishes. For middling accomplishments too, the same rite is taught. What is accomplished at midnight is taught to be used at that same time. The beginner uses it at evening; the middling ones at midnight; the best accomplishments are used at the time of dawn. The beginner's is the morning; for the other two there is no fixed rule. What is middling and used at night should not be used in the morning. For the lowest accomplishments there is no prohibition against using them in the morning. Beyond these time-rules, no other fixed procedures are known.

Whoever does not use it in the morning, and has not stabilized it either, discards it like something inferior, and it is called "already used up." For substances urged on by the practitioner, in which the splendor of the secret mantras is fully present, through the fault of passing beyond the proper time, most of them are entirely abandoned.

Those that have been suppressed may later again be used, until accomplishment is attained. As for substances that have been suppressed and set aside, one may use them as one wishes. If the accomplished substances have never been used, then at other times, at the junctures between the proper periods, one should also perform the recitation and then use them. For substances not yet accomplished, this very procedure is proclaimed. Even in each of the seasons, one should accomplish them by rites such as the generation of blessing. Whatever is not accomplished within three years will never be accomplished at all. For the substances of the supreme kind, this rite is fully proclaimed. In six months the middling ones will draw near to accomplishment. For the lesser ones, it is taught that there is no fixed rule about the times. This same rite is also taught for the deities of painted images. Rites such as the full taking up of the substance, and others besides these, may also be performed.

This is the section analyzing in order the rite for accomplishing the substances.

When substances accomplished through beings, whether visible or invisible, have been seized by others before they are accomplished, then this maṇḍala is to be drawn. No lunar dates are needed, nor lunar mansions, and it is said that no fasting is required. Abiding in the supreme wrathful state, one should at once draw this maṇḍala with the ashes of a charnel ground: a triangle, with its door opening to the west. Having drawn it thus, one should lay out around the outside the mantra-holder's mantras of accomplishment.

Then, in the east, is the Blessed One called the One Who Accomplishes Well, the fierce commander of the yakṣas, together with the Great Powerful Lord of Vidyā-mantras, Vajra Krodha, Vajradaṇḍa, Vajra Hūṃkāra, and the Iron-Hook Mother, Vajra Kīli of terrifying form, Sera Kyā, the Thunderous One, Lukgyü Namgyal Nakpo, and likewise at the gate one places the Joy-Maker. Then the Vajra Family, Pungzang, and those renowned as the Great Wrathful Ones. Having arranged these in order, the wise one makes offerings according to the rite of offering: with red flowers and the like, and with a torma together with blood, as is taught.

This rite of fierce action should be done in all the ways taught earlier. The wise one should set his own vidyā-mantra deity facing directly toward the gate, and with fragrant flowers should also make offerings according to the rite. As for the outer maṇḍala, one may do as one pleases, in accord with that. And whichever fierce deity one wishes, one should make offerings and the like to him.

In the middle of the essence maṇḍala, make a fire pit for the activity of fierce action, triangular and with a rim, and begin the burnt offering. With blazing wood from the yam tree, or sengdeng wood, or neem, or wood grown in a charnel ground, and with a paste made of one's own blood, or fresh ashes from the charnel ground, or poison and blood mixed with white mustard, one then offers the burning libation, reciting the name of the victim. It is dedicated for the sake of seizing the substance. One may either stay above the effigy, or cut the effigy into pieces over and over, and at the end also perform the burnt offerings. One who knows the rite of the wrathful ones is taught to perform this rite.

Terrified by the recitation of the secret mantras, the one who seized the substance, even if far away, takes the seized substance, comes before one, and gives it back. If he does so, one grants him freedom from fear, and performs pacifying rites as one wishes. If the pacifying rite is not performed, then he will die at once. Alternatively, for those who are invisible, one also places the substance at the gate of the maṇḍala. Or for those who wish to recite, the substances will be given in abundance.

This is the section analyzing in order the rite for summoning at once the one who seized the substance.

When the substance has been seized and a long time has passed, this maṇḍala is to be drawn. It is common to all three families, and one performs all activities for the sake of accomplishment. In every case, the king of vidyā-mantras called the One Who Accomplishes Well is drawn in the center of the essence maṇḍala, and one draws and sets out the mudrā. Vajra Krodha, Mahākrodha, Māmakī, and the Iron-Hook Mother.

Vajra Hūṃkāra, Vajra Crest, Vajra Staff, and Hammer; the Terrifier, Lug-gyud, and Kīla; Vajra Intelligence, the Invincible Mother; the Wrathful Lord of the secret mantras, the Messenger Woman; the Wondrous Subduer of Others, and Fulfiller of Wishes, Hayagrīva; Vajrapāṇi, Face-Cloth, and Great Multitude; the Blessed Ones of manifold forms. All of these are to be set out in the maṇḍala of the essence.

Then the Renowned Lady and the Great Glorious Lady, the White Lady and the White-Robed One; Tārā, Moon, and the Glorious Lady Gaṅgā; the Wondrous Subduers of Others; the Wheel-Turner and the other Uṣṇīṣa deities; Urṇā, and the Sugata's Eye; Fang, Short Spear, and the Great Maitrī Lady; again the Wondrous Subduer of Others, the Invincible One, and the rest. These are the vidyā-mantras taught for the families, the secret mantras that perform all activities, those renowned as the great mudrās, and the gatekeepers who guard the outer region. Whatever belongs to the rite, the retinue of each family is to be brought in and set out with great diligence.

Outside, the world guardians, Brahmā, the Great Fire God, and the Great Lord (Śiva) together with his consort, with the assembled hosts; the Mothers, the Seven Nāgas, and likewise the lords of the asuras, all who delight in the teaching, and every Wondrous Subduer of Others; and whichever deities were spoken of in the earlier maṇḍalas, and whatever the secret mantra rite prescribes. All these are said to receive the outer offerings.

Then, after that, one kindles the fire as before according to the rite. Making an image of the one who seized the substance from a mixture of fat and clay, one places it upon the disciple, and the burnt-offering rite is to be performed. Placing it also upon the offering ground, one cuts it again and again with weapons, and at the end burns it properly. As before, the rite of offering is performed and the work of the torma is proclaimed. All the wrathful rites of the Vajrapāṇi family are to be practiced; likewise one is to perform the wrathful rite with one's own vidyā-mantra.

If it has not been recovered within twenty-eight days, one uses wrathful action against the enemy for the sake of the substance. A rite performed on the fifth day of the waning moon is supreme. With switches and whips one beats the enemy, and likewise one torments him with fire. Adorned with various words, one pierces him with the sharp thorns of arrows. Having smeared him with mustard and salt, one cuts off his limbs as one wishes. Toward the enemy, with great anger, one punishes with whatever punishment one desires. Wrapping the image in red cloth, one binds it with red thread. One makes offerings with red flowers, and for as long as it remains there, one urges it on for the sake of the substance, and torments it day by day. If, because of this, he gives the substance back, then the punishment is to be set aside.

By these prescribed means for the substance, one performs the burnt offering at midnight. One burns the twigs of kovidāra wood, and burns grains roasted in butter in the fire, together with one's own blood and poison, white mustard seeds with salt, and mustard smeared on them. One performs a hundred and eight burnt offerings. Speaking the name of the one who seized the substance, and his nature, one is said to make the burnt offering especially with salt mixed with blood.

If the substance is not given back, one examines here one's own wicked state, and then performs the burnt offering with all the rites that bring about death. When the substance has been given back, or if some other gift is made, and it causes harm to the practitioner, then the wise one should pacify it. If one has heard that it is lost, or if it has been squandered through carelessness, then to that frightened person the practitioner should quickly give confidence. Then, through the Noble Vajra Vanquisher, or the Great Wrathful One quickly, or the One Without Torment, one swiftly urges the work to completion by the burnt offering. By the wise, through all three families as well, the maṇḍalas are to be made according to the rite, in order to summon back what was seized.

This secret mantra is to be performed as before:

Namo ratnatrayāya. Namaś caṇḍavajrapāṇaye. Mahāyakṣasenāpataye. Oṃ hara hara vajra, matha matha vajra, dhuna dhuna vajra, hana hana vajra, daha daha vajra, paca paca vajra, dhara dhara vajra, dhāraya dhāraya vajra, dāruṇa dāruṇa vajra, dāraya vidāraya vajra, chinda chinda vajra, bhinda bhinda vajra, hūṃ phaṭ.

If, through reciting this, the substance is recovered, or if others are able to obtain it, then, as before, one should guard it at the proper time and put it to use.

Moreover, for the middling accomplishments, and even for the slightest of the lesser ones, the rite is taught to be applied appropriately by one who knows the procedure, in accord with what is fitting for each.

As the share of the Blessed One, first offer one portion of the substance. Then the one endowed with intelligence, having paid homage to the master, offers the second. The third is offered outside the maṇḍala to those who have previously attained accomplishment. The fourth is given to one's companions. The fifth the practitioner uses himself.

Even within these, the shares may be divided into halves, and one half is sent forth by oneself. For substances that are to be examined, these rites have been explained. The share of the Blessed One one should send forth oneself, having first presented the offering water. The rites of worship and the like are taught in order to avoid deceiving the buddhas.

If there is no master present, one takes the master's share oneself. Showing special reverence in this matter is a way of not deceiving him.

Outside the maṇḍala, the practitioner presents the offering water and proclaims three times: "You who have attained accomplishment, accept this share of your own substance."

If there are no mantra-accomplished ones, then by reciting the mantra with their share in mind, and with a mind free of doubt, one should give it to one's companions.

Companions in reciting the secret mantra should be sought out constantly and with reverence for the sake of the aim. Since they have been fellow practitioners through austerities, they have been accomplished from the very beginning. Therefore, because it is fitting to take the substance when they give it, one should take it, saying in agreement, "We have been accomplished from before."

Since it is widely declared that the substances of accomplishment are to be divided in this way, one should oneself divide the substances among them. Some accomplished substances are divided in whatever measure is suitable, for example the eye ointment (añjana) of every kind, and likewise gorocanā and the like. Some, by those who know the rite, are divided only in the mind, as when one sees a sword and the like, and one thinks of benefiting others with it. Some accomplished substances are to be used by one person alone, for example cloth and the like, which one sees and then thinks of how to benefit others by it. Among the varieties of accomplished substances, some are divided equally, for example pills and the like; for these there is no division into halves.

For most substances it is said that one person alone may send out many shares. As for the share, whether wealth or instruction, one should trust what is taught in the tantras. By reciting, "Do these things," or, "This property of mine is yours," one apportions to one's companion in just this way.

If accomplishment is attained, then the wise, following the rite, should themselves bestow benefit as is fitting, in whatever way is suitable. When many kinds of accomplishments have been attained, one should discern their division, especially, with an unclouded mind. At the supreme place of the share-holder, one should offer a petition by recitation. Having known that the command has been fulfilled, the division is taught to follow.

Thus it is taught that one should divide well, as is fitting, for all. And in brief, the same rite is to be followed for all ordinary accomplishments too. Showing reverence, and making offerings of wealth, one benefits by acting according to the command. The master's share of the accomplished substance and the like: this is how the matter of not deceiving is explained.

Having offered one's own offering gift, one should divide the substances well, according to the scriptural instruction. Having paid homage to the Lord of Mantras, and having shown reverence to the Queen of Mantras as well, one should practice with the mind full of devotion toward the deities of the secret mantras.

Once the intelligent practitioner has attained accomplishment, he may travel through the sky wherever he pleases. Or else, why should he not remain together with the accomplished ones? Constantly reciting the mantra of one's own deity within the mind, and constantly looking upon and remembering the substances one has accomplished: then those who seek to harm, the hateful ones, cannot withstand it.

When the wise one has recited the mantra a hundred times, he becomes honored by the accomplished ones. If one recites the mantra of one's own family, the practitioner is freed from every fear.

When one person meets another, the wise one should speak courteously. Upon seeing him, one should first say, "Have you come well? Are you at ease?" One should also show the pledge, the mudrā of one's family, and the accomplished substance. If he asks, "Where have you come from?", one should give an answer to the question.

One should never pass by any place of the gods, nor a lone tree, nor a crossroads, nor a place of austerity, nor a place of the powerful, nor a place of offering, nor a dwelling of brahmins, nor non-Buddhist teachers and other accomplished ones. If one passes them by in pride, one will be disturbed and will quickly fall.

Whether through heedlessness one has broken the pledge, and has fallen or is about to fall, if one recites with the Lord of Mantras himself, then one will rise up again at once.

Even in the formless sky, the one fully accomplished, possessing the divine eye, comes to know his own actions, and the yogins go about just as they please. Therefore, from now on, by magical emanation, he comes and goes along the path.

By the power of merit, because of his dwelling, a house too appears just as he wishes. It is adorned all around with many trees; various birds fly and soar; the ornaments of the goddesses as they wander ring with the sound of tinkling bells; by the sweet sound of their song and dance, the qualities of delight are made clear. It is filled with the three kinds of excellent flowers, and the light of lamps shines brightly. Wherever one imagines it, there too is a great wish-fulfilling tree, and the marks of wonder appear, in which all desires are fulfilled.

There are jeweled slabs of stone for resting, and streams flowing from a ground of jewels. The earth is adorned with soft grass and appears so everywhere. Ornaments and garments, the apparel of play, and the glorious tree bearing fruits such as nectar: all these arise from it. Thus for those who wish for accomplishment, all that they desire is accomplished.

Once accomplished, one should then strive to protect the attainment. One may delight in solitary pleasure groves, or on mountain peaks, or on islands of the ocean, or on islets in great lakes. Born of one's own magical emanation, one acts there for the joy of accomplishment, together with other accomplished ones, or even in the places of those accomplished in former times.

Even if one has practiced in this way, if one does not attain accomplishment, one should keep the substance guarded and set aside, or remain there according to the rite. Having done the preliminary service as taught, and even more intensively, the wise one should recite again and accomplish the substance as before.

Yet if the substance is not accomplished even after a hundred recitations, one should generate steadfast diligence and practice the accomplishment there once more. If one accomplishes the substance up to seven times and it is still not accomplished, then whoever performs this rite will surely attain accomplishment. The practitioner should live on alms food and devote himself to recitation.
