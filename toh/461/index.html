About this text

Toh 461 in the Kangyur is a tantric scripture known in Sanskrit as the Nīlāmbaradhara Vajrapāṇi Yakṣa Mahārudra Vajrānala Jihvātantra and in Tibetan as the Vajra Flame Tongue of Vajrapāṇi, the Blue-Robed, the Fierce Great Yakṣa. It belongs to the class of action or ritual tantras and centers on Vajrapāṇi in his fierce, blue-robed form as a great yakṣa overlord, depicting him as a wrathful emanation of the Buddha presiding over vast assemblies of yakṣas and other fierce beings throughout the cosmos. The text opens with the Blessed One adopting a wrathful posture and surveying the ten directions, framing what follows as a revelation of fierce protective and ritual power associated with this form of Vajrapāṇi. As Toh 461 in the Kangyur, it is preserved among the tantric scriptures of the Tibetan Buddhist canon.

Translation

In the language of India: Nīlāmbaradhara Vajrapāṇi Yakṣa Mahārudra Vajrānala Jihvātantra nāma. In the language of Tibet: The Tantra Called the Vajra Flame Tongue of Vajrapāṇi, the Blue-Robed, the Fierce Great Yakṣa.

Homage to the noble Vajrapāṇi.

Thus did I hear. At one time the Blessed One was dwelling within the bhaga of the sovereign mother who is the source of all things. At that time ten billion fierce yakṣas, together with all the fierce and wrathful classes of beings throughout the great trichiliocosm of world-systems, were gathered in one place.

Then the Blessed One assumed the wrathful cross-legged posture, raised his right hand toward the sky, stretched his left hand down toward the earth, and, turning his gaze, looked in all ten directions. From his mouth there burst forth a great sound: the cry "Hūṃ Ha!" Thereupon all the assembled retinue fainted and remained for a long time insensible, their sovereign lord nowhere to be seen.

At that very instant, from the crown of the Blessed One's head, one called Vajrapāṇi — the image of the hand bearing the vajra, granted by all the buddhas, lord of the three classes of mantra, renowned for his deep blue color — who holds a nine-pointed vajra in his right hand and a fierce king of garuḍas in his left, plunged into the great ocean. Then, moving the wings of that garuḍa, he entered the ocean. The nāga kings Nanda and Upananda bowed their heads before their own lord. Joyful Companion and Conch-Bearer made earrings for him. Kulikā adorned him as a neck ornament. Padma and Mahāpadma became his bracelets. Cloud-Force bound itself at his ear as a topknot clasp. Hulu Hulu became his girdle. Thunderclap and Destroyer of Māra became his anklets and then returned to the left hand of their lord.

Then the ten billion yakṣas and all the wrathful classes, rousing themselves from their swoon, looked in every direction and saw Vajrapāṇi, the image of the hand bearing the vajra, abiding within the great ocean. With wonder they cried out: "Aho! Alala! Our sovereign lord himself is present!" And so saying, they went and approached him.

When they looked, they beheld one who was supremely fierce: his hair standing upright in a mass, his eyes blazing like heaps of firebrands, his fangs bared, his tongue flickering like lightning, his belly coiled like a pond. He stood with the depth of the ocean — eighty thousand leagues — reaching only to his knees. He was adorned with the seven ornaments of the nāgas. In his right hand he held a nine-pointed vajra; in his left, a fierce king of garuḍas. His body, radiant, measured two hundred and sixty thousand leagues.

Then the yakṣas made this petition: "Lord, may you please explain the fierce secret meaning at length to the Blessed One, for we ourselves did not dare to request it." Having said this, they offered Vajrapāṇi a blue upper robe dyed in molten vaiḍūrya, and bound six thousand tiger skins about him as a lower garment.

Then Vajrapāṇi went with wonder before the presence of the Blessed One, touched his head to the Blessed One's feet, and sat down in front of him in the cross-legged posture with full presence of mind. The Blessed One then gazed upon the sky and the earth and looked with his eye sweeping all around through the intermediate space. He then pronounced the mantra: "Khaṃ Thuṃ Saṃ." Thereupon Vajrapāṇi rose from his seat, pressed his vajra-joined palms to his own heart, and addressed the Blessed One in these words:

"O Blessed One, for the benefit of myself and all sentient beings, I request the fierce secret meaning. Does the Blessed One grant permission to ask?" The Blessed One, without saying anything, entered into meditative absorption in the samādhi known as "Certainly Taming the Host of the Wicked."

The very instant he entered that meditative absorption, every malevolent being dwelling in the great thousandfold world system of the three thousand worlds was seized with a trembling of the heart. Then the Blessed One arose from that meditative absorption, gazed upon Vajrapāṇi, and with his left hand drew forth the heartwood of the water-tree and bent it inward. With the water-tree of arising he fully extended it with a sound, and remained thus. Thereupon Vajrapāṇi, recognizing that very mudrā precisely, spoke these words: "You and the rest of the retinue — do not remain in this place; depart to another direction! If you do not go, your hearts and those of your retinue will be split asunder." At these words, the assemblies that obstruct through the apertures of sound departed and took their places as before.

This concludes the first chapter, the setting chapter, from the Tantra of the Vajra Flame: Vajrapāṇi of the Blue Garment.

Then Vajrapāṇi
addressed the body of the Sage in these words:
"I pray you, explain to me at length
the meaning of the fierce secret vajra.

O Lord of Secrets, listen well,
take this to heart, I pray you.
What is my secret, and what is
the fierce vajra —
I have no other defining characteristic besides these.
Set forth your own tantra
and explain the actions of your deeds.

Sentient beings encompassed by the four births,
tormented by malevolent ones —
explain with certainty the defining mark
of the skillful repulsion.

The syllable BA is the body of Vairocana;
the syllable JA is the body of Akṣobhya, the Sage;
the syllable RA is the body of Ratnasambhava —
these three syllables of the Flame:
the first, the principal, is to be explained.

PĀ is the destroyer of vajra;
ṆA is the destroyer of lotus;
I is the destroyer of all movement —
these three subscripts are joined at the end of the name.

E fully comprehends all;
RA is that which ripens;
PA is the gatherer of all;
SA performs the function of universal support;
YA is explained as setting in true motion.
Those endowed with sense organs, abiding above the second aperture-opener, are to be known as the noble ones.

From that, through definitive arising,
whatever appearance is Vairocana's is joined to the actuality.
In all the branch-methods of those
who enter into the actuality of intention,
what was stated above is here expressed.
For the sake of clear meaning, the words repeated
need not be elaborated at length.

For the forms accomplished through action,
the fierce is fully taught;
and since it is explained chiefly for yakṣas
who bear spears and weapons,
the words are definitively and correctly stated.

Through your joyful holding of the scriptural transmission,
that very [teaching] is one that has arisen in accord with your lineage;
all likewise delight in fierce action.
Though they do not see appearance as pure,
that very suchness is to be definitively stated.

Whatever was taught above
applies without doubt to each one individually.
When accomplished by means of those various applications,
the various prosperities that appear
become manifest, expansive, and clearly certain.

The great appearance, free of self,
is thoroughly established through the continuum.
The higher words of conventional truth
are seen as like relying on things.
Like the lower sound of ultimate truth,
all afflictions are burned away and,
by intrinsic nature,
abide in luminosity.

For entering the essence and ultimate truth,
one must rely upon the subsequent conventional.
At the outset, do not abide in the buddha-state,
but strive to engage in the actions of the former person.
Hold no conceit about having obtained empowerment.
From the definitive oral instructions of the guru,
serve the guru oneself
with veneration to the best of one's ability.

The vajra, which is the nature and source of all,
and whatever burns away all that is malevolent —
the higher sound of entering actuality
is expressed as the higher word."

This concludes the second chapter, the chapter of definitive words, from the Tantra of the Vajra Flame: Vajrapāṇi of the Blue Garment.

Then Vajrapāṇi of the Blue Garment
addressed the Blessed Buddha in these words:
"Please speak the maṇḍala of method
for what is to be accomplished."

The King of Awareness (Skt. vidyārāja) declared:

When the ultimate reality becomes manifest in actuality,
the maṇḍala deities are without form.
Because beings abide in conceptual thought,
and in order to dispel their mass of projections,
the practice of the deities shall be explained.

One who performs this with devotion
should establish a maṇḍala of three cubits, with four sides,
in a grove, a charnel ground, an empty house,
a temple, or at a stūpa —
one should begin it decisively in one of these places.

First, having entered the preliminary approach practice,
one performs a festival of sixteen maidens.
For three days one should bathe oneself,
and on the crown, the armpit, and the heart
one should inspect the syllables of the three secrets.
On cloth dyed with the appropriate dye,
apply saffron drops with precision,
then put it on.

As food, the three white substances should be offered,
and one should not encounter any man.
By reciting this very essence mantra
one hundred thousand times by oneself, one draws forth the awareness.

Then, when the approach practice is complete,
one speaks words to the maiden herself.
Lac, the five kinds of wax,
rām dye, and indigo bark — all combined —
are purchased without regard for price,
and wrapped within cloth of a matching kind;
she entrusts to oneself whatever is its dwelling place.
By engaging with her over three days
with the precise mudrā,
and using the practice of the essence mantra as before —
applying perfumed water and completing the prescribed count —
one should recite perfectly.

Then the blue thread
properly wound by the maiden,
purified by mantra and made beautiful,
is to be twisted assuredly into nine strands.
Wrapped within blue flowers,
all five khadira-wood pegs
are used to inscribe the thread throughout.
With a precise and settled measure,
speaking words over the mudrā,
placing it evenly in meditative equipoise —
the maṇḍala has been properly made.

With a height of one cubit,
beginning from the northeast corner first,
take the supporting base in the corresponding color of the appropriate class,
and compress it in its entirety with certainty.
The second is precisely a double measure.
The third has the measure of one.
Made with such proportional measures,
they are to be perfectly and evenly stretched.
Following that same procedure,
with the first thread
draw lines in yellow.
From the determined nine-part maṇḍala,
taking grain, earth, jewels,
and powder as designated,
in the center draw a lotus with sixteen petals
facing downward.
In the east, south, west, and north,
and likewise in the intermediate directions, draw the same.
The lotus of the le-tse variety
is correctly described as variegated.
In the intermediate directions, place in full
the goddess of offering.
At the four gates, the four wrathful goddesses
are to be properly arranged in their practice.
With jeweled ornamental fringe, offerings of desire,
and lattice-work and half-lattice complete,
adorned with four toraṇa archways,
resting on a base of various vajras —
parasols and deer, whatever of these belong,
adorned in full with all ornaments.

The mantra practitioner possessed of an awakening mind,
adorned with a garland of bones
and a garland of serpent heads,
and beautifully encircled on the outside
by a garland of blazing multicolored vajras —
in the four corners draw four vases
perfectly complete with their ornaments.

In the center of that maṇḍala is Vajrapāṇi himself,
dwelling in full according to his earlier described form.
In the east, Sarvavid Vairocana, the Conqueror of the Three Realms,
is to be drawn correctly.
His color is red,
with a form of three faces and six arms.
He stands with four legs, some extended and some drawn in.
The right hand holds a vajra with certainty,
the left is adorned with a battle-axe.
The middle two hands, those that bind,
have the ring finger and thumb knotted with certainty,
while the others are fully extended.
That same essence mantra
is grasped correctly at the heart.
Below, the right hand holds a lasso,
the left hand holds a sword.
This is the first one to be placed.

In the south is the Great Black One,
his color like a smoke-dark cloud,
with a form of one face and two arms.

The male holds sandalwood and great bliss,
the female fully grasps a lasso.
With a wrathful face of unbearable aspect,
he gazes out into all ten directions.
That very protector, whoever he may be,
is placed in the second direction.

For the third placement, in the western direction:
the great and mighty son of hideous body,
his color entirely green,
with eyes that are slightly wide.
One face, two arms,
the crown adorned with a vajra helmet.
The right hand holds a vajra,
the left grasps the hide of a mongoose.
The two feet stand perfectly upright,
gazing toward the east, he abides fully.

For the fourth placement, in the northern direction:
he who abides amid swirling nectar,
his color thoroughly blue,
with a face fierce, scowling, and magnificently wrathful.
Right drawn in, left extended — the stance of power.
The two hands are crossed firmly at the chest,
and he gazes slightly but with certainty to the left.
Facing south, he is to be correctly placed.

In the northeast: the Vajra Yakṣa,
his color a fearsome dark red.
Right extended, left drawn in, he abides firmly,
the right hand raising a sharp curved blade,
the left striking hard with a staff.
He is to be placed gazing toward the chief figure.

In the southeast: the Vajra Zombie (vetāla),
his color a blazing blue-grey,
of unbearably blazing and terrifying form.
He gazes with certainty slightly to the right,
right extended, left drawn in, standing so.
Grasping a vajra iron-chain,
the middle three fingers — ring finger and the rest — are firmly hooked and fully bound,
while the four thumb-and-index fingers are fully extended.
He holds them crossed with certainty at the crown of the head.

In the southwest: the Vajra Ḍākinī,
her color a reddish-white,
of utterly unbearable and terrifying form.
The two feet are planted sole-to-sole,
she is conceived as filling the four corners of the sky.
The two hands are joined palm-to-palm at the crown,
fully holding a blazing tongue of fire.
The head is adorned with five skulls.
She is to be placed shown facing the chief figure.

In the northwest: she who is called Vajra Terrifier,
her color a fearsome blue-black,
three faces, six arms,
the two feet as described above.
The first right and left hands hold fully extended elephant hide.
Below that, the right holds an enemy-axe,
the left holds a true vajra.
The third right hand holds an iron hook,
the left bears a fresh human head.
Standing in the manner of extended and drawn in,
she abides facing toward the chief figure.

At the cut corners of the boundary
are Luminosity-Producer, Mind-Stealer,
Samantabhadrā, and Melody-Bearer.
Their colors matching their respective directions,
exceedingly peaceful, with gentle, graceful mien.
In the right hand: flowers,
and incense, food offerings, and mirror images —
engaged in dance, truly replete with these.
With breasts slightly prominent,
the lower body draped in white silk,
gazing at the chief figure, their forms are beautiful.
They are to be placed in the outer corners.

At the gateways are the gate-guardians:
Life-Cutter, Breath-Drinker,
Blood-Drinker, and Flesh-Eater.
One face, two arms each,
perfectly abiding as tiger, lion, serpent,
and bear respectively.
The right hands hold a hammer, a mongoose hide,
a fire-hook, and a sharp curved blade.
The left hands each hold a lasso,
and all fully grip terrifying implements.
Their colors matching their respective directions,
each utterly scorched and black.
Left extended, right drawn in,
bodies snarling with bared fangs and fierce aspect,
faces magnificently wrathful,
abiding in great bliss through dancing posture.
Gazing slightly to the left,
they also cry out the sound "Hala! Hala!"

Furthermore, all those deities, every one of them,
are adorned with five skulls,
complete with the five ornaments,
and all are wrapped at the lower body
in tiger skin.
Above this, abiding upon their thrones,
the sun and moon circle at the shoulders
of the chief figure himself.
Brahmā, Śakra (Indra), and the Great God (Śiva) —

are trampled underfoot.
The Great God Victorious (Śiva as Vijaya) of the east
abides upon a skeleton throne.
All others likewise abide in that manner.

Whatever seed-syllables are present
should be known together with their sounds.
The first, which generates the chief figure,
is to be perfectly completed from HŪṂ,
and all the others should be understood as arising from that.

HŪṂ MĀṂ BHĀ ĀṂ, YĀṂ BHĀṂ ḌĀṂ BHHĀṂa, PRĀṂ MĀṂ SĀṂ ŚĀṂ, PRĀṂ ŚĀṂ RĀṂ MĀṂ.

The mantras arising from these
are present in full, with light, emerging
from the mouth of the Sugata.
From those, whatever is coarse
must be caused to blaze and radiate without fail.

In the beginning, by means of a single one for all,
from BHĀ arises the variegated —
it is brought into being perfectly.
Beneath that, from KĀ being fully generated,
it is to be contemplated as utterly dry.

Having perfectly completed these, the mantrin,
having fully actualized the body,
I myself accomplish the yoga perfectly.

Then the mantrin endowed with intelligence,
having determined the particular family that applies,
should engage in this method.

The great Lord who pervades, entering into all,
is encompassed everywhere by the chief of the family.
In front is the vajra family,
which without fail destroys asuras and the like.
What abides at the right
belongs to the jewel family,
which without fail destroys flesh-eaters and the like.
What abides behind
fully belongs to the lotus family,
and fulfills whatever is offered — wealth and the like — just as one hopes.
What abides at the left
belongs decidedly to the karma family,
and without fail destroys nāgas, yakṣas, and the like —
all those who are malevolent.

All remaining others besides these
abide without fail in the form of messengers.

When the sun first rises,
the various activities of karma —
each one with its own essence —
are to be recited one hundred and eight times, perfectly complete
from BŪṂ as described above.
Fully endowed with the sound of HŪṂ,
oneself's own mouth blazing brilliantly with fire,
having made the mudrā together with its visualization,
by that very dancing posture in motion,
all malevolent forces are incinerated,
and the Sugata is caused to be supremely delighted.

Taking up the vajra and bell,
by whatever constitutes the wrathful deportment,
the directions are caused to be stupefied.
By assuming the form of a goddess,
the minds of others too are captivated.
Between the half-open eyes,
draw blazing tongues of flame.
Beneath the wheel,
draw without fail the dharmodaya mudrā.
Endowed with skillful means and wisdom,
she is revealed without fail as the Mother who is the origin of all.

Through the proper arising of dependent origination,
ultimate truth depends upon conventional truth,
and conventional truth in turn
genuinely depends upon ultimate truth.

Therefore, since the appearance is not clearly manifest,
the dharmodaya mudrā in particular
is not expansive and the like
but is the wrathful maṇḍala,
and likewise, that which is self-luminous in form —
draw it with mindfulness and attention.

For a person endowed with power,
strength arises with utter certainty.
By accomplishing the two deities of the maṇḍala,
splendor enters into the body.

This is the third chapter, on the maṇḍala and the deity, from the tantra of Vajra Flame.

Vajrapāṇi, wearing a blue garment,
addressed the Protector of the World with these words:
"Please speak — what is the method
for gladdening the deities of the maṇḍala?"

"Having performed the inner self-purification
and the outer ablution and the like,
and having interrupted the stream of luminosity and the like,
in whatever year is suitable,
beginning from the autumn month of Rā,
the asura planetary lord and the first of the fierce flowers —
upon that very occasion I shall undertake the practice without fail.

Then, first, upon my own body,
I adorn it perfectly with the five ornaments.
On the body, garments that are perfectly blue —
an upper robe and a lower wrap, properly bound.

At the five places — the crown and so forth — one should bind them fully with serpents. Then, seating oneself at the eastern gate of the maṇḍala, one should begin the practice.

First, one canopy — a single one — should be erected above without fail. At each of the four corners, four banners of blue should be suspended all around. From the centers of those canopies, flower garlands threaded on cords should be suspended all around.

Then, abiding in the manner as before, at the places taught above, I shall without fail undertake this activity of mine. Having completed all the ritual activities of the torma (Skt. bali), the five kinds of offering, and the acacia stake of eight finger-widths, one should perfectly praise them with this mantra:

Oṃ vajrakīlaya sarva-vighnān hana hana hūṃ phaṭ. Oṃ vajrapañcajñāna śodhaya sarva cakṣukāraya hūṃ.

Having recited those mantras twenty-one times over the stake and the five-colored cords, one should properly saturate them fully with the five fragrances — sandalwood and the rest.

Then the mantra-holder endowed with intelligence should begin the wheel of self-protection. At the boundary just outside himself, he should meditate on the cool, unmoving activity. Above that, whatever is the substance that gathers — conceive it as perfectly black in color. From that, the all-pervading one of the fifth family should be properly contemplated. Then, from that completeness, imagining oneself suffused with swirling nectar, and with the vajra-pointing mudrā, one should expel the obstructors by reciting the mantra. At that very apex of the clan-lord, one should contemplate the body-protector arising from the syllable Kaṃ, and imagine it pervading everything by residing at the crown.

Then, with respect to that very torma — the incense offering and whatever accompanies it — in order that karmic obstacles may not arise for beings who have accepted rebirth and been gathered in, one should perfectly accomplish one's intention and make it of value.

Then oneself, with faith, should present the five kinds of offering in one's mind through mantra recitation. From the preceding posture, the right knee being drawn up without fail and the left extended well, holding the vajra itself in the right hand and raising it, then touching its tip to the skull-cup held in the left hand, one places them at the crown; then the skull-cup in the left hand is moved to the right side of the incense, and the right-hand vajra is grasped by the left hand and pressed to the heart. By turning the skull-cup with certainty, one places it definitively at the four directions and corners. From the mouth the sound of Hūṃ blazes forth, and by the dance-method of the multicolored vajra one should circle three times completely.

Then whatever is harmful, having gone to the four directions, the region of ground will surely be purified. Then, by drawing lines and the like, having settled well in preparation and performing the empowerment in due order, one should then determine the dream-omens without fail. Whatever good and bad omens descend, and whatever is to be repelled or not repelled — the one possessed of wisdom should investigate this clearly.

In order to construct the maṇḍala of the buddhas, I first, for the fierce ground, should consecrate five powders with mantras and mudrās. Then the pure vessel skull-cups should be sealed with the performed vajra-mudrā. One should without fail begin the karma yoga practice, and it is to be circumambulated in its direction. Whatever is the substance through which breath flows — with the letter appearing clearly, it should be written in an unbroken stream.

Knowing the right moment at the appropriate instant, one should display a fully wrathful expression. The first is written in black, the second should certainly be opened in white, the third itself is yellow, the fourth possesses full red, the fifth is to be written in green, and the sixth is adorned with various colors.

With the maṇḍala activities completed and fully provided with all material substances, at the four corners — beginning from the northeast corner — earth taken by a maiden, having been fashioned into ritual vases, four blue ones should be placed. At the top of those surrounding them, tying the thread mentioned above, with garlands of blue flowers —

adorned all around. Seventeen blue vases are to be placed without fail in the direction of the outermost boundary, filled well with medicine, incense, essences, rock salt, and tree-resin and the like, and kept there. Adorned completely with all ornaments, they are placed so as to face toward the body.

Then the wise mantra-practitioner, having precisely applied the counted recitations, is to set out properly: round food offerings without remainder, incense and lamps, anointing fragrances, sacred thread, garlands, swords, and clubs. Parasols, victory banners, and pennants are to be properly placed in the directions without fail.

Then the master himself, having written with saffron dots on blue silk cloth, is to present that very thing as an offering. The surroundings are to be screened off with blue curtains so that those outside cannot see. If it should become visible, everything apart from one's own body will be ruined. Translator's notes

The opening scene describing the Blessed One as dwelling "within the bhaga of the sovereign mother who is the source of all things" is a standard tantric literary convention. The "sovereign mother who is the source of all things" renders Tibetan thams cad 'byung ba'i yum btsun mo, which in tantric context denotes the primordial feminine principle, often identified with Prajñāpāramitā or a specific tantric consort deity. The phrase is left close to the Tibetan without over-specifying the identity.

The nāga kings named in this passage are well-known from canonical lists: Nanda, Upananda, and others. Some of the names in the passage (Joyful Companion, Conch-Bearer, Cloud-Force, Hulu Hulu, Thunderclap, Destroyer of Māra, Kulikā, Padma, Mahāpadma) are rendered by meaning or transliteration where Sanskrit equivalents are established by convention; the Tibetan forms have been followed where the Sanskrit is not fully recoverable from the available text alone.

The measurement "eighty thousand leagues reaching only to his knees" and "two hundred and sixty thousand leagues" for his body height are taken directly from the Tibetan and reflect the hyperbolic cosmological scale conventional in tantric literature. The syllable-analysis section (BA, JA, RA, PĀ, ṆA, I, E, RA, PA, SA, YA) is a seed-syllable exegesis of a mantra, almost certainly the mantra VAJRA PĀṆI ERA PASA YA or a related dhāraṇī string. The Tibetan gives phonetic equivalents and semantic glosses for each syllable in turn; the translation renders the glosses faithfully, but the precise mantra string being parsed is not identified in the passage itself and likely requires consultation of a commentarial tradition specific to this tantra.

The phrase rendered "the second aperture-opener" reflects the Tibetan "go-'byed gnyis-pa'i steng gnas-pa'i sna-ldan," a tantric technical term for a class of beings or a positional descriptor in the subtle-body system. Its precise referent here is genuinely uncertain without a commentary; the translation preserves the literal sense.

The repeated distinction between "higher" (gong-ma) and "lower" (og-ma) in the context of conventional and ultimate truth reflects a specific tantric hermeneutical structure in this text where the conventional is called "higher" and the ultimate "lower," which is the inverse of the usual Madhyamaka ordering. This inversion is preserved faithfully as it appears doctrinally intentional. The "three white substances" are milk, curd, and butter, a standard ritual purity offering in this context.

The term translated as "the Conqueror of the Three Realms" renders a figure whose Tibetan name literally means "the Victorious One of the Three Realms," likely corresponding to the Sanskrit Trailokyavijaya, a wrathful form commonly placed in the eastern direction of tantric maṇḍalas. The passage appears to conflate or identify this figure with a red form having three faces and six arms, which may differ from some canonical descriptions; this is a genuine doctrinal and iconographic point of uncertainty that a specialist editor should review.

The phrase rendered "le-tse lotus" is a type-name for a particular lotus variety whose precise botanical or ritual referent is uncertain; it is retained phonetically.

The term "toraṇa" for the archway ornaments is kept in Sanskrit as it is a standard architectural term for gateway ornaments in maṇḍala iconography, and no single English word captures it adequately.
