About this text

Toh 429 in the Kangyur is a tantric text known in Sanskrit as the Śicatuḥpīṭhākhyāta Tantra Rāja Mantrāṃśa Nāma and in Tibetan as the Dpal gdan bzhi pa'i bshad pa'i rgyud kyi rgyal po sngags kyi cha, rendered in English as "The Glorious Tantra King of Exposition of the Four Seats, Called 'The Mantra Section.'" It belongs to the class of Buddhist tantric literature, specifically presenting itself as a mantra section — the third part of the first tantra — associated with the tradition of the Four Seats, a cluster of sacred sites and related esoteric teachings connected with the Yoginī or Highest Yoga Tantra corpus. The text opens with homage to the Blessed One as the Glorious Yoga of Space and to the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara, along with the vajra ḍākinīs, before proceeding in verse to expound on the secret and supremely condensed suchness of mantra practice. As Toh 429 in the Kangyur, it is preserved among the canonical tantric scriptures of the Tibetan Buddhist tradition.

Translator's notes

The Sanskrit title transliterated in the Tibetan opening corresponds to a Caturpīṭha tantra text, specifically a section called the "Mantra Portion" of the Tantra King of the Four Seats exposition. The Sanskrit reconstruction is approximate, as the Tibetan phonetic transcription is irregular.

The term "neuter" (ma ning) throughout this passage is a technical tantric category referring to a class of being considered neither male nor female, used here in the context of seed-syllable generation and mantra practice. It should not be taken in a purely grammatical sense.

The term "bindu" (Tibetan bandu ka, here rendered as "bindu") is left in Sanskrit as it is a technical term from tantric physiology and mantra practice referring to the luminous drop or point of concentrated essence.

The passage as a whole is highly technical tantric instruction concerning seed-syllables, their colors, their placement, and their correspondences with the four activities of pacifying, enriching, magnetizing, and wrathful action. Much of the syntax is intentionally condensed and esoteric, and any complete interpretive rendering would require access to an authorized commentary. The translation follows the literal structure as closely as possible. The passage belongs to a tantric ritual-technical text describing the arrangement and application of seed-syllables, particularly the syllable Hūṃ and related mantra components, for the five types of ritual action: peaceful, enriching, magnetizing, wrathful or fierce, and destructive. Several terms are highly technical and their translation draws on standard Vajrayāna ritual categories rather than on unambiguous grammatical parsing alone.
