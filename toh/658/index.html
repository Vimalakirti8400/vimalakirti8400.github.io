About this text

Toh 658 in the Kangyur is a Mahāyāna sūtra known in Sanskrit as the Ārya-Mahāmegha-vāta-maṇḍala-sarva-nāga-hṛdaya, in Tibetan as the Phags pa sprin chen po rlung gi dkyil 'khor gyi le'u klu thams cad kyi snying po, and in English as the Noble Great Cloud, the Chapter of the Maṇḍala of Wind, the Heart of All Nāgas. It belongs to the broader family of Great Cloud (Mahāmegha) sūtras, texts concerned with rain, nāgas, and the ritual invocation of serpent deities for the benefit of the world. The sūtra is set in a great ocean palace where the Buddha addresses an assembly of a thousand powerful nāga kings, each endowed with great miraculous power and presiding over vast maṇḍalas, situating the teaching firmly within the Mahāyāna literature devoted to nāga lore, weather, and the protection of beings through the intercession of these powerful serpentine forces.
