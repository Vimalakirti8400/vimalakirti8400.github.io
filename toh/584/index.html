About this text

Toh 584 in the Kangyur is a collection of dhāraṇī texts — short, potent ritual formulas — each associated with a major canonical scripture, promising the practitioner that by holding or reciting the formula they gain the full merit and blessings of engaging with the root text itself. In Sanskrit the genre is known as dhāraṇī, and in Tibetan as gzungs. The collection includes formulas linked to celebrated works such as the Perfection of Wisdom in One Hundred Thousand Verses and the Avataṃsaka Sūtra, among others, making it a kind of condensed gateway to several foundational Mahāyāna scriptures. Sitting within the dhāraṇī section of the Kangyur, the text belongs to the broad class of canonical dhāraṇī literature that was gathered and preserved alongside the sūtras and tantras of the Tibetan Buddhist canon.

Translation

Prajñe śruti smṛti vijaye, dhīḥ dhāraṇīye svāhā.

By holding this, one will hold the Perfection of Wisdom in One Hundred Thousand Verses. The dhāraṇī for holding the Perfection of Wisdom in One Hundred Thousand Verses is complete.

Homage to the Three Jewels.

Oṃ nathāṃ namaḥ sarva buddhānāṃ. Oṃ māṃ. Bhrūṃ mūṃ. Tadyathā. Oṃ brūṃ.

By holding this, one will hold the noble Avataṃsaka. Inquiring into it and reading it are likewise equal to that. The dhāraṇī for holding the noble Avataṃsaka is complete.

Translator's notes

The passage consists of two short dhāraṇī texts in sequence. The first is a dhāraṇī said to confer the merit or retention equivalent to holding the Prajñāpāramitā in One Hundred Thousand Verses (the Śatasāhasrikā Prajñāpāramitā). The second similarly stands in for the Buddhāvataṃsaka Sūtra. Both are formulaic colophon-style entries from a dhāraṇī compendium. The Sanskrit syllables are rendered as they appear transliterated in the Tibetan; some syllables such as nathāṃ are not standard Sanskrit words but tantric seed syllables, and no normalization has been imposed.
