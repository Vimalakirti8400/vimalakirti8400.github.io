About this text

The Ritual Manual of Noble Acala, King of the Wrathful Ones, Taught for the Taming of the Valiant through the Immeasurable Power of All the Tathāgatas, known in Sanskrit as the Āryācalamahākrodharājasya Sarvatathāgatasya Balāparimitavīravinayasvākhyāto Nāma Kalpa and in Tibetan as De bzhin gshegs pa thams cad kyi khro bo'i rgyal po 'phags pa mi g.yo ba de'i stobs dpag tu med pa rtul phod pa 'dul bar gsungs pa, is Toh 495 in the Kangyur, where it belongs to the Tantra section. It is a ritual manual (kalpa) devoted to Acala, the fierce, unmoving wrathful deity who protects practitioners and removes obstacles. Set as a discourse of the Blessed One in a jeweled celestial realm, it teaches Acala's mantras together with the rites built around them, including painted images, recitation, fire offerings, and practices for subjugation, protection, wealth, and the attainment of powers such as the state of a vidyādhara. Its colophons record a translation made under the patronage of the Tangut court and later revisions checked against Indian manuscripts.

In the language of Tibet: The Ritual Manual Called "The Teaching on the Taming of the Valiant, the Immeasurable Power of Noble Acala, King of the Wrathful Ones of All the Tathāgatas."

Homage to the venerable noble Avalokiteśvara.

Thus did I hear at one time. The Blessed One was dwelling upon the wind. The place was arrayed with various jewels and was arranged and encircled by many countless koṭis of jeweled celestial palaces. Bodhisattva lion thrones were set out there, born from the merit of inconceivable, countless tathāgatas and adorned with jewels. It was encircled by walls of various jewels that illuminated countless world systems, and it was utterly enchanting. Above it was a jeweled canopy adorned with various jewels, illuminating and opening up the realm of space. There was an inconceivable king of lotuses, arisen from the substance of the seven precious things, which brought delight to the various wishes of all sentient beings, made great and supreme light rays shine forth, and cleared away all darkness. It was encircled by many koṭis of inconceivable lion thrones born from the primordial wisdom of all the bodhisattvas, encircled by countless bodhisattvas, and encircled by many gods, nāgas, yakṣas, gandharvas, asuras, garuḍas, kinnaras, mahoragas and others. There the Blessed One dwelt and taught the Dharma.

He was encircled by great bodhisattvas, namely the noble Avalokiteśvara, Mañjuśrī, Samantabhadra, Maitreya, Sarvanīvaraṇaviṣkambhin, Śāntamati, Various Array, Bearer of Sky Flowers, Ākāśagarbha, Utterly Pure, Vajrapāṇi and others. He was encircled by many koṭis of śrāvakas, namely Śāriputra, Maudgalyāyana, Aśvajit, Kāśyapa and others. He was encircled by Śakra, Brahmā, Viṣṇu, Kubera and others, of great renown and great fame.

The Blessed One, with a mind endowed with great compassion, was teaching the Dharma. That is to say, for those bodhisattvas who were hearing the Dharma discourses on entering the way of secret mantra and so on, for those śrāvakas, for those assemblies complete in every respect, and for the various wishes of the inconceivable realm of sentient beings, he engaged in conduct suited to their various needs and performed miraculous displays of magical power. He honored and fully pleased inconceivable buddhas. He also beheld many miraculous displays of the buddhas' magical power. He also brought forth many emanations of the buddha. He also produced great arrays. In short, whatever he saw would serve the welfare of sentient beings, he did all of it.

Then the Blessed One, the Tathāgata, the Arhat, the perfectly complete Buddha Śākyamuni, looking upon the great assemblies, addressed the bodhisattva mahāsattva Vajrapāṇi: "Vajrapāṇi, I have brought forth the maṇḍalas of the wheel of speech in its many varieties, arranging many words and letters, which delight various sentient beings, are distinguished by various mudrās and mantras, express the distinctions of the various classes of beings, fully pacify various sufferings, possess many powers and are enchanting. They are marked by the characteristics of single syllables, of eight thousand, of countless numbers and of three koṭis, of suffixes, of long and short, of vowels and of consonants. They are divided and determined as the examples of the bodhisattvas, such as the ten dharma realms of the tathāgatas, and as the śrāvakas, the pratyekabuddhas, the five mundane superknowledges, the divisions such as the abodes of the gods, the human realm, the pretas, and down to the realm of animals. These I have taught with the superior intention of benefiting sentient beings, for the sake of the beings of seventy-seven countless koṭis. They will also spread in this world system, Adorned with the Essence of the Flower Ground.

"Lord of Secrets, among all those seventy-seven countless koṭis, I have taught the doors of the rites of various methods to the great bodhisattvas, the noble Avalokiteśvara, Vajrapāṇi, Mañjuśrī and the other bodhisattvas. And to those Lords of Secrets I have also taught extensively the mudrās, the mantras and the ritual procedures of the male servants, the female servants, the male messengers, the female messengers and the others.

"I have also taught the means of accomplishment that possess various activities:

Fully endowed with various sādhanas,
the rites of painted scrolls are explained;
or else the rites of the activities are distinguished,
where the secret activities of great power,
the fire offerings, I have taught;
likewise the rest, the other lands and places;
then all the hearths, and
likewise pacifying, increasing,
subjugating and the fierce rites;
then the particular times,
and the maṇḍalas likewise are taught.
The mudrās too, all of them,
the seed syllables and the emblems,
the secret mantras themselves,
the emblems of the families, and likewise the bodies,
and the shapes of all of them as well:
activities such as these,
all of them I have taught.

The ritual manual called Acala
I shall now explain once again.
Whatever the mantrin of pure being,
endowed with intelligence, is to accomplish,
from the inconceivable variety
a sādhana of secret mantra
I have condensed in this ritual manual,
and I shall rightly explain its rite.
Serving the perfectly complete buddhas,
through accomplishment one attains that state;
as a supreme child of the tathāgatas
one will be born into what is called the Dharma.
All the buddha fields without exception
will through it be completely purified.
Always, continuously, free of conceptual thought,
abiding upon the path of the sky,
relying on a body that has form,
he comprehends the distinctions among beings.
For beings hard to pacify and tame,
for great obstructors and those who lead astray,
then, in order to tame them,
the perfect Buddha always emanates
the one proclaimed by the name Acala,
the wrathful one, the noble, the mighty,
the glorious tamer of those hard to tame,
the child of the children of the buddhas.
Here the supreme victors
and likewise the children of the victors always
bow down to him; Brahmā and the others
and all embodied beings worship him.
He repays the kindness of the buddhas,
and likewise of the children of the buddhas;
all of his aspirations are fulfilled,
his inconceivable primordial wisdom complete.
Because he has attained the ground called Unmoving,
Acala, the Unmoving, is also his name;
because he is not moved by any of the māras,
his permanent primordial wisdom is stainless and excellent.
The king of ritual manuals, of great magical power,
I shall explain: child, listen here.
Blessed by all the buddhas,
wholly devoted to the dharma realm,
he always grants swiftly the accomplishments
of long life and supreme freedom from illness;
he always dispels the obstructors
and likewise utterly destroys the māras.
For the happiness of sentient beings
this ritual manual is fully explained."

Then those bodhisattva mahāsattvas and those assemblies complete in every respect made this request with a single voice: "For the sake of all sentient beings, and out of love for us, Blessed One, please explain it. Sugata, please explain it."

When this request was made, the Blessed One, the Tathāgata, the Arhat, the perfectly complete Buddha Śākyamuni, knowing the request that had been made, illuminated the world system Adorned with the Essence of the Flower Ground with great light rays. A light ray called Arisen from the Śrīvatsa emerged, surrounded by many countless hundreds of thousands of koṭis of niyutas of light rays. Beginning from this world system Adorned with the Essence of the Flower Ground, it pervaded the world systems as numerous as the atoms of inexpressible, utterly inexpressible buddha fields, as far as the very limit of the realm of space, and circled them, turning to the right. Likewise it covered the north, the west, the south, the east, and the world systems of the ten directions. All the bodhisattvas dwelling in those world systems spoke this verse:

Where the protector of the Śākyas, the perfect Buddha,
dwells in great splendor,
let all the children of the Victor
born from the dharma realm go.
There, listen to the stainless true Dharma,
the virtuous way of the conduct of secret mantra,
by attaining which the protector of the world
found unconditioned awakening.

Then that light ray, having heard this verse, turned back and entered into the śrīvatsa of the Blessed One himself. Then those great bodhisattvas, through the power of the wrathful one, came from their own world systems to where the Blessed One Śākyamuni was.

Then the Blessed One Śākyamuni, knowing that the minds of the assemblies complete in every respect were one-pointed, once again entered into equipoise in the samādhi called Universal Arising of Flames that Burn the Vajra-Arrogant. As soon as the Blessed One had entered into equipoise, this great trichiliocosm world system moved in six ways. It moved, it moved strongly, it moved strongly all around. It shook, it shook strongly, it shook strongly all around. It rocked, it rocked strongly, it rocked strongly all around. It quaked, it quaked strongly, it quaked strongly all around. It roared, it roared strongly, it roared strongly all around. It resounded, it resounded strongly, it resounded strongly all around. An immeasurable, brilliant light appeared in the world. Inconceivable miracles were also seen.

This entering into equipoise was for the benefit and happiness of all sentient beings, through this heart of the king of great wrathful ones, the king of secret mantra: it fulfills the intentions of all the tathāgatas; it utterly destroys all the māras; it fulfills the wishes of all sentient beings; it liberates the realms of sentient beings in countless dharma realms; from it has arisen the fire of the nonconceptual primordial wisdom of the samādhi whose nature is the emptiness of all the tathāgatas; it reveals the essence of awakening of all the bodhisattvas; it accomplishes the maṇḍala of the wheel through various speech; it is the father of the bodhisattvas; it is the light for all sentient beings fallen into the darkness of the afflictions; it is the guide for all who have fallen into the lower realms and the bad destinies; it is the happiness of those tormented by suffering; in short, whatever suffering sentient beings have, it brings that suffering to complete peace.

namaḥ sarvva śapari pūrakebhyaḥ sarvva tathāgatebhyaḥ sarvvathā hāṃ

As soon as the Blessed One had spoken this heart of the wrathful one, it made all the abodes of the māras in this world system Adorned with the Essence of the Flower Ground, and those classes of māras, and all sentient beings dazed. It produced a light that brought every happiness to suffering sentient beings, and having fulfilled the wishes of all sentient beings, the ground of all the lords of existence without exception was shaken. Moreover, inconceivable wonders arose in the world.

Then the Blessed One Śākyamuni spoke thus to the Lord of Secrets, Vajrapāṇi: "Vajrapāṇi, this is the king of wrathful ones, the suchness of the supreme essence of the tathāgatas.

From this emptiness others have arisen;
it is the very nature of the dharma realm.
The victors are seen to arise from this;
here all the victors abide.
The realms of existence are emanated from this.
All the buddhas of the past,
and likewise the others who have not yet come,
all of them truly arose from this.
Stainless like the sky,
completely free of all elaboration,
utterly beyond the domain of speech,
it is always free of elaboration,
abiding like the sky.
Taking conventional expression first,
the secret mantras are then recited.
Conventional expression too is conceptual;
in reality there is nothing to express.
Relying on conventional expression,
the Protector taught reality itself.
Sentient beings do not exist; like an illusion
they are to be contemplated with the mind.
Just as an illusion is in some respect
also without intrinsic nature,
so conventional expressions are to be understood
as having emptiness as their nature.
Thus this protector, the secret mantra,
is beautiful in equality, without intrinsic nature;
it bestows the things that are desired
and brings them all to fulfillment."

Then all the assemblies, complete in every respect, of the bodhisattva mahāsattva Samantabhadra and the others, and Brahmā, Indra and the others, were utterly amazed and marveled, and gave up their pride. All of them looked toward the Blessed One Śākyamuni and spoke these words with a single voice: "Blessed One, you have turned this wheel of Dharma that we have never heard before. Blessed One, it is wonderful! Sugata, it is wonderful! Blessed One, we have come to possess supreme happiness. Blessed One, this is inconceivable. Blessed One, this is not the domain of any of the śrāvakas or the pratyekabuddhas. Blessed One, it cannot be overcome by the evil māras or by any who take the form of obstructors. Blessed One, this is the wish-fulfilling jewel of the ultimate for all sentient beings. Blessed One, this fulfills the aims one desires. Blessed One, this is the protector of the teaching of the tathāgatas and of sentient beings. Blessed One, this cannot be transgressed by any rākṣasas, kumbhāṇḍas and the like."

When they had made this request, the Blessed One, the Tathāgata, the Arhat, the perfectly complete Buddha Śākyamuni, gave his approval, saying "Excellent!" to those great bodhisattvas and to that assembly of gods and others: "The treatise is excellent, excellent! Again, the treatise is truly excellent! Just as the words of this treatise are spoken beautifully, so it is, exactly as it is. Yet I taught this text of the heart of the great king of wrathful ones, which protects the teaching of the treatise, looking upon suffering sentient beings veiled by the darkness of delusion.

This, which those who cannot hear it are unable to receive,
was taught by the perfect Buddha who attained the state.
For the sake of their doubts I have
truly lit the lamp of primordial wisdom.
Childish beings, wholly filled with suffering,
dwell here upon the face of the earth;
because they do not know this way,
they are bound by the tight bonds of the household."

Then the Lord of Secrets, Vajrapāṇi, gazing at the Blessed One with unblinking eyes, said this: "Blessed One, it is wonderful! Sugata, it is wonderful! Blessed One, this has great power. Blessed One, I cannot bear the splendor and power of the heart of that king of wrathful ones. Blessed One, this heart of the king of wrathful ones tames those who are hard to tame."

The Blessed One Śākyamuni declared: "Lord of Secrets, it is just as you have said. Moreover, Lord of Secrets, through the power of this king of secret mantra I have conquered all the afflictions and all the māras there are. Lord of Secrets, the maṇḍalas of the wheel of speech by which I delight sentient beings in the world systems with their various distinct desires, which I have taught to the distinct wishes of the various world systems, I taught through the blessing of this. Lord of Secrets, that the great bodhisattvas, having gained all mastery, have found the valiant power of primordial wisdom, this too comes from the blessing of the heart.

Striving single-mindedly for the aims of beings,
they dwell upon the face of the earth;
wishing to benefit sentient beings,
they also send forth emanations.
Always making offerings and giving delight,
they truly dwell in the fields of the victors.
For as long as the directions endure,
all the buddhas, who are always and continuously worshipped,
dwell equally in this
that is called awakening.
Phenomena that lack intrinsic nature
are what arise and cease.
Relying on conventional expression,
they are taught to be like an illusion.
Just as an illusion is emptiness,
so is the defining characteristic of phenomena.

Nevertheless, Lord of Secrets, for as many eons as countless hundreds of thousands of koṭis of niyutas of countless eons, I took on the bodies of various sentient beings and underwent suffering. I gave away my head, hands, feet, eyes, the best of my limbs, my wives, sons, daughters, elephants, oxen, chariots and so forth, and other external things as well. Having brought the six perfections to completion, in the manner of the various inconceivable world systems, I taught sounds, words, and letters to beings of various dispositions and various desires, and with the guise of signs of various modes of conduct and with distinctive forms, I worked for the benefit of sentient beings. And the defeat of the four māras at the seat of awakening: all of that was done through the power of this king of secret mantras. This is what makes the buddhas flourish, what brings victory even over the māras, what makes all the teachings fruitful, and what, furthermore, brings victory over all the various obstructors without exception.

There is no second other than this,
the unchanging mantra of the buddhas.
Therefore, having placed it in the heart,
one dwells at ease in whatever way one wishes.
There are no hostile obstructors,
nor any others who seek an opening.
There is never any fear of enemies,
nor any fear of thieves and the like;
one is wholly freed from every fear.
By meditating on it as abiding in the heart,
so that I might at long last be delivered
from the fearsome ocean of saṃsāra,
how shall I attain the supreme state
praised by the perfect buddhas?
While I had attained nothing at all,
by practicing in this way
that unchanging state is to be attained here,
and the fullness of buddhahood itself is attained.
One should rely on the means of accomplishment of secret mantra
taught by the perfect buddhas.
Then a body with the light of a wish-fulfilling jewel
will be attained right here.
For the accomplishment of those
who abide in this way,
when the mantra practitioner rests in meditative equipoise,
by that even what was not found is attained.
By this even the unsplittable is split;
even over the unconquered, all without exception is conquered.
Recitation of the greatly powerful secret mantra
bestows the state of perfect buddhahood.
Wherever the mantra knowledge of mindfulness,
understanding, and thought has gone,
just like a blazing fire,
the mantra practitioner will be clearly seen.
When one relies on the king of secret mantras,
there is nothing at all that comes of it without fruit.
Recollecting the greatly powerful secret mantra
brings about the desired actions.
This lord of all mantras without exception
will swiftly bring accomplishment.
When one desires the fruit of secret mantra,
one should have no doubt about this.

Then those bodhisattvas and that entire assembly, not abandoning their sense of wonder, spoke together in one voice, with a single sound:

How wonderful! Inconceivable Buddha,
you comprehend the aims of sentient beings;
and you, whose nature is free of conceptual thought,
proclaim the inconceivable state.
The person who holds it
has the lower realms cut off.
For whoever has it in hand,
accomplishment rests in the palm of his hand.
Without fear and without any sense of mine,
all doubts entirely abandoned,
dwelling on the ground of exhaustion,
he acts for beings without deliberation.
The king of secret mantras, the protector,
protects all of us.
Those who wish to accomplish their desired aims
meditate in their minds on what has been taught.

Then, as soon as this whole assembly had spoken these words, they fell silent. Then all were amazed, their eyes wide open, and they had no power of speech left to address the Delight of the Śākya Clan.

Then the bodhisattvas dwelling in the sky, having heard this wonder, filled their cupped hands with various flowers in the sky above the Blessed One Śākyamuni, and praised the Blessed One in verse:

Ever pure and stainless,
always and continuously free of conceptual thought,
ever abiding without conceptual thought,
great hero, I bow to you and praise you.
All your phenomena are unmoving;
since all is the domain of what is well composed,
you know them always to be unmoving.
To you, free of elaboration, I bow and offer praise.
Wishing to benefit sentient beings,
you taught the conduct that you practiced,
and from it the pure truly arise.
Pure hero, I bow to you and praise you.
Just as space is pure,
free of every particle,
so too your body, Protector,
has abandoned all conceptualization.
Utterly pure like a crystal orb,
like space, free of elaboration,
all things being completely pure,
ocean of wisdom, I bow to you and praise you.
Like a rainbow in the sky,
Lord, you are always seen;
so, by pure sentient beings,
you, Lord, are always seen.
All the lower realms are completely liberated:
great hero, you yourself were born,
the essence of the expanse truly arose,
a single wisdom of great splendor.
Throughout the whole earth, without fail,
you behold the suffering of sentient beings;
having brought this suffering to an end,
they become equal to the unsurpassed state.
This mantra, chief of the secret mantras,
in order to increase understanding of the Dharma,
gives birth to all the children of the victors.
Through what you, Lord, have spoken,
all things from the very first become perfect.
Victor, you yourself were born without cause;
fully freed from karma and from birth,
you are born as the supreme Victor, the Lord.
Flame of wisdom without intrinsic nature,
having abandoned all that is conditioned,
the single nature of the realm of phenomena,
unsurpassed Victor, I bow and pay homage.

Thus they spoke.

Then the Lord of Secrets, Vajrapāṇi, asked the Blessed One in verse about the meaning of the words of the mantra:

Blessed One, how is secret mantra to be accomplished?
What is the means of accomplishment?
What is called the root of the means of accomplishment?
And how, too, is it taught?
How, so that the mind may be pure,
Lord, what should one meditate on?
How is it that it is without fruit?
How should the mantra practitioner rely on it?

When he had asked this, the Blessed One declared to the bodhisattva Vajrapāṇi:

Having made the awakening mind firm,
I shall speak of the accomplishment of mantra.
This is the rite to be performed for the accomplishment of mantra;
otherwise everything is without fruit.
Just as, if its root has not grown,
a tree has neither flower nor fruit,
so know the fruit of secret mantra:
the awakening mind is its root.
Having generated the awakening mind,
one attains the unsurpassed state.
All phenomena that abide in this
come about through arising and abiding;
in brief, all things
are without intrinsic nature and beyond thought.
The awakening mind is certainly attained;
otherwise it remains something merely apart.
Son, it is like a lion:
one meditates on the conditioned as emptiness.
All existence is illusion,
whatever abides as the fruit of thought.
What the non-Buddhists (tīrthikas) assert,
the self, the life principle, and the person,
what they speak of as primal matter and the spirit (puruṣa),
and that which abides as all-pervading:
having thus imagined the round of saṃsāra,
these are nothing but things of saṃsāra.
Deluded by conceit,
the childish, for whom suffering is real,
are bound by the noose of what is false.
Seeing those who suffer,
it is to all of them that this is spoken.
These are the words that were declared:
There is no life principle, nor are there sentient beings;
there is no agent, nor any primal matter;
there is no pervader, nor are there things;
there is no nature of substance and the like.
The noose whose nature is wrong cognition,
the bondage of existence, is abandoned;
one is wholly parted from all things.
By meditating on the manifold just as it is,
one is wholly freed from all things
and attains the state of emptiness.
By way of the distinction of outer and inner,
meditate thus on everything.
Foam, water bubbles, and illusions
rise up from emptiness alone.
Having meditated thus on everything,
one discerns the aspects of the state of wisdom.
Freed from the ways of evil,
one is liberated into the deathless state.
Beyond the aggregates, the elements, and the sense sources,
and beyond apprehended and apprehender as well,
the noose of omniscient wisdom
should be thoroughly examined with regard to death.
Where there are no aggregates or elements,
and the sense sources do not arise,
that is complete liberation from all things:
so the supreme Victor has always taught.
Where there is no foundation consciousness,
no self, and not even a person,
this awakening of the buddhas here upon the earth,
there everything is taught to be an illusion.
I, who have gained mastery of omniscience,
comprehend the aims of sentient beings.
So that the rites may be undertaken,
I explain it to you, son, without omission.
That there is a foundation,
and that there is consciousness as well,
and that its self-nature, my son,
exists: this too was taught.
Seeing the sufferings of the childish,
who are possessed by the demon of things,
so that I might lead them in, son,
the extensive rite was taught.
There is no sentient being and no life principle,
no person and no agent,
no consciousness and no foundation;
that in which there is no self and no Lord (Īśvara),
no spirit and no pervader,
no things and not even illusion,
wholly freed from all things:
this is taught as the stainless true Dharma.
That itself is the supreme chief.
Rely on the state of perfect buddhahood;
constantly and continually rely on that state;
constantly and continually one should meditate.
Meditate on the awakening mind;
so as to be wholly free of apprehended and apprehender,
purify all things thoroughly.
The aggregates, the elements, and the sense sources,
all are emptiness, like an illusion:
this is the awakening mind in summary.
The intelligent bodhisattva
should meditate thus in the mind.
All excellences draw near.
If, with conscientiousness, one constantly relies
on the very mind of the buddhas,
the sprout of the awakening mind appears.
If one has done the approach, the fruit is small;
for those who are born through the conduct of secret mantra,
a body like a wish-fulfilling jewel
will be attained through the awakening mind.
A body unstained like space
will be attained completely.
The merit of meditating on the awakening mind
is said to be inconceivable;
all the buddhas of great miraculous power
have said its fruit, too, is inconceivable.

Then the bodhisattva, the great being Vajrapāṇi, rose from his seat, circumambulated the Blessed One three times, boldly took up his vajra in the proper manner, and addressed the Blessed One with these words:

I, your son, arisen from the ocean of wisdom,
am delighted and glad,
yet I have great doubts about these things.
How am I to understand them?
Maitreya and the others,
all those of the ten grounds without exception,
are in doubt about this.
Great Sage, please explain.
Out of loving concern for those
who have not worked for the benefit of beings,
who have abandoned merit and wisdom,
I make this request of the Blessed One.
Today, you who strive to benefit beings,
please tell me all of it without omission.
What is its might and power?
What is its overpowering of adversaries?
How have you, Lord, here truly spoken
of the one called Acala?
There is no state other than this.
Please speak of all these things.
The maṇḍala through which
that state is accomplished, please explain it too.
You have spoken of the inconceivable state
by which this earth is attained.
Please speak also of the measure of secret mantra;
and how, too, is the maṇḍala?
Protector, I am in doubt about this.
For the benefit of beings here,
please likewise speak of the accomplishments of mantra,
and of the divisions of its rites,
and explain the fierce rites as well.
Protector, how, through pacifying, enriching, and subjugating,
does the fruit of mantra recitation
come about very swiftly?
Protector, the characteristics of recitation,
how are they here upon the earth?
The rite of the fire hearth,
and the satisfying offerings, the purifying smoke and the torma offerings,
together with the king of wrathful ones:
Protector, please speak of all of these.
From the midday of a single day
to the instant of the eighth month,
please truly explain the accomplishments.
The healing treatments for the work of poison,
the various rites performed with substances,
and what arises from true invocation:
please explain them all without exception.
There is no other protector
through whom accomplishment is attained;
apart from you, Lord, there is no other.

The heroic bodhisattvas,
the śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas of great miraculous power,
and those renowned in the world,
Brahmā and the others said to have great miraculous power:
for them too, as protector and refuge,
apart from you, Lord, there is no other.

Then the bodhisattvas, Maitreya and the others, and Vajrapāṇi and the others, once again petitioned the Blessed One:

King of Dharma, the gateway of mantra,
Blessed One, please explain it.
For the sake of bodhisattvas
who practice the bodhisattva conduct,
the means of accomplishment for the conduct of secret mantra
that the Blessed One himself has seen:
those who have attained the joy and bliss of the Dharma
dwell upon the face of the earth.
Striving earnestly to please the secret mantra,
attaining bliss in this very life,
beings dwelling upon the earth
will without doubt come forth.
All will attain the state of sovereignty,
all fears entirely abandoned.
The king of secret mantras, of great miraculous power,
will appear in this world.
All the hostile rākṣasas,
the bhūtas, grahas, and piśācas,
and the harm that is said to come to beings
from the terrifying and the violent:
by recollecting the greatly powerful secret mantra,
none of these will occur.
Malevolent beings upon the earth,
and the various others that are spoken of:
in order to tame them,
this ritual manual has been taught.
Gods, nāgas, and yakṣas,
kumbhāṇḍas and kaṭapūtanas,
all the spirits that cause madness and the lunar mansions,
and the malevolent planets and others that are spoken of:
for those evil beings
this wrathful one has been taught.
All the hosts of grahas such as Brahmā,
likewise kings and śrāvakas,
and the bodhisattvas of the ten grounds,
cannot describe it;
it reduces all of them to ashes.
The recitation of secret mantra has great miraculous power;
contemplate it truly as abiding in the heart.
What is called secret mantra is hard to see,
and it destroys all to ashes without exception.
The maṇḍalas of Brahmā and the others,
the great-souled bodhisattvas,
and the lords of Indra's ten abodes
cannot withstand it.
The play of the king of wrathful ones:
hostile beings, whatever they may be,
should be regarded as like tufts of cotton blown away.
By the mere mention of his name
they scatter in the ten directions.
Therefore, in every way,
the recitation of secret mantra here
should be constantly and continually kept in mind.
One who desires the accomplishment of secret mantra,
fearless in every circumstance,
should make the mantra his abode here upon the earth.
Recollect the protector of secret mantra,
hold it, and constantly recite it.
Dwelling in the midst of fire,
through his body with bared fangs
foes scatter, and there is never any fear.
One rejoices as on a stairway to the higher realms.
When one is thronged by fear and terror,
the great king dispels that fear.
When one has fallen among bandits and thieves,
the body poised like a lion,
the king of wrathful ones of great miraculous power,
should always be held in mind.
Fevers, stabbing pains in the teeth and eyes,
those that arise in the waist, the ribs, and the flanks of the body,
the illnesses of embodied beings upon the earth,
of every various kind that arises:
by merely recollecting the secret mantra
all of these are brought to an end.
The wrathful king's inconceivable
might and power and valor:
upon the earth, apart from the omniscient one,
who could ever withstand them?
Just as the might and power of a universal monarch
cannot be expressed,
so too the wrathful king in his poised stance
cannot be described.
Just as the unshakable qualities
of a universal monarch cannot be counted,
so too the strength of the king of wrathful ones
cannot be expressed.
All the kings of wrathful ones
of what are called the vajra and lotus families,
the wrathful ones' envoys and messengers,
and the servants and messengers of great miraculous power:
they speak of this wrathful one
as the universal monarch.
The might and power so described
of the wise king of wrathful ones:
this sole glory of all the buddhas
is called their hero.
Because whatever he says has become words of truth,
he is undeceiving itself.
Therefore, because he is unmoving,
he is called Acala, the Immovable.
Having attained the Immovable ground,
he abides with a mind one-pointed in meditative concentration;
by the grounds of the śrāvakas and so forth,
and by all the māras, he is unmoved.
Then the Protector bestowed the name.
Perfect buddhas, supreme among humans,
heroic bodhisattvas:
this accomplishes all aims.
The buddhas of the past,
likewise those yet to come,
and the protectors who abide at present:
to benefit beings, this rite
was taught by all of them.
It is said to be called the Buddha Eye.
It guards the teaching
for all the realms of sentient beings.
It is the emanation of all the buddhas;
by the bodhisattvas of great power
innumerable secret mantras
are said to remain to benefit beings.
Wise Vajrapāṇi,
all these have been truly explained.
For people not obscured by evil
it brings forth light for beings.
Bound by the unbreakable noose,
they reach the unsurpassed state.
Through the wise king of wrathful ones,
even what the mantra practitioner has not accomplished is accomplished.
Through the blessing of the king of wrathful ones
all desires without exception will be fulfilled.

Then that entire assembly was amazed, and, joyful, their eyes wide open, they spoke in one voice and one speech with melodious words:

How wonderful! All the buddhas
are inconceivable in skillful means and wisdom.
With a mind that has become compassion,
please explain the incomparable pledge.
Whatever beings are thronged with fear,
all upon the earth become fearless;
and whoever has great devotion by nature,
for him the lower realms will be emptied.

Then the bodhisattva, the great being Vajrapāṇi, said to the Blessed One, "Blessed One, please explain how, merely by entering this maṇḍala of the king of wrathful ones, all sentient beings will attain the state of royal sovereignty, cannot be harmed by any obstructors or vināyakas, swiftly gain accomplishments, and become fully endowed with every kind of sovereignty."

When he had made this request, the Blessed One declared to the great bodhisattva Vajrapāṇi, "Vajrapāṇi, excellent, excellent! Vajrapāṇi, once again, most excellent!"

"Son of noble family, listen to this matter about which you have petitioned me. Through hearing this maṇḍala of the king of wrathful ones, everyone upon the earth is amazed, their eyes wide open; powerless, they cannot bear it, and then they fall to the ground."

Then, at that time, the Blessed One entered into the samādhi called Flame Arisen to Burn All Māras, and he spoke this mantra, the king of secret mantras, which fulfills the wishes of all sentient beings, which gives birth to all the bodhisattvas of the three times without exception, which illuminates the aims of all sentient beings, which is born from the inconceivable merit of all the tathāgatas, which is extremely difficult for any sentient being to see, which crushes the obstructors to seeing, which purifies all the lower realms, which completely pacifies all illness, which fulfills all wishes, which performs the restoration for all malevolent grahas, and which brings complete pacification from all the enemies that are māras:

namaḥ sarvva tathāgatebhyaḥ sarvvāśa paripūrakebhyaḥ sarvvathā caṇḍa mahāroṣaṇa sphoṭaya hūṃ traṭ hāṃ māṃ

As soon as the Blessed One had spoken the king of secret mantras, that entire assembly fell headlong, face down, like the trunks of plantain trees and like a great forest. This great trichiliocosm, this world system, shook in six ways: it swayed, it moved, it moved violently, it shook, it shook violently, it trembled, it trembled violently. When the east rose, the west sank; when the south rose, the north sank. Moreover, they beheld wonders and marvels and were greatly amazed, and in the world systems they also beheld various buddha fields. In this world system, Adorned with the Essence of the Flower Ground, the various obstructors, the various kinds of rākṣasas and māras, and all others with malicious minds were destroyed to ashes.

Some lay face down.
As at the eon of destruction,
smoke and smoky haze hung everywhere.
The sun and moon, of great miraculous power,
even their light turned to darkness.
The black darkness and gloom were utterly terrifying;
in the regions where lightning flashed it was dreadful;
in every direction there were cries of wailing;
the whole earth was in turmoil.
The planets and all the hosts of lunar mansions,
and whatever other chief gods there were,
all of them, terrified and panic-stricken,
fell senseless, face down.
Asuras, kinnaras, and nāgas,
yakṣas, rākṣasas, and kaṭapūtanas,
unable to bear it in their bodies,
swooned as though struck by poison.
All of these blazed as one,
as though arisen from the fire at the end of the eon;
all of these blazed and spread.
Throughout the buddha fields,
the great-souled bodhisattvas,
the lords dwelling on the ten grounds,
they too grew drowsy and swooned,
and fell face down upon the ground.
The wrathful women, the envoys and servants,
the male and female messengers with their slaves,
unable to withstand the stance of this body,
lost their senses and fell face down.
The śrāvakas and the rhinoceros-like solitary ones,
and the other great wonder-workers of the world,
like sawdust beaten down by iron,
they too fell where they stood.
Brahmā, Śakra, and the other gods,
the asuras and yakṣas of great miraculous power,
all of them turned to ashes.
When they were thronged with suffering,
a little while later, once again,
they turned to one another and spoke in fear:
"What will become of this only son?
This earth is utterly terrifying!
Apart from the state of the Sugata
we see no peace at all."
The forms of the māras and obstructors,
of every various kind, stood there;
their bodies as though transformed,
they stood upon the earth like trees.
The realms of Brahmā and the others,
all of these were seen ablaze.
Even bodies that had become like Avīci hell
were everywhere wrapped in smoke and haze.
The buddha fields without exception,
all of these it burns.
Imbued with compassion and truly endowed with it,
Vajrapāṇi made this request:
"Since the welfare of beings has not been considered,
Blessed One, please withdraw this display.
The childish, who are bound to suffering,
cannot bear this.
Protector of all the sentient beings in the world,
you bring forth compassion.
Born from the mountain of sole love,
you who free beings from suffering,
you alone are the protector of beings,
and you alone have become their friend.
The ferryman who carries them across the ocean of saṃsāra
is you alone.
You alone, who heal all illnesses,
are the physician.
You too are the one who shows the sacred path;
you are a father in the ocean of saṃsāra;
upon the earth there is you alone.
Protector, because you have generated compassion,
the whole earth truly flourishes.
Like a wish-fulfilling tree for those who seek their aims
are the true heroes, those with the awakening mind;
the lords dwelling on the ten grounds
flourish through you alone, Protector.
Your compassion is like a lotus born from the mud;
those whose minds have turned evil,
Protector of beings, please burn them.
They become as though without core,
like the trunk of a plantain tree.
Like moths, these people,
who have received the call of fierce summoning,
though they are burned, feel no stabbing pain.
So too here the childish,
all the obstructors and vināyakas,
and the lords of the strength of ten innumerables,
through the utterance of the king of secret mantras,
all of them are destroyed to ashes.
Therefore, in every way,
Blessed One, I petition you.
I, your son, am delighted and glad;
it is fitting that you hear this from me."

Then the Blessed One Śākyamuni possessed a mind endowed with compassion, and these words of petition that Vajrapāṇi had addressed to the supreme lord of sages for the benefit of sentient beings were most far-reaching.

Then, at that time, the Blessed One entered into the samādhi called Source of the Life of All Sentient Beings and Birth of Wisdom.

Having entered into it, he spoke this queen of knowledge mantras, which benefits all sentient beings, which wholly transforms all that is conditioned, which strikes terror into all terrors, which dispels all enemies, fire, and water, which brings all sentient beings to excellent glory and good fortune, which gives birth to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas, the śrāvakas and the pratyekabuddhas, which burns up all fears, which crushes all harm, and which is the intention of all the buddhas and bodhisattvas:

namaḥ sarvva tathāgatebhyaḥ sarvvāśa paripūrakebhyaḥ sarvvathā oṃ sarvva saṃskāra pariṣkāra viśuddha dharmmate mahānaya parivāre svāhā

Then, merely through his speaking this, throughout all the world systems of the great trichiliocosm all happiness was bestowed, and all were endowed with victory on every side.

Those who had gained the state of sovereignty,
by bringing their minds back into their own bodies,
truly came forth with excellent dispositions;
all their evils were completely pacified.
They came to behold the Blessed One.
Endowed with minds that had come to awareness,
all were possessed of their own lives;
having become valiant in miraculous power,
they attained a most wondrous state.
They stand before the supreme Victor.
Their own and others' disciplines become firm;
through the might of this knowledge mantra,
by their own miraculous power they are victorious in battle;
upon the earth they come to overpower adversaries;
all attain the state of sovereignty.
Those who had become chiefs of all the māras,
and all those who had come upon the earth,
were wholly freed from every fear.
What is spoken of as the lower realms,
abiding in the depths of extreme suffering:
all the lower realms were torn apart,
and they became vessels of happiness.
That which is called the fire of hell,
so hard to look upon and so dreadful,
at that moment became exceedingly cool,
like a portion of the autumn moon.
The places described as hells,
dense with bitterly cold black darkness,
as though by a fierce great blazing fire,
all of them became warm.
The suffering of those called pretas,
utterly unbearable and dreadful:
through the might of this knowledge mantra
all of these were freed from it.
And that entire assembly,
whose minds had been tormented by suffering,
were truly granted every happiness,
and they spoke joyful words to one another.
The great obstructors, the vināyakas,
and the other creatures besides,
and the bhūtas upon the earth, settled in their own minds,
having become gentle,
gazed upon one another.
The heroic bodhisattvas,
and Brahmā and the others of great miraculous power,
their own minds beautiful and at ease,
all of them attained happiness.
The messengers, envoys, servants, and attendants,
the bhūtas, grahas, and piśācas,
all attained the state of sovereignty
and dwelt upon the face of the earth.
The steadfast bodhisattvas
and the entire assembly
of great miraculous power
spoke these words for the benefit of beings:
"How wonderful! Omniscient one, without equal,
whose wisdom of skillful means is beyond thought,
upon the earth, apart from you,
who else is there to arise?
In a vessel of boundless happiness
you hold all of these.
All have gained the state of sovereignty
and dwell upon the face of the earth.
The suffering of existence is no longer as before,
so utterly unbearable and dreadful.
All become children of the buddhas."
Rejoicing in benefiting all sentient beings,
with minds that had become one-pointed,
they spoke with melodious words:
"The one who has become the friend of all
is none other than the Buddha.
Even those whose minds have become a cause for reproach
he brings to happiness, each one.
What he truly taught upon taking birth is this:
for as long as one has not passed into nirvāṇa,
for that long one enters saṃsāra;
this is the supreme way of the buddha vehicle."
All the bodhisattvas
and the assembly of Brahmā and the others
all rose from their seats,
and where the Lord was seated,
the bodhisattvas surrounded him.
The gods of great miraculous power, Brahmā and the others,
having arrived all together,
petition for the unsurpassed state.
Wishing to benefit all sentient beings,
they circumambulated the perfect Buddha,
bowed low to the Protector of the World,
and spoke these words:
"How wonderful! Great lord of buddhas,
who delights in healing all sentient beings,
Protector of the World, the means
by which you protect are incomparable and inconceivable.
Through what you have established here,
the ocean of suffering is purified.
Physician of beings, when you are seen,
happiness truly arises."

Then the Blessed One Śākyamuni, with a profound mind endowed with compassion, spoke these words in this melodious voice:

"Bodhisattvas of great miraculous power,
you who possess miraculous power, do not be afraid.
The perfect buddhas, supreme among humans,
have taught these inconceivable things.
For one who relies on such a Dharma,
the suffering by which one would again
be bound in this way within saṃsāra
will not arise, since one has performed the approach.
The perfect buddhas, supreme among humans,
truly taught the Great Vehicle.
Having truly gathered up the path,
all are completely freed from suffering.
That is the supreme suchness, peace;
it is called the vehicle of the buddhas.
Having taken up this sacred vehicle,
one will certainly, without doubt, become a buddha.
All the buddhas of the past,
likewise those yet to come,
and the protectors abiding at present:
from this very way of benefiting beings
all of them truly took it up,
and they attain the peace that ends suffering.
Therefore, in every way,
rely on such a true Dharma as this.
If one performs the approach to this Lord,
sufferings are dispelled."
Then the other bodhisattvas,
and Brahmā and the others of great miraculous power,
engaged in benefiting sentient beings,
spoke these melodious words in his presence:
"Protector, what you have spoken!
My mind has no other support;
there is nothing more supreme
than you, who have become our refuge.
All have gained the state of sovereignty;
they have come to take pride in their own miraculous powers,
are parted from every fear,
and have abandoned all suffering.
Toward the Buddha, the Dharma, and the Saṅgha,
those of great miraculous power are endowed with devotion;
truly abiding in the Great Vehicle,
they are fit for the deeds of awakening and the rest."

Thus they spoke.

Then that assembly, which included all the bodhisattvas, Vajrapāṇi and the others, touched their heads to the two feet of the Blessed One and made this request: "Blessed One, King of the Dharma, out of loving kindness for us, please explain that by which all sentient beings, through seeing the nature of reality of the maṇḍala of the King of the Wrathful Ones, will attain the state of Īśvara: that which causes all sentient beings to attain the state of Īśvara, which heals and cleanses all diseases, which is endowed with every glory, which destroys untimely death and all obstructing spirits, and which destroys all enemies, afflictions, bandits and thieves, savage men, harm from kings, lions, tigers and the like, fevers, tumors, eruptions that break out at night, and all harm from venomous snakes."

When this request had been made, the Blessed One,
the Protector of the Śākyas, of great miraculous power,
the great lord who arose from the vajra,
spoke these melodious words:
"So that I may explain the supreme maṇḍala
upon the surface of the earth, listen, my son!
The secret of all the buddhas, the protectors,
and of the bodhisattvas
I shall truly explain to you;
listen here with a mind intent on benefit.
The maṇḍala of the King of the Wrathful Ones,
all of this, which has not been taught before,
you, my son, must not give
to one whose mind is clouded by wickedness.
First I shall explain the master;
listen, my son, in meditative equipoise.
He becomes well established in every respect.
Of his many qualities, I shall tell a few.
He is perfect in moral discipline and endowed with compassion,
patient, diligent, and well settled,
pure and perfect in ritual;
he never disparages his guru;
he looks to the benefit of others as to his own;
he delights in giving and strives with resolve;
he is truthful in word and full of integrity;
he delights in generosity, is pleasant and gentle,
and his whole body is perfect in good qualities.
He constantly makes offerings
to the buddhas, the śrāvakas, and the children of the Victor;
he is devoted to his guru and without pride;
he is solely helpful and virtuous toward beings;
he is endowed with profound intelligence;
he knows what is timely and what is untimely;
he understands the faculties and the divisions of meaning;
he always delights in benefiting sentient beings;
he is greatly diligent in pleasing the secret mantras;
such a one is highly praised in this maṇḍala.
The gods of great miraculous power,
the buddhas, the śrāvakas, and the teachings,
and the bodhisattvas, the protectors:
knowing their divisions, he always
fully teaches the profound meaning.
In him there is no avarice at all;
even in wrath he speaks with love.
He knows the way of the four means of attraction,
is adorned with the six perfections,
has donned the armor of the bodhisattva,
and abides in the illusion-like state.
Just as it is taught for the śrāvakas,
who cultivate the Four Noble Truths,
so the mantra practitioner, as taught here,
cultivates them with the mind of awakening.
The bodhisattva endowed with intelligence
cultivates this in his mind
and attains the wisdom of intention.
Arising from the Great Vehicle,
the four means of attraction
and the six perfections are held
to bestow inconceivable fruits;
the one endowed with wisdom cultivates them.
Whether he is green, white, or yellow,
or in short even black,
tall or short, he has a fine complexion,
his hands reach down to his knees,
his face is always long,
his head is even and broad,
his eyebrows are unbroken, his nose is high,
and his ankles are slightly red.
Fully adorned with every good quality,
he purifies the ocean of evil.
He should honor all the teachings,
and he also teaches reality itself.
He knows the divisions of the maṇḍalas
and the assemblies of mantras and mudrās;
he obtains authorization and empowerment,
and becomes one of true wisdom.
He is devoted to his guru and to the deity,
and is endowed with the compendium of mantras and mudrās;
he knows the secret mantras of the King of Secret Mantras;
he pleases sentient beings and speaks with love;
he knows the reality of mantra recitation and the fire offering;
he knows the reality of pacifying and enriching rites;
he knows the fierce rites and subjugation;
he applies mantras and mudrās thoroughly;
he truly attends upon his guru
and regards him as the Teacher himself.
He makes offerings to all the bhūtas,
and he also knows the reality of worldly beings.
Perfect, free of indolence toward sentient beings,
he will display enthusiasm and teach.
He whose wisdom mantra of Īśvara
brings everything under control
constantly recites the mantras of the wrathful ones
and strives with resolve to please the deity.
Endowed with moral discipline and perfect,
truly arisen from enlightened body and speech,
fully adorned with the observances,
he always protects all.
Thus I have taught in brief
the characteristics of his qualities.
One who fully knows these
is highly praised as a master.
Otherwise, my son, the rite
of mantra recitation is fruitless.
The secret mantra is not accomplished by oneself,
and even when taught to others there is no accomplishment.
The maṇḍala does not bear fruit,
there are no means of accomplishing the mantra,
nothing at all is protected,
and there is no deity for the fire offering.
These are the marks of one who lacks meditation,
and they are to be utterly shunned.
Therefore the characteristics of the master
are praised in every respect.
Therefore this secret wisdom
should be understood as taught
to be the source of all the buddhas;
otherwise it will be fruitless.
The master and the disciple,
ever heedful,
should guard it constantly and without interruption,
just as they guard their own lives."

"Even in a hundred koṭis of eons
the appearance of a buddha is extremely rare,
and his teaching by the lord upon the earth
is likewise extremely rare.
Therefore the master,
fully adorned in every way with all good qualities,
should constantly recite
the fifteen-syllable secret mantra.
When he has recited it a hundred thousand times,
he should also begin the fire offering.
The one who knows the mantra and has done this
should then draw the supreme maṇḍala.
There will be no malevolent beings and no obstructing spirits,
nor any hosts of bhūtas and the like;
those who obstruct by force of their might
will have no hold on the state and its fruit.
All will attain the state of Īśvara,
all will be fully adorned with happiness,
and will be free of every fear;
of this there is no doubt.
Otherwise everything is fruitless:
the obstructing spirits look for an opening

As soon as the Blessed One had spoken this supremely secret great heart mantra, this great trichiliocosm world system shook in six ways. It moved, moved strongly, and moved strongly all over. It trembled, trembled strongly, and trembled strongly all over. It shook, shook strongly, and shook strongly all over. It swayed, swayed strongly, and swayed strongly all over. a common rite for the other obstructors as well.
The mantrin, filled with wrath,
should properly make images of them,
smear them with mustard seed and poison,
and anoint them with salt as well.
When they are burned in the fire,
they are consumed swiftly, that very instant.
Or else, once more I shall explain
striking all obstructors with the dagger.
Having made its effigy,
one should strike it with a vajra dagger.
Struck, it is completely quelled
and without any doubt it dies.
Or else I shall also explain
the paralyzing of all obstructors.
Having made its effigy,
visualize Mount Meru upon its body,
and press it down firmly
with a vajra that has eight points.
Then it is completely quelled,
and there is no doubt that it will die.
Even the obstructors of the god Brahmā
swiftly remain motionless and fixed.
Then, by all and for all,
the common rite should be performed.
Having made its effigy,
rightly begin the rite of fire offering.
Having chopped and cut it with a weapon,
offer it into the fire, beginning from the feet.
Once it is cut, at that very moment
it will be destroyed, without any doubt.
Thus, when the wise one performs the rite
upon all of them in every way,
all the obstructors
who have malicious minds are destroyed.
Beating, cutting, striking with the dagger,
piercing and likewise probing,
burning, and any other kind of subjugation:
the mantrin adorned
with the mind of awakening should do all of these.
Those with utterly malicious minds
cut off the lives of others
and harm sentient beings.
One who is the very ground of benefit,
having generated great compassion,
recollecting the Buddha and the Dharma,
speaks just as all the buddhas do:
he is the one who performs the fierce rites."

opening his eyes wide in wonder,
spoke with the pleasing words
of the Sage, the lord of the Dharma:
"How wonderful! The buddhas are inconceivable,
and the Dharma of the buddhas is inconceivable too!
As long as the very name of those

for that long the mind,
with its notions of help and harm belonging to the lower realms,
toward the suffering of Avīci

are wholly bent and go."

Then Vajrapāṇi again said this to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, please explain the great king of wrathful ones, authorized by all the tathāgatas, set out in mantras, mudrās and the stages of the mantra, swiftly marked by the marks of the mudrās, who, when applied together with all the gods, nāgas, yakṣas and rākṣasas, performs every action, unfailing and unobstructed."

Then the Blessed One said this to the bodhisattva Vajrapāṇi: "Excellent, excellent! Vajrapāṇi, excellent once again! Now listen well and keep it firmly in mind, and I shall explain it to you."

"Excellent, Blessed One," Vajrapāṇi said to the Blessed One.

those who do harm to the teaching,

Then the bodhisattva Vajrapāṇi bowed his head at the feet of the Blessed One and said this to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, it is wonderful! Sugata, it is wonderful! The fierce mudrās of the king of wrathful ones, all of them unfailing, unobstructed and overpowering, grant accomplishment in just the way the practitioner applies them. For the benefit and happiness of all sentient beings, I too wish to explain this means of accomplishment from the ritual manual of the great king of wrathful ones, who destroys all obstructors."

The Blessed One said, "Vajrapāṇi, if you think the time has come for it, speak."

Then the bodhisattva, the great being Vajrapāṇi, having received the Blessed One's permission, bowed to all the tathāgatas with a display that delighted and gladdened every intention, and entered into the absorption of the samādhi called Supreme Light of All Accomplishments. The very moment the Blessed One entered absorption, all the world systems were at once lit up with a great light, and all the buddha fields were filled with the bodies of Vajradhara; these circled through the ten directions and then entered back into the Blessed One's own body.

"Listen to the great miracles that make up the means of accomplishment of the great king of wrathful ones. Eating leaves and the like, one should recite three hundred thousand times. Then, remaining for three days, one should make a great offering before the painted scroll, and in front of it kindle a fire with kindling sticks of nimba wood, and with the heart mantra make a fire offering of eight thousand white mustard seeds smeared with ghee; this is after first having done the approach.

"The means of accomplishment of the great king of wrathful ones is this. Having drawn the maṇḍala, one should bind the sword mudrā, sit cross-legged, and recite until one becomes a vidyādhara. Honored by all the vidyādharas, one remains for an eon, with no time of death unless one wishes it; one travels through all the buddha fields, makes offerings to all the tathāgatas, and works for the benefit of all sentient beings.

"If one again recites three hundred thousand times, one can perform every rite one wishes. One makes words come true. One fells trees. One makes birds fall from the sky. One dries up ponds. One turns the wind elsewhere.

"On the night of a lunar eclipse, one should make a maṇḍala with cow dung that has not fallen to the ground and strew loose fragrant flowers. Then, before the painted scroll or a volume of the Perfection of Wisdom, one should put one measure of ghee from a cow whose calf is of the same color into a copper vessel, kindle a fire of khadira wood, and recite while heating it; the three accomplishments will then arise.

"Then, if one takes up residence on a mountain peak and, without eating food, recites a hundred thousand times, one will see treasure.

"If one wishes to stop a cattle plague, by burning a thousand oblations of milk it will be stopped. If one smears dūrvā grass with the three sweet substances and makes a hundred thousand fire offerings, even a great cattle plague will be stopped. If one smears lotuses with curd, honey and ghee and makes a hundred thousand fire offerings, one will see three supreme requests granted.

"When going to sea, if one enters the water of a river and recites three hundred thousand times, one will reach one's destination. If one burns flowers of a certain color, one will obtain clothing of that same color. By burning grain, one's grain too will become inexhaustible. By a fire offering of barley one becomes a great householder. With priyaṅgu one brings others under one's control. With fragrant wood one comes to have many attendants and servants. If one makes a hundred thousand fire offerings of bilva fruit smeared with the three sweet substances, one becomes a king.

"The proper rite for the painted scroll is this. One should paint the noble Acala with one face, wearing the hero's garment of saffron cloth, looking with a sidelong glance, holding a sword and a noose in his hands, his face furrowed with wrathful frowns, terrifying the three worlds, and clad in saffron. Then one should go to the ocean, spread out the painting, wear clean clothes on one's body, and, free of fear, keeping silence and living on alms, recite five hundred thousand times. On the final day, if one makes ten thousand fire offerings with kindling sticks of caṇaka, one will see Acala, will also attend upon the tathāgatas, and, gaining samādhi, will attain the samādhi of the bodhisattvas.

"Alternatively, one should paint the noble protector Acala on a cloth from a charnel ground and paint his eyes open with one's own blood. The practitioner, facing east, should spread out the painting opposite him, facing him; bathing three times a day and wearing wet clothes, he should recite three hundred thousand times in silence. On the night of the eighth day of the waning fortnight, he should give a torma of meat to all the bhūtas and then recite ten thousand times upon a large corpse. The body of the corpse will then move; he should not be afraid. Then he will see in its mouth the center of an open lotus. When he takes it and holds it, he becomes a vidyādhara and travels in the world of Brahmā in a divine form.

"Alternatively, if one recites before the painted scroll in three sessions a day for six months, makes offerings as one's means allow, and fumigates it with agaru incense, one will become the lord of a country.

"If one consecrates a banner with a thousand recitations and holds it out in front of the army, the enemy's army will be defeated in battle.

"One who wishes to paralyze an army should imagine a great body, yellow in color, with four faces and four arms, of ugly hue, baring its fangs, terrifying, and wrapped in a garland of flames, as if seizing the enemy's force; or else, if one paints it and displays it, the enemy's army will be paralyzed.

"One who wishes to stir up a quarrel should make a fire offering of crow and owl feathers; a quarrel will then arise.

"One who wishes to make an enemy intoxicated should make a hundred thousand fire offerings of oblations. Through the fire offering, Acala's servants bind him by the neck and carry him off to the south; by visualizing the enemy's body as intoxicated, vomiting blood, and terrified, he will perish together with his family line.

"If one empowers a human bone with a hundred and eight recitations and grinds it, whoever's name one utters will at once be struck by fever.

"If one smears nimba fruit with melted butter and makes a fire offering, the enemy will perish within three days.

"If one makes an effigy of the enemy out of salt and clay, pierces it with thorns of the intoxicating datura, and makes a fire offering of it chopped and cut into pieces, he will perish at once.

"If one utters the enemy's name and burns a hundred and eight of what is called salamari, the enemy will become poor.

"If one wishes to bring someone under control, one should make an effigy out of salt, cut it up and make a fire offering of it; then even a great man will come under one's control within seven days.

"If one makes a hundred thousand fire offerings of opened jasmine blossoms, a yakṣiṇī will come under one's control. By making a fire offering of the leaves of the magic-making plant, one causes madness. By a fire offering of salt one can summon even a daughter of the gods.

"By a fire offering of guggulu one will attain kingship.

"The rite for another painted scroll is this. In the center one should paint the Blessed One Śākyamuni; to his right, Mañjuśrī adorned with every ornament; and to his left, Vajradhara, his face gently smiling, holding a vajra in his hand. By reciting five hundred thousand times in its presence, one becomes able to perform every rite, and disease will break out in the enemy's army.

"If one binds the eye mudrā and, in wrath, recites the mantra that ends with syllables such as hūṃ, the enemy will be seized by a great graha or will surely perish.

"If one recites a hundred thousand times over ash from a charnel ground, whoever one gives it to will become one's servant.

"If one recites seven times over gorocanā and makes a forehead mark with it, all people will be delighted, and one will become invisible. All obstructors, vināyakas and the like will be made to blaze. If one applies it to oneself, one frees people from Brahmā and rākṣasas even from a hundred yojanas away.

"If one wishes to subdue the affliction of someone who has been seized by a black snake for six months, one should, in wrath, draw the sword emblem and surround it with figures of the family of the great mahoragas. Then one should consecrate the sword with a thousand recitations and set it in the center so that it seems to blaze. If it is shown to the patient, it will certainly take effect.

"If one recites the heart mantra of the noble one-eyed Acala a hundred and eight times in each of three sessions, one will be constantly connected with him.

"If one offers a fist-pressed ball of dough in a solitary place, he will grant the great supreme accomplishment.

"If one binds the heart mudrā and, in wrath, recites the syllable hūṃ, one splits the clouds.

"If one makes a fire offering of thorns smeared with mustard oil, one makes the wind and rain stand still like a pestle. It provides great protection for the practitioner.

"If one performs the practice pressing down with the left foot, people will obey one's commands.

"If one makes effigies of gods and humans and cuts them, they will be cut. If one makes effigies of all gods and humans and strikes them with an iron kīla, they will be pinned by the kīla. Then, if one wishes to bind them, one should consecrate a yellow thread and wind it around the figure; they will then be bound. If one wishes to crush them, one should make effigies of gods and the like and press them down beneath a board; they will then be crushed. Then, if one wishes to beat them, one should make effigies of humans and the like and strike them with a switch; they will come bowing and ask for one's commands. If one wishes to suppress them, one should make effigies of all gods and humans and press them down beneath a board or the like; they will then be suppressed.

"Thus, in whatever way this greatly miraculous king of wrathful ones is applied, in just that way he also performs thousands of other wondrous deeds."

Then all the māras and great māras of terrifying form, and the gods, nāgas, yakṣas and the rest, throughout all the world systems, were scorched by the splendor of the great wrathful one. Each of them went to his own tathāgata and pleaded, "Blessed One, please protect us! Sugata, please protect us from this burning!"

Then those tathāgatas spoke these words to the great obstructors of their own fields: "Sons of good family, keep in mind the great wrathful one called Acala, who acts on the command of all the tathāgatas and utterly destroys all the malicious. Otherwise your heads will be split into a hundred pieces by the vajra of Vajrapāṇi. Give rise to the thought that whatever exists is like an illusion. Give rise to a mind that seeks the benefit and happiness of all sentient beings. Keep in mind that sentient beings are free of all evil. Sons of good family, keep in mind the great king of wrathful ones, who is free of all suffering."

Then all those malicious ones went for refuge to that very great king of wrathful ones and said, "Blessed One, we will not cause harm in any region. Wherever this great king of wrathful ones abides, we will protect it. We will provide whatever is needed. We will also destroy every harm." Having made this pledge, they came to the feet of the Blessed One and sat down there.

Then the bodhisattva, the great being Vajrapāṇi, also taught the practitioners the rite for practicing the great king of wrathful ones: "This great king of wrathful ones should be recited in a solitary place by a person who keeps unbroken silence and lives on twigs, roots and fruit, or on water and air. This unfailing and unobstructed king of wrathful ones should be applied against malicious rākṣasas and the like, whose minds are turned away from the Three Jewels and who are greatly proud and arrogant. With a mind intent on their benefit, one should bring them to give rise to the mind set on supreme awakening and, through faith, to become devoted to the Three Jewels. Not even by the rites of other ritual manuals should it be applied against lowly, suffering beings. The great king of wrathful ones should be applied on behalf of those who have devotion and faith in the Three Jewels. Even one who has committed the five acts of immediate retribution will without doubt obtain the accomplishments he desires, in accordance with the rite." So spoke the Blessed One, Vajradhara.

you alone are my son,

The protection of my teaching, too,
rests in your hands.

the chief, the lord, the charioteer.

all awakening rests in the palm of your hand.

The marvel of the supreme secret
I shall explain without omission.
That which dwells in my heart
is blessed for the sake of others.
The secret of all dharmas, too,
never taught anywhere before this,

by which accomplishments are swiftly won,
abides here on the face of the earth
together with those of supreme form:
temples of many colors,
and likewise bearers of many forms,
of great miraculous power and great might,
who likewise uphold the Victor's sons,
truly endowed with the Victor's qualities,
supremely devoted to the Three Jewels.

those who do harm are burned.

it brings all sentient beings under control.

"Those given to sexual union,
whoever does evil with fire,
those of celibate conduct and likewise others,
whatever people there are on the earth:
all of these are harmed here.
The cities of the gods likewise:
with bodies malicious and ugly in hue,
the malevolent ones do harm.

you, too, ought to hear.
The mantra practitioner, having first done all this,
the perfect Buddha, light of the sun,
the omniscient lord endowed with understanding,
beyond all elaborations,
the utterly pure Dharma,
the great abode, peaceful from the very beginning,
wholly free of every fault,
bringing about every excellence:
this is the chief of the Three Jewels,

"The forms of the planetary spirits, their signs,
their attire and their very shapes
are not known to the stable and the moving;
so I shall explain them: son, listen.
This abode of wisdom of mine,
wondrous, of beautiful aspect,
in all the circle of the earth,
only son, I have never taught."

"The supreme forms of the planetary spirits,
supremely wondrous, beyond conception,
have become supremely beautiful;
relying on beautiful forms
that are wrathful, with ugly faces,
they themselves abide beautiful and beneficent.
When their form, beyond conception,
has become supremely beautiful,
then this is beyond conception,
not the domain of the meditation of sages.
Peaceful acts are done for sentient beings,
and the wrathful is wrathful indeed;
for sentient beings this one, as peace,
at that time acts in every way.

"If the wise make offerings to the Buddha,
the Dharma and likewise the Saṅgha,
their forms are represented,
and at that time one should make the offering.
Pacifying should be known as pacifying,
and enriching too, in its proper order;
this one called the fierce rite,
and the defining forms of bringing under control:
all of these, put in brief,
have been taught at length.
Through offering in deep equipoise
this is not pacifying and the rest;
one directly honors the Three Jewels,
who bring about every excellence.

"With the mind restrained
and with the five senses restrained,
when a day has passed with the great white fragrance,
one anoints the body with it and is adorned by bathing.
Having gone to a pleasing place,
very secluded and free of cattle,
one should always smear it with cow dung and the like,
and anoint it with fragrance.
The colors yellow and red,
white, or likewise green,
and black are rightly taught.
Having taken up the colors,
the wise, according to their understanding,
should make a pleasing maṇḍala.
The Buddha should be made yellow,
the Dharma should be made white,
the Saṅgha should be made green:
this secret is rightly proclaimed.
By the many colors of the planetary spirits
the maṇḍalas are marked out.
Beneath it the wise
should always place the body of the mantra practitioner.
One draws the side of the one who holds the mudrā,
each with his own mudrā, color and emblem.
The measure of the maṇḍala
is well known to be sixteen finger-widths;
or else, in whatever way pleases the mind,
in that way one should properly undertake it.
In accordance with the colors indicated,
one offers scents, flowers and the like.
In equipoise, for each in turn,
one recites their secret mantras.
With a one-pointed mind,
having meditated on oneself as the Sugata,
with each planetary spirit present before one,
one should utter the secret mantras.

every intention is completely fulfilled:

abiding in the very nature of affliction,
through offering according to the rite,
that affliction will no longer arise for him.
He abides always in happiness,
loving, kind and gentle to sentient beings,
his mind clear and joyful,
delighting in benefiting all beings.

"The benefit that comes from accomplishment:
a peaceful mind in equipoise,
outwardly keeping to discipline,
being lord and chief of all,
a king of great magical power,
and one who tames malicious beings:
this will come about without doubt;
so the Victor, speaker of truth, has declared.
Excessive rain and the failure of rain,
the onset of famine and the outbreak of strife:
when the maṇḍala has been honored,
these will not occur for him.

"When one undertakes this rite
for pacifying and for enriching,
scents, as taught in accordance with the rite,
should first be offered here.
Even for one who is laden with evil
it will be quickly accomplished.
He is freed from all diseases,
all faults are cast off,
he is freed from all evil,
all strife is cast off,
and everything will always be happy:
this will come about without doubt.
If one makes offerings by this rite of offering,
having done what is meritorious,
all the hosts of gods are pleased
and will bestow vast accomplishments.
All malicious vināyakas

do no harm

He is always free of the various diseases

through offering according to the rite

All the malicious ones of great might,
the terrifying planetary spirits and the rest,

this will come about without doubt.
Thus, having made offerings
in every way and in every respect,
one knows happiness in all things,
one casts off the ocean of suffering,
one is beautified by every kind of excellence,
likewise always endowed with royal power,
one's speech is pleasing everywhere,
one is adorned with lordship over all,
and one becomes supremely fortunate:
of this there is no doubt at all."

When he had spoken thus, the great yogin,
the Sage, lord of all dharmas,
fell completely silent.
Then the assembly said:
"Great hero, excellent being,
omniscient one, free of the sickness of fever,
you have taught the definitive meaning of the reality of mantra
completely, without remainder.
Protector, beings were utterly without it;
this way it has been declared in your presence,
this essence, drawn from the vast ocean
of secret mantra and condensed:
by obtaining it, this fearlessness becomes manifest.
All of them, without doubt,
having dealt with all who dwell beneath the earth,
are freed from all suffering
and truly endowed with all happiness.
Then everything will come about,
and all diseases will be cast off.
The malicious ones of terrifying form,
and whatever rākṣasas have fangs,
on seeing the secret mudrā,

will all of them perish."

This concludes the chapter on the vast offering to the planetary spirits.

The one called the protector Acala
watches over sentient beings.
He reveals the maṇḍala of Mañjuśrī.
Then all the beautiful accomplishments,
the vajra, the sword and the like as well,
within the later maṇḍala
the mantra practitioner accomplishes according to the rite.
Having seen the rite of the actions,
he properly examines the ground for its purity.
Wishing to benefit the disciples,
he draws the whole maṇḍala with care.
It measures five cubits on every side,
each side adorned with a courtyard.
Where the gateways have been set up,
one should visualize the western gate.
The rite of the gate, free of fear,
should be performed at the northern side.
Whoever performs it otherwise
will surely perish before long.

As before, according to the rite,
the lines should be struck as a square,
with gate recesses and with courtyards,
and well adorned with bands as well.
Then, in the central part, the mantra practitioner
draws the sword mudrā, of beautiful form,
in the middle of a fire maṇḍala,
set firmly upon a jewel stone,
wreathed in a blazing garland of splendor,
like the light of the rising sun.
With its own seed syllable, seed by seed,
he draws the perfect Buddha who is to be meditated upon.
This is the mantra of blessing:

namaḥ sarvva tathāgatebhyaḥ sarvva viśva mukhebhyaḥ he he he ājñā pari mārgaṇa āgaccha bhagavan sīghraṃ svāhā

To its north one should draw
a vase marked with a vajra,
peaceful, wreathed in a blazing garland,
the very nature of the Lord of the World.
In the southern direction, for the protector,
a vajra should be carefully drawn.
In the middle of a fire maṇḍala,
the wise one, keeping to the northeast, the direction of Īśāna,
should draw the beautiful image and mudrā of Vajrapāṇi,
set upon a jewel stone and blazing,
which is said to be the wish-fulfilling jewel:
the mudrā of the king of wrath, in heroic play.
Having drawn a lotus above it,
all the buddhas abide there.
Keeping to the southeast, the direction of Agni,
one should draw a vajra with great care,
with a terrifying garland of flames,
resting upon a lotus.
Below, the wise one
should draw the servant of the king of wrathful ones,
standing upon a vajra stone,
encircled by blazing vajras.
Within the middle of the wind maṇḍala,
one should draw there the noose mudrā,
wreathed with constant flames.

Then in the second section
one should draw the female messengers all around.
The female messenger called the Steadfast One
is properly placed in the north;
seated upon a water maṇḍala,
one should draw the Sky Slayer there.
In the southern direction the Terrifying Lady
is seated in the middle of a sun maṇḍala,
terrifying all obstructors on every side,
blazing like the fire of destruction.
She should be drawn upon a wheel stone.
For the sake of their retinues,
one should draw with great care.
For the Sky Slayer, on the band,
a pestle and a hammer
should be drawn with great care.
Then again, in the west,
one should draw the terrifying Blessed Lady.
The mudrā known as the ūrṇā
should be drawn in the eastern part.
The goddess is seated upon a white lotus,
adorned with garlands of jewels.
Thus, with certainty on every side,
when the mantra practitioner joins the assembly of virtuous mudrās
each with its own seed syllable,
the supreme maṇḍala is wondrous.
The mantra practitioner should please all these
by offering scents, flowers and the like, according to the rite.

Then Vajrapāṇi spoke the mantra of blessing:

Joining the palms of the hands
with the middle fingers inside:
holding this glorious mudrā
is declared to be the Lotus Treasury.
In the very presence of all the protectors,
it blesses the maṇḍala.

The mantra is this:

namaḥ sarvva tathāgatebhyaḥ sarvva sukhebhyaḥ hūṃ hūṃ vaḥ vaḥ maṃ maṃ svāhā

With this mantra, together with the mudrā, one should perform the invitation, the request to depart and the rest according to the rite. This is the great king of maṇḍalas of Mañjuśrī, the supremely secret protector Acala, taught by all the buddhas in order to protect the teaching, for the true Dharma to endure, and in order to protect sentient beings. Whoever, having entered here, has trained well in the knowledge of the mudrās of the directions has supremely entered the maṇḍala, the secret essence of all the tantras.

This maṇḍala of Mañjuśrī should not be explained to just anyone. Why is that? Because this maṇḍala has been taught as supreme.

Whatever mantra practitioner acts otherwise,
by corrupting the rite of the maṇḍala,
will surely, together with his disciples,
perish without doubt.

Then, having entered this maṇḍala of Mañjuśrī, one should bind the boundaries, and not otherwise. The master, having drawn the maṇḍala and entered it, should engage the disciple in whichever rites are appropriate.

Then Vajrapāṇi again spoke these words to Mañjuśrī, the youthful one: "Mañjuśrī, listen. The rite of the painted cloth that suppresses the immeasurable power of opponents is this. One should take cloth bought for more than its price, free of hair and the like. On it one should paint the king of wrathful ones, the Sole Hero, with his right hand raised, his right foot lifted up, his left foot also raised upward, his left hand making the threatening gesture with the forefinger, his face hideous, blazing within and above; and the Tathāgata above, reviving him with both hands. The king of flowers is to the north. To the right is the Blessed One Vairocana. Below, one should paint the practitioner raising his hands with a vessel of incense and gazing at the king of wrathful ones. Mañjuśrī, I have explained this rite of the painted cloth of the protector Acala out of a wish to benefit sentient beings.

"Mañjuśrī, listen to the rite of the means of accomplishment of the protector Acala, by which one obtains great accomplishment through an easy method. Here, first of all, a bodhisattva who practices the conduct of a bodhisattva by way of mantra should, alone and living on alms, in delightful places such as snow mountains, first perform the approach of a hundred thousand recitations before this painting. Then, taking up residence on a mountain summit, he should recite three hundred thousand times. Then, on the full moon, having made offerings as his means allow, he should recite until dawn. In the morning, at daybreak or after the sun has risen, the signs will appear: the sun will change and turn, and it will also become dark."

In the four directions a great sound will be heard, proclaiming "ha ha." The flower garlands of the king of wrathful ones will sway, the earth will quake, thunder will rumble and roar, and the vidyādharas will rain down flowers. Into the practitioner's presence the great king of wrathful ones himself will come, surrounded by all the gods, the nāgas, and the rest. At that time the sufferings of the hells will be completely pacified. Then one should offer water for welcome, prostrate, and remain there. Then the king of wrathful ones will speak, saying, "Son, I shall grant you one boon." One should then take whatever one wishes: the state of a vidyādhara, kingship, or invisibility.

If one becomes a vidyādhara, one will have the appearance of a sixteen-year-old, holding a sword in hand, mounted on a divine vehicle, surrounded by many vidyādharas, honored with great reverence by all the vidyādharas, surrounded by many buddha fields, and the buddhas, the blessed ones, will make offerings to one; and one will live for an eon.

Alternatively, if one wishes to accomplish an eye ointment, one should combine antimony, blue utpala, and costus, put them into a copper vessel, and at the time of a lunar eclipse recite the mantra until smoke arises. If that is applied to the eyes, one will become invisible within the desire realm.

Then, if one wishes to accomplish the sword, one should take a sword without crack or flaw, and on the eighth or the fourteenth day of the waning fortnight make vast offerings before the painting. Holding the protecting sword in the right hand, one should recite the mantra until it emits rays of light. When it blazes or radiates light, one alone will become a vidyādhara. Through its blazing one will become a king of vidyādharas. Unimpeded, one overpowers the strength of adversaries, and whoever sees one, in whosever presence one is seen, will gain faith.

Then, if one wishes to accomplish the Vajradhara attainment, one should take a vajra of wrought iron sixteen finger-widths long, three-pronged at both ends, and smear it with red sandalwood. Beginning on the first day of the month of miracles, one should make vast offerings before the painting and recite the mantra each day. On the full moon of the month of Caitra, one should make vast offerings, light a full hundred butter lamps, and sit close beside a bundle of kuśa grass. Holding the vajra with both hands, one should recite until it blazes; by holding it, one together with one's retinue will attain the state of a universal monarch of the vidyādharas. In the same way, one should also accomplish the rites of other ritual manuals for weapons such as the wheel and the spear, and for garments, sandals, the sacred thread, and the like. As before, the three kinds of accomplishment will arise.

Then, if one wishes to accomplish wealth, one should smear kindling sticks of khadira wood with curd and butter and make eight thousand burnt offerings; wealth will then be bestowed. If one goes to the bank of a river that flows into the ocean and sends eighty thousand lotuses anointed with sandalwood into the river, one will obtain wealth equal to the heap of lotuses.

If one wishes to bring the gods under one's control, one should anoint kindling sticks of aloeswood with curd, honey, and butter and make burnt offerings; they will then come under one's control. If one burns rice anointed with those three, the mantra will become inexhaustible.

If one wishes to bring the yakṣas under one's control, one should make burnt offerings of pills of guggulu mixed with the three sweet substances; they will then come under one's control. With kindling sticks of aśoka wood, the yakṣiṇīs will come under one's control. If one makes burnt offerings of white mustard seed for a king, the king will come under one's control.

If one goes to the bank of a river that flows into the ocean and makes a fire offering of one hundred thousand lotuses, Śrī, the goddess of fortune, will come and bestow wealth, or will grant the kingship of a single region. Then, if one offers three hundred thousand, one will become lord over all the lands. If one offers five hundred thousand, one will become lord of Jambudvīpa.

If one places the painting at the mouth of an asura cave and recites three hundred thousand times, all mantras will arise. If one enters without hesitation, the elixir of rejuvenation and the like will be obtained. Then, if one remains right there, the terror of Viṣṇu's discus will come about.

Beginning on the first day of the waxing fortnight, one should touch the toe of the Blessed One with eight thousand jasmine flowers. If one keeps pressing them on until light rays issue from the big toe of the foot, the practitioner's body will instantly become invisible, and his hair will become coiled matted locks. Together with his retinue he will attain the state of a king of vidyādharas and will remain for an eon.

One should draw an eight-petaled lotus on the ground and, seated upon it, recite one hundred thousand times; then, remaining right in that place, dwelling upon a lotus and possessing a retinue of a thousand, one will remain for an eon.

Then, if one wishes to extract a treasure, one should take a vase wound with black thread, smear it with sandalwood, fill it with water, consecrate it with eight thousand recitations, and set it down at the place where the treasure lies. When the ground there cracks open, one should sprinkle water on the ground, dig down about a cubit, and take the treasure.

Then, to accomplish the lion, one should make a lion out of earth from an anthill, smear it with gorocanā, and place it in a maṇḍala. Having made great offerings, one should recite until it moves. It will become a lion, and by mounting its back one's hair will curl into coils the color of bell metal; one will gain foreknowledge and will live for ten thousand years.

Mañjuśrī, if this rite taught in the ritual manual of noble Acala is practiced, it will bring swift results."

When the Blessed One had spoken thus, that entire assembly, complete in every respect, praised what the Blessed One had taught, rejoiced in it, rose from their seats, and departed.

This concludes the ritual manual called The Noble Great King of Wrathful Ones Acala, Taught to Tame the Valiant through the Immeasurable Power of All Tathāgatas.

At the command of the king of Minyak, the translator Lan Gelong Darma Lodrö translated and edited this from the presence of the Indian preceptor Ulaśamarakṣita. It was requested by the monk Yönten Dorjé.

Later, as urged by the command of the Dharma king Tashi Rabten Palzangpo, the translator, the monk Chökyong Zangpo, carefully checked it against an Indian manuscript and established the text. And again, during the lifetime of the great lord of men Tashi Rabten Sokpo Pak, the monk Ānandamaṅgalaṃ, at the temple of Palkhor Dechen, checked it against an Indian manuscript from the monastery of Vikramaśīla, properly corrected whatever could not be left uncorrected, and made it pure.

Translator's notes

"Various Array," "Bearer of Sky Flowers" and "Utterly Pure" are bodhisattva names I could not identify with confidence; they are translated by meaning. "Adorned with the Essence of the Flower Ground" appears to correspond to the world system Kusumatalagarbhālaṃkṛta, but I have kept a translation of the Tibetan. "Dwelling upon the wind" renders the Tibetan literally. The name of the samādhi, rendered "Universal Arising of Flames that Burn the Vajra-Arrogant," is uncertain in its exact sense. The verse is attributed in the Tibetan to the bodhisattvas of the other world systems, though its content seems addressed to them; I have followed the Tibetan as it stands. "Dazed" renders a Tibetan expression for being stupefied or overpowered. The verse beginning "This, which those who cannot hear it" is terse and its first two lines are construed with some uncertainty. ཤཱཀྱའི་རིགས་དགའ་བ is taken as an epithet of the Buddha, "the Delight of the Śākya Clan." The line ཟད་པར་ས་ལ་བཞུགས་ནས་སུ ("dwelling on the ground of exhaustion") and the following "acts for beings without deliberation" (མ་བསམ་བྱེད) are obscure; the rendering is literal. ལེགས་སྦྱར ("well composed") may refer to the Sanskrit language (saṃskṛta) rather than to good composition generally. The line གཞན་དུ་འབའ་ཞིག་པར་འགྱུར་རོ ("otherwise it remains something merely apart") is obscure and rendered literally. ཡེ་ཤེས་གོ་འཕང་ངོ་རྣམས་རྟོག is unclear (ངོ་རྣམས possibly for ངོ་བོ, "nature"). བསྙེན་པར་གྱུར་ན་འབྲས་བུ་ཆུང is rendered literally; it may mean that approach practice alone yields a small fruit. རྡོ་རྗེའི་སྐབས་སུ་རྟོལ་བ་བླངས་ཏེ is uncertain. In Vajrapāṇi's verses, several lines ending in གསུངས are taken as requests, following the surrounding གསུང་དུ་གསོལ and བཤད་དུ་གསོལ. དབང་པོའི་གནས་བཅུ་བདག་པོ ("the lords of Indra's ten abodes") is unclear and rendered literally; it may refer to the guardians of the ten directions. སངས་རྒྱས་སྤྱན ("the Buddha Eye") is also the name of the goddess Buddhalocanā, but here it is presented as a name for the rite. "The Immovable ground" (མི་གཡོ་བའི་ས) is the eighth bodhisattva ground, Acalā, which the text plays on in explaining the name Acala. གསོ་སྦྱོང་མཛད་པ, applied to malevolent grahas, is rendered with the glossary sense "restoration"; it may simply mean that the mantra restores and purifies them. ལྕགས་ནི་ཤིང་ཕྱེ་ཐུལ་བ་ལྟར ("like sawdust beaten down by iron") is obscure. བུ་གཅིག་འདི་ནི་ཇི་ལྟར་འགྱུར ("What will become of this only son?") is rendered literally; its reference is unclear. In Vajrapāṇi's plea, སེམས་ཅན་དོན་དུ་མ་བསམས་པས and the moth simile (དྲག་ཏུ་བོད་པའི་ལུང་ཐོབ་པ) are uncertain. In the closing prose, ཤིན་ཏུ་རྒྱ་ཚ་བུ is not a known expression and is taken as རྒྱ་ཆེ་བ ("far-reaching"); the syntax of that sentence is also uncertain. Several lines are obscure and rendered literally: རང་གི་ལུས་ལ་སེམས་བཅུག་པས ("by bringing their minds back into their own bodies," apparently recovering from the swoon of the previous scene), ས་སྟེངས་འབྱུང་པོ་རང་སེམས་གནས, the attendant term རྩོལ, and སྐྱེས་པས་ཡང་དག་བསྟན་པ ("what he truly taught upon taking birth"). In the opening request, གསོ་སྦྱོང is here taken in its literal sense of healing and cleansing diseases, not as the poṣadha ceremony, since its object is "all diseases." The ailment མཚན་བར་རྡོལ་བ is uncertain; it is rendered "eruptions that break out at night." In the description of the master's body, སློབ་པ ("to train") makes no sense and is read as སླེབས་པ, "reaching": the hands reach the knees. In the line སེམས་ཅན་སྙོམ་མེད, སྙོམ is taken as indolence (སྙོམ་ལས), which is uncertain. The line ཡིད་ཉིད་ཀྱིས་ནི་གཤེགས་སུ་གསོལ is taken in its usual ritual sense of dismissing the deity after worship, which fits the following steps of binding the directions and invoking the deity again; it could also be read as an invitation to come. In the northeast verses, བྱམས་པ ("loving-kindness") may instead refer to Maitreya, whose emblem is the water flask, though the verse names Samantabhadra. The compass directions are given as the Tibetan names them, and their order is that of the text. The last line breaks off mid-sentence and continues in the next piece. Several names in the lists could not be identified with confidence and are translated by meaning: "the fierce and mighty one with the clenched fist" (དྲག་ཤུལ་དབང་ཆེན་ཆངས་བུ་ཅན), the sages "Protector of Beings" (འགྲོ་སྐྱོང) and "Shunner of Evil" (ངན་སྤོང, also the guardian of the southern gate), "Space" (ནམ་མཁའ) among the goddesses, "the Black One" (ནག་པོ) at the eastern gate, and "Delight Maker" (དགའ་བྱེད) at the northern gate. The line ཀུན་དགའ་བོ་ནི་དགའ་ཞེས་བཤད is read as naming Ānanda and Nanda; it could instead mean "Ānanda, who is called Joyful." The meaning of the closing line, དམ་པའི་ཆོས་ནི་གནས་མེད་དོ, is unclear, and its sense may be completed in the following piece. མངོན་གསུངས་བཞིན་དུ ("as if directly taught") is read as the evident མངོན་སུམ་བཞིན་དུ, "as if directly present," which the text itself uses a few lines later. ཟུང་ཅིག་པར is taken as the imperative "hold!" (བཟུང). Several lines in the offering rite are elliptical: the line naming "the Protector of the World" and the line about the "lord of the offering" (མཆོད་སྦྱིན་བདག་ཉིད) are rendered as literally as the sense allows, and their exact reference is uncertain. The opening address, རྡོ་རྗེ་དག་ནི་ཆེན་པོ་བྱུང, could also be narrative ("the great vajra ones arose"). In the list of what is denied, གཙོ་བོ is taken as the Sāṃkhya primal matter (pradhāna), and ཕྲ་བ and གསལ་བ as the subtle and manifest states of that system. The line འདོད་མེད་འབའ་ཞིག་ལས་བྱུང་བ ("arising only from the absence of desire") is unclear as written. The precepts "ten, eight, and then five" are probably the novice, eight-limbed fasting, and lay precepts. The phrase གདོས་ཆད་པས་བཅིང is taken as bondage under punishment. "Humbly submitting one's own body" renders the obscure phrase "pressing the ears of one's own body" (rang gi lus ni snyan mnan), which seems to denote a gesture of submission; the sense is uncertain. "The son of Vinatā" takes the Tibetan "son of the tamed one" (dul ba'i sras po) as a rendering of Vinatā (literally "bowed, humble"), mother of Garuḍa; this identification is tentative. "Lordship" (dbang phyug gi go 'phang) is taken here as a state of sovereignty, not the god Īśvara. The opening lines seem to describe disposing of the disciple's garments or the ritual materials in a river; the object is unstated in the Tibetan. "With the hand of the guhyaka" renders gsang ba pa'i lag pa, perhaps meaning Vajrapāṇi's hand gesture; the sense is uncertain. "Attentive, not wavering for even an instant" renders the obscure tsham ngam skad cig mi g.yo bar; the first word is unclear. The Sanskrit word paryaṃkāśīn, kept in Tibetan letters, means "seated cross-legged".
