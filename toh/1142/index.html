About this text

The Praise of the Thirty-Five Sugatas, Adorned with Their Precious Names, known in Sanskrit as the Sugatapañcatriṃśatratnanāmamālāstotra, is a devotional hymn in verse honoring the thirty-five buddhas traditionally invoked in practices of confession and purification. After opening with homage and refuge in the Buddha, Dharma, and Saṅgha, it devotes a verse to each buddha in turn. It begins with Śākyamuni, who awakened through fierce diligence in an age of degeneration, then moves to figures such as Vajrapramardin, who crushed the hosts of Māra, and Ratnārciṣ, whose radiant light soothes the sufferings of the lower realms. Each stanza links a buddha's name to a quality or deed, so the poem works both as praise and as a way of contemplating these awakened beings. The work is Toh 1142 in the Tengyur, where it belongs to the collection of praises, the class of hymns and eulogies addressed to buddhas and other objects of devotion.

Translation

In Sanskrit: sugata pañcatriṃśata ratna nāma māla stotraṃ
In Tibetan: The Praise of the Thirty-Five Sugatas, Adorned with Their Precious Names.

Homage to the Three Jewels.

I bow to the Buddha, the chief;
I bow to the Dharma that protects;
I bow to the great Saṅgha;
In these three I always take refuge.

In this age of strife with its five degenerations,
So hard for all the buddhas to tame,
You awakened through fierce diligence:
I bow to Śākyamuni.

Through vajra-like concentration
You attained the essence, supreme awakening;
With the vajra essence you utterly crushed
The hosts of Māra: I bow to Vajrapramardin.

The light streaming from your precious body
Calms the sufferings of the lower realms
And fills the māras with terror:
I bow to Ratnārciṣ.

Just as the great lord of the nāgas
Satisfies the continents with rain,
You rain the Dharma on the trichiliocosm:
I bow to Nāgeśvararāja.

Hero, through this teaching of yours
You terrify the māras and the non-Buddhists (tīrthikas),
And you set those to be trained in omniscience:
I bow to Vīrasena.

Having cultivated with compassion the good of others,
When you gave away your own body and life
You rejoiced like a starving man revived:
To Vīranandin as well I bow.

You make the meaning clear to the learned,
You ripen the mindstreams of those of middling capacity,
And you burn away the afflictions of the lowest:
I bow to Ratnāgni.

Equal in fortune to all the buddhas,
You were born lighting up the directions
With the radiance of Dharma wisdom:
I bow to Ratnacandraprabha.

One never tires of beholding your body,
Ablaze with light a fathom wide;
For the fortunate who see you,
Seeing bears fruit: I bow to Amoghadarśin.

Your body, speech and mind are the supreme jewel;
Awakened, stainless, free of elaboration,
You dispel the torment of afflictions in all beings:
I bow to Ratnacandra.

With the stainless water of the true Dharma
First you purified yourself,
Then you supremely purified others:
I bow to Vimala.

That the accumulations of merit one has made
Be wholly dedicated to all sentient beings
Is what you, the Sage, teach:
To Śrīdatta as well I bow.

The brahmins hold that one endowed
With knowledge and conduct is Brahmā;
That knowledge and conduct are yours:
I bow to the one named Brahmā.

You fully cultivated the four feet of Brahmā,
You attained the supreme fruit of the holy life,
And Brahmā urged you to turn the wheel:
I bow to Brahmadatta.

Your mind made pure
The austerities and vows rightly undertaken;
You wash away afflictions and wrongdoing:
I bow to Varuṇa, god of the waters.

Worldly people worship Śaṅkara (Śiva);
Śaṅkara worships the great nāga Anavatapta;
And since that water god too worships you,
I bow to Varuṇadeva, god of the water gods.

Worshipped by all the guides,
You, peerless and utterly at peace,
Bring glory to all beings:
To Śrībhadra as well I bow.

All the afflictions that assail you
And seek to do you harm
You cool, as sandalwood applied cools:
I bow to Candanaśrī.

The glory and splendor of the Victor
No one can bear to rival,
As the eye cannot rival the disk of the sun:
I bow to Anantaujas.

Through the light rays streaming from the Sage
You calm those swollen with pride
And give heart to the lowly:
To Prabhāsaśrī as well I bow.

How wonderful! Peace beyond sorrow,
O Sugata, one like you, compassion itself,
Has gained mastery over it:
I bow to Aśokaśrī.

You made yourself familiar with freedom from craving
And became supreme among all guides,
Outshining all the mighty:
I bow to Nārāyaṇa.

Endowed with the best of every aspect,
You outshine all other beauty,
Flourishing with major and minor marks:
I bow to Kusumaśrī.

With Brahmā's voice and with light rays
You pervade every world:
I bow to Brahmajyotirvikrīḍitābhijña,
Who knows directly through the play of Brahmā's light.

Whoever your light touches
Feels unhindered bliss expand:
I bow to Padmajyotirvikrīḍitābhijña,
Who knows directly through the play of lotus light.

With the Sage's light rays of merit
You satisfy beings with material things,
And above all you give the wealth of the noble ones:
To Dhanaśrī as well I bow.

Having gained mastery over mindfulness,
You know fully and without obstruction;
Whoever remembers you is freed from want:
I bow to Smṛtiśrī.

The Sage turned the wheel of Dharma;
The banner of his fame resounds through the three realms;
The glory of a name of countless qualities
Is proclaimed far and wide: I bow to Suparikīrtitanāmaśrī.

Having attained the supreme powers and faculties,
You benefit even those on the dark side;
Indra bows his crown to you, banner of Indra:
I bow to Indraketudhvajarāja.

The logicians who proclaim things as real
You utterly overpower with the vajra of subtle wisdom,
As a lion overpowers an elephant:
I bow to Suvikrāntaśrī.

Having conquered the hosts of afflictions, your own and others',
There is no supreme hero other than you;
You are victorious in battle over the Lord of Desire:
I bow to Suvijitasaṃgrāma.

On the great path of the single way
You act as guide for all beings,
Freeing them from fear of the māras and the lower realms:
I bow to Vikrāntagāmin.

Through the wondrous miracles of the Sage
You send forth countless tens of millions of emanations,
Appearing to all who are to be trained:
I bow to Samantāvabhāsavyūhaśrī.

The non-Buddhists turn back from liberation,
The śrāvakas incline to peace alone,
But you tread the supreme precious grounds
As if on lotuses: I bow to Ratnapadmavikrāmin.

The jewel arising from the accumulation of merit,
The lotus seat of the perfected accumulation of wisdom,
On which the three bodies, spontaneously present, are firmly seated:
I bow to Ratnapadmasupratiṣṭhitaśailendrarāja.

By reciting this praise adorned with the precious names
Of the thirty-five sugatas,
May my conduct, too, become
Just like the conduct of the Lord of Sages.

This completes The Praise of the Thirty-Five Sugatas, Adorned with Their Precious Names, composed by the master Mātṛceṭa.

Translator's notes

Each stanza plays on the name of one of the thirty-five buddhas of confession. I have given the standard Sanskrit names, which hides the wordplay of the Tibetan. The sixth name is spelled "dpal dgyes" here rather than the usual "dpa' dgyes". I have kept the standard Vīranandin.

In stanza thirty, "tog gi pa" is read as "rtog ge pa," meaning logicians. In stanza eighteen, "spangs pa" does not fit the sandalwood simile, so it is taken in the evident sense of "applied" sandalwood.

The "four feet of Brahmā" are the four immeasurables (brahmavihāras). "Knowledge and conduct" renders the epithet vidyācaraṇasampanna. "Conduct" is literally "feet," which sets up the pun in the next stanza. The "Lord of Desire" is Māra.
