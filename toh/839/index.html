About this text

The Great Tantra called "The Unsurpassed Secret Intent of All the Tathāgatas, the Play of the Supreme Horse" (Tibetan: *de bzhin gshegs pa thams cad kyi dgongs pa bla na med pa gsang ba rta mchog rol pa'i rgyud chen po*) is a Buddhist tantra whose Sanskrit title appears in the text in a partly corrupt transliteration. It is a scripture of the Vajrayāna, opening with homage to Vajrasattva and then setting its teaching in the voice of the Blessed One as Vajra of Great Bliss, the source of all buddhas, who dwells in a richly adorned maṇḍala. The title points to the Hayagrīva, or "horse-necked," tradition of wrathful practice, here concerned with the secret intent of the tathāgatas and its expression as a kind of divine play. The text is Toh 839 in the Kangyur, belonging to the tantra section, where it is classed among the Great Tantras.

Translation

In the language of India: Sarva Tathāgata Buddhotra Punar Guhya Vanośi Aśvadda, Mahātantra nāma. In the language of Tibet: The Great Tantra called "The Unsurpassed Secret Intent of All the Tathāgatas, the Play of the Supreme Horse."

Homage to glorious Vajrasattva.

Thus did I hear at one time. The Blessed One, Vajra of Great Bliss, the source of all the buddhas of the ten directions and the three times, whose body, speech, mind, qualities, and activities are the vajra secret, surpassing all others, adorned with the full perfection of the major and minor marks, dwelt in a maṇḍala of ornamental array, piled up like Mount Meru, a source of precious qualities, whose very nature shines in manifold forms. He makes clear every profound and vast aspect, brings all phenomena to their utmost completion through the assembly of wisdoms, and is the very self whose activity is vastly and universally displayed.

He is glorious Bliss-Maker, Lotus Lord of Dance, who has attained the absorption of great passion and, through the unobstructed nature of reality, bestows empowerment upon all. This very place, where all the deities gather, had become a celestial palace. In Akaniṣṭha, the abode of the Vajra Mountain, a splendid mansion blazed with the radiance of manifold precious wisdom. Pleasing forms of many kinds, fragrances of many kinds, delicious tastes of many kinds, soft and gentle touches of many kinds, and many kinds of the bliss that arises from the Dharma, all objects of desire, radiated into the ten directions. The palace, densely arrayed, with lofty and spacious pinnacles, stood with four doors and their gatehouses distinctly formed.

At that time there were present the great Sugatas of the play: Universal Illumination, Glory of Light, Lord of Joy, Display of Miracles, All-Seeing, Immovable Vajra, Manifest Radiance, Lord of Stability, Thunder-Voice, Immaculate, Heap of Jewels, Treasury of Space, Jewel Net, Jewel Name, Jewel Crest, Radiant Light, Amitāyus, Lord of Dharma, Unattached Light, Joyful Eye, Accomplisher of All Aims, Powerful Diligence, Great Deed of Miracle, Subduer of Yakṣas, and Lord of Variety.

Moreover there were bodhisattvas, great beings: Blissful Intelligence, Ocean-Voice, Splendid Array, Sharp Wheel, Radiant Knowledge, Vajra Banner, Radiating Light, Fierce Power, Immaculate Being, Magical Emanation, Jewel King, Net-Holder, Excellent Hand, Radiating Golden Light, Jewel Sheaf, Dharma Height, Immaculate One, Dispeller of Darkness, Joyful Melody, Jewel Youth, Vajra Subduer, Dancer, Ocean of Delight, Unobstructed Array, Lotus of Joy, Lord of Rice-Seedlings, Jewel Heap, Garland of Clouds, Lovely Flower, Lamp of Wisdom, Pile of Eloquence, Transformer of the Wheel, Lord of Mindfulness, Lotus Intelligence, Power of Activity, and Essence of Intelligence. All of these dwelt in the manner of being accompanied by their own assemblies of consorts.

Moreover, Radiating Lotus, Lotus Holder, Lotus Resounder, Lotus Mover, Lotus Satisfier, Lotus Scatterer, Lotus Illuminator, and Lotus Empowerer were also arrayed, each by his own activity. Moreover there were the Great Terrifier, Lord of Loving-Kindness, Lord of Tenderness, Lord of Lotus Liberation, Lord of Liberation, and Lord of Generosity, and

Lords of Conduct, Lords of Speech, and Lords of Harmony were likewise seated, each with his own consort of awareness. Together with these, and with an assembly of the retinue, there dwelt twelve hundred thousand buddhas and bodhisattvas, who appeared both as distinguished and as undistinguished.

Then the Blessed One entered the meditative absorption called Lotus-Attached Eye, the king of absorptions. From the Śrīvatsa mark upon his heart, and from the other places of his body, he sent forth a hundred thousand maṇḍalas of radiant light. These illuminated all the world-systems of the ten directions, and eighteen great and wondrous signs appeared.

Then the vessel of the world and its contents blazed forth in clear radiance. The Blessed One roused the commitments of the buddhas and bodhisattvas of the ten directions, and they in turn, through the great seal called Arrayed Miraculous Display, caused the gathering to unfold within the courtyards of his wheel. This purified the buddha-fields, and the retinue, together with its own retinue, gathered once more into the place where the light rays radiated. As he sat there, delighting in the vast and wondrous play of such a teaching, and smiling, doubt arose in the entire assembly.

At that time they spoke together in harmony:

Kyema! Ho! Wondrous Dharma of many kinds!
How great are the wonders of the Victor's deeds!
What indeed is the purpose
Of such immeasurable miraculous displays?
Without cause or condition, the buddhas
Do not set forth the commitments of the three secrets.
These signs, too, have appeared;
Pray, urge the Lord of Secrets to speak.
You are the son of all the Victors,
A holder of the ocean of their intent.
Master of the Word, consider this;
Entreat him well, and make your request.

Then the Lord of Secrets, Vajrapāṇi, with his five hundred companions, rose from his seat before the Blessed One, in order to cut off the doubts of the others and to make his own gateway clear. He arranged vast clouds of offerings from the ten directions and made offerings to all the Victors. He circumambulated one hundred and eight times, bowed with single-pointed devotion, and with a joyful countenance made this request:

E ma! The very body of the Victor,
The maṇḍalas of miraculous display,
Arise from the abode of absorption and wisdom.
The joyous maṇḍala gathers together,
And with a great rain-cloud of awakening mind
Purifies the afflictions and the rest.
Through the wisdom of great compassion
You bring the fruit of the accumulations to perfect ripening.
With radiant body and joyful face,
Pray, teach the secret vehicle.
Your intent is hard to fathom,
Being profound, subtle, and vast.
The assembly is weighed down by the mountain of doubt;
You who possess great compassion, make it clear.
All of this is the vessel of the teaching.
Protector, you who are spontaneously established in equanimity,
This undertaking at this time is of great meaning;
Bring the fruit of aspiration to its completion.

When he had made this request, the Blessed One, the Vajra Holder himself, regarded through the gateway of single-pointed equanimity of mind all the maṇḍalas, both stable and moving, of the great thousand of the three thousand. He looked, and looked upon all. Having thus considered, he said: "Let all these maṇḍalas, which are beyond speech and thought, be expressed in many ways." Then, beholding the very thing itself in its real state, he rested in the meditative absorption of equanimity toward the limit of seeing, the limit of intention, and the limit of activity. He did not set forth, through arrangement, ordered arrangement, exposition, intention, thorough intention, play, great play, activity, or manifold activity,

These objects of the great thousand of the three thousand, gathered together in the aspect of the six worlds, he did not consider to appear as anything other than this maṇḍala of the buddhas. Nor did he look upon the time, the place, the distinctions of place, or the arrangements of the maṇḍalas. He did not look upon them all, yet he did not obstruct his intention either. They do not abide even at the limit, and he rested like space. Then, the moment he entered that meditative absorption, those maṇḍalas of the retinue also came to be realized as being, like space, the nature of the maṇḍala of all phenomena.

Then again the Lord of Secrets, Vajrapāṇi, urged him on through wonder, and stirred the minds of others toward their benefit, arousing him with a song:

Great king of the meaning of the way,
since you are the very continuum of all guides,
since you are the very continuum like space,
from the maṇḍala that is like space
reveal the wonder of great compassion.
Alas, protector, in whom all aims abide,
you who engage in the sacred work of peace,
the path of the bodhisattvas' compassion, free of moisture,
for those who are dry and parched, may the Dharma rain down.

Though he was petitioned in this way, he remained silent and said nothing at all.

Then once more the consort named Radiant Lotus urged him on with a song that rouses one from the absorption free of concept:

Non-apprehending absorption, beyond thought, free of expression,
great bliss, wisdom, the awakening mind:
by the unfailing pledge of non-apprehending compassion,
please reveal the vast display of your play.

After he had been urged on in this way, the protector considered the places of the protectors. He smiled, arranging rays of light, and the retinue, Good Giver, opened the occasion:

Excellent, excellent, holder of the vajra,
together with your companion who holds the lotus!
Since you urge me again and again,
to grant and to teach,
with a joyful and gentle gaze,
bring to peace whatever realization you desire.
All the realizations of the future
are grasped by the force of compassion.
By the light that pervades all meanings,
open the occasion for what is to be grasped.

Thus he spoke.

This is the first chapter, the chapter of manifestation, from the great tantra, the Play of the Supreme Horse, the unsurpassed secret intention of all the Tathāgatas.

Then, with joy, the retinue assembled,
and made their joyful faces bloom.
Just as the chief had declared,
they held the maṇḍala arising from compassion.
Then the lotus-bearer with radiant sight,
her smile blossoming, urged him on,
and with embraces, giving of milk, and the like,
all became joyful and passionate.

Ah, you, king of knowledge,
great compassion like the light of the sun,
hold the maṇḍala that arises from compassion.
Opening the way without partiality,
you comprehend all that becomes an object.
From the space of non-apprehending wonder,
arrangements like oceans and masses of clouds:
with the great rain of compassionate emanation,
purify the manifold eight activities.

Then the chief, the Vajra Holder,
rose from his meditative absorption.
Adorned with his own consort,
a king, overpowering and majestic,
he turned the wheel of his hands,
and looked into all directions.
In the pure expanse of non-apprehending space,
a vajra emanation body appeared,
and together with the great radiant Lotus
he sat in the manner of play.
With smiling eyes and faces full of desire,
he spoke the mantra that rouses the retinue.

Oṃ āḥ hūṃ mahā bhasmi śakuru vina hṛdaya aṃ bhava hi ala la ho.

With the non-dual awakening mind,
join and gather the maṇḍala.
The complete body of the accumulations of merit and wisdom
performs the play of the vajra dance.
The supreme joy, the vajra awakening mind,
rests in the smiling, coiling abode of the lotus.
Its nature is pure, brilliant white;
in the red, there is empowerment and non-duality,
radiant and pervasive, issuing forth everywhere.

From the emanated maṇḍala there issued forth limitless manifestations. Pacification, cessation, nirvāṇa, the protector of awakening, the abiding, the path: all such names he joined in non-duality, and he arranged them in the abode of his own maṇḍala.

Wondrous emanations played forth in full. With majesty he danced. He smiled and he played; he rose up on every side, and his lovely eyes gazed forth. He moved his long brows and transformed them; with the vajra dagger he stirred and roused them. He cast away, he gathered in, he set down, he sprang up. By dances of every manner of play he emanated the manifold activities of the families, and made them pervade the whole of space.

From within the bliss that arises in this play, the awakening mind and all else become peaceful and clear. When the very rain of play came down visibly, in the ten directions of the six worlds, all that is stable and all that moves, without remainder, the buddha-fields were purified. Then the gathering of awakening minds, in the play of striking, embracing, and restraining, joined, dissolved, and delighted; and the drop of wisdom, supremely joyful on every side, shone as the light of the sun in manifold appearances. Let it abide in true bliss.

This was the great gathering of assembled ones. From all the ten directions they received it, and with a ceaseless stream they made offerings of pure bodhisattvas blessed by means and wisdom. They sounded drums and cymbals in sweet sound; there were the pleasures of play that satisfy the senses, and dance, song, and the six kinds of music in every variety, filling the three thousand worlds with their voice. Thus the Blessed One, the Lotus, bliss and yet greater bliss, was honored together with his radiant consort.

Eḥ ma! From the midst of the assembled gathering, he of great compassion, like the orb of the sun, the Turner of all wheels, brought liberation, and so expanded his own maṇḍala. Through the supplication of Vajradhara, spontaneous perfection was complete in the maṇḍalas.

"Glorious Lotus, great being, purifier of all abodes of wisdom and activity: your feet arise through the power of compassion. You are the supreme wisdom of suchness, purifying the three realms without remainder. Protector, your kindness is excellent."

Thus he was entreated, and was pleased. He rested in the state of the great seal (mahāmudrā).

Then the joyful awakening mind, together with the bodhisattvas emanated from the former tantra and the four paths, and the companions of the path of the former tantra, took thought, and with the wisdom-mind of compassion, abiding in their nature, understood all. From the four directions they urged him on.

"Kyai kyai! Embodiment of the Three Jewels in bliss, you who hold the mind of loving-kindness and make the body radiant; Vajra King, awakening mind, you who think of me and accomplish the great aim of beings.

Kyai kyai! Gathering of jewel-like qualities, great wisdom of compassion and equality, King, Wheel, awakening itself, treasury of emanated manifold maṇḍalas.

Kyai kyai! Perfectly pure body, source from which an ocean of joy arises, discriminating the manifold individual wisdoms, suchness of all phenomena.

Kyai kyai! Great self of the aim of beings, who does not abandon equanimity yet casts away the manifold, supreme samādhi of accomplished action, showing the pure maṇḍalas of the ten directions."

By the joyful contracting, he moved; in the four directions he entered the joyful abode of awakening.

"Alas, beings! Suchness itself, through the magical display of the awakening mind, is blocked in its own limit and path by afflictions, the duality of grasped and grasper, and obscurations. These are fit to be places of compassion. By many means of luminous wisdom, liberate them all at once, and show your own great bliss, the supreme meaning."

Thus spoke the Lotus Vidyādhara, and the buddhas with their sons were delighted.

This is the second chapter, the chapter on the play of emanated compassion, from the Glorious Supreme Horse Play Tantra, the secret and unsurpassed intent of all the Tathāgatas.

Then the chief of the bodhisattvas, Vajradhara, rose from his seat, and began to speak in praise of the Sugata.

Bowing low, he made his request in melodious song:

Kyema ho! Three Jewels, body of the sacred pledge,
Lord of unsurpassed wisdom,
Very mandala of the awakening mind,
Now is the time of the play of emanated compassion.
Boundless are the bodies of the wheel of the families.
Today all auspiciousness is perfectly gathered.
If great compassion were cast aside and abandoned,
Why then would the supreme Great Vehicle be taught as mantra?
Having heard these words of rightful request,
Look upon the mandala of compassion.

The Protector, the very body of the Lotus,
Plays with his radiant consort,
Beholds the directions with stainless wisdom,
And the mandala of nondual awakening mind shines forth.
This path, from which the great secret vehicle arises,
Is the glorious supreme play.
He gathers the buddhas with their sons,
Shakes the assemblies of the abodes of the other emanations,
Moves through these three thousand worlds,
And enters into meditative absorption.
From his body he sends forth rays of light.
Pervaded by them, the mandala of the families shines clear.
The bodies of the heroes, in the radiance of wisdom light,
He sets in the five places with secret mantra.
The Great Seal, the perfectly complete body,
Has three faces and six arms, in a radiance of delight,
Bright red, with the glow of lotus-ruby.
His right and left faces are blue and yellow.
He is adorned with all the ornaments and marks of the family.
In his hands he holds a lotus, a bouquet, a vase,
A five-pointed vajra, a śrīvatsa,
And, with the last two, an arrow and a bow.
He wears garments of many kinds of jewels,
And plays together with his consort, the Seal.

The celestial palace has four gates,
With a many-jeweled gateway and entablature,
Lattices, half-lattices, strings of pearls,
Banners, and bells, and other adornments.
In the central mandala, the greatly exalted
Palace, its pillars and beams,
Are adorned with lotuses, vajras, and so forth,
A perfect gathering of desirable qualities.
In all the abodes are jeweled thrones,
Marked with lotus, wheel, sun, and moon.
Outside, the surrounding courtyard,
And in all the vessels, upon the lotus,
Distinguished as method and wisdom,
Sun and moon stand alone.

Then all the pure lotuses
Are seated upon the central throne,
And by the power of the stirring of the song and dance of joy
They bring method and wisdom to completion.
At that time the supreme mind, awakening mind,
Being utterly purified like the expanse of the sky,
The body of awareness and great bliss
Arises in manifold and varied forms.
His speech itself is a gathering of wheels of letters,
Blazing with rays of five-pointed light.
In due sequence, the limbs of the ritual,
The signs of the mind, the sacred pledges are performed,
Together with mantra, in the manner of emanating and gathering.
The king, the Sugata with his consort,
The bodhisattvas and the mind-born goddesses,
And the wrathful ones who perform activity, with their consorts,
Having become the body of the Great Seal,
Are beautified by the signs of the garland of the Victors of the families.
Dwelling in their own mandalas,
They sit in the ranks of the vajra secret.

At that time the Protector of great bliss,
Called Lord of All Families,
Brings forth the great mandala
Of the emanation of lotuses.
From the bodhisattva's Kam, emanated,
The Sugata, body of radiant light,
With the ornaments of face, hands, and so forth,
Exactly in the form of the chief deity,
Is joined with his consort in his own light.
To the auspicious, the possessor of marks, in bliss,
By saying "Rāga Padma Hūṃ,"
The supreme mantra of eight syllables
Brings about the accomplishment of all activities.
If to any lotus one adds
"Śāri" and "Buddha Hūṃ,"
Then pacifying, enriching, empowering, wrathful, and all activities,
And the assemblies of the eight great accomplishments,
Come to be accomplished by mere application,
And long remain.
At the time of emanating, to the secret mantra
One attaches the letter Jaḥ,
And joins the mandala of the families of emanation.
The lord of the family dwells joyfully in his abode.
Rouse him with songs and mantras that stir compassion,
And with the gathering of light rays.

Through the power of the mantra that reveals the three samayas,
Joy and attachment grow firm, and radiance is possessed.
Oṃ: the pure lotus, the realm of bliss,
The supreme one made clear through the blessing of wisdom.
The maṇḍala, the three secrets, the great samaya,
The equality, the lord of the family, the great sage.
Because the blessing is never abandoned,
The body, speech, and mind of all the buddhas,
The body that illumines and is without duality,
Abides in its own essential nature.

Rouse the chief figure with the mantra,
And gather the light rays with their ornaments into the heart.
From the letter Ā up to Aḥ,
The sixteen letters send forth rays of light.
Thereby all beings are arrayed as the body of a buddha,
And all the maṇḍalas of the corresponding families become radiant.
The light rays of the mantra, together with its sound,
Establish the sixteen phenomena, the pure realm,
Each in its own place,
And the thirty-two supreme major marks are completed.

In the same way, from Ka up to Kṣa,
The thirty-four become clear in their proper order,
Together with the six letters of the reflected image.
All the rays that radiate are the body of a buddha.
Having made the maṇḍala of wisdom clear,
Together with the light and sound of the mantra,
The many phenomena, the undefiled wisdom,
Again enter the abode of the body,
And the eighty excellent minor marks are completed.
Moreover, it is adorned with all things,
The seven precious possessions, the eight auspicious signs, and the rest.

From Khaṃ arises the great and boundless emanation,
One-faced, four-armed, with a radiance of white and red.
He holds a lotus and other things, and a sword,
Accomplished by the ornament of the radiance of the Dharma.
He is Padmālaṅkāra, of the middling type, supreme in his nature,
And he abides in the eastern maṇḍala.

From Gaṃ arises the emanation, the Sovereign of the Dharma,
Red-yellow, one-faced, two-armed,
Holding a lotus rosary and a vase.
He abides in his place in the maṇḍala.

From Ghaṃ arises the emanation, the Worthy One without Attachment,
One-faced, two-armed, with the glow of coral,
Seated on a lotus, holding a book and a mirror.
He abides in the western maṇḍala.

From Ṅaṃ arises the emanation, the Eyed One,
One-faced, two-armed, of the color of tin,
Holding a jeweled rosary and a cooling fan.
He abides in the northern maṇḍala.

From Caṃ arises the emanation, Akṣobhya,
One-faced, four-armed, of the color of crystal,
Holding a vajra and bell in embrace,
Adorned with a garland of light, with the śrīvatsa, a vase, and the rest.

From Chaṃ arises the emanation, the Lord of Horses,
One-faced, four-armed, the color of beryl,
Holding a jeweled mongoose and a conch bowl.
He abides in the southern maṇḍala.

From Jaṃ arises the emanation, the Sound of Thunder,
One-faced, two-armed, the color of saffron,
Holding a jeweled staff and a pot.
He abides in the western maṇḍala.

From Ñaṃ arises the emanation, Stainless Glory,
One-faced, four-armed, the color of emerald,
Holding a jeweled lotus, a guardian, and a quiver.
He abides in the northern maṇḍala.

From Ṭaṃ arises the emanation, the Jeweled Melody,
One-faced, four-armed, golden in color,
Holding a jeweled bell, embraced with his consort,
A treasury of jewels, a wheel, and the rest.

From Ṭhaṃ arises the emanation of the color of the sky,
One-faced, four-armed, with a bluish radiance,
Holding a jeweled flute and a lotus.
He abides in the eastern maṇḍala.

From Ḍaṃ arises the emanation, the Net-Bearer,
One-faced, four-armed, the color of dark blue,
Holding a jeweled rosary and a lotus.
He abides in the southern maṇḍala.

From Ḍhaṃ arises the emanation, the Ornament of the Jewels,
One-faced, four-armed, with a reddish radiance,
Holding a banner of the Dharma and a many-stringed instrument.
He abides in the western maṇḍala.

From Ṇaṃ arises the emanation, the Jeweled Crest,
One-faced, four-armed, the color of emerald,
Holding a wish-fulfilling vase and a wishing tree.
He abides in the northern maṇḍala.

From Taṃ arises the emanation, the One Who Appears Everywhere,
One-faced, four-armed, golden in color,
Holding a wheel, a bell, his consort, and a vase,
The stainless crown protuberance, holding a vajra.

From Thaṃ arises the emanation, the Glory of Light,
One-faced, four-armed, the color of crystal,
Holding a wheel, a net, and a sword.
He abides in the eastern maṇḍala.

From Daṃ arises the emanation, the Sovereign of Joy,
One-faced, four-armed, with the radiance of the sun,
Holding a wheel, a banner, and a treasury of joy.

He abides in the southern maṇḍala.
From Taṃ arises the emanation, the display of miraculous power,
One-faced, four-armed, with a reddish glow,
Adorned with a garland of sunlike rays and a vajra.

He abides in the western maṇḍala.
From Naṃ arises the emanation, the One Who Sees Everywhere,
One-faced, two-armed, with a green glow,
Radiant like the mango of the kuśa shoot.

He abides in the northern maṇḍala.
From Paṃ arises the emanation, the One of Meaningful Purpose,
One-faced, four-armed, of a bluish-green color,
Holding a net and a bell, embraced by his consort,
And a raised vajra and a sword.

From Phaṃ arises the emanation, Stirred Diligence,
One-faced, four-armed, in the manner of Kārttika,
Holding a net, a rosary, and a trident.

The Blessed One spoke the true word: "I shall explain; sons of the lineage, listen. Where there is no duality of ultimate and conventional, the meaning of true reality is taught. Shall I contend with those who have fallen to the lowest state? Likewise, these three worlds exist for the work of destroying and ruining existence, so I will command them all and turn them back from these evil deeds. So he thought.

Then all those of great power, authority, and splendor in the abodes of the three worlds gathered together, and he proclaimed: Hey, listen to me, all of you! If these world-systems do not come under the rule of a single king, all existence will be destroyed. Therefore let every direction assemble with its retinue, and heed my command! So he announced.

At that time the host of the great fierce lords of the world, each displaying his own miraculous powers, said: What sort of arrogant one are you? Each one of us is a great king who protects limitless retinues, and we hold sway over countless realms. It would be well if you yourself dwelt in peace, for you have become sacred only through the touch of others. So they spoke in agreement. At this the king of the māras grew exceedingly swollen with affliction, and said to the others:

All these worlds, together with their gods,
abide under my command.
All beings, without exception,
shall heed the king's secret command.
Lord of the three realms, god of gods,
the ground that supports all grounds,
I am the measure of power and miracle.
None of you can rival me.
If you transgress my command,
your lives and your dominions will be destroyed, and your dwellings scattered.

So he declared.

Then Brahmā, king of the gods, gathered his own limitless retinue, and standing in their midst in the form of a wrathful being with the body of a great rākṣasa, he emanated wrathful beings and hosts of weapons by his own power. In the same way Viṣṇu, the Great Lord (Maheśvara), Indra, the Lord of Hosts (Gaṇapati), Bhṛṅgi and Riti, and the six-faced youth (Kārttikeya) each gathered his own limitless retinue, and standing in their midst they unfolded inconceivable emanations and displayed their power and miracles. The four great kings who dwell on the terrace of Mount Meru likewise stood up in the midst of their own retinues and displayed their might and miracles.

The guardians of the directions who dwell on the golden mountain took their places, each with his own abode and with fearsome weapons: Indra, the Lord of Power (Īśāna), Yama, Nirṛti, the water god (Varuṇa), the yakṣa (Kubera), the sun, and the earth deities. The sun, the moon, Mars, Jupiter, Venus, Saturn, and the great planets likewise took their places, each with his own retinue, in hosts of fearsome wrathful beings and with weapons. Anantā, Takṣaka, Balahetu, Kulika, Vāsuki, Śaṅkhapāla, Padma, and the water gods took their places with their own attendants and fearsome emanations. In the same way the yakṣas, who abide as the proud hosts of the three realms, the gandharvas, the kumbhāṇḍas, the powerful and mighty chiefs of the six classes of beings, the mātṛkās, the Yamas, the asuras, the Suparṇas, the rākṣasas, the garuḍas, and the hosts of stars and constellations, each in the center of the maṇḍala of his own retinue, manifested and took their places, with fearsome bodily forms, weapons, and roars.

Then the king of the māras also gave rise to affliction. He assembled his own retinues, displayed all at once every power of miracle he possessed, and from a palace blazing with weapons of every kind he revealed himself in a fierce, blazing, terrifying form, with hosts of wrathful goddesses at play.

The assembled retinue, together with weapons blazing with fire and light, spread out in the ten directions and filled all of space, and hail, plague, and every kind of tumult arose. By this, all the gods of the world became intoxicated and bewildered. From every direction the hosts of terror struck and cut them down with the blazing weapons of that retinue, until each of their assemblies was seized with panic, and they offered up their own life-essences and begged for refuge.

Then the māras, who bring all the gods together, were dismissed with threats and made to take a binding pledge. Each took back his own life-essence. The whole assembly was separated from its lords, and the eight Great Gods took their places on their own summits. The four Great Kings took their places in the eastern continent. The yakṣas and the guardians of the directions took their places in the two minor continents of the east. The beings of the six classes were stationed in the south. The gandharvas and the mātṛkās were stationed in the minor continents of the south. The asuras were stationed in the west. The Yamas, the Suparṇas, and the rākṣasas were stationed in the two minor continents of the western continent. The planets and the constellations were stationed in the north. Thus, from the unsurpassed secret intention of all the Tathāgatas, the emanation of the māras was shown.

Then the king of the māras himself, greatly delighted, gave this command to his entire retinue: "One who dwells in the retinue of a single king dwells there by acting in accord with his wishes. So you too must listen to my command as I give it. Cut off this path of liberation, and show no reverence to any other teacher. Otherwise my world will be emptied. These beings, who lack any vast aspiration, are by nature deluded. They are like animals, so there is no point in their gaining any place of well-being."

And he spoke again in verse:

Alas, beings without discernment
Abandon their own happiness
And strive after another path to liberation.
Deluded, they ruin their own continuum.
They abandon well-being and wish for harm.
Pity these beings in their bewilderment.
Yet for now, those fortunate through compassion
Have gathered in my retinue.
You who wish to attain some higher state elsewhere,
Place faith and reverence in me.

So he commanded. And they too listened in this way. Though they had lost their own places and sorrow arose in their minds, they had no means of doing anything about it, and so they sat and remained there.

Then the king of the māras went to his own abode. With his retinue he indulged in joyful enjoyments and the qualities of desire, and took pleasure in delightful song, dance, and music. He thought: "Since I am the lord of all the three realms, I have seen no goal higher than this to be achieved. Now, if I were to bring the assemblies of buddhas and the bodhisattvas under my power, by what means should I begin?" And he questioned his own retinue as well.

Then he commanded all the cities of the māras: "Among the retinues of the buddhas, display everywhere every kind of inauspicious omen, evil sign, and wondrous magical contrivance. If you accomplish whatever they wish, they will take an interest in us. After that, when their chief figures have gathered in my retinue, I shall tame them by various means. If any are unable to come, I shall destroy them together with their very abodes." So he declared.

Then he emanated a host of wrathful beings with a multitude of weapons that shook the three thousand worlds. He arranged to crush utterly all beings who delight in the white class of virtue, and to destroy even the bodhisattvas who abide on the grounds. But the power of truth was harmed by nothing. Then, in a towering rage,

"I shall tame these three realms of mine all at once. As for those who practice, these wicked and deceitful ones, can they not be tamed? Therefore, rouse all those who dwell in my lower ranks, and let those who abide on the path of liberation be tamed in the proper manner." So he declared. Then he commanded Brahmā and the others, and they too sent forth their own emanations and weapons in every direction, and they recited their own groups of fierce secret mantras. Through this the world was shaken and convulsed, and everything fell into turmoil.

This is the ninth chapter, on the sudden arising of the host of Māras, from the Glorious Supreme Steed Play Tantra, the unsurpassed secret.

Then the Blessed One, supreme in great bliss, the very self of all the buddhas, in order to tame each of the three thousand worlds one by one, remained in the meditative absorption of magical display. Though he never stirred from suchness, by the power of his great compassion and his aspirations, he displayed various modes of thought in accord with the karma and conceptions of each being.

Those who are devoted to attachment and desire he tamed through great passion. Likewise, to tame aversion, delusion, pride, and jealousy, he tamed them through accordant appearances of many kinds, and also by means of the contrary.

Lotus, Sugata, Vajra-meaning, Jewel, and the varied Jewel-action, together with the great seals and others, he turned the wheel, and he accomplished things just as they are. The meaning of the great skillful means, the pure lotus, became the supreme emanation of wisdom, and so the lord of dance, great bliss, manifested the very view of the vajra.

Yet other vast deeds he performed, taming by various powers. He made a body accordant with pure beings, golden in radiance, endowed with the major and minor marks. He made known the unsurpassed vehicle, the profound secret mantra as it truly is. The Victor, the Sugata, took the body of the seal, and in various wondrous maṇḍalas, to some he appeared as a great supreme bodhisattva; for some he joined them correctly to the five paths; for some he completed the ten perfections; for some he directed them to the meaning of the result.

For some he went to the heaven of Tuṣita, the land of the gods. Entering the womb, being born, and engaging in play in accord with the conduct of the world, and other such deeds, he displayed in other bodies as things to be abandoned. All these were the inconceivable magical display of the one possessed of every pretense, the supreme activity of skillful means that enters in accord with beings. He performed austerities and engaged in difficult practices. At Vajrāsana he became a buddha, subduing Māra and awakening completely. Having urged the single-pointed absorption, he remained. Displaying miracles, he tamed all, and in due order turned the wheel of Dharma. He also showed the manner of passing into nirvāṇa, and the distribution of relics for the long term.

He whirled forth as peaceful hosts and as wrathful ones, and in the self-arisen varied forms of animals, in the whole world of vessels as a body of power, as Brahmā, Viṣṇu, and the guardians of the world, and as Māras and rākṣasas in their forms. He purified the karma and conceptions of beings, and he arranged the aim of his own wisdom.

At that time the abodes of beings and the hosts of weapons, struck down by the power of the hosts of karmic force, were by the power of suchness, through a great assembly, completely freed from the correct path. Then a throng of great arrogance and haughtiness arose, displaying its own power and miracles. Clinging to the path of affliction and karma, they gathered their own kind together.

"The three worlds are destroyed! Alas, alas! We, the chiefs of the three realms, who is it that emanates, alas, who is worthy to destroy us? He who dwells in the bliss of his own austerities holds the supreme path of passing beyond sorrow. What can another's teaching do? It is a limb of the fearful host of bliss. Who would abandon his own happiness and seek the aim of others' suffering? Even with many inconceivable emanated forms, it is hard to improve the beauty of one's own body. Even with many inconceivable emanated sounds, would they listen to our voice and be drawn on?"

By the light that is called mental conceptualization, they cannot become one-pointed in absorption. Those of fierce and supreme power, splendid in their radiance, dwell in bliss, each in his own conduct.

Then the first host of the māras, together with the Sovereign (Śiva), Brahmā, the great Viṣṇu, the Maker of Light, the Singer, and the Arrogant One, were exceedingly enraged. By their own power they displayed themselves and uttered fierce secret mantras, wrathful words, shouts, and cries. At that time, through the blessing and power of the king, he subdued them by means of the skillful method of entering in accord. The supreme unsurpassed secret vehicle, above all in guarding the three doors, and for the sake of arranging bliss in the true meaning, was brought into accord by great compassion. He taught the long path of entry as blissful, and made them happy through the door of joy. Yet through the force of their own afflictions and arrogance, the conduct that accords with entering the meaning was destroyed. Dense darkness of great delusion spread, and the mountain of pride was raised everywhere. The flames of aversion blazed and burned, envy stirred as a wind, and attachment became a river sweeping all away.

Greatly enraged by the power of arrogance, they urged their hosts upon one another. Drawing forth the strength of secret mantras and magical power, by the force of their loud proclamation they threw the three worlds into turmoil. Human plague and famine spread, weapons seized the harvests and their fruits, and terrifying spirits and hosts of evils arose in ceaseless circling. The earth blazed with fire, water-lords raged, poison and the poison of gems stirred like the wind. The paths of happiness, of higher rebirth, and of liberation were cut off. Overwhelmed by evil minds, beings were made to suffer, and so the buddhas were moved to concern.

Then the assemblies with the bodhisattvas all rose together to the teaching. They bowed toward the principal one, circled him many times, and prostrated. Delighting the Protector of bliss, they made offerings and offered this petition:

O you of great compassion, god of gods, by the nectar-water of loving-kindness and the other joys, you destroy them utterly together with their harmful spirits. May the vajra gathering of wisdom, which dissolves, show the maṇḍala of miraculous display. We beseech you to show the blessing of your enlightened activity. For these beings, stirred by waves of affliction, destroy the blissful path to liberation for themselves and others. If the very awakening mind, a path free of moisture, were to be left to vanish from the three realms of existence, then to what could great compassion be ascribed?

Emaho! The fruit, nirvāṇa! Emaho! The bodhisattva's lotus pond! Emaho! The inexpressible kumuda lily! Emaho! The tree with flowers and fruit! Since these maṇḍalas are utterly pure, they are the abode of the supreme lineage, of defining marks and qualities. How, at this time, would you not purify them? To what could great compassion be ascribed? These fortunate ones are vessels for the teaching, holders of the doctrine of the vajra vehicle. Now they are crushed beneath the mountain of conceptual thought. Since they cannot be tamed by skillful means, so that they may be liberated by force, we beseech you to show the manifold maṇḍalas.

Thus they petitioned.

This is the tenth chapter, on great enlightened activity, from the great tantra, the glorious Hayagrīva's Play, the supreme secret, the unsurpassed intention of all the Tathāgatas.
