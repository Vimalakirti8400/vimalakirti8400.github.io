About this text

The Rite of the Fire Offering, known in Sanskrit as the Homavidhi and in Tibetan as Jinsek gi Choga, is a short ritual manual in verse belonging to the cycle of the Hevajra Tantra, attributed in its colophon to the Indian master Saroruhavajra. Opening with homage to Hevajra and his consort Nairātmyā, it distinguishes an unsurpassed, an inner, and an outer fire offering, and then sets out the outer rite in practical detail: the shapes, sizes, colors, and emblems of the hearths for the rites of pacifying, increasing, subjugating, summoning, expelling, and fierce action; the substances, kindling sticks, firewood, ladles, and sources of fire suited to each; and the visualization, invitation, worship, and departure of the fire deity and of the maṇḍala deities, together with their mantras. It is Toh 1223 in the Tengyur, where it is placed among the tantra commentaries as one of the ritual texts of the Hevajra cycle.

Translation

In the language of India: homa vidhi. In the language of Tibet: The Rite of the Fire Offering.
Homage to glorious Hevajra.

Having bowed with reverence to glorious Hevajra
And to Nairātmyā,
Through the kindness of the vajra master
I shall explain the rite of the fire offering.

You who wish to benefit yourself and others,
Abandoning conceptual thought, pride, and the like,
With a mind that follows the Dharma,
Listen to the stages of the fire offering.

Unsurpassed, inner, and outer:
The fire offering has three divisions.
To burn the aggregates and the rest as fuel
In the fire deity of wisdom is the unsurpassed.
Modeled on the outer fire offering,
Eating food and the like is taught as the inner.

For the maṇḍala, for a deity's abode,
And at the time of accomplishment, the fire offering is to be performed.
Where the rites of the fire offering are absent,
Accomplishment will not come about;
Therefore, in order to accomplish every rite,
The wise should undertake the rites of the fire offering.

Pacifying is for the destruction of evil;
Increasing is the full embrace of merit;
Because passion is cultivated, it becomes subjugating;
Because conceptions are exhausted, it is killing.
For pacifying the hail of the afflictions
And for increasing meditative absorption,
Through the yoga of one's own deity,
One should perform it oneself with the mind, just as it is.

Next the outer is to be explained.
It becomes fivefold: the characteristics of the hearth and of the substances,
The practitioner, the deities,
And the means of accomplishment.

Now, as for the explanation of the hearth:
By the divisions of sentient beings it is limitless;
By the division of the rites it becomes four;
By the division of the families it becomes five.
For pacifying it is round;
For increasing it is square;
For subjugating it is in the shape of a bow;
For fierce rites it is triangular;
And likewise for expelling and summoning.

One cubit in width and one span in depth
Is the hearth for pacifying.
Two cubits in width and one cubit in depth
Is held to be for increasing.
One cubit in width and one span in depth
Is for subjugating.
Twenty finger-widths in width
And ten finger-widths in depth is for fierce rites,
And likewise for expelling and summoning.

For pacifying, make its color white;
For increasing, yellow;
For subjugating, make it red;
For fierce rites, black;
For summoning, make it red;
For expelling, make it black.

For pacifying, surround it with a rim of four finger-widths,
And on the inside too leave four finger-widths.
For increasing it is eight finger-widths,
And on the inside too leave the same.
For subjugating, surround it with a rim of four finger-widths,
And on the inside too leave four finger-widths.
For fierce rites it is three finger-widths,
And on the inside too leave three finger-widths;
And likewise for summoning and expelling.

In the center of the pacifying hearth
Draw a vajra and a curved knife,
And surround the rim with vajras.
For increasing, a jewel in the center,
And on the rim, jewels as well.
For subjugating, mark the center with a lotus,
And surround the rim with the same.
For fierce rites, a sword,
And surround the rim with swords as well.
For summoning, set a hook in the center,
And surround the rim with the same.
For expelling, in the center
A three-pronged vajra is to be placed,
And on the rim too draw three-pronged ones.
A curved knife in the center is common to all.
In the four directions within the hearth
Place, in order, a khaṭvāṅga
And the remaining hand emblems of the sugatas.
Every hearth is to be anointed
With a scent that matches its color.

Pacifying is mostly done in the first part of the night;
Increasing is done in the morning;
Subjugating is done at evening,
And summoning is the same.
Fierce rites are done at midnight and at midday,
And expelling is the same.
Pacifying is taught to be done in the east;
Increasing is done in the southern quarter;
The rite of subjugating is done in the west,
And summoning too is done in the west;
Fierce rites are to be undertaken in the north,
And the rite of expelling is the same.
Draw them with colored powders that accord with each,
Or else flowers will also do.
Thus are the hearths to be made.

The substances are to be known as follows:
For pacifying, sesame;
For increasing, rice with curd;
For subjugating and summoning, utpala lotuses;
For fierce rites, thorns;
For expelling too, thorns.
These are common to all:
Water for welcome and water to cool the feet,
The purifying torma, scent and unguent,
Flowers, lamps, and sesame,
Kindling sticks, butter, and rice with curd,
Kuśa grass, sprouts of dūrvā grass, barley,
Wheat, grain, and black gram,
Unbroken rice and aśvagandhā,
Lagor shosha fruit and nutmeg,
And other melted substances besides,
Nectar, fire, and firewood.
For fierce rites: an effigy
Of the one to be subdued, mustard seed, salt,
Blood, powdered human bone,
Poison, and the five heroes.

Sticks of udumbara and the like,
Taken from the tip portion of the branches,
A finger's length long and as thick as the breadth of the thumb,
Dipped in honey, milk,
And white sandalwood water,
Are offered one-pointedly for pacifying.
For increasing, sticks
Taken from the middle portion of the branch,
A cubit long, and in thickness
Filling the space between thumb and little finger,
Dipped in curd, butter, and yellow scent,
Are to be offered one-pointedly by the wise.
For subjugating, sticks dipped
In red sandalwood and red mustard,
Their measure matching those for pacifying.
For wrathful rites, thorny wood
Taken from the root portion,
Ten finger-widths long; as for thickness,
Both thin and thick are to be offered,
Dipped in blood, poison, salt,
And pungent scents.
Expelling and summoning are the same.
Avoid sticks that are too long or too short, cracked or too thin,
Or that harbor living creatures, and the like.

Firewood of udumbara and the like,
Sweet in taste and with milky sap,
Is used for the rite of pacifying;
Wood with a sour taste is for increasing;
For subjugating, wood that is red and has thorns
Shaped like hooks;
For summoning too it is the same;
For the wrathful, bitter
And hot in taste, with thorns;
For expelling too it is the same.
Make the firewood as befits each rite
In length, in color, and the rest.

The vessels for the welcome water and the rest,
For pacifying and the other rites in order,
Are made of silver, of gold, of copper,
Of iron, and so forth.

The filling ladle, the anointing ladle, has a handle a cubit long;
At its base make a vajra and a jewel.
Above that, four finger-widths in width
And two finger-widths in depth, with a vajra inside;
At the end of the four finger-widths, a vajra,
And in between, the shape of lotus petals.
The pouring ladle is lotus-shaped, two finger-widths across,
One finger-width deep, with a vajra inside.

Fire kindled from wood with milky sap,
Or a fire perpetually maintained, or a brahmin's
Ordinary household fire, is for pacifying.
For increasing, the fire of a king,
Of a kṣatriya, or of a minister,
Or else fire brought from a hermitage.

For subjugating and summoning, use fire
From the house of a courtesan or of a vaiśya.
For summoning, use fire
Taken from a dancer or a musician,
Or fire kindled by blowing on a road.
For fierce rites, use fire
From the house of a lord or of a caṇḍāla,
And likewise for expelling.
For all of them, fire from a charnel ground,
Or fire with live embers, may be used.

All the implements of the fire offering
Are to be arranged to one's right.
The vajra, likewise the bell,
And the vessel of welcome water are placed to the left.
The filling ladle, the pouring ladle,
And the butter are to be placed in front.
For fierce rites the positions are reversed.
Everything is to be purified and then set in place.

The master, or else the practitioner,
Fully endowed with every characteristic,
Skilled in the rites of the fire offering,
Should diligently perform the rite of the fire offering.
Here the deities are of three kinds:
The one who receives what is burned, the one who performs the rite,
And the fire deity who is to be worshiped.
Their characteristics are taught elsewhere.

I shall explain the procedure at the hearth.
Then that master,
Seated properly in the fire-offering house,
Having first performed the bathing and other preliminaries,
Through the rite drawn from the sādhana
Generates the pride of the deity
That accords with the rite,
And then properly blesses the substances.

First, over the vessel for the purifying sprinkling,
Recite this secret mantra:
oṃ rakṣa rakṣa hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ
With it, sprinkle everything for purification.
Then, over the welcome water and the rest,
Recite each one's own secret mantra,
Or else recite this secret mantra:
oṃ āḥ hūṃ
And imagine that everything is pure.
Take fire of the kinds described above,
And purify it by sprinkling with rakṣa.
Purify the hearth as well, put the fire into it,
And, together with this secret mantra,
Fan it into flame with a fan:
oṃ heruka mahā vajra jvala hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ

Then, in order to bind the obstructors,
Recite this secret mantra
Over green kuśa grass pulled up by the roots:
oṃ vajrasatva āyuṣe svāhā
And spread it out well to the east, the south, the west, and the north,
And in the center as well.

Then, in that well-purified place,
Visualize the hearth,
Arising from the syllable hūṃ,
That accords with the rite of pacifying or another.
In its center visualize clearly
A lotus of eight petals,
And through the five awakenings
Generate Vajrasattva.
By way of great passion,
From Vajrasattva, wholly transformed,
Imagine the fire deity arising from raṃ.
The fire deity for pacifying is white.
For increasing, it is the fire of wisdom;
For subjugating and summoning, the fire of desire;
For the fierce rite and for expelling,
The wise imagine the fire of wind.

He has three faces and six arms and is white in color,
Adorned with the sacred thread and the rest.
With the first pair of arms he embraces his consort;
His other two right hands hold a staff and show fearlessness;
His other two left hands hold a rosary and a water pot.
For increasing, he is the same but yellow in color.
For subjugating and summoning, he is red,
And his middle right and left hands
Hold a hook and a noose.
For killing and expelling, he is black,
His hair standing on end, like a devourer,
And in his right hand he holds a sword.

By joining mantra and mudrā,
Invite the wisdom fire deity.
The right hand is in the mudrā of fearlessness,
With the thumb bent to the joint of the ring finger.
oṃ agna mahā teja sarva kāmartha sādhaka karu cuda kritva svāhā asmi sannihito bhava
This is the mudrā and mantra for inviting the fire deity.

Expel the obstructors, purify by sprinkling, and so forth.
By saying jaḥ hūṃ vaṃ hoḥ,
Summon him, likewise cause him to enter,
Bind him, and delight him.
satiṃ hūṃ gaccha havyabhuka agamiśasi jatakāle sarva sidhi kuru śame
This is the mantra for delighting the fire.

Then, with light rays from the seed syllable at the heart,
Invoke the tathāgatas,
And with the water of the nectar vases
Confer empowerment
On the fire deity, who is not two from oneself.
For the pacifying fire, Vairocana
Is made the crown ornament;
For increasing, Ratnasambhava;
For subjugating and summoning, Amitāyus;
For killing and the other fierce rites, Akṣobhya.

Then, with the mantra proper to each,
Offer the purifying sprinkling, the welcome water, and the water to cool the feet.
Then, in the mouth of the fire deity
And within the filling ladle and the pouring ladle,
Set out well the syllable raṃ,
And with the mantra of the fire deity
Offer seven, three, or one
Full ladlefuls of butter:
oṃ heruka agnaye amokasya śāntiṃ guru svāhā
Combine it as well with the mantra proper to each rite.
Then examine the signs of the blaze;
If they are bad, perform the protection.

Then worship that fire well,
Making offering with every kind of offering.
oṃ rakṣa rakṣa hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ
This is for giving the purifying sprinkling.
oṃ kha raṃ khā hi svāhā
This is the mantra of the welcome water.
oṃ niriti hūṃ
This is the mantra of the water to cool the feet.
oṃ vajra gandhe hūṃ
This is the mantra of scent.
oṃ vajra puṣpe hūṃ
This is the mantra of flowers.
oṃ vajra dhūpe hūṃ
This is the mantra of incense.
oṃ vajra dīpe hūṃ
This is the mantra of the lamp.
oṃ vajra naividye hūṃ
This is the mantra of the food offering.

Then offer sounds and music,
And properly purify the substances.
At the beginning and at the end, offer with the filling ladle;
At other times, with the pouring ladle.
oṃ agnaye svāhā
This is for butter.
oṃ vajra yajñānaya svāhā
This is for the kindling sticks.
oṃ sarva pābaṃ dahana vajraye svāhā
This is for sesame and white mustard seed.
oṃ sarva saṃpade svāhā
This is for curd and rice.
oṃ abratihata vajraye svāhā
This is for kuśa grass.
oṃ vajra āyuṣe svāhā
This is for the sprouts of dūrvā grass.
oṃ vajra mahā vegāya svāhā
This is for barley.
oṃ vajra ghasmari svāhā
This is for wheat.
oṃ vajra vijāya svāhā
This is for grain.
oṃ mahā balāya svāhā
This is for black gram.
oṃ vajra puṣṭaye svāhā
This is for unbroken rice.
oṃ mahā śiya svāhā
This is for aśvagandhā, lagor shosha fruit, and nutmeg.
The remaining melted substances
Are offered into the fire with the mantra of the fire deity.
oṃ bodhi vṛkṣaye svāhā
This is for wood of the bodhi tree.
oṃ vajra rtatāya svāhā
This is for nyagrodha wood.
oṃ vajra satakarāya svāhā
This is for mango wood.

The kindling sticks go into his maṇḍala of light;
Sesame, cooked rice, and curd into his hands;
Kuśa and dūrvā grass onto the crown of his head;
Barley, wheat, rice,
And lentils into his mouth;
The melted substances too go into his mouth.
Scent is offered at his heart, flowers on his head,
Water to rinse the mouth at his mouth,
And the welcome water and the water for sipping into his hands.

The lamp is to be offered in front of him.
Then give the purifying sprinkling and offer praise:

hūṃ Son of the lord of the world, of mighty Brahmā, the protector,
King of fire deities, empowered by Ṭakki,
Who burns up every affliction with supreme wisdom:
To you, the fire deity who seizes the offerings, I bow down.

This is the praise.
Then offer nectar as well,
And charge him with the rites.
This concludes the part concerning the fire deity.

Then, at the heart of the fire deity,
Imagine clearly a sun disk,
And there generate the deities.
Then, with light rays from the heart,
Invite the wisdom maṇḍala.
Expel the obstructors, and give the purifying sprinkling and the welcome water.
Saying jaḥ hūṃ vaṃ hoḥ,
Through the four goddesses, Gaurī and the others,
Summon them, cause them to enter, bind them, and bring forth their delight.
With light rays from the seed syllable at the heart,
Invite the wisdom maṇḍala,
And by it have empowerment properly conferred.
Then offer the water for sipping and the welcome water.
Placing hūṃ on the tongues of the deities,
In the order taught before,
Worship them in sequence with every offering.
The substances specially described
Are to be offered as far as one is able.
At the end, having offered the full ladles,
Generate splendor in the deities.

For pacifying, at the end of the mantra
Add śīntaṃ kuru svāhā.
For increasing, at the end of the mantra
Add puṣṭiṃ kuru oṃ.
For subjugating, at the end of the mantra
Add vaśaṃ kuru ho.
For summoning, at the end of the mantra
Add ākārṣa and jaḥ.
For fierce rites, at the end of the mantra
Add māraya phaṭ.

Then, having offered the purifying sprinkling,
Praise them properly with hymns of praise,
And taste the nectar as well.
Request whatever accomplishment you wish,
Request the wisdom maṇḍala to depart again,
And gather the commitment deities into the heart.
This concludes the part concerning the deities.

Then, in order to pacify all hindrances,
With the mantra beginning a kā,
Offer torma to the guests,
The guardians of the directions and the rest.
Then, to the fire deity, the remaining substances,
Having purified them with mantra, in order:
The purifying sprinkling, the welcome water, and the rest
Are to be offered, each with its own mantra.
The sounds of bells and music,
And the praise too, are done as before.
Having tasted the nectar,
Request the wisdom fire deity to depart:
oṃ agnaye yahi yatisva sarva cevā para arthaṃ castihaṃ gaccha havya buka
Reversing the rite of invitation,
The thumb is flicked outward, and he departs.
This is the mantra and mudrā for the departure.

Then imagine that the commitment fire deity
Turns into the form of fire.
Dedicate the roots of virtue,
And remain as you please.

Like grass sprouting from the surface of a diamond
Through rain falling from the sky,
By the blessing of the masters,
I, a mere reasoner, have composed this.

The Rite of the Fire Offering, composed by the master Saroruhavajra, is complete.

Translator's notes

In the list of four rites, དབང is the subjugating or magnetizing rite (vaśya), not empowerment; the glossary sense does not fit this context. The line "one should perform it oneself with the mind, just as it is" is terse; it appears to mean that the inner and unsurpassed offerings are performed mentally while in the yoga of one's own deity. The phrase "the filling ladle, the anointing ladle" (dgang gzar byugs gzar) is unclear; the measurements that follow fit the larger filling ladle, and byugs may be a slip. In the line on increasing sticks, the syllable ro before rgyas makes no sense and is read as a slip, giving "for increasing". The identification of rta sga as aśvagandhā is tentative, and lagor shosha is a Tibetan materia medica term left in phonetic form. "The five heroes" (dpa' bo lnga) is rendered literally; it presumably names a set of fierce ritual substances. "The fire deity for pacifying is white" reads dkar po for the written dka' po ("difficult"), an evident slip. In the line on fire for fierce rites, rje bo is rendered "a lord"; the sense in a list of low or impure sources of fire is unclear, and a caste term may be intended. Here bsang gtor is plainly a purificatory sprinkling (Skt. prokṣaṇa), and is so rendered; the same item in the earlier list of common substances is best understood the same way. Ṭakki, by whom the fire deity is said to be empowered, is probably the wrathful vidyārāja Ṭakkirāja. The first line of the praise is compressed; it is read as calling the fire deity the son of Brahmā, styled lord of the world and protector, in keeping with the Indian tradition that makes Agni a son of Brahmā. The epithet rendered "who seizes the offerings" ('dzin byed) is literally "the seizer". The four through whom the wisdom beings are summoned, made to enter, bound, and delighted are presumably the four gatekeeper goddesses of the Hevajra maṇḍala, Gaurī and the others.
