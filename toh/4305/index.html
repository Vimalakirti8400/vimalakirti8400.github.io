About this text

A Praise in a Garland of Metres, known in Sanskrit as the Vṛttamālāstuti and in Tibetan as Debjor gyi Trengwé Töpa, is a hymn to the bodhisattva Mañjuśrī, the Lord of Speech, that doubles as a showcase of Indian prosody. Composed according to its colophon by the great scholar and elder Jñānaśrīmitra, it praises Mañjuśrī's compassion, wisdom, beauty and power in a long sequence of ornate verses, each written in a different metre, grouped into the three classes of even, half-even and uneven metres and often playing on the names and rules of the metres themselves. The work is Toh 4305 in the Tengyur, the Tibetan collection of Indian treatises and commentaries, where it belongs among the hymns of praise closely tied to the literary science of metrics. Its Tibetan translation was begun by the great translator Shongtön Dorjé Gyaltsen and completed by Pang Lotsāwa Palden Lodrö Tenpa, who relied on a commentary by the master Śākyarakṣita.

Translation

In the language of India: bṛttamālāstūti. In the language of Tibet: A Praise in a Garland of Meters.

Homage to Mañjunātha.

For the sake of accomplishing the aims of others, the modes of conduct of the Lord of Speech (Vāgīśvara),
called even, half-even and uneven:
these three kinds are the summary,
while in their full extent they are taught as infinite.

Wherein the well-defined forms are renowned,
and the marks of the supreme sages are rightly proclaimed,
just as, through many varied arrangements,
the words of metrical composition too become supremely elaborated.

From merely seeing each one's form,
the wise are able to count out, in order,
all the heavy and the light syllables;
what need is there here to append anything else?

That which was born from a well-formed mouth,
the one innate ornament of all words:
when it is contemplated with devotion,
may you all become able to destroy delusion.

Wisdom,
voice,
glory,
all!

Yourself,
the sole one's,
earth-filling,
all-beautiful.

Always, too,
among gods and
asuras and the rest,
fully arisen.

Aspiring
to his qualities,
truly relying on them,
they are free of stain.

In not one existence only,
growing through familiarity,
the special realizations
are relied on by those who hold to the good.

In meter, like the gā ya tri,
first proclaimed by the holy,
he in whom various
supreme virtues and excellences arise:

with the blue utpala in hand,
the Teacher, whose body,
the abode of graceful play, is slender of waist:
may this one be victorious!

Blessed One, take the lead!
You alone are the refuge.
Upon this foolish person
cast your glance, if only a little.

Like the inexhaustible rays of the sun
joined with the surplus of summer,
the teaching of the Sugata,
having gained you, blazes forth.

Though the young ones of the Victors
bear the play of youth,
your body alone
is certainly the one of true grace.

By merely recollecting you,
the forms of the multitudes of beings
become free of a lowly state
and come to possess an upward course.

From the full praise of your qualities,
those born into the highest station
proclaim you, to their companions and fellow praisers,
as the one and only learned one.

You, for beings whose eye of intellect
is firmly obstructed
by the dreadful darkness of delusion,
became like a jeweled staff that lights the way for all alike.

That very guide of all, our own,
surpassing the three worlds,
is the radiant one who takes the measure
of the perfections of the Lord of Sages.

Able to destroy the torment of unbearable afflictions,
in the family of the perfect buddhas
this beauty of yours bears the ornament,
just as a garland of lightning does among the clouds.

As beings who have become like children
stray into wrong ways,
you, who hold the staff of the signs,
are beautiful in the conduct of a holy guru.

Making your own way clear
to the three worlds, O wise one of great cause,
the movements of your palms
are like leafy branches.

O supreme Lord, because your speech
is endowed with brilliant words,
all at once, at the same moment,
lay hold of the taste of reality together with understanding.

Whoever, even for an instant,
receives the sign of your sidelong glance resting on him,
by that alone fully attains
a station established in vast prosperity.

Growing familiar with your very eyes,
this multitude of beings, brought close to excellence,
is beautiful like ground
on which the face of the plowshare has been ceaselessly set.

For in this field, brought to full ripeness by that giver of the supreme,
which gives birth to reality itself,
when the seed of your speech is sown,
it ripens into the fruit of a peaceful form.

Lord, who here could truly
comprehend your conduct?
It is like the course of young snakes
through water at a time of darkness.

Yet, Lord, the delight born from hearing your fame
overwhelms all restraint,
and strips from the world's mouth the ornament
that knowing the measure of its own ability had set there.

Having reached the far shore of knowledge and conduct,
your body, abode of glory, skilled in graceful play,
is beautiful amid the garland of the Buddha's sons,
as the a ra bindha is among the water-born flowers.

Your renowned lineage is the Mountain of Sunrise;
the light of wisdom, abiding on the chariot of skillful means,
able to vanquish foolishness,
sun of the lotus pool of the three worlds, to you I bow.

The ground from which the Buddha's sons arise,
your wealth, Protector, is supremely beautiful,
as the summit ground of the most excellent of mountains
is beautiful with golden, jeweled shoots.

Protector who illuminates the world,
the words of all the learned, full of delight,
swell your sound of victory and are its cause,
as though it were proclaimed everywhere with no one to proclaim it.

The lotus on the heads of the foremost among the hosts of disputants,
your speech, become the abode of the glory of the wise,
drunk on the blended mead of good qualities,
intoxicates even the learned of the world, and sports at its ease.

In you, the ornament of the family
of the wheel-turners among all the Victors,
what is clearly held to be called mere youthfulness
is like falling short beside a peacock's gait.

In each of your pure and lovely limbs
shines forth your own perfect beauty,
with garlands of light rays streaming in many colors:
by you all garlands of ornaments have been outshone.

As you, master of speakers, explain and establish the way of reality,
may the bracelets that ring out sweet sounds
as your moving hands meet one another
protect all beings by their movement.

Having truly seen the whole of these surpassing riches,
your form, your host of qualities and your fame,
beings grasp their own objects without delight,
and come to hold even the much-praised three as lowly.

Chief, your body, endowed with excellence in every way,
like the very essence of no small delights,
streams forth a current of incomparable beauty:
whose joy does it not awaken?

Shedding rays like the nectar-rayed moon of autumn,
white and stainless like snow,
this round garland of pearls upon your heart
adorns you like the gathering of your qualities.

Drawn by the sweet fragrance spreading far,
swarms of bees arrive and hum, and so,
as the fame of the jasmine, noblest of the noble flowers,
jā ti, is proclaimed as victorious among them,

surpassing treasury of honey, its fragrance clear and fine,
holding its pollen as though on level ground,
where the play of the bees resting at ease on its petals is never done:
this splendor of your blue lotus is beautiful.

Those who have gained a host of eyes
to drink this unblinking for a long time,
because they have grown accustomed to the elixir of your form,
hold the god of wealth alone as the supreme one to be much worshipped.

With their jeweled crests bowed, full of joy,
their palms joined together, the fortunate,
reverently and for a long time, with no other task,
have attended upon these two feet of yours.

Lord of intellect, if attendance on your lotus feet
is truly accomplished,
what use is the wealth of Indra, or the glory of the Brahmās?
What use even a perfection that surpasses these?

Those turned away from attending on you, helpless,
are surely brought to ruin by fierce karma and afflictions
in this birth so hard to cross,
like a ship at the mercy of wind and waves in the midst of the ocean.

Throughout existences that are utterly without end,
for the pure-minded who have long seen the faultless,
you, fully attended, have become the lofty chariot of the mind:
how then could the stream of their mind be scattered?

Even by your sidelong glance, on an occasion of merit,
the nectar of the noble truths is gained,
and joy in the cutting off of the four delusions,
and whatever is endowed with fully expanded wisdom, comes to be.

Abode of the hosts of praises that cut through torment,
your fame, most holy and captivating,
like the moonlight of the lovely moon, like moist sandalwood,
richly adorns the ten directions with its whiteness.

Lofty with many peaks and towering high,
the stubborn views of sentient beings,
through reliance on you, gradually crumble,
like the shape of a mountain struck and shattered by Indra's irresistible vajra.

Master of the vast Dharma, endowed with excellent things,
seated perfectly on the lofty lion throne,
you, an Indra of peace, wielding the vajra weapon of Upendra,
are attended upon by those who long for peace.

The moment they are spoken of, rich in their marks,
your feet, worthy to be heard of, and the unfolding of their steps,
those within whose eyes' range they come
are near kin to the gods and worthy of praise.

You whose very being is longed for, who carry away every virtue:
though others exist, beyond doubt you
are the lord of an intelligence not shared with others,
said to abide in the family of the Victor over the Bodiless One.

With the form of a child, beautiful, with dazzling ornaments,
golden in color, with an unshakable diadem,
ornament of the family of the Lord of Sages, become the glory of beings:
to the Lord of Speech I bow with speech, mind and body.

Even with the very many words of eloquent poets,
the far shore of your qualities, supreme Protector, is not reached.
Yet this most meager praise of mine, my Teacher,
is good fortune enough, if it brings only joy to myself.

O lord of Sarasvatī, in you the qualities of Sarasvatī blaze.
Radiant, praised by every bright ornament,
found arising from the lotus of the face that is rich in merit,
she herself, with her garland of holy qualities, attends closely upon you.

Shattering the arrows of the Five-Arrowed One,
repelling the impurities of aversion and surely dispelling the folly of delusion,
Protector whose form is joined to the three worlds by caring for them,
from your power alone are manifold qualities well attained.

Brilliant with the jewels of a host of qualities born of utter steadfastness,
with the manner of a mass of waters through its inner fullness,
with streams of rain, the water of the holy Dharma that quenches the fire of total affliction,
you bear the play of a garland of clouds.

Before you, the speakers who long most to be foremost and excellent,
because they are bereft of confidence,
even in the words they utter, fast or slow,
are mostly deemed to fall into the state of the mute.

Purifying even the three worlds,
with a light as brilliantly white as the rays of the cool-rayed moon,
your long-enduring fame flows forth from you,
descending gently even from the summit of the mountain of the gods.

By this fame of yours, the very home of love,
the hearts of the wise are utterly stolen away,
as by masses of flowers heaped up in every direction
from kunda vines blossoming everywhere.

Wearing the matted locks of the lords of speech of the dharmadhātu,
skilled in emanating the deeds of many buddhas,
ocean of love, bearer of a wealth of beauty:
who is this who destroys the evils of beings?

Like the shimmer of the moving waves of the sky's river
touching a mass of light rays,
blue as the swell of the waters of the Yamunā,
your sword sports on other occasions.

This was translated by the eldest son of the great scholar Takdewa Sengé Gyaltsen, Shangtön Dorjé Gyaltsen, who had crossed to the far shore of the ocean of non-Buddhist and Buddhist treatises, the great scholar without rival in his time, the crown jewel of those who speak both languages.

siddhi rastu

Protector, lord of your own form, where is there a form
like yours, so delightful with its captivating garland of necklaces?
And where is one who wears terrifying ornaments of snakes,
radiating forth to pacify beings most hard to tame?

Who would not have their heart stolen by this body of yours,
beautifully adorned with masses of the finest pearls strung without a gap,
as though it wore a fresh garland of flowers
fit to be worn in the season of the lovely ma lī kśa?

O Protector, if the ocean of nectar of your beauty,
having raised great waves, shows the utmost weariness,
the eyes of beings, longing to perceive its great beauty,
are set moving, filled with desire.

Giver of the supreme, gone beyond existence: though my limbs,
beyond question and plainly, attend on you bereft of joy,
this joined pair of ears, become the vessel
for drinking the nectar of your speech, is a cause of joy.

Though one of unequalled might and power attends on you,
a person like me, foolish, with a wandering mind,
bereft of delight in the essences of the nectar of peace,
begs of you this one supreme thing: that such a state may not arise.

Moon that brings about a feast for the eyes of embodied beings,
through your kindness, by the power of perfectly increased virtue,
the learned go with ease alone along the path,
with its eight divisions, of those who have destroyed existence.

The fortunate who, with an intellect that relies on your feet, King of Speech,
have gained the essence, in every destiny,
though the causes of happiness, suffering, eminence and the like arise,
never come to lose their mindfulness.

The minds of those fit to attend fully upon your feet,
free of every mass of stains, clear by nature,
intellects made great by fully expanded qualities,
bring forth for beings every happiness that is perfect in nature.

With a body of refined gold, beautiful and supremely charming,
attended by the geese of clear speech,
the one swan singing sweetly in the Victor's lake of mind with its lotus stems,
renowned here as Mañjughoṣa: may he protect you.

Protector, if even a subtle discernment
of these three afflictions, so hard to bear, hard as it is to achieve,
were to become clear, where indeed in these three worlds
is one who has gained such a body, delighting eye and mind?

If one were to clear the waters in the ocean
that holds masses of jewels radiating great light
and look closely, then even such a stream of beauty,
most lovely, on the body of ones like you, this too.

Protector, your conduct, which with deep and lofty melodies
rains down streams of pleasing nectar on whoever comes to you,
and inspires joy and good fortune, is most lovely,
and clearly follows the summer rain cloud; and as that cloud intoxicates the peacocks,

so, like the month of spring, in you the three worlds rejoice:
unsurpassed giver of growth, branch of the utpala tree,
seen with flowers lovely to the eye, whose bees, drunk on honey,
hum in the manner of a gentle melody: may this one be victorious!

Your conduct alone, which has become the ornament of the Victors' way,
which has gained supreme joy through every exalted quality,
fit to be proclaimed by the wise who keep the vow of the bee:
is it not, I wonder, the fresh cū ta blossoms, the forehead ornament of spring?

Riding the king of the lords of beasts, whose face turns as your face is turned,
supreme in compassion, the root of high estate,
by sheer beauty holding its legs of gold and snow,
to the very god of the family of speakers I bow without delay.

Because all elaborations are fully pacified and you are without obscuration,
relying on the nectar of fully expanded intellect and samādhi,
your conduct as a sage, overpowering and destroying what is harmful,
tames the three existences, which others cannot conquer.

To bring about complete peace for exceedingly terrifying beings
through conduct without mercy, at times,
out of compassion, like the all-destroying garland in your hand,
terrifying weapons of many kinds, ringing out, sport at play.

Giver of the supreme: enjoyment of virtue and excellence, freedom from attachment to objects,
freedom from delusion, the utter destruction of Māra, perfect benefit for the three existences,
the state hard to attain that cannot be stolen: for those who long for these,
that too lies in devotion to you; there is no other path that is free of harm.

If, restraining the senses, one meditates with devotion
at times on any aspect of you, whose mind is unwavering,
before long this earth, adorned with the girdles of the oceans,
will surely choose that person of her own accord.

In your presence the immortal women, though surely they hold themselves lightly,
as though longing for beauty, are dusted with pollen, their body hair thrilling in play;
their breasts, swelling high, burst forth, their girdles are bound ever tighter,
and they take on the proud bearing of the god of desire, that mighty one, trembling more and more.

Whose limbs, possessed of a delightfulness beyond compare,
steal away all delight from every other form, however vast;
body undiminished, supreme foundation that subdues Indra's bow,
bearing garlands of light rays of many hues: may this be victorious!

O heir of the Victors, smiling of face, in your presence, with joy,
the fine sweet sound rung out by cymbals never parted from them,
blended with the unstirred strain, like the lovely fifth note,
those who wish for their own greatness give voice to their craving for melody.

Protector, adorned with armlets like nets of iron chains
that hold motionless the elephant that defeats beings,
the great bolt on the door to the path of the lower realms, destroyer of the most arrogant Mind-Churner,
joy of the Sugata: may the tree of your arm be victorious!

Lord, the glory of fame perfectly gathered
by your supreme victory, perfect in qualities great at the summit of existence,
which you bring forth, pure through being free of every stain,
moves in the likeness of the digits of the hare-marked moon of autumn.

The maidens that make honey from the sweet fragrances clearly gathered there;
the garland, rich in the juice of flavor, which increases the bliss
of the blue Sugata who endured saṃsāra, arranged by the women of the lord of the immortals:
this sports upon your crown.

Because those whose minds have passed beyond love go to a state that is not that of the ascetics,
while the learned who benefit other beings you cause to flourish greatly,
so the beauty of the heaped snow of your qualities brings it about
that even the sky has its own jewel, the sun, overpowered.

How could even the supreme ones, the well-restrained who strive in their vows,
who praise you, whose speech gives great delight, unceasingly, fresh and ever fresh,
truly take the measure of the extent of the mass of your qualities, qualities beyond counting,
as though measuring the masses of jewels in the oceans?

O greatly compassionate one, amid all the many beings fashioned by the childish-minded,
who sport in the play of manifold minds,
may this body of yours, steadfast because unmoving,
like the ground of the enjoyments fit for every being, be victorious here.

In the eyes of the foolish-minded, compassion truly surpassing and weighty,
the perfect gathering born of virtue, holding the utpala in your palm:
"It is no hard thing for the round expanse of the ground of the Victors' family to be entrusted to you as your burden":
so even those of small intellect proclaim here.

Protector, bearing the sweet fragrance of liquor spread through the sky,
you crush the herds of trunked ones, drunk and trumpeting their loud cries,
and, striving in your roaring, you take up within these many forests
the vast play of the lord of elephants that surpasses the clouds.

You who have bound on armor with the resolve to accomplish the benefit of beings,
where is there for you even the slightest torment? O, your vow never falters!
For the hands of the folk of Māra's wives are listless indeed
at painting fresh rouge on the fair round of their cheeks.

The hosts of Victors, whose forms are delightful beyond compare and who dispel sorrow,
behold your youthful play, with tiger-claw designs
drawn in fine gold and the most lovely five crest-locks arranged:
by this you bring the eight guardians of the world to great delight.

Just as here, in you alone, there has truly come to be the whole of that wealth
which longs for the supreme distinction of sentiment and bears the tip of the bough of bliss,
so, with the thought, "How might beings become, through me, as if wholly pervaded by virtues?"
your aspiration has slowly come here to full ripeness.

Your chest is broad as the flank of the mountain of the immortals, an image of a lotus forest sprung from the plain of rays cast by the branches of your feet, adorned with a splendor brighter than hundreds of suns. Crusher of the pride of Antaka (Death), bearer of the grounds of the protector of the three existences, may you be victorious!

Those firmly bound by the vast mass of the darkness of delusion,
if they do not revere the blissful feast of attending upon your lotus feet,
dwell to no purpose in the vast caverns beneath the earth and in narrow existences,
and for a long time they are vanquished by the māras, O youthful sun of the lineage of the Victors!

For you of the lineage of ascetics, who practice a love that leans toward your lord,
the urging of these most unsteady lives by those who are supremely assembled
is made into something like a great fruit accomplished hundreds of times over,
just as water on a fallen lotus petal becomes a thing fit for adornment.

O Granter of the Supreme, purified by intimacy with the full flowering of your virtues,
the one rich in speech, honey by nature, the ground of sweet verse, and full of delight,
endowed with sacrifice, become the ground of worship as nectar for the ears:
that vase-bearing face of exegesis is wholly attained by that single maker of fair faces alone.

O Witness, in the spring of the Victors' diligence, which grants the three existences their joy,
where the mango blossoms spread forth in worship, through your activity,
with the joy of drinking the nectar, stilling the defilements, from the blossom clusters of the scriptures,
you nourish the ascetics as the six-legged bees and the learned as the sweetly calling cuckoos.

You who destroy the elephants of the many disputants, dwelling in the deep cave chamber
of the mountain that is the mind of the Sugata, lord of the forest of the three existences:
even at a single moment, through fear of you, the fierce great army of Māra
flees far away, like does born on the pleasure hills by the sea.

Protector renowned as the Subjugator, in order to tame those hard to tame,
at times, through the force of compassion, with the tip of your foot,
the three worlds, which the terrifying, smoke-like sound turns single-mindedly to longing to listen,
though lowly, are pressed down; they sink and swiftly go from their own places: such is the form you assume.

The chief wish-granting tree, which holds fine vases that are like the fruits of virtue,
that which has become perfect in beauty, is brought forth upon the crown by a single guru;
and as you teach, this branch of your arm, made supremely bright by the fingers of the waving hand,
appears like a flowering creeper swayed by the wind.

These ways of conduct, a treasury of portions free of turbidity, of the entire lineage of the buddhas:
if they are laid upon the heart, the great cool necklace of pearls loses its charm;
if the tongue savors them as it wishes, what is the renown of camphor?
If they are made to fill the ears, jeweled earrings are surely outdone.

O great being without equal, who has amassed the collections of merit,
the jeweled pedestal of your footstool, as though unsatisfied
by those who serve it bestowing the comfort of the palms of their hands,
when Indra of the hundred sacrifices bows down, receives tamāla leaves from his head, and itself shines forth.

Lord of the most steadfast who abide in the benefit of others, who through compassion
has become the abode of the manifold sentiment of longing gathered in: hear me! It is fitting that I ask.
O guru, by whom my swollen sins are utterly crushed,
bring about, even for a single instant, that state which purifies the removers and the purifiers.

In swelling the ocean of the lineage of the supreme Victors, the nectar-rayed moon; in keeping company with the lovely lady Wisdom, disdaining the talk of the Mind-Churner; amid the gathered, terrifying night of delusion, keeping the vow of the sun; in bringing about the utter destruction of the manifold beasts of affliction: may the glory of the tiger's play always protect you all!

To bring delight to all, from a moon-like form encircled by the light of the stainless autumn moon,
with a face possessing the priceless wealth of smiles, striving out of compassion itself
to tame the fierce grahas: having seen your state of victory over the three realms,
the hosts of divine sages, their body hair bristling like sprouts of inner fear, were overwhelmed.

For the immortals, whose mounts were broken down by fear, who came from very far away seeking steadfast refuge,
you are like the very cause that makes clear their minds, eminent in weighty reverence;
your might is measureless as the sky, your lion's roar surpasses the thunder of the clouds at the end of time;
you have brought the māras to utter ruin: may your lion's play be for the good of beings!

Pervading the assemblies, intent above all with a mind utterly pure like the center of the autumn sky,
rich in sublime compassion, swayed by the realm of measureless sentient beings tormented by unbearable anguish,
never abiding either in birth or in nirvāṇa, become supreme among all who possess speech:
conduct such as this is not to be found as the attainment of another, not even in name.

In the instant you restrain the corner of your eye, which is like rays of iron melted by fierce heat, he whose very self is born exalted by nature, lord of the four quarters, mastered by manifold radiance, the Five-Arrowed One, who spreads forth the vast display of magical transformation, together with his destroyers and his lovely women, even through the nature of the ten persons, makes whatever wondrous glory is most to be desired increase.

The vidyādhara maidens, who long to worship you who have vanquished ignorance, lovely women holding flower garlands, in supreme play, where swarms of bees drawn by their fragrance hum with joy, fill the whole sky with the radiating rays of their lovely sidelong glances, as if it were densely massed with clusters of full-blown kunda flowers, and as if washed again and again by limbs moist with dripping pollen.

O Lord, Blessed One, revered as guru, ocean of virtues that words cannot fathom:
the person traveling the path of existence who ever sings songs whose substance is your name
by this surely and easily attains a stainless fame, supremely beautiful like the autumn moon,
proclaiming the utmost perfection of form, of birth, of wealth and of conquest.

O Lord of Speech, Granter of the Supreme, one of excellent understanding, who, free of delight in the objects of wealth, has single-pointedly offered torma to you, is bathed in streams of nectar, and in the heavens, where swarms of honey-makers circle above, deeply intoxicated and singing, the one abode of joyous play, which can even sway the minds of sages, surely experiences sport with the glory of powerful enjoyments.

The person who takes refuge in you alone, treasury of the powers, comes to rely on an army with hosts of lordly elephants, towering high, announced by temples heavy with ichor, outmatching the clouds, stained with ichor, with throngs of foot soldiers girt in garments that radiate the rays of all their sharpened weapons, with fine warriors riding the most excellent chariots, captivating to the mind, and with the play of the finest horses that rest, advance, and obey.

O Lord, dwelling on the ground of the learned, become the abode of joy, attending upon the supreme ocean of generous giving, you whose rank is ultimate among those who speak, who have destroyed each doubt: whoever constantly recollects you is delighted by the leisure of a company of ministers sporting in their youth, giving voice to the fifth note, and, under the sway of that lovely woman whose face is the cool-rayed moon, this one becomes a lord of men together with Fortune.

O Lord, solely and wholly devoted to the welfare of the manifold, whose activity as the inner cause of patience is preeminent! Whoever holds your name as supreme, like a treasury of wealth, before that one appears a lake in the abode of the gods, playful and deeply perfumed by the sweet scent of ichor flowing from the lowered temples of the elephants of the quarters, marked by the feet of geese, swans and cranes, with swarms of bees humming high over forests of golden lotuses: that too is accomplished.

Translator's notes

This is a hymn to Mañjuśrī in which each verse exemplifies a Sanskrit meter, and many verses work the meter's name into their wording; the Tibetan preserves several of these as images, such as the gāyatrī, the "play of youth" (kumāralalitā), the "garland of lightning" (vidyunmālā), the "course of young snakes" (bhujagaśiśusṛtā) and the aravinda. The opening "modes of conduct" plays on vṛtta, which means both conduct and meter, and the three kinds are the classes of even, half-even and uneven meters. The short verses of one to five syllables a line are rendered word by word as the Tibetan gives them, so they read as fragments. In the verse on beings like children, the text's "into lightning" (glog tu) makes no sense and is read as "into wrong ways" (log tu). As in the first part, the verses work Sanskrit meter names into their imagery, for instance the "well-arrived" bees (svāgatā) and the vajra of Indra and Upendra (indravajrā, upendravajrā). The "four delusions" are taken as the four inverted views. The verse on those with a host of eyes, which alludes to the thousand-eyed Indra and the unblinking gaze of the gods, is obscure in the Tibetan, and its last line, naming the god of wealth (nor lha, possibly the Vasus), is translated literally. The "Bodiless One" and the "Five-Arrowed One" are both Kāma, the god of desire. As elsewhere in this hymn, several verses work Sanskrit meter names into their imagery; "the forehead ornament of spring" renders vasantatilaka, one such name, and "gentle melody" plays on Mañjughoṣa. The prayer at the end of the verse beginning "Though one of unequalled might" is obscure in the Tibetan and is translated literally. The verse on clearing the waters of the ocean ends without a main verb in the Tibetan, and is left incomplete in the same way. The "path with its eight divisions" is the noble eightfold path.
