About this text

The Five Great Instructions on Kurukullā, Arisen from Hevajra, called in Sanskrit the Hevajrodbhava Kurukullāyāḥ Pañca Mahopadeśāḥ and in Tibetan Kye'i rdo rje las byung ba'i ku ru kul le'i man ngag chen po lnga, is a short verse work of tantric yoga instructions centered on the red goddess Kurukullā. In five compact sections it teaches how the practitioner's seed syllable, driven by the winds through the subtle channels and the goddess's flowered arrow, is used to summon a person bodily, to summon a person on foot, to clear away snake poison, to increase wisdom, and to bring beings, even the king of the gods, under control, and it closes with a series of Kurukullā mantras for these rites. Its colophon attributes it to the master Śāntarakṣita and names the paṇḍita Dānaśīla as translator. It is Toh 1316 in the Tengyur, among the tantra commentaries associated with the Hevajra cycle.

Translation

In the language of India: hevajrodbhava kurukulleyā pañca mahopadeśaḥ. In the language of Tibet: The Five Great Instructions on Kurukullā, Arisen from Hevajra.

Homage to the Blessed Hevajra.

From the center of the heart, impelled by the supreme life wind, it enters within the supreme central channel and goes upward; swiftly it comes out from the center of the moon at the crown, and, moving gradually and clearly, it circles the left of the head; it comes out from the opening of the right ear, swiftly enters the opening of the arrow in the hand, and, the great seal having been meditated upon, the king of seeds itself, most swift through the vajra, blazing with red light rays, widens the opening at the tip of the arrow in the hand of the one to be accomplished, goes on ahead, and fully enters the opening of the ear; and, with the wind that moves clearly through the right nostril of the head, the mind, the syllable hrīḥ that abides in the center of the lotus of that one's heart, is swiftly carried off, and following that same path in order, it comes out from there and abides firmly in the center of the heart. Thus you bring under control the gods, together with the whole circle of the earth. This is the summoning of the body.

As before, coming out from the arrow, it enters the left side of the lotus of the one to be accomplished,
goes upward, makes the moon at the crown drip gradually, and enters as before.
When the yogin meditates firmly on it abiding in the anthers of the lotus,
through the act of dripping with passion it intoxicates the people of a woman of royal rank.
This is the summoning of the feet.

Clearly seeing it abiding in the center of the moon at the heart of one seized by a snake, one clears away the snake's poison with the bath of its light rays, a stream of abundant nectar; from the channel openings filled with light rays, too, the poison is destroyed, and it abides in the center of the radiant moon. Thus, for one seized by a snake, you, goddess, destroy the poison out of loving-kindness. This is the clearing away of snake poison.

If, with a steadily focused mind,
one imagines it at the tip of the tongue
in the form of a steady lamp,
the cataract of ignorance will be cleared away.
This is the increase of wisdom.

Clearly placed in the maṇḍala of wind, pierced in the heart by the hook,
bound at the neck by the noose, hair loosened, naked and distraught,
the earth itself, which you, goddess, have summoned by the power of yoga,
falls at my feet and forever takes the place of a servant.
Fully bound by a net of radiating red light,
fallen at my feet with hair loosened, struck by the arrow in the hand,
drawn in by the left wind, bound, and placed within the lotus,
it enters the center of the lotus at the navel, devoted, with palms joined.
When the yogin meditates in this way, bathing the crown with blood dripping from the lotus of the hero,
even Indra, lord of the gods, comes under control.
This is the bringing under control.

The Five Great Instructions on Kurukullā, Arisen from Hevajra, composed by the master Śāntarakṣita, are complete. They were translated by the paṇḍita Dānaśīla himself.

Translator's notes

The verses are compressed yogic instructions in which the subject, the practitioner's seed syllable, is largely implicit; the rendering supplies only what the lines require. "Sprul" in the snake-poison verse is read as "sbrul", snake, as the same verse itself spells it. "A woman of royal rank" (rgyal can ma) and "the earth itself" (nor 'dzin, which could also mean a wealthy person) are tentative. The translator's name is spelled Hānaśīla in this witness; it is read as Dānaśīla, the translator of the neighboring Kurukullā texts.
