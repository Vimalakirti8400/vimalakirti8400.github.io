About this text

An Extensive Commentary on the Eighteenth Chapter, known in Sanskrit as the Aṣṭādaśapaṭalavistaravyākhyā and in Tibetan as Le'u bco brgyad pa'i rgya cher 'grel pa, is a tantric commentary devoted to the eighteenth chapter of the Guhyasamāja Tantra, the so-called later tantra that analyzes and clarifies the teachings of the seventeen chapters of the root tantra. Working through that chapter phrase by phrase, it explains the secret of the body, speech and mind of all the tathāgatas, the stages of generation and completion, the empowerments, the four limbs of approach and accomplishment, the fierce rites understood as acts of compassion, and the praises offered by the bodhisattvas to Vajradhara. Its closing colophon attributes it to the venerable Nāgārjuna and records that it was translated and finalized by the Indian preceptor Kumārakalaśa. It is Toh 1784 in the Tengyur, where it belongs to the section of commentaries on the tantras, among the works of the Guhyasamāja cycle.

Now the meaning of the words will be explained. In the passage beginning "Then," the word "then" applies in the sense of "immediately after" the seventeenth chapter. As it is said:

Reference to what has gone before,
auspiciousness, and governing:
the word "then" and the word "therefore"
show the sequence of the true Dharma and the meaning of the tantra.

"Maitreya and the others" means the bodhisattvas Maitreya and the rest. Maitreya is named because he is the perfectly complete buddha who is chief of the future kingdom of the Dharma, since the future is spoken of as though it were past. "Great" refers to establishing all the tantras; one in whom there is "being," that is, the wish to understand, is a great being. "The secret of the body, speech and mind of all the tathāgatas" is the hidden meaning of the glorious Guhyasamāja, that is, its vajra words. "To be explained" means for the sake of resolving them once the questions have been asked. "By all things" means by body and the rest. "As they are" means unerringly. "How the pledge" means by not abandoning the two pledges, those to be guarded and those to be eaten. "Having heard" means having listened to the seventeen chapters. "Seen and realized" means through reflection and meditation. "All those tathāgatas" are Akṣobhya and the rest, who are of one continuum with the great Vajradhara. "In this very life" means to the one who grants the state of the great Vajradhara in this very life; and "they supplicated thus" is said in order to introduce what is explained below. That is the practice.

"How wonderful!" expresses amazement. "Samantabhadra" is the Blessed One, the great Vajradhara. "The establishing of body, speech and mind": the generation of a deity's body, distinct from the ordinary body and the rest, is wonderful. Likewise, those, body and the rest, are the three vajras, that is, the three natures of the three tathāgatas; "fully entered into" the great Vajradhara, the nature of the three realms, means that they have become the very self of the three realms, have entered, and have become of one taste, and that too is wonderful. These are the generation and completion stages.

Not only that: "as many as all sentient beings" means those dwelling within the realms, born from eggs and so on; "Vajradhara" means that through the light rays they arise as knowledge, resembling the great Vajradhara. Likewise, "the vajra nature of the three times" means that the tathāgatas dwelling in the three times arise, and that is wonderful. For that very reason, "the vajra of awakening" is the great Vajradhara, and "his state" means attaining a state like his. "Buddha vajra" is the world-transcending accomplishment, and "great miraculous power" is the worldly accomplishment; that all sentient beings come to attain it is wonderful. These are the generation and completion stages.

Likewise "ema" means great wonder. "This is supremely wonderful" means attaining vajra nature through the stages of entering and the rest. Likewise "peace" is the clear light, which is the nature of consciousness and has the characteristic of the cessation of the natures; "beyond the faculties" means attaining what is beyond the conceptions of faculties, objects and consciousnesses, and that too is wonderful. Then "the continuum of saṃsāra," and likewise "supreme": the characteristic of the wisdom body of illusory nature, arisen from the clear light, is endowed with the power to act for the benefit of all sentient beings, and that is wonderful. Likewise "nirvāṇa," whose characteristic is the gradual pacification of attachment and the rest, is the attainment of reality, whose characteristic is like the very essence of liberation, and that too is wonderful. Or else, "the continuum of saṃsāra" is nonabiding nirvāṇa: remaining, through the continuum of emanation bodies, in order to act constantly for the benefit of beings; and the samādhi of union is wonderful. These are the stages of vajra recitation, focusing on the mind, self-blessing, full awakening, and union.

Having heard the words of the bodhisattvas, "all the tathāgatas," Akṣobhya and the rest, having become one with the great Vajradhara, "spoke to the bodhisattvas": "Bodhisattvas," that is, Maitreya and the rest, is an address. "It is so" means: as you have explained it, so it is, and not otherwise. This is what is being said: just as the Blessed One Vajradhara taught the king of tantras through his words by way of explaining sixteen points, so you too have made it manifest by way of hearing, reflection and meditation. That is the meaning of the words.

In the passage beginning "Then," "the bodhisattvas" means in their thought and the rest; "the great beings," endowed with the intention to ask, "assembled," that is, became one. To "all the tathāgatas," Akṣobhya and the rest, they made offerings with the secret and outer offerings, the nectars, and the five offerings, prostrated, bowed and praised them, and "with one voice," that is, in a single tone, "supplicated," that is, requested as follows. That is the supreme victor of action. "O Blessed Ones, all you tathāgatas, this" is what is called the Guhyasamāja. "The king of tantras, very hard to obtain," is hard to come by because, in the manner explained before, it was not taught even in the time of the former tathāgata Kāśyapa.

Because the pledge is the chief thing, it is pledge, and not a multitude of activities. Because its methods are many, it is method. Therefore "accomplishing awakening" is the tantra that brings about the attainment of the state of Vajradhara; "the later of the later" relative to kriyā, yoga and the rest means that it is later even than the tantra of the later yoga: it is the tantra of great yoga. "Therefore, by the kindness of the Blessed One, we have for now understood." Through question and answer on words that are difficult and that have been set aside, "sentient beings," that is, listeners who understand through elaboration, they wish to benefit; and with the wish to take them under care, "we wish to supplicate you, the one great Vajradhara, who are of one nature with all of you," means "we must ask." That is the explanation by the sixteen points of word meaning.

Then "all those tathāgatas," Akṣobhya and the rest, "spoke thus to those bodhisattvas," that is, they said: "O assembled bodhisattvas, excellent, excellent!" These two words come from delight. For listeners who understand through elaboration and possess such qualities, in order that the meaning of the hidden words be understood, you are a "source," that is, a source of questions; and since this acts for the benefit of others, it is the second "well spoken." The reason for calling it well spoken is to be shown: "whatever hidden words of the tantra" means all those vajra words, the words of the glorious Guhyasamāja that are hard to understand and are found in each of its chapters. "Wishing to ask about all of them" is the intention underlying the words "your wish." Those are the sixteen points of word meaning.

In the passage beginning "That," "greatly delighted at hearing the well-spoken words" means this: in order to make very clear their reflection on the profound questions that dwelt in their minds, they opened their eyes wide, and again and again, by the force of faith, they prostrated and bowed; and "the doubts" to be explained below "became questioned," that is, they asked. "The bodhisattvas Maitreya and the rest" connects with what came before. Those are the sixteen points of word meaning. From "the secret" up to "How will the variety be performed?" is easy to understand, since the seventy-two questions were explained in the context of the condensed meaning.

Then "all those tathāgatas" are Mind Vajra and the rest. "The words that were chiefly requested by the bodhisattvas" are the words of the request. "Having heard" means heard. "Great" is the three wisdoms, and "awakening" is the natures. "Vajra" means that, destroying those, they focused on the clear light and entered it; "for an instant," that is, for a moment, "they became silent," that is, they became single-pointed in that samādhi. That is the stage of focusing on the mind.

The intention is this. In order to make the profound meaning clear, the tathāgatas first recollected reality and remained so, and knowing that they too had comprehended the clear light, "the bodhisattvas, greatly delighted," thought: "Surely they will cut through our doubts. Otherwise, why would the Blessed Ones be remaining in meditation?" For that very reason, to the one "whose self is compassion," that is, who is endowed with great compassion, the "perfect buddha," that is, the omniscient one, the "Sugata," who knows suchness well, the "protector," the teacher of all in the three realms, they, again and again eager to listen, "urged" him: wondering, "How will they cut through our doubts?", they became intent on gazing at their faces. That is the practice.

Rising from the samādhi of clear light, the tathāgatas spoke to the bodhisattvas in order to resolve their questions: "O bodhisattvas," that is, Maitreya and the rest, "your vast request," that is, the answer to your vast doubt, "listen to it," that is, hear it. How are they to listen? "With the vajra of body, speech and mind" means with body, speech and mind single-pointed. If one asks what sort of vast request it is: the words left unstated are "the vajra of body, speech and mind is the nature of body and the rest, which has become the tathāgatagarbha." Those are the sixteen points of word meaning.

In the passage beginning "Then," "all the bodhisattvas" are Maitreya and the rest. "All those tathāgatas" means endowed with the connection of Akṣobhya and the rest. "The command of acceptance" is the instruction by which they are taken under care. "Received it on the crown of the head" means received it with faith. "Each their own" means of each one's own self. "The great bodhisattvas, holders of the vajra," are the body and the rest of glorious Vajrasattva, the nature of all the tathāgatas. Toward whatever is the nature of the world-transcending deity, they "took up the focus," with well-spoken words going before. "They became silent" means that they became single-pointed in order to listen to the teaching of the Dharma. That is the stage of what is to be accomplished and the generation stage.

In the passage beginning "Then," the meaning is that the tathāgatas, seeing the bodhisattvas endowed with minds of faith in the Dharma and with single-pointedness, generated great compassion in their own minds, and in order to remove with one voice the doubts in their questions, "spoke," that is, said. That is the practice.

In the passage beginning "Body, speech and mind," "threefold" is the three, body, speech and mind. They are free of ordinary nature and enter emptiness through recollection of the mantra beginning oṃ śūnyatā; and since none of this can be attained by the śrāvakas and the rest, it is called "secret." That is the initial union. Likewise, the three, body and the rest, emanating as the forms of deities, is secret: that is the supreme victor of the maṇḍala. The three, body and the rest, melting into light, is secret: that is the supreme victor of action. The three channels that are the cause of producing beings, the parasol of desire and the rest, are secret: that is the generation stage. The withdrawal of body and the rest, the subtle drop abiding at the three tips of the nose, is secret: that is the completion stage. Body, speech and mind are secret because, through the stages of the entering and the rest of the three syllables, they attain the state of clear light: that is the stage of vajra recitation.

The three that are the cause of body, speech and mind are the consciousnesses and the groups of natures; because these dissolve into the clear light, they are secret: that is the stage of focusing on the mind. Body, speech and mind are the dharma body, the enjoyment body and the emanation body; because these arise from the clear light like a rainbow, they are secret: that is the stage of self-blessing. Desire, intermediate desire and freedom from desire are the three, body, speech and mind; because these are purified by the fourth, they are secret: that is the stage of full awakening. The mingling of both the conventional and the ultimate is the three, body, speech and mind; because they abide as the very essence of nonabiding nirvāṇa, they are secret: that is the stage of union.

The three, body and the rest, are method and wisdom, whose nature is the three beings; because these are joined, they are secret. Body, speech and mind, blessed by the three beginning with rakta, are the vajra and the lotus, and their union is secret: that is the cause. The vajra and lotus, generated by the three syllables hūṃ, phaṭ and the rest and marked by them, being set in motion is secret: that is the action. Body, speech and mind are secret because the peace that is the nature of great bliss is realized: that is the result.

Object, faculty and consciousness are three; because, when these meet, one enjoys that object and body and the rest give rise to bliss, they are secret: that is individual withdrawal. Realizing that very nature of the three, body and the rest, as the self of the five tathāgatas is secret: that is meditative concentration. Joining by the sequence of going and coming through the stages of entering and the rest, the emanation and withdrawal of a drop the size of a white mustard seed, which is the nature of the three, body and the rest, is secret: that is life and exertion (prāṇāyāma). Holding the drop at the three tips of the nose, vajra and the rest, by withdrawing the three, body and the rest, is secret: that is retention. Emanating all the tathāgatas, the essence of the three, body and the rest, into the realm of space is secret: that is recollection. The three, body and the rest: withdrawing them and, through the yoga of devotion, focusing on the body of the great Vajradhara, the essence of the expanse of nonabiding nirvāṇa, is secret: that is samādhi. Putting into practice the pledges of the three, body and the rest, to be guarded and to be eaten, is secret: that is the pledge. And by taking up the vows of the tathāgata families of body and the rest, accomplishing the collections of acts, pacifying and the rest, is secret: that is the vow. This is the establishment. Thus, by teaching the sixteen points of word meaning through the word "secret," the whole meaning of the tantra is taught.

In the passage beginning "Assembly": it is "assembly" because it gathers together, and that is the brief teaching. "All the buddhas," the aggregates or the tathāgatas, are assembled; the meaning is that this is the defining characteristic of that king of tantras. Moon, sun, seed syllable, emblem, emanation and generation of the body, that is, the five full awakenings, and the emanation and withdrawal of the five tathāgatas, are the initial union and the supreme victor of the maṇḍala. When the body is complete, all the tathāgatas abiding there is the assembly: that is the supreme victor of action. Because the mind of awakening emanates from the seventy-two thousand channels and generates the three realms, the nature of all the buddhas, it is assembly: that is the generation stage. Through the union of method and wisdom, by the sound of supreme passion the host of victors, without exception, enters and becomes of one taste at the tip of the vajra jewel; the essence of the drop of the mind of awakening is the assembly: that is the completion stage. Or else, emanating the drop of the mind of awakening through the vajra path and, within the center of the lotus, emanating the pledge maṇḍala in the form of the deities of the maṇḍala, is the assembly: that is the generation stage. At the time of the irreversible empowerment, the entering of all the tathāgatas through the Brahmā aperture of the disciple is the assembly: that is the supreme victor of action.

Because the forty-nine letters of the alphabet, whose nature is the masculine, feminine and neuter genders, are gathered into the three syllables, it is assembly: that is the stage of vajra recitation. Because the hundred and sixty natures are gathered into the three, wisdom, method and ignorance, it is assembly: that is the stage of focusing on the mind. Because the three realms, or the three bodies, are gathered into illusoriness itself, it is assembly: that is the stage of self-blessing. Because desire and the rest are gathered into the clear light, it is assembly: that is the stage of full awakening. The assembly in which the conventional and the ultimate are gathered together is the stage of union. The assembly that is the perfect union of method and wisdom is the cause. "All the buddhas" means setting out oṃ and the rest, and setting the vajra and lotus in motion, is the assembly: that is the action. Gathering all the aggregates and the rest is the nature of great bliss; the ultimate is the assembly: that is the result.

The assembly of object and faculty is individual withdrawal. Meditating on them in the form of the tathāgatas is assembly: that is meditative concentration. Urging the mind of awakening, the nature of all the elements, out of all the limbs, gathering it at the base of the three tips of the nose, and emanating and withdrawing it through the stages of entering and the rest, is assembly: that is life and exertion. That same mind of awakening, free of entering and the rest and gathered in a steady stream like the flame of a clear lamp, is retention. Emanating buddhas and bodhisattvas from it and gathering the three realms into the expanse of space in the manner of a maṇḍala is assembly: that is recollection. Withdrawing them and gathering them before the great Vajradhara, the nature of all the buddhas, is assembly: that is samādhi. The five hooks and the five jewels: gathering the nature of all the buddhas into the essence of the mind of awakening through the stages of purification and the rest is assembly: that is the accomplishment.

Thus these words of the Guhyasamāja, which teach by means of the sixteen points of word meaning, are, in their nature as what expresses and what is expressed, the tantra of the glorious Guhyasamāja. Of these, what expresses is the words of the assembly; and although the words of the secret are a special feature of it, they are what is expressed.

Now I shall teach the connection. It should be understood that, when all the chapters are analyzed, they stand set out in three and are distinguished accordingly. This is what is taught in the passage beginning "That very fifth."

The fifth is the chapter on conduct; the ninth is the chapter on the samādhi of the yogin whose character runs counter to convention, which definitively explains the vajra words spoken there; the seventeenth is the chapter on pledges and vows; and the thirteenth is the chapter on the full array of rites. Buddhas are the perfected yogins and bodhisattvas the yogins not yet perfected, and the accomplishment taught by them is what tames beings. Therefore, since the perfection of others' benefit is foremost here, it is "great," that is, supreme. For it establishes that the yogin is to act for the benefit of sentient beings by summoning, through the three kinds of conduct, the consciousness and so forth of others, the malicious and the rest; through the full array of rites, pacifying and the rest; and also by way of pledges and vows.

Likewise, the fourth is the chapter on the mind maṇḍala; the sixteenth is the chapter on the body and speech maṇḍalas; the eighth is the chapter on the secret empowerment; and the twelfth is the chapter on transformation into the great seal (mahāmudrā). "The master's work" is the activity of the master; "the common work" is the teaching of accomplishments in order to take care of disciples; "observance" is entering the maṇḍala according to one's inclination; and "vow" is meditating on the form of the deity, that is, the generation stage.

Likewise, the sixth is the chapter on the blessing of body and the rest; the second is the chapter on the awakening mind; the tenth is the chapter on exhorting the tathāgata essence by means of mantra; and the fourteenth is the chapter on the full array of rites in which mantra is foremost. "Fierce conduct" is the passage beginning oṃ sarbba tathāgatā kāya bajra svabhāba ātmako'haṃ; "meditating on the absence of entities" is the passage beginning "entity"; and there are the passages beginning oṃ āḥ hūṃ and oṃ ruru sphuru jvala tiṣṭha siddhi locani sarbba artha sādhani svāhā. In these, "attachment" is the blessing of the body vajra and the rest, meditating on emptiness, exhorting the essence, and performing pacifying and the rest, in order that these be taught. For that very reason it is near accomplishment, that is, the near accomplishment of the state of the great Vajradhara; and "vow" is that in which training is foremost. These are the initial union, the supreme victor of action, and the accomplishments.

The seventh is the chapter on special conduct; the third is the chapter on the deity; the eleventh is the chapter on the causes of mantra and the gnosis body; and the fifteenth is the chapter on the illusion-like deity body. "The field of accomplishment" means that the marks of the deity bodies of Vajradhara and the rest serve as its cause. For that very reason, the vow of approach and accomplishment is what accomplishes the body of the Tathāgata, and it is foremost in accomplishing one's own benefit. For through conduct and meditation on the deity of realization, through meditation on the deity fully perfected from the first conception, and through the ordinary body being purified by the illusion-like samādhi, one comes to attain the special state of the deity. That is accomplishment, the generation stage, and the stage of self-blessing.

Thus, through the seventh and the rest, the perfection of one's own benefit is to be accomplished; through the sixth and the rest, the perfection of the means to it; through the fifth and the rest, the perfection of others' benefit; and through the fourth and the rest, the perfection of the means to it. In this way, the teaching of the chapters in which the perfection of one's own benefit, the perfection of others' benefit, and the perfection of the means to both are respectively foremost is the connection. Its first chapter is what becomes the result, and so is not included within the connection; and its eighteenth chapter, by way of explaining the vajra words, has the nature of release; it does not state the connection.

Some say that the chapters, in their four groups, express the four branches, approach and the rest. That is not right, because not even a single chapter is found that leaves out approach and the rest, since in all of them that is foremost; and because, even if it were accepted, it would be mistaken. To explain: if the fifth and the rest are held to be great accomplishment, then great accomplishment, in its nature as the placing of the syllables, is absent from them, because conduct, the summoning of consciousness and the rest, the classification of rites, and pledges and vows are what is foremost there. In the fourth and the rest, the accomplishing of the body for the sake of entering the maṇḍala and taking care of disciples is not approach, because emptiness is absent there. Since the sixth and the rest accomplish the blessing of body and the rest, it is not right that they are near accomplishment in its nature as the gathering of the seed. Then, if the seventh and the rest accomplish the deity body in order to accomplish one's own benefit, emptiness is not approach.

As for what expresses the completion stage, approach and the rest understood as the union of method and wisdom and so forth are not the meaning of the fifth and the rest. Approach and the rest according to the sequence of the four means are not right, and approach and the rest according to the sequence of the maṇḍala rite are not right either, because the fourth chapter and the rest state the maṇḍala rite and so forth, while the others do not state it.

For, first of all, in the generation stage, according to the sequence of the four vajras, approach and the rest consist of emptiness, the gathering of the seed, the completion of the form, the placing of the syllables, and so forth. In the completion stage and the rest as well, method and wisdom, the union of vajra and lotus, its stirring, and the experience of great bliss are approach and the rest. Likewise, in the completion stage too, meditating on the six-branch yoga is approach. Likewise, in the generation stage too, the four, namely conciliation, giving, division, and punishment, are approach, near accomplishment, accomplishment, and great accomplishment.

In the context of the maṇḍala, too, meditating on Uṣṇīṣacakravartin, on Amṛtakuṇḍalin, on one's own special deity, and on Vajradhara are approach and the rest. Having summarized it in this way, the sixteen divisions made by approach and the rest are included within the two stages. It is therefore not right to say that the chapters and so forth express approach and the rest, as though it were not found in the same way in the others. Therefore an explainer who relies on his own invention leads worldly people into error, because he has neither scripture nor reasoning. Since that view goes much too far, this has been the explanation of the rite, that is, of the genuine meaning.

Now I shall explain the purpose of the three verses. On the passage beginning "Tathāgata": the work of all the tathāgatas is enlightened activity. "Subduing" is worldly, such as cutting down, and transcendent, such as entering the buddha field of Akṣobhya. "Benefiting" is worldly activity, such as pacifying, and transcendent activity, such as accomplishing the deity body; it is conduct, that is, capacity, for the sake of attaining the state of Vajradhara. "Taming" applies to those endowed with qualities such as faith; "the hard to tame" are those who harm the Three Jewels; and "peaceful sentient beings" are people free from faults such as attachment. "Entering" extends as far as "the Blessed One taught definitively, in order that they enter suchness."

Likewise, the connection with the generation stage: by way of the generation stage, the four vajra rites, approach and the rest, proceed in the sequence of the four vajras. "All accomplishments" are the common body of the five tathāgatas. Whatever meditation of the generation stage and of accomplishment the Blessed One taught is the very text connected with accomplishing one's own benefit; that intention, following the sequence of the chapters, is one's own benefit. This is the threefold set, the initial union and the rest.

On the passage beginning "maṇḍala": "maṇḍala" is the mind maṇḍala and the rest, and it is for the disciple who has entered it that the master acts. "Observance" is the vows and so forth. "Connection" is the empowerment of the union of method and wisdom, preceded by the knowledge empowerment. "Text" is the words on the suchness of the maṇḍala. "The perfect master" is the one who accomplishes the perfection of his own and others' benefit, who shows the encouragement of both the Tathāgata and Vajradhara. And the full ripening of the disciple by the guru from the path of mantra, that is, by the rite of the Guhyasamāja, namely whoever is to be tamed: that is the meaning of "purpose." This explains the accomplishment of the perfection of others' benefit.

I shall explain the purpose of the purpose. "Of good observance" means possessing the vows; "empowered" means having obtained empowerment; "great-souled" means one who realizes the state of Vajradhara through the sequence of hearing and the rest. "Buddhas and bodhisattvas," by way of teaching those of sharp, middling, and dull faculties: "complete liberation" is whatever purifies attachment and the rest and makes them into luminosity itself. That is the meaning of "purpose": it is accomplishment.

On the passage beginning "wisdom": wisdom is the characteristic of the selflessness of persons and phenomena, that is, emptiness. Method is great compassion. Their union is explained as the ultimate meditation of the Blessed One abiding on the thirteenth ground, that of Vajradhara: perfect union.

In the extended explanation, "that which is the absence of entities" is wisdom, the emptiness of all things, because it is supreme gnosis. Likewise, method is "the characteristic of entities," that is, the range of synonyms for the nature called compassion. This is the completion stage.

Alternatively, wisdom is seeing oneself as like a cloudless sky by meditating on the three doors of liberation, emptiness and the rest. Method is accomplishing the body by way of gathering the seed and so forth. Their union is the blessing of body and the rest, and also the praise.

To explain this: "that which is the absence of entities" is meditation on emptiness, that is, wisdom. Method is "the characteristic of entities": the meaning is that one focuses on the deity body capable of acting for the benefit of beings, and by that the benefit of beings will be accomplished. This is the initial union, the supreme king of the maṇḍala, and the supreme king of action.

Alternatively, wisdom is the five channels, beginning with the so-called parasol of desire, in the middle of the seventy-two thousand channels. Method is the awakening mind that moves within them, and their union is their becoming of one taste in the central channel. "That which is an entity" is the channels that serve as the basis of great bliss: that is wisdom. Method is "the characteristic of entities," which serves as the cause that produces beings without exception; it is the sign that indicates the state of the great Vajradhara, meaning the awakening mind. This is the generation stage.

Wisdom is the three seeds of the tathāgatas, whose nature is supreme gnosis. Method is the three winds, the life wind and the rest. One recites through the sequence of their union and so forth. "That which is the absence of entities" is the three syllables, which serve as the sign of luminosity, the state of Vajradhara: that is wisdom. "Method, the characteristic of entities": since it comes to indicate the suchness of the tathāgatas Vairocana and the rest, method is wind. This is the stage of vajra recitation.

Wisdom is the thirty-three natures of wisdom. Method is the forty natures of method. Union is the seven natures of ignorance. Yoga is the characteristic of their entering luminosity. "That which" is as before. This is the stage of focusing on the mind.

That which emanates all at once from luminosity, whose nature is method and wisdom, is called the yoga of union; it is called the yoga of the great seal. "That which" is as before. This is the stage of self-blessing. Method and wisdom are the samādhis of holding as a whole and of subsequent dissolution.

Their union is called yoga because it makes this manifest. "That which is the absence of entities" is the samādhi of holding as a whole: that is wisdom. "Entity" is the samādhi of subsequent dissolution, whose characteristic is to make the three realms disappear: that is method. This is the stage of full awakening.

Wisdom and method are the ultimate and the conventional. Their union, having become indivisible, is yoga, and it is called the samādhi of union. "That which" is easy to understand. This is the stage of union.

Method and wisdom are the consort and the man, and their union is called yoga. "That which" is as before. This is the nature.

Wisdom and method are the lotus and the vajra. Their union is yoga. This is the cause.

Wisdom and method: placing hūṃ and phaṭ and so forth is the yoga of union. This is the action.

Union in the drop of the awakening mind, whose nature is wisdom and method, holding it at the tip of the vajra, is yoga. This is the result.

Wisdom is the senses and method is their objects. Their union and enjoyment is yoga. This is withdrawal.

Wisdom is the senses; method is the tathāgatas. Their union is yoga. This is meditative concentration.

Wisdom is the ultimate awakening mind, and method is the conventional awakening mind. Their union is yoga. Their emanation and gathering back, in the sequence of that and of what follows, are breath and its control.

As before, holding the drop, the size of a mustard seed, whose nature is wisdom and method, at the tip of the nose and the other tips, is union and yoga. This is retention.

The nature of wisdom and method is the tathāgatas embraced by goddesses. Their union, emanating into the sphere of space, is yoga. This is recollection.

Wisdom and method are the dharma body and the enjoyment body. Their union, joined together with the emanation body, is yoga. This is samādhi.

Wisdom is the five seeds and method is the five nectars. Their union, the eating of the nectars blessed by the seeds, is yoga. This is accomplishment.

By the passage beginning "Tantra," the meaning of tantra is to be taught. In particular, it is a continuum because it is adorned by a continuum of collections of speech in which the sixteen points of word meaning are foremost; that itself is what is called tantra. Because it expands the sixteen points of word meaning, it is tantra, that is, a treatise whose nature is a collection of sounds. Its words are the sixteen meanings.

"That tantra becomes threefold" is the brief teaching. The passage beginning "ground" is the extensive explanation. "Ground" is the tantra of method, because it holds cause and result; it is the words that express mantras, mudrās, and the rest. "Nature" is the tantra of the cause, in which the awakening mind, lacking intrinsic nature, becomes the cause for attaining the state of Vajradhara. "The unassailable," that which does not perish, is the tantra of the result, in which, through the generation stage, or through the subtle yoga and the rest of the completion stage, or through vajra recitation and the rest, or through the six-branch yoga, the body of Vajradhara and the rest is shown. These are the three divisions of tantra.

Glorious Vajradhara is also threefold: the causal Vajradhara, the resultant Vajradhara, and the Vajradhara of method. Of these, the causal Vajradhara is the great Vajradhara, the primordial protector. The resultant Vajradhara is the tathāgatas, Akṣobhya and the rest, who arose from him and arose from following after him. The Vajradhara of method is threefold: protecting the place, protecting oneself, and protecting the yoga. Of these, protecting the place is emanating the ten wrathful ones and driving away the obstructors. Protecting oneself is meditating solely on ultimate truth; its characteristic is entering emptiness by recollecting the mantra beginning oṃ śūnyatā. Protecting the yoga is twofold: that which has the character of the accumulation of merit and that which has the character of the accumulation of gnosis. Of these, that which has the character of the accumulation of merit is, having generated the mind of great compassion, meditating on the four abodes of Brahmā. That which has the character of the accumulation of gnosis is the meditation on the doors of liberation. This is called the Vajradhara of method.

I shall explain this extensively. The ground is emptiness and its mantra, and that is the cause; it is called the nature and the essence. As it is said:

Just as sweetness is of molasses
And heat is the nature of fire,
So emptiness is held to be
The very nature of all phenomena.

The Blessed Maitreya also says:

Because the dharmadhātu is undifferentiated,
A division of lineage is not tenable;
It is through the division of the phenomena that rest upon it
That its division is well known.

Likewise, "the unassailable" is the indestructible: the dharma body, permanent by nature; the enjoyment body, permanent in not perishing; and the emanation body, permanent as a continuum. The unassailable is the essence of the great seal, the nature of the great Vajradhara, who is the nature of the three bodies. Not being of a perishable character, it is called the result. As for "ground," it is the ground because, in the essence of method, it holds and sets in place cause and result. Whatever one meditates on in order to attain the great seal, following the stages of meditation on mantras, mudrās, and the rest, is method. The tantra of these three is summarized by meaning: tantra, whose nature is a collection of sounds, and Vajradhara, who is expressed by its meaning, are summarized, and so the meaning should be understood as summarized in three ways. This is the threefold set, the initial union and the rest.

By the passage beginning "five families," suchness and the rest are to be taught in brief. "Nature" is the secret. "Awakening vajra" was spoken by the Blessed One, the great Vajradhara. I shall explain this extensively. "That itself in five aspects" is the three realms, whose nature is the five aggregates, because that is their nature. This is the generation stage.

Its purified nature is the nature of the five tathāgatas: this is the generation stage alone. Suchness, whose nature is the five gnoses, the mirror-like and the rest, which are the purified essence of the five consciousnesses, is the completion stage. Suchness as the five aspects of full awakening and so forth is the initial union, the supreme king of the maṇḍala, and the supreme king of action.

The purification of speech, mind, body, the conventional, and both together, through the sequence of vajra recitation and the rest, is the fivefold suchness: vajra recitation, focusing on the mind, self-blessing, the stage of full awakening, and the stage of union.

When object and sense are made one, that is the fivefold suchness: it is withdrawal. Devotion to the five tathāgatas, and the five-limbed meditative concentration, namely initial thought, analysis, joy, bliss, and one-pointedness of mind, is the fivefold suchness: it is meditative concentration. Emanating the five winds, the life wind and the rest, in the form of drops is the fivefold suchness: it is breath and its control. Holding the drop that has become the essence of the five aggregates at the center of the lotus is the fivefold suchness: it is retention. Emanating buddhas and bodhisattvas whose nature is the five tathāgatas is the fivefold suchness: it is recollection. The five bodies, the five eyes, the five superknowledges, and the five gnoses; enjoying the five qualities of desire; purifying the five destinies; and emanating and gathering back the five rays of light, which are the essence of the five great elements and the nature of the five tathāgatas: the great seal body of Vajradhara is the fivefold suchness: it is samādhi.

The blessing by the five hooks, the five nectars, and the five seeds, whose nature is the five tathāgatas, is the fivefold suchness: it is the pledge. Taking up the training connected with the five families is the fivefold suchness: it is the vow. That is the summary: the sixteen meanings.

The three secrets have already been explained. As for "secret": because it is secret, the surpassing deity is the great Vajradhara; this is the generation stage. Because it is the nature of the five gnoses it is surpassing, and because it is the nature of the secret it is also surpassing. The surpassing deity is a drop the size of a mustard seed, or the size of a hundred-thousandth part of the tip of a hair; this is the completion stage.

The pledge is the supreme. Because it liberates the realm of sentient beings to be tamed in accordance with their dispositions, although the family of Vajradhara is one, it becomes a hundredfold, because each tathāgata is divided into five and so on; so runs the passage. That is merely an illustration: for as long as there exist the coarse and subtle habitual tendencies of the minds and mental factors of sentient beings, so many families did the Blessed One teach as means for their purification. Therefore the families are equal to the sands of the river Ganges; since they are families without number, it is vast. This is the generation stage.

I shall explain the awakening mind. Without beginning or end, permanent by nature, the essence of the dharma body, free from arising and perishing: for that very reason it is peaceful, because the conceptions and stains of the habitual tendencies of affliction and of purification are pacified. Entities are the five aggregates and the rest, the nature of the three realms, and non-entities are emptiness and the rest; their exhaustion is freedom from their separate appearances. For that very reason, since it is the basis of all beings without exception and the cause of the two bodies, it is chief, that is, the lord. Emptiness is the emptiness of all things. Compassion is great compassion. Their being indivisible, having become one, is the essence of gnosis free from obscuration, the nature of awakening; and the mind is the supreme suchness experienced directly by yoga. That is what is called the awakening mind. This is the completion stage.

Alternatively, the beginning is appearance and the increase of appearance; the end is the attainment of appearance; their absence is peace, that is, luminosity. Entity is the nature of wisdom and method. Non-entity is the nature of ignorance, because in luminosity there is no conception. Because that nature of entity and non-entity is exhausted, it is exhaustion, that is, the emptiness of all. As for the awakening mind, awakening is luminosity, and the mind that has entered luminosity is the three consciousnesses; the Blessed One called this the awakening mind. This is the stage of focusing on the mind.

Alternatively, the beginning is attachment and freedom from attachment; the end is middling attachment; being free from these is being without beginning or end. Peace is self-awareness, that is, the dharma body. Entities are twilight, night, and day. Non-entities are the five signs, the mirage and the rest. Because these are exhausted and attainment occurs, entity and non-entity are exhausted. Because it has become the result, it is chief. Emptiness is wisdom and compassion is method; their being inseparable is the awakening attained through the power of union, that is, full awakening, and the mind that is its nature is called the awakening mind. This is the stage of full awakening.

Alternatively, because it proceeds as a continuum, it is without beginning or end. Because it gives rise to one-pointedness of mind, it is peaceful. Entity and non-entity are the world of sentient beings and the vessel world; although they are its nature, because they have become meditation, their conceptions are exhausted, and so entity and non-entity are exhausted. Because it gives rise to the three realms, it is chief. Emptiness is ultimate emptiness, and compassion is conventional form. "Inseparable" means their indivisibility: the indivisibility of the conventional and the ultimate is called the awakening mind. This is the result.

Alternatively, what is called the awakening mind is the wish for perfect, complete awakening for the sake of others. As it is said:

Generating the mind is, for the sake of others,
The wish for perfect, complete awakening.

It has two divisions: the aspiring awakening mind and the engaging awakening mind.

Of these, the aspiring awakening mind is the pursuit of the profound and the vast, through the wish, "Having become a buddha, I shall act for the benefit of sentient beings," together with compassion. Engaging in the actual undertaking of the conduct of awakening and so forth is the engaging awakening mind. These are the two. As it is said in the Avataṃsaka Sūtra: "Son of noble family, hard to find are sentient beings who generate the mind toward unsurpassed, perfect, complete awakening; and those who actually engage in perfect, complete awakening are far harder to find even than they."

Then, through the divisions of grounds and perfections taught by the Blessed One, it becomes manifold. Here, for the present, it is tenfold: the benefits of the awakening mind; the sevenfold worship, beginning with the confession of misdeeds; taking up the awakening mind; heedfulness; guarding it with vigilance; patience; diligence; meditative concentration; wisdom; and dedication. These are the ten. That is extensive, and should be looked up in the Compendium of Training.

Likewise, it also becomes twenty-twofold. As the noble Maitreya says:

It is like earth, gold, the moon, fire,
A treasure, a source of jewels, the ocean,
A vajra, a mountain, medicine, a spiritual friend,
A wish-fulfilling jewel, the sun, a song,
A king, a treasury, a great highway,
A mount, a spring,
A pleasant sound, a river, and a cloud:
Thus it is of twenty-two kinds.

This should be viewed as follows:

By division according to the occasions of aspiration,
The aspiring mind is of three kinds;
The minds called engaging
Are, by that, nineteen.

Of these, the aspiring mind has three divisions. That which is accompanied by aspiration has the characteristic of wishing to realize all phenomena fully; it is like the earth. That which is accompanied by intention has the characteristic of wishing to practice the six perfections; because it does not rely on what will come about in the future or here in this life, it is like fine gold. That which is accompanied by superior intention has the characteristic of wishing to meditate on the thirty-seven factors of awakening; because it proceeds with the increase of the entire white side, it is like the new moon.

The engaging mind is of nineteen kinds: that accompanied by application; the ten perfections, those accompanied by generosity and the rest; that accompanied by the superknowledges; that accompanied by the accumulations of merit and gnosis; that accompanied by the factors of awakening; that accompanied by calm abiding and insight; that accompanied by dhāraṇī and eloquence; that accompanied by the teaching of the Dharma; that accompanied by the path of the one vehicle; and that accompanied by the dharma body.

Then, by four occasions,
All of these are pervaded:
By the divisions of result, cause, application and the rest,
And of the beginner.

Of these, the first three belong to the occasion of the beginner; the fourth belongs to that and to the occasion of the portions conducive to definite penetration. The minds of the perfection of generosity and the rest belong to the occasion of the cause. The eight minds accompanied by the superknowledges and the rest belong to the occasion of the result.

In the first, the kind of aspiration,
The Protector taught it rightly;
The kind of engagement, generosity and the rest,
Is well known as the second.

The aspiring mind, of the kind of aspiration, is explained as threefold. From the point at which, preceded by taking the vow, one engages in the collections of generosity and the rest, it is the engaging mind. The vow, moreover, should be taken from a master who holds the vow and is learned and powerful. If such a one is not present, it should be taken from the buddhas and bodhisattvas, visualizing them in the mind as present before one. Moreover:

Without method there is no wisdom;
Generosity and the rest without wisdom are not perfections.
Further, having generated calm abiding,
One should therefore generate wisdom.

Why is that? Because generosity and the rest without wisdom do not obtain the name of perfection, nor do they become method. Therefore, in order to generate wisdom, one should strive to generate calm abiding.

Whoever has moral discipline and bears suffering patiently,
Whoever has diligence, is steadfast and heedful,
And applies himself thoroughly and correctly: at that time
His samādhi will swiftly be accomplished.

Abiding in the three, moral discipline and the rest, and in the collection of conditions for calm abiding, in a place agreeable to the mind, one should sit on a soft seat with the body upright and, preceded by great compassion, with the intention of delivering all sentient beings, generate the samādhi whose characteristic is one-pointedness of mind. This is the division of the awakening mind in brief.

Emptiness is the nature of the perfection of wisdom. Compassion is the five perfections, generosity and the rest. Their inseparability is called the awakening mind. For without wisdom a bodhisattva does not obtain the name of the perfection of generosity, and wisdom apart from the perfections of generosity and the rest is not wisdom, because it becomes a nihilistic view. Therefore, by focusing on the awakening mind in which emptiness and compassion are not different, one attains buddhahood itself, the dharma body, which is without beginning or end and free from entity and non-entity. This is the completion stage.

In order to answer the question about the man of knowledge, the word "body" is stated. The vajra of body, speech, and mind is the great Vajradhara. The indivisible essence is following him and becoming one with him. The divisible essence is appearing as distinct. "Knowledge" is being joined with the gnosis seal, that is, embracing her; "man" is the mantra body and the gnosis body. That is what is called the man of knowledge. What is being said is this: having transformed into the great Vajradhara, meditating on Mind Vajradhara and the rest through the two stages is what is called the man of knowledge. These are the generation stage and the stage of self-blessing.

This explains the meaning of the thirteen extracted mantras. "Fivefold assembled" means this: the five consciousnesses that are bound up with the afflictions are the nature of the five lower prongs, and likewise the five upper prongs are the nature of the five wisdoms, the mirror-like and the rest. That which abides in the manner of the discordant factor and its antidote is called the vajra. Because the fivefold assembled is abandonment and the fivefold knowing is knowledge, it is vajra by the meaning of the two letters va and jra. Since one holds it, one is Vajradhara: this is the completion stage. Alternatively, the five channels are gathered within the bhaga; abandoning them as distinct, and "knowing," since great bliss is realized: this is the vajra banner. Holding it, through being fit for the work of holding it: the consciousness, the mentation, which is the awakening mind, is called the vajra holder. This is its nature, cause, action and result.

In the passage beginning "exists": those who say that things emanated by the supreme Lord exist are the proponents of the Lord (Īśvara). Those who say that things exist as transformations through the three times are the Sāṃkhyas. Those who say that there is what is presided over by the self are the Naiyāyikas. Those who say that things exist as the nature of Brahmā are the Mīmāṃsakas. The proponents of transformation, who say that even curd turns back into what it was before, are the Nirgranthas (Jains). Those who say that beings ripen through time are the proponents of time. Because it is born again and again and flows away again and again, it is the person; those who say that things exist as its nature are the proponents of the person. Those who say that sound is the supreme Brahman and that all things exist as its nature are the grammarians. Since by one single power all these are sent forth and gathered back, those who say that all things exist as its nature are the proponents of power. Those who say that all conditioned things are impermanent, suffering, empty and selfless are the Vaibhāṣikas. Those who say that cognition exists but things do not are the pratyekabuddhas. Those who say that cognition itself, free from external aspects, like a cloudless sky, is manifold are the Investigators. Those who say that the apprehended, together with the apprehender, is empty because it is free from being one or many are the Mādhyamikas. All these are proponents of existence.

The proponent of non-existence says:

As long as you live, live happily;
For you there is no death and passing on.
Once dead and turned to ashes,
How could there be any coming back?

Those who hold that only the elements are to be known are the Lokāyatas (materialists). The middle between those, existence and non-existence, transcending the nature of both sides, is "free": this is the mirror-like wisdom. "The elements" are the four subtle elements, earth and the rest. "Arisen from the elements" is what is fit to be produced by them. "Arising from that" means born; its purified nature is "the pure nature." "It is the body of all beings": since it grasps distinctions and binds, the body is delusion, which has become discordant with suchness. Victorious over beings, it is the victor; since it is victorious even over that and overpowers it, it is jinajik: Vairocana, the nature of mirror-like wisdom. This is the completion stage.

Now, mind is the nature of equality; since it is like a jewel, it is the jewel. That very mind, through the purity of the space element, by the sky-treasury samādhi, is "all aims," arising in the sequence of what is wished for: the cause of producing them. Therefore, since it purifies consciousness as the self of the felt, the feeler and feeling, it is the jewel; since one holds a jewel like that, it is ratnadhṛk. This is the completion stage.

The defining mark is the property that establishes the uncommon reality of a thing, as with the pot, the big-bellied one. The self that apprehends it is perception. Its object is the defining mark of the pot and so on. That by which it is marked is the defining mark of action. That which purifies the aggregate of perception, the self of these three, is the "thing." Likewise "all" is the fire element. "By all" means pervading; since it purifies discriminating wisdom, it is the "thing." The defining mark of joy is the woman's sign; the thing marked is focusing and arising, that is, attachment. Since that very wisdom conquers it, it is ārolik: the wisdom whose nature is Amitābha. This is the completion stage.

"Consummate discerning wisdom" has the nature of earnestly accomplishing deeds and works for the benefit of beings; since it destroys the affliction of jealousy, it is called wisdom. Therefore volition, the formations, is the purified aggregate of formations, and "holding" purifies the wind element; so that very wisdom is prajñādhṛk: the wisdom whose nature is Amoghasiddhi. This is the completion stage.

To make this very meaning clear, I shall explain. Family means continuum: the continuum of the five tathāgata families. The continuum is the first, the great Vajradhara. "Fearless, unborn" is that protector himself; it is his distinguishing quality. By "the knowledge of abandonment" and the rest, the purification of aversion, belonging to consciousness and to wisdom as the discordant side and the side of the antidote, is explained as the wisdom of the dharma realm and as Akṣobhya. Likewise form, the affliction of delusion, and the nature of inert bondage are the earth element; their purified nature is Vairocana. The aggregate of feeling, and pride and arrogance, the secondary afflictions, purified: their nature is Ratnasambhava. The aggregate of perception, attachment to things, which is the affliction of desire, and the purified fire element: their nature is Amitābha. Formations, jealousy, and the nature that is driven by conditions are the purified wind element: Amoghasiddhi. In just that way, the essential nature of the awakening mind is all of existence and its origination; the one who gives rise to it should be known as the self of the five tathāgatas.

In order to teach the families: attachment and aversion, bound up with affliction, and in order to overcome the pride or arrogance born from their cooperating cause, and in order to overcome the pride of beings filled with the arrogance of wealth, there is the jewel family. This means the family of Ratnasambhava, which fulfills every wish with a rain of precious things of every kind. "The samaya family" resembles the result born from the various deeds of beings; since it destroys, by bestowing samaya and vow, the obscurations of afflictions and of the knowable, the samaya family is called the family of Amoghasiddhi. Likewise "nondual wisdom" is the quality of the mirror-like wisdom; what takes pride in things of its nature is the pervading one, bewilderment, and the nature attached to that is called the delusion family. Then, in that nondual wisdom, through the purification of the afflictions, there is mutual separation: what is set up as aversion and the wisdom of the dharma realm, in the manner of the discordant factor and its antidote, is aversion; it is called the aversion family. Attachment, whose defining mark is worldly craving, is joined through the means of wisdom; therefore it is the attachment family.

Those families, delusion and the rest, in which there is delight in the nature of wisdom, the afflictions purified, are Moharati and the others: this is the ultimate side. The conventional side is this: woman by her nature focuses on form, and as a means of winning over beings of the attachment family, Vairocana and the rest, the purified nature of delusion and the rest, together with the goddesses Locanā and the others, who join with them in union and bring about the experience of bliss, are explained as Moharati and the others. Thus there are the three afflictions, and likewise the vajra, that is, jealousy; in order to purify these, the delighting goddesses are Locanā and the others. For that very reason, Buddha is all beings; in order to tame all beings, means is the cause. That itself is the Vajrayāna, and as regards accomplishing the uninterrupted benefit of beings, "it is not" is said. This is the completion stage.

I shall explain the purification of the gatekeepers. "The indestructible self" is the nature of the door of liberation that is emptiness. "Unborn in essence" is the nature unborn from the very beginning. "Phenomena" are the aggregates and the rest. Not fully knowing their nature is Yama; "the samaya of all its things" is the ending of these, the nature of the door of liberation that is emptiness: this is Yamāntaka. Likewise "not consciousness, the selfless phenomena" are free from causes such as the Lord (Īśvara). "Ultimately unchanging, purely transformed" is the nature of the door of purification that is signlessness. "The samaya of all minds": the mark of not knowing that is prajñā, and since it ends that, it is Prajñāntaka. Because it is wishless, it cannot be shown; therefore it is the self that is inexpressible. For that very reason, by the reasoning explained before, "name and form, mind, without things" is freedom from the aggregate of formations and the rest. Since their nature is not known, being inert, it is the lotus. "The samaya of all phenomena," the nature of the door of liberation that is wishlessness, is Padmāntaka. The nonconceptual self of the door of liberation that is the unconditioned, and all phenomena pure by nature: not fully knowing their nature is the obstructors. Since it ends them, the nature of the door of liberation that is the unconditioned is Vighnāntaka. By the passage beginning "indestructible," the four doors of liberation, emptiness and the rest, are summed up. This is the completion stage.

Delusion is lack of faith. Its enjoyment is what abandons it, lack of faith. Delusion is the view of the perishing collection. That which exhausts it; likewise the body is the māra of the aggregates. The knowable is the obscuration of the knowable. Since these kill those in saṃsāra, they are Yama. Since faith ends them, it is Yamāntaka.

The fault is the view that holds to extremes. The enjoyment of the fault is its purification. The fault is laziness in virtuous action; for that, prajñā, since it is the ending brought about by diligence, is Prajñāntaka. Therefore, through the purification of the view that holds to extremes, it is also the ender of aversion, and through the purification of laziness it is Prajñāntaka. Mind is the māra of the afflictions; since it is purified, there is the ender of mind. Affliction is the obscuration of the afflictions; through its purification there is the ender of affliction. Therefore the nature of diligence is Prajñāntaka.

Attachment is forgetfulness. Again, attachment is the māra of the Lord of Death. The exhaustion of these is Padmāntaka. Again, attachment is wrong view; by ending it there is the ender of craving. Absorption is passing over into another existence: the obscuration of birth. Since it is purified, the ender of absorption is the nature of mindfulness: this is Padmāntaka.

"All afflictions": since it has become the cause of all afflictions, it is distraction. Since it destroys all beings, it is the vajra; being like a vajra, it is the māra who is a son of the gods. "Obscuration" is the obscuration of karma, and "all" is holding views as supreme; since it purifies that, it is Vighnāntaka.

I shall explain the result of purification. "Afflictions" are the impurities and the rest; since they are firm, they are "lord." When the mind that is their obscuration becomes purified, "all actions," virtuous and nonvirtuous actions, become purified. When actions have become purified, "the result of the yogin" is the pure dharma body, whose defining mark is attaining the state of the great Vajradhara: the yogin will attain it. That is what is meant. Therefore the summary is this: lack of faith, the māra of the aggregates, the view of the perishing collection, and the obscuration of the knowable; what destroys these, the nature of faith, is Yamāntaka.

Laziness, the māra of the afflictions, the view that grasps, and the obscuration of the afflictions: since it destroys these, the nature of diligence is Prajñāntaka. Forgetfulness, the māra of the Lord of Death, wrong view, and the obscuration of birth: since it abandons these, it is the ground of mindfulness, Padmāntaka. Distraction, the māra who is a son of the gods, holding views as supreme, and the obscuration of karma: through the purification of these, the nature of samādhi is Vighnāntaka. That is the completion stage.

I shall explain the purification of Uṣṇīṣa and the others. Since things are apprehended by it, it is the apprehending faculty. "Attachment" is what produces attachment, that is, the object. "Aspect" is attention. "Unmoving" is that by whose power consciousness apprehends its object without hindrance, namely light. These four, the eye, form, light and attention, stand in the division into the appropriating cause and the cooperating causes. Whatever moment of eye consciousness and the rest has arisen immediately before, that earlier moment is the cause. Eye consciousness is the result: mind arises from the six causes. Or else mind arises from the faculties, from the eye and the rest up to the mind sense source. Or mind arises from the six that are stated thus:

The efficient, the co-arising,
The maturing and the associated,
With the universal and the homogeneous:
Causes are explained as of six kinds.

Their purification is the six wrathful ones that are explained, from Ṭakkirāja up to Uṣṇīṣa. That is the purification of consciousness, because it is said, "The wrathful ones arise from the aversion family."

The six beginning with Samayavajra are the six bodhisattvas, Sarvanīvaraṇaviṣkambhin and the rest, by the reasoning explained in the first chapter. They abide in the element of consciousness by the nature of the five elements, earth and the rest, that is, earth as an element and as what is derived from the elements: they are the purification of the elements and what is derived from them. Maitreya and Samantabhadra are the nature of the purification of all the sinews and all the joints. Rūpavajrā and the others are the six goddesses: they are the purification of the form sense source and the rest. Therefore "knowledge" is Rūpavajrā and the others, and their king is the king of knowledge, that is, the bodhisattvas. "Vajra" is the bodhisattva; the lord of these, the one who is chief among them, is the lord of the vajra. Thus Rūpavajrā and the others are explained. That is the completion stage.

I shall explain the all-good conduct. Focusing on the body and the rest and the aggregates and the rest, on the union of means and wisdom, and on entering absorption nondually with the gnosis seal, the yogin, all at once possessed of the form of the great Vajradhara, resolves, "All these beings are pure by nature." "All fears" are abandoned, that is, freed from conceptions of arising, perishing and the like. Like a lion, without misgiving, free from conceptions of what may be eaten and what may not, eating whatever there is, or eating the food of meditative concentration; when not in meditative equipoise, in all four modes of conduct, seeing everything as the nature of one's own deity through deity yoga, one rests "always," at all times, in equipoise; and whatever conduct is carried out with "every mind," with a mind free from all defilements, is called the all-good conduct. It is conduct entirely free of elaboration, the practice.

I shall explain the mantra conduct. When the faculties rely on and take up their objects through the absorption of wisdom and means, then in the mind, wisdom whose nature is bliss will arise. Because it makes attention discordant with that powerless, and because it guards reality and so protects, it is mantra: the bliss that arises from the absorption of wisdom and means. Focusing on that, "worldly conduct" is this: taking up the body free from ordinary conceptions as meditated in the form of the deity, one enjoys the five sense pleasures, or begs alms while wandering from place to place; whatever conduct is undertaken in order to attain the state of Samantabhadra is called the mantra conduct, the practice. That is the conduct without elaboration.

I shall explain recitation. "One's own essential nature" is through the sequence of entering, abiding and arising. "Contemplating the essence of the vajra words that are beyond words" means contemplating with an understanding of the meaning. "Emanation and withdrawal" are through entering, abiding and arising. "Mantra" is the three syllables, and its recitation is the joining of going and coming. "By its division" means by the division into upward, sideways, first and downward. "Recitation performs it" means training by the method of the water wheel. Thus recitation with the three syllables in sequence is explained.

"The various buddhas, the lords of the vajra" are the tathāgatas whose nature is the wisdoms, the mirror-like and the rest. "Gather them with the seeds of wisdom" means one should make them dwell within the syllable oṃ. "Awakening, selflessness" is the seed of Amitābha, whose nature is discriminating wisdom, that is, the syllable a. "Selflessness" means one should meditate on the tathāgatas of that family and then place them in his heart. Likewise "variously, as the various," one sends them forth: one should send forth, with the syllable hūṃ, the tathāgatas of the three times of the Akṣobhya family, whose nature is the wisdom of the dharma realm. That is called vajra recitation: the stage of vajra recitation.

I shall explain verbal recitation. "Recitation" is this: resolving that one is saying "Listen!" to all the tathāgatas dwelling throughout all the realm of space, "reciting all speech" is whatever recitation is made with the mantras of the serpent and the rest, with the words of the wrathful ones; that is called recitation. It is the samādhi of the supreme king of action. "Mantra" is invoking, speaking in secret. "Likewise, that itself" is the nature of the tathāgatas Vairocana and the rest. "Urging them" means, relying on their divisions, uttering oṃ and the rest. "Uttering vajra recitation" is the stage of recitation.

Next I shall explain the sealing. The sealing is of four kinds: the sealing by causing the wisdom being to enter, the sealing of the samādhi being, the sealing with the crown after empowerment has been received, and the sealing of offering. I shall explain these. Just as one meditates on the pledge being, Vairocana or another, so one should seal with the wisdom being, which is the blessing of the mind; and in his heart one should also seal by meditating on a corresponding seed, the samādhi being. Having received the empowerment of all the tathāgatas, one should seal the crown of the head with the tathāgata, as a crown that corresponds to the self of the three beings. Then, at the time of offering too, one should send forth the offering goddesses, corresponding to the self of the three beings, and make offerings. This is the supreme king of the maṇḍala and the supreme king of action.

To make this clear I shall explain. "Whoever is the vajra holder" means that the practitioner should meditate on the yogin, Vairocana or any of the others, endowed with the wisdom of the consort. His four enjoyments have the defining mark of the wisdom being, the samādhi being, praise, and sealing with offering. "Self-blessing" and the rest means one should understand the wisdom being and the rest as corresponding to oneself. That is the supreme king of the maṇḍala and of action.

I shall explain it in general and in particular. "The lord" is the tathāgatas. "The single vajra" means that the great Vajradhara empowers the crown of each one; this should be understood as the crown. For it is said in the fifteenth chapter:

For all the mantras,
Meditate properly on Vajrasattva.

"Heart" is the wisdom being, "seal" is the samādhi being, and "mere mantra" is the offering goddesses. One should seal these in sequence by self-awareness: this is the particular explanation. This is the supreme king of the maṇḍala and the supreme king of action.

I shall explain the kinds of sealing. "The result" is the body of Vajradhara as Vairocana and the rest. "The cause" is the great Vajradhara. One should seal by following him: the result is the body of Vairocana and the rest, and the cause is Vajrasattva; one should seal by the family explained before, and one should meditate in that way. Otherwise, if one meditates on cause and result as separate, one will not attain accomplishment even in a hundred koṭis of eons. The same should be understood for the generation of the moon, the vajra and the rest. That is the generation stage.

I shall explain the etymology of the source of phenomena. "The four enjoyments" are the four, Locanā and the rest. "Fully endowed with them" means surrounded: the man of knowledge, the man together with Sparśavajrā, that is, Vajradhara. Having transformed into that Vajradhara, one meditates on Dveṣavajra in the center, and on the tathāgatas, Kāyavajra and the rest, in the triangle; in the center of that source of phenomena one meditates on a yellow ten-spoked wheel arisen from bhūṃ, and on the ten spokes one sets the ten wrathful ones, noble Yamāntaka and the rest. "Restraining vajra" means harming the adversary. "Accomplishment" is generation. "Supremely beautiful" means abiding within Māmakī's lotus. "The blazing vajra blessed by Vajradhara" means radiating sparks. "Saṃsāra" is revolving all around; "unmoving" is space; "like it" means like the rainbow that abides in it. Such a source of phenomena is the cause from which phenomena arise. "Stainless in nature" means one should meditate on the nature of the utterly pure wisdom of the dharma realm, and perform the activities of fierce rites and the rest. "Should perform" is the remainder of the phrase. That is the generation stage.

When that very source of phenomena, white like the autumn moon, whose essence is clear light, has at its navel a drop of nectar the size of a mustard seed, and one meditates on this subtle yoga, then, because one experiences it instantly by meditating on it, Buddhalocanā becomes like the great Vajradhara: so it is said. This is the completion stage.

The summary of this is as follows. In Māmakī's lotus, one imagines a blazing red-yellow ten-spoked wheel arisen from the syllable bhrūṃ. In the center of its hub one imagines oneself as Dveṣavajra, as described, and from the union the wrathful ones are sent forth. On the four spokes, slightly inclined, that rest on the places of Vairocana and the others, one sets the four, noble Yamāntaka and the rest; likewise in the places of the slightly slanted spokes and the rest, in order, one sets noble Ṭakkirāja, Nīladaṇḍa, Acala and Mahābala. On the slightly slanted spoke that stands in the middle of the quarter between Indra and Īśāna, one imagines Uṣṇīṣacakravartin; and likewise on the spoke that stands in the middle of the quarter between Varuṇa and Nairṛti, the wrathful one, the glorious Sumbharāja. This is the generation stage. On the wheel of ten channels one imagines the ten kinds, by the division into winds and secondary winds, and one should abide in the form of the drop, the result. This is the completion stage. That is by the division of the seal of the source of phenomena.

Now I shall explain the division into the two stages. On "relying properly on the two stages": by the division into beginner and completion, "the vajra holder" is the tathāgatas teaching the Dharma, divided by the generation and completion stages; this is the brief teaching. "Accomplishment" is the limbs, approach and the rest, that is, the four vajras, or the sequence of means such as the vase, or the sequence of the maṇḍala. "Diligence" has the defining mark of pledge and vow; that too is the generation stage. "Previously," that is, whatever wisdom nectar was found, the defining mark of the six-limbed yoga explained before and taught extensively in the twelfth chapter and elsewhere: that too is the completion stage. This is the division of the stages.

If one asks how there are two stages in the context of explaining the sixteen word meanings: that is no fault, because the two stages are explained in the root tantra, and in the explanatory tantra they are taught otherwise.

Here the three, beginning with the first union, and accomplishment make four: these are the limbs of the generation stage. Vajra recitation and the rest, and the natural ones and the rest, are ten: these are the limbs of the completion stage. These are the generation and completion stages.

I shall state the pledge. The five objects, Rūpavajrā and the rest, are inferior, middling and supreme, divided into the pleasing, the unpleasing and both; they are pleasure, pain and both, that is, of the nature of pleasure and pain. Focusing on them, one realizes clear light, and attachment, aversion and delusion are purified and turned back: one attains the distinguished great bliss. That is the pledge of the yogin who has transcended the world. Likewise, through attachment, the pledge of attachment is the nature of attachment, the purification of attachment. Likewise, "vajra": having seen the thing that is the focus of aversion, the vajra, aversion, is purified, and the jewel, the wisdom of clear light, arises from the yogin. Likewise, having seen the thing whose nature is ignorance, focused on as delusion, one purifies it and realizes clear light, which, like a jewel, grants whatever aims are wished for. Therefore the objects of desire are five; even if one remains ordinary, in keeping with the pledge, for the yogin they become the pledge itself. That is the purification of mind and accomplishment.

The subjugation and benefiting explained earlier: I shall explain the meditation on aversion and the rest. First, "with aspects": the stage of abiding, of sending forth and withdrawing the three syllables possessed of shape and color, is recitation in the sequence of entering and the rest; one should do vajra recitation. "Without aspects" means withdrawing the syllables in sequence; "all-pervading" means free from direction, place, time, shape and color. One should perform the neuter recitation, nonconceptual, the essence of the great Vajradhara. This is the stage of vajra recitation.

Having attained one-pointedness of mind through vajra recitation, I shall again explain the generation stage. "The three forms," Dveṣavajra and the rest, generate the three realms. "The pure secret" means sealing by blessing the body and the rest. "Doing the work of the vajra holder" means performing the act of subjugating the malicious and the like. How is this done? In the sequence explained before, in Māmakī's lotus one generates the "wheel of aversion," the wheel of wrathful ones blessed by Akṣobhya. By sending forth its light rays one sends out the wrathful ones, and with the emblems held in their own hands they slay the one hard to tame. Then, with those same light rays, one draws in his consciousness, whose self is the three syllables interspersed with two syllables hoḥ, and one should gather it into the seed of the wisdom being in one's own heart: at that moment he will die. Further, if one sends forth the self of the three syllables in the form of the one hard to tame, in the earlier sequence, so that it enters the body of the one hard to tame, and "realization" is awakened, then at that moment he will live: that is the meaning. Joined thus, the yogin training in samādhi becomes himself like aversion and the vajra: he becomes able to subjugate and to benefit. "Even in the three realms, in a mere instant," that is, merely by meditating on that samādhi, "slaying" is killing, and "raising" is making them rise again: thus it addresses a doubt. "Able" means when meditative concentration has become clear and stable.

Likewise, the Vairocana yogin generates in Buddhalocanā's lotus the "wheel of delusion," the wheel of wrathful ones blessed by Vairocana. He sends forth wrathful ones holding hooks and nooses and imagines them entering the treasury of a miser. The wrathful ones, of a color in keeping with that, take the jewels and the rest and draw them away by means of the wind maṇḍala. "The mind of delusion" is the Vairocana yogin; "his ocean" is his own treasury. One should meditate that it is "filled with all jewels," brimming with sapphires (indranīla) and the rest seized from the miserly person. Taking those streams, one urges on the wheel of command, and in the house of a person endowed with good qualities but lacking wealth and the like, "all who hold the wheel of delusion," the wrathful ones, are urged on, and one should let fall "a rain of giving," a rain of heaps of jewels. That giving and that taking: when the mind has become stable in samādhi like a vajra, then the taking of all his goods and the giving are done in a mere instant.

The Amitābha yogin imagines the wheel of Pāṇḍaravāsinī, the lotus, Hayagrīva and the rest. Sending them forth, he summons the daughters of gods, asuras and others, makes them enter Pāṇḍaravāsinī, and should delight them by union nondual with her. I shall explain this. "Joy" is light, "delight" is the spreading of light, "bliss" is the culmination of light, and "satisfaction" is clear light. "The magical display of the play of desire" means one should experience it through the varieties of embracing and the rest. That is the summoning of the goddesses and the rest. Then, by means of light rays, one sends them forth from the heart of Pāṇḍaravāsinī, and "all who hold the wheel" should give those goddesses and the others to beings who desire attachment, in order to tame them.

"Women born in the realms through that very samādhi" is a word of eminence, because women cannot exist in the form and formless realms, since those are free from attachment. Those who are connected by way of gods, asuras and the rest: "in an instant," that is, through stable samādhi, one instantly summons them for the enjoyment of desire, enjoys them, and gives them to others as well; what need then to mention human women? This shows the greatness of samādhi. That is the generation stage.

Having relied on taming beings by way of attachment and the rest, I shall now show the answer concerning the drawing of the maṇḍala. If one asks why that which is called a maṇḍala, the basis of all the Tathāgatas, is drawn: the empowerments, which will be explained below, are of four kinds, and it is in order to obtain them; likewise in order to perform the collection of rites; practice, in order to generate the deity's body of the generation stage; and blessing, in order to attain the state of Vajradhara through the completion stage. For these reasons the maṇḍala is drawn, that is, by yogins of conceptual practice. The progress of the accomplished is different, because they abide in that completion stage. The progress of those who have reached the end is the mode of the maṇḍala; the other is of another kind.

I shall explain that very point. Bhaga is the mudrā of the source of phenomena. When it becomes the essence, it is called a maṇḍala: the wheel of deities beginning with the awakening mind. The awakening mind, too, is a maṇḍala, because in the conventional mode the five Tathāgatas abide in it, and because in the mode of great bliss it is the support of Vajradhara. "Body" means the body; that too is a maṇḍala, because it is the support of all the deities. For the seventeenth chapter says:

The five aggregates, in brief,
are proclaimed to be the five buddhas.

That is the generation stage and the completion stage.

Showing the answer to the question about the mudrā: "all," that is, the aggregates and so on and the afflictions and so on as well, being sealed with the seal, means being sealed with the seal of great bliss. That very maṇḍala seals the maṇḍala of beings; therefore it is called a mudrā. That is the completion stage.

Alternatively, what is called mudrā is the sign of the consort. Because by it "all," that is, beings whose nature is the aggregates and so on, are sealed, it is a mudrā. That, moreover, has the character of vajra and lotus: because by the vajra and lotus mudrās all beings connected with the four modes of birth of sentient beings are sealed, they are therefore the vajra mudrā and the lotus mudrā, and they are to be understood as having the nature of skillful means and wisdom. That is the completion stage.

Alternatively, the mudrā is the mudrā of the source of phenomena, which generates the nature of the aggregates and so on. Because one seals by it, the mudrās are the action mudrā (karmamudrā) and the pledge mudrā (samayamudrā); the wisdom that meditates on suchness is the wisdom mudrā (jñānamudrā); and the character of the purity of the aggregates and so on is the great mudrā (mahāmudrā). Because all beings are sealed by these, they are mudrās.

Alternatively, the nature of the aggregates, the sense sources and the elements is the four elements, earth and the rest: Locanā, Māmakī, Pāṇḍaravāsinī and Tārā. Because all their maṇḍalas seal the three realms, they are called mudrās. That is generation and completion.

Following the outer sequence, I shall explain the emblem mudrās. The four, the hook and the rest, are the four, Ṭakkirāja and the rest. "Corners" means they should be placed in the corners of the gates. Their wheel and vajra are the emblems of Uṣṇīṣacakravartin and Sumbharāja, and these should be placed above and below. The six kings of knowledge are the six kings of desire. As it is said:

The six mudrās
are proclaimed as six emblems.

From earth up to the element of consciousness, whichever goddesses are Rūpavajrā and the rest, in their maṇḍala, in due order: the mirror, of the nature of a reflection; the vīṇā, of the nature of sound, which is like an echo; the conch, the moral discipline of the Dharma, of the nature of fragrance; the vessel filled with the five nectars, of the nature of the purity of the enjoyment of the five sense pleasures; that which arises from attachment, of the nature of the bliss of transcendent knowledge, because it dispels the heat of attachment and the rest; and the source of phenomena, of the nature of body, speech and mind, the three bodies, and the three samādhis. These six mudrās, of the lord of the vajra: the one who possesses the vajra lord is the lord of the vajra. The mudrās of Rūpavajrā and the rest should be placed.

Whatever are the mudrās of the buddhas, bodhisattvas and wrathful kings, namely vajra, wheel, jewel, lotus, sword, hammer and so on, together with the colors of their families, the five colors, should be placed. Besides these, "single hero" refers to the mudrās of Kṣitigarbha and the others. The colors of the mudrā families, considered as following the families of Vairocana and the rest, should be placed. That is the generation and completion stages.

I shall explain the answer to the question about the flower. "Flower" means the space element of the goddess: within the center of the lotus of the wisdom consort, the fresh element, that is, the rakta, is to be drawn out at once. That is the division of body, speech and mind: it is taken up with the tongue from the maṇḍalas of body and the rest, and that is the sequence of the families. Blessing it with the seeds of the three families and placing it is to be enacted, that is, accomplished.

Alternatively, the space element of the consort is the space of the wisdom consort. In it, on each channel, there is an eight-petaled lotus together with its center, making nine. The elements arising from it are rakta, śukra, and urine. Kāyavajra and the rest have the nature of Vairocana and the rest. Placing them as before is enacting, that is, accomplishing.

Alternatively, the goddess is wisdom without object. Her space element is the emptiness of all. In it, body and the rest, the elements, and consciousness: placing is to be brought near, and this is called the flower. That is the purification of mind.

To show the answer to the question about the stūpa, I shall explain. "All buddhas" are the Tathāgatas whose nature is the Dharma body, the enjoyment body and the emanation body. Their basis is their abode, and "basis" means the body. That is the completion stage.

Alternatively, "all buddhas" are the five elements, śukra and the rest, and their abode is the basis, which is called the lotus of the wisdom consort. The lotus of the wisdom consort is called the stūpa. Moreover, according to the reasoning taught in the Vajrāmṛta Tantra, the five spaces, the eight petals and the center are called the fourteen wheels of that very stūpa.

Alternatively, the vajra whose nature is to hold the drop of the awakening mind, the nature of all buddhas, is called the stūpa; that is the cause. The wisdom being, established by its own nature, entering the expanse of space, touches it: that is "endowed with it." The wheel is the wheel of the maṇḍala, and it is called the wheel of wisdom. Some say that what is called "abiding at the heart" is the supreme king of maṇḍalas.

I shall explain the seed syllables. The seed syllables are the syllables that serve for generation. "Abode" means the purpose of generation, endowed with indestructible power. The three vajras, the vajras of body, speech and mind, the immutability of Vairocana and the rest, are the seeds. Likewise, the syllables are the essential nature of the mirror-like wisdom and the rest. Because the nature of reality is by nature permanent and indestructible, the syllables are the seeds. That is the generation stage.

I shall explain the urging. "When awakened": awakening is causing those who abide in luminosity to rise for the benefit of sentient beings. Causing the suchness of body, speech and mind to rise from luminosity is called urging; it is the samādhi of the supreme king of action. Alternatively, body, speech and mind are the parasol of desire and the rest, the three principal channels. Their awakening is urging them with the finger or the tongue, and this is called urging. Alternatively, the suchness of body, speech and mind is the three seeds. Awakening their five nectars is urging by making them luminous, that is, accomplishing.

I shall explain the answer to the question about sending forth. Emanating light rays, sending out light rays into the world systems of the ten directions in order to act for the benefit of sentient beings, is called sending forth; that is the supreme king of action. Further, whatever sending forth of attendants there is into the world systems of the ten directions, in order to summon the powers of the deities or in order to tame those hard to tame, is called sending forth; that is accomplishing.

To show the answer to the question about inviting: "inviting" means that all the vajra holders abiding in the expanse of space are summoned and made to enter by whichever of the three samādhis; that is called inviting, and it is the generation stage. Their mode as a single mass: gathering them into the form of a drop of the mantra syllables, or else into the form of a drop of the awakening mind, making them one, and binding them at the heart, in the vajra and lotus, or at the tip of the nose on the face, is called restraining or holding. Further, making it unmoving and bringing it under control is called binding. That is the supreme king of action and yoga.

I shall explain the answer to the question about the drawn maṇḍala. "Vajra of body, speech and mind" means this: when the yogins of Vairocana and the rest draw the maṇḍalas of Kāyavajra and the rest, the maṇḍalas of Vairocana and the rest, then the disciples who hold the chalked cord, who have the nature of body and the rest, are generated by being sealed with the essence of Vairocana and the rest, and the drawn maṇḍala is to be laid out, that is, its lines are to be struck. The intention here is as follows. A yogin of Vairocana who wishes to draw the maṇḍala of body meditates on himself as Vairocana, and should visualize the disciple as Amitābha, Akṣobhya, or Amṛtakuṇḍalin. If a yogin of Amitābha wishes to draw the maṇḍala of speech, he meditates on himself as Amitābha, and should visualize the disciple as Vairocana, Akṣobhya, or Amṛtakuṇḍalin. If a yogin of Akṣobhya wishes to draw the maṇḍala of mind, he meditates on himself as Akṣobhya, and should visualize the disciple as Vairocana, Amitābha, or Amṛtakuṇḍalin. Alternatively, he should visualize the disciple as Vajrasattva. That is the generation stage.

I shall explain the answer about empowerment. What is called empowerment is, in this tantra, the glorious Guhyasamāja, threefold, because it purifies body, speech and mind. Of these, the vase empowerment purifies the body, because the generation stage is principal in it. The secret empowerment purifies speech, because it has the nature of tasting the nectar. The wisdom empowerment purifies the mind, because the completion stage is principal in it. Their divisions and sequence are explained in the sixteenth chapter.

"The fourth is likewise so" means that this very wisdom-gnosis, as taught by the guru, is called the fourth. Here wisdom-gnosis is explained as great bliss, and that is of two kinds: the bliss of the example and the bliss arising from the example. Of these, the bliss of the example is the wisdom-gnosis, while the bliss arising from the example is what the word "likewise" expresses, because it is to be realized through the valid cognition of one's own direct perception, and because it is nonconceptual. For that very reason it is not referred to by the word "gnosis."

If one objects: how then can nonconceptual reality arise from the bliss of the example, which is conceptual, since a result accords with its cause? It is not so, because reality has no cause, since it is permanent. If it had a cause, it would follow that it is momentary and perishable, like a pot. Because the nature of reality abides like space through its natural permanence, and because the bliss of the example is momentary, it is not tenable that it be the cause of that reality. It is accepted as what illustrates reality, but being what illustrates it does not make it a cause; otherwise it would follow that the finger pointing at the moon is also a cause. What is neither cause nor result is established by its own nature, like space. The removal of stains, which are like adventitious clouds, is the reasoning that relies on the example.

For if that were not accepted, it would follow that everyone attains buddhahood, that meditation and the rest would be meaningless and without anything to support them, and that merit, wrongdoing and the rest would not exist. Therefore the bliss of the example is not the cause, because it is conceptual. That it merely illustrates is tenable, like a lamp, as has already been explained above. Enough of this, lest it be carried too far.

In the context of the observance of knowledge, since the two empowerments are difficult, I shall explain their sequence. "Suitable as a vessel in the way of mantra" means suitable in the way of mantra. "Suitable as a vessel" is by way of the division of families. For a woman of the family of aversion belongs to the family of Akṣobhya, and likewise those of the families of delusion and the rest belong to the families of Vairocana and the rest. By color, too: one who is black is of the family of Akṣobhya, and likewise the colors white and the rest are to be known as the families of Vairocana and the rest. The family is also to be known by action, because they perform fierce and other actions. Having examined the family in this way, the wisdom consort is suitable as a vessel for mantra.

"Wide-eyed" shows the perfection of her form. "With flowers" means she is of the right age; if she is not of the right age, she has no flowers. "Seed flows" means that she is what is known in the world as "seed-dripping," and is a padminī. Alternatively, when her flower appears, then she is to be taken. "To be known as similar to that" means that the mantrin who keeps well the pledges and the rest and holds the recitation should bestow on the disciple the secret empowerment, that is, the hidden kindness.

I shall explain its sequence. In the center of the space element, the vases filled with the five nectars, feces, urine and the rest, are imagined as the Blessed Ones, the Tathāgatas. The vajra master, abiding with her in the union of vajra and lotus, summons them with the sound of supreme joy and places them in the heart. The vajra of body, speech and mind melts in the central channel, where the three channels have become of one taste. The awakening mind, which has come forth by the path of the vajra and abides in the lotus, is taken up in a conch cup or the like and is to be given into the disciple's mouth. Some say that the vajra itself is to be placed in the mouth.

The empowerment is supreme, that is, excellent, because it exhausts conceptual thought. Likewise, all secret mantras are the "state," because they are the cause of attaining the body and the rest of the Tathāgatas. Therefore, by this empowerment, all the mantras, that is, the transcendent accomplishments, and the hosts of supreme actions, that is, the worldly accomplishments of pacifying and the rest, will be accomplished by the yogin: these words are to be supplied. That is the generation stage and accomplishing.

Further, the wisdom empowerment is to be conferred. "Having supreme faith" means having a mind of faith in the Three Jewels. "Great wisdom" means having attained excellent wisdom through hearing and the rest in sequence. "Beautiful form" means endowed with the qualities of beauty, grace, youth and so on. "Delighting in the practitioner" means in harmony with yoga. "Single union" is the union without equal, that is, the way of mantra. "Practiced in action therein" means she meditates and so on on that very path. "Pledges": among the pledges of the action tantras and the rest, "possessing the pledge" means endowed with the conduct of the pledges taught in the tantras of great yoga. Having taken such a wisdom consort, the disciple, preceded by offerings, praises and service, should present her to the guru as the offering fee. "The disciple" is a word to be supplied. The vajra master, for his part, blesses the five families and the seeds of body, speech and mind, generates her in the form of Māmakī and the rest, and should give her to the practitioner who is endowed with the samādhi of Vairocana.

Then, after the pledges and vows have been taken, looking upon the disciple who, entering with the serviceable vajra, is joined in union with the wisdom consort, he emanates from his own heart Moharati and the others and should place them in her mouth. I shall explain this. "Union of delusion": in union with Vairocana, Moharati is emanated, placed in her mouth and enters it; if the empowerment is conferred with the stream of the awakening mind arising from that, the disciple becomes the vajra of delusion. Likewise, Akṣobhya, Amitābha and Amoghasiddhi, the nature of aversion, attachment, and wisdom and gnosis, are emanated in union with Dveṣarati, Rāgarati and Vajrarati, are placed in her mouth, and with the stream of the awakening mind arising from that, in order to purify aversion and the rest, one should empower the disciple with the stream of aversion and the rest. That is the rite of the empowerment of wisdom-gnosis; it is the generation stage and the result.

I shall explain the observance of knowledge. Then that very goddess, endowed with the host of qualities explained above, is taken, and the disciple, the vajra holder, takes her by both hands. The master, standing between them, places his hand on the disciple's crown and, taking the Tathāgatas abiding in the expanse of space as witnesses, should give her to him in their presence.

This, too, should be said: "By other methods," that is, the qualities of training of the śrāvakas and the rest, the leisure of the body and so on, and the extremely difficult vows of the bodhisattvas and the rest, "one does not awaken," that is, one does not attain the state of Vajradhara, because these possess impure method. Therefore "this knowledge" is the wisdom consort, known through this knowledge of luminosity. "This is blessed by me and is supreme": "take the supreme pledge and vow" is to be supplied. "Because all phenomena," that is, the aggregates and the rest, are free of the conception of grasped and grasper, they are nondual, that is, free from the two.

Deluded, as by a city of the gandharvas, the two entities appear, that is, things appear as the entities of grasped and grasper. Therefore, in order to destroy the conception of grasped and grasper, you must not be separated from this knowledge, which has the essence of the Lady of the Dharma Realm (Dharmadhātvīśvarī). "You must not be separated from this" is the observance of knowledge. Because this is the cause of all buddhas, it is the method of the buddhas, by the same reasoning as the saying "ghee is life." This is the observance of knowledge. "Observance" means vow. "Unsurpassed" means hard to find for those who have entered the action tantras and the rest. "Whoever, deluded," that is, through confusion or forgetfulness, transgresses it, that is, does not guard it, "will not achieve the supreme accomplishment," which is characterized as the state of the great Vajradhara. That is accomplishing.

I shall explain the answer to the question about the five nectars. "Ordinary" means established by nature. "Impurities" are the five, feces and the rest. "Five" means the five hooks. "Appear" means they appear as pure, because all phenomena are pure by nature. Therefore, if a yogin who does not apprehend the three spheres uses the nectars and the hooks, there is not the slightest fault in it. Nevertheless, in order to free the mind from the bondage of conceptual thought, he should rely on a special meditation.

I shall explain that. The five wisdoms are the five seeds of the Tathāgatas, oṃ and the rest. When the five nectars are blessed by these, they become like nectar, removing faults such as fever. "Kindling": with the syllable oṃ one should send forth the flowers. "Burning" is done by the maṇḍalas of wind and fire located beneath the lotus vessel. "Illuminating" is awakening: by turning to the left, one makes it neither saṃsāra nor inert matter. "Form appearing" is purifying: one makes it of the essential nature of purity. These are done with those syllables and with the syllables āḥ and hūṃ. "Thoroughly joining the mantra body": through union with one's own chosen deity, one purifies the hands and throat, and by the sequence of the inner fire offering one should eat the five nectars, the nectar that arises from gathering the five substances, which has the essence of the awakening mind.

I shall explain this in sequence. The "sky" is the upper part of the vessel. There one imagines a vajra arising from the syllable hūṃ. Below, one meditates on a vessel marked with an eight-petaled lotus arising from the syllable āḥ, and imagines the nectar within it as having the nature of nectar that satisfies, through the syllable oṃ. One kindles it with the maṇḍalas of wind and fire arising from yaṃ and raṃ. Joining the vajra and the lotus above and below, as in worship, one imagines what is to satisfy as the nectar of wisdom, pure like crystal. With the weapon of the mass of sharp light rays that issue from one's own heart, one summons the Tathāgatas who dwell in the world systems of the ten directions and makes them enter the nectar. The five seeds are transformed into the five Tathāgatas, and these, being transformed through union with their own goddesses, become the essence of the awakening mind, blessed by the three syllables. With that very nectar one performs the purification and the rest, and by the sequence of purifying the throat, the tongue and so on, one should enact the eating. Otherwise, if the yogin does not do it in this way, then "it will not be accomplished," that is, he will not attain the state of the great Vajradhara. That is accomplishing.

I shall explain the ordinary accomplishments. Invisibility and the rest, the accomplishments that come by holding pills and the like, and the accomplishments of eye ointment, swift feet, the knowledge-holder of the wheel and so on: these are ordinary accomplishments, because they bestow enjoyments. Likewise, "buddha": because it is accomplished by the yogin of the completion stage, it is "accomplished by the buddhas," namely Vajradhara-hood and buddhahood, the state of the five Tathāgatas. Both of these, characterized as the encouragement given by the Tathāgatas and Vajradhara, are called the supreme accomplishment, because they bestow liberation. That is accomplishing and the completion stage.

I shall explain approach and the rest in the second stage. "The four methods" are the four divisions. "Awakening vajra" means by the great Vajradhara. "Accomplishing" means accomplishing the body of the deity and realizing luminosity. "Taught" means the yoga tantras were taught. All the yoga tantras are the Vajrabhairava, the Vajrapātāla Tantra and the rest. "Yogin" means a yogin of both kinds; "mantrin": these were taught in order to attain the great Vajradhara. That is the generation stage and the completion stage.

"Approach and the rest" is the brief teaching of the four branches. The passage beginning "Common" shows them as twofold, divided into ordinary and supreme. The four vajras are the ordinary approach and the rest, by the sequence of the five awakenings. The supreme approach is by the yoga of six branches. Emptiness and the rest, taught in all the tantras, are included within the three samādhis, the initial union and the rest. That is the characteristic of the four vajras, the ordinary approach and the rest; it is the initial union, the supreme king of maṇḍalas, and the supreme king of action.

"Gnosis" is the completion stage. Because it brings attainment of the state free from aging and death, it is nectar, and by it approach and the rest are supreme; that is the meaning of "excellent." For that very reason, among the four branches, approach alone is done "by the six unions," that is, by the six unions of withdrawal and the rest. Otherwise the supreme accomplishment would not become the accomplishment of the great Vajradhara. That is the completion stage.

The passage beginning "Withdrawal" briefly teaches the yoga of six branches, and the passage beginning "The ten faculties" will explain it in detail. The faculties are the five, the eye and the rest. The objects are the five sense objects, form and the rest. Gathering them is what "the faculties" means. These ten, the faculties and their objects, have the characteristic of grasping their own objects.

Having taken them up, "individually" means engaging each with its own object; and because one engages the five sense pleasures, this is called withdrawal (pratyāhāra). It is the first branch.

In order to purify it, I shall explain meditative concentration. The five sense pleasures are the objects, form and the rest. Making them one together with the faculties, one should regard them as having the nature of the five Tathāgatas; this is called meditative concentration. It too has five divisions. Conception and analysis are the coarse and subtle conceptions, of the nature of attachment. Joy and bliss are of the nature of physical and mental bliss, the essence of attachment and freedom from attachment. One-pointedness of mind is the nature of luminosity, without attachment, freedom from attachment, or the intermediate state of attachment.

I shall explain this. "The arising of the three secrets": whatever has the character of the agitated bliss that arises from the contact of object, faculty and consciousness is the union. Experiencing it after completely abandoning the three conceptions is the enjoyment. These two are attachment and appearance. Then, after completely abandoning the conceptions of all the faculties, the mere pliancy of the body is joy. Then, being free of physical pliancy, there is pliancy of mind: the bliss that has been gathered, that is, "gathered." Experiencing it is the fourth. These two are attachment and freedom from attachment, that is, the increase of appearance and the culmination of appearance. Then the nonconceptual state, in which the knowing and the known that had arisen are exhausted, the nature of the one buddha, "all space," that is, one's own mind, the essence of all beings who dwell in the three realms, is to be known as the fifth, the nature of luminosity.

In order to stabilize this, I shall explain the third branch. "The nature of the five wisdoms" means the essence of the mirror-like wisdom and the rest. Of these, the drop, clarity itself, is the mirror-like wisdom. The sameness of all the elements is the wisdom of equality. Its enjoyment of great bliss is the discriminating wisdom. Acting for the benefit of beings by emitting and gathering its light rays is the wisdom that accomplishes activities. The Perfection of Wisdom and the essence of the ultimate awakening mind is the wisdom of the utterly pure Dharma realm. Therefore it is "the nature of the five wisdoms." Because it gathers in the ten winds, the winds and secondary winds, in the state of abiding it is the breath. "The essential nature of the five elements" is the body, because, being its core, it possesses solidity, moisture, warmth, going and coming, and hollowness. "That very form of the drop": in the form of a mass, the essence that gathers all the aggregates and the rest, merely the size of a white mustard seed, is drawn out by way of the central channel and the path of the vajra, and imagined at the tip of the nose, that is, at its tip; this is to be gathered. "The precious five-colored one": the mass of light rays of the five Tathāgatas is also emitted from above. "Life force and exertion" is the control of the breath (prāṇāyāma); it is the third branch.

"One's own mantra" is the great Vajradhara. "Imagined at the heart" is meditation. "Life force" is the wind of five colors that moves above and below. "Having become a drop" means it is placed so that it becomes inseparable from the drop; this is to be consumed. Then "one's own faculty," as before, means faculty, object and consciousness. Likewise "jewel": when that jewel of the drop is pure, "held" means whatever gathering in and abiding of the life force and the rest there is at the tip of the vajra or at the center of the lotus; that is retention, the fourth branch.

"Cessation entering the vajra" is the mind that has entered luminosity, which, blessed by the mind, has become the aggregates, elements and sense sources. "Fully holds that sign" means spontaneously: when the individual appearances arise, they arise. That sign, too, "the awakening vajra," that is, the great Vajradhara, "taught as fivefold," that is, declared. When the earth element dissolves into water, there is an appearance like a mirage. When the water element dissolves into fire, there is an appearance like smoke. When the fire element dissolves into wind, there is what resembles space, "sky-appearance," a flickering like fireflies. When the wind element dissolves into consciousness, it is like a lamp. When consciousness dissolves into luminosity, there appears something like a sky at midday, stainless and without wind.

Having seen these signs stabilized, one emits the light rays of the drop by the path of the vajra, imagines that they fill the element of space, and focusing on what is similar and what follows in similarity, emanates Tathāgatas like the body of the great Vajradhara. That is recollection, the fifth branch. "In it, its individual appearances" means that things appear individually like an illusion, a mirage, or a city of the gandharvas.

Then "the union of method and wisdom": by joining nondually with the wisdom mudrā, "all things," that is, what abides divided into sentient beings and the container world, and what was emanated before, are "gathered" in the manner of a mass, that is, made one. They are gathered in the manner of the inseparability of the conventional and the ultimate. "In the center," that is, in the center where those two are inseparable, "form": one should meditate on the body of the great Vajradhara. Moreover, "as many sentient beings as there are, complete" means the completion of the expanse of wisdom of nonabiding nirvāṇa. What is called samādhi is renowned as abiding in the sixth branch.

I shall explain the benefits. "Entering equipoise in withdrawal" means: if a yogin who keeps in mind the transcendent path engages with objects by the three kinds of conduct, then "all mantras," that is, all the Tathāgatas, Akṣobhya and the rest, bless him, and he comes to have their nature. That is the encouragement of the Tathāgatas. "Meditative concentration" is the five-branched one. "Wisdom" means realizing it directly; having entered equipoise in it and attained it, he will attain "the five superknowledges," the divine eye and the rest, because he fully knows the nature of objects and faculties. Then "life force and exertion": through the samādhi characterized by full knowledge of the reality of wind and the reality of mantra, he is "beheld by the bodhisattvas," that is, by the distinguished ones such as the knowledge-holders of the wheel; "beheld" means he becomes seen.

Likewise, "by the power of retention," that is, by its very stability, "Vajrasattva," that is, the great Vajradhara, "fully enters the body," meaning that one attains this, because retention has the nature of luminosity. Likewise, "the application of recollection": by attaining recollection, the maṇḍala of light arises, because it is the essence of the emanation of recollection. "And in omniscience" too, the deities of the maṇḍala of light outshine with their mass of light and subdue. "Merely by mastery of samādhi" means when the samādhi has become stable. "One becomes free of the obscurations" means the yogin becomes free of defilements. Thus the yogin who meditates on the six branches attains the encouragement of the Tathāgatas, the encouragement of Vajradhara, the five superknowledges and the rest: that is the yoga.

Now the four methods are to be explained, in the passage beginning "That essence." First of all, the yogin endowed with the yoga of any one deity, such as the great Vajradhara, having first meditated on emptiness, should visualize, upon a moon arisen from the transformation of the three wisdoms, a vajra arisen from the syllable hūṃ, and set the syllable hūṃ at its hub. From it the mass of light rays, "the supreme weapon," that is, their sharp points, are emanated into space, and with them one draws in the wheel of goddesses as desired and makes it enter. "Mind in the form of mind, the host of deities, made into the essence of mantra" means imagining them as the nature of all the Tathāgatas; from their transformation comes "the application of the mantra body," that is, one generates a body whose nature is all the Tathāgatas. One should also recite "the verse of awakening," namely the passage beginning "Glorious Buddha, holder of the body," together with the words and mantras of blessing by body and the rest, such as oṃ sarva tathāgata kāya vajra svabhāva ātmako'haṃ.

Further, "one's own maṇḍala" means emanating the host of maṇḍala deities. The yogin dwelling in its center practices "the meditation of the union of awakening": through the union of the equipoise of method and wisdom, "that mind," that is, the body of the principal deity dwelling in the center of the maṇḍala deities, that form of wisdom, melts into light, and one should meditate on it as a drop the size of a white mustard seed. This is the approach. That is the method of the three: the first union, the supreme king of maṇḍalas, and the supreme king of actions.

If, even having done so, the yogin has not attained accomplishment, then he should undertake the method of offering, in the passage beginning "For as long as." "Two kinds": if accomplishment has not been gained in two months, a month and a half, one month, half a month, seven days, or six days, then, by that very application, one emanates the goddesses such as Rūpavajrā and should worship the Tathāgatas dwelling in the element of space in the following sequence. Emanating Rūpavajrā, one worships the Tathāgatas with flowers of five kinds, saying oṃ vajra puṣpa āḥ hūṃ. Then, emanating Śabdavajrā, one should offer music, worshipping with oṃ sarvva tathāgata śapta pūja megha samudra pharaṇa samaya śriye hūṃ. Then, emanating Gandhavajrā, one should anoint the Blessed One with the fragrance of the four equal ingredients held in a conch bowl, saying oṃ vajra gandhe āḥ hūṃ svāhā. Emanating Rasavajrā, one emanates the five nectars resting in a vessel and worships the Tathāgatas, and "they too," that is, they partake of it with the intention of supreme great bliss: oṃ vajra amṛta āḥ hūṃ svāhā. Then, emanating Sparśavajrā, one should offer garments of various kinds, saying oṃ sparśe vajra āḥ hūṃ svāhā. Then, emanating Dharmadhātuvajrā, one worships the Tathāgatas with embraces, kisses and the like, saying oṃ sarvva tathāgata anurāgaṇa vajra svabhāva ātmako'haṃ. At the end of the offering one withdraws the goddesses and praises the Tathāgatas with the verses beginning "Akṣobhyavajra." Having received empowerment, they are sealed by the lord of their own family, and the Tathāgatas come to dwell within one through the aperture of Brahmā. Saying oṃ sarva tathāgata paramanobaregateta svabhāva ātmako'haṃ, one should rise and remain so. Then it will be accomplished. Thus the three, the first union and the rest, and the method of offering.

If, even having done so for six months and so on, it is still not accomplished, then one should undertake the method of division. "If it is not seen within six months" means if the buddhas do not grant the accomplishment characterized as the great Vajradhara, as explained. "Three times" refers to the division into the commitment being, the wisdom being and the samādhi being. One who keeps the commitments, vows and conduct of protection and the rest, as explained, and who is endowed with the yoga of the body explained earlier, should visualize a sharp vajra in each one's heart. From its hūṃ the hooks of light rays are sent out by the path of speech, and from their transformation one should visualize Sumbharāja, the king of wrathful ones. One commands him, and riding the chariot of the wind maṇḍala he goes before the Tathāgatas, strikes them at the heart with a vajra hammer, and should say this:

This so-and-so, O great vajra,
Stands firm through all of you;
He is ever devoted to you,
Diligent in guarding the commitments,
And has received empowerment and authorization:
Grant accomplishment to him!

By saying this, they will grant accomplishment within seven days. This is the three samādhis and the method of division.

If, even having done so, it is not accomplished, then one should undertake the application of punishment, in the passage beginning "When." "When awakening," that is, the state of Vajradhara itself, "is not accomplished," that is, has not been attained, then one should accomplish it by "the fierce application," that is, by the application of the method of punishment. Then "his wisdom is accomplished," meaning that accomplishment will certainly arise.

The sequence for this is as follows. In the lotus of Māmakī or another consort one meditates on a variegated vajra arisen from ha and a maṇḍala of light rays arisen from hūṃ. Above it, on a fire maṇḍala arisen from raṃ, one sets the syllable phaṭ, and one imagines one's own secret vajra as well, blazing like the fire at the end of an eon. Through the variegated light rays arisen from the seed of the wisdom being one draws in the Tathāgatas. "The division of families": by wrathful forms that accord with each family, they are made to enter the lotus of the wisdom goddess, are struck with the vajra peg and brought under control, and one meditates on oneself as not separate from them. One emanates the wheel of wrathful ones outside and should guard one's own deity body. That is the three samādhis and the method of punishment, one part of the practice.

Here is the summary of this. The Blessed One has taught it thus, unfailingly: when one who has faith, abides in the vows, and has generated the deity body with diligence, through the authorization of the deities, is not accomplished in six months, two months, one month or half a month, then one should undertake the practice of seven days. One endowed with the yoga of the vajra family, in the form of whichever deity, such as the great Vajradhara, dwelling at the center of the maṇḍala laid out within a celestial mansion arisen from the seed bhrūṃ, worships with unsurpassed offerings, through the host of goddesses issuing from his own pores, the wisdom deities who dwell in the buddha field of Abhirati. The goddesses, too, supplicate them with sweet and pleasing words and invite them; they enter one's own body, and one should meditate on oneself as not separate from them. Then it will be accomplished in seven days.

If, even having done so, it is not accomplished, then, from the hūṃ on the lotus, moon and vajra dwelling in the heart of one's own wisdom being, a vajra hook resembling Sumbharāja, consecrated by the three seed syllables, is sent out from one's own mouth. From its transformation, Sumbharāja is set upon a triangular chariot arisen from raṃ, above a wind maṇḍala arisen from yaṃ; blazing like the fire at the end of an eon and like the sun, he should be commanded and dispatched. He goes and strikes the heart of that very wisdom being with a blazing hammer, saying:

This so-and-so, O great vajra,
Stands firm through all of you;
He is ever devoted to you,
Diligent in guarding the commitments,
And has received empowerment and authorization:
Grant accomplishment to him!

Then one imagines him returning. If this is done, it will be accomplished in seven days.

If, even so, they do not grant accomplishment, then one imagines within the lotus of the consort a variegated vajra arisen from ha, and at its hub a sun maṇḍala born from hūṃ, equal to ten million suns. Above it the syllables phaṭ and raṃ transform, and one imagines a triangle blazing like the fire at the end of an eon. One imagines the secret vajra blazing as before, and likewise emanates the vajra hook and makes it firm. With it one pierces the heart of the wisdom deity and draws it in; it enters, and one places it in the mirror maṇḍala at the site of the goddess's lotus. That secret vajra too, emanating firelight, enters the lotus, and one imagines that the garland of mantra placed in the consort's mouth is brought down upon its crown, blazing like the fire at the end of an eon. One imagines all of them being reduced to ash. Their consciousness, like a lamp flame, in the form of the syllable hūṃ, transforms, and from it one meditates on the body of Vajradhara; it enters one's own body, and if one meditates on oneself as indivisible from it, then within just seven days one will certainly be accomplished in the knowledge that rouses the secret commitment.

By these four kinds, in their order, one should understand the sequence of conciliation, offering, division and punishment. That is one part of the practice.

Now samādhi will be explained by way of great passion. The yogin of the great Vajradhara or another deity enters union with his own consort, and with the sound of supreme passion draws in the wheel of wisdom and makes it enter his own body. He emanates it in the form of the nine goddesses, beginning with Locanā and ending with Sparśavajrā, and through them he accomplishes the aims of beings whose conduct is passion. He draws them back, and what is to be accomplished enters the body of his own consort. Again he emanates the four mudrās, Locanā and the rest, and imagines himself, as the embodiment of the five, at the center of the mudrā maṇḍala placed in the four directions. Gathering in all the wheel-holders and pleasing the Tathāgata who embodies the five, the yogin whose nature is all mantras falls silent at the moment of supreme joy. Then "awakening," that is, meditating on the reality of luminosity, means entering and dissolving into it. Then, urged by the four verses beginning "You, Vajrasattva," one awakens and acts for the benefit of beings. That is the first union, the king of maṇḍalas, and the supreme king of actions.

Empowerment will now be explained by the stages of the inner self. "The vajra of the three times" is the Tathāgatas of the three times. "Its nature" is their essence. "Wisdom" means imagining the wisdom of their very self gathered together. "Likewise" means like the Tathāgatas of the three times; "the body" means one should meditate on the body as well. Then one enters the lotus of the consort of the Tathāgatas of the three times. "That very color" is the color resembling the great Vajradhara, that is, white. Drawn forth from the heart, "the essence" is the awakening mind; one emanates it by the path of the vajra, and should confer empowerment on "one's own cause" within the lotus of the consort. "Opened" means what is to be accomplished; "the fruit" means for the sake of attaining the state of the great Vajradhara itself. Then, as one who wishes to act for the sake of others, "mantra" is the extracted mantra, that is, each deity's own mantra. "Dharma" means being three-faced and so on. "Having become divided" means one should emanate: "family," that is, the action mudrā, or "the Dharma mudrā," should be emanated from within the lotus of wisdom. If one asks what that is: "the wheel-turning monarchs" are the six Uṣṇīṣas, Ṭakkirāja and the rest. "The queens of knowledge" are Rūpavajrā and the rest.

"Of great magical power" means the five Tathāgatas. "The wheel-turning monarchs" are the Uṣṇīṣas, Ṭakkirāja and the rest. "All desires" means Yamāntaka and the rest. "The vajra lords" are the bodhisattvas. One emanates them, the wheel of meditation taught in the seventeenth chapter, and "empowerment in the maṇḍala of the inner self" means one should confer the knowledge empowerment. "The application of the rite" means through the application of meditation. "Wisdom" is the wisdom of supreme joy: the yogin of passion makes "the vajra of body, speech and mind" nondual with the deities of the maṇḍala of the consort's lotus, that is, makes them of one taste, and this is called "accomplishment." Further, "by the sequence explained earlier": "the vajra-bearing man of knowledge," the yogin of the great Vajradhara in union with the wisdom mudrā, emanates them from that very maṇḍala of the consort's lotus to the outside of the maṇḍala of his own self. They purify beings, and then, in forms such as the three-faced one, they enter in order into the east and the other directions, and one should meditate on the great maṇḍala. This is explained as the great accomplishment.

The rite of drawing the lines of the maṇḍala will now be explained. "Taking possession of the ground" is the time of approach. Then one should meditate on the form of Uṣṇīṣacakravartin, because he has power over everything. Then one digs the ground and fills it in, and if one cannot remove all the faults of buried thorns and the like, then, at the time of close approach, the time of purifying the ground, one imagines oneself in the form of Amṛtakuṇḍalin; if one places one's hand at the center of the ground with the mantra oṃ amṛta kuṇḍali āḥ hūṃ phaṭ, it will be purified by that powerful one.

Then "accomplishment": at the time of drawing the maṇḍala, the yogin who wishes to draw the maṇḍala of Kāyavajra or another should meditate on "his own self," that is, on the application of his own deity, the body of the deity Vairocana or another. "The time of great accomplishment" is the time of snapping the lines. There, "body" means the disciple and Amṛtakuṇḍalin. "The lord of the buddhas": with respect to the body maṇḍala, one meditates in the form of Amitābha or Akṣobhya; with respect to the speech and mind maṇḍalas, one meditates in the form of Vairocana, or Akṣobhya, or Amitābha, or Vairocana; and as "the all-pervading lord" one should meditate on Vajrasattva.

"This is the secret of all the buddhas" means it is kept secret by all yogins: it should not be taught to yogins of the Kriyā tantra and the like. "Supreme" means the holy yogin; the meaning is that it belongs to the practitioners. When it has been divided in this way, having known and realized the divisions of approach and the rest, "accomplishment" means one should accomplish the two kinds of accomplishment. Otherwise it will not be accomplished even in ten million eons. "Supreme" means supramundane; "peaceful" means uncommon. "The buddhas" are the Tathāgatas, and "the protector" is the great Vajradhara; the statement "even they will not accomplish it" is a statement of emphasis. That is the generation stage.

Approach and the rest in the completion stage will now be explained. "What is to be accomplished and the accomplisher" are wisdom and method. Their union is characterized by kissing (cumbana), embracing (āliṅgana), scratching with the nails (nakhaduśana) and striking; this external activity is explained as approach. Joining the vajra and the lotus in equality, preceded as before by generation from mantra, is close approach. Consecrating them with the seed syllables is their accomplishment. Experiencing self-awareness, by nature luminous, as great bliss, is called great accomplishment. "That itself does not arise from method": this is to be shown. "That wisdom itself" is the great Vajradhara, lord of all the buddhas. "Glorious" means endowed with merit and wisdom, the state of the great Vajradhara. "All the buddhas" are the five Tathāgatas; for the sake of attaining them, method is the cause, and this arises from method. "For that very reason, the dharmas": the reality of things is dharmatā; "nature" and "reality" are synonyms. The Treatise also says:

Here the yogin of completion
Is himself the great Vajradhara;
Endowed with the supreme of all aspects,
He then becomes omniscient.
The three realms of existence,
Past, future and present,
Through the purity of luminosity
He sees entirely in that instant.

That is the nature, the cause, the action and the fruit.

Now the answer to the question about the set of actions will be explained, in the passage beginning "Action." Whatever action the yogin undertakes to perform, "the application similar to it" means he should focus on the corresponding samādhi. "One should make a peaceful or a fierce or an appropriate maṇḍala" is the remainder of the sentence. "Together": in a fierce action, one generates the maṇḍala of the wrathful ones and should request of the Tathāgatas dwelling in the expanse of space "the terrible commitment," that is, to meditate on fierce conduct. "The commitment" is one's own body: "the vajra of body, speech and mind" should be consecrated with the seed syllables of the Tathāgatas and protected. Or else, "the Uṣṇīṣa commitment" means emanating the wheel of wrathful ones, Uṣṇīṣacakravartin and the rest; "arising from the rite as taught" means that one should bind the protection wheel arising from the sequence of the rite taught in the Kalpa. That is the wheel of the king of wrathful ones. "In accordance with it" means that, in the manner of the principal deity at the center resembling it, one should command "one's own wheel," the wrathful retinue. Likewise, "the practice": one should meditate on the body of the malicious one, whoever it may be, at the center of the maṇḍala as well. Then "his offense" is evil action characterized by harming the Three Jewels and the like. "To the wheel dwelling in his body," that is, to the Tathāgatas who pervade all beings, one should proclaim it, as follows:

This one harms the Three Jewels,
Delights in killing father and mother,
Is intent on reviling the holy Dharma,
Kills his teacher, and is hard to tame;
Even through teaching and the other stages
He does not rely upon the path.
Blessed One, today
I shall perform a fierce rite against him.

With this one proclaims the commitment.

Then, for the purpose of killing that malicious being, "those wrathful ones having arisen" means one should meditate on them going forth to the ten directions. They draw him in and place him at the center of "one's own wheel," the maṇḍala of the wrathful ones, and one should meditate on cutting him with swords and the like. Or else, "as taught" refers to the substance taught in the fifteenth chapter; "arising from it" means generating; "an effigy" is a form resembling him. "With a mind pervaded by wrath" means with a mind full of ferocity, naked and with hair unbound, pressing the effigy down with the left foot marked with a vajra hook and standing with the right leg extended, facing south. Reciting the mantras of the wrathful ones such as Yamāntaka, one emanates the wheel of wrathful ones from one's own heart; "various" means surrounded by hosts of male and female servants, male and female messengers, and male and female attendants, that is, with their retinue. One should command them to strike with the syllable hūṃ. They go, and "the vajra-bearing practice" means that, in order to carry out the yogin's command, "the target" is bound with a noose and struck with a hammer. "The king of wrathful ones": he is drawn in by the host of wrathful ones; "terrified" means pervaded by terror; "beaten" means without refuge; "frightened" means bereft of protection; "changed" means with eyes rolled back. One should draw in the target. Then the practitioner sees the target actually present, and "casting him into the effigy" means one should make him enter it. "His three vajras" are the seed syllables of body, speech and mind standing in a row. "The peg mantra" means one should strike with the mantra of Sumbharāja. This means striking with the peg the forms of the seed syllables of body, speech and mind at the crown, the throat and the heart.

Then "plague" is suffering. "Affliction" means piśācas and the like; "disease" means fever and the like; "arising from various regions" means the poison called the black heap and the like; "arisen beings" means that from which something arises: the snakes whose poison lies in the fangs, in thought, in touch, in sight, and so on. One draws in the poison of those snakes, places it into the head and other parts of the effigy, and should strike with the peg as before. Then "with all applications, performing the rite exactly as taught" means: "with mantras" in accordance with direction, place, time and sequence, that is, the wrathful mantras; "yantra," that is, the application of yantras; by the word "and so on," the substances and the like; "and with the mudrās too one should apply": one should complete it by the sequence of the four applications. Or else, one endowed with the wrathful form and the wrathful mantra explained earlier, reciting the wrathful mantra, takes an effigy of the being on ground marked with a single sign or the like; "consecrated by the vajra hook" means pressing it down with the left foot. In a fire of madana wood, "dwelling at the center of the wrathful maṇḍala," that is, as the yogin of Akṣobhya, if one cuts the effigy piece by piece and burns it, the enemy will die.

Or else, "malicious beings": first, those that move in the sky, such as crows, vultures and herons; those that move on the ground, such as wolves, bears and brown bears; and those that move underground, such as makaras, turtles and the like. "They having become enraged" means with jaws gaping, terrifying, eyes rolling, their bodies smeared with dust; blood comes forth from above and below, and "devoured" means devoured piece by piece. If one imagines this, closely examined, the enemy will die. "The distinction": one should imagine his consciousness, in the form of the syllable hūṃ, going, in the form of glorious Akṣobhya surrounded by the maṇḍala, into the presence of one who teaches the Dharma. Or else, it enters the drop of the seed syllable of the wisdom being in one's own heart.

Now, if one objects: "The Blessed One has said in this and that sūtra that taking life has a ripening hard to bear:

Whoever takes the life of the harmless,
Fierce, his hands smeared with blood,
Who engages in every evil deed,
Will be born in the Raurava hell forever;
Likewise through harming, life is cut short.

Since he said this and more, how is this not in contradiction with the word of the Blessed One?" The reply: "A living creature" means all of them. This is not harming. Rather, when someone whose actions are greatly evil cannot be tamed even by skillful means and would fall into the Avīci hell, then, in order to protect him, the yogin endowed with great compassion kills him through the sequence of mantra and mudrā and generates the body of a Tathāgata: by way of destroying him, he benefits him. This is the meaning of the saying:

If one is killed
And many come to have happiness,
That is a great mass of merit;
Otherwise it is a great evil.

Then, if one objects: "Killing is separation from life. Just as cutting the vital points with weapons and the like is of the nature of suffering, so too is it through the sequence of mantra and mudrā; therefore its ripening is the same. For the other does not wish to be separated from his own life, since it is said that everyone desires it." Do not say so. When life is drawn out through the sequence of mantra and the rest, suffering does not arise for the other; rather, he experiences mental happiness, as though seeing various parks and the like in a dream. That is how the comparison stands. And as for what was said, that all hold life dear, that too is not reasonable. If it were so, then since everyone also holds dear desire and the rest and the enjoyments of existence, even when they are not contrived, the twelve branches of the Blessed One's scripture, which serve to abandon attachment, would be pointless. Therefore, even though life is held dear, there is no fault whatsoever in drawing it out for the purpose of removing his evil; just as Prince Sarvārthasiddha abandoned the enjoyments of the kingdom of a wheel-turning monarch. Otherwise the consequence would go too far: this is the proof.

The peaceful rite and the others will now be explained. For the yogin who wishes to pacify, "a peaceful mind" is a gentle mind, and the external implements too become gentle. If one wishes to increase, the mind is expansive, and the substances too should be made gold-colored. "Power": when bringing kings and others under control, the mind is passionate, and the external substances too should be made red.

"Ferocious" means the fierce rite: when performing the rite of killing, the mind is wrathful, and the external substances too should be made black.

The hearths have the shapes of a circle, a square, a triangle and a crescent. The fire comes from the houses of the brahmin, kṣatriya, vaiśya and śūdra castes. The kindling sticks are wood of milky-sapped trees, madana wood, lotus roots and thorns. The mantra interweavings are vaśaṃ, bauṣaṭa, jaḥhri and hūṃ phaṭ. The garments are white, yellow, red and black; the other things are likewise. The rosaries are of crystal, gold, the four equal substances, and human bone. The substances for the fire offering are milk, butter, lotus, bilva, saffron, sandalwood, the great flesh and the like. The postures are the sattva cross-legged posture, the vajra cross-legged posture, the right leg extended, and the left leg extended. The gazes are fixed on the tip of the nose, level, glancing sideways to the left, and looking at the forehead. The dress is the hair even, the hair bound, the hair slanting to the left, and the hair loose. The vajras and bells are five-pronged, nine-pronged, three-pronged and one-pronged. The pegs are forged from silver, gold, copper and iron. The marking cords are white, yellow, red and black. The water offerings are filled with milk, water, camphor water and blood. The songs are the glorious melody, the joyful melody, the fierce melody and the desirous melody. The deity yogas are Vairocana, Ratnasambhava, Amitābha and Akṣobhya. The places are a temple, a mansion, a royal grove and a charnel ground. The directions are east, north, west and south. The times are evening, dawn, midday and midnight. The lunar mansions are the upper Victory, the lower Victory, the Hand, and the upper Sky-seeing. The planets are the Moon, Mercury, Venus, Mars and Saturn. The attendants are children, youths, those in their prime, and the old. The seasons are the rainy season, autumn, spring and early summer. The months are the middle month of the rains, the last month of the rains, the first month of autumn, the middle month of autumn, the middle month of spring, the last month of spring, and the first month of the rains. The bells are one-tongued, two-tongued, three-tongued and four-tongued. The instruments are the conch, the clay drum, the lute and the copper horn. The commitment mudrās are a brahmin woman, a kṣatriya woman, a vaiśya woman and a freckled woman. The ornaments are pearl, gold, coral and human bone. The zodiac houses are the Ram, the Bull, the Couple and the Lion.

At the time of a lunar eclipse, the peaceful and increasing rites; at the time of a solar eclipse, the rites of power and the fierce rites. At the time when the sun turns northward, the peaceful and increasing rites; at the time when it turns southward, the rites of power and the fierce rites.

The signs of the fire are swirling to the right, swirling to the left, going upward, and going to the south. The omens are the cries of the goose, the cuckoo, the pigeon and the owl. The chance signs are a scholar, a merchant, a prince, or one who is one-eyed, hunchbacked, lame, crippled, mute, deaf, blind and the like. The crests are the parasol, the victory banner, the pennant and the pegs. The hand emblems are the spear, the arrow, the wheel, the utpala, the lotus, the bow, the skull cup and the sword. The treasures are the precious horse, the precious elephant, the precious householder, the precious wish-fulfilling jewel, the precious queen, the precious general and the precious wheel. The food for the deities is the three sweet substances; curd and milk; and meat, liquor and the three hot spices. These are the tormas. The mudrās are the mudrā encircled by a lotus, the utpala mudrā, the mudrā of the source of phenomena, and the gathering mudrā. The incenses are sandalwood, aloeswood, camphor and the great flesh. The lamps are mustard-seed oil, sesame oil, butter, fragrant sesame oil and the great oil. The wicks are lotus-root fiber, bark of a milky-sapped tree, red cloth and fat. The drinks are royal sugar, sugar, liquor and blood. The flowers are mallikā, jāti, blue utpala and karavīra. The special places are the confluence of two rivers, the root of a tree, a temple and a crossroads. The days are windless, cloudless, windy and cloudy. The special bodily dress is bathing in a river, bathing in a lake, bathing with sandalwood and the like, and being unwashed and dirty.

That sets out the two hundred particulars. Other kinds should be followed according to the rites of the Piṭaka of the knowledge-holders. That is the practice.

"Then" and what follows: having answered the doubts of the bodhisattvas Maitreya and the rest, that is, the questions explained earlier that they had put forward, "all the Tathāgatas," because the task is complete, "the vajra of body, speech and mind," that is, they dwell in body, speech and mind just as is known, and "come to dwell without guarding" means they become of one taste. "Then" and what follows: merely because a question remains, "those Tathāgatas," Maitreya and the others, will praise the great Vajradhara, the nature of all the Tathāgatas, with "this," the king of praises explained below, the supreme praise. With "All body" and what follows: the nature of all the body families, the nature of all the mind families, the nature of all the speech families, and "the doer of all," the nature of Ratnasambhava and Amoghasiddhi; "Great Vajradhara, homage to you" shows bowing with body, speech and mind. A second homage is taught in order to show the special quality of faith. As the Treatise also says, with "homage, I bow" and so on, six homages are taught according to the special quality of faith. That is the samādhi of the supreme king of actions.

"O Blessed One, Tathāgatas of vajra body, speech and mind": because they have become of one taste with Vairocana and the others, this is Great Vajradhara himself. "In order to attain his body and the rest": because they have the nature of body and the rest, all sentient beings are equal to all buddhas, since it is said at length, "All sentient beings possess his lineage." Since sentient beings are like that, if one should kill them by the fierce rites explained earlier, then one would be killing all the buddhas as well. Since all the buddhas would be killed, how is it that "the lords of practice," the yogins, are not stained by sin? And if they were stained, how could the result, Vajradhara himself, be accomplished? That is the completion stage.

With "Then" and what follows: the Tathāgata, that is, Great Vajradhara, who has the nature of all tathāgatas, spoke thus to those bodhisattvas. "Great bodhisattvas" have the nature of Kāyavajra, and "great offering" has the nature of Great Vajradhara; this is an address to the great bodhisattvas. For that very reason there are four "excellents": because they have entered an utterly supreme state, the tathāgatas said, "Excellent! Excellent!" For the yogin master who has been empowered in the Guhyasamāja is, by the reasoning explained earlier, not different from them. "Lord of all buddhas" is Great Vajradhara; because he possesses the accumulations of merit and wisdom, he is "glorious." "Possessor of the vajra of awakening" refers to the tathāgatas: because he is their nature, he is the possessor of the vajra of awakening. "All things" means knowing them to be like an illusion, and working for the benefit of sentient beings through the sequence of emanation and withdrawal.

For the yogin who has attained nondual wisdom there is no conceptualization of merit and sin, because for one who abides in luminosity there is neither merit nor sin, nor their results, the happy and unhappy destinies. Then awakening, Great Vajradhara himself, is proclaimed to be liberation. As it says:

The destinies too have been rightly explained,
And actions together with their results;
Through fully knowing their nature,
Their unarisen state is also taught.

That is the completion stage.

"That protector" is the yogin who is like Vajradhara. If he performs fierce rites, through the sequence of mantra and mudrā, against the malicious who revile the guru and others, then all those beings, by the karma they themselves have done, will not be born in the lower realms, Avīci and the rest. Rather, awakening, which is extremely hard to find and is attained over ten million eons, namely Vajradhara himself, is "not hard to find," meaning it is easy to find. Because those people, through karma done previously, would experience the inexhaustible result of hell, if they are born in hell, they are sealed by that yogin with wisdom, the wisdom of the dharmadhātu, that is, they are blessed, and they become "possessors of the vajra of awakening," the very essence of Great Vajradhara. Therefore that is "supreme benefit": it should be known that the yogin has done a supreme kindness to those beings, the malicious ones. "Should be known" means should be understood. That is the completion stage.

"I shall explain the method": "the great being" is the yogin. If that yogin performs the rites of "benefiting," pacifying and so on, and of "subduing," killing and so on, then, having performed the great practice, that is, having generated the supreme king of actions by the generation stage and great bliss by the completion stage, if he then undertakes the rites explained earlier, they will be accomplished. That is the initial union and the rest, and the intrinsic nature and the rest.

In "Then" and what follows: "all the vajras" are those of the class in which the Mind Vajra of Great Vajradhara is foremost. Having heard that even the act of killing is done out of great compassion, "having heard and listened, delighted" refers to hearing; "satisfied" refers to reflection; "overjoyed" means they became joyful through meditation; and they said to the Blessed One, Great Vajradhara, "Excellent!" "Great Protector" is Great Vajradhara. "Great Sage" is Vairocana. "Great in dharmas" is Amitābha. "Great compassion" is Akṣobhya. Because they have that nature, "Excellent!" is said four times. The word is doubled out of faith. Some say it is because of the nature of method and wisdom. That is the supreme king of actions.

"How wonderful!" expresses amazement. "Samantabhadra" is the great compassion of Great Vajradhara. "Supreme" means surpassing the śrāvakas and the rest. "Stainless" means embraced by skill in means itself. The meaning is: whoever, even while performing the act of killing through dreadful action, "bestows," that is, gives, the very fruit of buddhahood to malicious beings. Therefore, "Blessed One, we have attained freedom from doubt": that is the practice of accomplishment.

With "Then" and what follows: Great Vajradhara, the nature of all tathāgatas, declared this to the bodhisattvas. "Sons of good family" is an address: the bodhisattvas who have arisen from the vajra family and the rest. "Whoever is empowered in this tantra of the glorious Guhyasamāja, the nature of all tathāgatas" is the mantra practitioner. Having heard the profound Dharma, that yogin should not, relying merely on the literal sound of the words, become frightened; that is, he should not reject it. "Should not become terrified" refers to reflection. "Should not become utterly terrified" refers to meditation.

With "Why is that?" the reason is stated: "Because the bodhisattvas have arisen from the vajra of awakening." Whatever bodies have the nature of the wrathful kings born from aversion, and whatever enlightened activity of killing and so on they perform, that is said to be "wrathful, born from the abode of aversion." Therefore all of them "are born," that is, have arisen, from "the vajra of awakening," Great Vajradhara. By the reasoning that all phenomena are by nature luminous, if one wishes to perform rites through the yoga of any family whatever, and one focuses on the nature of reality, pure by nature, and on great compassion, then there is no fault at all. However, one should not grasp merely the literal sound, abandon the nature of reality, and abandon the examination of what comes before and after, and so "reject the Dharma," that is, revile it; for the speech of the Tathāgata is profound and has many meanings. Therefore, O bodhisattvas, "the tantra" is the nature of reality, and "in brief" means that one should recollect it and reflect on it. Having thought, "All phenomena are pure by nature, and I too am completely pure by nature," one enters "emptiness," the nature of reality, and performs all rites through nondual yoga. That is the completion stage.

In "Then" and what follows: the bodhisattvas, Maitreya and the others, became free of doubt, and praised, that is, bowed to, the Blessed One, all the tathāgatas, with the verses explained below. I shall explain them. The praise is threefold, distinguished as praise of the Teacher, of the teaching, and of those to be taught. Of these, the Teacher is Vajradhara. I shall explain the praise of him together with his retinue.

"Who, the vajra of the three times," that is, having the nature of the tathāgatas of the three times. "Mind" is the nature of emanation. "Become existence and liberation" means granting liberation while not abiding in either of them. "Peaceful" is the nature of emptiness; "unobscured" is the nature of signlessness; "pure" is the nature of wishlessness; and "the essence of space" is the nature of the unconditioned. "The supreme awakening of the buddhas" is the five wisdoms, and "the sublime lords" are the nature of the wisdom of the dharmadhātu. "Awakening" is Great Vajradhara: to you who are such, I always bow with body, speech and mind.

The five aggregates, form and the rest, characterized as completely purified, are the nature of the five tathāgatas; the six sense sources, completely purified, are the nature of the bodhisattvas; the objects, completely purified, are the nature of Rūpavajrā and the others; and the elements, earth and the rest, completely purified, are the nature of Locanā, Māmakī, Pāṇḍaravāsinī, Tārā and Great Māmakī. "The awakening mind" is the ultimate, and "like it" is the conventional; to you who are its nature, I bow with body and the rest.

Likewise, "delusion and the rest" are the afflictive obscurations and the rest, the māras of the aggregates and the rest, and wrong views and the rest. Their completely purified essence is the nature of the four gatekeepers, noble Yamāntaka and the others. "Consort" means the wrathful goddesses, Vajravetālī and the others. "United with her" means through absorption; "arising" means producing. "Always closely joined" means at all times. "Various kinds" refers to the act itself, the actions of the limbs and so on; with these as foremost, "joy" is intense joy, and "the state intoxicated by it" is joy and the rest. "Become that" means having attained it: these are the wrathful kings. When examined thus, they are "like the awakening mind," that is, equal to Great Vajradhara: to them I bow. "Elaboration and the rest" are what was explained earlier as the cause of the arising of mind; their nature is the six, Ṭakkirāja and the others, and Uṣṇīṣa. Likewise, "bewilderment" is delusion, "hatred" is aversion, and "desire" is attachment. These belong to the three realms, and they themselves are the obscurations. "Vajra" is said of one in whom compassion and wisdom are present; the meaning is "having the nature of the wisdom and method of the three realms." "Like the awakening mind" is as before, and is construed with "I bow."

I shall explain the praise of the teaching. In "Whoever" and what follows: "beginningless, without entity" is what serves as the means of attaining Great Vajradhara; "arisen from wisdom and method" means having the nature of emptiness and compassion; "free from elaboration" means free from all conceptual thought. This is the characteristic of the wisdom-knowledge empowerment. Likewise the characteristic of the secret empowerment; "observance" is the awareness observance; and "union with the samaya" is meditation on the samaya and the rest. Whoever meditates on these is "like the vajra of awakening," like Great Vajradhara: to him I always bow. "Whoever," with the four limbs, approach and the rest, as foremost; "the supreme means of siddhi" is what is to be accomplished; those who meditate with the completion stage as foremost, as in "and the yoga of accomplishment" and what follows: regarding them as like Great Vajradhara, one should always bow to them. Likewise, those who accomplish those same limbs, approach and the rest, through the sequence of the four vajras; for "lesser siddhis," the buddhas who practice the deities of the Kalpa; and "through the guru's kindness," those who accomplish through the kindness of the vajra master, the nature of all tathāgatas: these are as before.

I shall explain the praise of those to be taught. When this tantra is being explained, "listening" is hearing the mere sound without the meaning; "reciting" is reciting it by heart without interruption; "reading" is reciting it from time to time; "reflecting" is contemplating it after hearing it; or else worshipping the volume with faith, or writing it oneself, or having others write it: "to them" is as before. "Whoever," the practitioner who accomplishes the set of rites of this very tantra, pacifying and so on, and its mantras, yantras and mudrās: "to him" is likewise as before.

Likewise, "whoever sees" means seeing merely the volume; "touches" means touching it with the hand; or, in a town, a forest retreat or elsewhere, recalls the volume or recalls its meaning; or obtains "the name," that is, "Samāja"; "merely" refers to the name of a part, that is, hears "Guhya" or "Samāja"; or whoever writes it, has it written, listens to it, reads it, reflects on it, meditates on it and so on; "has faith" means makes the mind faithful. Likewise, recollecting it in full was explained earlier, and recollecting a part should also be explained: recollecting a part is recollecting part of the letters or part of the meaning. Whoever does so is like "great awakening," Great Vajradhara; therefore "I always bow to him" means having bowed and paid homage.

After the verses of praise, I shall explain the rejoicing. "These words of praise": having praised and spoken with the words of praise explained earlier, "to all the guides," that is, to Great Vajradhara together with the deities of the maṇḍala. Its meaning is that, having praised the forms of the teaching, the practitioners, and those to be taught, those bodhisattvas then spoke. "Tantra" means continuum, and it is threefold, divided into cause, method and result. "All tantras" are the Kriyā, Caryā and Unsurpassed Yoga tantras; because it is foremost among their siddhis and samādhis, it is "the lord." Because it is the means of bliss, it is "supreme." Because it is the cause that gives birth to all tathāgatas, it is "of all tathāgatas." "Secret" means it should not be taught to those who have entered the Kriyā tantras and the rest. Because it gathers all tathāgatas, it is "Samāja," the assembly. Because it is the cause of mundane and supramundane siddhis, it is "the source." Because it is well heard, it is "well spoken," that is, well declared. "This is the tantra of the glorious Guhyasamāja." "Rejoicing" means delighting in it and summing it up. That is the supreme king of actions.

"Having spoken thus" means having uttered the words of praise. "The tathāgatas and bodhisattvas" are Akṣobhya and the others, and Maitreya and the others. "Their own body, speech and mind" entered the body and the rest of Great Vajradhara, and became one with them. "Nondual samādhi"; "became silent" means they remained focused on great bliss. That is the completion stage.

"All tathāgatas" are Akṣobhya and the others; their "body, speech and mind" are mantra or form; their "secret" is what is to be concealed. "The glorious Guhyasamāja," possessing the accumulations of merit and wisdom, is "all the secrets" of the king of tantras, namely the words of questioning; and "their teaching" is the words of the path. Whatever expresses the blessing of these is called "the blessing by the vajra wisdom that teaches all secrets."

As for "chapter": just as a cloth protects from gnats and the like, so, because it removes the afflictive obscurations, it is like a cloth; and because it holds and takes it up, it is patala. The body of text that expresses this is also called a chapter. The meanings of the words are sixteen.

From the commentary on the tantra, the extensive explanation of the meaning of the words of the eighteenth chapter.

Thus, having received from the guru's lips
What fully illuminates the awakening mind
Of the Guhyasamāja,
I have composed this very tantra commentary.

Clear in its words, drawn from sūtras and tantras alike,
Blazing with the fire of method and wisdom,
I, Nāgārjunagarbha,
Have in brief gathered the commentary into a summary.

Through great faith in glorious Vajrasattva,
In the Guhyasamāja, king of tantras,
And in the guru,
I am one who brings benefit to beings.

When that glorious king was young,
At the time of his eighteenth year,
In the glorious place built by Āryadeva, in the east,
Clearly set on another side of the monks' monastery,
By the kindness of Vajrasattva I composed this tantra commentary.

By this very commentary on the glorious Samāja tantra,
A lamp fully expanded,
May the darkness of the afflictive and cognitive obscurations

The commentary on the glorious Guhyasamāja, king of tantras, a work of the Great Vajra Vehicle, composed by the venerable glorious Nāgārjuna, is complete. It was translated and finalized by the great Indian preceptor, the venerable Śrī Kumārakalaśa.

Translator's notes

This is a commentary on the eighteenth chapter of the Guhyasamāja Tantra, its "later tantra" (Uttaratantra), here explained question by question; the quoted words are the opening words of the root-text passages and are rendered literally. "The verse on generating" renders བསྐྱེད་པའི་ཚིགས་སུ་བཅད་པ, the verse whose opening word is "generating." The phrase "teaches the question about the acts of the disciple" follows the Tibetan, which here says "question" rather than "answer to the question." The commentary maps the bodhisattvas' praises onto the generation and completion stages and onto the five stages of the Guhyasamāja completion stage. In that list མཆོད་པར་བྱང་ཆུབ is read as མངོན་པར་བྱང་ཆུབ, "full awakening" (abhisaṃbodhi), the fourth of the five stages; the written "offering awakening" makes no sense there. The phrase "That is the supreme victor of action" (ལས་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་བ་མཆོག) is translated literally; its precise reference in the commentary's scheme is unclear. "That is the practice" renders the terse དེ་ནི་སྒྲུབ་པའོ. "The initial union," "the supreme victor of the maṇḍala" and "the supreme victor of action" are the three samādhis of the Guhyasamāja sādhana (ādiyoga, maṇḍalarājāgrī and karmarājāgrī). In "again and again eager to listen," the written ཡང་དང་ཡང་དུ་མི་ཉན་པར་འདོད་པས ("wishing not to listen") contradicts the context, and the negative is taken as a slip; the reading is uncertain. "The words that are difficult and that have been set aside" renders the obscure དཀའ་བ་དང་སྤངས་པའི་ཚིག literally. The sixteen points through which each key word is explained are the stages of the Guhyasamāja path: the initial union, the supreme victor of the maṇḍala, the supreme victor of action, the generation and completion stages, the five stages from vajra recitation to union, cause, action and result, and the six-branch yoga (individual withdrawal, meditative concentration, life and exertion or prāṇāyāma, retention, recollection and samādhi), together with pledge, vow and accomplishment. "Purified by the fourth" refers to the clear light as fourth beyond the three phases of appearance; "the hundred and sixty natures" are the conceptual natures of the completion-stage literature. The opening clause (gnas shing gsum bor zhing 'bral bar) is obscure; it is rendered as describing the chapters being arranged and distinguished, apparently into the three perfections (own benefit, others' benefit, and the means to both) set out below. In the gloss on the second group, slob dpon gyi lan ("the master's answer") is read as slob dpon gyi las, "the master's work," parallel to the following "common work." In the sixth-chapter group, ས་སྐུ is taken as a slip for སྐུ ("body"). The sentence on the fourth chapter literally says the accomplishing of the body "does not exist" there; it is rendered according to the evident argument, that it cannot count as approach because emptiness is absent. The four "means" are the classical political expedients sāma, dāna, bheda and daṇḍa; ཆད་པ here is therefore "punishment," not "annihilation." The tathāgata essence in the tenth chapter refers to the heart (essence) of the tathāgatas that is exhorted by mantra, not to buddha-nature doctrine. "Entering suchness" reads ཉེས་པར་གསུངས ("taught faults") as ངེས་པར་གསུངས ("taught definitively"), an evident slip. In the list of the three samādhis, དབང་པོའི་སྦྱོར་བ is read as དང་པོའི་སྦྱོར་བ, "the initial union," which the commentary names elsewhere. Likewise ཐབས་ནས in the extended explanation is taken as ཐབས་ནི. "One's own special deity" (rang gi lhag pa'i lha) and "the parasol of desire" ('dod pa'i gdugs, apparently a name for one of the principal channels) are rendered literally; their exact identification is uncertain. The thirty-three, forty and seven natures together are the eighty natural conceptions of the Guhyasamāja completion-stage system. "The unassailable" renders mi 'phrogs pa (Skt. asaṃhārya), one of the three tantras of the Guhyasamāja; it is glossed as mi 'jigs pa, which as written means "fearless," but which here evidently stands for mi 'jig pa, "not perishing," as the following account of the three kinds of permanence of the three bodies requires. "Akṣobhya" reads ma bskyed pa ("the unproduced") as mi bskyod pa, an evident slip in a list of the five tathāgatas. In the protection of the yoga, ཚངས་པའི་གནས་བཞི་སྟོང་པ is read as ...སྒོམ་པ, "meditating on the four abodes of Brahmā" (the four immeasurables). The phrase "the tip of the nose and the other tips" renders སྣའི་རྩེ་མོ་གསུམ, literally "three tips of the nose"; the intended three points are unclear. The second verse corresponds to the Abhisamayālaṃkāra's verse on spiritual lineage, attributed to Maitreya; the source of the first verse is not identified here. "That is the summary" reads སྡོམ་པ ("vow") as བསྡོམས་པ ("summary, total"), since the sentence closes the list of sixteen points. "Peace" reads ཞིབ ("fine, minute") as ཞི་བ, matching the root verse's "peaceful" (śānta) being glossed throughout. "Training" reads སློབ་པ as བསླབ་པ. The quoted lines are the Abhisamayālaṃkāra's definition of generating the mind of awakening. "Heedfulness" reads བག་མེད་པ ("heedlessness") as བག་ཡོད་པ, since the list follows the topics of the Compendium of Training (Śikṣāsamuccaya), where heedfulness is the topic. "Occasions of aspiration" reads འདུམ་པ as འདུན་པ, which the gloss that follows treats as the first kind of aspiring mind; the line is uncertain. The twenty-two similes are those of the Abhisamayālaṃkāra. The verses on method and wisdom and on discipline and samādhi echo the Bodhipathapradīpa and its sources. The Tibetan says the Vaibhāṣikas hold all conditioned things to be "permanent" (rtag pa); this contradicts the standard formula listed alongside suffering, empty and selfless, and is read as a scribal slip for mi rtag pa, "impermanent." The Lokāyata name is written rgyad pan pa, read as rgyang phan pa. The school called rnam par dpyod pa pa, holding a pure, aspectless yet manifold cognition, could not be identified with confidence and is rendered literally as "the Investigators." The words jinajik, ratnadhṛk, ārolik and prajñādhṛk are the Sanskrit mantra-names being glossed from the root tantra. "Faith ends them" reads dad pa for the written dang pa, which does not fit the context. The phrase rendered "as a means of winning over" (gdon pa'i thabs) is uncertain. The clause "as regards accomplishing the uninterrupted benefit of beings, 'it is not' is said" is rendered literally; its point is unclear. The four delighting goddesses (Moharati and the others) and the four gatekeepers (Yamāntaka, Prajñāntaka, Padmāntaka, Vighnāntaka) follow the Guhyasamāja root tantra being glossed. The phrase "mantras of the serpent and the rest" (sbrul la sogs pa'i sngags) is rendered literally; its reference is unclear. The water wheel (zo chun gyi 'khrul 'khor) is the image of breath rising and falling like the pots of a water wheel. The names of the six bodhisattvas and goddesses follow the Guhyasamāja maṇḍala; the commentator names only the first of each set. The ten wrathful ones are given by their standard Guhyasamāja identities: dbyig sngon as Nīladaṇḍa, stobs po che as Mahābala, gnod mdzes kyi rgyal po as Sumbharāja. The directions of the two extra spokes are named by their guardians, Indra and Īśāna (northeast), Varuṇa and Nairṛti (southwest). The reading "slightly inclined" for the first set of spokes (cung zad g.yo ba) beside "slightly slanted" (cung zad yo ba) follows the text as written; both seem to describe the placement of spokes on the wheel. The phrase "thus it addresses a doubt" (the tshom byed do) is rendered literally; it may mean that the root text's "raising" anticipates the doubt whether the slain can be revived. "Women born in the realms" is explained as hyperbole, since the commentator notes women do not exist in the form and formless realms. The sentence "the six kings of knowledge are the six kings of desire" renders འདོད་པའི་རྒྱལ་པོ literally here; elsewhere in this text the same phrase is the name Ṭakkirāja (one of the door guardians), but in this context it seems to refer to the six sense-object goddesses (Rūpavajrā and the rest) and their emblems, so it is not given as the proper name. "That which arises from attachment" (འདོད་ཆགས་ལས) is the emblem of the touch goddess; the reading is somewhat uncertain. Rakta and śukra are the red and white generative fluids. In the first sentence the text reads "their abode is four" (བཞི); this is taken as a slip for "basis" (གཞི), which matches the preceding passage ("their basis is the abode"). "The parasol of desire" renders འདོད་པའི་གདུགས literally as the name of one of the three principal channels; its exact identification is uncertain. "Abode" in the seed-syllable section renders གནས as it stands in the text. "Taking the Tathāgatas as witnesses" renders དབང་པོར་བྱས་ལ, literally "making them the lords"; here the word is not the name Indra used elsewhere in the text. Dveṣarati, Rāgarati and Vajrarati are reconstructed from the Tibetan on the pattern of Moharati. "Flowers" refers to menstruation, as the commentary's own gloss on the right age makes clear. "By the same reasoning as the saying 'ghee is life'" renders མར་ནི་ཚེ་ཞེས་བྱ་བའི་རིག་པ, read as the Indian stock example of naming a cause by its effect (āyur ghṛtam). The text's རྡོ་རྗེས་འོག་གི་རྒྱུད is read as རྡོ་རྗེ་ས་འོག, the Vajrapātāla Tantra. In this passage དབང་པོ means the sense faculties, not the god Indra. "The five hooks" refers to the five meats. "A flickering like fireflies" renders གཡོ་བ་སྲོག་ཆགས་ཀྱི་ནོར་བུ, literally "the moving jewel of creatures," taken as the firefly sign of the completion-stage dissolution sequence. "This is to be consumed" renders བསྲེག་པར་བྱའོ ("to be burned") as written; the sense in context is somewhat uncertain. In this passage དབང་པོ means the sense faculties, not Indra. ཆད་པ here is rendered "punishment" (the coercive fourth method directed at the deities), not "annihilation" as in the general glossary, since the ritual context requires it. གནོད་མཛེས་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་པོ is identified as Sumbharāja, the wrathful deity used for such compulsion in the Guhyasamāja. In the opening sentence the noun "vajra" after "variegated" is supplied from the parallel visualization later in the passage, where the same seed ha yields "a variegated vajra." The phrase rendered "if it is not accomplished" reads in the Tibetan "if it is accomplished" (འགྲུབ་པར་འགྱུར་ན); the negative is evidently dropped, since the sequence escalates only on failure. The words རྡོ་རྗེའི་ཆང་ཟུངས་ཀྱི་ཧཱུཾ are obscure; they are taken simply as the hūṃ upon the lotus, moon and vajra. The four methods, conciliation, offering (gifts), division and punishment, correspond to the classical Indian four means (sāma, dāna, bheda, daṇḍa), here applied to compelling the deities to grant accomplishment; ཆད་པ is therefore "punishment," not "annihilation." "The Treatise" (རབ་ཏུ་བྱེད་པ, prakaraṇa) is not further identified in the text; the verses quoted resemble those of the Pañcakrama tradition. The list of Vairocana, Akṣobhya, Amitābha and again Vairocana for the speech and mind maṇḍalas is translated as it stands. The first line of the quoted verse reads literally "whoever takes life, the not malicious"; it is taken as "takes the life of the harmless," which fits the argument that follows. The phrase rendered "ground marked with a single sign" (མཚན་མ་གཅིག་ལ་སོགས་པའི་ས) is obscure and translated literally. ངུ་འབོད is the Raurava ("Howling") hell. The objections and replies defend the fierce rite as a compassionate act of liberation for one otherwise bound for the Avīci hell. The planet written ལག་པ is evidently ལྷག་པ, Mercury. The four lunar mansions (རྒྱལ་སྟོད, རྒྱལ་སྨད, ལག་པ, ནམ་མཐོང་གོང་མ) are given by their literal meanings apart from the Hand (Hasta), since their Sanskrit identifications are not certain. Throughout this list each set of four corresponds in order to the peaceful, increasing, power and fierce rites; "the great flesh" and "the great oil" are human flesh and human fat. The commentary glosses the root tantra's passage on the fierce rites (the killing of malicious beings) as an act of compassion that, in the completion-stage view, carries no sin and leads the victims to awakening; this is a doctrinal claim within the tantric context, translated as it stands. "That is the completion" (rdzogs pa'o) in the first paragraph is taken as shorthand for the completion stage, as elsewhere in the passage. "Supreme victor of actions" (las kyi rgyal ba mchog) is read as "supreme king of actions" (rgyal po), the term used throughout the text. Several syllables read as evident copyists' slips: rnam par spyod pa as rnam par dpyod pa, "examination"; rigs pa'i brtul zhugs as rig pa'i brtul zhugs, the "awareness observance" (vidyāvrata); rnyed pa as bsnyen pa, "approach," the first of the four limbs of approach and accomplishment; rtogs pa'i lha as rtog pa'i lha, "the deities of the Kalpa"; and 'dri ba, "asking," as 'bri ba, "writing," given the context of copying the volume. The phrase about "lesser siddhis" and "buddhas" is terse and its construal is uncertain. The fifth goddess of the elements is named in the text as Great Māmakī, though Māmakī is also listed separately; this is kept as written.
