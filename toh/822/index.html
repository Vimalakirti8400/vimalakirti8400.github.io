About this text

The Verses of Auspiciousness of the Five Tathāgatas, in Sanskrit Pañcatathāgatamaṅgalagāthā, is a short devotional text in verse that invokes the blessings of five buddhas in turn. After an opening homage to the Three Jewels, each stanza praises one buddha, beginning with Vairocana and Akṣobhya, recalls his qualities and realization, and concludes with the wish that the auspiciousness of that buddha bring all beings to peace. The Tibetan title line is not fully preserved in the source, so the Tibetan form of the title is not given here; a closing line within the text also refers to the verses of auspiciousness of the Seven Buddhas, so the work appears to circulate under more than one title or in related versions. This work is Toh 822 in the Kangyur, the Tibetan canon of translated buddha-word, and belongs to the class of auspicious verses, texts recited to invoke good fortune and well-being.

Translation

May there be, here and now, the complete auspiciousness of the Sugata. Thus ends the verses of auspiciousness of the Seven Buddhas.

In the language of India: Pañcatathāgatamaṅgalagāthā. In the language of Tibet: Verses of Auspiciousness of the Five Tathāgatas.

Homage to the Three Jewels.

Vairocana, who holds the ocean of sublime wisdom,
Whose body is like the full moon, who has reached the far limit of existence,
The auspiciousness of his complete and perfect realization of all things:
By that auspiciousness, may all beings be brought to peace.

Akṣobhya, the king who purifies existence and delusion,
The protector, made auspicious by the fullness of all qualities,
The auspiciousness of one whose mind is equal and attentive to all:
By that auspiciousness, may all beings be brought to peace.

The Buddha adorned with the ornaments of all excellent qualities,
Who unfailingly bestows the riches of true reality,
The auspiciousness of the one called Ratnasambhava, the Source of Jewels:
By that auspiciousness, may all beings be brought to peace.

The abode of meditative concentration, absorption, and sublime wisdom, gone to the other shore,
Seated upon the lotus of wisdom, Amitāyus, the Lord of Limitless Life,
The auspiciousness of bestowing a life without measure:
By that auspiciousness, may all beings be brought to peace.

Who directs the mind toward acting for the benefit of sentient beings,
Who frees all beings from the bonds that bind them,
The auspiciousness of the supreme Sage, Amoghasiddhi, the Accomplisher of Aims:
By that auspiciousness, may all beings be brought to peace.

Translator's notes

The passage begins mid-verse, as the closing of the preceding Seven Buddhas text, and ends mid-sentence; the final line is left unfinished as in the source. The Sanskrit title is given as it appears in the Tibetan transliteration. Amitābha appears here under the name Amitāyus, as the text states. The names Akṣobhya and Amoghasiddhi are supplied from the standard identification of the five Tathāgatas, since the Tibetan names them only by epithet in these verses. The phrase "dge mnyam bsam sems ldan pa" in the Akṣobhya verse is rendered according to its most natural reading, and a more literal sense might be "possessing the thought of equality in virtue."
