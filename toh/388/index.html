About this text

The Glorious King of Tantras of Body, Speech, and Mind, known in Sanskrit as the Śrī Kāya Vāk Citta Tantra Rāja and in Tibetan as the Palden Ku Sung Tuk Gi Gyud Kyi Gyalpo, is a Buddhist tantric scripture centering on the inseparable nature of body, speech, and mind as understood within Vajrayana practice. The text opens with a dialogue between a supplicant and Vajradhara, who expounds on the symbolic identities of body, speech, and mind with the vase, the secret, and primordial wisdom respectively, situating these teachings within a broader mandala of buddha figures including Māmakī, Samayatārā, and Pāṇḍaravāsinī. It is classified as a tantra and belongs to the tantric section of the Kangyur, where it is catalogued as Toh 388. The text was translated from Sanskrit into Tibetan by the Indian paṇḍita Gayādhara together with the monk-translator Śākyaśes, a well-known collaborative pair responsible for transmitting several important tantric works into Tibet.

Translation

The King of Tantras of the Great One is complete.

Translated by the Indian paṇḍita Gayādhara and the translator, the monk Śākyaśes.

In the Sanskrit language: Śrī Kāya Vāk Citta Tantra Rāja. In the Tibetan language: The Glorious King of Tantras of Body, Speech, and Mind.

Homage to the glorious Vajrasattva.

"Thus have I greatly desired this,
and I truly dwell in what is supreme.
O Protector endowed with great compassion,
I pray that you explain this to me."

Vajradhara declared:

"The body is of the essential nature of the vase.
Speech is held to be of the nature of the secret.
Mind too is wisdom and primordial gnosis.
The buddha, the goddess Māmakī,
Samayatārā, and White-Robed Pāṇḍaravāsinī —
thus was it said to Vajradhara.

One who is thoroughly familiar with the meaning of the tantra,
who is not confused by the words of explanation,
and who is not defeated by others' debate —
yet if one has not received empowerment and oral instructions,
then empowerment, consecration, fire offering, and the like,
and the practice of mantra, are all fruitless toil.
After death, one is born as a hell-being.
Therefore I pray that the chief one fully explain
the authentic oral instructions of empowerment."

Vajradhara declared:

"For the vase empowerment sequence to begin,
one must first seek out a place that is highly praised,
auspicious, pleasing, and very pleasant.
Auspicious birds should cry out their sweet song.
One should hold that a wish-fulfilling tree is present.
Alternatively, a charnel ground with a single tree,
or a riverbank, and such places —
to the lord of the land and the earth goddess,
offer a torma, a fire offering, and an ablution offering,
then press down the earth and take its measure with plumb lines.

Then first present the approach practice.
The three levels of approach are superior, middling, and lesser,
defined by signs, numbers, and times.
For each of these three, there are three:
signs are form, light, and sound;
numbers are one thousand, ten thousand, and one hundred thousand;
times are fifteen, thirty, and seven days.
When the approach practice is complete,
after pleasing the deities through the approach,
all the substances for the deity and the disciples
should be properly arranged in an excellent manner.

A girl of auspicious years
should gather kuśa grass from a direction compatible with the earth element,
and check for signs.
On the tip of a tooth-stick, place flowers and fragrance.
Check the signs on the protection cord and so forth.
The maṇḍala of empowerment is then to be generated.

The maṇḍala is for disciples of superior, middling, and lesser capacity,
together with samādhi, flowers, colored powder, and sequence —
whatever applies, that very one it is.

Then set out the offering substances in order.
With the khaṃvīra mantra,
the vajra action is to be performed upon them.
Then with the kuśa grass and so forth,
the sweeping ritual together with its rites is to be performed.

Furthermore, as preliminary substances
for pleasing the lama,
and in order to make offerings to the deities of the maṇḍala —
horses, elephants, buffalo,
jewels and other treasures,
incense, lamps, and so forth —
one hundred, or one thousand, or ten thousand, or one hundred thousand —
with an upper garment, ornaments, banners, and the like,
having consecrated them, gather the accumulations and set them outside.

Then the disciple is to be introduced.
Anoint with bath-water and fragrant substances upon the limbs.

'How wonderful! Who are you?
If you have awakened from the illusions of the world,
do you sport in the great maṇḍala
of great-famed Heruka?
With faith, be joyful.
Free from conceptual thought, Vajrasattva,
you of great bliss — show yourself to me, the Teacher.
I pray that Vajradhara take notice.
He who was prophesied for supreme awakening —
I seek you, the Great Protector.
Grant me that very pledge.
Grant me the awakening mind.
Grant me what has arisen from the lotus.
Grant me the secret delight.
I pray that the Protector lead me
into the supreme city of great liberation.'

'Excellent! Excellent, noble child!
You are the supreme among all the Victor's sons.
Since you are the destroyer of all conceptual thought,
you who are nondual should take this up.
Equal to Akṣobhya, the Blessed One,
you who are beyond subjugation should take it up.
Take up that very supreme reality
of the conduct and practice of the pledges.
Skillful means and wisdom joined as one —
you must never abandon them.
In pure conduct and likewise in meditative concentration,
apply yourself with effort and make the mind stable.'"

One should drink alcohol regularly and not become angry. Likewise, one should understand the nature of meat. One should rely upon the mudrā of another. These are the eight sacred pledges (samaya).

Take refuge in the Three Jewels. Bestow the common vows and the generation of awakening mind. Perform three circumambulations and three prostrations. Having drawn a curtain across the middle, cover the five senses — the eyes and the rest. Offer flowers to the many deities. Purify and cleanse all by means of the all-creating water. The vajra together with the flower should be placed upon that one's heart and meditated upon. One should confess the faults of transgressions — such as showing contempt toward the master and the like. Bring down the outer vajra-holder. First, confess negativities three times.

Through the eye, seeing forms; through the ear, sounds; through the nose, smells; through the tongue, tastes; through the body, tactile sensations — the accomplishments (siddhi) and the enlightened activities respectively: the skilled one should know how to examine these. Having cast the flower, make offering to the deity as well.

The retinue members are the limbs of the principal deity. One should know the defining marks of the principal. Just as a healing physician draws out the thorns from the five sense faculties, so too do the great bodhisattvas and the Victors perfectly dispel all the cataracts of ignorance.

Thereupon the maṇḍala of the mirror is revealed to that one. In the empowerment maṇḍala to the northeast, on an eight-petalled lotus, place it and confer the five empowerments of awareness. Through the water element, Akṣobhya, and the mirror, the enlightened activity of the Vajra family is accomplished. The crown ornament possesses the precious gemstone: by the nature of the wisdom of equality (samatā jñāna), the enlightened activity of the Jewel family is accomplished. The vajra empowerment of Amitābha: by the nature of discriminating wisdom (pratyavekṣaṇā jñāna), the Lotus family performs enlightened activity. The bell of meaningful accomplishment: through the wisdom of all-accomplishing activity, various enlightened activities are performed.

The empowerment of the name, drawn from the family, is joined with vajra — vajra mind. Being the very essence of the great Vairocana, from the nature of the wisdom of the dharmadhātu, it holds the defining marks of suchness. On a fearless lion throne, the master who engenders the family should confer the empowerment. Through the subjugation of the four māras, the four great kings — in the form of victory banners through which phenomena never decline — pervade all through the dharmakāya. One should cover from above with a parasol.

The vajra and bell are the two ascetic practices. The mudrā and sacred pledge are the third. Relief and prophecy, permission and the irreversible, stabilization — these are the five aspects. Furthermore, all the particular material substances — the seven precious things and everything else — I have declared to be held in common.

For the vase empowerment there are three divisions: the first is entering the maṇḍala; the second is the empowerment of awareness; the third is stabilization.

Then the secret is to be explained. Having established the bodhicitta in the body as Mount Meru, resting upon a seat of bliss — one should seek the first wisdom-consort (vidyā) who has become the support of the secret empowerment. First, examination through wisdom; then, receiving the self-empowerment; purifying faults; generating the maṇḍala; pacifying obstructing spirits and obstacles; protection; and offering with assemblies to it. Make many drops of perfumed liquid. That very one who abides at the left — having rubbed and performed the union with what is rubbed — through the stream of bliss arising from the rubbing, pervading the entire body secretly, let it flow like white upon a red vein. As with entering the maṇḍala before — visualizing a syllable JAḤ at the wheel of Romatī, having blessed the tongue as a lotus, visualizing BHYAḤ at the tip of the tongue, blended with camphor from Siṃhala, settling BHYAḤ there and dissolving into it. Enjoy and unite the two by means of both, and meditate in the center without any object. For the secret empowerment there are three divisions: conferring the vajra upon the principal consort; conferring it by means of the fierce wrathful one, the broad one;

conferring the vajra upon the brahma-channel.

As for the wisdom-gnosis empowerment as well, first comes the examination of the wisdom consort. Through body, speech, and mind — all three — the skilled one should examine and come to know her. Then, having examined the five distinguishing features — family, youth, wealth, adornment, and form — these five distinctive marks of the body constitute what is taught as the outer examination.

Stability of mind is the family's distinguishing mark of the vajra — so have I declared. Having little conceptual thought is taught as the distinguishing mark of vajra youth. Being adorned with non-conceptuality is what one should understand as the distinguishing mark of vajra adornment. That suchness itself does not change into a threefold state, and is the unfailing victory banner of Dharma — this is the distinguishing mark of vajra form. Enjoying all phenomena in the flavor of emptiness is the distinguishing mark of vajra wealth. Kuṇḍalamā is the family of the mind — this is the distinguishing mark of family. Being erect like an arrow of suchness is the distinguishing mark of the youth of mind. The vein being clear and red, and the awakening mind being clear and white, shining in a non-dual manner — like a clear jewel threaded through silk — this is the distinguishing mark of the form of mind. For some, the nāḍī and rasanā, and the avadhūtī as the non-dual — this is the distinguishing mark of the wealth of mind.

The first is the outer examination, the second is the inner, the third is the secret. Examination of the body is the body of the deity; examination of speech becomes the speech; examination of the mind becomes the mind. Know that the intent pervades all under the manner of mere illusion. The six of the body, the seven of speech, and the seven of the mind — in the manner of illusion. The first is confusion of a mirage for water; the second is the mirage itself; the third is like a mirage that does not appear — so should one understand it from the mouth of the teacher.

For water there are seven, for butter one; for smell there are seven, for meat two; for eggs four, for milk three. These are taught as the support of what is sought, for a period of three to four years.

For kara there are two, for sugar one; for beer know there are two; for adornments there are various particular kinds. Meat of winter and likewise of autumn — if kept one day it possesses essence. Meat of summer and spring as well — if it has warmth it possesses essence. Meat that has died by its own nature: if it is sought out and used, the samaya is impaired. It should be combined with the five roots along with their branches, and further with medicines such as fruits, together with butter. Meat is likewise used in the manner of butter. Snow produces the essence of everything; sweetness causes everything to be relished. Essence-producing meat is called youthful; relished meat has fifteen portions. Essence-producing meat and butter, both, are mixed in proportions of five and five. Beer is taught to be equal to these. For relished meat as well, in equal portions of milk and egg — take five and three, then five and two as the chief ones. With sour beer or milk, give them in equal measure with the meat. This is the branch with elaboration.

Then come delimitation of boundaries, service, and the various samaya practices — these are the branches without elaboration. I have taught these for the sake of attaining empowerment: the meditation that precedes the conferral of empowerment. Entrust the wisdom consort, give the pith instructions, perform the signs, and examine the marks of desire. The examination being utterly without elaboration, embrace with the hands and feet and so forth — this is taught as the initial method of union. Diadem, neck ornament, and so forth — this is called the great method of union. Standing, squatting, supine, face-down, sideways — these are the five. The profound intent of all of these: one who practices without understanding that intent thereby creates the cause of birth in the Avīci hell. Transformed a hundredfold from the ordinary, it is connected with the vase empowerment. The vase and the secret — beyond the secret is the third. The karmic ripening thereof cannot be fully described. The sequence for entering the maṇḍala should be learned from the mouth of the guru. The actual practice of cultivating the seven branches, arising from three distinct forms: mantra and medicine are the first, the method of union is taught as the second,

The third is meditative absorption (samādhi). By means of mantra and medicine, the kundā is straightened. The measurements of the girdle, the measurements for remaining in place, and the measurements for straightening are all to be known. The girdle that accords with the four elements is equal to the measurement of the waist. Three or four finger-widths of silk or cotton: the measurement for remaining in place is of six kinds. It abides in the palace of liberation's secret, abides in the sense sources with awareness, abides in the empty wheel of Hayagrīva, abides in the elements, the secret, and the bliss-sustaining place, abides in the emanation wheel on the petals, abides at the heart with perfect abandonment, and abides undivided in Heruka, unencircled by vajra sovereignty. The measurement for straightening arises at four finger-widths. First one bathes; for the second, medicine, oil, and fragrance, three in three twos, are applied to the upper and lower body and smoked over fire. The three-pronged root is profoundly subtle. Spru-ba, lca-ba, ra-mnye-ba, nyeu-shing, ra-mo-shags and its branches, stones and the like — five are combined; and also medicine, oil, and fragrance are poured into silk and so forth, and the fumigation is applied through both openings. If one applies the profound mantra and cultivates it, one should first come to know the signs: the various glorious signs of attachment arise, and at the latest they arise in three or seven instances. Having come to know the particular intent, this is the distinction between mantra and medicine. If one does not understand the intent and seeks medicine and the like out of mere desire, the demerit of this is inconceivable.

If one comes to understand the method of fumigation, the opening of the central channel is declared to be the distinctive method of application. The reversed application of great bliss, the reversed application of the cakras, the reversed application of the body, the reversed application of gnosis, the reversed application of the elements, the reversed application of the mother, the reversed application of the bindu (Skt. bindu), the reversed application of meditative concentration, the reversed application of syllables, the reversed application of emptiness, the reversed application of breath, the reversed application of joy, the reversed application of caṇḍālī, the reversed application of the channels, the reversed application of bodhicitta, the reversed application of left and right, the reversed application of signs, and the reversed application of the support — these were not beheld even by the Omniscient One; they are the secret of the minds of all buddhas, the mother of all bodhisattvas, the uncommon generation of all their sons. This is the distinction of meditative absorption.

Without measuring the distinction of this, there is no profound meaning. It is also taught that the pith instructions are given prior to conferring empowerment; through those pith instructions, empowerment will be obtained. Because one relies upon the wisdom-mother (prajñā) and generates the gnosis of bliss, it is taught as the empowerment of wisdom and gnosis. Sindhura is wisdom and camphor is gnosis; the bliss of that is gnosis. Sindhura is called wisdom, and camphor is explained as gnosis. The union of the three channels is wisdom, and its bindu is gnosis. For some, nālinī is gnosis and rasanā is wisdom. The channel is called wisdom and the awakening mind is gnosis. The ḍākinī is wisdom and the hero (dāka) is explained as gnosis. The three joys are gnosis and innate joy is wisdom. Below is called wisdom and above is also explained as gnosis. The left is called wisdom and the right is also explained as gnosis. The dharmadhātu is called wisdom and the four gnoses are gnosis. Mudrā-signs, names, and syllables — still further distinctions are to be known.

Regarding wisdom and gnosis, there are also three: training in words is permitted; regarding reflections, it is stated; and cultivation through direct realization is taught.

The four aspects of the syllables E and Vaṃ, and of Māyā: E is earth, Vaṃ is water, Ma is fire, and Ya is wind.

Translator's notes

The title inscription at the opening marks the colophon of a preceding tantra and the incipit of the present one. The text moves from colophon to title page to the body of the Śrī Kāya Vāk Citta Tantra Rāja without a clear structural break in the Tibetan; this translation renders them in sequence as they appear.

The term "khaṃvīra mantra" is a phonetic rendering of a Sanskrit mantra seed-syllable compound found in tantric ritual texts; it is left transliterated as no standard English rendering exists and the Tibetan gives only the phonetic form.

"Samayatārā" and "White-Robed Pāṇḍaravāsinī" are two distinct tantric goddess figures named in the list of buddha-consorts; Pāṇḍaravāsinī is rendered by her Sanskrit name as it is a proper epithet meaning "White-Robed One." The passage is a Vajrayāna ritual text dealing with tantric empowerment procedures. Several technical points deserve mention.

The opening line "One should drink alcohol regularly and not become angry" renders a formulation common in tantric samaya lists where alcohol is a ritual substance, not a license for indulgence. The eight samaya items enumerated across the preceding context are a standard pledge list.

The syllables JAḤ and BHYAḤ are tantric seed syllables left in transliterated form as they appear in the source.

"Romatī" is the transliteration of the Tibetan ro ma, one of the subtle body channels, here in the form of a proper name for the channel wheel at that location.

"Camphor from Siṃhala" refers to camphor from Sri Lanka, renowned as the finest quality in classical Indian sources.

The final incomplete sentence — "conferring it by means of the fierce wrathful one, the broad one" — corresponds to the text's evident continuation; the passage ends mid-enumeration of the three divisions of the secret empowerment. The term Kuṇḍalamā rendered from the Tibetan ཀུན་འདར་མ is a technical tantric designation for the consort or for a specific nāḍī; the reading is uncertain and some editions may differ. It is treated here as a proper name or epithet of the mind-family.

The phrase rendered "seven of speech" and "seven of the mind" with "six of the body" reflects a numerological list whose full referents are not spelled out in this passage; the structure mirrors three levels of illusory examples that follow immediately.

The phrase ཀ་ར་གཉིས is read as "two kara," where kara likely refers to a measure or type of sugar product; the exact referent is uncertain in context. The passage presents a dense technical exposition of Yoginītantra or Anuttarayogatantra practice instructions, combining subtle-body physiology with symbolic correspondences. Several terms here are technical transliterations rather than translatable Tibetan words: kundā, sindhura, nālinī, rasanā, spru-ba, lca-ba, ra-mnye-ba, nyeu-shing, and ra-mo-shags are all specific medicinal or ritual substances whose exact botanical or material identities are uncertain from the text alone and have been left as transliterations accordingly.

The phrase rendered "reversed application" translates go-bzlog sbyor-ba, a technical term for a method of practice involving inversion or reversal of the normal flow or order; the precise meaning varies across traditions and is left somewhat open in translation.
