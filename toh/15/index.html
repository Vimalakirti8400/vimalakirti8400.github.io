About this text

Toh 15 in the Kangyur is the Noble Perfection of Wisdom in Five Hundred Lines, known in Sanskrit as the Āryapañcaśatikāprajñāpāramitā and in Tibetan as the Phags pa shes rab kyi pha rol tu phyin pa lnga brgya pa. It belongs to the Perfection of Wisdom class of sūtras, a body of canonical Buddhist scripture that explores the nature of emptiness and the transcendent wisdom cultivated on the bodhisattva path. Like others in this genre, it takes the form of a discourse delivered by the Buddha — here on Vulture Peak Mountain at Rājagṛha — in which he instructs the Venerable Subhūti on the insubstantial nature of the aggregates, sense bases, and all conditioned phenomena. As one of the shorter Prajñāpāramitā sūtras, it distills these profound themes concisely, sitting within the broader collection of Perfection of Wisdom texts that forms a central section of the Kangyur.

Homage to all buddhas and bodhisattvas.

Thus did I hear. At one time the Blessed One was dwelling at Rājagṛha, on Vulture Peak Mountain, together with a great community of monks and a great assembly of bodhisattvas.

Then the Blessed One declared to the Venerable Subhūti: "Subhūti, form is without substance, of base substance, and yet of some substance. Subhūti, feeling, perception, formations, and consciousness are without substance, of base substance, and yet of some substance. Subhūti, the eye, forms, and eye-consciousness; the ear, sounds, and ear-consciousness; the nose, odors, and nose-consciousness; the tongue, tastes, and tongue-consciousness; the body, tactile objects, and body-consciousness; the mind, phenomena, and mind-consciousness — all of these are without substance, of base substance, and yet of some substance.

"In this way, because ordinary childish persons do not fully know form as it truly and completely is — as set out in these three chapters — they cling to that form, they affirm it, and they obscure themselves with it. Because they cling to form, affirm it, and obscure themselves with it, they will not attain definite emergence even by way of the vehicle of the śrāvakas or the vehicle of the pratyekabuddhas — let alone by way of the Great Vehicle.
