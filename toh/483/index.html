About this text

Toh 483 in the Kangyur is a tantric text known in Sanskrit as the Sarva-durgati-pariśodhana-tejorāja-tathāgatārhat-samyaksambuddhasya-kalpa and in Tibetan as the Dé zhin shekpa dra chompa yangdakpar dzokpé sangyé ngenso tamché yongsu jongwa zibrijé kyi... — rendered in English as "The Tantra Called the Examination of the Tathāgata, Arhat, Perfectly Awakened Buddha, King of Radiant Splendor, Purifier of All Lower Destinies." It is a ritual tantra concerned with the purification of beings from the lower realms of rebirth, drawing on the power and radiance of a specific transcendent buddha figure to cleanse negative karma and free sentient beings from unfortunate destinies. The text opens in the manner of a sūtra, with the Buddha residing in a celestial grove of great magnificence surrounded by divine assemblies, suggesting a blending of sūtra-style framing with tantric content and practice. As Toh 483 in the Kangyur, it belongs to the tantric section of the canon and is associated with the broader Sarva-durgati-pariśodhana cycle, a well-known corpus of kriyā or caryā tantra literature devoted to funerary rites, purification, and the liberation of beings from lower existences.

Translation

In the Indian language: Sarva-durgati-pariśodhana-tejoräja-tathāgatārhat-samyaksambuddhasya-kalpa-nāma.

In the Tibetan language: The Tantra Called the Examination of the Tathāgata, Arhat, Perfectly Awakened Buddha, King of Radiant Splendor, Purifier of All Lower Destinies. The First Fascicle.

Homage to glorious Vajrasattva.

Thus did I hear. At one time, the Blessed One dwelt in the supremely delightful grove of all the assemblies of gods, a place adorned with jewels, gold, golden trees with their branches and creepers, sala trees, medicinal plants, and flowers — lotus blossoms, blue water-lilies, karṇikā, bakkula, tilaka, aśoka, māndārava, and great māndārava, and many other varied blossoms — and with wish-fulfilling trees (kalpataru); adorned with silk banners of many kinds; resounding with the sweet calls of many varied birds; filled with the sounds of drums, small drums, cymbals, flutes, conch shells, and many other instruments; where Śakra (Indra, lord of the gods), Brahmā, and other gods and divine maidens of every kind took their delight; and where all the buddhas and bodhisattvas had bestowed their blessing.

There, in the midst of that great assembly, the Great Brahmā was seated on a lotus throne. He entered into the meditative absorption called the Purification of All Streams of Lower Destinies. Then, from the hair-tuft (ūrṇā) at the brow of that very same bodhisattva's meditative absorption called Liberation from the Streams of the Three Lower Destinies, there streamed forth a great garland of many rays of light, expanding and gathering. Those rays illuminated all the world-realms of this great trichiliocosm without exception.

Having illuminated them, the minds of all sentient beings were freed from the bonds of mental affliction, and each attained their own happiness. Having illuminated all the delightful groves, he made manifest veneration through clouds of varied offerings. Having circumambulated a hundredfold a thousandfold, and prostrated with his head a hundredfold a thousandfold, he placed an immaculate jewel before the Blessed One and spoke these words:

How wonderful, O Buddha! O Buddha!
How wonderful the deeds of the Buddha!
For you have purified us from the lower destinies
and established us in the conduct of awakening.

Then Śakra, lord of the gods, circumambulated the Blessed One a hundredfold a thousandfold, prostrated, and addressed the Blessed One with these words:

Blessed One, by virtue of what cause did the rays of the Buddha's light, by illuminating everything, bring liberation from the streams of the lower destinies...

and established on the path of complete liberation — how wonderful, O Blessed One! How wonderful, O Sugata!

The Blessed One declared: "Lord of the gods, this is not so very wonderful. Lord of the gods, this is not so very wonderful — for the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, have perfectly gathered accumulations of merit beyond measure. Lord of the gods, the perfectly and completely awakened buddhas have become the source of precious qualities beyond measure. Lord of the gods, the perfectly and completely awakened buddhas have perfectly and completely mastered skillful means beyond measure. Lord of the gods, the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, have accumulated wisdom beyond measure. The conduct of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, is beyond measure. The buddhas, the Blessed Ones, have made beings beyond measure into fit vessels. The buddhas, the Blessed Ones, are endowed with wisdom unequalled and equal to the unequalled. The buddhas, the Blessed Ones, are endowed with miraculous powers unequalled and equal to the unequalled. The buddhas, the Blessed Ones, are endowed with the power of aspiration unequalled and equal to the unequalled. Therefore, lord of the gods, know that the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, work for the benefit of sentient beings according to their capacity as vessels, work for the benefit of sentient beings according to how they are to be trained, and work for the benefit of sentient beings according to their inclinations. Entertain no doubt, no wavering, no uncertainty about this. There is no situation in which the Tathāgatas cannot tame."

Then Śakra, lord of the gods, rose from his seat, made a grand and extensive offering to the Blessed One, prostrated, and addressed the Blessed One with these words: "May I, Blessed One, please grant me the courage to work solely for the benefit of all sentient beings, to feel tenderness toward all sentient beings, to be one who is tender-hearted, to be one who possesses great tenderness, and to bring all intentions to complete fulfilment. O Blessed One, please grant me this courage. O Sugata, please grant me this courage. Blessed One, from this very heaven of the Thirty-Three gods, a divine youth called Jewel of Stainless Light has died and passed on. He has exceeded his time and is now seven years old. Blessed One, where has he been reborn? What pleasures and sufferings will he experience? Blessed One, please give a prophecy. O Sugata, please give a prophecy."

The Blessed One declared: "Lord of the gods, if you know that the right moment has come, then listen." The lord of the gods said: "Blessed One, now is the moment. O Sugata, now is the time and the occasion."

The Blessed One declared: "Lord of the gods, that divine youth called Jewel of Stainless Light has died and passed from here and has been born in the great Avīci hell. Having experienced terrible and unbearable suffering there for twelve thousand years, he will then experience suffering in the lesser hells for ten thousand years. After that he will experience suffering in the realms of animals and hungry ghosts for ten thousand years. He will then be born as a human being in a remote border region, dull-witted, deaf, and mute by nature, with unclear speech, and will experience suffering for sixty thousand years. For eighty-four thousand years he will be tormented by festering wounds oozing pus and blood, by leprosy and eye disease, scorned by the many, abandoned by all,

being born into a lowly lineage, experiencing suffering and transmitting it to others, harming others, creating all manner of karmic obscurations without respite, and beyond that, experiencing suffering upon suffering in an unbroken chain, again and again. Upon hearing this, Śakra (Indra, lord of the gods) and all the other sons of the gods were filled with profound distress and terror, and overcome with sorrow, they fell face-down upon the ground. Then they rose once more and spoke these words: "Blessed One, how will this being be liberated from this unbroken transmission of great suffering? O Sugata, how shall liberation be found? Blessed One, we beseech you to protect this being so that it may be completely freed from this mass of suffering. O Sugata, we beseech you to protect this being."

The Blessed One declared: "Lord of the gods, this teaching has been expounded by eighty-four million buddhas, and I too shall expound it — listen well."

Then Śakra, lord of the gods, again made abundant offerings to the Blessed One with māndārava flowers and great māndārava flowers, with flowers of many kinds, with jeweled crowns, with bracelets, armlets, earrings, necklaces, anklets, and all manner of splendid ornaments, circumambulating him hundreds and thousands of times and prostrating, and he pleased the Blessed One by saying: "Excellent, Blessed One! Excellent, Sugata!" Then he made this request: "Blessed One, for the benefit and happiness of the world with its gods, and so that sentient beings in future times may be fully liberated from the three lower realms, please teach this well. O Sugata, please teach this well."

Then Brahmā and the many other gods likewise spoke with one voice: "Blessed One, excellent! Sugata, excellent! So that sentient beings in future times, by merely hearing the name of this teaching, may be freed from the path of the three lower realms, be born in the fortunate realms of gods or of humans, and attain unsurpassed, perfect, and complete awakening — please teach this."

Then the Blessed One, in order to bless Śakra, Brahmā, and all those sons of the gods by means of the essence of all Tathāgatas, entered into meditative absorption called "The Indestructible Blessing of the Vajra, Certain and Unfailing." Then from within his very heart he drew forth this mantra and blessed those gods with the samaya:

Om sarva vidyā vajra adhiṣṭhāna jñāna samaya hūṃ.

Having thus entered into meditative absorption, and having blessed them through the indestructible vajra blessing that is certain and unfailing, he then drew forth from within his own heart this essence of all Tathāgatas known as the King Who Thoroughly Purifies All Lower Realms:

Om śodhani śodhani sarva pāpaṃ viśodhane, śuddhe viśuddhe, sarva karma āvaraṇa viśuddhe svāhā.

The very moment this great vidyā was proclaimed, all the bonds of the lower-realm continuum for all sentient beings were completely severed. All lower rebirths were thoroughly purified. Unbearable sufferings were utterly pacified, and the multitude of beings came to be endowed with happiness.

Then he further proclaimed this secret essence:

Om śodhane śodhane, om sarva apāya na, sarva sattvebhyo hūṃ.

"Lord of the gods, this too is the essence of all Tathāgatas:

Om sarva apāya viśodhane hūṃ phaṭ.

"Lord of the gods, this too is the essence of all Tathāgatas and the near-essence:

Om traṭ.

"Lord of the gods, this too is the essence that thoroughly purifies all lower realms:

Hūṃ."

"Lord of the gods, moreover, in brief, this is what even the mere recollection of which easily liberates sentient beings of little merit from every thread of lower rebirth:

Namo bhagavate sarva durgati pariśodhana rājāya, tathāgatāya, arhate samyaksaṃbuddhāya. Tadyathā: Oṃ śodhane śodhane, sarva pāpaṃ viśodhane, śuddhe viśuddhe, sarva karma āvaraṇa viśuddhe svāhā.

"This is the root essence.

Oṃ sarvavid sarva āvaraṇa viśodhaya hana hūṃ phaṭ. Oṃ sarvavid hūṃ. Oṃ sarvavid phaṭ. Oṃ sarvavid a. Oṃ sarvavid trāṭ. Oṃ sarvavid oṃ. Oṃ sarvavid sva. Oṃ sarvavid a. Oṃ sarvavid hūṃ. Oṃ sarvavid treṭ. Oṃ śa. Oṃ bha.

"Lord of the gods, whoever — any son or daughter of good family — merely hears the name of this secret essence of the Resplendent King, the Tathāgata who thoroughly purifies all lower realms, or holds it in mind, or recites it, or writes it and then wears it upon their head, at the crown, upon their shoulder, or about their neck — for that person, in this very life, neither the eight untimely deaths nor dream-visions connected with the eight untimely deaths, nor any omen associated with lower rebirths, shall arise even in a dream. As for one who properly enters the maṇḍala, receives empowerment, recites the essence, and meditates on the meaning of the mantra — what need is there even to speak of that? No wrongdoing whatsoever shall come near to them, and they shall not go to the lower realms.

"Whether it be a man, a woman, a god, a nāga, a yakṣa, a rakṣasa, an animal, a hungry ghost, a hell-being, or any other being whatsoever — if the body of one who has died is placed within the maṇḍala and empowerment is conferred, then even if that being has been reborn in a hell realm, they shall be immediately liberated and reborn among the race of gods. Having been born in that divine lineage, they shall directly realize the nature of reality of all Tathāgatas, render service and homage to all Tathāgatas, and become irreversible. Their stream of being shall become definite. They shall be born in the lineage of the Tathāgatas. Having thoroughly abandoned all obscurations, they shall come to experience happiness in the lineage of all Tathāgatas, in the lineage of the gods, or in whatever other birth is fitting.

"Lord of the gods, in brief, they shall come to experience all benefit and happiness, both worldly and supramundane."

Then Śakra, the Lord of the Gods (Indra, king of the gods), circumambulated the Blessed One, prostrated before him, and spoke thus: "Blessed One, please speak for the benefit and happiness of those who have fallen under the power of all lower realms, so that those sentient beings may turn their backs upon every lower realm and easily realize unsurpassable, perfectly complete awakening. Lord, please speak. Please, speak."

Then the Blessed One, Śākyamuni, entered into the meditative absorption called the Vajra of Wisdom that Thoroughly Purifies All Lower Realms of All Tathāgatas, and proclaimed this great maṇḍala called the Resplendent King, the Thoroughly Purifying of All Lower Realms of All Tathāgatas:

The awakening mind of all buddhas,
the realization of the unsurpassable —
whatever wisdom the Victor has proclaimed,
that, it is said, is the very heart of the maṇḍala.

Then again, the Blessed One, out of compassion for all sentient beings, proclaimed this outer maṇḍala:

Temples and pleasure groves,
stūpas, divine halls, and the like —
through the correct ritual procedure,
begin by consecrating the site with blessing.

In that site thus blessed,
draw the outer maṇḍala.
It is square with four gates,
adorned with four archways,
a garland of four stairways,
and decorated with lions, elephants, and bulls.
Adorned with silks, bells, pendant streamers and garlands,
with small bells and tassels.
Adorned with vajras, jewels, and lotuses,
and with the mudrā of crossed vajras.
Adorned with eight cords
and the outer door-frames.
Of the nine divisions,
gates and door-frames shall be three divisions each,
and the lines shall be drawn with vajra cords.

Draw the central maṇḍala's lines
to resemble the wheel of Dharma,
with a hub and sixteen spokes,
and of three tiers —
the spokes shall be doubled.

In the central direction, draw Sarvavid (the All-Knowing One),
white as a conch and like the moon of Kunda flowers,
four-faced, seated upon a lion throne of vajra,
bearing the mudrā of meditative absorption,
adorned with all ornaments.

Before him, draw the King who purifies all lower realms,
his two hands folded in the manner of meditative absorption,
of the color of the full moon and of a lotus,
perfectly complete with all ornaments,
the space filled with supreme royal beings.

Behind him, draw the Lord of the Śākya clan,
the chief Sage, Śākyamuni.

In all the corner directions to the left and right,
draw the two supreme royal jeweled flowers:
Locana and Māmakī,
and likewise White Tārā,
each bearing the insignia of their respective family.

Within all the spokes, throughout,
draw in proper sequence
the foremost bodhisattvas such as Vajrasattva and others,
and the bodhisattvas of great miraculous power.

The skilled artist shall draw the offering goddesses
Lāsyā and the rest in the corner directions.
In all the directions of the gates, draw the four gate-keepers.
In the porticoes of all the archways, draw Maitreya and the rest.

All the victorious śrāvakas,
and likewise the foremost solitary buddhas,
and the wrathful ones, messengers, and envoys —
draw these, or write their names.

Outside the outer maṇḍala, draw the four continents.
In Jambudvīpa, draw Brahmā.
In the northern direction, draw the Great Lord (Śiva).
In the eastern and western continents,
draw Śakra (Indra) with all his desires,
together with his retinue.

All the others likewise:
the asuras, gandharvas, and garuḍas,
yakṣas, rākṣasas, and great serpents,
elemental spirits, hungry ghosts, and demons,
nāgas, kumbhāṇḍas, and mountain spirits,
and the four great kings — draw them all.

The planets, stars, and comets,
the wrathful, inauspicious, and harmful
obstructors (vighnāntakas) and vināyakas,
the great sages of good fortune who bring benefit to all sentient beings,
the pramathas and women —
draw them, and likewise the sisters who are yoginīs,
and the great gods of the four elements,
those dwelling in houses and on mountains,
in forests, pleasure groves, and the like,
those dwelling in charnel grounds and in cities —
write their names and symbols,
or draw their spheres.

Then the skilled artist shall place upon the maṇḍala:
fluttering victory banners,
parasols, fly-whisks with five tails,
offering pendants, and various food offerings,
golden birds — eight or sixteen or more —
and five vases filled with the precious water of empowerment.

Lamps, torma offerings, vases,
and beautiful food offerings shall be placed there;
food and drink well prepared,
and various furnishings set in place.
Various torma offerings of many kinds shall be set out
as food for the yakṣas and rākṣasas.
Likewise for elemental spirits, nāgas,
humans, and non-humans:
various flowers and fragrances,
milk, butter, and molasses,
and a victorious vase fully filled
with perfumed water shall be placed there.

Then, having bathed and performed the preparatory rites, the vajra master, adorned with fragrance and a garland of the finest flowers, his mouth fragrant with perfumed breath, and beautifully adorned, enters and sits facing the door holding flowers in hand.

The chief retinue — the many assemblies of the Victors and their bodhisattva heirs — are then invited. Endowed with mudrā and mantra, the yoga of the hook (ankuśa) and the rest is performed. Reciting "Jaḥ Hūṃ Baṃ Hoḥ — Lord of Compassion, come forth!" the secret binding is applied: with the two index fingers shaped into a hook mudrā, then joined as a lasso, then formed into the manner of a chain, and with the two thumbs bent inward — when moved, they come under one's power.

Then, when the signs arise and satisfaction is expressed, the beginningless and endless is directly perceived. One makes excellent offerings with the finest arghya water, with secret and further offerings, with melodious praise in song, and with the supreme and sublime ritual substances of the gods.

Then the torma (ritual offering) for all elemental spirits shall be made. Examine attentively those who dwell in the directions, those tormented by hunger and thirst. The torma for them shall be made in the appropriate manner.

Then the mantra practitioner shall ripen the disciples fully in the ground of unsurpassed awakening. In order to bring benefit to beings and to swiftly reach awakening, the disciples shall be led to generate the supreme awakening mind (bodhicitta). They shall be blessed, and aspirational prayers for awakening shall be established for them. The profound and vast Dharma shall be proclaimed. Having been protected and dwelling in the vow of ascetic discipline, they shall be led to recite mantra from the lips of the supremely peaceful one.

With silk and other cloth the two eyes are covered. The two hands are placed in the lotus gesture of the glorious vajra holder. Adorned with flowers, jewels, and the like, with the hands filled with honorarium, they shall be led in.

The mantras are these:

Oṃ vajra adhitiṣṭha hūṃ. Oṃ vajra samaya hūṃ. Oṃ vajra praveśa hūṃ. A same tri same mahā samaye hūṃ.

Then, with these mantras, flowers and the like shall be cast.

In order to attain the siddhis of buddhahood,
may the great chief who brings benefit to beings
think of me with loving care
and accept these with the thought of healing.

Oṃ vajra pratigṛhṇa hūṃ. Oṃ vajra pratīccha vajra samaya stvaṃ.

Whatever flowers, jewels, and the like fall —
the deity of the family upon whom they land —
if one accomplishes the principal of that family,
siddhi will come, without any doubt.

Then the skilled mantra practitioner shall bestow the sacred commitment (samaya) with this mantra:

Oṃ vajra samaya hūṃ phaṭ.

Then the mudrā of the family is bound at the heart, and the mantra for establishing the deity of the sacred commitment within the heart is:

Oṃ vajra samaya hṛdayaṃ me praveśa dṛḍha tiṣṭha hūṃ.

Then the sacred commitment shall be proclaimed with these words:

This is your water of hell —
if you transgress the sacred commitment, it burns.
If you uphold the sacred commitment,
siddhi is accomplished through this water of vajra ambrosia.
But if you transgress the sacred commitment,
you are immediately summoned and depart.
The victorious vajra holder, enraged,
destroys seven generations of your lineage.

Therefore, child, you must always
guard with utmost devotion
the sacred commitments proclaimed by the Victors,
the vows, and the words of the guru.

You shall not kill living beings.
You shall not take what is not given.
Lying and engaging in sensual misconduct —
these must not be done by one who desires siddhi.
You shall not drink alcohol,
nor eat meat and the like.
Acts that harm sentient beings
must never be undertaken.
You shall never entirely abandon the Three Jewels.
At all times: the awakening mind,
the essence mantra, mudrā,
the deity and the guru — likewise.
Whatever sins and the like there may be,
you shall not transgress the guru's word.
Offerings made to the deity — do not step on their remnants
nor tread on their shadow.
The same applies to images bearing mudrā —
do not revile mantra or deity.
Do not engage in harmful spirit-working rites and the like.
Do not revile non-Buddhist adherents (tīrthikas).

In brief, do not be of two minds, doubting and uncertain.

One's own suchness, and mantra and the rest — it is likewise the same. Having taken the vow with firm and unwavering devotion, Sarvavid (the All-Knowing) bestows empowerment (abhiṣeka) exactly as prescribed. Using as many vases and the like as there are available, the ten masters each confer whichever of the ten empowerments they wish. Thereupon, a bell and a vajra are placed in the hand, along with the wheel and the rest of the seven precious articles. This is the empowerment conferring the sovereignty of a lord of men, and it is to be given for the destruction of misdeeds. To a devoted disciple for the accomplishment of mantra, the mantra master is to give full instruction in the word. That disciple too, with devotion, pays homage with the crown of the head, and if they have wealth, they are to present it to the guru. Wealth, treasure, grain, goods, and gold; mounts, home, bedding, sons and daughters; men and women too; villages and towns — likewise whatever is desired — with their own mind filled with trust and faith, they are to offer it as the supreme gift, truly offering it. For swift accomplishment, with devotion to the guru, in brief, one's own body too is to be offered.

All the happiness of this life without remainder,
the supreme happiness of future worlds,
and even buddhahood itself shall be attained —
what need then to speak of the happiness of the gods?

Equal to all buddhas is the vajra master (vajrācārya).
If one holds him in contempt,
one will perpetually encounter suffering —
therefore one must not show even the slightest disrespect.

Toward vajra brothers and sisters,
and toward the vajra consorts themselves,
a yogin must not be contemptuous,
and must never harbor ill will.

Toward those who harm the Three Jewels,
who revile the guru and are hostile,
who have transgressed their sacred vow,
and those who commit similar wrongs —
one is not to cultivate patience.
If one acts accordingly, Sarvavid
has declared that one will obtain attainments (siddhis),
and with compassion for all sentient beings
one will attain the loving-kindness attainment.

Thereupon the Blessed One proclaimed this sādhana rite for the benefit and welfare of Śakra (Indra), lord of the gods, together with the divine world, and for the sake of their benefit and happiness.

On cloth one is to paint the Blessed One Sarvavid exactly as described. To the right is to be the Tathāgata Sarvaduṛgatipariśodhana Tejaḥrāja — the King of Splendor, Purifier of All Lower Realms. To the left is Śākyamuni. Directly below the Purifier of All Lower Realms is Avalokiteśvara, sovereign of vision — moon-white in color, holding a lotus in his hand. Below Śākyamuni is Vajrapāṇi. Between those two is the Medicine Master — lapis blue in color, with one hand holding the fruit āmalaka and the other hand in the gesture of supreme giving. Hayagrīva, and Vijayī of the Three Worlds — the one who strives to subdue the hostile — these two are to be painted in attendance upon their respective deities. In the center between those two, Locanā, Māmakī, Pāṇḍarāvāsinī, and Tārā are each to be painted bearing their own attributes in their hands. Below them, a lotus pond is to be painted, complete with water, makaras, fish, tortoises, white frogs, carp and the like, and filled entirely with the many varieties of water-born flowers. Incense, lamps, fragrance, garlands, food offerings, flowers, and various kinds of fruit are to be painted. Below that, the practitioner is to be painted with palms joined, paying homage, seated.

Then this painted image is to be venerated with full honor. One is oneself to perform the eye-opening ceremony, and one is to visualize it as consecrated by truth.

Then, if one sees a sign, one will swiftly attain the attainment. But if one does not see one, the attainment will come after a long time.

If one hears laughter, the sound of drums,
the sound of bells, the sound of thunder,
and sees monks, brahmins, men and women,
and fruits — then swiftly
the supreme, middling, and lesser attainments will be accomplished.

Then that image, having been consecrated with the secret mantra and the mudrā, is to be worshipped with whatever one possesses. Even while remaining constantly in direct presence —

Having been victorious over the three worlds,
after performing self-protection and the like,
meditating on one's own suchness,
until the signs of attainment arise,
one recites three hundred thousand or six hundred thousand times.
Then, in a solitary place,
one is to complete a full eight hundred recitations.
The mantra is to be recited without error or confusion.
Then, at the close of that recitation,
one visualizes the maṇḍala as before,
performs extensive offerings,
and recites continuously, session after session, without break.
If one then beholds the principal deity, or that deity's offspring,
or any of the gods,
one should request from that vessel of merit
the supreme attainment, as one desires.
The deity, ever delighting in that being,
will bestow the fruit of the attainment.
One pays homage and receives the attainment and the rest.
One then offers a share to the Three Jewels and to one's teacher,
and thereafter uses the remainder oneself always.
If the attainment is not present, a wise person
should take it up and make use of it personally.
One will then remain for many eons,
striving to benefit all sentient beings.
But even if the supreme attainment is not obtained,
all activities will become fully efficacious.
Yakṣas, shooting stars, possessing spirits, and the like —
cut off by punishment and subdued by mere command —
all activities of pacification and the rest
will be accomplished as if by a servant.

Then Śakra (Indra), lord of the gods, addressed the Blessed One with these words: Blessed One, how may those sentient beings who, through having committed evil deeds, have come under the power of hell and the other lower states of existence, be delivered from the suffering of those states?

The Blessed One declared: O lord of the gods, listen well. Hear now how those sentient beings who commit evil deeds, who commit great evil deeds, and who thereby come under the power of hell and the other lower states, may be easily liberated from the sufferings of hell.

Just as a maṇḍala is drawn and then,
as before, one recites into a vase
one hundred and eight times,
one is to perform the empowerment most carefully.
Through this, all evil is thoroughly purified,
and one is swiftly liberated
from the suffering of hell and the other lower states.
That very being, freed from evil and purified,
is born into the family of the pure gods,
and thereafter will always hear
the Buddha and the Dharma proclaimed.

Whether one draws an image of that deity
or writes even the name in saffron —
in order to liberate sentient beings
from the suffering of the three lower realms,
a compassionate one skilled in mantra
bestows the empowerment as a benefit to others.
Then a yogin
confers the empowerment with mantra and mudrā.
Having been contemplated in the form of the deity,
it is to be placed at the center of a stūpa.
One writes the essence mantra of one's own deity
or of another deity upon the chest,
arouses the mind in likeness to that deity,
and places it within a home.
Having proclaimed that deity's name,
one writes the mantra in fine saffron.
Then gradually, until one hundred thousand recitations are complete,
one performs the rite from the stūpa.
In order to exhaust the evil of an evildoer,
one completes ten million recitations.
If this is done in this way, without fail
that being will be liberated from hell.
Freed from the suffering of hungry ghosts and animals,
one is born in a divine abode.
Calling upon that deity by name
and reciting the mantra as taught —
whether completing a thousand hundred-thousand recitations,
or a thousand hundred-thousands,
or reciting up to ten million times —
one will certainly be born in a divine abode.
Calling again upon that deity's name,
if a skilled practitioner performs the fire offering
for one hundred hundred-thousands
or for a thousand hundreds,
one is liberated from the evil
that had consigned one to the great hell.
For as long as one is born
in a place of blazing signs of existence —
until the true signs arise —

With sesame, white mustard, rice and other grains,
together with goat's milk,
and firewood anointed with fragrant substances,
one performs the fire offering according to the rite.

Thereafter, one will certainly be born again
in a divine abode,
and the signs of that will be revealed.
Or one may become supreme among the gods.

Or within the fire-offering pit
the fire will display auspicious signs,
and the signs of being born in a divine abode
and of evil being vanquished are these:
the flame burns white and to the right,
it burns upward, it is stainless,
it is round, it does not scatter,
it is steady, lightning-bright, and without impurity.

Or the fire deity himself
will manifest in that very form —
white as a stainless moon,
with a bright white complexion, blazing steadily.

By these signs, one should understand
that one has been liberated from hell and the like,
that evil has been overcome,
and that one has been born in a fortunate realm.

One digs a pit according to the rite,
measuring four cubits in depth,
and in its center one draws
a wheel encircled by a vajra border.
The mudrās of the five families
one draws in their respective directions.
Likewise one draws the bodhisattvas
and the Lords of the World and the rest.
Birds and full vases,
vessels filled with torma offerings —
these are arranged in a set of eight,
or alternatively in a set of sixteen.
Food offerings and ritual cakes,
garlands of flowers and the like in the same way,
banners of silk, victory standards and streamers,
and the finest parasols — all finely arranged.

In the sacred maṇḍala of the fire offering
one performs the fire offering supremely.
Having drawn it according to the rite and with knowledge of the ceremony,
one invites the many gods,
and knowing the mantras and the mudrās of the mantras,
one presents the arghya water offering,
then, in brief, having made the offerings,
one performs the consecration with the divine engagement.

With camphor, saffron and the like,
ghee and milk and sesame,
sesame and white mustard, rice and other grains,
one fills the vessel excellently with fragrant water.
Rice, hare, musk deer,
honey, meat, sugar —
one recites the mantra over these many hundreds of times,
and likewise over the firewood;
the wood consecrated with one's own mantra
is to be placed inside the pit.
The offering substance, having been consecrated with the mantra,
is then washed with the finest water
and anointed with the fragrance of the recitation.
Covered with cloth and ornaments,
one fumigates with incense consecrated by mantra,
and makes offerings with flower garlands and the like.
Upon the topknot and the two shoulders, likewise,
one places written mantras.
At the heart, the hidden place, and facing the mouth,
one consecrates with Sarvavid.
At the forehead, the ūrṇā hair, the two ears,
the crown of the head, the topknot, the two shoulders,
the nose, the waist, the knees,
the tip of the finger, the tip of the nose,
the two eyes, and the abode
of the faculties of the secret place —
upon each of these places one affixes the written mantra syllables.
This is the supreme arrangement of virtue.

Then, for the purification of the lower realms,
it is to be placed, together with its seat,
directly in the center of the hearth.

Then the mantra-holder covers the offering
with a cloth consecrated by mantra.
Next, one kindles the virtue of the bali offering —
the body blazing with a mass of a thousand tongues of flame,
the body peaceful as the white moon and the kunda flower,
with a form of limitless body, one draws in the fire,
and having contemplated the arghya offering accordingly,
one places form and other offerings before the supreme Wisdom.
Desiring the qualities as they have been taught,
one invites the assembly of Tathāgatas accordingly,
and presenting the arghya and the rest accordingly,
one performs the offerings and the rest as the Sugata has taught.

Then one should examine the bali offering.
Having been burned and filled, it is set ablaze again.
Then, having been burned and filled, in sequence
one examines it at a hundred and eight for the Victor and the rest.
Then, to the king of purifying mantras —

One should examine twenty-one things to be burned. Then, having invited the assembly and presented the ritual implements three times, one makes offerings to the white-faced Lotus — the arghya and the rest. Vajrapāṇi, bearing a lotus lasso, his form victorious over the three worlds, presses down upon wrong with the lotus of his feet, and all his ornaments are fully perfect, the crown of a fully awakened buddha rising high — one should visualize this, or alternatively draw it.

By the heart-mantra of that one, likewise, one should perform the fire offering one hundred thousand times, one thousand times, or indeed up to ten million times. If signs such as marks appear, this is called the continuum of the sinful one. Then, when it has been reduced to ash, one should gather it with the mantra of the vajra-gathering, according to the ritual procedure.

That ash and the dust of bone, infused water prepared with mantras, lotus water, milk, and the rest — five substances — the wise one should sprinkle these with one hundred thousand recitations of the purifying mantra. Then, having blended them with camphor fragrance and ointment, one may form them into a figure, or alternatively fashion them in the manner of a stūpa — one, two, three, or five, or up to one hundred and eight. Having sealed them with mantra and mudrā, one should recite up to two hundred thousand times.

Then, if the stūpa blazes, or the face of the image smiles, if fragrance, incense, or light appear, if radiance or emanation arises, if forms of gods and other beings appear in diverse aspects, if miraculous transformations and displays occur, if flowers and such are scattered, if the sounds of conch shells, drums, flutes, lutes, and the like are heard — if the sinful one does not see these signs in the mind, then, having made offerings to the Tathāgata, one should recite in meditative equipoise — eight hundred thousand, or however many recitations are needed until the signs arise. And at the end, the expert should recite without sleeping for even a single moment marked by a sign.

Then one will clearly see that person's negativity has been purified. One will see that the body has taken on a divine form, and one will come to recognize this within one's own continuum. Having known these signs, even with a non-conceptual mind, possessed of loving-kindness and compassion, one will perform all activities.

If, however, these are not present, then through the manner of reciting and meditating, one should write out even just the name clearly, and make a garland of stūpas, or alternatively draw an image. If one then performs a fire offering empowerment, one will without fail take rebirth in a higher realm. The ash, along with mustard seeds, sand, and such things, should have one's own name spoken and mantras applied to them; then, having performed recitations, one should go and cast them into a river flowing into the ocean.

Even one who is sinful, if by such means they are freed from the lower realms — then how much more for one who generates the highest virtue, who holds the seed of awakening, who possesses the causes for the fruit of buddhahood, who is endowed with the perfection of generosity, who is fully possessed of the continuum of moral discipline, who has cultivated the causes through patience, diligence, meditative equipoise, and wisdom — how much more is the merit of such a worldly being! Their lower realms will swiftly be destroyed — of this there is no doubt.

But for one who has not generated roots of virtue, who holds the view of their non-existence, who has turned away from the path of awakening, who acts with aversion toward the teaching and causes harm to it, who sees their own sinful nature, who has abandoned mother and father, who has relied upon the awakening mind and then killed those free from attachment — or if the gods are absent, and likewise the Buddha, the Saṅgha, the Dharma, the hosts of mantras and mudrās, wisdom, and skillful means are absent — then those sinful ones will not be freed. This is what the Sugata, the Victor himself, declared.

Then the gods, beginning with Indra (Śakra), opened their eyes like lotuses, expressed their delight with the word "Excellent!", and having made offerings with great gladness, Indra, exerting himself for the benefit of others —

In accordance with the declaration that was spoken, the Lord of the Śākyas declared it to be the very truth. Then, having been freed from the suffering of the hells, all sentient beings were born in the Joyful Heaven (Tuṣita) for the benefit of others, born in the likeness of the Sugata.

Then the greatly renowned gods, together with Indra, performed dances, and with boundless clouds of offerings they assembled the hosts of gods before them in order to make offering to the Tathāgata. In order to make offering in this very life, they also aroused the awakening mind. They filled the Grove of Joy with divine substances of every variety — flowers, incense, and ointments; parasols, victory banners and standards; ornaments of every beautiful kind; precious garments like a rainfall of jewels — and made it entirely full. Then, struck with great wonder, the gods too bowed in prostration, and they asked: "What is the origin of all this variety that has appeared, filling the charming Grove of Joy on every side?"

The Blessed One declared: "Friends, know this: this miraculous display of transformation is the son of the gods named Vimalaraśmi Ratna — Jewel of Stainless Radiance. That one who had fallen under the power of the uninterrupted hells (ānarṭhya, the most extreme) and yet has come under the power of a fortunate destiny in this very present experience — that is wondrous, O Blessed One! Wondrous is the Sugata!"

Then all the sons of the gods together with Indra spoke in unison these words:

There is no teacher comparable to the Buddha.
There is no virtue comparable to the Dharma.
There is no vessel comparable to the Saṅgha.
There is no guide comparable to mantra.

Then those sons of the gods addressed the Blessed One thus: "O Blessed One, what manner of being is this son of the gods born into the divine class of the Joyful Heaven through the blessing of the Blessed One? What is the nature of his miraculous power and might? We wish to behold this son of the gods called Jewel of Stainless Radiance."

Then the Blessed One emitted from both great toes of his feet a garland of light-rays named "Rouser of the Hearts of All Gods." That garland illuminated the entire body of the one called Jewel of Stainless Radiance, entered his heart and roused him, and then disappeared in that very place.

Thereupon that son of the gods was filled with supreme joy, and with the light-rays of his body he illuminated the entire face of the sky for the measure of a thousand leagues. That son of the gods, encircled by a retinue of many thousands, with them assembled before him, came by great divine miraculous power without being visible, and illuminated and made radiant the whole of the Grove of Joy. He outshone with his own radiance all the gods except the Blessed One and Indra.

Then that son of the gods prostrated at the feet of the Blessed One and uttered this solemn declaration:

How wondrous is the buddha-activity
of the Protector of the Śākyas!
For thereby sentient beings fallen
into the lower realms are swiftly freed,
like a flash of lightning.

Then at that very instant there appeared before the Blessed One: drums the size of a chariot-wheel, all fashioned of precious substances, sending forth a cloud of fragrance a thousand leagues in extent; melodious sounds of divine song; wondrous displays of many marvels; and charming sounds of divine music — song, dance, percussion, flutes, drums, and cymbals. Seeing this, the assembly was seized with great wonder and spoke these words:

The qualities of the Tathāgata are inconceivable.
The many qualities of mantra are inconceivable.
Those who are the support of awakening are also inconceivable.
The pure discipline of the holy life arises beyond thought.

Then those divine sons, wishing to see more, again addressed the Blessed One: "We wish to see that divine son and the maṇḍalas of his retinue. We wish to see the Blessed One. We wish to see the Sugata."

Then the Blessed One sent forth rays of light from both his thumbs, and those immaculate rays of light illumined that divine son and all his divine retinue, stirring them into wakefulness, and then became invisible in that very place.

After that, the divine sons of the pure abodes — beginning with the one known as Vimalaprabharatna, "Immaculate Jewel-Light" — set all the groves of joy in motion, with the exception of the seats of the Blessed One and of Śakra (Indra, lord of the gods), circumambulating many hundreds of thousands of times. Having circumambulated, they again and again spoke these words:

How wondrous! How great is the blessing of mantra
of all the buddhas!
For a sentient being who had committed a crime
of immediate retribution
was swiftly reborn in a divine realm.

Then those divine sons — beginning with the divine son known as Vimalaprabharatna — offered to the Blessed One jewels, gold, silver, necklaces, pearl garlands, bracelets, armlets, earrings, and many diverse and excellent adornments of various kinds; parasols, victory banners, pennants, tinkling bells, yak-tail fans, and many diverse and excellent implements of various kinds; lion thrones, bedding, palaces, immeasurable mansions, multi-storied pavilions, and many diverse and excellent dwellings of various kinds; flowers, incense, and many diverse and excellent offerings of various kinds. They also offered their own bodies, speech, and minds. And they offered many diverse and excellent mounts — cattle, horses, lions, tigers, monkeys, leopards, bears, buffalo, donkeys, deer, makaras (mythical water-beasts), pigs, goats, oxen, cows, and various desirable riding animals of many kinds. They then reverently honored the maṇḍalas of the retinue of the Blessed One, and reverently honored the maṇḍalas of the retinue of Śakra, lord of the gods. Saying "Excellent!" they honored the Blessed One and Śakra as their supreme teachers and objects of veneration, prostrated, and departed accordingly.

Then Śakra, Brahmā, and the other gods were utterly seized with wonder, and again and again praised and honored the Blessed One with the words "Excellent!", prostrated, and spoke thus: "Blessed One, for the sake of inspiring the ordinary persons of the future to hold and recite it, please teach the virtues of the recitation of the names of the Tathāgata."

"Divine sons of Śakra, Brahmā, and their company, I shall explain. Listen well and hold it in mind. Divine sons, these teachings will reach the ears of sentient beings who are endowed with the ten virtues, but not those of base sentient beings. They will reach the ears of sentient beings who are devoted to the ten perfections, but not those of common, base sentient beings. They will reach the ears of sentient beings whose aspirations are certain, and of sentient beings who ardently desire the many qualities of non-regression. In brief, they will reach the ears of sentient beings who rely upon omniscient wisdom."

Then Śakra, Brahmā, and the other divine sons gave rise to genuine joy and spoke thus: "Blessed One, we have become vessels for this. Sugata, we have become vessels for this."

"It is for this reason that we have heard, from the very lips of the Blessed One with the lotus-face, the recitation of the name of the Tathāgata, arhat, perfectly and fully awakened buddha called the Glorious King Who Completely Purifies All Miserable Destinies — and having heard it, we shall not be afraid of miserable destinies, we shall not be afraid of untimely death, we shall not be afraid of death, of downfalls, of dispersion, of destruction, and the like. Blessed One, we have without any doubt become vessels destined to attain buddhahood itself."

"Blessed One, those noble beings who perform this king of examinations for the benefit of sentient beings — what will their fruit be? What will their karmic maturation be?"

The Blessed One declared: "Śakra, they should be regarded not merely as noble beings but as perfectly and fully awakened buddhas themselves. Śakra, it is because they utterly remove the various sufferings of sentient beings born in the great hells of uninterrupted torment and the like, who have passed to the other world; because they firmly establish them in an unwavering course toward awakening without any doubt; and because they place them on the bodhisattva grounds from which there is no regression — just as you, Śakra, once performed the benefit of the divine son called Stainless Light of the Jewel in the great hell of uninterrupted torment. Śakra, in the same way, do you think those sentient beings who perform the benefit of future sentient beings are ordinary persons?"

They replied: "Blessed One, no. Sugata, no."

"Śakra, those sentient beings are great noble beings, benefactors of others — worthy of veneration, offerings, respect, honor, and salutation even by the Tathāgatas. What need is there even to speak of the world with its gods?

Then Śakra, Brahmā, and the other divine sons, the Four Great Kings, the ten direction-guardians, twenty-eight yakṣa commanders, the great yakṣa Precious Excellence and great Precious Excellence and others who are fully established on the bodhisattva grounds, the nāga kings Nandā and Upanandā with their retinues, together with Harītī surrounded by her five hundred children, the great yakṣa commander Pañcika and great Pañcika and others, and all the other vast and mighty gods, nāgas, yakṣas, rākṣasas, asuras, garuḍas, gandharvas, kinnaras, great serpents, bhūtas, pretas, piśācas, epidemic-spirits, malevolent spirits, poisons, stars, and all the rest — all of them spoke with one voice:

"Blessed One, our entire assembled gathering will take up the two feet of that great noble being upon our heads. In brief, always and continuously, at all times, we will protect, guard, and shelter him."

"Blessed One, wherever a son or daughter of good family receives this king of examinations with faith, or reads it, or extensively and correctly explains it to others, or makes offerings with parasols, victory banners, pennants, and the like, or wherever anyone — be they of the warrior caste, the brahmin caste, the merchant caste, or the common caste, or again a king, a prince, a princess, a queen, a male member of the royal retinue, a female member of the royal retinue, or anyone else — holds and reads it,

and extensively and correctly explains it to others — Blessed One, all of us assembled together here become servants of that person. We will thoroughly protect the people of his own household, the people of his kinsmen, and the people of his community, as well as his villages, towns, cities, districts, and the precincts of his royal palace. In due season we will cause rain to fall. We will cause grain and the like to grow. We will work for the welfare and happiness of the many. We will crush the many plots of foreign adversaries. Whatever malevolent sentient beings, harmful and violent, conceive thoughts inimical to that person — we will work to lay them to ruin. We will act against them. We will do to them the work of māras. We will draw near to those who cause harm. In brief, Blessed One, this king of examinations will stand for future sentient beings as the very activity of the buddhas. Therefore, Blessed One, we will protect it as we would a child."

"Śakra (Indra, lord of the gods), what you have said is so. This king of examinations should indeed be viewed in just that way. Excellent, Śakra! Strive long and diligently for the welfare and happiness of those sentient beings dwelling in Jambudvīpa who practice evil Dharmas constantly, who have come under the power of the hells, who have sunk into the foul pit of saṃsāra."

This concludes the first examination from the king of examinations known as the Resplendent King That Thoroughly Purifies All Lower Realms.

The Second Fascicle.

Thereupon, by the power of the Blessed One, the Blessed One's bodhisattva, the great being Vajrapāṇi, set forth this subsequent examination — this king of examinations.

The hero rose from his seat,
rejoiced, and brandished the vajra,
gladdening the lord of the Śākyas.
Then, having paid homage to the chief of sages,

he entered into meditative absorption called the Vajra That Completely Purifies All Obscurations, and from the very heart of this dhāraṇī — called That Which Completely Purifies All Lower Realms — he drew forth the following:

Oṃ sarva-pāpaṃ dahana vajra hūṃ phaṭ. Oṃ sarva-avāya-viśodhana vajra hūṃ phaṭ. Oṃ sarva-karma-āvaraṇāni bhasmiṃ kuru hūṃ phaṭ. Oṃ bruṃ bhrūṃ vināśaya āvaraṇāni hūṃ phaṭ. Oṃ bruṃ viśodhaya āvaraṇāni hūṃ phaṭ. Oṃ jvala jvalaṅgha kaṅgha kaha hana hana sarva āvaraṇāni hūṃ phaṭ. Oṃ sruṃ sara sara prasara prasara sarva āvaraṇāni hūṃ phaṭ. Oṃ hūṃ hara hara sarva āparaṇāni hūṃ phaṭ. Oṃ hūṃ phaṭ sarva āvaraṇāni visphōṭaya hūṃ phaṭ. Oṃ bṛta bṛta sarva āvaraṇāni phaṭ. Oṃ traṭa traṭa sarva āvaraṇāni hūṃ phaṭ. Oṃ chinda chinda sarva āvaraṇāni hūṃ phaṭ. Oṃ daha daha sarva naraka-gati-hetuṃ hūṃ phaṭ. Oṃ paca paca sarva preta-gata-hituṃ hūṃ phaṭ. Oṃ matha matha sarva tiryak-gati-hetuṃ hūṃ phaṭ.

Then the ritual accessories for these are set forth.

Oṃ sarva-apāya-viśodhani. Dhama dhama dhūpaya hūṃ phaṭ. Oṃ sarva-durgati-viśodhani puṣpa-vilokini hūṃ phaṭ. Oṃ sarva-apāya-viśodhani jñāna-āloka-kari hūṃ phaṭ. Oṃ sarva-apāya-gati-gandha-nāśani gandha-vati hūṃ phaṭ.

Then the mantras for the guardians:

Oṃ sarva-naraka-gati-ākarṣaṇi hūṃ jaḥ phaṭ. Oṃ sarva-naraka-gati-abhyuddharaṇi hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ. Oṃ sarva-avāya-bandhana-vimocani hūṃ baṃ phaṭ. Oṃ sarva-apāya-gati-gahana-vināśani hūṃ hoḥ phaṭ.

Then the maṇḍala is described:

A maṇḍala in the likeness of a wheel,
adorned with eight exceedingly beautiful spokes,
complete with hub and with the rims that bear the spokes —

having drawn the inner sequence, in the hub one should place Śākyamuni, the lord of the Śākyas, the Sage. In front of him, draw the great and mighty Vajrapāṇi, the hero. Behind him, draw the Wheel-Turner. To the right direction, draw the Victor's Uṣṇīṣa; to the left, draw Vijaya. In the direction of fire, draw the mass of splendor; in the direction of Indra, the White Parasol; in the direction of wind, Vikīrṇa; in the southwest direction, draw Vijaya. Having drawn the inner portion, then draw the exterior: a square with four gateways, adorned with lion-thrones and stairways. The heroes are to be placed with hooks, nooses, chains, and pleasing bells. In all the corner directions, incense and the like are to be drawn.

Then, having anointed one's own body with fragrant perfumes and the like, one who holds the vajra enters and invokes all the deities with this mantra: Oṃ jaḥ hūṃ baṃ hoḥ. Bhagavan, ehyehi, mahākāruṇika, dṛśya hoḥ, samayastvaṃ. Then, having cultivated the resolve for the deities to depart, and having made offerings, one introduces them accordingly and confers empowerment. Through this, one is fully liberated from all lower realms, and one will be reborn in higher realms and heavenly worlds, ascending from ground to ground ever higher. All spiritual attainments will be accomplished, and without any doubt, buddhahood itself will be accomplished. As before, all activities will be performed, and one will become unobstructed in all things. Through the performance of the rites, one will be freed from all epidemic diseases, harmful spirits, poisons, and the like. Through Vajrapāṇi's hūṃ, all activities will be accomplished. All the ritual procedures of other tantras will also be accomplished.

In this very continent of Jambudvīpa, in a border region, there was a king of bad family called Unwell Body. Through ignorance, he relied upon an unwholesome spiritual companion, and for the sake of desire that king came to be separated from his father, who supported him; and his mother, tormented by grief for him, also died before her time. On one occasion, for the sake of play and pleasure, he went to another dense forest. There dwelt a sage named Maitrī who sought nothing in return. When the king saw him, he was greatly astonished and thought to himself: 'Here, where lions and tigers and deer and wild boars and cattle and buffalo and the like, and snakes and scorpions and insects and mosquitoes and venomous serpents, afflict and devour one another; where one is cut off from food and drink and clothing and comforts; where one is utterly tormented and harmed by wind and cold — in this place this great being dwells.' So thinking and reflecting, he approached him and, having come before him, said: 'O great sage, why do you harm me with the sufferings of wind, heat, and the rest?'

The sage replied: 'O great man, rather than experiencing the suffering of obscuration for many hundreds and thousands of aeons, one who takes up even a small measure of suffering's burden will come to full and perfect awakening in unsurpassed, perfect enlightenment (bodhi). Therefore this is not suffering. If instead one enjoys the pleasures of the senses even for a moment, for an instant, one will thereafter experience the sufferings of hell and the rest for countless hundreds and thousands of aeons without end. When one desires sensory pleasure, one will even kill one's own parents — to say nothing of other living beings — and because one utterly fails to know the Buddha, the Dharma, and the Saṅgha, one will commit every kind of evil deed.'

Upon hearing those words of the sage, the king was seized with great fear and dread and dismay, overwhelmed by all these sufferings, and said: 'What are the Buddha, the Dharma, and the Saṅgha? If one fully comes to know the Buddha, the Dharma, and the Saṅgha, what merit does one thereby fully obtain? If one has not killed one's parents and has not killed living beings, what merit does one obtain? What result does one obtain? If one abandons the enjoyments of desire, what merit and what result does one obtain?'

The opening Sanskrit title is transcribed in Tibetan phonetic script and has been restored here in reconstructed Sanskrit for clarity. The passage ends mid-sentence in the source, corresponding to a question Śakra is in the process of asking, and the translation preserves that open ending faithfully.

The phrase rendered "hair-tuft at the brow" translates the Tibetan for ūrṇā, the tuft of white hair between the eyebrows traditionally regarded as one of the major marks of a great being. Here it belongs syntactically to a bodhisattva's radiance emanating from the meditative absorption itself; the grammar is unusual and the referent of "that very same bodhisattva" is somewhat elliptical in context, most likely referring to the Great Brahmā in his capacity as a great bodhisattva.
