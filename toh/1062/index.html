About this text

The Great Sūtra Called the Sūtra of the Great Assembly, known in Sanskrit as the Mahāsamājasūtra and in Tibetan as Do chenpo düpa chenpö do, is a discourse of the Buddha that belongs to the genre of the great sūtras, or mahāsūtras, which were valued for their protective power. It opens with the Blessed One staying in a forest grove at Kapilavastu, the homeland of the Śākyas, together with five hundred monks, all of them arhats whose minds were fully liberated. As the Buddha teaches them about nirvāṇa, powerful gods and spirits gather in a vast assembly to see him and his community, and the sūtra goes on to recount this gathering, which gives the text its name. It is Toh 1062 in the Kangyur, where it appears among the texts collected for their protective and incantatory use, alongside the dhāraṇīs.

Homage to the Three Jewels.

Thus did I hear at one time. The Blessed One was staying in the forest grove at Kapilavastu of the Śākyas, together with five hundred monks. Every one of them was an arhat: their defilements were exhausted, they had done what was to be done, they had accomplished their task, they had laid down the burden, they had attained their own aim, they had completely exhausted the fetters that bind to existence, and their minds were thoroughly liberated through perfect knowledge.

Then the Blessed One was teaching the community of monks a discourse on the Dharma connected with nirvāṇa. At that time gods renowned for their great power, each renowned for great power, came from the world systems of all the ten directions and paid homage to the Blessed One and the community of monks.

Then four gods of the Brahmakāyika realm, who dwelt in the world of Brahmā, thought: "The Buddha, the Blessed One, is staying in the forest grove at Kapilavastu of the Śākyas, together with five hundred monks. Every one of them is an arhat: their defilements are exhausted, they have done what was to be done, they have accomplished their task, they have laid down the burden, they have attained their own aim, they have completely exhausted the fetters that bind to existence, and their minds are thoroughly liberated through perfect knowledge. And the Blessed One is teaching the community of monks a discourse on the Dharma connected with nirvāṇa. At that time gods renowned for their great power, each renowned for great power, have come from the world systems of all the ten directions and are paying homage to the Blessed One and the community of monks. So it would surely be fitting for us too to go and praise the Blessed One, each of us with a verse."

Then, in the time it takes a strong man to stretch out his bent arm or bend his outstretched arm, those four gods of the Brahmakāyika realm vanished from the world of Brahmā and came before the Blessed One. They sat down to one side, and the first Brahmakāyika god addressed him in verse:

"Devoted to this great assembly,
The hosts of gods have gathered from everywhere;
To behold the Saṅgha, whom no other can overcome,
We have come here."

The second Brahmakāyika god addressed him in verse:

"With minds that have become upright,
The monks here strive with great diligence;
Like charioteers who have taken up the reins,
They keep their senses well restrained."

The third Brahmakāyika god addressed him in verse:

"The Indra-post, the bar, and the stakes like a moat
They have utterly destroyed; they cannot be led astray.
Free of craving, they live in purity,
Tamed by the Guide like young elephants."

The fourth Brahmakāyika god addressed him in verse:

"Those who go to the Buddha for refuge
Will not go to the lower realms;
And when they have left the human body behind,
They will attain the body of a god."

When the four gods of the Brahmakāyika realm had spoken thus, they sat right there in quiet composure.

Then the Blessed One looked over the whole assembly of gods and said to the monks: "Monks, oh, this assembly of gods is great! Monks, oh, this assembly of gods is exceedingly great! Then the gods of the Pure Abodes, renowned as exceedingly powerful, were delighted by the teaching of the sūtra, and at that time they recited the Great Assembly Sūtra with these words: "Blessed One, we too shall rightly recite this sūtra with these words and rightly proclaim it. The words of the secret mantra are these:

tadyathā teje tejavati cidtre cidtravati late latavati khame khamavati kṣeme kṣemavati bhadre bhadravati yaśe yaśavati dūte dūtavati capale capalavati khace khacavati ti

Blessed One, these are the secret mantra words of the gods of the Pure Abodes. Blessed One, whoever holds this sūtra and these secret mantra words without confusion will be protected from all bhūtas for as long as they live. All bhūtas will be pleased with them as well. No contagious illness will be able to harm them. Wherever it is recited, there will be well-being, and those there will also be protected for seven years. Rain will fall as much as is wished, and it will also cease; hail will fall, and it will also cease. If one strokes it over any contagious illness, or recites it over water and gives that to drink, it will completely pacify all of that person's contagious illnesses."

Then Brahmā said to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, these are also the vidyā-mantras of the higher classes of form-realm gods, apart from those of the Pure Abodes, and these are the subtle rites of the secret mantra. This is how the sūtra is upheld here."

tadyathā bhale bhalamale duradule hime phubpise kācākṣikucesī maminīṣerethoraṇipamaphibamapoṭisteke tarake guru garo guggumiṭitāre rakṣasūtramanupālayathā

This is our detailed vidyā-mantra rite. If anyone holds or recites this sūtra and these mantra words without letting them become fragmented, then neither humans nor nonhumans will come to that person, nor will malicious beings come, nor snakes, nor poisons, and harms will not take hold of that person's body. All the bhūtas will also be pleased. For as long as that person lives, there will also be protection from all bhūtas. Whoever recites it and whoever listens to it will be protected for seven years. If one recites it over water and then sprinkles the water or gives it to drink, all of that person's contagious illnesses will be completely pacified.

Then all the Lords of Desire said to the Blessed One, "Blessed One, this is also our domain, and this is the vidyā-mantra for upholding this sūtra:

tadyathā linte lipe lope pape paṃkekama kācekācekṣi kācapiṃgale dumedumedume taṭetadtraṭe tratrākṣi kolā koli kikakolike sādhū sūtramanupālayathā udgṛhṇayaceti

"Blessed One, whoever upholds these mantra words and this well-spoken sūtra without fragmenting them and without letting them decline, and guards them, will be protected from everything. All the bhūtas will also be pleased. As much rain as is wished for will fall, and it will also stop; hail will fall, and it will also stop. One will also be completely freed from bhūtas and grahas. Poison will also be drawn out. If one recites it seven times over water and then sprinkles the water or gives it to drink, by that all contagious illnesses will be completely pacified."

Then Śakra (Indra), lord of the gods, said to the Blessed One, "Blessed One, this vidyā-mantra of ours likewise upholds this sūtra. The higher gods who dwell in the desire realm have rejoiced in it, and the lower ones too have rejoiced in it well:

tadyathā vīre vīravati toṭe toṭavati bhadre bhadravati gone gonavati meghe meghavati jvalane jvalanavati pūte bhutavati khorate khoratavati idanasūtramanugṛhṇatha anupālayathaceti

"Blessed One, whoever upholds this sūtra and these mantra words without letting them become fragmented will be protected from all bhūtas for as long as he lives. No human or nonhuman whatsoever will harm him. Wherever it is recited, there will be protection for as long as life lasts. All sentient beings will also be joyful. No one will ever overpower him. Contagious illnesses will not take hold of his body. He will also bring benefit to all sentient beings. Wherever it is recited, there will be protection for seven years. One will also be freed from bhūtas and grahas. Poison will also be destroyed. All contagious illnesses will also be dispelled. If one recites it over a thread or over water and then ties the thread or sprinkles the water, one will also be free of all contagious illnesses."

Then the Four Great Kings said to the Blessed One, "Blessed One, this vidyā-mantra of us Four Great Kings also upholds this sūtra and brings it to accomplishment."

Virūpākṣa said:
cakre cakriṇi cakrapāgini bhitirirmini śimiśimiśāmaneti

"Blessed One, whoever properly upholds these four detailed vidyā-mantra rites spoken by the Four Great Kings, together with this sūtra, without letting them become fragmented and without misusing them, and guards them, will be protected for as long as he lives. All bhūtas and all contagious illnesses will not come back to him. He will also be freed from seizure by malicious beings and from all poisons. If one recites it over a thread or over water and then ties the thread, or sprinkles the water and gives it to drink, by that rite it will be accomplished for him."

Then the asuras and Vāyu, god of the wind, said to the Blessed One, "Blessed One, this vidyā-mantra of ours also upholds this sūtra:

tadyathā gale gagagale galegani khinikhini ghuṣu ghuṣughughuṣu khanekhakhene khanakhani lupelupi lupalupeti

"Blessed One, if one upholds these mantras and this sūtra without letting them become fragmented and without misusing them, one will be protected for as long as one lives. All of one's contagious illnesses will also be completely pacified, and poison will not take hold of one's body."

Then the gods of the Pure Abodes spoke these words to the Blessed One: "Venerable Blessed One, since those who have come from the world systems of the ten directions have spoken vidyā-mantras in order to uphold the intent of this sūtra, may the Blessed One, together with the assembly of śrāvakas, also rejoice in them completely. Please explain well how they are to be upheld, guarded, and accomplished. In that way, this Dharma discourse offered by those sons of good family will come to bear fruit."

Then the Blessed One said, "This secret mantra that you have fully held is accomplished. I too, together with the assembly of śrāvakas, shall explain the accomplishment, the protection, and the practice of this sūtra. Listen to it, and I shall explain."

The gods of the Pure Abodes said, "Blessed One, so it is. Blessed One, together with the assembly of śrāvakas, please explain the protection and the practice, so that this great sūtra may be accomplished."

Then, so that this sūtra might be accomplished, the Blessed One declared the protection and the practice.

The accomplishment of the stream-enterers is this: tāla tāla tāna tāla tato talemata.

The accomplishment of the once-returners is this: sale sasaliti.

The accomplishment of the non-returners is this: late late lolati.

The accomplishment of the arhats is this: dhupu dhupu dhuru dhuru kacabi kaci kacapi kacati.

Then the accomplishment of the Blessed One is this: dhame dhume dhase dhase dhaso dhase dhare dhadhare dharo dhiddhiti.

The protection for it is this: tinatiri cirakṣa bigalakṣi rakṣa.

The sealing of the boundary is this: baddha baddhoti baddha sirimiti baddha ghuṣe baddha ghughuśe baddha ghuṣagaṣeyama baddhabhi.

The practice is this: pikhe sādhaya tutule sādhayeti.

Having observed a fast for three days, on the fifteenth day one should make offerings to the Blessed One with these words of secret mantra and with this sūtra. Facing east, one should recite the protection a hundred times. Having done a hundred recitations, if one does one hundred and eight for each knot, protection has been made, and one's hair will also stand on end three times. The ripening of former karma, however, is excepted.

On the twelfth day of the waxing moon, having made offerings to the Blessed One, one should take that thread in hand, recite, and seal the boundary. Reciting one hundred and eight times and tying one hundred and eight knots, when one seals the boundary, one's hair will also stand on end seven times. If a female jackal cries out three times from the south and then stays there, one should know from this that the boundary has been sealed.

Then, having fasted in the same way, on the fifteenth day one should bathe, make offerings to the Blessed One, and take up the thread. With the words of the śrāvakas and the detailed rite of the Blessed One, one recites seven times, performs one hundred and eight recitations, and ties one hundred and eight knots. Taking the thread in hand, one recites all the vidyā-mantras once. When one performs the practice, a great rain and a great wind will arise. They will rise and fall outside the boundary, and will cease in an instant. One should recite up to seven times.

Then two sons of the gods, one called Mahāmegha and one called Sumegha, will come, bow their heads at that person's feet, grant accomplishment, and also rejoice. The accomplishments here are these. That of Mahāmegha is called kucu kuca. That of Sumegha is this: it is called kara kara kārara.

If, with these words and with the thread taken in hand, one contemplates the sūtra and the secret mantras, one will accomplish everything one wishes to do. If one recites over water or over a thread with the Blessed One's sūtra and all these vidyā-mantras, there is nothing that cannot be done; every action will be accomplished. For as long as one lives, one should keep this sūtra in order to protect oneself and others.

When the Blessed One had spoken thus, those monks rejoiced and praised what the Blessed One had said.

Whatever bhūtas have gathered here,
Those that dwell on the earth and in the sky,
May they always show love toward all beings,
And practice the Dharma by day and by night.

The Great Sūtra Called the Sūtra of the Great Assembly is complete.

Translator's notes

The third verse is obscure in the Tibetan. It corresponds to the Pāli Mahāsamaya Sutta verse on cutting the stake, the crossbar, and the Indra-post. The Tibetan wording is difficult, especially the syllables rendered "the bar" and "like a moat." The rendering follows the sense suggested by that parallel only as far as the Tibetan words allow. "Haimavatas" renders གསེར་ཅན ("golden ones"), taken as the Himalayan yakṣas (Pali hemavatā) of the parallel Mahāsamaya Sutta. "Even Mountain" (རི་མཉམ) and "the Grove of Victory" (རྣམ་རྒྱལ་ཚལ) are translated literally. The first may correspond to the Sātāgira yakṣas of the Pali, but the identification is uncertain. ཡངས་པ, usually Vaiśālī, is taken here as Mount Vipula near Rājagṛha, following the Pali parallel (Vepulla). "Friends of All" and "God of All" are literal renderings; they might reflect Sanskrit names such as Viśvāmitra and Viśvadeva, but this cannot be confirmed. This is the Mahāsamāja Sūtra, the counterpart of the Pāli Mahāsamaya Sutta. Many names in its catalogue could not be identified with confidence. These are given in English according to their meaning: the servants, many of the yakṣas, several nāgas, the asura called Released and Held (perhaps Namuci), and several goddesses. Mātali for "ma ldan" and Durgā for "dka' zlog ma" follow the parallel lists and standard usage, but they are not certain.

"The Mountain-Like Ones" and "the Golden Ones" probably correspond to the Pāli Sātāgirā and Hemavatā, two groups of yakṣas.

"Yangs pa" among the nāgas is rendered "those of Vaiśālī," following the Pāli Vesālā. Elsewhere this text's drafts render it as Vipula.

"Tshig lnga" is read as "gtsug lnga," Pañcaśikha, the gandharva named at this point in the parallel list. In the suparṇa verse, "de min" ("other than that") makes little sense. The line is read as "de ming" ("their name"), which matches the parallel "citrā supaṇṇā iti tesaṃ nāmāni."

The honorific "gsung" shows that the gentle words are the Buddha's. In the Pāli, the nāgas and suparṇas call upon him with gentle words, but the Tibetan follows a different construal.

"Lag na rdo rje," rendered Vajrapāṇi per the glossary, corresponds to the Pāli Vajirahattha, which here is an epithet of Indra.

"The Elephant" among the nāgas probably stands for Airāvaṇa. The text says Bali had eight sons, where the Pāli has a hundred; the number is kept as the text gives it. Many of the divine names in these verses are Tibetan translations of Indian group names whose Sanskrit forms cannot be recovered with confidence. Some correspond loosely to the Pāli Mahāsamaya Sutta, for example "Mind-Disturbed Ones" for Manopadosikā and "White-Haired Ones" for Odātagayhā. These have been rendered by their meaning. They include the Mind-Disturbed Ones, Seekers, Gentle Ones, White Ones, Intoxicating Ones, Compassion, Blue-Robed, White-Haired, Yellow-Robed, Mixed, Pure, Radiant, Bliss-Makers, Dark-Set Ones, Stars, Good Aspiration Ones, All-Scattering Youth, Golden Ones, Mountain-Like Ones, Host-Bearing Ones and Blue Ones.

"Lumpinī" and "Lumpi" follow the Tibetan transliteration. "Hārītī, ever intoxicated" may instead be two separate groups; the Pāli has Hāragajā and Sadāmattā. Likewise, the Hārītī surrounded by her children corresponds to the Brahmā Hārita in the Pāli. The Tibetan reads both as the feminine 'phrog ma, and the name has been kept as Hārītī.

"The star Tiṣya" renders skar rgyal. Rgyal is the Tibetan name of the lunar mansion Tiṣya/Puṣya; compare Pāli Tisso. "The Varuṇa goddesses" renders chu lha mo.

The "Dark One" who leads the army is Māra. "Contagious illness" renders bro nad, whose exact sense is uncertain. The term rendered "contagious illnesses" (bro nad) is uncommon. It may refer more specifically to fevers or epidemic disease. It is rendered as in the preceding passage. "Without misusing them" renders a phrase that could also mean "without altering them." The "Lords of Desire" appear to be the ruling gods of the desire realm. The two sons of the gods are rendered Mahāmegha ("Great Cloud") and Sumegha ("Good Cloud"). The second Sanskrit form is reconstructed from the Tibetan and is not fully certain. Where the Tibetan introduces the first accomplishment-mantra, it has only "Cloud" (sprin). This is taken as an abbreviation of Mahāmegha. "Hair will stand on end" renders spu zing, a sign of the mantra's efficacy. The phrase about the ripening of former karma means the protection works against all harm except the fruition of one's own past deeds.
