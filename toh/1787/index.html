About this text

The Lamp of All Secrets, an extensive commentary known in Sanskrit as the Sarvaguhyapradīpaṭīkā and in Tibetan as Gsang ba thams cad kyi sgron ma'i rgya cher 'grel pa, is Toh 1787 in the Tengyur, where it belongs among the commentaries on the tantras. It explains the Later Tantra, the eighteenth chapter of the Guhyasamāja Tantra, which gathers up and clarifies the meaning of the seventeen chapters of the root tantra and shows that its chief subject is the fourfold practice of approach and accomplishment. Following the Illuminating Lamp and the instructions of Nāgārjuna, Āryadeva, Nāgabodhi, Śākyamitra and Candrakīrti, it reads the tantra through the generation and completion stages, treating the four empowerments, the deity yoga of the maṇḍala, the ritual activities, luminosity, and the final union of the two truths embodied as the great Vajradhara. Its colophon credits its composition to an author whose name means Excellent Renown and records that the Indian scholar Smṛtijñānakīrti translated it himself into Tibetan.

Translation

In the language of India: sarbba guhya pratīpa ṭīka. In the language of Tibet: An Extensive Commentary, the Lamp of All Secrets.

Homage to glorious Vajratīkṣṇa.

Empty, very empty,
Great empty, and all-empty:
Bowing to Vajrasattva, the union,
I shall explain the Later Tantra.

The Later Tantra of the Guhyasamāja Tantra of the body, speech, and mind of all tathāgatas says "Then," and so on, in order to show clearly all the meaning of the root tantra's seventeen chapters above, to expand the points left incomplete, to show the definitive divisions of each chapter, and to show that the chief subject of this king of tantras is the fourfold approach and accomplishment. The meaning of this eighteenth chapter, too, is the showing of the praise and the showing of the one who requests the meaning of the Later Tantra.

The showing of the praise, in turn, has three parts. The root tantra having been completed, there is praise of the indivisibility of support and supported; praise of the Tathāgata once all doubts have been cut; and praise by way of gathering up all the meaning of the tantra.

Here, as for "Then," the meaning of the word atha is that it is a word of good fortune. It connects the occasion of explaining the Later Tantra once the root tantra is complete: this is the literal meaning. As for atha, "a kā ro mu khaṃ sarbba dharmā nāṃ" means that all phenomena lack intrinsic nature: this is the accumulation of wisdom. Tha is the accumulation of merit. "The tantra that teaches the two accumulations": so it is called, and this is the general meaning. A is the lotus of wisdom, which gives rise to the sphere of reality (dharmadhātu). Tha is the vajra of method: this is the hidden meaning. A is unborn, completely pure wisdom. Tha is objectless compassion: this is the ultimate explanation.

As for "Maitreya and the others": Maitreya is a bodhisattva bound to one more birth, and he requested the tantra for the sake of beings in the future: this is the provisional meaning. Maitreya, the Loving One, is the great Vajradhara himself, who, through great love for sentient beings, emanated as the retinue and made the request in order to clarify the root tantra: this is the definitive meaning. "Bodhisattva" will be shown below.

As for "tathāgata": because they have gone to suchness, they are called tathāgatas: this is the general meaning. "Tathāgata" means this: just as, at the time of generation, what has not yet come is utterly destroyed, so too one goes in that way, and so is thus-gone: this is the hidden meaning. "Tathāgata" means: because they possess perfect wisdom, they are tathāgatas: this is the ultimate explanation. Empowerment and the rest will also be explained below.

"All things" means this: realizing through the whole of their own wisdom, they saw the meaning of the nature of reality just as it is and without error by way of listening, heard it by way of reflecting, and came to understand it by way of meditating. Phenomena are the aggregates and so on. "Beholding" means beholding all as empty. "All of them" is said because the great Vajradhara is the gathered nature of all the buddhas of the ten directions.

Now, of the three praises, first, to praise the completion of the root tantra, "Emaho" and so on is spoken. "Emaho" is wonder at the explanation of the Guhyasamāja root tantra. At what is there wonder? "Samantabhadra" refers to the great Vajradhara, free of all the elaborations of conceptual thought. His body, speech, and mind are Vairocana, Amitābha, and Akṣobhya. As for "through the three definite vajras of those three," and "abiding fully in the three vajras of body, speech, and mind of those three": this refers to his falling silent, focused on the illusion-like sameness of the body, speech, and mind of all tathāgatas.

All sentient beings, through the three times,
Have truly arisen as the vajra's essence.

This shows that the bodies of all sentient beings and Vairocana are not two as support and supported, being perfected in suchness, and that body and speech not yet perfected are indivisible from them. Likewise it shows that whatever speech and mind belong to the three realms are one with Amitābha and Akṣobhya. This is what it teaches: the immeasurable, limitless emanation bodies endowed with the five families, the buddhas, the bodhisattvas, the kings of the wrathful ones, and all the rest, attain the very state of Vairocana, Amitābha, and Akṣobhya.

"All the buddhas, of great miraculous power": because all the buddhas have become the three families. "Have attained the state of the vajra of awakening": all the sentient beings of the three realms too have attained the state that is indivisible from the three families as support and supported. "Emaho, this is supremely wondrous! Emaho, peace beyond the senses!": because all the buddhas gather into the three families and, having the nature of luminosity, fall silent. What passes beyond the sphere of the senses is peace, and the intent is: in this there is great wonder. "Emaho, the stream of saṃsāra": this is wonder that the stream of body, speech, and mind of all sentient beings of the three realms is, as the three families, supremely in nirvāṇa. This, moreover, is a praise of what is said in the seventeenth chapter: "Then the Tathāgata Body Vajra Vairocana, dwelling in the bodies of all the three realms, focused on the sameness of the bodies of all tathāgatas and fell silent."

The great Vajradhara, delighted by the praise, spoke in response: "Then," and so on. "It is thus" means having become buddha. "It is thus indeed" means that all the three realms too appear as the three families.

Having thus shown the praise, next comes the showing of the request for the meaning of the Later Tantra: "Then the bodhisattvas," and so on, is taught. "The bodhisattva great beings assembled" refers to Maitreya and the others, beyond all telling.

"Secret offering" and so on shows the preparation for making the request. The secret offering is the offering of vajra and lotus. The outer offerings are the five sense pleasures, the seven precious things, and so on. "Having prostrated" means touching the five limbs of the body to the ground: this is the provisional meaning. The nature of reality of one's own mind becomes the awareness of wisdom, and that itself is prostration: this is the definitive meaning.

To show the general request for the meaning of the Later Tantra, "Emaho" and so on is spoken. "Extremely hard to find" shows that the tantra is hard to find, in order to increase faith. It shows that even the Buddha Dīpaṃkara did not teach it, and that after the Tathāgata Kāśyapa passed into nirvāṇa it did not appear either. Why is that? Because until that time sentient beings were especially devoted to the lesser, to generosity, moral discipline, and the rest, the Dharma and Vinaya that are free of attachment, and had minds that would be frightened if the profound were taught. Therefore, since they lacked the fortune, the explanation of the meaning of the great secret was not given at that time. But in this age, knowing the inclinations of sentient beings who engage in attachment and in whom the five degenerations are rampant, he came; and so it was taught by Vajradhara alone.

"This method of accomplishing awakening, extremely hard to find" means this: the awakening that bodhisattvas do not attain even though they strive and exert themselves for eons as countless as the sands of the river Ganges, bodhisattvas who delight in the Guhyasamāja attain. Through the stage of inner full awakening they enter ultimate truth and wash away all stains of turbidity; then, in an instant, a moment, a short while, the mere blink of an eye, they rise in the stage of self-blessing and become endowed with the perfection of omniscience.

"The cause has become supreme": the root tantra of all tantras is the Tattvasaṃgraha, its later tantra is the Guhyasamāja, and what has become, in turn, its later tantra is this eighteenth chapter. "Concealed" means that, like a hidden treasure, the Dharma of attachment and so on was taught in the root tantra with its words and meaning unclear, and the Later Tantra is requested in order to realize it. Having thus made the request for his own sake, the request made for the sake of others is "for sentient beings." "What has been explained" is the root tantra.

To show Vajradhara's response, "Then" and so on is spoken. "Excellent!" is said because the request was made for the sake of all sentient beings.

To show the requesting of the doubts about the Later Tantra, "Then all the bodhisattvas, eyes wide with great joy," and so on is spoken. "Again and again" is said because great meaning is obtained through many questions. If one asks what the name of this tantra is, it is shown as "the Glorious Guhyasamāja Yoga Tantra." Four doubts are put, about secret, assembly, the connected meaning of "glorious," tantra, and yoga; this is a request that sums up all the meaning of the tantra.

The request about the teaching on the five families in the first chapter is: "In how many ways is that itself explained?" To show it in the manner of body, speech, and mind: "Likewise, of how many kinds is the secret?" The request about the meaning of the great Vajradhara as the single one is: "And what is called the great secret?" The request at length about isolation of body is: "Of how many kinds is the sublime?"

The request about the meaning of the teaching on bodhicitta in the second chapter is: "What is called bodhicitta?" The request about the meaning of the form of the great knowledge being taught in the first chapter is: "the knowledge being." The sixteen doubts requested about the meaning of the mantras of the thirteen deities taught in the first chapter are shown from "vajra dhraṃ ka" up to "likewise bighnanta krita."

The request about the meaning of the teaching on the conduct of bodhisattvas and the conduct of tathāgatas in the ninth chapter is: "What is the all-pervading conduct?" The request about the meaning of the teaching on mantra conduct in the fifth and seventh chapters is: "Likewise, what is the conduct of mantra?", that is, the conduct of yoga. The request about the meaning of the recitation taught in the third chapter is: "What is recitation?" The request about the meaning of the empowerment of the deities in the fifteenth chapter is: "What is called sealing?" The request about the meditation of the ten wrathful ones taught in the thirteenth chapter is: "the source of phenomena." The request for the condensed vow of all the seventeen chapters is: "What is the vow like?" The request about the meaning of the subduing of the afflictions taught in the ninth chapter is: "How does one act for the benefit of sentient beings?"

The request about the meaning of the maṇḍala taught in the fourth and sixteenth chapters is: "What is called a maṇḍala?" The request about the meaning of those same chapters is: "What is the placing of the mudrās like?" The request about the meaning of what is taught in the eighth chapter is: "flower" and "stūpa." The request about the meaning of the maṇḍala taught in the thirteenth chapter is: "the wisdom maṇḍala" and "What here is called the abode?" The request about the meaning of the commissioning taught in the sixteenth chapter is: urging the Lord, sending, inviting, and binding. The request about the meaning of the eighth and sixteenth chapters is: "How is empowerment given?" What is taught in the sixteenth chapter is requested with "the knowledge observance." The request about the meaning of what is taught in the fourth, fifth, sixth, eighth, twelfth, fifteenth, sixteenth, and seventeenth chapters is: "the five nectars" and "the five senses." What speaks to the meaning of the eleventh and seventeenth chapters is: "What are the lowest and the supreme accomplishments like?" The request about the meaning of the beginning and end of the tantra is: "Of how many kinds is method?" and "Likewise, what is the arising of method like?"

About the meaning of the thirteenth, fourteenth, and fifteenth chapters, they asked, "How is the command to be given?" To show that the great Vajradhara granted this, and to show that he entered luminosity and fell silent, "Then all those tathāgatas" and so on is spoken. "Focusing on the Vajra Holder" means nondual wisdom.

Next, to show that Vajradhara was urged again and again to rise from his absorption in luminosity, "Then the great bodhisattvas" and so on is spoken. "Whose very nature is compassion" means that the bodhisattvas have come under the power of others through compassion.

To show that, having explained a special Dharma, meditative equipoise is needed, "Then the Tathāgata" and so on is spoken. "Body, speech, and mind in the vajra" is shown from the second chapter: "Generate mind like body, as the body vajra; body like mind, as the mind vajra; and mind like words spoken, as the speech vajra." That is, generate awakening with the essential nature of the three indivisible vajras. "Having placed body, speech, and mind in the vajra" means: truly join body, speech, and mind in the essential nature of emptiness, which is like space, make them one, and enter equipoise at length upon what the lords of bodhisattvas have asked: this is the provisional meaning. "Make body, speech, and mind vajra" means: generate the body from the nature of mind. That is, mind like body is whatever body is generated, together with the subtle elements of the three minds, like the enjoyment body; body like mind is that there is nothing at all other than mind itself; and mind like words spoken is whatever is spoken in words, and mind itself is to be generated as its essence. "Having placed body, speech, and mind in the vajra, lords of bodhisattvas" means: you lords of bodhisattvas, truly join body, speech, and mind in luminosity, the essential nature of space, make them one, and generate yourselves as the great Vajradhara.

To show that same point, "Then" is spoken. "They received the command upon the crowns of their heads" means that, pleased, they said "Excellent," and settled in samādhi as instructed.

To show the meaning of what the bodhisattvas had asked, "Then" and so on is spoken. "Focusing on his own great vajra mind" means that all the buddhas and bodhisattvas, abiding in the meaning of Vajradhara, declared with a single voice the meaning of what had been asked. This is what it teaches: all the buddhas of the ten directions gathered in the great celestial palace, became like a pod full of sesame seeds, and with a single intention taught, through suchness, one meaning with one voice.

Now, to show the name of the tantra:

Body, speech, and mind, the three kinds,
Are what is taught as "secret."

Here, the pledges, vows, and the rest, fully transformed into the three families, is the explanation of the literal meaning. The secret is the three syllables oṃ āḥ hūṃ: this is the general meaning. The secret is the three wisdoms, the luminous appearances by their nature, endowed with wind as their mount: this is the hidden meaning. The secret is the body whose nature is wisdom: this is the ultimate explanation.

To show what is called "assembly":

What is called "all the buddhas,"
Gathered, is explained as the assembly.

Here, the gathering of all the buddhas, two becoming one, is the assembly: this is the literal meaning. "All the buddhas" is Vajradhara with his consort, and the true union of his vajra and lotus is the gathering and assembly: this is the general meaning. "All the buddhas" refers to method and wisdom, and the gathering and true union of those two is the assembly: this is the hidden meaning. "All the buddhas" are the conventional and ultimate truths, and the assembling and gathering of those two, what is called the great Vajradhara, whose nature is nondual wisdom, is the assembly: this is the ultimate meaning.

This is what it teaches. Body, speech, and mind, appearing as the three, Tathāgata, Lotus, and Vajra, become one as Buddha, Lotus, and Vajradhara: this is the Guhyasamāja of the literal meaning. From the three syllables one becomes the body of the great Vajradhara, and the consort and the vajra and lotus come together: this is called the Guhyasamāja of the general meaning. That which is endowed with the threefold wind as its mount becoming the illusion-like body whose nature is method and wisdom: this is the Guhyasamāja in the hidden meaning. The two truths becoming nondual in union: this is called the Guhyasamāja in the ultimate meaning. This teaches all the meaning of the tantra. The connection of the tantra is taught by the word "glorious," the perfection of every aim.

In general, "the fifth and the ninth as well" and so on is spoken. The fifth chapter teaches yogic conduct. The ninth chapter teaches the marvelous prophecy. As for "the seventeenth and the thirteenth": the seventeenth chapter fully teaches the pledges and vows, and the thirteenth chapter teaches the collection of rites supremely directed inward. "Buddha" means that these chapters teach the conduct of the great practice of buddhas and bodhisattvas.

This is what it teaches. In the fifth, one masters through experience the conduct of the three yogic conducts, with elaboration, without elaboration, and utterly without elaboration; then one undertakes the marvelous conduct of the five afflictions of the ninth chapter. When that has become manifest, one engages in all worldly and transworldly conduct by way of the seventeenth. Having thus realized the three elaborations of yoga, the conduct of bodhisattvas, and having fulfilled one's own aim by practicing the marvelous conduct of the tathāgatas and the pledges and vows, one then, for the sake of others, being supremely directed inward, will practice the collections of rites of the thirteenth chapter. So it teaches.

As for "the fourth and the sixteenth, the eighth, and likewise the twelfth": the fourth chapter teaches the maṇḍala of empowerment.

The sixteenth chapter teaches at length the colored-powder maṇḍala of the fourth, elaborating it. That is, the fourth is the maṇḍala for the disciple, and the sixteenth is the maṇḍala for accomplishment. The eighth chapter teaches the secret empowerment. The twelfth chapter teaches full transformation into the great seal (mahāmudrā).

"The common rites of the master" means that all the activities of every master are drawing the maṇḍala and conferring the empowerments of the vase, the secret, and wisdom-gnosis. "Accomplishment is the observance and the vow" means that, having drawn the maṇḍala and with the knowledge observance gone before, one accomplishes the two kinds of accomplishment by way of the seal of the knowledge observance. This is what it will teach: for one's own sake, one draws the maṇḍala and should accomplish the accomplishments through the conduct of the knowledge observance; and for the sake of others, one draws the maṇḍala, brings the disciples in by the rite of entry, and confers the four kinds of empowerment. This is accomplishment. So it will teach.

As for "the sixth and the second, the tenth and the fourteenth": the sixth chapter teaches the blessing of body, speech, and mind. The second chapter teaches the completion stage alone. The tenth chapter teaches the urging of the heart essence whose nature is outer and inner, in order to make the desired accomplishments manifest. The fourteenth chapter teaches the collection of rites supremely directed toward mantra. "Fierce conduct, passionate attachment, and the vow of near accomplishment" means this: the tenth and fourteenth chapters are fierce conduct, and the sixteenth chapter teaches attachment through method and wisdom; these are the vow of near accomplishment of one's own deity. This is what it will teach: if the yogin endowed with the generation stage of the sixth chapter and the completion stage of the second chapter has not succeeded, he acts as in the tenth, urging his own deity; and when his own aim is fulfilled, he will act for the aim of others by being supremely directed toward mantra, by way of the fourteenth. So it teaches.

As for "the seventh and the third, the eleventh and the fifteenth": the seventh chapter teaches the three elaborations of yogic conduct. The third chapter teaches the yoga of the deity, both fully pure and not fully pure. The eleventh chapter teaches the generation of the mantra body of the generation stage and the wisdom body of the completion stage. The fifteenth chapter teaches the collections of rites, all the accomplishments, the signs in dreams, and the illusion-like samādhi. "The four accomplishments" are the collection of rites of the fifteenth chapter and the uncommon accomplishment of the eleventh chapter. "Signs" teaches the dream signs of the fifteenth chapter and the illusion-like signs; these are the vow of approach. This is what it teaches: for one's own sake, one accomplishes by way of the two stages that arise from the third and eleventh, and brings this to completion with the help of the yogic conduct of the seventh; and for the sake of others, one accomplishes through the collection of rites of the fifteenth chapter. So it teaches.

The condensed meaning is this. The signs of accomplishment in approach arise; through near accomplishment one engages in urging the deity for the sake of others; one who has thus fulfilled his own aim, by that accomplishment, accomplishes the maṇḍala for the sake of others and the common accomplishments for his own sake; and the supreme accomplishment of the great seal he accomplishes by way of great accomplishment. This is the connection among the separate chapters and the condensed meaning of the body of the tantra.

As for "the first chapter, the eleventh": "ten" is included within what is left unstated. It is the chapter that teaches in brief the fourfold approach and accomplishment. It should be understood that this chapter, having conferred the knowledge empowerment on those with the fortune to hear, in the maṇḍala of accomplishment of the great Vajradhara whose nature is what is expressed and what expresses it, sets out the analysis by way of the distinction between provisional and definitive meaning and the individual methods of accomplishment of the completion stage, and teaches in brief the meaning of all the sixteen chapters that come after it.

Now, to show clearly what the meaning of those chapters is:

The activities of all the tathāgatas,
Which subdue and which bring benefit.

This means that the four chapters on near accomplishment show that all the tathāgatas lead sentient beings who are tamed and peaceful into the Buddha's teaching through conduct that benefits them, and lead beings who are hard to tame into it through conduct that subdues them. Therefore it says:

Leading in sentient beings
Who are tamed, hard to tame, and peaceful.

Generation stage and completion:
The approach, the fourfold vajra rite.

This refers to the four chapters on approach, because by teaching the generation stage and the completion stage, the fourfold vajra rite, they accomplish the common accomplishments. Therefore it says:

Connected with the common accomplishments,
It is the supreme scripture itself.

"Connected with maṇḍala and observance" refers to the four chapters on accomplishment. When accomplishing the maṇḍala and the accomplishments, they are connected with the knowledge observance; and at the time of the maṇḍala and the master's rites, they are connected with the full ripening of the disciple. This, moreover, is taught by "the guru, through the path of mantra."

"And the excellent observance" teaches the four chapters on great accomplishment. It teaches that one who has become one's own disciple through empowerment enters the doorway of the excellent observance of yogic conduct, the conduct with elaboration, the conduct of the great-souled buddhas and bodhisattvas, and the teaching on the marvelous conduct and the conduct of pledges and vows; through this one's own mind is fully liberated, and one attains luminosity.

Having thus explained, of the tantra's name, what is called "the Glorious Guhyasamāja," now, to show what is called "Yoga Tantra," the answer to "What is called yoga?" is taught by "The union of method and wisdom," and so on. "Wisdom" refers to appearance, emptiness, and the wisdom that all phenomena, characterized as ultimate, are without entity.

"Method" refers to increase of appearance, the very empty, method, and the method characterized as the illusion-like entity of all phenomena, which are characterized as conventional. "Union" is the illusion-like yoga in which attainment of appearance, the great empty, and near attainment are nondual: this is the hidden meaning.

"Wisdom" is the wisdom that all phenomena, the ultimate truth, are without entity. As it is said:

That which is not the ground of any object
And has not become words,
By the entry and by the mind vajra,
Is explained as wisdom.

Method refers to the three appearances, characterized as conventional entities. As the master Nāgārjuna explains:

The teaching of the three appearances
Is known as the conventional.

"Union" means this: the yogin of the conventional and the ultimate makes all moving and unmoving phenomena one through the form of the great seal, and the form of the great Vajradhara whose nature is union is what is called yoga. This is the ultimate meaning of the tantra.

To show the meaning of "tantra":

Tantra is known as continuity.

This gives the etymology of tantra. Through the word tantra, the meaning of the four of approach and accomplishment, though it appears scattered among the chapters, is connected in meaning, and this is called tantra. Or else, the three essential natures of tantra that come below are continuously connected, and this is explained as tantra, or continuity.

Now, to explain the essential nature of tantra through its divisions, it says:

The classes of tantra become threefold:
The four, and likewise nature,
And the inalienable: so they are divided.

and so on. It is divided into three: the tantra of the cause, the nature, the tantra of method, and the inalienable tantra of the result. Their division into the two, cause and result, is:

The four and nature are the cause;
Likewise the inalienable is the result.

To show what the four are:

The four are what is called method.

This means that the four limbs of approach and accomplishment are the tantra of method of this tantra. As this very text says:

The methods are of four kinds,
Taught by the one with the vajra of awakening.
In all the Yoga Tantras
They are praised as the yogin's examination.
The rite of approach is the first;
Near accomplishment is the second;
Accomplishment is the third;
Great accomplishment is the fourth.

Therefore, the tantra of the cause is the nature of the person, the practitioner who is like a jewel; the tantra of method is the four limbs of approach and accomplishment; and the tantra of the result is the nonabiding nirvāṇa, the body of Vajradhara in union, which others cannot take away. These three are the essential nature of tantra, and therefore the meaning of tantra is gathered up in these three.

Now, to show the division of families, "The families are five and three," and so on is spoken. The brief statement "the families are five" is clearly set out by "That itself is explained as fivefold." From those four elements the aggregate of the body comes to be formed: it arises and is born from the gathering of the elements. Therefore, whatever is derived from the elements, the form aggregate and so on, belongs to the Tathāgata family, and it comes about through gathering the earth element. Likewise, those of the Jewel family are the water element, those of the Lotus family are the fire element, those of the Action family are the wind element, and those of the Vajra family are the gathered element of consciousness. In this way, through the division into the elements and what is derived from the elements in the maṇḍala of one's own body, the aggregates, having the nature of the five secrets, become the reality of the five great elements. The root tantra too says:

In brief, the five aggregates
Are renowned as the five buddhas.

The brief statement "and three" is clearly explained by "The secret is explained as threefold." The five tathāgatas are to be gathered into three. That is, Ratnasambhava becomes nondual with the body vajra, Amoghasiddhi becomes nondual with the speech vajra, and Akṣobhya becomes nondual with the sixth, Vajradhara. Thus all the buddhas and bodhisattvas are explained as three because they are gathered into one's own body, speech, and mind. The Candraguhyatilaka too says:

Through the union of body, speech, and mind,
They also become three.

The brief statement of the essential nature is clearly explained by "The supreme deity is the great secret." Having special devotion to the great Vajradhara, characterized by the three indivisible vajras, is the yoga of blessing, and this is said of the first arising as the body of the completion stage. Or else, the great Vajradhara is the body that gathers all the buddhas of the ten directions; and the body appearing as the essential nature of all worldly and transworldly deities, the wisdom body of union, is called the supreme deity. That is the Guhyasamāja of body, speech, and mind, and because it has become utterly great completion, it is so called. The Saṃvara too says:

This is the self-arisen Blessed One,
The single, supremely great deity.

Next is the clear explanation of what was briefly taught as "a hundred families":

The supreme families are of a hundred kinds.

The five aggregates and the four elements, the five sense faculties and the five objects, each taken in terms of the five families, become a hundred families. What are they? Explained in terms of the samādhi of Vairocana, there are the five families of the aggregate of form, blessed by the five Tathāgatas. Explained in terms of the samādhi of Ratnasambhava, there are the five families of the aggregate of feeling, blessed by the five Tathāgatas. Explained in terms of the samādhi of Amitābha, there are the five families of the aggregate of perception, blessed by the five Tathāgatas. Explained in terms of the samādhi of Amoghasiddhi, there are the five families of the aggregate of formations, blessed by the five Tathāgatas. Explained in terms of the samādhi of Akṣobhya, there are the five families of the aggregate of consciousness, blessed by the five Tathāgatas. Altogether, the supreme families taken in terms of the five aggregates are twenty-five.

As for the earth element, explained in terms of the samādhi of Buddhalocanā: the outer and inner earth elements are blessed by the five Tathāgatas, and although they thus become ten, they are counted as five families. Explained in terms of the samādhi of Māmakī: the outer and inner water elements are blessed by the five Tathāgatas, and although they thus become ten, they are counted as five families. Explained in terms of the samādhi of Pāṇḍaravāsinī: the outer and inner fire elements are blessed by the five Tathāgatas, and although by virtue of outer and inner they become ten, they are counted as five families. Explained in terms of the samādhi of Tārā: the outer and inner wind elements are blessed by the five Tathāgatas, and although by virtue of outer and inner they become ten, because they are one as wind they are counted as five families. Altogether, the supreme families are twenty.

Among those explained in terms of the samādhis of the bodhisattvas, the families that come to fifty are these. In terms of the samādhi of Kṣitigarbha, there are the five families of the eye sense source, blessed by the five Tathāgatas. Explained in terms of the samādhi of Vajrapāṇi, there are the five families of the ear sense source, blessed by the five Tathāgatas. In terms of the samādhi of Ākāśagarbha, there are the five families of the nose sense source, blessed by the five Tathāgatas. Explained in terms of the samādhi of Lokeśvara, there are the five families of the tongue sense source, blessed by the five Tathāgatas. Explained in terms of the samādhi of Sarvanivaraṇaviṣkambhin, there are the five families of the body sense source, blessed by the five Tathāgatas. Altogether, the supreme families are one hundred and twenty-five.

The objects, explained in terms of the samādhis of the goddesses, are the families that come to fifty, as follows. Explained in terms of the samādhi of Rūpavajrā, when forms are blessed by the five Tathāgatas, there are the five Tathāgata families. Explained in terms of the samādhi of Śabdavajrā, there are the five families of sounds, blessed by the five Tathāgatas. Explained in terms of the samādhi of Gandhavajrā, there are the five families of smells, blessed by the five Tathāgatas. Explained in terms of the samādhi of Rasavajrā, there are the five families of tastes, blessed by the five Tathāgatas. Explained in terms of the samādhi of Sparśavajrā, there are the five families of all tangibles, blessed by the five Tathāgatas. Altogether, the supreme families are one hundred and twenty-five.

With the five wisdoms that enter into the five stages themselves, the supreme families altogether are a hundred. As the Candraguhyatilaka says:

The families are explained as a hundred kinds;
Gathered in brief, they are of five kinds.

As for the faculty of mental consciousness, there are three points. It is the lord of all the faculties. It has no outer object, but, taking the sphere of reality (dharmadhātu) as its object through individual self-awareness, it is called the illusion-like samādhi of the great Vajradhara. And it is said to pervade all of the hundred families.

As for the line "Was spoken together with Bodhivajra": here the great Vajradhara has another name, Bodhicittavajra, the vajra of the awakening mind. "Together with" him means what appears, from the five families up to the hundred. None of these were spoken in the great root tantra; because they are explained and taught here in this later tantra, it is called the Later Tantra.

Having thus shown the name of the tantra, now, in order to show the name that reveals the meaning of the tantra, there is "the being of awareness, the awakening mind, called Vajradhara":

Without beginning or end, peaceful,
Existent and nonexistent, the formidable lord.

With regard to "awakening," the "beginning" is appearance, like the disk of the moon. The "end" is the attainment of appearance, like darkness. By implication, the middle is the increase of appearance, like the disk of the sun. "Without" means that these three appearances of conventional truth dissolve into emptiness, and that peace is what is meant here. "Existent" refers to the three appearances of conventional truth. "Nonexistent" means that, in the state of the all-empty, the ultimate truth, the three appearances have no entity and are not apprehended; this is called awakening. As the Abhisaṃbodhi Tantra also says:

Awakening has the characteristic of space,
Free of all conceptual imagining.
One who wishes to realize it thus
Is called a bodhisattva.

"Lord of Secrets, what then is awakening? It is to realize one's own mind just as it truly is; and that is unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening." So it is said.

"The formidable lord": the body of union is called formidable, or the lord. The master Nāgārjuna also said:

The lord, whose nature is all bliss,
The great being, whether going or staying,
Obtains dwelling, food, clothing and the rest
From the sky in a single instant.
The self whose nature is wisdom, the lion among men,
Attains the formidable body, the cause of all beings.

Next:

Emptiness and compassion inseparable
Is what is called the awakening mind.

This is said of that very lord, union, in which luminosity and the great compassion that pervades all sentient beings are not different and are indivisible. Accordingly, the master Nāgārjuna said:

Knowing wisdom and compassion as one,
One engages in them as a pair:
This is what is called union.
This stage is the domain of the buddhas.

Now, in order to explain the meaning of "the being of awareness":

By the vajra of body, speech and mind,
Indivisible...

Here, the self that consists of the three appearances, body, speech and mind, is called the being. "By the vajra" refers to awareness, that is, the ultimate truth. "Indivisible" means that from these arises the being of awareness, endowed with the supreme of all aspects and the rest; it is certain.

Regarding the lines

By the vajra of body, speech and mind,
The very entity of the indivisible divided,

body, speech and mind are the three syllables. "By the vajra" refers to the moon, the sun and the lotus, and to what at the end becomes a vajra from the syllables. From the entity of these, distinguished one by one, the form of the being is generated; the meaning is that the being is generated from the awareness of the three syllables. This is what it teaches: in the provisional sense, in accordance with the sādhanas, the form body of all the mantras of the stages of the four kinds of yoga is generated as Vajradhara; in the definitive sense, it teaches arising, out of the two truths, as the body of union.

The name of the teacher of the tantra is "the being of awareness, the awakening mind, called Vajradhara." Now, in order to show the meaning of the word "Vajradhara":

The five causes and the five results
Are explained as the vajra.

The five causes are the five consciousnesses. The five results are the five wisdoms. Because he holds the vajra that genuinely shows the five awakenings of that very reality, he is Vajradhara; this is the provisional meaning. The five causes are conventional truth. The five results are ultimate truth. Consciousness, or mind, is the three appearances, and "vajra" indicates the result, ultimate truth.

"Dhara" is known as "holding."

This means that because he holds the vajra wisdom whose nature is the two truths, he is Vajradhara; this is the definitive meaning. The master Nāgārjuna also said:

The samādhi of self-blessing,
And those that are the abode of luminosity,
By the distinction of cause and result,
Are taught as the two truths.

Or else, to show another position:

The five causes and the five results
Are taught as the vajra.

This means: these causes and results, of outer and inner nature, the appearing objects and the faculties, are by nature luminous, and therefore they are without entity. Hence phenomena do not exist, and their nature of reality does not exist either, because phenomena do not exist. Therefore this delightful luminosity of consciousness, like space, whose nature is selflessness, is what is called the vajra; and because one holds it, one is called the holder.

In order to show the awakening mind of Vairocana:

Beyond existence, nonexistence and the middle,
Arisen from the elements and what derives from the elements,
It is the body of all sentient beings.

"From birth" means being free of all things that make up the container world. The lines

Arisen from the elements and what derives from the elements,
It is the body of all sentient beings,

refer again to the aggregates, and mean that grasped and grasper, whose nature is the elements and sense sources, are entirely abandoned.

Beyond existence, nonexistence and the middle

means this: the phenomenon that is by nature luminous, selfless, sameness, nondual, is existent, yet not permanent; it is nonexistent, yet not annihilated; and it is the sameness of phenomena that is beyond the middle between them.

"Victor over birth" is said because one's own mind, unborn from the very beginning, the body of the three indivisible vajras, the essence of emptiness, is merely the domain of the eye. As for "jinajik": because he has the character of a victor, he is the Victor; because he is victorious over the māras with the twelve-spoked wheel of Dharma, he is the Victor; this is the provisional meaning. Because he overpowers the four māras with the vajra-like samādhi, he is victorious, and so he is the Victor; this is the definitive meaning.

In order to show the awakening mind of Ratnasambhava, there is "By feeling, through feeling." Feelings are those with defilements and those without defilements. "Definitively feeling" is said because, since feeling is without entity and without sign, the characteristic of phenomena is not established.

Then how is the body of the deity generated?

Because all aims arise from it,
The mind is called the jewel.

This means that from that selfless phenomenon, the ultimate truth, everything arises through the stage of self-blessing: it arises like the precious wish-fulfilling jewel, through the force of former propulsion. As for "ratnadhrika": because he holds the jewel that completely fulfills all wishes, he is the Jewel Holder; this is the provisional meaning. Because he holds the jewel of the true limit of reality, he is the Jewel Holder; this is the definitive meaning.

In order to show the awakening mind of Amitābha, there is "By the basis of characterization, the characteristic and the entity." The bases of characterization are the conditioned and the unconditioned. "Characteristic": if the nature of reality of phenomena is not established, where could their characteristics be? It is like the green or white color of a barren woman's son. "In all" means that there is nothing to be meditated on. "By all" means that there is no meditation.

"In every way" is said because there is no intrinsic nature of meditation or of what is to be meditated on.

Then how does it become the yoga of the deity?

The characteristic of joy and the basis of characterization...

Here, "the characteristic of joy" is the joy of perception, that is, conventional truth. "The basis of characterization" is that, ultimately, conditioned and unconditioned phenomena are without intrinsic nature. From joining these two as one, the body of the deity arises. As for "āroḷka": "ā" means without remainder; "ro" is saṃsāra; "ḷka" means passing beyond it; hence it is ārolik; this is the provisional meaning. "ā" means without remainder; "ro" is the three wisdoms; therefore it means proceeding to the ultimate truth; this is the definitive meaning.

In order to show the awakening mind of Amoghasiddhi: "consciousness" is conventional truth. "That which brings forth to the utmost end" is ultimate truth. To perceive outer and inner things as one in essential nature with the two truths is wisdom.

When the being of formations holds...

This means that the mind, the being of formations, realizes and holds the tantra of realization and the two awakenings that are the essential nature of the result, cause and result being indivisible. The very full knowing of cause and result as indivisible is called the Wisdom Holder. As for "prajñādhraka": because he holds the characteristic of thoroughly holding the Dharma, he is the Wisdom Holder; this is the provisional meaning. Wisdom is the moon, appearance; because he holds the subtle element, he is the Wisdom Holder; this is the definitive meaning.

Now, in teaching the meaning of the family of aversion and the rest, first, in order to show the basis of all the families:

The family is explained as what is called continuum;
The continuum is said to be the beginning.

The family is what is called the continuum of one's own mind. The nature of one's own mind, the illusory wisdom appearing in correct conventional truth since beginningless time, is called beginningless, and is called family, stream and continuum. When that very mind is realized correctly as utterly pure, it is called the true family.

That which is indestructible and unborn...

Here, the continuum of mind that is ultimately unborn is called indestructible because, conventionally, the continuum of mind appears like an illusion. Therefore that indestructible, unborn mind is called the lord, the protector, the support, the cause, or the beginning. This is what it teaches: from realizing that the continuum of one's own mind, which appears like an illusion, is ultimately without arising and ceasing, all the families arise in the manner of the five wisdoms.

Now, in order to show what is called the family of aversion, there is "In both cause and result." From the cause, consciousness, the result, aversion, arises. Because it is averse to the arising of these two, through the wisdom of the sphere of reality which is unborn, consciousness itself, purified, is called aversion. This is what it teaches: the wrathful one of the means of great compassion is aversion, and his family is the family of aversion.

In order to show delusion:

Having the nature of inert matter, bound...

This means: the continuum of one's own mind appearing from emptiness as the form body of mirror-like wisdom is free of the inert matter of conceptual thought about grasped and grasper; therefore its nature is called delusion, and that peaceful character is called form and delusion. This is what it teaches: because it has a peaceful character for the sake of purifying deluded minds, it is delusion, and that is its family.

In order to show pride:

By the essence of training as a self,
Feeling is explained to be arrogance.

The very continuum of one's own mind, without arising and ceasing, through the great essence of a disciple in the wisdom of the sameness of self and all phenomena, is definitively arrogance. This is what it teaches: because it fulfills all wishes, true feeling is the wish-fulfilling jewel, and that is called its family.

In order to show attachment:

Having the characteristic of attachment to things,
Perception is truly the self of attachment.

The very continuum of one's own mind, nondual, appearing as discriminating wisdom, is attached with compassion to all things, and through the perception of true wisdom is attached to the true limit of reality; that self is what is so called. This is what it teaches: the discriminating wisdom that is thoroughly attached is attachment, and that is the family.

In order to show envy:

Having the self that is led by conditions,
Perception is always envy.

The very nondual continuum of mind, appearing as the wisdom of accomplishing activity, led by the condition of the pledge of great compassion, has the nature of delivering sentient beings; that true formation, and the envy that gives no opportunity to others to work for the benefit of beings, is what is so called. That very one is called the family of the pledge.

Now, in order to sum up the meaning thus taught:

In all of existence and its origin,
The essential nature of the awakening mind...

This means that although all the characteristics of existence, attachment and the rest, appear to arise from the desire of the mind, ultimately the characteristic of the awakening mind is explained as unborn. With this the meaning of the conclusion on the five families is summed up.

Now, in order to show the meaning of the mantras of the four consorts, beginning with "moharatī," the passage beginning "Resembling the various pledges" is spoken. In "moharatī," moha, delusion, is Vairocana; because she delights in him, she is Delusion Delight; this is the provisional meaning.

Toward the hosts of nondual wisdom
She makes pride...

This means that to take true pride in nondual wisdom, delusion purified, is bewilderment; that is Delusion Delight; this is the definitive meaning.

Mutually clasping one another
Is what is called aversion.

Aversion is Akṣobhya. Because she delights in him, she is Aversion Delight; this is the provisional meaning. Because she delights in mutual harm in the state where the affliction of aversion is purified, she is Aversion Delight; this is the definitive meaning.

The characteristic is attachment, clinging.

Attachment is Amitābha. The one whose characteristic is to cling to him and be attached to him is Pāṇḍaravāsinī; this is the provisional meaning. Clinging to the purified characteristic of attachment is Attachment Delight; this is the definitive meaning.

The means of wisdom is explained as the vajra.

Here the vajra is Amoghasiddhi. Because she delights in him through the means of wisdom, she is Vajra Delight; this is the provisional meaning. Because of the delight of the means of vajra wisdom, she is Vajra Delight; this is the definitive meaning.

In order to show the words with which the consort gathers up the meaning of perfection:

The supreme enjoyment, perfect in joy...

This means that, through joy, that is, through practicing together with these four goddesses, one accomplishes the supreme enjoyment of the bliss of all the Tathāgatas.

The woman, perfect in supreme bliss...

This means that because she is the special lord of desire, the supreme queen accomplishes the qualities of the perfect pledge. "Delusion" and the rest are as taught above. These are the means of the buddhas, the Vajra Vehicle, which is also called the nondual vehicle.

Now, in order to show the mantras of the four great wrathful ones, the passage beginning "The self of indestructible phenomena" is spoken. "The self of indestructible phenomena": conventionally, all the phenomena of the body are like illusions, and so they are called indestructible.

The essential nature of the unborn...

This means that ultimately they are unborn and without arising. Since things thus do not pass beyond appearing as the two truths, out of the pledge of all things, and because that very one is liberated from the Yama of permanence and annihilation, it is called "yamantakṛta."

In order to show the mantra of the Invincible One (Aparājita):

The phenomena of consciousness, the eye, the self...

The phenomena that are parts of the mind of consciousness are not themselves real: ultimately they do not become the nature of reality, and the purity of grasped and grasper, because it does not pass beyond that pledge of mind, is wisdom; it is called the liberation of the mind into the true limit of reality.

In order to show the mantra of Hayagrīva:

The phenomena whose self is inexpressible...

To realize ultimately that the names spoken by speech and the conceptions of the intellect regarding form are inexpressible does not pass beyond the pledge of phenomena; therefore it is speech gone to the true limit of reality.

In order to show the meaning of Amṛtakuṇḍalin:

The phenomena whose self is nonconceptual...

To realize the phenomena of body, speech and mind as empty and at peace: "all the vajras" means that this is called the pledge of all the buddhas, not to be transgressed. "Indestructible" and the rest are words that sum up the section.

Having thus shown the definitive meaning of the four wrathful ones, now, in order to show the four wrathful ones through both the provisional and the definitive meanings: "delusion" is the darkness of bewilderment, that is, ignorance. "Through enjoying delusion" refers to true delusion, the ultimate truth; because he brings it to its end and destroys it, he is Yamāntaka; this is the provisional meaning. "Yamāntaka": Yama is the hells. The Blessed One, through the samādhi of the great wrathful one, brings them to an end and destroys them. Therefore, because the body does not go to the lower realms, he ends the body; and for that reason, ignorance being purified, the obscuration to the knowable is purified and brought to an end; this is the definitive meaning.

Through enjoying the exhaustion of faults...

This means that because he destroys the faults of the afflictions of wisdom, he exhausts those faults, and so he is Prajñāntaka; this is the provisional meaning. The self of conceptual thought is wisdom, that is, the fault. Because, through the fault of luminosity, the fault of enjoyment is exhausted, he is Prajñāntaka. Therefore, since he purifies the mind, the obscuration of the afflictions is brought to an end; this is the definitive meaning.

Through enjoying attachment and desire...

This means that through the samādhi of attachment, like a lotus unstained by water, he is not stained by the afflictions, and because he enjoys the perverse and so destroys it, he is Padmāntaka; this is the provisional meaning. The lotus is the womb. The "end" is the inside: because there the two organs of passion unite, he produces the bliss of supreme joy, the means of bliss, and so he ends perverse attachment; and therefore he ends clinging. Likewise, union is attachment, and it is brought to its end; this is the definitive meaning.

That which is the exhaustion of all afflictions...

This shows the samādhi of Amṛtakuṇḍalin. The afflictions are delusion, fault and attachment. It is what is called the wisdom in which these are all exhausted, and likewise the wisdom in which whatever actions of body, speech and mind there are have been exhausted, the wisdom in which all the conceptions of the obscuration to the knowable, the obscuration of the afflictions and the obscuration of absorption are exhausted. Thus, because all afflictions, obscurations and actions are purified, the result of great wisdom is called Amṛtakuṇḍalin.

"Ender of obstructors" is said because he brings to an end and destroys many thousands of seizing spirits; so he is Vighnāntaka; this is the provisional meaning. The obstructors are the four; because they torment the body by increasing and decreasing, they are obstructors. Because he destroys them through the dawning of the wisdom of means, he is Vighnāntaka; this is the definitive meaning.

In order to show, in connection with the four great wrathful ones, the six wrathful ones, the passage beginning "Grasping and attachment itself" is spoken. To make clear who the six wrathful ones are, it is taught as "the six, beginning with the King of Desire"; below, in the lines

The King of Desire, Blue Staff, Power, the Immovable,
The Wheel Above, and the Beautiful Harmer Below,

they are to be applied in order: Ṭakkirāja, Nīladaṇḍa, Mahābala, Acala, Uṣṇīṣacakravartin and Sumbharāja.

From the six the mind arises completely.

This means that the six consciousnesses are applied in order to the consciousnesses and so on of the six wrathful ones.

Having thus shown the thirteen deities of the introductory section and the six wrathful ones, now, in order to show that the other maṇḍalas arise incidentally, there is "Pledge vajra" and the rest. "Pledge vajra" is Sarvanivaraṇaviṣkambhin. "And the rest": the body vajra is Kṣitigarbha; the speech vajra is Lokeśvara; the mind vajra is Vajrapāṇi; the samādhi vajra is Ākāśagarbha; the victor vajra is Maitreya, the sixth. Earth is Buddhalocanā; by "and the rest," water is Māmakī; fire is Pāṇḍaravāsinī; wind is Samayatārā; space is Mañjuśrī. "The elements" are the four consorts. "What derives from the elements" are the six faculties, that is, the bodhisattvas. "Touch" refers to the goddesses. "Victor" refers to the bodhisattvas.

Whatever is the vajra of form and the rest...

These are the six: Rūpavajrā, Śabdavajrā, Gandhavajrā, Rasavajrā, Sparśavajrā and Dharmadhātuvajrā, together with Samantabhadra. Having shown the maṇḍala fully and completely up to this point, it is said that the lord of them is Vajradhara, and that is why he is explained as the Lord of the Vajra. Therefore, whereas the root tantra teaches thirteen deities, the later tantra fills them out to thirty-two deities. In order to show that such deities are emanated from the great Vajradhara, and to show that they arise from the three syllables, it is taught that from the vajra of body, speech and mind the great self brings forth all the thirty-two deities.

Now, in order to show the conduct of the yogin, the passage beginning "Arisen from means and wisdom" is spoken. Endowed with the conduct that arises from the means of yogic conduct and from the conduct of wisdom, namely with elaboration, without elaboration, and utterly without elaboration, one joins "the aggregates," that is, the thirty-two deities, in the completion stage, and by that joining, "having done so," that is, having ascertained them as the three appearances, one makes that very thing "all pure," that is, one directs devotion to luminosity.

Then, in order to show the marvelous conduct:

Having abandoned all fears...

These are the māras: the seizing spirits, the gods and the rest. "Without dread" refers to the lower realms and the like. "Lion" means that one should practice the conduct that is like a lion: realizing all phenomena as illusions, one fulfills the aims of beings with great compassion. That lion-like conduct is shown in detail in the passage beginning "Here there is nothing at all that is not to be done."

Joined in equipoise and out of equipoise...

This means that, from realizing all phenomena as illusions, there is no conceptual thought of being in equipoise or out of equipoise; because one roams about like someone completely mad, it is called the conduct of all good. That conduct is again of two kinds. The conduct of the bodhisattva is this: whether it conflicts with worldly ways or does not conflict, the mantrins engage, as far as they are able, in whatever form fulfills the aims of sentient beings. The conduct of the Tathāgata is the conduct of those jewel-like persons, the masters who expound and repeat suchness: having established the sequence of completion, those who practice by the joining that is out of equipoise transform their own essential nature in this very life.

Now, in order to show the definitive etymology of mantra:

By the faculties and the objects...

This means that the mind and mental factors, and the mind of ignorance, arising through the condition of the five faculties, the eye and the rest, and the five objects, form and the rest, are what is called "man." "'tra' has the sense of protecting" means this: having known conventionally the triad of mind, mental factors and ignorance as the three, appearance, increase of appearance and attainment of appearance; as wisdom, means and near-attainment; and as emptiness, extreme emptiness and great emptiness; at the time when one realizes the all-empty, the ultimate truth, the mind of the hundred and sixty natures is protected, and therefore it is called mantra.

In order to show the conduct of mantra:

Completely liberated from worldly conduct...

This means that, endowed with that conduct, one is freed from the various kinds of worldly conduct. And what is that?

The pledges and vows explained before...

This means that the pledges and vows taught earlier in the seventeenth chapter of the root tantra, those of the gods, nāgas, asuras, rākṣasas, piśācas, ḍākinīs and others, when practiced and guarded by the yogin, are called the conduct of mantra.

Now, in order to show recitation, the passage beginning "Each one's own essential nature" is spoken. "Each one's own" refers to body, speech, mind, jewel and the unobstructed. "Essential nature" is the culmination of the single Vajra Holder.

Contemplating the essence with the mind...

This means that one should understand the three, oṃ āḥ hūṃ, as the essence of entering, abiding and arising.

Through the divisions of the recitation of secret mantra...

This means that the emanations of each deity too are through the three divisions of body, speech and mind.

One recites, sends forth and gathers back.

This means that one sends them forth separately and performs the act of bringing them out from the maṇḍala of one's own body.

In order to show the joining in union:

The various buddhas, lords of the vajra,
Are gathered back by the seeds of wisdom.

This speaks of the bodies of the emanated deities: moving from their place, they are sent forth, and are to be established as one's own self through the nature of the aggregates and the rest; and in the state of union they are thoroughly gathered back, and one is to settle in the mind of samādhi.

Awakening, the seed of selflessness:
The one with the discipline takes up selflessness.

This means that body, speech and mind, without entity and selfless, are awakening. "Discipline" means that one is to take up, send forth and make clear the special wisdom whose nature is discrimination, as selflessness.

Various, likewise, the various are sent forth.

This means that through the divisions of body, speech and mind one brings them out and sends them forth from the maṇḍala of one's own body, as above.

One recites with the seeds of the three times.

This means: the seed of the body vajra, oṃ, practiced without a grasped thing or a grasping thing, is the recitation of the body. The seed of the speech vajra, āḥ, is the full awakening of articulate and inarticulate sound. The seed of the mind vajra, hūṃ: to seek through full awakening to the mind just as it is, is the recitation of the mind.

Having thus explained mental recitation, now, in order to show verbal recitation:

Recitation is known as speaking aloud.

This means that, at times such as expelling according to the pledge of the wrathful ones Yamāntaka and the rest, one proclaims and speaks the sound with one's own tongue, so that oneself or others hear it. "All speech is recitation" refers to the words of mantra beginning "emanate." "Mantra is invoking" means proclaiming the sound with the tongue and uttering it aloud.

Speaking that very thing as an urging...

This means urging the maṇḍala of one's own deity.

In order to show the meaning of sealing:

Just as mind and the rest are consecrated...

This means that, having consecrated body, speech and mind, one generates the wisdom being; and the samādhi being should likewise be understood. Having thus sealed with the four seals, in order to show how the crown ornament is done:

Those are empowered at the crown...

This means placing a deity at the crown of each deity.

Likewise, offering on all sides.

This means that, having made offerings to the empowering deities, one supplicates them and confers empowerment.

Of whoever is Vajradhara,
the application that possesses awareness,
and self-consecration and the rest, likewise...

This means that, for the deities of the maṇḍala, it is as taught in the fifteenth chapter of the root tantra: glorious Vajrasattva empowers the head of Vajradhara. The awareness consorts, Locanā and the rest, are each empowered by Vairocana and the others. Likewise, the vajra family is empowered by Akṣobhya. The tathāgata family is empowered by Body Vajra. The lotus family is empowered by Speech Vajra. The jewel family is empowered by Ratnasambhava. The action family is empowered by Amoghasiddhi. Therefore the consecration of oneself with the crown ornament should be understood in the same way.

Likewise, know them as the four enjoyments.

This refers to the four seals. Having thus shown the crown ornament of the maṇḍala, now, in order to show the crown ornament of the solitary hero, there is the passage beginning:

Of the solitary hero, the vajra...

The mode of the crown ornament is also threefold. "Heart essence" means the three, oṃ āḥ hūṃ. The seal is the body of the deity's form. The mantra is vajradhṛka and the rest. "Self-awareness" has already been explained. "Result" means Akṣobhya and the rest. "Cause" means Vajradhara.

In order to show the source of phenomena, there is the passage beginning:

Fully endowed with the four enjoyments...

"The four enjoyments" means Body Vajra, Speech Vajra, Mind Vajra, and the wisdom being. Then "fully endowed" and the rest. "The person of awareness" means the great Vajradhara. "Triangle" means that one meditates on the distinction of body, speech and mind as a triangular source of phenomena; and in order to meditate on the wheel of command outside it:

The wheel entirely yellow...

One meditates on the source of phenomena in the center, and in its center one meditates on the person of awareness, Vajradhara.

On all the spokes, the ten wrathful ones.

This means that one meditates on the wrathful ones, Yamāntaka and the rest, on the ten spokes as the maṇḍala of meditative concentration; by commanding the guardians of the directions, Indra and the rest, those who make obstructions are to be guarded against and turned back; and it is in order to guard the minds of the practitioners against distraction. "The vajra of cessation" means abiding one-pointedly.

It turns, equal to the unmoving.

This means that the wheel turns with the deities unmoving. "Buddha" means the wisdom of the buddhas.

Now, in order to show the summary of the tantra:

Teaching the Dharma of Vajradhara...

Because he holds the vajra, he is the Vajra Holder: this is the literal meaning. Because he holds the vajra by way of the nature of the five wisdoms, the mirror-like and the rest, he is the Vajra Holder: this is the general meaning. Because he holds a body, a form whose nature is mind, he is the Vajra Holder: this is the hidden meaning. The vajra is nondual wisdom; because he holds it as his essential nature, he is the Vajra Holder: this is the ultimate meaning.

The stage of generation itself, and...

This means the following: first, the ascertainment of the place; focusing on great compassion; focusing on the wheel of command and the wheel of protection; focusing on emptiness; generating the ground and the celestial palace; generating the deities through intense devotion; placing them in the ultimate maṇḍala; then again focusing on the sun; finally focusing on the moon disc; generating the great Vajradhara; consecration by mantra; purification of the aggregates and the rest; consecration of body, speech and mind; meditation on the threefold stacked being; joining vajra and lotus and consecrating them; passionate union; the offering mantras; the inner maṇḍala; the emanated maṇḍala; verbal recitation; vajra recitation; entering the ultimate; urging; praise; withdrawal; the rites of supreme devotion to mantra; the rites of supreme devotion to the inner; the meditation on food; and accomplishing the eight common accomplishments. These are the stage of generation.

Likewise, the stage of completion.

This means the suchness of mantra, the suchness of mudrā, the suchness of the self, and the suchness of wisdom.

"Is accomplishment" means the drawn maṇḍala, the sets of rites, the eight accomplishments, and the accomplishing of buddhahood itself. "Diligence" means urging the heart essence of one's own deity, whose nature is outer and inner, in order to make the desired accomplishment manifest. "Likewise" refers to the fifth, seventh, ninth and seventeenth chapters of the root tantra. "Pledges and vows" means yogic conduct, wondrous conduct, and the pledges and vows.

All these were set out earlier in detail,
divided by the distinction of stages.

This means that they were clearly explained earlier in those chapters of the root tantra. It teaches this: the summary of the tantra, namely the stage of generation, the stage of completion, accomplishment in the maṇḍala, urging the heart essence with diligence, yogic conduct, wondrous conduct, the pledges, and the vows, divided by the distinction of stages, were taught earlier in the root tantra. This is the summary that condenses the meaning of the tantra.

In order to show the meditation of the three, attachment, aversion and delusion, the passage beginning:

Desires, form, sound and the rest...

is spoken. Pleasure is attachment. Suffering is aversion.

Those desires are equal to the pledge.

This means that all things are declared to be empty, luminous, and the pledge.

Now, in order to show both the true aspect and the false aspect:

With aspect and without aspect.

The three syllables are the three, oṃ āḥ hūṃ. The three grounds are body, speech and mind.

"The pure secret" means the three families. Having thus briefly taught the samādhi by way of the three, now, in order to explain those very things in detail, there is the line:

The yogin, by the application of aversion...

On this: when Akṣobhya is generated from the transformation of the great Vajradhara, one visualizes a vajra and the rest in his hands and generates the deities of the maṇḍala. One visualizes light rays streaming from the vajra in his hand, and focuses on the tathāgatas who have arisen in the three times; by it they are ground to powder and destroyed. Through such emanation and withdrawal one becomes capable of the set of fierce rites and attains mastery over the mind.

The yogin, by the application of delusion,
having generated the wheel of delusion...

This means that one visualizes Vairocana as the principal of the maṇḍala and emanates the retinue, Akṣobhya and the rest. Whatever ornaments of jewels and the like there are, matching the color of whichever tathāgata it may be, each of these is to be drawn forth and urged. Having thus fully sent forth all the tathāgatas, the practitioner binds the desired kind of jewel with nooses of white light rays and the rest and makes it immovable. Then, in order to draw it in with the hook of the syllable hri that arises from the lotus of one's own tongue and place it in one's own house, there is the line:

Completely filled with every jewel.

In order to purify the stain of the affliction of avarice, there is the line:

Meditate on the ocean of the mind of delusion.

Rain down a shower of giving.

This means that one should then rain down a shower of giving upon the needy and give to them.

Having generated the wheel of attachment...

This means that from the transformation of the great Vajradhara one generates Amitābha, and the deities of the maṇḍala are purified and emanated throughout the inside of the celestial palace.

By carrying off all the goddesses...

This means that, preceded by the deities of the maṇḍala, father and mother, delighting one another and so forth, one joins vajra and lotus in union and enjoys bliss; and by sporting oneself, one should partake of it for a moment. To show its qualities, it is taught by the passage beginning:

Of those arising in the three realms...

Now, in order to show the purpose of engaging in that conduct: "And the maṇḍala" means the emanated maṇḍala of the stage of generation, and the maṇḍala of body, speech and mind in union, elaborated in the stage of completion. "And empowerment" means the secret, the wisdom-knowledge, and the fourth. "The supreme path, the accumulations" means the accumulation of supreme devotion to the inner. "The progress of accomplishment" means that these are accomplished in reliance on the seal. This is the provisional meaning. The definitive meaning is explained in the ninth chapter of the Revelation of the Intention (Sandhivyākaraṇa). It teaches this: the pledge of aversion is that ignorance becomes luminosity, turning into a body whose nature is wisdom. The pledge of delusion is what accomplishes perfect awakening. The pledge of attachment, it teaches, is to join with the four emptinesses and then to unite the lord of the two truths in union.

The vajra net, the source of jewels...

This means that, from the transformation of the great Vajradhara, one visualizes Ratnaśrī and emanates the deities of the maṇḍala so that they fill the inside of the celestial palace. One should speak with harsh, merciless words, so that every aim gathered in them may come about. This is the provisional meaning.

Words that teach the nonexistence of the aggregates and the rest,
false speech whose meaning is the truth, and the rest:
if these are consecrated, one will attain awakening.

This is the Tathāgata's meaning.

The pledge is said to be like the jewel...

This means that from the transformation of the great Vajradhara one visualizes Amoghasiddhi, and below him one should visualize the whole maṇḍala. Then one visualizes all sentient beings as abiding in that very state as well. Since for them all phenomena are like illusions, one abides in the meaning of the vajra words, "All phenomena are false"; and visualizing oneself speaking words to all the tathāgatas and all sentient beings with the essential nature of a deceiver is the provisional meaning.

Whoever holds that all these,
outer, inner, and the great self, are false,
is freed from the bonds of conceptual thought: this is awakening.
This is the pledge of Amoghasiddhi.

This is the definitive meaning.

In order to show the meaning of the maṇḍala:

Known as the bhaga maṇḍala, and...

This means the maṇḍala abiding within and the maṇḍala emanated outward.

The maṇḍala of the awakening mind.

This means the ultimate maṇḍala.

Called the maṇḍala of the body, and...

This means the maṇḍala of the body, the arrangement of the thirty-two deities on the body of Vajradhara.

In order to show the meaning of placing the seals:

The aggregates, elements and sense sources...

This is in order to show in detail the maṇḍala of the body mentioned above; that very placing of the deities on the body is called placing the seals. Now, in order to place in the maṇḍala the deities of the maṇḍala taught above, the seals need to be shown. The thirteen deities are taught in the fourth chapter, and in order to show the seals of the rest of them, the passage beginning "the hook, the staff" teaches the seals of the six wrathful ones, the eight bodhisattvas, the five goddesses, and the four consorts.

In order to show the meaning of the word "flower":

The nine goddesses, and...

These are the wind called life, the wind that clears downward, the wind that moves upward, the moving, the perfectly moving, the intensely moving, the definitely moving, and the all-moving. "The space element" is the pervading wind. "Body, speech and mind" are the winds characterized by entering, abiding and rising. "The order of the families" means that these very winds are to be known as the three syllables, and it should be understood that they teach the mantras of the three families.

In order to show the meaning of the word "stupa": the celestial palace of the maṇḍala, made of the four kinds of precious substance, is like a stupa, and so of it the text says:

The stupa is explained to be
the palace where all the buddhas dwell.

In order to show the meaning of the wheel of wisdom:

Whatever the wisdom being touches...

This means that the wheel of all the tathāgatas, by the light rays of the seed at the heart, touches the wisdom maṇḍala. In order to show what that seed is:

The three unchanging vajra syllables.

This teaches oṃ āḥ hūṃ.

In order to show the four, urging and the rest: "The reality of body, speech and mind" is the three syllables. "To be awakened" means that by the command of the great Vajradhara one supplicates and urges; this is done by Yamāntaka.

In the world-realms of the ten directions...

This means joining the holy ones to that very pledge and accomplishing the purpose of the pledge; this is Amṛtakuṇḍalin, and the showing of the pledge.

Inviting all the vajra-holders...

This means invoking the pledge: from that pledge one invokes them, joining it with their names, and makes them bound by the pledge through the Invincible One (Aparājita).

Of that abode of the vajra-holders...

This means binding the pledge: Hayagrīva binds them there, joins them firmly by fierce means, and they are bound.

In order to show the meaning of empowerment:

By the vajra of body, speech and mind,
in the maṇḍala of body, speech and mind...

This means the maṇḍalas of Vairocana, Amitābha and Akṣobhya.

Empowerment is divided into three kinds;
in this tantra it is well examined.

This is the summary.

The vase empowerment is the first...

This means that, having given the awareness empowerments, one is to confer the master empowerment. Moreover, according to the guru's oral instructions, it is preceded by the rite of bringing the disciple in: the binding on the head of that very deity together with it; the untying of the face covering, which is the showing of the maṇḍala; bathing with the vase transformed into Akṣobhya; empowering with the crown ornament visualized as Ratnasambhava; empowering with the vajra visualized as Amitābha; empowering with the bell visualized as Amoghasiddhi; showing the mirror; empowering by visualizing Vairocana at the crown of the head; and giving the pledges of the five families. Having conferred the master empowerment, one shows that disciple the maṇḍala and the suchness of the deities. Then there are the observance, the prophecy as Śākyamuni, the encouragement by the Vajra Holder, the authorization, and the rest. These nine empowerments, the awareness empowerments conferred in the maṇḍala emanated as the great Vajradhara, are the rite of the vase empowerment.

In order to explain in detail "the secret empowerment in the second," it is taught by the passage beginning:

The wide-eyed one, fit to be a vessel of mantra...

Because she is fit to be a vessel of mantra, the wide-eyed one is the mudrā woman, who is nondual. "Flower" means the self-arisen. "Seed" means semen. "The secret empowerment" means that this rite, characterized by the union of method and wisdom, is called the secret offering of suchness, because it makes directly manifest the purification of the bases of the afflictions of the families of attachment, freedom from attachment, and middling attachment. "The mantrin" is the master. "Give to the disciples" means as it will come below. "The sphere of space" means: from the meeting of the master, father and mother, in the space element of vajra and lotus; "urine and marrow," that is, the seed; and "fully endowed" means combining the latter nectars.

It teaches this: following the rite offered by the disciple, just as they abide in the beautiful body, one consecrates the seeds, oṃ and the rest, as the five families, Vairocana and the rest. Then, by the rite of union, one places the awakening mind in a conch cup or the like, mixes it with urine, and, preceded by verses of auspiciousness and the like, one is to empower the disciple:

The awakening mind, the blazing jewel drawn forth,
upon the bodies of all the Victors without exception,
the water of wisdom arisen from the pure vajra:
with this stainless jewel poured on the crown, I empower you.

The disciple, too, with a resolve that does not waver from awakening, should partake of it, thinking, "I shall satisfy the tathāgatas who abide in my own body." "Eat" means one should partake of it; one tastes it. The guru, too, teaches just as it is the conventional instruction illustrated by the twelve similes of illusion, the instruction of all the buddhas that has come down through the lineage of gurus.

Having thus shown the secret empowerment, now, in order to show the supremely secret empowerment:

By the union of vajra and lotus,
all the vajra-holders are fully gathered.

This means that one generates the vajra arisen from hūṃ and the lotus from a, and emanates light rays at the junction of vajra and lotus. All the buddhas of the ten directions assembled, the thirty-two deities abiding in the maṇḍala of one's own four bodies, are gathered into one's heart, sent forth by the vajra path in the form of the seed, and should be let fall into the disciple's mouth.

The supreme state of empowerment...

This means that this is the rite of the even more supremely secret empowerment. Its qualities are taught by the passage beginning "supreme king of actions." Moreover, the Candraguhyatilaka says:

Drawn out from the vajra and lotus,
the thick one and the lord of empowerment
should be placed in the vajra-holder.
Permanent, joyful, great bliss:
oh, uttering this pledge,
oh, one should take up the vajra.
That is called the secret,
the supreme method of empowerment.
The vajra master, the great being,
places the vajra in his own mouth;
when the great being draws it forth,
the bliss of the mind's awakening is attained.
This is the supremely secret,
the bliss of empowerment.
To disciples who do not understand
none of this should be proclaimed.
What the vajra master does
he should do after examining it again and again.
As soon as he is empowered by this,
the disciple becomes a lord of the assembly,
and blissfully becomes the very master
of the buddha, the Vajra Holder.
The pure lord of the consorts
is declared to be the vajra of the buddhas.
He comes to the conduct of sky-going,
lord of the mountains and of all beings.
Therefore the wisdom-knowledge empowerment
should be given to the supreme disciple.

Having thus conferred the secret empowerment, now, for the wisdom-knowledge empowerment, it is taught by the passage beginning:

Supremely faithful and of great wisdom...

The characteristics of the wisdom consort are great faith, great wisdom, a beautiful form, devotion to the practitioner, familiarity with yoga, and possession of the pledges. She is offered to the guru; the guru consecrates her as will come below, and gives her to the practitioner by the rite that will come below.

The method of consecration is taught in detail:

The deluded one joins himself to delusion...

This means that the yogin visualizes delusion as Vairocana and consecrates the consort as Delusion Delight; if he confers the empowerment abiding in the continuum of Vairocana's wisdom, he becomes Vairocana.

In order to show the observance of awareness, the rite of giving the wisdom consort to the disciple is taught:

That very goddess of awareness...

This means whichever consort was consecrated above. "In the hand" means that the consort's hand is placed in the disciple's hand and the master's hand is placed on the disciple's head. The verses of instruction to the disciple are taught by the passage beginning:

By no other method does one become a buddha...

Their meaning is this: the disciple, afterwards, by the stages of outer awakening and inner awakening, makes manifest the wisdom of luminosity by the sequence of attachment, freedom from attachment, and middling attachment, and accomplishes the great seal; for this one should obtain the wisdom-knowledge empowerment. The fourth I have not explained.

Now, in order to show the method of eating the nectars, on the line:

In the dharma of the ordinary body...

The substances that come from oneself, the five, namely excrement, urine, semen, blood and spittle, are to be consecrated as the five wisdoms and eaten. "Kindle, and likewise burn" means that above a wind maṇḍala from yaṃ one imagines a fire maṇḍala from raṃ, and from a one visualizes a lotus kapāla, a skull cup. Then the wind makes the fire blaze, and the fire boils the nectars, so that they take the form of nectar. "The vajra arisen from hūṃ" means that in the sky, above a sun, one visualizes a five-pronged vajra arisen from hūṃ and marked with hūṃ, and the nectar itself marked with the syllable oṃ; it is gathered by the light rays of oṃ and becomes nectar by the light rays of hūṃ. From the place of union of oneself as father and mother, too, light rays like these are emanated; the wisdom nectar of the ten directions is drawn in with hooks and falls into the nectar, and one should eat it.

One should eat by the application of eating.

This means that one who possesses the yoga of one's own deity purifies the gullet and the throat. In order to show the five heroes:

By the rite of the accomplishment to be accomplished...

The rite of the five nectars was taught above. By the rite, the five heroes, that is, the five meats, are to be imagined as oṃ āḥ hūṃ and eaten.

What is the lowest accomplishment like?

This shows the meaning: on "the accomplishments of invisibility and the rest," invisibility, the higher knowledges, the knowledge-holder, the commissioning of servants, and the sets of rites are ordinary accomplishments, shared in common with gods, humans, yakṣas and the rest. The explanatory tantra, the Questions of the Four Goddesses (Caturdevīparipṛcchā), says:

The accomplishment of pills and of eye salve,
the accomplishment of the underworld and of the sword,
flying in the sky, and eye salve,
deathlessness, and the destruction of disease:
these are known as the eight accomplishments.

In order to show the supreme accomplishment: "Buddhahood itself is accomplished" means that the accomplishment of the great seal, the nondual wisdom characterized by union, endowed with twenty-one branches, is called "the supreme accomplishment of the buddhas." It is explained that it is spoken of as the tantra of the result, called the inalienable.

Because the Guhyasamāja tantra is known as being a tantra of method, there is the line:

Method is of four kinds.

On this:

In all the yoga tantras...

This means all the tantras that teach nonduality.

The rite of approach is the first...

This passage and what follows means that Bodhicittavajra taught that the yoga of the two stages, the stage of generation and the stage of completion, is included in the four of approach and accomplishment. In order to explain in detail the four of approach and accomplishment in the manner of the two stages, on the lines:

By the distinction of common and supreme,
approach is taught to be of two kinds.

By generating Vajrasattva, who is to be pleased by the sequence of the four yogas, one accomplishes the common accomplishments; and by generating the great Vajradhara of the stage of completion through the six branches, one accomplishes the supreme accomplishment; therefore it is said to be twofold. Here, this tantra teaches the four of approach and accomplishment of the stage of generation and the four of approach and accomplishment of the stage of completion. First, the approach and accomplishment of completion is taught. First, approach is twofold: the common approach, the four vajras of Vajradhara; and the supreme approach, which teaches the supreme nectar of wisdom.

In order to show the four vajras, on the line:

First, emptiness, awakening...

One meditates on emptiness, beginning with meditating on the absence of entities; having generated the form of the great seal by the arranging of syllables, beginning with arranging the three syllables, one arranges syllables on the sense sources; and the deities are generated by the syllables of body, speech and mind, the samādhi being, the body of the consort, the syllables of vajra and lotus, the syllables of emanation, and the rest. This is the provisional meaning. Those who abide in the supreme accomplishment, too, should strive, for the four kinds of vajra are the four kinds of emptiness; with them one should perform the approach, in order to attain the state of the great Vajradhara.

In order to show the supreme nectar of wisdom, it is taught by the passage beginning:

The supreme wisdom, the nectar itself...

The supreme wisdom is the wisdom of luminosity. "The nectar itself" means what is accomplished by the drop of seed. It teaches that the method for it is accomplished through the six-branched yoga, and is not accomplished by any other. In order to show the six-branched yoga, the passage beginning "withdrawal, and" is the brief teaching. To teach it in detail: "the ten senses" means the five senses and the objects of the senses; both are called senses. "Their own field of engagement" means that the objects and subjects of the ten senses engage as the nature of the grasped and the grasper; since they engage there, it is their own field of engagement. "All" is by the distinction of inferior, middling and supreme. "By desire" means that, because they are to be desired and clung to, they are "the desirable," namely form and the rest. On "withdrawal": because the senses gather in and take up those objects, each separately, again and again, it is called withdrawal. This is the explanation of the first branch.

In order to purify withdrawal, and to show the second branch, the text speaks of "the five desirables." The five desirables, which have become the objects of the five senses, are form and the rest. "In brief" means having become one with the five senses.

By joining them as the five buddhas...

This refers to the eye and the rest. Their joining is a perfect joining, so it is a joining as the five buddhas; therefore form and the rest, too, are buddhas.

It is explained as meditative concentration with examination.

"It is explained" means that it is explained as the completely pure meditative concentration that has come about in this way. This, moreover, becomes fivefold by the division of examination and the rest; the passage beginning "examination, and" is the brief teaching. In order to show the divisions in detail, on the line:

Examination arising from the three secrets...

The three secrets are the five senses, the objects, and the sense consciousnesses: these are the three secrets. Examination is examining that the five senses, the sense consciousnesses and their objects have the nature of the five tathāgatas. "Of its enjoyment" means that engaging in that very thing is engaging in abiding. Having engaged in this way, whatever is directed toward entering suchness, whatever is characterized by bliss, is "like it." Whatever bliss is attained by having entered suchness, characterized by the complete purification of the body and the rest, is "gathered by bliss." In order that purification and the rest be brought to their utmost culmination, on the line:

Where knower and known that have arisen are exhausted...

The knower is the engaging consciousness of the six, the eye and the rest; the known, which has arisen, ranges from form up to the element of phenomena; the known arises from the knower. "Exhausted" means the emptiness of grasped and grasper by the mind. Therefore it is said that in the one-pointed mind characterized by wisdom one should discern one's own mind. Such a mind, on the line:

The fourth, the reality of all the buddhas...

means that mere appearance and the all-empty become one and reach their culmination. These are the five divisions of the second branch.

In order to show vajra recitation, the passage beginning:

The breath whose nature is the five wisdoms...

is spoken. It is the very breath of the five wisdoms, the mirror-like and the rest; and since that very breath on the right is the mirror-like and the rest, it is said:

The essential nature of the five elements.

"Sent out in the form of a ball" means that, again having the nature of that very thing and the rest, it is sent out from the opening of its own vajra, and at the tip of the lotus of the nose, in the form of a ball, that is, in the form of a drop of awakening mind, one should visualize it.

Of five jewel colors...

This means that that very breath, going upward, has five colors and has the nature of the five tathāgatas. "By that very thing, the life and exertion of the jewel" means that one imagines a short a from the lotus at one's own heart.

Place the life as having become a drop.

This means that it is set in the conception of the indestructible. This is the fourth branch.

"Its own power" means one's own form and the rest. "The senses" are the eye and the rest. There, the two senses are stopped, and their wind definitely dissolves. "Stopping the jewel" means that below this is the jewel that is the support of objects and senses: the jewel of mind, together with life and exertion, is stopped and has set, and whatever is held then is explained as retention. If one asks what that is, it is the limit of reality. These teach the fourth branch, called retention.

The mind becomes the vajra of cessation.

The vajra of cessation is luminosity. To have become that is for the mind to have become that.

The close grasping of signs will arise.

The close grasping of signs means grasping signs closely, that is, appearing as signs. "Will arise" means will be born.

The element of signs is of five kinds:
so Bodhivajra has taught.

From earth having dissolved into water comes what is like a mirage: this is the first sign. Likewise, from water having dissolved into fire comes what is like smoke: this is the second. From fire having dissolved into wind comes something like the sky appearing: this is the third. From the subtle element that exists as the three appearances comes the essential nature of a cluster appearing like a lamp: this is the fourth. From this having dissolved into the universal appearance of self and other, what becomes mere luminosity, the ever-present appearance with the nature of a cloudless sky, is the fifth. Through these five signs nirvāṇa is attained. As the Analysis of the Limits of Action (Karmāntavibhaṅga) says:

Earth first dissolves into water;
water dissolves into fire;
fire dissolves into wind;
wind, too, enters consciousness;
and that very consciousness, the grasped,
enters luminosity as well.

In order to show recollection: having become stable and purified, and entering luminosity by the sequence of the five signs without passing out of the vajra path:

One should emanate into the element of space.

This means one should emanate into the world-realms and pervade them in the manner of the dharma body.

Whatever recollection one cultivates...

This means having cultivated and made manifest what has thus become clear.

One should emanate in the same way.

This means that one should emanate and generate in just the way, and by just the sequence, in which one previously attained the limit of reality through recollection, in the aspects like a mirage and the rest. What has just been explained is to be called recollection and is to be understood.

From that, the individual appearance arises.

The individual appearance means that in that conventional the branch of recollection arises, and not otherwise.

In order to show samādhi, the text speaks of "method and wisdom": this is by the union of the conventional and the ultimate. "All things, in brief" means abiding as the moving and the unmoving. "In the manner of a ball" means that, having made them one in the form of the great seal, one should know to visualize, in the midst of that moving and unmoving, the form of the great Vajradhara whose nature is union. "By this very sequence, in a moment" means in a mere instant. "Perfecting wisdom" means perfecting the wisdom body; this is samādhi, the sixth branch.

Now, in order to show the qualities of the six-branched yoga, there is the passage beginning:

If one is absorbed in withdrawal,
all the mantras are consecrated...

This is easy to understand.

Having thus taught approach, now, for near accomplishment, it is taught by the passage beginning:

That essence at the heart...

Here "essence" means that one should meditate on a moon at the heart, lodged in the center of the heart of each of the deities of the maṇḍala, and on the wisdom being abiding there.

Looking at the union of moon and vajra...

This means that the wisdom beings are made to enter the moons at the hearts of those deities, relying on their respective hand emblems.

The mind is made into the substance of mantra...

This means that on the hand emblem, the vase, one should imagine one's own mind as the word of the supreme, unchanging syllable.

Having drawn them in with the supreme weapon...

This means that then the lord and the rest, whose nature is the three beings, with the mass of light rays of the members of the maṇḍala become one and turned into something like a hook, should urge on the one known as the three beings at the heart of the one to be accomplished. Having thus struck with the vajra hook, after the generation of what is to be set up for the practitioner, in order to command Śumbharāja, there is the line:

In the manner of the form of mantra...

This means that one imagines summoning King Śumbha before oneself and having him receive the command.

One should recite the verse of awakening.

This means that the rite of commanding, in which one has Śumbha say, "Swiftly bestow the special accomplishment of awakening upon the practitioner of all the tathāgatas," is the rite given above. "One's own maṇḍala" means one's own principal deity. "That mind is the form of wisdom" shows the rousing of one's own deity by way of the completion stage. Having made the aggregates and the rest disappear through the perfection of wisdom, and finally having made the relative and ultimate truths one, one should meditate on union: this is near accomplishment. It shows the meaning of the tenth chapter.

Now, in order to teach accomplishment, the passage beginning:

As long as it is seen...

is spoken. Its condensed meaning is the samādhi of enjoying the objects of desire, the rite of extension, and the fierce rite. Of these, in order to teach the samādhi of enjoying the objects of desire in brief, "as long as it is seen" refers to the common and the supreme accomplishments. "Six months" means that, through the samādhi that follows below, one should enjoy the objects of desire for six months. Throughout all the boundless and limitless world systems, one should fully conceive all the nectars said to exist, divided into superior, middling and inferior, as Rūpavajrā and the rest, and engage in conduct with the three elaborations, by means of the mantra body and the wisdom body.

As for the line:

If in six months...

it means: if accomplishment is not attained through the six-month observance of awareness. In order to teach the rite of extension:

One should undertake the vow three times.

This means that one should carry out the conduct of the three elaborations for eighteen months. If even by that unsurpassed awakening is not accomplished, then, in order to teach the fierce rite:

One should accomplish the fierce application.

This is taught by the lines:

Through the application of the division of families,
one should strike with the vajra kīla, with the kīla.

By uniting vajra and lotus equally, one should strike with the kīla. This is what it shows. Endowed with the application of Vairocana, one places Buddhalocanā before oneself, and embracing and entwining each other with the arms and so forth is the rite of the body. Endowed with the application of Amitābha, one places Pāṇḍaravāsinī before oneself, meditates on a five-pronged vajra at the bhaga of the goddess, the lotus from which the three, appearance, increase of appearance and attainment of appearance, arise, and makes it enter within the source of phenomena that is there. Endowed with the application of Akṣobhya, one places Māmakī before oneself, meditates on one's own vajra as five-pointed, and if, with one's own vajra, one strikes and pounds the seat endowed with the fire maṇḍala lodged within the source of phenomena of one's own consort, one will attain luminosity.

In order to teach the samādhi of enjoying the objects of desire, "in the manner of great attachment" means: in order to abandon attachment through attachment, having given up the body free of attachment, he emanated himself in the body of the great Vajradhara. And there too, in order to ripen and liberate sentient beings, he engaged with attachment to sense objects: it is the samādhi endowed with that manner.

One should gather those who possess the wheel of wisdom.

This means that, with the hook of light rays from one's own seed syllable, one draws in the deities of one's own maṇḍala, and they come to enter one's own body as the very nature of the aggregates, elements and so on.

Having emanated the nine goddesses...

This means that, in order to delight the Blessed One with sounds of supreme joy, they gave up their former state, manifestly emanated in the bodies of daughters of the gods, and came forth from the body of the Blessed One. As for the nine goddesses: the earth element is like Buddhalocanā, the water element like Māmakī, the fire element like Pāṇḍaravāsinī, the wind element like Tārā; Vairocana is like the nature of form, Ratnasambhava like the nature of sound, Amitābha like the nature of smell, Amoghasiddhi like the nature of taste, and Akṣobhya like the nature of touch. "Having emanated" is said in this way: this is the provisional meaning.

"In the manner of great attachment" is the samādhi of the method that makes manifest purity through the sequence of attachment, the one who is attached, and so forth.

One should gather those who possess the wheel of wisdom.

This means that, having given up the clinging that takes those outer wheels of the tathāgatas as objects, one is endowed with the blessing: "I am the body that accomplishes for all of them." The nine goddesses are as before: this is the definitive meaning.

Having thus shown, by emanating the six form maṇḍalas, the lord of desire, now, since in this tantra mind is principal, in order to show the activity of the maṇḍala gathered by Akṣobhya, it is taught by the line:

From transformation, the four mudrās...

Having set the goddesses on the body, from that transformation, at the place of wisdom, he blessed and emanated the maṇḍala of the four mudrās, square and stainless, the maṇḍala of the great pledge.

Having established oneself in the center...

This means that the great Vajradhara placed himself in the center of the maṇḍala of the great pledge just described.

One should gather all the wheel-bearers.

This means that the wheel in which the vidyā-mantras were gathered once again entered the body of Bodhicittavajra.

Having made all into the substance of vajra,
one should meditate on all as awakening.

This means Bodhicittavajra: this is the provisional meaning. "The maṇḍala of the four mudrās" is the aggregate of form, and it is the consort, the perfection of wisdom, whom they taught and made the place where they dwell. That itself is the maṇḍala; since it is the cause that gives birth to the buddhas, it is the place of birth. As the Tantra Explaining the Intention also says:

That which all the buddhas taught,
the thirteenth, which has become the ground,
is rightly explained as the consort;
it is called the bhaga of the true Dharma.

This is the definitive meaning.

Having established oneself in the center...

This means that he places himself, abiding there in that stainless great pledge.

One should gather all the wheel-bearers.

This means that the maṇḍala of the great pledge fully became luminosity.

Having made all into the substance of vajra,
at that time one should meditate on awakening.

The meaning of this is that then, after entering luminosity, the Blessed One, the great Vajradhara, whose nature is all the families, himself arose from ultimate truth and, with the essential nature in which existence and nirvāṇa are of one taste, placed the three worlds as himself in the center of the maṇḍala of the great pledge. And so it is explained in the Supreme Bliss:

In the expanse of all of space,
glorious Vajrasattva fully abides.

Having thus shown that the five tathāgatas have become of the nature of the sixth, then, in order to show that the four consorts, wishing to behold the great Vajradhara with minds of faith and longing, speak with compassion, there is the line:

By the four songs of urging...

That is, the four, Locanā and the others, urge him on, preceded by praise with graceful and melodious songs in ṣaḍja and the other notes. This is the song beginning:

You, lord of those who dwell in the realm of beings, are the vajra mind.

By the goddesses urging the essence...

This means that he was thus rightly urged on by the goddesses, who have the nature of the four abodes of Brahmā.

The mind whose nature is the vajra of the three times...

This means that he was urged on. Rising from the yoga of emptiness by the force of his former aspiration, he shows the enjoyment of attachment with the bliss of glorious supreme joy. That is, by fully understanding the essential nature of the objects, form and the rest, he makes the eye and the other senses one, and having sported with Locanā and the others in the state of all-joyous bliss, he fell silent.

One meditates on the body as wisdom.

This extends up to the words, "free from the activities of the afflictions and so forth, he abides in union with the wisdom mudrā." Then, after he has entered into union with the wisdom mudrā by way of the completion stage, this expanse of space is filled with the host of tathāgatas, Vairocana and the rest, whose essential nature is Vajradhara and who are fully endowed with the three vajras, without any gap, like a pod filled with sesame seeds, and it remains still and unmoving, like a vase of supreme joy.

Having thus shown the Blessed One's entering into union, then, in order to show his acting for the benefit of beings, there is the line:

By the four the colors are distinguished.

This means that the sentient beings dwelling in the three realms are touched by the splendor of the great Vajradhara and empowered by the vajra light rays, and all who are touched by it and made its object attain tathāgatahood itself.

Likewise, it is the essence of taste.

This means that, empowering with the vajra light rays, which is the activity of vajra wisdom, he makes his own the bliss of union of all sentient beings who experience the bliss of his own union.

The empowerment is one's own continuum.

This means that, beginning from the empowerment by the vajra light rays, they were empowered by the body, speech and mind of all the tathāgatas, and came to bear a name of unprecedented meaning: "Samantabhadra, Samantabhadra." "One's own continuum" refers to the four: through empowerment by the vajra light, the result became Samantabhadra.

The dharmas of mantra will be distinguished.

This means that from the assembly of syllables that act through attachment, one comes to realize and attain the great Vajradhara himself; it shows without doubt that attachment and the rest are purified.

And awareness, by the Dharma mudrā.

This means that the wisdom mudrā is also shown by another name, the Dharma mudrā. Or else, since that very wisdom mudrā is entirely pure and enters into the all-empty, it is called "of many means."

In order to show in detail that result, in which the sentient beings of the three realms, through empowerment, have become Samantabhadra, there is the line:

The six universal monarchs...

These are the five families and the great Vajradhara. As for the crown protuberance, since it is sealed by the lord of the family, it abides as the unchanging bliss of firm nectar. "The four enjoyments" are Locanā and the others. "The vajra lords" are the goddesses of the five objects of desire. "The vajra of meditative concentration" means near accomplishment by that rite of empowering sentient beings with the light rays of the essential nature of those deities. "Together with the wisdom body" means the wisdom mudrā.

The vajra of body, speech and mind...

This means that accomplishing union, in which, through the conduct of attachment, the all-empty and nonduality are united, is the meaning of what is called accomplishment.

Now, in order to teach great accomplishment: "by the sequence explained before" means by the sequence taught in the first chapter of the root tantra.

The knowledge-being who bears the vajra...

This refers to the passage running from the generation of Vajradhara above to "one generates the maṇḍala like oneself, the knowledge-being." Having arisen from the samādhi of natural luminosity, he looked upon the realm of sentient beings pervaded by the five afflictions, and knew: "The very full understanding of the nature of the afflictions is awakening, and its essential nature is myself." Knowing this, in order that those to be trained might purify the afflictions, aversion and the rest, he brought forth the five families; and further, for the enjoyment of Vairocana and the rest, he brought forth the four goddesses; and likewise generating the maṇḍala in which the five goddesses, the eight bodhisattvas and the ten wrathful ones are brought forth is called great accomplishment.

Having thus taught the four, approach and accomplishment, of the completion stage, now, in order to teach the approach and accomplishment of the generation stage, the passage beginning:

At the time of approach, the great crown protuberance...

is spoken. "Approach" means placing the mind one-pointedly on the awakening mind as its object; that is suchness itself, and since one approaches it and takes it as an object, it is approach.

The very application of the great crown protuberance...

This means right application. What is it? It is generating the celestial palace, from the ground portion and so forth, out of the crown protuberance syllable bhrūṃ. "Visualizing as form" means generating the maṇḍala wheel of the principal deity in which one has special devotion. Having done that, and having examined the meaning of the mantra oṃ śūnyatājñāna vajra svabhāva ātmako'haṃ, one meditates on the one possessing the marks by means of the ultimate awakening mind.

At the time of near accomplishment...

This means that, having focused on emptiness, one places sun, moon, lotus and the rest in sequence from above, sets out the syllables upon them, imagines all of them gathered into a moon maṇḍala, and upon that again sets out the three syllables. From their transformation comes the hand emblem, and from the transformation of the hand emblem the form of the great seal is generated. Directing oneself closely to it, accomplishing and generating it at the vital point, is near accomplishment.

Up to the form of Amṛtakuṇḍalin...

This means that on that form of the great seal one sets out all without exception of the mantra syllables of the deities, from Vairocana and the rest up to Amṛtakuṇḍalin at the northern gate, and one should meditate with full understanding of the cause and result of the aggregate of form and the rest. "The mantrin who knows the supreme" means this: at the head, throat and heart, on moon, sun and sun, are the three, oṃ āḥ hūṃ; one brings forth from them Locanā, Pāṇḍaravāsinī and Māmakī; emanating, gathering and urging Body Vajra, Speech Vajra and Mind Vajra, together with aspiration and mantra recitation, is the rite of blessing body, speech and mind. "Accomplishment" means supplicating for the sake of establishing accomplishment. "By the application of one's own deity" means accomplishing the pledge being, the wisdom being and the samādhi being. "Great accomplishment" means the culmination of the supreme king of the maṇḍala and the supreme king of activity; since it accomplishes the great benefit of others, it is great accomplishment.

The principal, the body of the lord of the buddhas...

This refers to the time when that great accomplishment is generated. "All vajra-bearers" means this: by the vajra-bearer, that is, by the mantrin endowed with the application of his own principal deity, the vajra enters fully into the lotus, and by vajradhṛka and the rest, the vajra-bearers that arise from that, the tathāgatas, are the great secret mantra of supreme yoga.

Having thus known the distinctions...

This means that, having distinguished the forms of the great seal of those deities and meditated on each separately, one should distinguish the families of the crown ornaments. That is, one imagines the lord, the great Vajradhara, on the matted locks and crown ornaments of the great-seal forms of the five tathāgatas; and having come to know that the crowning of the rest by his family is distinguished by Vairocana and the rest, one will gain accomplishment. The meaning is that if the rite is impaired, not even a buddha would accomplish it in as many as ten million eons, so what need to mention anyone else?

Now, in order to teach the means of becoming fully awakened through the dharma of attachment, the passage beginning:

The one to be accomplished, the accomplishing, right application...

is spoken. "The one to be accomplished" is nondual wisdom. "The accomplishing" is luminosity. "Right application" is relative truth. Not apprehending the three is the purity of meditation: that is approach. "Vajra" means having arisen with the characteristic of the union of method and wisdom; "lotus" means through definite cessation, by having become the essential nature of wisdom. That is near accomplishment.

Endowed with the syllables hūṃ and phaṭ...

This means that, by going before, one accomplishes luminosity, and it arises through attaining fully the appearance of all. "Accomplished by the accomplishing" means that it is accomplished at the time when that instant of ignorance has passed and been abandoned: it is accomplished through the causes and conditions of emptiness, very emptiness and great emptiness. What is that? "The essential nature is peace and bliss." "Great accomplishment" means that the accomplishing of the great seal by making luminosity manifest is explained as great accomplishment. In order to show what arises from method, there is the line:

The glorious one, the self of all the buddhas...

Here "the self of all the buddhas" is the lord of body. The great Vajradhara is the lord of mind.

That very thing is the dharmatā of all dharmas.

This is the lord of speech.

Brought forth from the method of all the buddhas...

This means that attaining the eight lordly powers is the result of that method, arising from the method. It is what is spoken in the thirteenth chapter of the root tantra: "The Teacher Vajrapāṇi spoke thus to all the tathāgatas: 'Blessed Ones, all you tathāgatas, if they are endowed with the three syllables, all those bodhisattvas will have faith in and meditate on this vajra of the conduct of all the tathāgatas.'"

All the tathāgatas asked, "What are the three?" The Vajra Holder declared, "The body vajra of all the tathāgatas, the speech vajra of all the tathāgatas and the mind vajra of all the tathāgatas: these are the three." So it is spoken. The master Candrakīrti also says:

As far as ya, ra, la, up to ha;
ka, kha, gha, the endless, ending in ja;
ending in da and dha, together with ending in ma:
the root of each of these is the three syllables.

This explains the attainment of lordship over the body, speech and mind vajras, which arises from the method of all the tantras that possess those endings. Body, speech and mind are merely illustrative:

As long as, from the great and supreme glorious vajra,
lordship of body, lordship of speech,
and likewise lordship of mind,
lordship of miraculous power that goes everywhere,
producing whatever is wished: the eight lordly powers.

The meaning of this is that one will attain the lordship of miraculous power, the lordship of reaching everywhere, the lordship of desire, the lordship of place and the lordship of retinue.

In order to show the difficulty at the time when the yogins of the individual maṇḍalas perform the collections of rites, the passage beginning:

By whatever accords with the rite...

is spoken. "Accords with the rite" refers to the different deities able to accomplish pacifying and the other rites. "Yoga" means, for pacifying, focusing on the application of Vairocana and so forth. "Together with the maṇḍala" means that for pacifying one generates Vairocana. "Generated by that" means that one generates Locanā in front. "The terrible pledge" means that one gives the command with secret mantras such as oṃ ru ru sphu ru, and accomplishes each rite separately. "Body, speech and mind vajra" are the buddhas of the three families. "The pledge of the crown protuberance" is the great Vajradhara. It should be understood that the command is twofold: the command given at the time of accomplishing the rites of pacifying and the rest, and the command given when the obstructors are driven out by the wheel of command.

Now, in order to teach how the various rites are performed, there is the line:

Upon whatever body is to be accomplished...

This means as follows. One imagines that the enemy, holding an axe and a hammer in his hands, his mind enraged, fills this expanse of space with śrāvakas, pratyekabuddhas and perfectly complete buddhas, and likewise with monks who abide in the Buddha's teaching and possess the qualities of moral discipline and the rest, laymen who keep the five trainings, and all the sentient beings of the three realms; and one imagines that the enemy beats them with the hammer and cuts them with the axe, and so one meditates that he is killing them. Then, having meditated on the maṇḍala of Guhyasamāja in the body of the enemy, one should proclaim his faults to the deities of the maṇḍala dwelling in that enemy's body.

Then, having been destroyed by them...

This means that the deities in the enemy's body become enraged, destroy and kill the enemy, and he is cast out. "By all things" means that one draws the enemy's deities into one's own deity, and should make the enemy's deities enter one's own wheel. "With the substances just as taught" refers to the fifteenth and fourteenth chapters. "Pervaded by wrath" means meditating on oneself as a wrathful one. "Then the wrathful one who received the command" means that, as taught in the fifteenth and thirteenth chapters, one dispatches one's own retinue of wrathful ones, rākṣasas, birds, jackals, snakes and the like, and by them the enemy is summoned and brought down into the effigy.

At the crown, the throat and the heart...

This is the rite of the kīla, as spoken in the thirteenth and fourteenth chapters. "Epidemics and harms" means that, as it occurs in the thirteenth chapter, one should strike with a kīla that is a five-pronged vajra arising from hūṃ. "All those applications" means joining them with the yantra and the recitation of mantra. The meaning is that it includes the collection of rites such as meditative concentration and the like, the fire offering, and pressing down the sign. What is spoken about each separate rite, beginning:

For pacifying, with a peaceful mind as well...

is easy to understand.

Now, in order to show praise by way of cutting off doubts, the passage beginning "Then the Blessed One" is spoken: the great Vajradhara, entered into luminosity, is praised with a king of praises. "Killing all the buddhas" is the doubt raised about the fierce rite spoken just before. Its answer is the passage beginning "Then all the tathāgatas."

The master who possesses the awakening vajra...

This means one who has realized ultimate awakening.

All things are like an illusion.

This means that, through meditating on the body of union, they are like an illusion. Therefore it is said that one does not go to the lower realms through evil, nor to the higher realms through merit: the result is awakening. "Awakening vajra" is the all-empty. "Sealed with wisdom" means meditating on the two truths as inseparable. "Having brought great accomplishment to its culmination, one should undertake the rites" shows the following: abiding in the generation stage, the mind becomes stable on the subtle, and when the number is completed through the rite of recitation, one should undertake the rites wholly devoted to mantra; abiding in the completion stage, the mind having become stable through the conduct of the three elaborations, and vajra recitation having become manifest, one should undertake the rites wholly devoted inwardly. To this meaning the bodhisattvas said, "Excellent!" This is the passage beginning "Then the bodhisattvas."

The passage beginning "Then those tathāgatas" is spoken to show that the tathāgatas, having received the empowerment of Guhyasamāja and abiding in union, are without fear of anything.

Now, in order to show that the third praise is spoken by way of gathering all the meaning of the Guhyasamāja Tantra, the passage beginning "Then the great bodhisattvas" is spoken. First, the praise of the Buddha Jewel by way of the thirty-two deities of Guhyasamāja: "You are the mind of the vajra of the three times" refers to the great Vajradhara. He himself has pacified both existence, which is saṃsāra, and liberation, which is nirvāṇa; having abandoned all obscurations and realized the luminosity of space, he is awakened. The meaning is: "Homage to him of whom Vairocana and the rest say, 'The body of union, endowed with the eight lordly powers, the awakening mind, the knowledge-being, is Vajradhara.'" "Form and feeling" are the five families. The six consciousnesses are the wrathful Ṭakkirāja and the rest. The sense sources are the five goddesses. The sense source of phenomena is Samantabhadra. The six faculties are the six, Kṣitigarbha and the rest. Earth and the rest are the four consorts. The space vajra is Mañjuśrī. In order to teach the four wrathful gatekeepers, there is the passage beginning "Delusion." In order to teach the six wrathful ones, Ṭakkirāja and the rest, there is the passage beginning "Delight and joy and the aspects."

Having thus praised by way of the deities of the maṇḍala, now the praise is spoken by way of the Dharma Jewel, the Guhyasamāja Tantra. It is shown by the passage beginning:

Whoever, beginningless, with entities pure...

The vase empowerment, the secret empowerment, and the observance, that is, the wisdom-knowledge empowerment, and "free of elaboration": by these the meaning is "Homage to the fourth empowerments."

Whoever meditates on this, the method of the supreme accomplishment...

This is homage to the four branches, approach and accomplishment, of the two stages. "Invisibility and the rest" means: "Homage to accomplishing the common accomplishments by way of the four supreme vajras, and to the method of realizing without error the accomplishment of the great seal, and to what arises from that method."

Now, in order to show that the Saṅgha Jewel, the yogins who enter Guhyasamāja, also have a result like the great Vajradhara, the passage of the tantra beginning:

Whoever listens to the tantra of the most secret assembly...

is spoken. It shows that there are good qualities in listening to and reflecting on the maṇḍala that is the special tantra. In order to show the good qualities of those who abide in meditation, there is the line:

Whoever, by secret conduct...

This shows that the first good quality of meditation is being able to perform the collections of rites and the yantras. Why is that? Because they are easy to accomplish at the beginning. In order to show that once meditation has become habitual, that very practitioner has good qualities equal to the great Vajradhara, the passage beginning:

Whoever is the principal practitioner, the higher realms...

is spoken. Now, in order to teach the benefits of that praise, there is the passage beginning:

By these peaceful words of praise...

In order that the tathāgatas might praise with rejoicing the tantra completed by making this praise, there is the passage beginning "The bodhisattvas of great renown." Because the king of tantras, the glorious Guhyasamāja, has been fully opened, there is the passage beginning:

Arisen from the secret assembly...

In order to show that, even when the explanation of the later tantra is complete, they enter into the sameness of body, speech and mind just as when the root tantra was complete, the passage beginning "All those tathāgatas" is spoken.

As for "The eighteenth chapter, called the Blessing of Vajra Wisdom that Teaches All Secrets, is complete": it is called "all secrets" because here the secret meanings of the root tantra are taught. "Vajra" is the all-empty. "Wisdom" is nondual wisdom. The meaning is that this lineage has been blessed by the great Vajradhara. The chapter, as above, averts the harm of the afflictions. "Eighteenth" means: the first chapter teaches the basis of the two stages, the sixteenth chapter teaches the meaning of the four, approach and accomplishment, and with the eighteenth all of this, which teaches the secret instructions, is said to be complete.
