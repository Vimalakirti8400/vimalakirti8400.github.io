About this text

The Great King of Tantras, the Great Secret of the Body, Speech, and Mind of All Tathāgatas, Called the Guhyasamāja, known in Sanskrit as the Sarvatathāgatakāyavākcittarahasya Guhyasamāja and in Tibetan as Sangwa Düpa, the "Secret Assembly," is one of the most influential scriptures of Indian and Tibetan Vajrayāna Buddhism. Taught by the Buddha while dwelling in the secret abode of the vajra consorts, amid an assembly of vajra bodhisattvas, it unfolds over seventeen chapters the maṇḍala of the five tathāgatas and their consorts, the pledges and vows of tantric practice, mantras and rites, and the transformation of body, speech, and mind into the three vajras of awakening. It is the root scripture of a major lineage of highest yoga tantra, interpreted in both the Ārya and Jñānapāda traditions of commentary, and was translated into Tibetan by Śraddhākaravarman and Rinchen Zangpo and later revised by Chak Lotsāwa Chöjé Pal. The text is Toh 442 in the Kangyur, where it belongs to the Tantra section among the unexcelled yoga tantras.

In the language of India: sarbatathāgatakāyavākcittarahasyoguhyasamājanāmamahākalparāja. In the language of Tibet: The Great King of Tantras, the Great Secret of the Body, Speech, and Mind of All Tathāgatas, Called the Guhyasamāja.

Homage to glorious Vajrasattva.

Thus did I hear at one time. The Blessed One was dwelling in the bhagas of the vajra consorts, the essence of the body, speech, and mind of all the tathāgatas. He was together with bodhisattva great beings as numerous as the atoms in an inexpressible, inexpressibly inexpressible number of buddha fields, namely: the bodhisattva great being Samaya Vajra, the bodhisattva great being Body Vajra, the bodhisattva great being Speech Vajra, the bodhisattva great being Mind Vajra, the bodhisattva great being Samādhi Vajra, the bodhisattva great being Victor Vajra, the bodhisattva great being Earth Vajra, the bodhisattva great being Water Vajra, the bodhisattva great being Fire Vajra, the bodhisattva great being Wind Vajra, the bodhisattva great being Space Vajra, the bodhisattva great being Form Vajra, the bodhisattva great being Sound Vajra, the bodhisattva great being Smell Vajra, the bodhisattva great being Taste Vajra, the bodhisattva great being Touch Vajra, and the bodhisattva great being Vajra of the Essential Nature of the Dharma Realm. With these at their head were bodhisattva great beings as numerous as the atoms in an inexpressible, inexpressibly inexpressible number of buddha fields. He was also together with tathāgatas equal to the element of space, namely: the Tathāgata Vajra Akṣobhya, the Tathāgata Vajra Vairocana, the Tathāgata Vajra Ratnaketu, the Tathāgata Vajra Amitābha, the Tathāgata Vajra Amogha, and, with these at their head, tathāgatas equal to the whole element of space. As for their dwelling together, it was like a pod filled with sesame seeds: the whole element of space appeared completely filled with all the tathāgatas.

Then the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Great Vajra Vairocana, entered into the meditative absorption called the Way of the Great Passion of All Tathāgatas, and caused all the arrays of the tathāgatas to enter into his own body. Then all those tathāgatas, in order to fully delight the Blessed One, the lord of the body, speech, and mind of all tathāgatas, emanated their own bodies in the form of women and came forth from the body of the Blessed One, Great Vairocana. Of these, some abided in the form of Buddhalocanā, some in the form of Māmakī, some in the form of Pāṇḍaravāsinī, and some in the form of Samaya Tārā. Some abided in the form of the very nature of visible form, some in the form of the very nature of sound, some in the form of the very nature of smell, some in the form of the very nature of taste, and some in the form of the very nature of touch.

Then the Tathāgata Akṣobhya blessed, in the bhaga of the consort of all the tathāgatas, the square, stainless maṇḍala of the great samaya:

Its very nature is radiant clarity,
It is filled throughout with manifold forms,
Clouds of buddhas are spread everywhere,
Masses of sparks stream forth in profusion;
Endowed with the maṇḍalas of clarity and the rest,
It is the abode of all the tathāgatas.

Then the Blessed One, the lord of the vajra body, speech, and mind of all the tathāgatas, placed himself in the center of the maṇḍala of the great samaya of all the tathāgatas. Then the Tathāgata Akṣobhya, the Tathāgata Ratnaketu, the Tathāgata Amitābha, the Tathāgata Amoghasiddhi, and the Tathāgata Vairocana dwelt in the heart of the Tathāgata Awakening Mind Vajra.

Then the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Awakening Mind Vajra, entered into the meditative absorption called the Vajra That Overpowers All Tathāgatas. As soon as the Blessed One, the lord of all tathāgatas, had entered into absorption, this entire expanse of space came to abide as the vajra nature of all the tathāgatas. Then, through the blessing of Vajrasattva, all the sentient beings, however many dwell throughout the expanse of space, attained the bliss and the mental joy of all the tathāgatas.

Then the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Awakening Mind Vajra, entered into the meditative absorption called the Vajra Arisen from the Samaya of the Vajra Body, Speech, and Mind of All Tathāgatas, and blessed the great knowledge in the form of a man with the blessing of the mantras of all the tathāgatas. The very instant it was blessed, all the tathāgatas beheld that same Blessed One, the Tathāgata Awakening Mind Vajra, in a three-faced form.

Then the tathāgatas, Akṣobhya and the rest, came forth from the heart of the Blessed One, Great Vairocana, and uttered this inspired utterance:

How wonderful! The awakening mind
Of all the buddhas fully enters;
The secret of all the tathāgatas
Is utterly free of concepts and without abode.

Then all the tathāgatas gathered together once more, and having worshipped the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Awakening Mind Vajra, with clouds of the precious suchness that is the samaya of the outpouring of offerings of all the tathāgatas, and having bowed to him, they addressed him thus:

Arisen from the secret assembly,
The secret of all the tathāgatas,
The compendium of the vajra essence:
Blessed One, please explain that very reality.

Then the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Awakening Mind Vajra, spoke these words to all those tathāgatas: "Blessed Ones, all you tathāgatas, excellent, excellent! And yet this is something that gives rise to doubt even in all the tathāgatas; what need is there to speak of the other bodhisattvas?"

Then all the blessed tathāgatas were amazed and filled with wonder, and they addressed these words to the Blessed One, the lord of all the tathāgatas, who cuts through all the doubts of all the tathāgatas: "Blessed One, you who are not eager to teach the secret of the body, speech, and mind of all the tathāgatas in the assembly of all the tathāgatas, even to those with such distinguished qualities, please bless all the blessed tathāgatas, and with words from which the vajra samaya of all the tathāgatas arises, please teach it perfectly, for the sake of causing them to experience the bliss and the mental joy of all the tathāgatas, up to causing them to attain the fruit of the wisdom and the higher knowledges of all the tathāgatas."

Then the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Body, Speech, and Mind Vajra of All Tathāgatas, knowing the request that all the tathāgatas had made, entered into the meditative absorption called the Vajra Lamp of Wisdom, and drew forth from his own body, speech, and mind vajra this essence of the essence of the supreme mantra of the family of aversion: vajradhrik.

Then, the instant this was drawn forth, that very knowledge-person of the body, speech, and mind of all the blessed tathāgatas, through the supreme state of union with the great mudrā of Akṣobhya, in a form black, white, and red, took his seat at the body, speech, and mind vajra of the body, speech, and mind vajra of all the tathāgatas.

Then the Blessed One entered into the meditative absorption called the Vajra from Which the Samaya of All Tathāgatas Arises, and drew forth from his own body, speech, and mind vajra this essence of the essence of the supreme mantra of the family of delusion: jinajik.

Then, the instant this was drawn forth, that very knowledge-person of the speech and mind of all the blessed tathāgatas, through the supreme state of union with the great mudrā of Vairocana, in a form white, black, and red, took his seat in front of the body, speech, and mind vajra of all the tathāgatas.

Then the Blessed One entered into the meditative absorption called the Glorious Vajra from Which the Jewels of All Tathāgatas Arise, and drew forth from his own body, speech, and mind vajra this essence of the essence of the supreme mantra of the family of the wish-fulfilling jewel: ratnadhṛk.

Then, the instant this was drawn forth, that very knowledge-person of the body, speech, and mind of all the blessed tathāgatas, through the supreme state of space, union with the great mudrā of Ratnaketu, in a form yellow, black, and white, took his seat to the right of the body, speech, and mind vajra of all the tathāgatas.

Then the Blessed One entered into the meditative absorption called the Vajra from Which the Stream of the Great Passion of All Tathāgatas Arises, and drew forth from his own body, speech, and mind vajra this essence of the essence of the supreme mantra of the family of vajra passion: ārolik.

Then, the instant this was drawn forth, that very knowledge-person of the body, speech, and mind of all the blessed tathāgatas, through the supreme state of union with the great mudrā of Lokeśvara, the great lord of knowledge, in a form red, black, and white, took his seat behind the body, speech, and mind vajra of all the tathāgatas.

Then the Blessed One entered into the meditative absorption called the Vajra from Which the Samaya of the Supreme Unfailing Accomplishment of All Tathāgatas Arises, and drew forth from his own body, speech, and mind vajra this essence of the essence of the supreme mantra of the family of the samaya of summoning: prajñādhṛk.

Then, the instant this was drawn forth, that very knowledge-person of the body, speech, and mind of all the blessed tathāgatas, through the supreme state of union with the great mudrā of Vajra Amogha, in a form green, black, and white, took his seat to the north of the body, speech, and mind vajra of all the tathāgatas.

Aversion, delusion, and likewise passion,
The wish-fulfilling jewel, and samaya:
These very five families
Accomplish both desires and liberation.

Then again the Blessed One entered into the meditative absorption called the Samaya of Passionate Love of the Vajra Holder of All Tathāgatas, and drew forth from his own body, speech, and mind vajra this supreme consort of all vajra holders: dveṣarati.

Then, the instant this was drawn forth, that very knowledge-person of the body, speech, and mind of all the blessed tathāgatas took on the form of a woman and took her seat in the eastern corner.

Then the Blessed One entered into the meditative absorption called the Vajra of Passionate Love of All Tathāgatas, and drew forth from his own body, speech, and mind vajra this supreme consort of all the tathāgatas: moharati.

Then, the instant this was drawn forth, that very knowledge-person of the body, speech, and mind of all the blessed tathāgatas took on the form of a woman and took her seat in the southern corner.

Then the Blessed One entered into the meditative absorption called the Vajra of Passionate Love That Holds the Passion of All Tathāgatas, and drew forth from his own body, speech, and mind vajra this supreme consort who holds the passion of all the tathāgatas: rāgarati.

Then, the instant this was drawn forth, that very knowledge-person of the body, speech, and mind of all the blessed tathāgatas took on the form of a woman and took her seat in the western corner.

Then the Blessed One entered into the meditative absorption called the Vajra That Beguiles the Body, Speech, and Mind of All Tathāgatas, and drew forth from his own body, speech, and mind vajra this vajra of passionate love, holder of the wisdom of all the tathāgatas: vajrarati.

Then, the instant this was drawn forth, that very knowledge-person of the body, speech, and mind of all the blessed tathāgatas took on the form of a woman and took her seat in the northern corner. These are the perfect, supreme consorts of all the tathāgatas.

Then the Blessed One entered into the meditative absorption called the Vairocana Vajra, and drew forth from his own body, speech, and mind vajra this great wrathful one called Blessing the Maṇḍala of All Tathāgatas: yamāntakrīt.

Then, the instant this was drawn forth, that very knowledge-person of the body, speech, and mind of all the blessed tathāgatas, in a form that terrifies all the tathāgatas, took his seat at the eastern gate.

Then the Blessed One entered into the meditative absorption called the Vajra of the Complete and Perfect Awakening of All Tathāgatas, and drew forth from his own body, speech, and mind vajra this great wrathful one called Blessing the Maṇḍala of All Tathāgatas: prarluntakṛt.

Then, the instant this was drawn forth, that very knowledge-person of the body, speech, and mind of all the blessed tathāgatas, in a form that terrifies the vajra samaya, took his seat at the southern gate.

Then the Blessed One entered into the meditative absorption called the Vajra Source of the Dharma Jewel of All Tathāgatas, and drew forth from his own body, speech, and mind vajra this great wrathful one called Blessing the Maṇḍala That Holds the Passion of All Tathāgatas: padmantakṛt.

Then, the instant it was drawn forth, that very knowledge-person of the body, speech, and mind of all the blessed tathāgatas, in a form that terrifies the speech of all the tathāgatas, took his seat at the western gate.

Then the Blessed One entered into the meditative absorption called the Body, Speech, and Mind Vajra of All Tathāgatas, and drew forth from his own body, speech, and mind vajra this great wrathful one, Blessing the Maṇḍala of the Body, Speech, and Mind of All Tathāgatas: bighantakṛt.

Then, the instant it was drawn forth, that very knowledge-person of the body, speech, and mind of all the blessed tathāgatas, in a form that terrifies the body, speech, and mind of all the tathāgatas, took his seat at the northern gate.

These are the samaya beings of the maṇḍala, who delight the body, speech, and mind of all the tathāgatas.

This concludes the first chapter, on blessing the maṇḍala of the meditative absorption of all the tathāgatas, from the Great Secret of the Body, Speech, and Mind of All Tathāgatas, the Guhyasamāja.

Then all the blessed tathāgatas made offerings to the Blessed One, the lord of the body, speech, and mind of all the tathāgatas, bowed to him, and addressed him thus:

The essence, supreme body, speech, and mind,
The secret of all the tathāgatas,
The unsurpassed awakening mind:
May the Blessed One please explain it.

Then the Blessed One, the lord of the body, speech, and mind vajra of all the tathāgatas, knowing the request that all the tathāgatas had made, and knowing with his mind the thoughts in the minds of all the bodhisattvas, spoke these words to those bodhisattvas: "Generate the mind in the aspect of body, the body in the aspect of mind, and the mind through verbal expression!"

Then those bodhisattva great beings, having perfectly joined the body, speech, and mind of all the tathāgatas as being like space, uttered this inspired utterance:

How wonderful! O you who possess the vajra
Of Samantabhadra's body, speech, and mind,
Through the union of non-arising
This arising is proclaimed.

Then the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Body, Speech, and Mind Vajra of All Tathāgatas, entered into the meditative absorption called the Vajra Way of the Complete and Perfect Awakening of All Tathāgatas, and spoke this awakening mind:

Where there is no entity, there is no meditation;
What is to be meditated on is not meditation.
Thus, since entities are not entities,
Meditation has no object to be found.

So spoke the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Body, Speech, and Mind Vajra of All Tathāgatas.

Then the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Vairocana Vajra, entered into the meditative absorption called the Vajra of the Realization of All Tathāgatas, and spoke this awakening mind:

Free of all entities, of aggregates, elements,
And sense bases, rid of apprehended and apprehender,
Through the sameness of the selflessness of phenomena, one's own mind
Is unborn from the very beginning, its nature emptiness.

So spoke the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Vajra Vairocana.

Then the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Vajra Akṣobhya, entered into the meditative absorption called the Inexhaustible Vajra of All Tathāgatas, and spoke this awakening mind:

These entities are unborn;
There are neither phenomena nor the nature of phenomena.
Selfless, just like space:
This way of awakening is firm.

So spoke the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Vajra Akṣobhya.

Then the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Vajra Ratnaketu, entered into the meditative absorption called the Vajra of the Selflessness of All Tathāgatas, and spoke this awakening mind:

All phenomena are without entity,
Free of the characteristics of phenomena;
Arisen from the selflessness of phenomena,
This way of awakening is firm.

So spoke the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Ratnaketu.

Then the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Vajra Amitāyus, entered into the meditative absorption called the Vajra Lamp of the Light Rays of Wisdom, and spoke this awakening mind:

For phenomena that are unborn
There is no essence, and no meditation either.
Through union with the state of space,
By this the entity is proclaimed.

So spoke the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Vajra Amitāyus.

Then the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Vajra Amoghasiddhi, entered into the meditative absorption called the Vajra That Overpowers All Tathāgatas, and spoke this awakening mind:

Phenomena are luminous by nature,
Pure from the very beginning, like space.
There is no awakening, no mind, and no realization:
This way of awakening is firm.

So spoke the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Vajra Amoghasiddhi.

Then the bodhisattva great beings, Maitreya and the rest, having heard the syllables of the nature of reality of the secret of the body, speech, and mind of all the tathāgatas, were amazed and filled with wonder, and uttered this inspired utterance:

How wonderful the Buddha! How wonderful the Dharma!
The teaching of the Dharma is a great marvel.
The perfect meaning, the meaning of purity:
Awakening mind, homage to you!

Truly arisen from the selflessness of phenomena,
Bringing the awakening of the buddhas to completion,
Free of concepts, without any object:
Awakening mind, homage to you!

Samantabhadra, the aim of all,
The awakening mind that fully revolves,
The great vajra of awakened conduct:
Awakening mind, homage to you!

Mind, the purity of the tathāgatas,
Possessed of vajra body, speech, and mind,
Fully revealing the awakening of the buddhas:
Awakening mind, homage to you!

This concludes the second chapter, on the awakening mind, from the Great Secret of the Body, Speech, and Mind of All Tathāgatas, the Guhyasamāja.

Then the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Body, Speech, and Mind Vajra of All Tathāgatas, entered into the meditative absorption called the Vajra Array of the Radiating Clouds of All Tathāgatas, and spoke this chapter on the meditative absorption called the Vajra Array:

oṃ śūnyatājñānavajrasvabhāvātmako'haṃ

In the middle of the element of space
One should meditate on the maṇḍala of the buddhas:
A great array of clouds of light rays,
Equal to the light of blazing buddhas,
Pervaded throughout by five light rays,
Perfectly round on every side,
Embellished with the five sense pleasures,
Adorned with the five kinds of offerings.

Having meditated, then by gathering it in,
One should meditate on the form in the center:
Vairocana, the great mudrā,
Marked with body, speech, and mind.
One should meditate too on the mudrā
Of the vajra of body, speech, and mind.

Through the union of the nondual gathering,
The supreme, excellent mudrā of Akṣobhya,
The great mudrā of Ratnaketu,
The light-maker of Amitāyus,
And the great mudrās of Amoghasiddhi:
Meditate on these in the maṇḍala of the buddhas.

The one with the vajra of body, speech, and mind,
Like the color of sapphire,
Holding a blazing vajra in his hand,
Fangs bared, radiant, terrifying.

Like the light of crystal and of the moon,
Adorned with a topknot and diadem,
Holding a blazing wheel in his hand,
Beautifully adorned with various ornaments.

Like the golden light of the Jambū River,
Clouds of buddhas massing all about him,
A great nine-pronged vajra:
Visualize it in his hand.

Like the light of emerald,
Adorned with blazing vajras,
Pervaded by clouds blazing all around:
Visualize him holding a jewel in his hand.

Like the color of ruby,
The lord who holds the blazing light of passion,
Holding a blazing lotus in his hand:
Meditate on the one with the vajra of passion.

Like the color of the five light rays,
The one with the manifold Amogha vajra,
Peaceful, holding a sword in his hand:
Meditate on him in the maṇḍala of the buddhas.

Then the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Body, Speech, and Mind Vajra of All Tathāgatas, entered into the meditative absorption called the Vajra of the Essential Nature of the Dharma Realm, and spoke this mantra that blesses body, speech, and mind:

oṃ dharmadhātusvabhāva ātmako'haṃ

A great jewel of five colors,
Just the size of a mustard seed,
At the tip of the nose, with diligence,
The yogin should always meditate on.
When the jewel has become stable, emanate;
If it has not become stable, do not emanate at all.

Light equal to that of blazing buddhas,
Clouds most supreme, should be emanated.
Holding as weapons the wheel, vajra, and jewel,
The lotus and the sword,
Great clouds of bodhisattvas
Are emanated by one whose nature is emanation.

In the middle of the element of space
One should draw the moon maṇḍala.
In the middle of the maṇḍala of clarity
One should meditate on the wheel maṇḍala.
The lotus maṇḍala, perfectly clear,
One should meditate on through lotus meditation.
The jewel maṇḍala, perfectly clear,
One should meditate on with the diligence of the jewel.
Arising from the maṇḍala of union,
It should be drawn so as to be like space.

These very supreme buddhas,
By accomplishing body, speech, and mind,
Arising from the vajra maṇḍala,
Attain the way of omniscience.

This concludes the third chapter, called the Meditative Absorption of the Vajra Array, from the Great Secret of the Body, Speech, and Mind of All Tathāgatas, the Guhyasamāja.

Then all the blessed tathāgatas gathered once more and made their request to the Blessed One, the lord of the body, speech, and mind of all the tathāgatas, with this king of praises:

Peace of all the tathāgatas,
Abode of all the tathāgatas,
Supreme selflessness of all phenomena:
Please explain the sublime maṇḍala.

All characteristics perfectly complete,
All that are not characteristics abandoned,
Supreme body of Samantabhadra:
Please explain the sublime maṇḍala.

Arisen from the supreme Dharma of peace,
Purifying the conduct of wisdom,
Supreme speech of Samantabhadra:
Please explain the sublime maṇḍala.

Great mind of all sentient beings,
Pure by nature and without stain,
Supreme mind of Samantabhadra, the guide:
Please explain the pure maṇḍala.

Then the Teacher, the Vajra Holder,
Of the three worlds, of the three realms,
Supreme in the three worlds, the vajra lord,
Supreme teacher of the three worlds,

The abode of all the tathāgatas,
Fully explained the mind maṇḍala
Of all the tathāgatas, the way of the maṇḍala,
The delightful maṇḍala:

"Then, therefore, the mind maṇḍala,
Supreme, closely resembling the mind vajra,
The maṇḍala of body, speech, and mind,
I shall now perfectly explain.

Through meditating on body, speech, and mind,
The wise one should lay down the lines.
With a new cord well stretched,
With fine and beautiful proportions,
The sublime mind maṇḍala
Should be made twelve cubits in measure.
Its four sides and its four corners,
Four gates and the spaces between, are to be laid out well.
In its center a wheel,
Perfectly round, should be drawn.
Through the rite as it has been seen,
The mudrās should then be set out.

In its center one draws a vajra
Like the color of sapphire,
Greatly blazing, with five prongs,
Terrifying even the terrifying.
In the east, a great wheel
Adorned with blazing vajras;

This is the meditative absorption called the Food of the Wisdom Vajra of All Pledges.

The tongue is the supreme vajra pledge.
Having meditated on the syllable hūṃ bearing a vajra,
If one eats through the yoga of the five nectars,
One will attain the three vajras.
This is the supreme mode of the vajra;
The pledges of āḥ and oṃ as well,
Through yoga such as this,
Make one become like Vajrasattva.

This is the meditative absorption called the Garland of Nectar of the Vajra Pledge.

Supreme in accomplishing the three vajra pledges,
One becomes a holder of the vajra of the three bodies,
And becomes an ocean of wish-fulfilling jewels
For all sentient beings of the ten directions.
In every world system
One makes the vajra nature appear.
Supreme in accomplishing the pledge of the wheel,
One becomes equal to the body of the buddhas.
In places as many as the sands of the Ganges,
One moves about, accomplished in every way.
Through the supreme pledge of all,
One becomes the chief of the knowledge-holders.
The supreme form of the pledge of all:
By meditating intently on the vajra body,
One makes all that was invisible
Appear, alone, throughout the trichiliocosm.
One steals away what belongs to all the buddhas,
And enjoys the daughters of the supreme gods.
The buddhas as many as the sands of the Ganges,
Dwelling in the abode of the three vajras,
One sees with the vajra eye
As if they were a myrobalan fruit placed in one's own hand.
However many sounds resound
In realms as many as the sands of the Ganges,
The supreme lord of superknowledge hears them
As though they were sounding right beside his ear.
The minds of all sentient beings
In realms as many as the sands of the Ganges,
Bearing the characteristics of body, speech, and mind,
He knows as they arise, like a display.
The circumstances of former abodes,
Of dwelling in saṃsāra
For eons as many as the sands of the Ganges,
He remembers as though three days had passed.
With bodies as many as the sands of the Ganges,
Adorned with clouds of buddhas and the like,
For eons as many as the sands of the Ganges
He fills all with the vajra of supreme miraculous power.

Thus spoke the Blessed One, Superknower of the Pledge.

Here the five superknowledges are the vajra eye, the vajra ear, the vajra mind, the vajra of former abodes, and the vajra of miraculous power.

Having accomplished the aim of the buddhas' superknowledge,
One becomes like the body of the buddhas.
Encircled by a retinue
Countless as the sands of the Ganges,
With vajra body, speech, and mind
One travels through all the world systems.

The yoga of the pledge of approach,
The arising of near accomplishment,
The pledge for the sake of accomplishment,
And the great accomplishment make four.
Having understood them through the distinctions of the vajra,
One should then accomplish the activities.
In approach, intently applying meditative absorption,
One should meditate on supreme awakening.
In near accomplishment, the supreme attainment,
One investigates the vajra sense bases.
In accomplishment, the urging is proclaimed,
Meditating on the mantra and the lord.
At the time of performing the great accomplishment,
Imagine the form of the vajra holder of your own mantra,
With the lord upon the crown ornament:
The wisdom vajra will be accomplished.
Approach is to be done with the nectar of wisdom,
In all things and at all times.
This is what accomplishes
The mantra and the reality of all mantras.
In the region of a great wilderness,
Utterly secluded from people,
On mountains, at forest edges, or on riverbanks,
One will always attain accomplishment.

Thus spoke the Blessed One, Vajra of Great Accomplishment.

Alternatively, through the four vajras,
One of steadfast discipline should perform the approach.
If one meditates through that equality of the three vajra bodies,
One will attain accomplishment.
Through intent application in the four sessions,
With the mind fixed on the five places,
Having meditated on the syllable oṃ,
The vow is taught by the wisdom vajra.
Seven days, half a month,
One month, and a month and a half:
If one accomplishes the vajra pledge,
One will swiftly attain accomplishment.
By distinguishing the different numbers of days,
I have taught it in full.
Those arisen from the supreme secret
Have declared accomplishment within a fortnight.

Here, this is the domain of the vow of near accomplishment:

Glorious holder of the buddha body,
Reality indivisible from the three vajras,
Make me today an abode of blessing:
Vajra of Body, I pray you, do so.
Buddhas dwelling in the ten directions,
Reality indivisible from the three vajras,
Make me today an abode of blessing:
Vajra of Body, I pray you, do so.

Here, this is the domain of the vow of accomplishment:

Glorious pathway of speech, the nature of reality,
Reality indivisible from the three vajras,
Make me today an abode of blessing:
Vajra of Speech, I pray you, do so.
Buddhas dwelling in the ten directions,
Reality indivisible from the three vajras,
Make me today an abode of blessing:
Vajra of Speech, I pray you, do so.

Here, this is the domain of the vow of great accomplishment:

Glorious holder of the vajra mind,
Reality indivisible from the three vajras,
Make me today an abode of blessing:
Vajra of Mind, I pray you, do so.
Buddhas dwelling in the ten directions,
Reality indivisible from the three vajras,
Make me today an abode of blessing:
Vajra of Mind, I pray you, do so.

Whether a buddha, or Vajrasattva,
Or even a Dharmasattva,
If, deluded, one transgresses,
One will without doubt shatter to pieces here.

This is the twelfth chapter, the Definitive Teaching on the Supreme Accomplishment of the Pledge, the Vajra Yoga of All Tathāgatas, from the Guhyasamāja, the Great Secret of the Body, Speech, and Mind of All Tathāgatas.

Then all the Blessed Sugatas,
With the holder of the supreme wisdom vajra,
And the wise bodhisattvas,
Arising truly for the benefit of all beings,
Bowed to the great Teacher,
The Sage, the vajra holder of all aims,
And, making offerings to the one who knows the reality of the pledge,
Sang this vajra song:

"How wonderful! How excellent the way of the buddhas!
How wonderful! The supreme way of awakening!
How wonderful! How peaceful the way of the Dharma!
How wonderful! The way of mantra that is taught!
Of phenomena that are unborn,
Distinguished by their essential nature,
In the genuine, free of conceptualization,
You have proclaimed that wisdom arises.
The delightful compendium of all mantras,
The vajra recitation, the great wisdom,
The maṇḍala of the indivisible three bodies,
Through which the wisdom of the buddhas is attained;
By meditating on the three indivisible vajras,
Through the yoga of the vajra recitation,
One is blessed by all the buddhas.
The families of all mantras,
The characteristics of body, speech, and mind,
The recitation of mantra: proclaim them today.
Ocean of wisdom, hear us,
Holder of the vajra of body, speech, and mind!
The buddhas who arose in the three times,
By meditating intently on vajra mantra,
Attained the unequaled wisdom:
Blessed One, please explain it."

Then the Teacher, the Vajra Holder,
Arisen from the wisdom of the space vajra,
The sublime and supreme chief who creates and generates,
Spoke this vajra recitation.

The recitation of the meaning of all mantras
Has the character of the three indivisible vajras.
At the end of the vajra of three divisions,
The three arrayed vajras should be uttered.
In the places of body, speech, and mind,
Perform the threefold emanation.
Through this vajra recitation,
One becomes like the mind of the three vajras.
Having meditated on the body, speech, and mind of the buddhas,
And visualized the supreme offering,
If one does this with the wisdom vajra,
One becomes like the mind of the three vajras.
Having imagined the body, speech, and mind of the buddhas,
And visualized the supreme offering,
Do this intently with the wisdom vajra:
This is the attainment of supreme awakening.
Alternatively, through the three divisions,
Emanate each one separately.
Body, speech, and mind, which are selfless,
Emanate truly by the mind of wisdom.
Having uttered them, emanate the vajras,
And when complete, gather them back in.
This belongs to all the buddhas
Whose eye of wisdom has opened.

The vajra of body, perfect awakening,
Investigating entity and non-entity,
Is declared to be the body of the buddhas:
That is explained as the recitation of body.
The pledge of speech, perfect awakening,
Investigating sound and non-sound,
Is declared to be the vajra of speech:
That is explained as the recitation of speech.
The pledge of mind, perfect awakening,
Investigating the abiding vajra,
Is declared to be the vajra of mind:
That is explained as the recitation of mind.
The recitation of the realization of the meaning,
Through the excellent one arisen from essential nature,
Investigating the buddhas of the three times:
That is explained as the jewel recitation.
Emanating clouds of bodies
Into all the buddha fields,
Going and coming, the vajra meaning:
That is explained as the Amogha recitation.
Through knowing the pledge of the wrathful ones,
The maṇḍala proclaimed by one's own vajra,
Hearing the words of the syllables of mantra:
That is explained as the recitation of the wrathful.
Intoxicated by the objects of desire,
Without help or joy for any sentient being,
It places beings in the state of delusion:
That is explained as the recitation of delusion.
The words arisen from the vajra of attachment,
Abiding in body, speech, and mind,
Place beings in the lake of attachment:
That is explained as the recitation of attachment.
The mind arisen from the vajra of aversion,
Abiding in body, speech, and mind,
Places beings in the state of aversion:
That is explained as the recitation of aversion.
That very pledge of the three vajras,
The middle, holder of the vajra pledge:
That itself is explained as the neuter recitation
Of the vajra pledge.

Meditating on the meaning that is the reality of attachment,
All the vajra lords,
Wishing to benefit all sentient beings,
Bring about awakening born from attachment.
The great knowledge goddesses, Locanā and the others,
Always striving for the aims of desire,
Through the enjoyments of desire,
Succeed when they rely on them as they wish.
Arisen from the pledge of delusion,
The king of knowledge, the vajra holder,
Accomplishing the state of the neuter,
Bestows the sublime accomplishment.
The wrathful ones, born from the state of aversion,
Who always strive to kill,
The practitioner, for the purpose of killing,
Will succeed through those of supreme character.

Thus spoke the Blessed One, Pledge of the Great Person.

The one who possesses the wisdom wheel
Should meditate on a wheel in the center of the heart.
Abiding in the center of the radiant maṇḍala,
Meditate in the center on the meaning of the wheel.
The one who possesses the wisdom vajra
Should meditate on a vajra in the center of the heart.
Abiding in the center of the vajra maṇḍala,
One should meditate on the meaning of the vajra mantra.
The one who possesses the jewel wisdom
Should meditate on a jewel in the center of the heart.
Abiding in the center of the jewel maṇḍala,
One should meditate on the meaning of the jewel mantra.
The one who possesses the lotus wisdom
Should meditate on a lotus in the center of the heart.
Abiding in the center of the Dharma maṇḍala,
One should meditate on the meaning of the lotus mantra.
The one who possesses the sword wisdom
Should meditate on a sword in the center of the heart.
Abiding in the center of the pledge maṇḍala,
One should meditate on the meaning of the sword mantra.

On the sides of all the maṇḍalas
One should place the five buddhas.
If one emanates them through the distinctions of the five rays,
One will attain awakening.
The emanation of all the mantras
Is proclaimed in two distinct kinds.
Through the distinctions of the three bodies' vajras,
One should emanate and gather back.
The body is the essential nature of the enlightened body;
The mind is the essential nature of the enlightened mind;
The speech, by its intrinsic nature, is enlightened speech.
If one makes offerings thus, one attains the state worthy of offerings.

With the wheel of the great maṇḍala
One should meditate on the five vajras.
In the center of the vajra of body and speech,
Having imagined the form of one's own lord,
The vajra form of one's own mantra,
And the four kinds of maṇḍala,
Visualize them in the four colors,
And meditate on the meaning of the mantra at the heart.
The activity of the four kinds of vajra
Is performed by those who hold the vajra of meditative concentration.
This, of all mantras,
Is the supreme, eternal great secret.

For pacifying, the mode of Locanā;
For increasing, the vajra holder of the lotus;
For subjugating, abide as Vairocana;
For fierce rites, the vajra wrathful one.
This, of all the vajras,
Because it arises from the secret of the three bodies,
Is the emanation, marked by the play
Of the activities of all mantras.

Toward beings who speak without faith,
Those who revile the vajra master,
And other malicious beings too,
One should perform this urging.

Thus spoke the Blessed One, Great Wisdom Wheel Vajra.

Meditate on the beings abiding in the three realms
As the body of the buddhas,
And having imagined the enemy killed,
Then undertake the rites.
Abiding in the center of the expanse of space,
Five-pronged vajra, four-faced,
Endowed with the supreme of all aspects:
Meditate on oneself as Vajrasattva.
Meditate on the wheel of the buddhas
Arisen from the pledge of the three times;
With the right hand perform this
Mighty wheel of the buddhas.
The beings that have come from the ten directions,
Through the distinctions of the buddha body,
Gather them together in the manner of a ball
And place them into one's own body.
The one who holds the wheel of the buddhas' wisdom
Should then emanate them again intently.
Imagine the wrathful one, agitated with fury,
Hideous, vivid, and terrifying,
Holding the supreme of various weapons,
Intent supremely on the aim of killing:
He kills the greatly malicious.
Vajrasattva himself as well,
Granting the supreme three bodies of the buddhas,
Abiding in the maṇḍala of the three vajras,
Will bestow accomplishment even on one of deluded nature:
Have no doubt about this.

This is to be done for seven days;
Even the buddhas will not succeed against it.

This is the samādhi called the Wheel of Command of Vajra Samaya Wisdom.

Abiding in the center of the sphere of space,
Adorned with a blazing vajra wheel,
Endowed with the supreme of all aspects,
Meditate intently on Vairocana.
Arisen from the samaya of the three times,
Vajrasattva of great renown:
Imagine that he holds in his hand
A vajra spraying sparks of fire.
The beings who come from the ten directions,
Through the distinctions of the vajra body,
Are gathered in the manner of light rays,
And one inserts them into one's own body.
The emanation of all the vajras
Is to be done by the kinsman of supreme wisdom.
"Endowed with the union of body, speech, and mind,
All you buddhas, listen to me!
I am the glorious Vajradhara.
Through applying the wheel of command,
If you transgress the samaya,
With a form of the vajra blazing all around,
Arisen from the three bodies, I shall split you apart.
You will be destroyed here; do not doubt it."

This is the samādhi called the Wisdom Vajra of the Samaya of the Wheel.

Imagine the buddha maṇḍala with its vajras
Abiding in the center of the space vajra.
Yamāntaka, the great wheel,
Visualize intently as one's own vajra.
The buddhas and sattvas of the three times
Who dwell in the maṇḍala of the three bodies,
Buddhas and Yamāntakas, again
Are to be emanated, alike in aspect.
The beings who dwell in the three times,
The malicious and the enemies,
Meditate that all of them are killed in wrath.
This is the vajra maṇḍala of command.

This is the samādhi called the Wisdom Vajra of the Three Bodies of Yamāntaka, Arisen from All Samayas.

With the vajra of body, speech, and mind,
Or with the quality of the meaning of one's own mantra,
Or else with the uṣṇīṣa samaya,
One should apply the wheel of command.
To protect all mantras, supreme wisdom
Is to be employed by the vajra holder.
This is called the means
Of guarding the awakening of all the vajras.

Thus declared the Blessed One, Bodhisattvavajra.

Abiding in the center of the space vajra,
The samaya Dharma of the three bodies
Arising from Vairocana's supreme body:
Having imagined this, visualize that seat intently.
Meditate that the sphere of space
Is completely filled with all the buddhas.
The wisdom abiding in the syllables of one's own mantra
Should be meditated on in the form of the mind.
Then again the buddhas who were meditated on
As mind alone are fully gathered in;
Having made them into the mind vajra,
Insert them into the three bodies.

Thus declared the Blessed One, Space Vajra.

This is the samādhi called the Vajra Mantra that Fully Illuminates the Jewel.

Meditate intently on Vajrasattva,
Endowed with the supreme of all aspects.
Having arranged the buddhas in sequence,
One should tread upon the water.
Through the yoga of setting the feet evenly,
Meditate on the feet upon the crown of the head.

Thus declared the Blessed One, Svabhāvaśuddha.

This is the samādhi called the Vajra that Presses Down the Ocean with the Feet.

In the center, imagine the Mahendra maṇḍala
And place the family of the wrathful ones there.
Meditate upon its crown
On Karma Vajra, who presses down the place.

This is the samādhi called Suppressing All the Non-Buddhists (tīrthikas) and the Contrivances of Adversaries.

The three wrathful forms, the supreme vajra,
Resembling the color of yellow saffron,
All of them like the king of mountains:
Imagine them on the crown and meditate intently.
If it suppresses even the army of the buddhas,
There is no doubt here as to a lord of men.

Thus declared the Blessed One, Arisen from the Body, Speech, and Mind of All the Tathāgatas.

This is the samādhi called Suppressing All Armies.

For the samaya of terrifying enemies,
Contemplate this meditative concentration intently.
Even if the buddhas should proceed against it,
It will become manifest; there is no doubt of this.

The Blessed One, Samaya of the Three Vajras, spoke thus:

From the syllable hūṃ comes a kīla;
Its measure is that it is five-pointed.
Meditate on it as a vajra kīla,
And with it meditate intently on this at the heart.
Even the army of the buddhas, enraged,
Will be destroyed here; there is no doubt of this.

This is the samādhi called Suppressing Deluded Enemies.

In a city, or else in a village,
Or in a region as well, it should be fully applied.
The pacification that removes all diseases
Should also always be applied by this means.
Meditate on a vajra abiding in the sky,
Five-pointed,
Imagine it like the burning at the end of the eon,
And then gather it in again.
The emanations too are to be done again.
Having meditated on the light of the precious wish-fulfilling jewel
And on the clouds of Dharma,
Perform the empowerment correctly.
Through this vajra meditative concentration,
Even what is hard to fill is completely filled.
The vajra of generosity shines forth brightly;
The wish-fulfilling jewel becomes glorious.
There one should emanate the clouds
Of the great Dharma of the buddhas and of Vajrasattva.
Even abiding for three countless eons,
It is blessed by all the buddhas.
This is the secret of the stainless body
Of all the buddhas.

This is the samādhi called the Vajra Source that Eliminates All Diseases.

The pledge arising from meditative concentration:
In whatever place it is to be undertaken,
If one abides there in the yoga of concentration through this,
The buddhas bestow their blessing.
Meditate intently on the great king Vajra Amṛta
As a vajra kīla,
And strike the supreme maṇḍalas of the ten directions
With it, as with blazing sparks of fire.
Thus too it was spoken.

This is the samādhi called the Peaceful Vajra that Tames Beings.

Abiding in the center of the sphere of space,
Imagine the sublime peaceful maṇḍala.
Having imagined the form of Vairocana,
Place the petitioner at one's own heart.
Meditate that the sphere of space
Is completely filled with supreme Locanās.
Gathering them in, with a ball of light
Let it descend upon the sick person;
Then from the tips of the pores
The one who keeps the vows emanates clouds of buddhas.
At that time the clouds of buddhas
Bestow empowerment upon him.
The sick person, a samaya vajra,
In an instant becomes glorious.

This is the samādhi called the Array of Clouds of the Samaya of the Buddhas.

Abiding in the center of the space vajra,
Imagine the sublime Mahendra maṇḍala;
Imagine the form of Dharma Holder.

Place the petitioner at one's heart.
Fill the delightful abode of the sphere of space
Completely with the one called the White Lady.
The precious light of the wish-fulfilling jewel,
A ball of light, is to be gathered in;
Let these descend upon the place
Of the body, speech, and mind of the one desired.
From the tips of the pores, clouds of jewels
Are emanated by the one who keeps the vows.
One should meditate on clouds of Dharma
And perform the empowerment correctly.
Through this vajra meditative concentration,
Even what is hard to fill is completely filled.
That is the glorious wish-fulfilling jewel,
The brilliant shining of the vajra of generosity.

This is the samādhi called the Array of the Samaya of the Clouds of Dharma.

Abiding in the center of the sphere of space,
Imagine the vajra maṇḍala of the half moon.
The form of the Dharma of the vajra space before one,
Place the one desired at its heart.
With buddhas and bodhisattvas
The maṇḍala of space is completely filled.
Through the yoga of the five light rays,
Let splendor descend upon that one.
Immediately thereupon,
He enters the mind of all the buddhas.
Equal to Mañjuśrī and radiant,
There he becomes one who performs the work of that state.
Those whose nature is delight confer empowerment on him
And grant him a great festival.
All beings who come upon him,
Merely by seeing him, come under his power.

This is the samādhi called the Array of the Clouds of the Samaya of the Jewel.

In the space vajra, meditate that it is filled
With rākṣasas, fierce, wrathful, and savage,
With jackals and all kinds of crows,
With vultures and with dogs.
Meditate constantly on the enemies
Abiding in the maṇḍala of fire.
Imagining those who do harm to all the buddhas,
One should apply the yoga.
Meditate that their entrails, marrow, blood, and the rest
Are dragged out by all of these.
They are crushed by those bearing various weapons;
If one meditates thus, the enemies will die.
If one meditates intently in this way,
Even a buddha or a vajra holder
Will die within a full half month;
There is no doubt of this.

This is the samādhi called the Array of the Samaya of the Vajra Cloud.

Imagine Vairocana with three faces,
Like the color of an autumn crystal,
White, black, and deep red,
Adorned with topknot and crown ornaments.
Imagine the Vajra Bearer with three faces,
His faces black, white, and red,
With a blazing topknot and crown ornaments,
He who stirs up the world systems.
Imagine the Passionate One with three faces,
His faces red, black, and white,
With topknot, crown ornaments, and enjoyments.
If one meditates thus, one will surely succeed.
Meditate on the wheel, the vajra, and the great lotus
In their right hands.
Meditate on the excellent six-armed vajra ones
Holding various weapons.
She who wishes to benefit all sentient beings,
Locanā, imagine with three faces,
White, black, and deep red;
Meditate intently on her in a beautiful form.
The Space-born Eye, the great queen,
Stainless and three-faced,
In the manner of black, red, and white:
Meditate on her always in a beautiful form.
The Eye of Speech Vajra, the great queen,
In the aspect of red, black, and white,
Three-faced, swiftly brings accomplishment;
Meditate intently on her beautiful colors.
The consort holding the vajra utpala,
With three faces of brilliant radiance,
In the aspect of yellow, black, and white:
If one meditates on her, one will attain wisdom.
The great wrathful one Yamāntaka,
Three-faced, with fierce and excellent light,
Sharp, terrifies even the terrifying;
Meditate intently on his black color.
The great wrathful one Aparājita,
Who sends forth a sound of fierce laughter,
Three-faced, with many light rays:
Imagine them streaming forth intensely.
The great wrathful one Hayagrīva
Blazes intensely like the fire at the end of the eon;
Three-faced, he crushes the malicious with his feet.
The one possessed of yoga should always meditate on him.
Vajra Amṛta, the great wrathful one,
With streaming light rays, is intensely agitated;
Fierce, like a blazing vajra,
He terrifies even the terrifying.
The great wrathful one, wisdom of desire,
Three-faced, he causes terror;
Four-armed, supreme among those who cause terror:
Meditate intently on Ṭakkirāja.
Mahābala, the great vajra,
Who dispels the darkness of the three worlds
And vanquishes all the malicious:
Always meditate on him as three-faced.
The great wrathful one Nīladaṇḍa
Terrifies the three realms;
Three-faced, arisen from the vajra,
Meditate on him blazing and sharp.
The vajra Acala, the great wrathful one,
Arisen from the vajra, glances sidelong;
Peaceful, he holds a sword and a noose.
The one who keeps the vows should meditate on him as three-faced.
The Great Uṣṇīṣa, the single syllable,
Sends light rays streaming forth everywhere;
Three-faced, with a blazing wheel,
One should meditate on the excellent light of concentration.
Sumbha, holding supreme wisdom,
Truly makes an ocean of terror;
His body three-faced, streaming blazing light:
One should meditate on the maṇḍala of concentration.
Tejorāśi, the uṣṇīṣa king,
And whoever else possesses a wheel of mantra:
Through these samaya enjoyments
One should meditate on each of them individually.
The samādhis of the mantras
Have been taught as immeasurable.
Each one of the wrathful ones as well
Far surpasses the expanse of space.

Abiding in the center of the sphere of space,
Imagine the sublime maṇḍala of clarity.
Meditating intently on the form of the buddha,
Imagine Vairocana:
Clear and peaceful like the moon,
With light like light rays of many kinds,
Truly arisen like a mirror,
The ornament of the three realms,
Adorned with every ornament.
If one imagines him thus, one sees awakening.
One who meditates intently on the vajra of concentration
Will, in this very life, attain
The great nature of the buddhas,
Which holds sway over all the worlds.

This is the samādhi called the Beautiful Vajra, the Source of the Samaya of Vairocana.

Abiding in the center of the sphere of space,
Imagine the sublime vajra maṇḍala.
Meditating intently on the form of the buddha,
Imagine Vajrasattva,
Like a body wrathful and radiant,
With an even light of many kinds of blaze,
Endowed with the supreme of all aspects,
Fully adorned with every ornament.
If one meditates on the abode of peaceful wisdom,
One swiftly attains vajrahood itself.
Through this, one who intently urges on the vajra of concentration
Will, in this very life, attain
The great nature of the vajra,
Which holds sway over all sentient beings.

Then the Blessed One, the great Vajradhara, lord of the samaya of all the tathāgatas, entered into equipoise in the samādhi called the Supreme Samaya of Peace, and drew forth from his body, speech, and mind vajra this very consort of all the tathāgatas:

Who fully accomplishes all rites,

Accomplishes them merely through recitation.

by the defining mark of the arising of the application.

Now, this is the heart mantra for transferring all poisons: oṃ.

The vajra poison of the gaze and the like,
and other unbearable poisons as well,
Wisdom Vajra summons
and makes enter the maṇḍala of Space Vajra.

The Blessed One, Vajra of the great pledge of poison, spoke thus.

Now, this is the heart mantra of Samaya Vajra for curing poison: hūṃ.

This heart mantra, the great vajra,
one should visualize as white in color,
a cloud of light, greatly blazing,
cool like the rays of the moon.
By applying it at the four places,
the victim at once rises up as the poison is gathered.
Having meditated on it twice or three times,
if one imagines the poison being vomited out,
even what fills all the realms of space
is in an instant made free of poison.

Thus too he spoke.

Now, this is the heart mantra for drawing out all secondary poisons: āḥ.

Tumors, blisters and pustules,
and whatever else is described as disease,
in accordance with the command of Vajrapāṇi,
vanish merely by being imagined.
A great lotus of eight petals,
stainless like the hare-marked moon:
imagine oneself seated at its center,
fully endowed with five rays of light.
In tormenting, the pledge is black;
in urging, it is like white.
This state of meditative concentration is secret,
the great secret, stainless wisdom.

Now, these are the syllable words of the vajra heart mantra for curing outer and inner diseases: jinajik ārolik bajradhṛk.

Whatever syllable word one wishes,
through faith one will gain its qualities.
One should meditate on such a form.
By urging the vajra of disease,
the pledge in the form of a monkey,
or else one that comes and carries it off,
should be imagined as emerging
from the seats of one's own body, speech and mind.
Whether a wheel or a vajra,
meditate on it abiding in the vajra state,
and meditate that by it the pledges
of body, speech and mind are crushed to powder.
From then on the perfect buddhas
and the greatly renowned bodhisattvas
bestow the sublime state of blessing
with delighted eyes.

He also spoke thus:

In the vajra of one's own body, speech and mind,
imagine clouds of buddhas.
Meditate on the vajra king, a great cloud,
and on being freed from disease.
All the buddhas of the ten directions
and the wise bodhisattvas
should be imagined as wrathful toward that one:
causing death is the sublime aim.
Through these applications of meditative concentration,
whatever is known as born of karma,
through the practice of reciting one hundred and eight times,
will be destroyed within seven days.
Or else, with one's own king of mantras,
contemplate the rite of vajra meditative concentration.
This is the pledge for all diseases,
exceedingly hard to transgress.

Then the king, the Vajra Holder,
the great light of wisdom,
the great vajra, liberation through desire,
spoke these words:

Phenomena are like dreams,
unborn by their essential nature.
With regard to that reality, pure by intrinsic nature,
the vajra of illusion is proclaimed.
The practitioner devoted to the aim
of meditative concentration and mantra recitation
constantly sees the buddhas and bodhisattvas,
seeing them in two kinds of division.

Now, this is the pledge state of the great dream.

One who has gained the supreme wisdom of awakening
sees the light of the buddhas as excellent,
and quickly sees himself as well
in the body of the buddhas' perfect enjoyment.
He sees great beings
of the three realms making offerings to him.
His own form equal in light to great wisdom,
he sees buddhas and bodhisattvas
making offerings to him without fail
with the five qualities of sense pleasure.
As the great form of Vajrasattva,
as greatly renowned Vajradharma,
as the greatly renowned Secret Vajra:
thus in dreams he sees his own form.
Bodhisattvas and the Vajra Bearer
and great buddhas, paying homage,
bestow the accomplishments of body, speech and mind:
one will see dreams such as these.
If one sees lovely goddesses
fully adorned with every ornament,
and boys and girls,
one will attain accomplishment.
He will surely see all the buddhas
of the ten directions dwelling in their fields,
and they, their minds delighted,
bestow the delightful treasury of Dharma.
His body abiding in the wheel of Dharma,
encircled by all the buddhas:
this he will see through the application of the pledge
abiding in the vajra of meditative concentration.
Varied groves of delight,
adorned with goddesses and the like,

are seen by one who keeps the pledge of meditative concentration.
He sees himself fully empowered
by the buddhas and bodhisattvas,
and surely sees great kings
of the vidyādharas making offerings to him.

Thus too he spoke.

Seeing various stainless dreams
that have arisen from the vajra,
he will accomplish the unsurpassed
born from the vajra of body, speech and mind.
If, with vajra intellect, one sees
outcastes, those who deal with dogs, and the like,
the wise one will accomplish
the state of the mind of Vajrasattva.

Now, this is the heart of the pledge of examining dreams.

In one's own mind, the mind as the object of focus,
all phenomena are firmly established.
This abode of the mind of Space Vajra
is without phenomena, without the nature of phenomena.

Then all the Blessed Ones, the tathāgatas, were amazed and filled with wonder, and they asked Vajrasattva, who cuts off doubts about the pledges of the body, speech and mind of all the tathāgatas:

"Blessed One, what is this?
In phenomena that lack essential nature
the reality of phenomena is fully taught;
space is meditated upon in space.
How wonderful! A true marvel has occurred!"

Then the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Vajrapāṇi, the body, speech and mind of all the tathāgatas, spoke thus to all the tathāgatas:

"Blessed Ones, all you tathāgatas, space is not endowed with any phenomenon whatsoever, nor is it not endowed with one. Space is included in all phenomena, yet it does not think, 'I follow along with all.' In the same way, Blessed Ones, all you tathāgatas, dreams and what arises from dreams should be understood.

"Blessed Ones, all you tathāgatas, it is furthermore like this: space cannot be examined, cannot be seen, and is unobstructed. In the same way, Blessed Ones, all you tathāgatas, all phenomena too should be understood.

"Blessed Ones, all you tathāgatas, it is furthermore like this: the pledge of body, speech and mind, of all phenomena, and of the vajra state pervades everything and is of a single essential nature, namely the essential nature of mind. Whatever is the element of body, speech and mind, and the element of space: these two are nondual and cannot be made two.

"Blessed Ones, all you tathāgatas, it is furthermore like this: all sentient beings abide in the element of space, and that element of space does not abide in the desire realm, does not abide in the form realm, and does not abide in the formless realm either. Whatever phenomenon does not abide in the three realms is without birth. That which is without birth cannot be examined by any phenomenon at all. Therefore, Blessed Ones, all you tathāgatas, all phenomena are without essential nature.

"O Blessed Ones, all you tathāgatas, it is furthermore like this: the awakening mind is the aspect of the vajra state that gives rise to the wisdom of all the tathāgatas. That awakening mind does not abide in body, does not abide in speech, and does not abide in mind. Whatever phenomenon does not abide in the three realms has no birth. This is the vajra state that gives rise to the wisdom of all the tathāgatas.

"O Blessed Ones, all you tathāgatas, a dream does not think, 'I shall see the state of dreaming in the three realms.' Nor does a person think, 'I shall see a dream.' All the activities of the three realms are like a dream, resemble a dream, and arise from a dream. In the same way, Blessed Ones, all you tathāgatas, all the buddhas and bodhisattvas, however many reside in all the world systems of the ten directions, and all sentient beings, however many there are, should be understood as being, in a dream, in the state of selflessness.

"Blessed Ones, all you tathāgatas, it is like this. For example, the precious wish-fulfilling jewel is the chief of all jewels and is endowed with every quality. Whatever sentient beings pray to that which is the chief of all jewels and endowed with every quality, whether for gold, for jewels or for silver, it provides it all to them merely by their thinking of it. Yet those jewels and the rest do not abide in the mind, nor do they abide in the wish-fulfilling jewel. In the same way, Blessed Ones, all you tathāgatas, all phenomena and all the qualities of a buddha too should be understood."

Then all those Blessed Ones, the tathāgatas, opening wide their delighted eyes, said this to the Tathāgata Body, Speech, and Mind Vajra of all the tathāgatas:

"Blessed One, how wonderful it is that the qualities of a buddha follow along with all phenomena, which follow along with the element of space!"

Then all those buddhas and bodhisattvas bowed at the feet of the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Vajrapāṇi, and said this: "The Blessed One has taught the accomplishments of the vajra of all mantras, compiled in full. Where do those accomplishments, compiled in full from the vajra of all mantras, abide? From where have they arisen?"

Then Vajrapāṇi, having said "Excellent!" to all the tathāgatas and to all those bodhisattvas, spoke thus to all those tathāgatas: "Blessed Ones, all you tathāgatas, the accomplishment compiled in full from the vajra of all mantras does not abide in the body, speech and mind of all mantras. Why is that? Rather, Blessed Ones, all you tathāgatas, all accomplishments and all the qualities of a buddha abide in the vajra of one's own body, speech and mind. And those vajras of body, speech and mind do not abide in the desire realm, do not abide in the form realm, and do not abide in the formless realm. Mind does not abide in body, nor does body abide in mind. Speech does not abide in mind, nor does mind abide in speech. Why is that? Because, like space, they are pure in their essential nature."

Then those tathāgatas said this to the Body, Speech, and Mind Vajra of all the tathāgatas: "Blessed One, where does the Dharma of all the tathāgatas abide? From where has it arisen?"

Vajradhara declared: "It abides in one's own body, speech and mind. It has arisen from one's own body, speech and mind."

All the Blessed Ones, the tathāgatas, asked: "Where does mind abide?"

He declared: "It abides in space."

They asked: "Where does space abide?"

He declared: "It does not abide anywhere at all."

Then all those buddhas and bodhisattvas were amazed and filled with wonder, and abiding in the true nature of their own minds, they sat reflecting, without saying a word.

This concludes the fifteenth chapter, called "The Arising of the Vajra, the Heart of the Pledge of All Sentient Beings," from the Guhyasamāja, the great secret of the body, speech and mind of all the tathāgatas.

the sublime awakening.

wise Vajrasattva,

exceedingly hard to transgress.

Meditate well on all the buddhas

Jambhala, who accomplishes wealth,
the great yakṣa, should be generated:
peaceful, holding the form of a yakṣa,
with a topknot and a crown ornament, a vajra bearer.
By the rite, the five buddhas
should be meditated upon in the five places.
His vajra amṛta water
one offers while abiding in the state of meditative concentration.
By the rite of Vajrasattva,
imagine his crown ornament.
When this is done, the lord of yakṣas,
the lord who dwells in the wilds, Great Light, is pleased.

This is the meditative absorption called "the glory of the rite for accomplishing the pledge substances of the vajra mudrā."

Then the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Vajrapāṇi, entered into equipoise in the meditative absorption called "the glory of enjoying the objects of desire," and drew forth these very vajra words of the pledge of all yakṣiṇīs from his own body, speech and mind vajra: kṣiṃ.

Abiding in the center of the vajra of the expanse of space,
a square, level and exceedingly beautiful,
made entirely of the four precious substances,
heaped all about with flowers and incense:
imagine the expanse of space
completely filled with all the yakṣiṇīs.
Having imagined clouds of forms,
draw them down through the union of the three vajras.
The forms should be imagined as one.
Distinguishing them by the three vajras,
one should meditate on their meditative concentration.
Abiding in the absorption of Mañjuvajra,
meditate on the wrathful one upon the crown ornament.
Having meditated on the words of the mantra at the heart,
one should undertake the vajra practice.

This is the meditative absorption called "the vajra of meditating on all yakṣiṇīs as abiding in sameness."

Then the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Vajrapāṇi, entered into equipoise in the meditative absorption called "the vajra of the magical display of the accomplishment of all vajra mantras," and drew forth these very subtle accomplishments from his own body, speech and mind vajra.

Body, speech and mind perfectly accomplished,
holding the form of a buddha, with excellent light
like the golden light of Jambu River gold,
abiding in the subtle accomplishments,
accomplishing all, invisibility and the rest,
one becomes the lord Vajradhara.
Through the great accomplishment of the yakṣa queen,
one becomes the chief of the vidyādharas.

Herein, these are the mantra accomplishments, the secret form of all vajra accomplishments.

Through accomplishment of the mantra, all
lovely and beautiful forms,
throughout all the world systems,
bring delight at the mere sight.
One becomes the master of the wish-fulfilling jewel
that accomplishes every uṣṇīṣa.
This is supreme for bringing about the awakening of the buddhas;
one meditates fully on buddhahood itself.

So declared the Blessed One, the vajra of the complete fulfillment of all intentions.

Then the Tathāgata Vajrapāṇi, lord of all tathāgatas, drew forth from his own body, speech and mind vajra this very conduct of fully taking up the knowledge observance of the body, speech and mind of all tathāgatas.

The body, speech and mind vajras,
the focus on body, speech and mind,
are to be done through one's own form itself;
in this way accomplishment will be attained.

Herein, this is the knowledge observance of the body, speech and mind vajra.

The form wears a topknot and crown ornament,
great, and like white in color.
Do everything according to the rite,
fully bound by the vow of mantra.
One who has reached sixteen years of age,
adorned with all ornaments,
lovely of face, with long eyes:
having found her, practice the knowledge observance.
Enjoying the state of Locanā,
one should act with the sign of the vajra.
She who knows the rite of mudrā and mantra,
and is well trained in mantra and tantra,
should be made the consort of the Tathāgata,
firmly established in the awakening of the buddhas.
With the great observance, in the four sessions,
perform the secret offering thoroughly.
Stalks, roots and fruits,
all foods to eat and to chew, should be enjoyed.
By this one quickly becomes a buddha,
the lord, the great ocean of wisdom.
Within six months, all of these
will without doubt be attained here.
Always stealing the wealth of others,
and killing too, is a great marvel.
Practicing the abode of the vajra of desire,
one is fully bound by this vow.
The consort, the hook of the vajra of desire,
Māmakī of the lineage of qualities,
through the mind of the supreme speech vajra:
this is fitting in every way.
Imagine her as one's own mudrā, or with the mind:
the meditative concentration, the three syllables, the Vajra Bearer.
In this way even the omniscient buddhas
will be pleased here without doubt.
The practitioner of steadfast mind
seeks alms in the forests.
Excellent food, set out in rows,
they will give in fear and dread.
If one transgresses the very self of the three vajras,
one perishes and becomes vajra letters.
A goddess, a nāginī, a yakṣiṇī,
an asura woman, or even a human woman:
having found her, perform the knowledge observance;
the wisdom of the three vajras is taught.
This is the great mode of the secret suchness
of all the mantras;
the wisdom of the three vajras arises:
it is full entry into the awakening of the buddhas.

So declared the Blessed One, the vajra of the suchness of the pledge of the knowledge observance of all tathāgatas.

From the Great Secret of the Body, Speech and Mind of All Tathāgatas, the Guhyasamāja, this is the sixteenth chapter, called "the vajra of the manifest and perfect awakening of the maṇḍala of all accomplishments."

Then all the blessed tathāgatas assembled once more and supplicated the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Body, Speech, and Mind Vajra of all tathāgatas:

Akṣobhya Vajra, great wisdom,
great sage of the vajra expanse,
supreme three vajras, the three maṇḍalas,
you who proclaim the secret, homage to you.

Vairocana, great purity,
vajra peace, great joy,
teach the phenomena that by nature are luminous;
Vajra, homage to you.
King of jewels, most profound,
vajra space, stainless sky,
by nature pure and untainted,
proclaim the secret! Homage to you.
Vajra Amitābha, great king,
nonconceptual Vajra Bearer of space,
who has reached the far shore of desire,
proclaimer of the vajra, homage to you.
Amogha Vajra, perfect buddha,
who completely fulfills all intentions,
arisen from pure essential nature,
Vajrasattva, homage to you.

If with these peaceful words of praise,
urged by all the buddhas,
one praises the vajra enjoyment,
one too becomes equal to the buddhas.

Then the Teacher, the Vajra Holder,
out of compassion for all the buddhas,
spoke the vajra speech,
the pure place of the vajra secret:
How wonderful! The great letter
of the sphere of reality of all the buddhas,
by nature luminous and pure,
is stainless like the expanse of space.

Then Vajrapāṇi, lord of all tathāgatas, drew forth this very pledge of the body vajra of all the buddhas from his own body, speech and mind vajra.

By the ocean-light of the wisdom of the buddhas,
the four pledges are to be guarded;
the great flesh should always be eaten.
This is the supreme pledge.

Then Vajrapāṇi, lord of the tathāgatas, drew forth this very pledge of the speech vajra of all the buddhas from his own body, speech and mind vajra.

The four pledges are to be guarded;
by the great letter of the speech vajra,
excrement and urine should always be eaten.
This is the marvelous secret.

Then Vajrapāṇi, lord of all tathāgatas, drew forth this very pledge of the mind vajra of all vajra holders from his own body, speech and mind vajra.

By Vajrasattva of great magical power,
the four pledges are to be guarded;
blood and semen mixed together
should always be eaten by one firm in observance.
This pledge of the body, speech and mind vajra
is a great marvel.
The constant pledge of all the buddhas
is perfectly guarded for the sake of holding the vajra.
Whoever guards this pledge,
Vajrasattva of great light,
realizing his body, speech and mind,
at once becomes a buddha.

Then Vajrapāṇi, lord of all tathāgatas, drew forth this very pledge for the aim of the pratyekabuddhas from his own body, speech and mind vajra.

Teaching with the body, they
abide fully in the vajra body;
this is the way in which beings enter,
the pledge of supreme permanence.

Then Vajrapāṇi, lord of all tathāgatas, drew forth this very pledge of the training of the śrāvakas from his own body, speech and mind vajra.

Those who practice the paths of action of the ten virtues
and have abandoned wisdom:
for all who aspire to the lesser,
that pledge is a great marvel.

Then Vajrapāṇi, lord of all tathāgatas, drew forth this very pledge of Brahmā from his own body, speech and mind vajra.

Whatever actions done by the path of delusion
are terrifying and repulsive,
they teach the awakening of the buddhas
and become the body vajra itself.

Then Vajrapāṇi, lord of all tathāgatas, drew forth this very pledge of Rudra from his own body, speech and mind vajra.

All the women born of the vajra
who dwell in the three realms:
to enjoy them with various things
is the supreme and marvelous pledge.

Then Vajrapāṇi, lord of all tathāgatas, drew forth this very pledge of Viṣṇu from his own body, speech and mind vajra.

As many sentient beings as are born,
abiding in the indivisible three bodies,
should be slain with the vajra of meditative concentration,
even one who is the very self of the vajra space element.

Then Vajrapāṇi, lord of all tathāgatas, drew forth this very vajra of the pledge of the three vajras from his own body, speech and mind vajra.

The body vajra becomes Brahmā,
the speech vajra the Great Lord (Maheśvara, that is, Śiva),
the king of mind, the Vajra Holder,
is himself Viṣṇu of great magical power.

Then Vajrapāṇi, lord of all tathāgatas, drew forth this very pledge of all the yakṣiṇīs from his own body, speech and mind vajra.

Eating flesh and blood,
always intent on the supreme desire for women:
with the pledge of these great vajras, hard to keep,
they should be pleased.

Then Vajrapāṇi, lord of all tathāgatas, drew forth this very pledge of all the queens of the nāga lords from his own body, speech and mind vajra.

Slander, and drinking milk,
always striving at desire and impurity:
by these pledges one should accomplish them;
otherwise one will surely be afflicted.

Then Vajrapāṇi, lord of all tathāgatas, drew forth this very pledge of the daughters of the asuras from his own body, speech and mind vajra.

Wrathful, and weighed down by the burden of pride,
born to the enjoyment of incense and flowers:
the Samaya Vajra beneath the earth,
the hard to tame, the vajra terrifier.

Then Vajrapāṇi, lord of all tathāgatas, drew forth this very pledge of the rākṣasīs from his own body, speech and mind vajra.

With skulls and bones, incense, sesame oil,
and fat, they become greatly delighted.
This pledge of all the bhūtas
is the purifying vajra that accomplishes the great aim.

Then Vajrapāṇi, lord of all tathāgatas, drew forth this very pledge of all the vajra ḍākinīs from his own body, speech and mind vajra.

Eating excrement, urine and blood,
always drinking liquor and the like,
through the union of the vajra ḍākinī,
keep it secret by the characteristics of the places.
Arising everywhere by their essential nature,
they move about throughout the three realms.
All the pledges without remainder, all the accomplishments,
should be practiced by one who wishes to benefit.

This is the meditative absorption called "following the vajra pledge of all the three realms."

Then Vajrapāṇi, lord of all tathāgatas, drew forth the very vajra pledge of bodily accomplishment from his own body, speech and mind vajra.

All three kinds of body
should be made to arise perfectly as vajra;
the realm of sentient beings, in every way,
is always made the body of the buddhas.

Then Vajrapāṇi, lord of all tathāgatas, drew forth the very vajra pledge of the accomplishment of speech from his own body, speech and mind vajra.

All the paths of action of speech without remainder,
in the stainless maṇḍala of the three realms:
this delightful abode of the accomplishment of speech
is a pledge most difficult to transgress.

Then Vajrapāṇi, lord of all tathāgatas, drew forth the very pledge of the complete purification of mind from his own body, speech and mind vajra.

All that is the nature of the mind vajra,
the steadfast one meditates on with a vajra intellect.
This is the explanation of the pledge
endowed with the threefold indivisible vajra.

So declared the Blessed One, Samantabhadra Vajrasattva.

Then Vajrapāṇi, lord of all tathāgatas, drew forth the very vajra pledge of all mantras from his own body, speech and mind vajra.

Buddhas and bodhisattvas,
and likewise pratyekabuddhas and śrāvakas:
if one bows to them through the union of body, speech and mind,
one will fall from the pledge.

Then Vajrapāṇi, lord of all tathāgatas, drew forth the very pledge of the meditative concentration of the body, speech and mind vajra of all tathāgatas from his own body, speech and mind vajra.

With the maṇḍala of Vajrasattva,
body, speech and mind in every way,
through the union of the three vajras,
one should meditate by concentration and by reciting mantra.

Then Vajrapāṇi, lord of all tathāgatas, drew forth the very vow of all mantra vajras from his own body, speech and mind vajra.

Gathering together the realms of sentient beings,
one should urge them with the vajra of meditative concentration.
The supreme of the supreme homage to the three vajras:
this is the source of the Samaya Vajra.

Then Vajrapāṇi, lord of all tathāgatas, drew forth the very vow of approach, accomplishment, close accomplishment and great accomplishment from his own body, speech and mind vajra.

Imagine the expanse of space
completely filled with vajra excrement and urine,
and offer it to the vajras of the three times.
This is the most constant pledge.

Then Vajrapāṇi, lord of all tathāgatas, drew forth the very pledge of the invisibility of all vajras from his own body, speech and mind vajra.

That which is supreme in the four sessions:
practice with a vajra lady each day,
and again, always steal substances as well.
This is the pledge that perfects the vajra.

Then Vajrapāṇi, lord of all tathāgatas, drew forth the very pledge of the vidyādharas of the sky from his own body, speech and mind vajra.

The body, speech and mind vajras:
meditate on the meditative concentration of the crown ornament.
All the pledges of the three vajras
the wrathful ones cannot overcome.

Then Vajrapāṇi, lord of all tathāgatas, drew forth the very pledge of all beginner mantra holders from his own body, speech and mind vajra.

In eating food, be the body vajra;
outwardly, be the Vajra Holder;
and always be Vajradharma
in the ritual act of reciting the sūtras.

So declared the Blessed One, Svabhāvaśuddha Vajra.

Then Vajrapāṇi, lord of all tathāgatas, contemplating the vajra sameness, the vajra essence of the body, speech and mind of all vajra holders, fell silent.

Then bodhisattva great beings, equal in number to the finest atoms of the Mount Merus of inexpressible, inexpressible buddhafields, bowed to all the tathāgatas and said, "Blessed Ones, why does this lord of all tathāgatas remain silent in the midst of the maṇḍala of all the tathāgatas and the retinue of bodhisattvas?"

Then all the blessed tathāgatas said to those bodhisattvas, "Sons of noble family, this lord of the body, speech and mind of all tathāgatas remains silent, having investigated the unchanging state, without essential nature, that is the unchanging state of the essential nature of the body, speech and mind vajra, which cannot be apprehended. Sons of noble family, this lord of all tathāgatas thought thus:

The letter of the body is unborn;
speech and mind too are without characteristics.
Arisen in the conceptions of Space Vajra,
they are grasped through wrong grasping."

Then the bodhisattva great beings, Mañjuśrī and the others, said to all those tathāgatas, "Blessed tathāgatas, please do not regard the vajra words of speech as a source of error. Why is that? Because the one who goes and comes practicing the essential nature of the vajra voice of all tathāgatas is this lord of all tathāgatas. Why is that? Because the great bodhisattvas, beginning with Brahmā, who have attained the wisdom of the great superknowledges, do not know the essential nature of the characteristics of all phenomena, and so they speculate in this way. Some even think, 'Does this great vajra self expound the secret letters without fully knowing the vajra suchness of all the phenomena of all the tathāgatas?'"

Then all the blessed tathāgatas said to those bodhisattvas, "Leave aside you great bodhisattvas; what need is there to speak of you? Even we, who have found the secret letter of the body, speech and mind of all tathāgatas, do not know the awakening of body, speech and mind. Why is that? Because it is manifestly and perfectly awakened as the unborn vajra, without essential nature, arisen from the unchanging. Sons of noble family, as many sentient beings as there are, included among sentient beings, all of them, abiding in awakening, are buddha vajras. Why is that? Because these sentient beings, having taken up the very state of the Vajra Holder of the three bodies, have attained the wisdom of the body, speech and mind vajra."

Then Vajrapāṇi, lord of the body, speech and mind vajra of all tathāgatas, said to all the tathāgatas and to those bodhisattvas:

"Pure by nature and selfless,
the sphere of reality abides without remainder.
Conception has arisen perfectly as vajra;
it is not to be spoken, not to be spoken."

Then all the blessed tathāgatas bowed to the Blessed One, the great Vajrapāṇi, lord of all tathāgatas, and said, "Blessed One, from where does the accomplishment of the body, speech and mind vajra of all tathāgatas arise? Where does it abide, and where does it cease to be?"

Vajradhara, lord of all tathāgatas, said, "Blessed Ones, the vajra abides fully in the continuum of one's own body, speech and mind; and so do all the accomplishments of all tathāgatas, the wisdom of omniscience, and the three realms in their entirety."

All the tathāgatas spoke again: "Blessed One, where do the accomplishment of the body, speech and mind vajra of all tathāgatas and all of the three realms abide?"

The lord of Wisdom Vajra said, "Blessed Ones, the accomplishment of the body, speech and mind of all tathāgatas and all of the three realms abide in the element of space."

All the tathāgatas said, "Blessed One, where does space abide?"

The Vajra Holder said, "Nowhere at all."

Then all the tathāgatas and those bodhisattvas were amazed and astonished, and they uttered these melodious words:

"How wonderful, vajra! O vajra!
How wonderful is the teaching of the vajra!
Where there is no body, speech or mind,
there the body is meditated upon."

Then the Teacher, the Vajra Holder,
to whom all the buddhas pay homage,
the supreme of the three vajras, supreme of the great supreme,
the lord supreme of the three vajras,
spoke of the meditation on the knowledge being
of all accomplishments.
Abiding in the center of the vajra of the expanse of space,
one should meditate on the maṇḍala of the buddhas.
Having meditated fully on the body vajra,
meditate fully on a vajra at the crown of the head.
Three faces and three bodies perfectly arisen
should be imagined radiating forth.
If one imagines holding the vajra wheel,
one will quickly attain awakening.
According to the distinctions of all the families,
imagine this secret.
Those who meditate otherwise
will not gain the supreme accomplishment.

So declared the Blessed One, the knowledge being Vajra Secret.

Imagine pleasing the great queen
born from the three realms.
By this the supreme bull is pleased,
those marked by the three vajra bodies.
Whoever among them meditate on this,
the greatly renowned bodhisattvas,
with great light, within seven days,
will attain the accomplishment of the three bodies.

Then again the Blessed One, Vajrapāṇi, lord of all tathāgatas, drew forth the very great secret, the secret that is the compendium of the body, speech and mind vajra, from his own body, speech and mind vajra.

The pledge of body, speech and mind
is the realization of the meaning of the great mudrā.
If one meditates according to the rite, with all its characteristics,
one will attain buddhahood itself.

Then Vajrapāṇi, lord of all tathāgatas, drew forth once more the very great secret of the body, speech and mind of all practitioners from his own body, speech and mind vajra.

If you desire supreme awakening,
do not bind the mudrās of the hands.
This pledge of all the mantras
even the victors do not transgress.

Then again Vajrapāṇi, lord of all tathāgatas, drew forth the very pledge of all the buddhas from his own body, speech and mind vajra.

Excrement, urine, semen and blood
should never be treated as contemptible.
Always eat them according to the rite.
This secret arises from the three vajras.

Then again the Blessed One, Vajrapāṇi, lord of all tathāgatas, drew forth the very pledge of the speech vajra from his own body, speech and mind vajra.

On the delightful path of the three realms,
as many women as there are,
enjoy them all according to the rite;
do not disparage the speech vajra.

Then again Vajrapāṇi, lord of all tathāgatas, drew forth this very pledge of the mind vajra from his own body, speech and mind vajra.

All the pledges, as many as there are,
abiding in the three vajra bodies,
should be satisfied by the pledge of the three vajras;
do not disparage the mind vajra.

So declared the Blessed One, the Great Samaya of the Three Vajras.

Then again Vajrapāṇi, lord of all tathāgatas, drew forth the very secret vajra of all tathāgatas from his own body, speech and mind vajra.

In brief, the five aggregates
are proclaimed to be the five buddhas.
The vajra sense sources themselves
are the supreme maṇḍala of bodhisattvas.

Then Vajrapāṇi, lord of all tathāgatas, drew forth the very vajra of the accumulation of the three realms from his own body, speech and mind vajra.

Earth is called Locanā;
the water element is called Māmakī;
those called Pāṇḍarā and Tārā
are proclaimed to be fire and wind.
The pledge of the vajra of the expanse of space
is the Vajra Holder himself.

So declared the Blessed One, the great Vajrasattva, lord of the abode of all tathāgatas.

Then the Blessed One, the Body, Speech, and Mind Vajra of all tathāgatas, entered into equipoise in the meditative absorption called "the abode of the sameness of all tathāgatas." Having entered into equipoise, he looked upon the maṇḍala of the retinue of all the tathāgatas and fell silent.

Then the bodhisattva great being Maitreya bowed to all the tathāgatas and said, "Blessed Ones, how should all the tathāgatas and all the bodhisattvas regard the master who has been empowered in the Guhyasamāja, the Great Secret of the body, speech and mind vajra of all tathāgatas?"

All the tathāgatas said, "Son of noble family, that master is a tathāgata: all the tathāgatas and all the bodhisattvas should regard him as the very vajra of the awakening mind."

"Why is that? Because the vajra of the awakening mind and this master are not two and cannot be divided into two. Son of noble family, to teach it in brief: all the buddhas and bodhisattvas who dwell, live and abide in the world systems of the ten directions come at the three times, worship that vajra master perfectly with the offerings of all the tathāgatas, and go back to their own buddhafields; and they also utter these words in the letters of the vajra speech: 'He is the father of all of us tathāgatas. He is the mother of all of us tathāgatas. He is the teacher of all of us tathāgatas,' and so forth.

"Furthermore, son of noble family, the merit of a single pore of the master's body far surpasses even the whole mass of merit arising from the body, speech and mind vajra of all the blessed buddhas, however many dwell in the ten directions. Why is that? Because, son of noble family, the awakening mind has become the essence of the wisdom of all the buddhas, generated and abiding, and so on, up to: it is the source of the wisdom of omniscience."

Then once more the great bodhisattva Maitreya, his mind frightened and terrified, fell silent.

Then the Tathāgata Akṣobhya, the Tathāgata Ratnaketu, the Tathāgata Amitābha, the Tathāgata Amoghasiddhi and the Tathāgata Vairocana entered into equipoise in the meditative absorption called "the vajra that focuses on all the pledges of accomplishment of the Vajra Holder," and said to all those bodhisattvas, "All you bodhisattvas, listen! Even the blessed buddhas of the ten directions, who have arisen perfectly from the vajra wisdom of the three times, come and make offerings and bow to the master of the Guhyasamāja. Why is that? Because he himself is the Teacher of all the tathāgatas and all the bodhisattvas, the Blessed One, the great Vajradhara, lord of the wisdom of all the buddhas."

Then those bodhisattva great beings said to all the tathāgatas, "Blessed Ones, where do the accomplishments of the body, speech and mind of all tathāgatas abide?"

All the tathāgatas said, "The body, speech and mind of the secret of the three bodies abide in the body, speech and mind vajra of the vajra master."

The great bodhisattvas asked, "Where do body, speech and mind vajra abide?"

All the tathāgatas said, "They abide nowhere at all."

Then those great bodhisattvas were amazed and astonished, and remained silent.

Then the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Vajrapāṇi, arose from the meditative absorption of the secret vajra and said to all the tathāgatas and bodhisattvas, "All you tathāgatas and all you bodhisattvas, listen to the great maṇḍala of the meditative absorption called 'the vajra of the arising of the awakening mind of all tathāgatas.'"

Then once more all the tathāgatas and all those bodhisattvas joined their palms and said to the Blessed One Vajradhara, "Blessed One, please explain the great maṇḍala! Sugata, please explain it!"

Imagine, abiding in the center of the expanse of space,
a square, level and exceedingly beautiful.
Through the union of the maṇḍala of the buddhas,
fully urge on the vajra of meditative concentration.
Through concentration on the vajra maṇḍala,
make seats for all the wheel bearers.
With that very rite of offering,
the intelligent one should always perform it.
Having meditated fully on the master at the heart,
perform the empowerment perfectly.
Having imagined the expanse of space
completely filled with all the buddhas,
draw them all down according to the rite
through the three places of empowerment.
By this one who wishes to benefit all sentient beings
will attain awakening,
and all accomplishments, a great marvel:
body, speech and mind will be accomplished.

This is the maṇḍala of meditative concentration called "the vajra of the pledge of all buddhas and bodhisattvas."

Then once again Vajrapāṇi, lord of all tathāgatas, drew forth from his own body, speech and mind vajra the very secret of the body, speech and mind vajra called "the vajra union of all tathāgatas": hūṃ hrīḥ khaṃ.

Imagine the maṇḍala of flesh, bones and the rest
abiding in the center of the expanse of space.
Meditate on Vajrasattva,
the essence of the three, body, speech and mind,
fierce, ugly, wrathful in every way,
like the color of a blue utpala lotus.
Four-armed, according to the rite,

Thus spoke the Blessed One Samantabhadra.

Then all the blessed tathāgatas said this to Vajrapāṇi, lord of all tathāgatas: "Blessed One, if they possess realization of how many secret syllables will those bodhisattva great beings have faith in this vajra of conduct through the secret pledge of all tathāgatas, and meditate on it?"

Then Vajrapāṇi, lord of all tathāgatas, said this to all those tathāgatas: "Blessed Ones, all tathāgatas, if they possess realization of three secret syllables, those bodhisattvas will have faith in this, which is the vajra of the conduct of awakening of all tathāgatas, and will meditate on it."

All the tathāgatas asked, "What are the three?"

The Vajra Holder said, "The body vajra of all tathāgatas, the speech vajra of all tathāgatas, and the mind vajra of all tathāgatas: these are the three."

Then all those tathāgatas bowed at the feet of the Blessed One Vajrapāṇi and remained silent.

Then Vajrapāṇi, lord of all tathāgatas, said to all those tathāgatas and to all those bodhisattvas: "Blessed Ones, all tathāgatas, in the past, after eons as many as the atoms of inexpressibly, inexpressibly many buddhafields had passed, from the time of the Blessed One, the Tathāgata, the Arhat, the perfectly complete Buddha Dīpaṃkara, until the great sage Kāśyapa attained complete and perfect awakening, this was not taught. Why was that? The meaning of the state of the great secret was not explained because at that time, in that age, sentient beings lacked the fortune for it. Nevertheless, Blessed Ones, all tathāgatas, in this Guhyasamāja the awakening of a buddha is perfected in an instant, a moment, a mere second. That awakening which bodhisattvas do not attain, though they strive and search for it for eons as countless as the sands of the river Ganges, the bodhisattva who delights in the Guhyasamāja, in this very life, comes to be counted among those called buddhas of all tathāgatas."

Then those bodhisattvas, having heard these syllabled words of the Speech Vajra, wept profusely. Then all those tathāgatas said this to those bodhisattvas: "Bodhisattvas, do not weep! Do not give rise to the three sufferings!"

Then those bodhisattvas said this to all those tathāgatas:

"Blessed Ones, all tathāgatas, how can we not weep? How can we not give rise to the three sufferings? And why is that? Blessed Ones, we have no fortune for the syllables of the three secrets; we have not even the fortune of hearing their name."

All the tathāgatas declared: "Sons of noble family, do not say that! The syllables are held in common. Sons of noble family, just as you do not know them and have not heard them, so too we, all the tathāgatas, and all the bodhisattvas as well, sons of noble family, have not truly attained these syllables of the three secrets, nor have we become completely and perfectly awakened. And why is that? Because the syllables of the three secrets are utterly pure."

Then all those bodhisattvas remained silent.

Then all the blessed tathāgatas dwelt in the bhagas of the vajra consorts, the essence of the body, speech and mind of all tathāgatas.

Then the consort of the body, speech and mind of all tathāgatas praised with joy the Blessed One, lord of all tathāgatas, the great Vajradhara:

"The realm of sentient beings is your abode, O lord; you are the vajra mind.
Desiring one who is joyful, delightful and meaningful, please protect me.
Protector, if today you wish that I should live,
father, supreme great kinsman of beings, please give me delight."

Then Māmakī, the consort of the mind of all tathāgatas, fully praised the Blessed One, lord of all tathāgatas, the great Vajradhara, with these syllabled words, the supreme offering of vajra desire and joy:

"You, vajra body, whose wheel of command benefits many beings,
who for the aim of the buddhas, the aim of supreme awakening, teach what brings benefit:
Protector, if today you wish that I should live,
with passion please delight me, the pledge of passion."

Then Locanā of the Body, Speech, and Mind Vajra, the consort of Lokeśvara, fully praised the Blessed One, lord of all tathāgatas, the great Vajradhara, with the pledge of enjoying desires:

"You are the vajra speech, benefiting all, full of loving compassion,
ever striving to accomplish the needed aims of the worlds.
Protector, if today you wish that I should live,
delight me through the supremely joyful conduct of Samantabhadra."

Then the consort of the Samaya Vajra of the body, speech and mind of all tathāgatas praised the Blessed One, lord of all tathāgatas, the great Vajradhara, with this joy of bliss and gladness of mind:

"You, the vajra desire, of the supreme pledge, of great beneficial aim,
supreme lineage of the perfect buddhas, compassionate in accord with sameness:
Protector, if today you wish that I should live,
delight me, who have become a treasure of sacred, precious qualities."

Then the Blessed One Vajrapāṇi entered into equipoise in the samādhi called the glory of the tathāgata who enjoys all desires, and with the wheel of the pledge he enjoyed desire with those consorts of all tathāgatas, and remained silent.

Then all these realms of space became completely filled with the semen of the vajra pledge of the body, speech and mind of all tathāgatas, and remained like a vase filled with vajra water.

Then all those sentient beings in this vajra realm of space in whom the three bodies truly arise, and who truly know through the vajra glory, all became tathāgatas, arhats, perfectly complete buddhas, possessing the wisdom of the three vajras.

From then on, all sentient beings were empowered by the body, speech and mind vajra of all tathāgatas as "wholly good, Samantabhadra."

Then Vajrapāṇi, lord of all tathāgatas, said this to all those tathāgatas: "Blessed Ones, all tathāgatas, do you see the sameness of all phenomena?"

Then all those tathāgatas said this to Vajrapāṇi, lord of all tathāgatas: "Blessed One, we see the sameness of the wisdom of all tathāgatas and the conduct of vajra wisdom. Sugata, we see it."

Then all the blessed tathāgatas emerged from the bhaga of the consort of all tathāgatas and said this to the Blessed One, the Tathāgata, the great Vajrapāṇi: "That the awakening of a buddha is to be realized through the syllabled words of desire, Blessed One, is a wonder! Sugata, it is a wonder!"

Then Vajrapāṇi, lord of all tathāgatas, said this to all those tathāgatas: "Blessed Ones, all tathāgatas, do not speak so! And why? Because all phenomena are equal to the pledge of the Space Vajra, there is no aggregate of form, no aggregate of feeling, no aggregate of perception, no aggregate of formations, no aggregate of consciousness; there are no elements and no sense sources. Likewise there is no attachment, no aversion, no delusion; there is no dharma, and no non-dharma either."

Then all those tathāgatas fell silent.

Then the Blessed One Vajrapāṇi, lord of all tathāgatas, declared to all the tathāgatas and to those bodhisattvas: "You should meditate on this secret of the body, speech and mind vajra of all tathāgatas in all world systems. And why? Because these bodhisattvas dwelling in the ten directions have the fortune for this Dharma discourse."

Then Vajrapāṇi, lord of all tathāgatas, declared to Vajradharma: "Son of noble family, hold this suchness of the pledge of all tathāgatas. You are empowered by all the tathāgatas with the name Vajra, Lord of All Dharmas."

Then the bodhisattva great being Vajradharma said, "I shall do so," and fell silent.

Then all those tathāgatas caused their own body, speech and mind to enter the three syllables of Vajrasattva.

Then the Tathāgata Vairocana, abiding in the body vajra of all who dwell in the three realms, focused on the sameness of the body of all tathāgatas and fell silent.

Then the Tathāgata Speech Vajra, abiding in the speech vajra of all who dwell in the three realms, focused on the sameness of the speech of all tathāgatas and fell silent.

Then the Tathāgata Vajrapāṇi, abiding in the mind vajra of all who dwell in the three realms, focused on the sameness of the mind of all tathāgatas and fell silent.

Thus it was declared.

This is the seventeenth chapter, called the King of the Vajra Blessing of the Pledges and Vows of All Tathāgatas, from the Guhyasamāja, the great secret of the body, speech and mind of all tathāgatas.

The great king of tantras called the Guhyasamāja, the great secret of the body, speech and mind of all tathāgatas, is complete.

It was translated, edited and finalized by the Indian preceptor Śraddhākaravarman and the chief editor-translator, the monk Rinchen Zangpo. Later, in the presence of the paṇḍita and lama, the great vajra holder, the venerable Ravīndra, the translator Chak Lotsāwa, the monk Chöjé Pal, respectfully edited and corrected it and finalized it.

Translator's notes

"Vajra consorts" renders rdo rje btsun mo (vajrayoṣit); bhaga, the female organ understood esoterically, is kept as in the Tibetan. "Great knowledge in the form of a man" renders rig pa chen po skyes bu'i gzugs, the great vidyā embodied as a male figure. The fifth tathāgata in the opening list is called simply Vajra Amogha (rdo rje don yod pa) here, and Amoghasiddhi later in the passage; both are kept as written. "Knowledge-person" renders rig pa'i skyes bu (vidyāpuruṣa), the embodied mantra-person that emerges with each heart mantra. In the second emission the Tibetan reads only "speech and mind" rather than "body, speech, and mind"; it is kept as written. Lokeśvara here renders 'jig rten dbang phyug, the form of Amitābha's family. The four wrathful ones drawn forth at the gates correspond to Yamāntaka, Prajñāntaka, Padmāntaka, and Vighnāntaka; the Tibetan names them only by their mantras and by the epithet "Blessing the Maṇḍala." "Beguiles" renders slu ba (deceive, enchant). The chapter division falls within this piece; the second chapter begins with the request in verse. In the verse beginning "Light equal to that of blazing buddhas," the Tibetan reads mnyan pa'i 'od ("light of listening"), which makes no sense here; it is taken as mnyam pa'i 'od, "equal light," echoing the earlier line "equal to the light of blazing buddhas." "Amoghasiddhi" in the verse renders the shortened form don grub. The verses on the deities' colors and emblems are rendered in the order the Tibetan gives them. The opening lines complete a sentence begun at the end of the previous piece ("Then the Teacher, the Vajra Holder ..."). In "Locanā is drawn in the eastern corner," the Tibetan has gla ru, which does not fit; it is read as grwa ru, "in the corner." The antinomian verses of the fifth chapter are rendered literally, as in the Tibetan; the tradition reads them through the higher, esoteric interpretation of the explanatory tantras, and the "mother" of the chief of buddhas is understood as the consort. "The great meat" (mahāmāṃsa) is understood in the commentarial tradition as human flesh; the eating of feces, urine, and the meats listed belongs to the transgressive samaya practices that the exegetical tradition reads both literally for qualified practitioners and in an internal, yogic sense. The line "at the tip of the nose this is auspicious" renders the Tibetan as written (shis). In the verse on austerities the Tibetan reads bstan pas ("taught"), evidently for bsten pas ("relied upon"), which matches the parallel lines; the latter is translated. The Source of the Dharma of Passion is an epithet standing for Amitābha, lord of the passion family, in the sequence of the five buddhas; it is translated literally rather than given a Sanskrit form. The slaying of the host of tathāgatas, the drinking of the pledge substances, and the sexual yoga are read in the exegetical tradition as esoteric instructions on the completion stage, not as literal injunctions to violence. The first line of the verse on alms is terse and its exact force uncertain. The commands to kill all beings, to crush the buddhas of the three times, and to steal all riches are read in the exegetical tradition, for example in the Pradīpoddyotana, as esoteric instructions on the dissolution of ordinary appearances, emptiness, and the seizing of the vital winds, not as literal injunctions. "The supreme young woman of Ratnasambhava" could also be read as "the supreme young woman, a source of jewels." The phrase rendered "you do not care with your gaze for" (spyan ras kyis mi 'tsho) is obscure, and its relation to the following clause, which in the next piece speaks of the tathāgatas watching over these practitioners with their eyes, is uncertain. The injunctions to false speech, deceiving the victors, and harsh speech are understood in the commentarial tradition as figurative teachings on the illusory nature of speech and appearances. In the verse on the three syllables, the word rendered "bond" ('jing) is obscure; the reading is uncertain. In the verse on the buddhas and bodhisattvas who "transgress," the sense, following the context of summoning, is that the deities who fail to come when summoned forfeit their lives. "Rite" renders the Sanskrit loanword kalpa written in Tibetan letters. In the line on the pledge of the three rites, the particle written ག makes no sense and is read as the genitive or locative. The line "a syllable blazing brightly with light rays" is a syllable short and does not name the syllable in the verse itself; the seed syllable thlīṃ follows the stanza. The chapter colophon closes chapter eleven; the next chapter begins with "Then the Teacher, the Vajra Holder." "Rite" renders kalpa (Tibetan rtog pa / kalpa). The phrase rendered "wide-eyed, holding it in the hand" is terse; it may instead refer to holding the sword as one of wide vision, and commentaries differ. "The fierce ones" may denote the Rudras. The word rendered "hook" (kyo ba) in "body, speech, and mind, the hook and the rest" is uncertain; it may denote some other implement held in the hands. "Great flesh" is the standard tantric term for human flesh. "A month and a half" renders the Tibetan idiom "half and two months." The line "Those arisen from the supreme secret have declared accomplishment within a fortnight" is terse; the word rendered "fortnight" may simply mean "a portion" of the period. "Subjugating" renders dbang, here the rite of bringing under control rather than empowerment. In the final lines, "even on one of deluded nature" interprets the terse rmongs bdag nyid; it could also be read as an address to the deluded practitioner. The opening line "even the buddhas will not succeed" is terse; it most likely means that even the buddhas cannot withstand or counteract the rite. Likewise "even if the buddhas should proceed against it" in the verse on terrifying enemies is uncertain in sense. "The petitioner" renders don gnyer, the one who seeks the benefit of the rite, here apparently the sick person to be healed. The Mahendra maṇḍala is the square earth maṇḍala. "The White Lady" renders dkar mo, which may stand for the consort Pāṇḍarā, but this is not certain here. The Vajra Bearer and the Passionate One are epithets apparently of Akṣobhya and Amitābha. The Space-born Eye and the Eye of Speech Vajra appear to be Māmakī and Pāṇḍaravāsinī, and the consort holding the utpala Tārā, following the usual set of four consorts; the Tibetan names them only by these descriptions. In "even a buddha or a vajra holder will die," the Tibetan has an instrumental, which makes the construction awkward; the sense seems to be that the rite is effective even against such beings. The consort meditated on in the third section is called "the supreme Space Vajra" and, in the samādhi's title, "Space Mother"; she is evidently Māmakī. Vajradharmā in the following section corresponds to Pāṇḍaravāsinī. The four mantras are those of the four consorts, Locanā, Māmakī, Pāṇḍaravāsinī, and Tārā, as their wording shows; the Tibetan does not name them here. In the opening section "the blue vajra" refers to the wrathful Nīladaṇḍa, the bearer of the vajra staff. The closing verses of each mantra section are compressed, and their subjects (the buddhas who rejoice, and the consort who acts) are taken from context. The lotus-born vajra wrathful one is Hayagrīva, as his mantra shows. The piece ends in the middle of Nīladaṇḍa's mantra, which continues in the next piece. The "nāgas of great strength" may also be read as "the nāgas of Mahābala," since the mantra just drawn forth is Mahābala's. The line rendered "summoned, bodily, with palms joined" (ཁ་སྦྱར་ལུས་ཀྱིས) is uncertain; ཁ་སྦྱར might instead describe the bodies as bent or bound together. "Lotus of the sky" (ནམ་མཁའི་པདྨོ) is translated literally; it probably denotes celestial women. "Stone fiber" (རྡོ་རྒྱུས) is a literal rendering of an uncertain substance used alongside red ochre in the rite. ཁྲོ་གཉེར་ཅན ("the frowning one") is taken as Bhṛkuṭī, whose name appears in the mantra that follows. མཚན་མ་གཅིག ("a lone sign") is understood as a solitary liṅga or sacred marker, a standard site for fierce rites alongside a lone tree. The epithet ཚམ་ངམ qualifying the vajra peg is obscure; "forceful" is a tentative rendering. The lines "even one who grants the supreme three bodies of the buddhas, who has abandoned both knowing and not knowing" describe the target of the rite and are translated literally. "A girl marked with a mole" renders སྨེ་ཤ་ཅན literally; since the following verses name a dyer's daughter, a dancer's daughter, and the four castes, it may stand for a particular low-status class whose Sanskrit name cannot be confidently identified. "The two organs" (དབང་པོ་གཉིས) are the male and female sexual organs of the union practice. In "Excrement, or else food" the text reads བཤད་པ ("explanation"), which makes no sense among substances to be eaten; it is taken as a slip for བཤང་བ ("excrement"), the substance used throughout the surrounding verses. "The great meat" is human flesh. The line ལྷ་ཡི་ལོངས་སྤྱོད་རིགས་བརྟུལ་ཞུགས, rendered "the divine enjoyments of the vowed families," is obscure. "Countless syllables" interprets བསམ་ཡས ("inconceivable") as referring to what is written on the effigy. "The unfailing wisdom" (གདོན་མི་ཟ་བའི་ཡེ་ཤེས) probably alludes to Amoghasiddhi, lord of the action family, matching the preceding pairings of deity classes with Ratnaketu and Amitāyus. "Descent" (དབབ་པ) refers to rites in which a deity is made to descend into a medium, here a child. The lines "Urging and stiffening, the Great God (Śiva), and the four noble grounds" are obscure and are translated literally. "The vajra poison of the gaze" renders lta ba rdo rje'i dug, taken as the poison that acts through sight (dṛṣṭiviṣa) rather than "view." The line rendered "or else one that comes and carries it off" (ཡང་ན་ཁྱེར་ནི་བྱུང་བ) is obscure in the Tibetan. In "In tormenting, the pledge is black," སྡུག་པ could also be read as "lovely, attractive"; the contrast with urging is uncertain. In the final verse ཡེ་ཤེས་ཀྱོ་བ is unclear; it is rendered loosely as "the light of wisdom" following the next phrase. "Those who deal with dogs" renders ཁྱི་ཡི་སྦྱོར, which probably refers to the outcaste dog-eaters or dog-keepers (śvapāka) paired with the caṇḍālas; the exact sense is uncertain. In "Space is included in all phenomena," the text reads ནམ་མཁའ་དེང་ནི, evidently a slip for ནམ་མཁའ་དེ་ནི, "that space." Secret Vajra translates གསང་བ་རྡོ་རྗེ literally. The chapter colophon in this piece numbers the chapter as the fifteenth, as the Tibetan states. "Lord of knowledge" renders རིག་པའི་དབང་པོ (vidyā-indra), an epithet of Vajradharma here. The line "should not be done with the mind of mantra" renders སྔགས་ཀྱི་སེམས་པས་མི་བྱ literally; its sense is disputed. The couplet "swiftly, of themselves, the supreme ones and the lords of mind arrive" renders the obscure རང་། །རབ་དང་ཡིད་དབང་རྣམས་བྱོན. "No fault will arise" reads ཉེས་མི་འགྱུར for the written ཉེ་མི་འགྱུར. "Striking terror and fear" takes འཇིགས་ཤིང་སྐྲག as Vajrasattva terrifying the obstructors. The commands to kill, lie, steal and resort to others' women are the well-known antinomian pledge of this tantra, which the commentarial tradition reads esoterically; they are given here as the text states them. Bodhi Vajra renders བྱང་ཆུབ་རྡོ་རྗེ as a name. "The great flesh" (ཤ་ཆེན) is the tantric term for human flesh. "The great suffering should be urged with the heart mantra" renders སྡུག་བསྔལ་ནི་ཆེན་པོ་སྙིང་པོས་བསྐུལ literally; the object of the urging is unclear. "Servants" renders མངག་གཞུག་པ, the attendant spirits dispatched in rites. In "with the great laughter of the vajra body," དགོད་པ may instead mean "placing, setting out." "Knowledge observance" renders rig pa'i brtul zhugs (vidyāvrata), the observance practiced with a consort (vidyā). The epithet of the yakṣa lord, 'brog gnas dbang po, "lord who dwells in the wilds," is translated literally, as is "Great Light" ('od chen); neither is identified with confidence.
