About this text

The Noble Teaching on the Inconceivable Qualities of the Dharma of the Buddhas, known in Sanskrit as the Ārya-buddha-dharma-acintya-nirdeśa and in Tibetan as Phags pa sangs rgyas kyi chos bsam gyis mi khyab pa bstan pa, is a Mahāyāna sūtra exploring the inconceivable and transcendent qualities of the buddhas and their dharma. The text unfolds as a teaching prompted by the reflections of a great assembly of bodhisattvas, each contemplating a different aspect of the buddhas' inexhaustible qualities — from their purified buddha-fields and aspirations to their lineage, arising, and bodies — suggesting a wide-ranging meditation on the nature of awakening that lies beyond ordinary conceptual grasp. It belongs to the genre of discourses in which the wonders of the buddhas serve as the occasion for doctrinal elaboration directed at the bodhisattva path. This text is Toh 187 in the Kangyur, where it is classified among the Mahāyāna sūtras.

Translation

In the language of India: Ārya-buddha-dharma-acintya-nirdeśa. In the language of Tibet: The Noble Teaching on the Inconceivable Qualities of the Dharma of the Buddhas. First section.

Homage to the assembly of buddhas and the many bodhisattvas.

Then, from among the many bodhisattvas gathered there, certain bodhisattvas gave rise to various thoughts. Some thought: "The buddha-fields of the blessed buddhas are inconceivable." Others thought: "The purified aspirations born from the previous prayers of the blessed buddhas are inconceivable." Others thought: "The lineage of the Tathāgatas belonging to the blessed buddhas is inconceivable." Others thought: "The arising of the buddhas among the blessed ones is inconceivable." Others thought: "The body of the Tathāgata belonging to the blessed buddhas is inconceivable." Others thought: "The voice of the Tathāgata belonging to the blessed buddhas is inconceivable." Others thought: "The primordial wisdom of the Tathāgata belonging to the blessed buddhas is inconceivable." Others thought: "The emanations of the Tathāgata belonging to the blessed buddhas are inconceivable." Others thought: "The conduct free from attachment of the blessed buddhas is inconceivable." And others thought: "The liberation of the buddhas among the blessed ones is inconceivable."

Then the Blessed One, knowing with his mind the thoughts arising in the minds of those bodhisattvas, blessed the bodhisattva Utpalahṛdaya with the blessing of the buddha's blessing power. Through this, that bodhisattva entered into the flow of the buddha's primordial wisdom; he became illuminated by the radiance of the Tathāgata; he became permeated throughout with the splendor of the buddha; he came to abide in the fearlessness of the buddha; he came to be endowed with the entry into and the flowing-forth within the dharmadhātu, the basic space of phenomena, of the buddhas; he entered into that domain through the greatness and the miraculous powers of the buddhas; he came to abide in the ten-fold conduct free from attachment and free from obstruction; he came to know exclusively, one after another, the lineage of all the buddhas; and he came to engage in and accomplish the ways of the Dharma of the buddhas, which are beyond telling.

Then the bodhisattva Utpalahṛdaya, having fully understood and realized the entry into the dharmadhātu, accomplished it within the domain of a mind free from attachment. Through the conduct of a bodhisattva, he abandoned the obscurations of all phenomena; he entered into and was endowed with the aspiration of Samantabhadra; he fully understood all the buddhas and beheld the buddhas; he donned the great armor of great compassion and gazed upon all sentient beings, purifying them completely; he established the maṇḍala of the sacred commitments of the conduct of all bodhisattvas; he reached the furthest limit of moments; he manifestly accomplished the primordial wisdom of the buddhas; he beheld the inexhaustible; he was to make omniscience directly present; all dhāraṇīs became manifest to him; and having realized the four correct and individual knowledges, the bodhisattva Utpalahṛdaya then, through the power of the buddhas, spoke thus to the bodhisattva Padmahṛdaya:

"O son of the Victors, the conduct of the blessed buddhas is immeasurable! O son of the Victors, the blessed buddhas abide in great emanation! O son of the Victors, the activities of all the marks of the body of the blessed buddhas have been fully acquired! The blessed buddhas, endowed with the intention of meditative absorption, turn the wheel of Dharma. The blessed buddhas demonstrate the four correct and individual knowledges in a manner beyond all expression."

The blessed buddhas abide in the inconceivable Dharma of all the blessed buddhas. The blessed buddhas send forth immeasurable buddha voice and melody. The blessed buddhas realize and penetrate the inexpressible dharmadhātu. The blessed buddhas are the supreme sovereign among all superknowledges, fully awakened.

O son of the Victors, in brief, the blessed buddhas enter into the boundless expanse of the dharmadhātu, free from all obscuration at its edges. O son of the Victors, the dharmadhātu of the blessed buddhas is without edge or center.

There are ten qualities of a buddha. What are these ten?

The form body of the blessed buddhas, being without edge or center, is perfectly pure and unstained, having abandoned all wandering about. The blessed buddhas, through the eye of non-attachment toward all phenomena, which is without edge or center, attain fully manifest awakening in the very evenness of that eye. The blessed buddhas, through the ear of non-attachment, which is without edge or center, hear without obstruction the sounds of all beings. The blessed buddhas, through the sense base of the nose, which is without edge or center, have attained supreme dominion over all buddhas. The blessed buddhas, through the tongue of the Blessed One, vast and subtle, without edge or center, move through the voice and melody of the entire expanse of all phenomena. The blessed buddhas, through the body without edge or center, exhausting without remainder all residues of karma, display and make accessible the Tathāgata in the mindstreams of all sentient beings throughout every realm of sentient beings. The blessed buddhas, through the mental action without edge or center, inseparable from the dharmakāya and not attached to the three times, follow in a course of pure activity. The blessed buddhas, abiding in the liberation of a buddha, without edge or center, display inexhaustible buddha emanations and miraculous displays. The blessed buddhas, through the perfect purity of every world-realm, without edge or center, bestow blessings of inexpressible adornment and display, just as intended, the pure buddha-fields free of affliction. The blessed buddhas fully awaken to all phenomena through the distinctive conduct of all bodhisattvas, the perfect completion of aspirations, the sport of primordial wisdom, the perfection of Dharma, and the perfection of the Dharma of the sport of primordial wisdom.

O son of the Victors, these are the ten qualities of a buddha that belong to the dharmadhātu without edge or center of the Tathāgatas, arhats, perfectly and fully awakened buddhas.

O son of the Victors, the blessed buddhas display ten characteristics by which the defining marks of buddha wisdom are fully accomplished. What are these ten?

In a single instant, they display passing away from the Tuṣita heaven in every world-realm. In a single instant, they display the bodhisattva life in every world-realm. In a single instant, they display taking birth in every world-realm. In a single instant, they display sitting beneath the tree of awakening and attaining full manifest awakening in every world-realm. In a single instant, they display turning the wheel of Dharma in every world-realm.

In a single instant, they display in every world-realm the knowing of exactly when sentient beings are ripe and ready to be tamed, and the satisfying of them accordingly. In a single instant, they display in every world-realm the inconceivably varied forms of the Tathāgata's body, each appearing in accordance with how the sentient beings there are to be tamed. In a single instant, they display in every world-realm the treasury of the Tathāgata's omniscience — supreme among the noble ones, adorned with inconceivably varied ornaments. In a single instant, they display in every world-realm the complete purity of all things. In a single instant, they display in every world-realm the arising of the buddhas of past, present, and future; the graduated conduct practiced through varied faculties, diligence, and faith; and the direct knowledge of lineage by the buddhas of the three times.

O sons of the Victor, these are the ten characteristics by which the blessed buddhas display the defining marks of fully accomplishing buddha wisdom.

O sons of the Victor, the blessed buddhas have ten timings they never transgress. What are these ten?

The blessed buddhas, knowing the right time, never transgress the time of attaining full manifest awakening. The blessed buddhas, knowing the right time, never transgress the ripening — the karmic maturation of perfectly virtuous ripening. The blessed buddhas, knowing the right time, give prophecies to bodhisattvas who are fully ripened. The blessed buddhas, knowing the right time, display miracles in accordance with the intentions of sentient beings. The blessed buddhas, knowing the right time, display the blessing of the buddha's body in accordance with the faith of sentient beings. The blessed buddhas, knowing the right time, abide in equanimity. The blessed buddhas, knowing the right time, enter villages, towns, and cities. The blessed buddhas, knowing the right time, accept those sentient beings who are thoroughly purified in mind. The blessed buddhas, knowing the right time, tame without force those sentient beings who are afflicted and difficult to tame. The blessed buddhas, knowing the right time, display inconceivably varied emanations of the buddha.

O sons of the Victor, these are the timings from which the blessed buddhas never transgress.

O sons of the Victor, the blessed buddhas have ten domains that are unequalled and inconceivable. What are these ten?

With a single cross-legged seat, pervading all world-realms without exception. With a single verse at its root, perfectly revealing all buddhas. With a single ray of light, illuminating all world-realms without exception. With a single body, displaying all bodies. With a body that follows after all world-realms without exception, dwelling focused in one direction. With a single wisdom, penetrating all phenomena without obstruction. Knowing the right time, with a single arising of mind, pervading without remainder the expanse of all phenomena. Following after the immeasurable greatness of buddhahood, in a single step revealing suchness. Throughout all three times, pervading with the glorious splendor of buddha-fields. The buddhas, unlike all beings, move in a single essential nature of past, present, and future — acting in non-duality.

O Children of the Victor, these are the ten incomparable and inconceivable domains of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones.

O Children of the Victor, the Tathāgatas possess ten knowledges of accomplishment through the blessing of buddhahood. What are these ten? The knowledge of accomplishing aspirational prayers by engaging in all phenomena without abiding anywhere. The knowledge of accomplishing a body without a body. The knowledge of accomplishing the full and perfect awakening through the non-duality of all phenomena. The knowledge of accomplishing the taming of sentient beings while abiding in the ultimate truth of the selflessness of sentient beings. The knowledge of accomplishing the manifestation of diverse characteristics even though all phenomena are without characteristics. The knowledge of accomplishing the gathering and transformation of all world-realms and their objects without remainder. The knowledge of accomplishing the maturation of karma without grasping at any object whatsoever. The knowledge of accomplishing all phenomena within the expanse that is inexpressible. The knowledge of accomplishing that which is neither defiled nor purified. And the knowledge of accomplishing the arising of characteristics of birth and arising, together with the assembly of conditions and combinations, even though all phenomena are without birth.

O Children of the Victor, these are the ten knowledges of accomplishment through the blessing of buddhahood possessed by the Tathāgatas.

O Children of the Victor, the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, possess ten immeasurable attributes. What are these ten? Following after all three times is an attribute of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones. Possessing the three miracles that tame all sentient beings is an attribute of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones. Being skilled in the thorough analysis of the root of the buddha-Dharma, dhāraṇīs, and verses is an attribute of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones. Possessing all turnings of the wheel of Dharma and the Tathāgata's four kinds of correct and thorough knowledge is an attribute of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones. Never abandoning any sentient being through great compassion is an attribute of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones. At all times beholding all sentient beings and constantly benefiting them with equanimity is an attribute of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones. Taming all sentient beings through the roots of virtue is an attribute of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones. Abiding without attachment to the expanse of all phenomena is an attribute of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones. In a single moment, encompassing all the arising of buddhas of the past, future, and present is an attribute of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones. And in a single day following after and revealing all the kalpas of the three times is an attribute of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones.

O Children of the Victor, these are the ten immeasurable attributes of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones.

O Children of the Victor, the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, possess ten supreme and utterly difficult-to-bear excellences of Dharma. What are these ten? Vanquishing and demolishing the assaults of Māra is the great supreme and utterly difficult-to-bear excellence of Dharma of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones. Utterly cutting through all adversaries who draw near on one side and all opposing forces on the other is a great supreme and utterly difficult-to-bear excellence of Dharma. Taming and satisfying all sentient beings is a great supreme and utterly difficult-to-bear excellence of Dharma of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones. Going to all world-realms by virtue of the power to tame sentient beings is a great supreme and utterly difficult-to-bear excellence of Dharma of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones. Thoroughly penetrating and knowing the expanse of Dharma is a great supreme and utterly difficult-to-bear excellence of Dharma of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones.

Following all world-realms without exception with a body that is non-dual, and manifesting the varied forms of a buddha's body — this is a great supreme and utterly difficult-to-bear excellence of Dharma of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones. The buddhas, the Blessed Ones, entering into the domain of the four kinds of precise knowledge through all the branches of melodious voice, and teaching those four kinds of precise knowledge, and taming through hearing sentient beings who are free of delusion and whose minds are clear — this is a great supreme and utterly difficult-to-bear excellence of Dharma.

The buddhas, the Blessed Ones arising from every pore of the body — just as from a single pore — as many as the subtle dust-particles of all the elements of the body, and in like manner from every pore of a buddha's body bringing forth in succession sentient beings as numerous as the subtle dust-particles of all world-realms — this is a great supreme and utterly difficult-to-bear excellence of Dharma of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones.

A Tathāgata, arhat, perfectly and completely awakened buddha displaying, within a single subtle dust-particle of his domain, buddha-fields as numerous as the subtle dust-particles of all world-realms; the adornments of those buddha-fields blazing forth by their very nature, turning the wheel of Dharma, taming sentient beings in an uninterrupted stream, and yet without sending anything forth, without harm, and without harm throughout all world-realms — without having enlarged that subtle dust-particle and without having diminished the world-realm — abiding having thoroughly realized and known the dharmadhātu, the basic space of phenomena: this is a great supreme and utterly difficult-to-bear excellence of Dharma of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones.

The Tathāgata attaining complete and perfect awakening in a realm devoid of darkness, and then appearing luminously even within that which is accompanied by darkness; leading sentient beings to the state of the Dharma of the buddhas, which is free of obscuration and utterly free of dimness; and in that state following in accord with the ten powers of the Tathāgata, the Blessed One: this is the tenth great supreme and utterly difficult-to-bear excellence of Dharma of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones.

O son of the Victors, these are the ten great supreme and utterly difficult-to-bear excellences of Dharma of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, by which they overwhelm all others.

O son of the Victors, there are ten ways in which the greatness of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, is in all respects blameless and utterly pure. What are these ten?

The greatness of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, is perfectly accomplished and in all respects utterly blameless and pure.

The supremely lofty lineage of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones — surpassing all the buddha lineages of past, future, and present — is in all respects blameless and utterly pure, and the Tathāgata's lineage is pure.

The buddhas, the Blessed Ones, not abiding in consciousness, and the greatness of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, who are the last among those yet to come — in all respects blameless and pure.

The buddhas, the Blessed Ones, who have vanquished abiding in all phenomena and who conduct themselves without abiding in the three times — this is the greatness of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, blameless in all respects and utterly pure.

Because the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, take up nothing manifold, they are of a single adornment in nature — this is the greatness of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, blameless in all respects and utterly pure.

The buddhas, the Blessed Ones, possessed of merit that is inexhaustible, abiding in the dharmadhātu, the basic space of phenomena, which is equal to the limits of the past and the limits of the future — endowed with this inexhaustible merit,

this is the greatness of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, blameless in all respects and utterly pure.

For the sake of taming sentient beings, the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, abide without attachment throughout all world-systems, in boundless forms and colors. This is the greatness of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, blameless in all respects and utterly pure.

The buddhas, the Blessed Ones, having attained the four kinds of fearlessness, abide without anxiety, roar the lion's roar as the supreme of herds, crush their challengers like a great tiger, and teach the Dharma with a mind like blazing fire. This is the greatness of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, blameless in all respects and utterly pure.

The buddhas, the Blessed Ones, passed into complete nirvāṇa inconceivably many and more than inconceivably many eons ago, and yet by means of many names rightly deployed they continue to purify the field of giving of sentient beings, exactly in accordance with their entry into the teaching. And for as long as that lasts, even those who passed into complete nirvāṇa long ago — the merit-field created by making offerings to the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, will never be exhausted. This is the greatness of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, blameless in all respects and utterly pure.

The buddhas, the Blessed Ones, though dwelling in world-systems inconceivably many and more than inconceivably many times more distant than all other world-systems, nevertheless, whenever a sentient being dwelling in this world-system mindfully recollects a buddha, the Blessed One, they cause their form body to appear before that being. This is the greatness of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, blameless in all respects and utterly pure.

O sons of the Victor, these are the ten greatnesses of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, blameless in all respects and utterly pure.

O sons of the Victor, the ten utter purities of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones — what are the ten? The utter purity of the former aspirational prayers of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones; the holy conduct of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones; the moral discipline of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, which is pure and utterly pure, free from attachment; the fact that the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, utterly purely cause all sentient beings to emerge from the aggregates; the utter purity of the buddha-field of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones; the utter purity of the retinue of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones; the utter purity of the dharmakāya of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones; the purity and utter purity of the major marks and excellent minor marks of the form body of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones; the utter purity of the dharmakāya of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones; and the utter purity of the unobscured aggregate of the gnosis of omniscience of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones; and the utter purity of the supreme excellence attained by the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, in that they accomplish the liberation and the overcoming of all buddhas. O sons of the Victor, these are the ten utter purities of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones.

O sons of the Victor, at all times and throughout all world-systems and realms, there are ten activities of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones. What are the ten? At all times, revealing buddhas to sentient beings who have succeeded in mindfully recollecting a buddha; at all times, teaching the Dharma to sentient beings who are aware as sentient beings;

at all times, generating immeasurable roots of virtue in sentient beings whose minds are utterly purified with respect to the Buddha; at all times, causing sentient beings who have passed along the path of the unchanging Dharma to realize gnosis; at all times, never interrupting the activity of the buddha for all beings throughout their destinies; at all times, the Tathāgatas passing through all world-systems without attachment; at all times, never interrupting great compassion toward all sentient beings; at all times, the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, abiding in emanation; at all times, the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, abiding as manifold emanated buddhas; and at all times, the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, abiding in the dharmadhātu, the basic space of phenomena, by the power of the buddhas. O sons of the Victor, these are the ten activities of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, present at all times throughout all world-systems and realms.

O sons of the Victor, there are ten inexhaustible oceans of wisdom of the Tathāgatas, the arhats, the perfectly and completely awakened buddhas. What are the ten? The inexhaustible ocean of wisdom of the body of the Tathāgata of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones; the inexhaustible ocean of wisdom through which the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, have attained immeasurable buddha activities; the inexhaustible ocean of wisdom of the domain of the buddha's vision of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones; the inexhaustible ocean of wisdom of the immeasurable and inconceivable roots of virtue of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones; the inexhaustible ocean of wisdom through which the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, cause the rain of all Dharmas to fall; the inexhaustible ocean of wisdom of the buddha qualities and the radiant colors of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones; the inexhaustible ocean of wisdom through which the omniscient buddhas, the Blessed Ones, have engaged in aspirational prayer from the beginning of time; the inexhaustible ocean of wisdom through which the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, never interrupt buddha activity for all beings unto the final end of time; the inexhaustible ocean of wisdom of the conduct of the minds of all classes of sentient beings without exception of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones; and the inexhaustible ocean of wisdom of omniscience that is not overshadowed by merit of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones. O sons of the Victor, these are the ten inexhaustible oceans of wisdom of the Tathāgatas, the arhats, the perfectly and completely awakened buddhas.

O sons of the Victor, there are ten eyes of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones. What are the ten? There is the eye that is this: the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, have gone beyond and arisen from all things; the eye that is this: the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, have no instance of not knowing any phenomenon; the eye that is this: the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, have great compassion toward all; the eye that is this: the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, are endowed with all ten of the immeasurable powers; the eye that is this: the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, have turned the unsurpassed wheel of Dharma; the eye that is this: the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, have entered upon the taming of all sentient beings; the eye that is this: the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, cause all beings throughout their destinies to fully and perfectly awaken directly; the eye that is this: the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, through their abiding, tame those sentient beings who are to be tamed; the eye that is this: the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, have mastery over the defining characteristics of Dharma that proceed in non-duality; and the eye that is this: the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, having tamed sentient beings and caused them to ripen in such a way that

they have reached the limit of the domain of buddhas, the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, demonstrate their complete passage into nirvāṇa. O children of the Victors, these are the ten buddha-eyes of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones.

O children of the Victors, the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, possess ten gateways through which they demonstrate the boundless Dharma of buddhas. What are those ten? The gateway through which the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, demonstrate the boundless element of sentient beings; the gateway through which the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, demonstrate the boundless variety of conduct among sentient beings; the gateway through which the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, demonstrate the boundless karmic maturation of all sentient beings; the gateway through which the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, demonstrate the boundless bestowal of guidance upon all sentient beings; the gateway through which the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, demonstrate the boundless diligence in attending to the element of sentient beings; the gateway through which the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, present the boundless guidance setting forth the conduct of bodhisattvas; the gateway through which the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, demonstrate the boundless arising, through aspiration, in accordance with the successive distinctions of the bodhisattva stages; the gateway through which the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, demonstrate the boundless gathering and transformation encompassing the entire element of sentient beings without remainder; the gateway through which the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, display the purely adorned buddha-fields in accordance with the intention and faith of bodhisattvas; and the gateway, boundless in the discriminating wisdom of all buddhas, through which all worlds throughout all world-systems, in the past, the future, and the present, in each and every one of those realms, where buddhas appear one after another through successive aeons, are gathered by the power of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, and made fully manifest. O children of the Victors, these are the ten gateways through which the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, demonstrate the boundless Dharma of buddhas.

O children of the Victors, the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, perform ten buddha-deeds. What are those ten? The buddhas, the Blessed Ones, perform buddha-deeds for sentient beings even through the display of their form body; the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, perform buddha-deeds for sentient beings even through their speech; the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, perform buddha-deeds for sentient beings even through grasping; the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, perform buddha-deeds for sentient beings even through non-grasping; the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, perform buddha-deeds for sentient beings even through earth, water, fire, and wind; the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, perform buddha-deeds for sentient beings even through all objects that arise by virtue of objects, by means of their blessing; the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, perform buddha-deeds for sentient beings even through their major marks; the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, perform buddha-deeds for sentient beings even through the fashioning of buddha-fields; the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, perform buddha-deeds for sentient beings even through the perfect purity of their buddha-fields; and the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, perform buddha-deeds for sentient beings even through utter silence. O children of the Victors, these are the ten buddha-deeds that the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, perform for sentient beings.

O children of the Victors, the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, possess ten essential qualities of Dharma for sentient beings. What are those ten? Uninterrupted aspiration and firm aspiration, speaking of non-duality, and acting in accordance with whatever one has declared — this is the first essential quality of Dharma for sentient beings.

Striving earnestly in the conduct of a bodhisattva, never growing weary, advancing to the furthest end of future time, and completing the adornment of all qualities — this is the second essential quality of Dharma for sentient beings.

Seeing even a single sentient being in an inexpressible, utterly inexpressible world-realm who is worthy of being tamed, one does not remain there but journeys to all world-realms without exception — this is the third essential quality of Dharma for sentient beings.

When the minds of sentient beings are distracted, great compassion is brought to bear equally upon them; and when the minds of sentient beings are utterly clear and serene, great compassion is equally brought to bear upon them — this is the fourth essential quality of Dharma for sentient beings.

A Tathāgata, arhat, perfectly and completely awakened buddha neither has arisen nor has not arisen; yet from the moment one enters upon unsurpassed, perfect, and complete awakening until one actually and fully awakens to that unsurpassed, perfect, and complete awakening, the awakening mind does not perish — this is the fifth essential quality of Dharma for sentient beings.

Gathering all roots of virtue and dedicating them to omniscience, while not acting for the sake of worldly concerns — this is the sixth essential quality of Dharma for sentient beings.

From the very first moment, one trains in the physical actions, verbal actions, and mental actions following in the wake of the buddha-conduct of all buddhas, dedicating everything to omniscience, and — rather than following the conduct of śrāvakas or pratyekabuddhas — one fully and completely awakens to unsurpassed, perfect, and complete awakening — this is the seventh essential quality of Dharma for sentient beings.

The boundless, all-pervading radiance, the illumination of all buddha-qualities, and this pure mind of a bodhisattva: all appearances, equally and without exception, are thereby brought to perfect completion in omniscience — this is the eighth essential quality of Dharma for sentient beings.

Abandoning all worldly pleasure, having no desire for worldly joy, and possessing aspirations unstained by any trace of the world, equally and without exception — endowed thereby with all the wellbeing of the world, one destroys all suffering and all conceptual elaboration is no more — this is the ninth essential quality of Dharma for sentient beings.

Through faith in the domain of buddhas, one arouses the mind of hardship and austerity. Striving to guard the lineage of all buddhas, one comes to understand all the suffering of every class of beings through direct knowing, and reaches the ultimate limit culminating in the powers and the ten grounds — this is the tenth essential quality of Dharma for sentient beings.

O children of the Victors, these are the ten essential qualities of Dharma for sentient beings that the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, possess.

O children of the Victor, there are ten qualities of sovereignty and lordship of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones. What are these ten? The buddhas, the Blessed Ones, by sovereign lordship over all phenomena, cause all phenomena to be illuminated. They possess boundless confidence and freedom from attachment with respect to words and letters without limit. They expound all phenomena without exception.

This is the first quality of sovereignty and lordship of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones.

By sovereign lordship, the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, form aspirations and teach the Dharma in accordance with how sentient beings dwell and according to how they are to be tamed and fully ripened. This is the second quality of sovereign lordship of the buddhas.

By sovereign lordship, the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, can shake all world-systems without remainder in six ways, yet without causing harm to even a single sentient being — adorned with immeasurable and incalculable adornments, reaching to the limits of the expanse of space, lifting all world-systems without remainder one from another, setting them down, awakening them, and hurling them, placing world-systems from one to another and from one direction to another — and yet throughout the entire realm of sentient beings there is no delusion and no doubt. This is the third quality of sovereign lordship of the buddhas.

In a single moment of the sovereign mind of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, all world-systems without remainder are blessed with the blessing of wisdom. They bless world-systems one from another with varied adornments, display in all world-systems magnificent splendor and ornaments, and throughout innumerable eons, in all world-systems without remainder, display and manifest an unafflicted body in magnificent splendor, showing the colors of all adornments inexhaustibly, and bless all the pure buddha-fields so that they flow together into a single buddha-field. This is the fourth quality of sovereign lordship of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones.

By sovereign lordship, the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, behold the sentient beings to be tamed by a buddha, and for the sake of a single sentient being sit in cross-legged posture for inexpressible and again inexpressible eons without stirring, attending to that individual with care, yet the body and mind of the Tathāgata know no weariness, and at the time of taming they do not pass beyond that being. Just as they do so for each and every individual, so likewise, proceeding gradually, they abide having blessed all world-systems without remainder, to the end of time, sovereign over life — yet the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, know no fatigue anywhere and no weariness. In just this way, for the sake of each individual among all sentient beings without remainder who is to be tamed by a buddha, they guide them gradually. This is the fifth quality of sovereign lordship of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones.

By sovereign lordship, in a single moment of mind the Tathāgata courses and goes throughout all world-systems without remainder. Traversing without measure the network of lineages of all world-systems in every direction, the ocean of transformations of the world-systems of the ten directions — every last particle of them — blessing all of them from one dharmadhātu to another, they display the dharmadhātu, turn the wheel of Dharma that cannot be seized away. This is the sixth quality of sovereign lordship of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones.

By sovereign lordship, in a single moment of mind, for the sake of taming the realm of sentient beings, there is no one in that single moment of mind who has become a fully and perfectly awakened buddha with unsurpassed, genuinely complete awakening in just this way, nor has there been any such fully and perfectly awakened one, nor is there anyone who will become fully and perfectly awakened, nor any who abides on the ground of learners, nor any who has been opened to fully perfect awakening, nor any who comprehends and knows all the qualities of a buddha without obscuration.

For the sake of taming sentient beings, the unbroken stream of the domain of the great buddha-emanations never ceases, and immeasurable primordial wisdom is attained. This is the seventh quality belonging to the sovereignty of buddhahood of the blessed buddha Bhagavāns.

Through its sovereignty, whatever activity of a buddha the master of the blessed Bhagavān performs by means of the sense base of the eye, that same activity is fully accomplished through the sense base of the ear. Whatever activity of a buddha is performed by means of the sense base of the ear, that same activity is fully accomplished through the sense base of the nose. Whatever activity of a buddha is performed by means of the sense base of the nose, that same activity is fully accomplished through the sense base of the tongue. Whatever activity of a buddha is performed by means of the sense base of the tongue, that same activity is fully accomplished through the sense base of the body. Whatever activity of a buddha is performed by means of the sense base of the body, that same activity is fully accomplished through the sense base of the mind. And whatever activity of a buddha is performed by means of the sense base of the mind — through that, in all world-realms without exception, that activity is undertaken in manifold ways, and a discernment that is both worldly and transcendent is attained. In that same way, each and every undertaking is accomplished and, through emanation, however one single performance of a buddha-activity is undertaken, so too are all performances without exception. This is the eighth quality belonging to the sovereignty of buddhahood of the blessed buddha Bhagavāns.

Through its sovereign faculties, the blessed buddha Bhagavān gathers into the pore of a single hair of his body the entire realm of sentient beings — each individual sentient being, a realm of sentient beings as vast as the buddhas who are inexpressible and inexpressibly inexpressible in their greatness — drawing them all inside without remainder. And yet each individual sentient being may dwell there for countless aeons without any harm coming to them whatsoever; there is no harm of any kind.

Proceeding from one world-realm to another, realm after realm, step by step, through worlds inexpressible in number, it is known that in each of those world-realms buddhas have arisen and are arising, that having passed through those inexpressible world-realms, the essence of sentient beings there has caused inexpressible and inexpressibly inexpressible wheels of Dharma to be turned. And within the pores of those hairs, inexpressible numbers of sentient beings, one after another, become buddhas; one after another, they turn inexpressible wheels of Dharma; one after another, they display the inexpressible dharmadhātu, the basic space of phenomena; one after another, they make manifest inexpressible numbers of the past, inexpressible numbers of the future, and inexpressible numbers of the present. They display the opening of the expanse of space that has no border and no center. This is the ninth quality belonging to the sovereignty of buddhahood of the blessed buddha Bhagavāns.

O sons of the Victor, the ten masteries of skillful means of the Blessed Buddhas: what are the ten? Knowing all phenomena that are not blessed by the Blessed Buddha to be free from conceptual elaboration and wholesome, and then displaying and illuminating the blessing of the Blessed Buddha — this is the first mastery of skillful means of the Blessed Buddhas. The Blessed Buddhas, knowing all phenomena to be without appearance, not cognizable in relation to one another, not bound, not liberated —

having no multiplicity, being beyond contact, and having attained the supreme perfection of sovereignty over all phenomena that have not arisen — the Blessed Buddhas train with great mastery in that state of non-attachment to the ultimate limit of suchness of all things: that is, all phenomena being truly such, being the real, being unerring suchness, being in no way other, being without self, and being phenomena with nothing to apprehend. Following that training and beholding all phenomena — this is the second mastery of skillful means of the Blessed Buddhas.

The Blessed Buddhas, free from the extreme of all characteristics and not abiding in any characteristic, not attached by virtue of their direct knowledge of characteristics — following the path of how those who speak of essential nature speak of essential nature and of the absence of essential nature — display, in a form body perfectly pure and consisting of an inconceivable assembly of form-characteristics, the exhaustion of the characteristics and behavioral characteristics of all the various realms. They perfectly hold the mass of perceptions of darkness, and display a great lamp illuminating all beings who wander in darkness. This is the third mastery of skillful means of the Blessed Buddhas.

Not attached to the beginning limit, the end limit, or what presently arises; not abiding in the past, the future, the present, or the dharmadhātu; causing the arising of buddhas of the past, the future, and the present — whose defining mark of suchness has not arisen — to appear, and teaching it as inexpressible; and thus, on account of lineages beyond measure, beholding for the sake of the buddhas, and surveying the entire domain of the buddhas — this is the fourth mastery of skillful means of the Blessed Buddhas.

The Blessed Buddhas do not accumulate actions of body, speech, or mind; they abide at the limit of what is without appropriation and without boundary; they have gone to the far shore of the abode of phenomena where nothing is cast aside. From the treasury of undetermined aspects, and through activity belonging to enumeration, they accomplish boundless accumulation; following the various worldly and supramundane gradations that belong to enumeration; manifesting diverse magical powers, limitless and boundless, through the vision of unobscured wisdom; and taming and making aware all sentient beings throughout the entirety of the dharmadhātu — this is the fifth mastery of skillful means of the Blessed Buddhas.

All phenomena proceeding as having no appearance and no variety — proceeding as having no non-existence, proceeding as not having arisen, proceeding as without defining mark, proceeding as without variety, proceeding as without adornment, proceeding as without appropriation, proceeding as without essential nature — and yet fully knowing all phenomena, and supremely knowing how to draw them forth in sequence from the expanse of phenomena; making them non-contradictory with worldly phenomena; beholding without truly existent essential nature; knowing all things and seeing all things; seeing all things without remainder through wisdom; causing all phenomena to appear through mastery, and not shifting from the essential nature of suchness — this is the sixth mastery of skillful means of the Blessed Buddhas.

Through skillful means of many kinds one performs the deeds of a buddha throughout every world without exception. Through their diversity one performs the deeds of a buddha in the world. Through every lineage of sentient beings without exception one performs the deeds of a buddha in the world. Through dying and being reborn, and through every lineage without exception, one performs the deeds of a buddha in the world. Through great emanation, through lineage, through the lineage of buddhas, and through the wheel of the gateway of mindfulness of the Dharma one performs the deeds of a buddha. This is the second of the buddha deeds of the blessed buddhas.

Through purification arising from virtuous conduct, and through all births; through wisdom and pure conduct; through following the demonstration of birth; through taming sentient beings and bringing them to full maturity; through bringing all roots of virtue to complete fulfillment; through following the guidance that shows sentient beings what they need — while all buddhas, free from attachment, dwell in dwelling and in non-dwelling; through individually discerning, free from every sound and voice; through discerning all existence, and what arises in existence among human beings; through letting all things enter into primordial wisdom; through completely fulfilling all ethical discipline to the culmination of omniscience; through surveying beings with the eye of great compassion, as if with a hook; through individually discerning the objects of mind with great loving-kindness; through making the joy of the world known by means of great joy; through ruling and engaging the mind with great equanimity;

This is the third deed of the buddha among the Blessed One buddhas.

With the great and magnificent victory of one who has fully gone forth, adorned with ornaments of immeasurable rays of light, abandoning all that is pleasing, showing the conduct of renunciation, drawing in sentient beings who dwell in the world; making the world become deeply attached; making the world understand that the number of worlds is incalculable; turning back attachment to the many kinds of worlds; cutting off craving for the world's pleasures; leading the world, with afflictions relinquished, into purification; making all aims and purposes be kept in mind; guiding beings for the sake of going forth; showing the relinquishment of all the marks of the householder; applying and establishing beings in the affliction-free Dharma; completely fulfilling all former aspirational prayers; illuminating the maṇḍala of immeasurable qualities; individually finding the maṇḍala of great primordial wisdom, while beholding the cataract of ignorance among sentient beings; and, having oneself become the supreme unsurpassable field of merit, drawing all beings near; gathering all roots of virtue and praising before all living beings every root of virtue born from the buddhas; being a Teacher who sees the aim of primordial wisdom; proclaiming to sentient beings the gateway of complete purity; introducing sentient beings to the gateway of blamelessness; fully showing to all the world the gateway of renunciation; going forth and raising aloft the victory banner of omniscience. This is the fourth deed of the buddha among the Blessed One buddhas.

Proceeding to the foot of the tree of awakening, endowed with manifold deeds and primordial wisdoms, and reaching the culmination of the primordial wisdom of omniscience, becoming fully and manifestly awakened under awakening, and thus comprehending all phenomena at heart —

By displaying a form-body that one can never tire of beholding and that has nothing discordant in it, the Blessed One buddhas perform deeds of a buddha for sentient beings. By displaying the major marks and minor features, the Blessed One buddhas perform deeds of a buddha. By gazing in every direction, by looking about with wide eyes, by extending and drawing in the limbs, the Blessed One buddhas perform deeds of a buddha for sentient beings. By teaching the Dharma and by remaining utterly silent, the Blessed One buddhas perform deeds of a buddha for sentient beings. By the miraculous display of supernormal powers, by the miraculous display of teaching, and by the miraculous display of further instruction, the Blessed One buddhas perform deeds of a buddha for sentient beings.

This is the tenth buddha-deed of the blessed buddhas.

O sons of the Victor, this is the vast, immeasurable, and inconceivable scope of the buddha-deeds of the blessed buddhas. Apart from the blessing of the buddhas, no god together with all the worlds of every realm could measure it, and even the śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas of the past, the future, and the present are not capable of penetrating it.

The noble text setting forth the inconceivable qualities of the buddhas — the third section.

O sons of the Victor, the blessed buddhas proceed in ten non-dual ways through the Dharma of the non-dual essential nature of the chief among dharmas. What are the ten? This is the first: all the blessed buddhas prophecy and proceed in non-duality —

The blessed buddhas proceed in non-duality through the minds and intentions of the countless, innumerable, and inexpressible varieties of sentient beings who recollect the buddhas. Non-dual is the body of the perfectly awakened one who has awakened to the exhaustive totality of the meaning of the Dharma. Non-dual is the wisdom of the Tathāgata with regard to past, future, and present as they arise in their essential nature. Non-dual is the wisdom of the very last moment of arising, and non-dual are the first and final extremes and the present moment. Non-dual is the wisdom that reveals the following of all the buddha-fields of the buddhas of the three times and the following of the field of a single buddha. Non-dual is the speech of a single buddha and the speech of all the buddhas. Non-dual are the discipline, the reproach, and the discernment of all the Tathāgatas. Non-dual are the nature of reality of all worlds and the nature of reality of the buddhas. Non-dual are all the roots of virtue of all the buddhas of the past, the future, and the present, and the roots of virtue of any single one among them. O sons of the Victor, this is how the blessed buddhas proceed in non-duality through the Dharma of the chief among dharmas, which is the non-dual Dharma.

O sons of the Victor, the blessed buddhas, the Tathāgatas, abide with diligent attention to all phenomena in ten ways. What are the ten? The blessed buddhas abide in the dharmadhātu, the basic space of phenomena, and abide in awakening following after wisdom. The blessed buddhas abide in great compassion. The blessed buddhas abide in the arising of aspirations, however they have been made. The blessed buddhas abide in disciplining sentient beings without ever abandoning them. The blessed buddhas abide in the nature of reality that is thoroughly established in having no essential nature. The blessed buddhas abide in what is not purposeless, and they abide in not regarding the Tathāgatas as beings of equal fortune. The blessed buddhas constantly abide in equanimity of mind. The blessed buddhas abide in the faculty by which all phenomena remain undiminished. The blessed buddhas abide in conduct free of attachment. The blessed buddhas enter into the equality of all phenomena and abide in the characteristic of authentic ultimate fearlessness. O sons of the Victor, these are the ten ways in which the blessed buddhas, the Tathāgatas, abide with diligent attention to all phenomena.

O sons of the Victor, the blessed buddhas have ten modes of direct, fully knowing awareness of all phenomena without exception. What are the ten? They are as follows: the direct knowing of all the enumerations of the past within the entirety of the dharmadhātu; the direct knowing of all the enumerations of the future; the direct knowing of all the enumerations of the present; the direct knowing of all the paths of all speech; the direct knowing of all the modes of engagement of all worlds; the direct knowing of all the wisdom of the essential nature of all sentient beings; the direct knowing of the defining characteristics of the small, middling, and great roots of virtue of all bodhisattvas, together with their various states, and of the wisdom at every moment of an uninterrupted boundary that perfects all the roots of virtue and the immeasurable aspirations; the direct knowing of the full comprehension of the assembly of conditions and accumulations of all phenomena; the direct knowing of seeing the wisdom that thoroughly distinguishes the lineages of all world-systems; and the direct knowing of the dharmadhātu, its distinctions laid out in full, like a master illusionist elaborating forms —

O sons of the Victor, these are the ten direct knowings through which the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, directly know, without remainder, all phenomena. O sons of the Victor, there are ten Dharma qualities abiding in the splendor of great power and strength that belong to the buddhas, the Blessed Ones — the great power, the power, the immeasurable power, the magnificent nature of power, the power that is difficult to withstand, the power that is wholly undiminished, the essential power, the unsplittable power, the power inconceivable to all worlds, and the power that cannot be seized by any sentient being — the power of the buddhas' great might. What are the ten?

The first is this: the body of a buddha cannot be split open by any opposing adversary; the faculty of life cannot be cut short by all the world-systems together, nor is it frightened by poison; it cannot be burned by the kalpa-fire of conflagration that consumes all world-systems; it cannot be drowned by masses of water that flood and submerge all world-systems; it is unassailable by the strength of the eyes of all māras and all the hosts of māras, all gods, all nāgas, all yakṣas, gandharvas, asuras, garuḍas, kiṃnaras, great mahoragas, humans and non-humans, flesh-eaters, all blazing spirits, and every sentient being without exception. Even a rain of vajras equal to Mount Meru were to fall, and further a rain of vajras equal to the great encircling iron mountains, and still further a rain of vajras the full measure of the great trichiliocosm — not a single body-hair of that Blessed One, the Tathāgata, the arhat, the perfectly and completely awakened Buddha, would be stirred; he would feel not even a moment of discomfort; there would be no abandoning of the comportments of walking, standing, sitting, and lying down in whatever realm that Tathāgata abided; and the Tathāgata would not descend to that realm in any measure less than he wished to be present everywhere. The praises of the Buddha would not be diminished, nor would the beings blessed by the Buddha's consecration be harmed — how much less, then, could the body of the Tathāgata be disturbed. This is the first Dharma quality abiding in the splendor of the great power and strength of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones.

Furthermore, if the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, so wished, all world-systems without remainder — together with the supreme expanse of all phenomena, all world-systems, Mount Meru and its surrounding ranges, the great encircling iron mountains, the ocean mountains and dense forests, the various dwelling places of sentient beings, and every one of those world-systems without exception — could be gathered into a single pore of a Tathāgata's body-hair and held there for the duration of a kalpa at the outermost limit of the final end of time. Apart from the Tathāgata's own blessing, no other sentient being could know this. Those innumerable world-systems, all of them gathered into a single pore of the body-hair of the Tathāgata himself, would be held there for kalpas reaching to the ultimate outermost limit of time; and though the Tathāgata were to rise, walk, sit, lie down, and travel through kalpas, not a single instant of suffering would arise in the Tathāgata's mind. There would be no weariness, no diminishment of power and might, and no conceptual thought concerning his comportments.

O sons of the Victor, consider this analogy: space encompasses without remainder all the dharmadhātu and all world-systems, yet it is not oppressed, not subdued, not increased, not diminished, not arisen, not born, and has no form.

In the same way, the blessed buddhas may gather within a single hair-pore all world-systems without remainder, yet on that account not a single mind-moment of painful feeling arises in them. This is a quality attained by the blessed buddhas through abiding in the splendor of the power of the great might of buddhahood.

Furthermore, in the course of a single step, a blessed buddha traverses buddha-fields equal in number to the infinitely infinite subtlest dust-particles of the inexpressible, yet-again-inexpressible buddha-fields. He travels through kalpas equal in number to those same infinitely infinite subtlest dust-particles of the inexpressible, yet-again-inexpressible buddha-fields. He goes forth in that way, with many mind-moments arising and entering, traveling through kalpas equal in number to the subtlest dust-particles of all world-systems. He travels through kalpas corresponding to the arising of awakening mind in all world-systems without remainder, pervading all world-systems equal in number to the subtlest dust-particles of all those world-systems — limitless and measureless — and passing through buddha-fields equal in number to the subtlest dust-particles of distinct buddha-fields corresponding to the inexpressible, yet-again-inexpressible turnings of the great surrounding mountain ranges, all converging into a single hair-pore. Just as with that one hair-pore, so it is with every hair-pore of his body: all sentient beings, however many there are, come to rest within those hair-pores. With all those sentient beings pressing down upon him, the Tathāgata holds them at the tip of a single finger through kalpas reaching to the ultimate outermost limit of time. Just as he first holds them with one finger, so he holds them with the tips of all his fingers — yet on that account not the slightest forgetting of the Tathāgata's body arises, nor does weariness arise in his mind, nor does even the subtlest trace of a thought arise that he is experiencing the touch of suffering. In that same way, he goes and enters each and every world-system; he goes and enters into space; he goes and enters in all the directions of the vast expanse; he goes and enters to the very ends of all the reaches of the space element; he enters to the very ends of the dharmadhātu; he enters into every element reaching to the measure of the tips of hairs; and for each hair-tip's measure, he enters into all the reaches of the space element, filling numberless space-elements equal to the measure of each hair-tip. Throughout all of this, from the first extreme to the last extreme of the Tathāgata's conduct, through kalpas reaching their exhaustion and passing, he sits in a single cross-legged posture in each and every direction, fully at ease and enjoying complete presence. This is the fourth quality of the blessed buddhas, attained through abiding in the splendor of the power of the great might of buddhahood.

Furthermore, the blessed buddha manifests bodies as numerous as the infinitely infinite subtlest dust-particles of the inexpressible, yet-again-inexpressible buddha-fields, and likewise countless heads. In each head arise tongues as numerous as the infinitely infinite subtlest dust-particles of the inexpressible, yet-again-inexpressible buddha-fields, and from those tongues pour forth notes of sound, syllables of voice, and utterances — making the entire dharmadhātu, without remainder, comprehensible to the realm of sentient beings. Each note, each syllable of voice, each utterance fully expounds holy sūtras as numerous as the infinitely infinite subtlest dust-particles of the inexpressible, yet-again-inexpressible buddha-fields; each sūtra is followed by the teaching of Dharma in topics as numerous as the infinitely infinite subtlest dust-particles of the inexpressible, yet-again-inexpressible buddha-fields; and for each teaching of Dharma there flows forth an exposition of Dharma-words and analyses as numerous as the infinitely infinite subtlest dust-particles of the inexpressible, yet-again-inexpressible buddha-fields.

Just as Dharma is taught for any given purpose, so all phenomena are taught. Each is taught for aeons as numerous as the infinitely infinite subtlest dust-particles of the inexpressible, yet-again-inexpressible buddha-fields; and when those aeons as numerous as the infinitely infinite subtlest dust-particles of the inexpressible, yet-again-inexpressible buddha-fields are utterly exhausted, the teaching continues again for aeons as numerous as the infinitely infinite subtlest dust-particles of the inexpressible, yet-again-inexpressible buddha-fields. This teaching expounds for aeons as numerous as the subtlest dust-particles of all world-systems; and even when aeons as numerous as the subtlest dust-particles of all world-systems are exhausted, even when aeons equal to the movements of the minds of all sentient beings have passed away, the Tathāgata's emanation is not thereby diminished. All phenomena are engaged like a firebrand wheel, and all phenomena are taught through wisdom. The wheel of Dharma that communicates and cuts through all doubt turns; the wheel of Dharma that illuminates all phenomena turns; the wheel of the treasure-store that is free from attachment turns; the wheel of Dharma adorned with the training that satisfies limitless sentient beings turns; the wheel adorned with the summation of all bodhisattva conduct turns; the wheel of the continuous stream of the great maṇḍala of the sun of primordial wisdom turns; the wheel of the continuous stream of the great lamp of primordial wisdom for all beings without exception turns; and the wheel of Dharma adorned with the great fearlessness and the analytical knowledges (pratisaṃvid) turns. Indeed, even the great cloud of the turning wheel of Dharma produced by the blessing of the body of a single buddha is beyond all comparison and simile.

Thus, throughout the entirety of the dharmadhātu (basic space of phenomena), through to the farthest reaches of the expanse of space — even to the tips of a hair's breadth, in every direction, without remainder — the emanations of buddhas as numerous as the moments of mind equal to the infinitely infinite subtlest dust-particles of the inexpressible, yet-again-inexpressible buddha-fields arise with each and every moment of mind; and until the final kalpa, each body of the nirmāṇakāya (emanation body) of all the Tathāgatas is multiplied to that same measure, each of those bodies becoming a head of that same number upon number, and upon each head that same number upon number of tongues, upon each tongue that same number upon number of voice-branches, speech, body, and vocal activity — making all of the dharmadhātu without remainder resound throughout the realm of sentient beings. Through each voice-branch, each sound, each utterance, that same number upon number of sūtras are fully expounded; for each sūtra that same number upon number of Dharma-teachings follow; for each Dharma-teaching that same number upon number of Dharma-words and analyses flow forth; and each Dharma-word and analysis makes all of the dharmadhātu without remainder clearly resound and causes understanding in the entire realm of sentient beings without exception. Yet the speech of the Tathāgata neither grasps, nor degrades, nor is exhausted, nor is impeded. Abiding thus until the final boundary of the ultimate kalpa, it turns the wheel of Dharma. This is the fifth quality of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, which abides in the glory of the power of great buddha-might.

Furthermore, the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, are endowed with the continuous stream of the calf of the glory of their enlightened mind,

Those held in the great vajra-essence proceed to the foot of the tree of awakening, and there, before all sentient beings in their entirety, they cause the hosts of sinful māras, in their many and varied forms — repulsive and supremely terrifying — to be scattered and dispelled, transforming them into forms that inspire the arising of the awakening mind in every being in the world.

Having gone to the foot of that supreme tree of awakening, seizing the repulsive forms of māra at the sky's edge in the expanse of all phenomena, creating great terror through tremendous fright, destroying all beings, and shattering the entire world — in that very moment, the Tathāgata arrives at the foot of the tree of awakening, gazes upon all of this, and having gazed, feels no fear, no shame, not a single hair stirs. He holds no attachment to the variegated marks of varied forms; not the subtlest movement of mind is present. He abides unmoved, unstirred, unchanging, free of conceptual thought, profoundly and utterly at peace. There is not the slightest trace of trembling or hair standing on end, not the slightest trace of desire or anger. He performs the deeds of a buddha, releasing them through the power of great loving-kindness free of attachment. He conceals his faculties and abides in great compassion. With a mind whose essential quality is shamelessness inseparable from its ground, a mind that is neither broken nor overwhelmed, he subdues and utterly vanquishes māra, all the gods of the classes of māra, and all the many and varied hosts of māra — subduing them from the very beginning. Once subdued, these very beings go to the Tathāgata for refuge. The Blessed One then teaches them through the three miraculous deeds and through the discipline of the Tathāgata, and they become irreversible with respect to unsurpassable, genuine, and complete awakening.

Such, then, is the sixth quality of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, which abides in the glory of the power of great buddha-might.

Furthermore, the speech of the buddhas, unimpeded, pervades all world-systems without remainder, and in each and every one, being fully recognized by all beings in the world and for the sake of taming sentient beings, the sound of the Tathāgata is never obstructed by any mountain. It is not impeded by Mount Meru, nor by mountains of jewels, nor by the surrounding ring-mountains great and small, nor by the abodes of gods, the abodes of nāgas, the abodes of yakṣas, the abodes of gandharvas, the abodes of asuras, the abodes of garuḍas, the abodes of kiṃnaras, or the abodes of beings human and non-human. Even if it passes through all world-systems, it clings to none. The great sound of all the world-systems of saṃsāra, and the manifold and fully perfected sounds of every world, are covered over by the sound of the Tathāgata's speech. The ears of those sentient beings who are to be tamed fully perceive it, and what is thus perceived by ear does not lose the clarity of its words and their distinctions. The sound of the Tathāgata is like this: clear, and clearer still, and lucidly resonant. This is the seventh quality of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones — the sound of Dharma which abides in the glory of the power of great buddha-might.

Furthermore, the mind of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, purified through inconceivably and inexpressibly many tens of millions of hundreds of thousands of aeons, is free of attachment. It is the adornment of all buddhas of the past, the future, and the present, unstained by any defilement. It abides in all phenomena that are without self. It is neither interior nor exterior. It opens and analyzes in full all undertakings with respect to what is unborn. It is at peace. It has no fixed abode. It is free of all kinds of elaboration.

It is the cessation of perception that is conceptual thought regarding all essences. Its intrinsic nature is luminosity. It is utterly and completely pure. It follows in the wake of all phenomena that are free from affliction, unattached to every undertaking and mental engagement. Pure and free from attachment in the perfection of the real, it has gone to the ultimate limit of revealing the dharmadhātu, the basic space of phenomena. It follows the inexhaustible expanse of the dharmadhātu. With all perceptions, with the limited and the unlimited, with the conditioned and the unconditioned, and with all assertions having ceased, wisdom goes forth and enters into the frontier of the inexhaustible, unattached expanse of the inexpressible ultimate. It is adorned with the ten powers and all phenomena are thoroughly purified. Entering into skillful means, it teaches the many and various phenomena, the multitude of phenomena, and all phenomena without contradiction, undivided across the three times. It has gone beyond the sovereign power of the non-existence of all dharmadhātus, all phenomena, and all phenomena. Mindful of the character of all phenomena in the distinctive treasury of the source of the sovereignty of all phenomena, and possessed of the sovereignty that moves through all worlds steeped in bewilderment, it does not rest even on a single particle at the uttermost edge of the immeasurable, all-pervasive directions. Abiding firmly and without fluctuation, the view of gnosis reaches to the very limit of all phenomena without exception. All defilements are utterly exhausted and there is no defilement. The mind is fully liberated. Wisdom is fully liberated. Dwelling in supreme non-attachment at the inexhaustible ultimate of perfect, thorough liberation, it constantly abides in perfect equanimity, abiding without attachment to the three times, and within a single moment of mind it fully knows, without remainder, all the mental conduct of the three times.

This is the eighth power: abiding in the glory of the power of great buddha-might of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones.

Furthermore, the body of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, is a unity in which the sound of the Dharma and the body are merged into one. It is the completely accomplished body of all buddhas, a body of immeasurable domain, without boundary or center. It is the body that wholly encompasses all worlds. It is the body unstained by the three realms. Recognizing the body of conditioned appearances, it presents to those who call to mind with elevated intention the assembly of blessings. It is a body that is neither false nor deceptive, the perfectly pure body of equality. It is the assembly of the dharmakāya without coming and without going. It is a body that is unconditioned and indestructible. It is the body of the essential nature of the Dharma that has a single defining characteristic and is beyond defining characteristics. It is a body that abides in no domain and abides in no direction. It is the body of all assemblies without remainder. It is the body of the supreme leader of buddhas manifesting in emanations. It is a body of boundless color. All bodies follow in its wake. It is recognized in the variety of bodies. It follows in the mode of the dharmakāya. It is the body that is the treasury of the maṇḍala of the essence of gnosis, appearing in boundless radiance. It has no diverse activities. It is the body that reveals the expanse of all phenomena. It is the assembly of the body that pervades all sentient beings throughout the whole realm of sentient beings in response to their prayers. It is the perfectly pure body that is unmoved and non-conceptual. It is the body differentiated by existence and non-existence, by what is proper and what is improper. Following in accord with the diverse faiths of all sentient beings, it is the body that tames them. It is the body that raises aloft all the rare and precious qualities, the sources of all jewels, looking upon those to be tamed with subsiding and non-subsiding. It is the body that gathers all phenomena into buddhadharma and suchness. It is the body at the ultimate that is by its very nature supremely at peace and unobscured. It is the body born from all phenomena of non-obstruction. It follows all, and the dharmadhātu is pure.

It is the body that is fully differentiated throughout all worlds, never turning back from what it has undertaken, following in the wake of all the gnosis of liberation of every buddha. It is the body that goes after all that follows. This is the ninth, the dharmakāya of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, gathered together in the glory and power of their great might.

Furthermore, all the Tathāgatas are born from all the practices of bodhisattvas, utterly imbued with the gnosis of equality and the equality of the Blessed Ones, the buddhas. They arise distinctly out of those practices. Their distinctive aspirational prayers, their pure conduct as bodhisattvas, the gnosis that perfectly completes all bodhisattva practices — all the buddhas who are without the diversified activity of conceptualization, born from all bodhisattva practices, knowing the rites of every conduct of the ocean of bodhisattva practices, possessed of great aspiration, and whose conduct is not slight. Not content to see all practices perfectly completed, arising from all practices difficult to bear through the power of conduct, with all practices fully pure. Practicing the excellent practice of bodhisattvas inexhaustibly. Abiding in all immeasurable meditative absorptions of all the buddhas. Teaching all paths within a domain of activity that is boundless. Entering into all paths and all domains that are not the path. Knowing the rites of the power of supreme gnosis. Turning the wheel of Dharma without hesitation. The gnosis of equality and the gnosis that is equal to equality, supremely and perfectly pure in showing the mode of the venerable Dharma whether virtuous conduct is adorned or not adorned — the three kinds of conduct of body, speech, and mind being perfectly pure. Entering into the family of all the Tathāgatas, into the gnosis of the buddhas, and into all their deeds through conduct reached by the gateway not found apart from the buddhas. Abiding in omniscience. Dwelling in the teaching of what is immeasurable. The gnosis of all the supernormal knowledges that has neither root nor limit, being inconceivable. The gnosis that follows along with the minds of the sentient beings of the world. The gnosis that sees all phenomena of absolutely every being throughout every destiny, subtle without remainder, a gnosis without periphery or center. Comprehending through subtle gnosis the modes of entering all three times. Every world-realm without remainder being completely purified, and the gnosis that includes all world-realms together with what transcends the world — meaning, gnosis, and the deeds of the buddhas being inexpressible — the body of all the buddhas in an irreversible gnosis. Perfectly ascertaining through gnosis that enters into enumeration all phenomena of Dharma without syllables. The gnosis that is inexpressible, diversified and differentiated, that comprehends all syllables as inexpressible. The sequence of the gnosis of Samantabhadra, wherein all phenomena of virtuous conduct in gnosis are completely pure. The gnosis by which all phenomena are thoroughly understood through the gnosis that is endowed with a single moment of mind, in whatever way is fitting. The gnosis that reflects thoroughly on the manner in which one abandons the phenomena of sense sources in the body of every sentient being — not a gnosis whose domain is bounded by the domain of any phenomenon. Following in the wake of absolutely all world-realms, all destinies without remainder, all sentient beings without remainder, all the dharmadhātu without remainder, and the domain seen by the gnosis of non-attachment to a single moment of mind across the three times — entering into the domain in which all Tathāgatas without remainder give rise to buddhahood, and following in the wake of every domain of taming all beings without remainder.

Awakening in complete purity at the boundary of buddha-mind, with direct knowledge and manifest awakening beyond all objects. Comprehending without remainder all the minds and mental consciousnesses of sentient beings. Awakening in the wake of gnosis through each moment of mind divided by the three times, and awakening without interruption. Going without limit to all sentient beings, going without end throughout all world-realms, going without limit throughout all world-realms, going immeasurably throughout all three times, going to all the supreme herds of buddhas, going without remainder to all that is thoroughly realized, manifestly and perfectly awakening from all of those without remainder — following in the wake of all thorough realizations and never relinquishing the gnosis of the buddhas. Abiding in the magical emanations of all buddhas.

Therein: the Blessed One, the buddhas, abiding without boundary or center. The body of the supreme herd of buddhas abiding without boundary or center. Abiding with a mind free from attachment. Abiding engaged in great compassion without boundary or center. Abiding at all times, always uninterruptedly teaching the Dharma. This is the tenth quality of abiding in the great wisdom — the splendor of the great buddha-power of the Blessed One, the buddhas.

O sons of the Victor, these are the ten qualities of abiding in the splendor of the power of the great buddha-power of the Blessed One, the buddhas — inconceivable and immeasurable to all sentient beings and to all śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas of the past, future, and present.

O sons of the Victor, the ten qualities in which the Blessed One, the buddhas, abide — what are the ten? The Blessed One, the buddhas, abide in Tuṣita for as long as they wish and then transfer when their lifespan is exhausted. The Blessed One, the buddhas, display a lifespan of abiding for ten months. The Blessed One, the buddhas, abide with the intention of manifestly going forth and wishing to go forth. The Blessed One, the buddhas, abide at the foot of the awakening tree, manifestly and perfectly awakening through all buddha-qualities. The Blessed One, the buddhas, abide having manifestly and perfectly awakened to all buddha-qualities in a single moment, and display the emanations of all Tathāgatas throughout all world-realms. The Blessed One, the buddhas, abide turning the wheel of Dharma in accordance with the time for taming beings. The Blessed One, the buddhas, abide at the appropriate time displaying and indicating the roots of virtue to be cultivated. The Blessed One, the buddhas, abide at the appropriate time engaged in the deeds of a buddha. The Blessed One, the buddhas, abide giving instruction to perfectly consummate bodhisattvas. The Blessed One, the buddhas, abide giving instruction in a single moment to all questions asked by all sentient beings in that single moment.

O sons of the Victor, these are the ten qualities in which the Blessed One, the buddhas, abide.

O sons of the Victor, the ten qualities of the swift Tathāgata-vision of the Blessed One, the buddhas — what are the ten? The Blessed One, the buddhas, upon immediately seeing sentient beings heading toward an evil destiny, swiftly turn those beings back.

The Blessed One, the buddhas, upon immediately seeing sentient beings, swiftly bring it about that their merit goes on to perfect completion. The Blessed One, the buddhas, upon immediately seeing sentient beings, swiftly bring about the full ripening of their roots of virtue. The Blessed One, the buddhas, upon immediately seeing sentient beings, swiftly bring it about that they individually attain pure birth in the higher realms. The Blessed One, the buddhas, upon immediately seeing sentient beings, swiftly draw near to crushing doubt and indecision. The Blessed One, the buddhas, upon immediately seeing sentient beings who have generated the awakening mind toward awakening, swiftly come to them so as to render them irreversible, and they inspire faith in the vast mind. The Blessed One, the buddhas, bring it about that beings fit to be trained by a buddha who have not yet generated the awakening mind do generate the awakening mind directed toward unsurpassable, perfectly complete awakening. The Blessed One, the buddhas, upon immediately seeing those who have entered the womb, regard them as their object of focus and firmly grasp the cause of their entry into the womb. The Blessed One, the buddhas, upon immediately seeing sentient beings, swiftly bring it about that the faculties of those sentient beings are purified beyond the world. The Blessed One, the buddhas, upon immediately seeing sentient beings, swiftly come to bring about the exhaustion of all obscurations. The Blessed One, the buddhas, upon immediately seeing sentient beings, swiftly find illumination individually and, being without timidity, begin to arouse confidence in fearlessness.

O sons of the Victor, these are the ten qualities of the swift Tathāgata-vision of the Blessed One, the buddhas.

O sons of the Victor, the ten pure recollections of the self of the Blessed One, the buddhas — what are the ten? The Blessed One, the buddhas, recollect the mental conduct of all bodhisattvas. The Blessed One, the buddhas, recollect the perfect completion of all the perfections of bodhisattvas. The Blessed One, the buddhas, recollect the perfect completion of the great aspirations of bodhisattvas. The Blessed One, the buddhas, recollect the accomplishments of bodhisattvas. The Blessed One, the buddhas, recollect both their own previous holy life as buddhas and that of bodhisattvas as well. The Blessed One, the buddhas, recollect the gateway through which bodhisattvas manifestly and perfectly awaken. The Blessed One, the buddhas, recollect the measureless and boundless form bodies of bodhisattvas. The Blessed One, the buddhas, recollect the measureless and boundless buddha-fields and emanations, and those of bodhisattvas as well. The Blessed One, the buddhas, recollect the ten powers and the fearlessness of the minds of bodhisattvas.

O sons of the Victor, these are the ten pure recollections of the self of the Blessed One, the buddhas.

O sons of the Victor, the ten abidances of the Tathāgatas in omniscience — what are the ten? They are as follows: knowing the complete and entire expanse of all phenomena; knowing the various minds of all sentient beings of the three times; knowing in a single moment of mind the conduct of a single mind and the turning back of distraction; knowing the actions of all sentient beings of all three times without remainder, along with their various associations with action, their aspects, their sequence,

the growth and accumulation of actions, and the sequence of their subsequent karmic maturation — knowing all of this in a single moment of mind; knowing the entire expanse of the dharmadhātu without remainder, together with the various classes of sentient beings and a single class of sentient beings, and all sentient beings without exception — knowing, at the right time, the miraculous display of taming and teaching sentient beings, the miraculous display of subsequent instruction, and the blessing of miraculous display through supernatural power; knowing the entire dharmadhātu without remainder, all the directions of the vast expanse, all world-systems — having gone to them all, being perceived as a mark by the minds of all sentient beings — and knowing the distinct appearances of the arising of buddhas; knowing the intentions, the faith, and the capacity for training of all sentient beings in all world-systems without remainder, and in a single moment, through a sequence of immeasurable stages, their beholding of buddhas, and knowing the blessing of clear manifestation; knowing how to display, to all sentient beings in all world-systems without remainder, the blessing of the buddhas according to what their minds aspire to from the other side; knowing, through non-grasping at the entire dharmadhātu without remainder, how — just as sentient beings are to be tamed — to guide and subsequently instruct them, coming from the buddhas, and knowing how to display the arising of Tathāgatas; knowing the diligence, in a moment of mind, of going to the entire dharmadhātu without remainder and to all sentient beings — going to each of those various places; knowing, in a single moment of mind, the sending forth and the engagement of the recollection of buddhas in all directions to all sentient beings throughout the entire dharmadhātu without remainder; and knowing, in a single moment of mind, how — in accordance with the immeasurable faith of all sentient beings throughout the entire dharmadhātu without remainder — to display the beholding of the bodies of buddhas and the boundless range of colors of the form bodies.

O sons of the Victor, these are the ten abidances of the Tathāgatas, arhats, perfectly and completely awakened buddhas, in omniscience.

O sons of the Victor, the ten immeasurable, inconceivable meditative absorptions (samādhi) of the buddhas — the Tathāgatas, arhats, perfectly and completely awakened buddhas — what are the ten? They are as follows:

The Blessed One, the buddha, ever enters into meditative absorption with respect to all phenomena, and in a single moment of mind teaches the Dharma of the entire dharmadhātu without remainder to sentient beings.

The Blessed One, the buddha, enters into meditative absorption with respect to the entire dharmadhātu without remainder, and in a single moment of mind illuminates throughout the sequence of the various elements of sentient beings, which has reached the culmination of selflessness.

The Blessed One, the buddha, enters into meditative absorption with respect to the entire dharmadhātu without remainder and with respect to the three times, abiding in meditative absorption at the very limit of a single moment of mind.

The Blessed One, the buddha, ever enters into meditative absorption with respect to the entire dharmadhātu without remainder — throughout all the vast reaches of innumerable world-systems fine as dust — and with respect to all the families and pure lands of the buddhas, abiding at the inexhaustible limit.

The Blessed One, the buddha, enters into meditative absorption with respect to the entire dharmadhātu without remainder and with respect to the boundless bodies of the buddhas, and in a single moment displays the clear manifestation of the various bodies of buddhas throughout all worlds.

The Blessed One, the buddha, ever enters into meditative absorption with respect to the entire dharmadhātu without remainder and with respect to the complete luminosity of body, speech, and mind, abiding as the treasury of the various minds of all sentient beings, and in a single moment, in accordance with their faith, acts in mind —

The Blessed One, the buddha, ever enters into meditative absorption with respect to the entire dharmadhātu without remainder and with respect to the essential nature of all phenomena, and has arrived at the ultimate end of freedom from attachment; and the Blessed One, the buddha, ever enters into meditative absorption with respect to all of the entire dharmadhātu without remainder and with respect to the conditions of all worlds, and in a single moment displays the conditions of speech; and the Blessed One, the buddha, ever enters into meditative absorption with respect to all of the entire dharmadhātu without remainder and with respect to all the measureless dust-mote-like worlds both within the world and beyond the world, and in a single moment follows after boundless conditions — whereby the view toward the buddha is unobstructed, and he causes boundless sentient beings and buddhas to be seen; and the Blessed One, the buddha, ever enters into meditative absorption with respect to all of the entire dharmadhātu without remainder, in the mode of non-striving, and in a single moment, having understood and known all the qualities of a buddha, attains the boundless perfections of a buddha's liberation.

O child of the Victors, these are the ten boundless and inconceivable meditative absorptions of the buddhas — the Blessed Ones, the Tathāgatas, the arhats, the perfectly and completely awakened buddhas.

O child of the Victors, the ten liberations of the Blessed One, the buddhas — what are these ten?

The first is the liberation of displaying the arising of inexpressibly inexpressible buddhas within a single exceedingly subtle dust-mote.

The second is the liberation of displaying the turning of an inexpressibly inexpressible wheel of Dharma within a single exceedingly subtle dust-mote.

The third is the liberation of displaying the taming and completely ripening of an inexpressibly inexpressible number of sentient beings within a single exceedingly subtle dust-mote.

The fourth is the liberation of displaying an inexpressibly inexpressible number of pure lands (buddha-fields) within a single exceedingly subtle dust-mote.

The fifth is the liberation of displaying, within a single exceedingly subtle dust-mote, the arising of buddhas of the past, the future, and the present.

The sixth is the liberation of displaying, within a single exceedingly subtle dust-mote, the passing away of buddhas of the past, the future, and the present.

The seventh is the liberation of displaying, within a single exceedingly subtle dust-mote, the miraculous powers and emanations of all Tathāgatas of the past, the future, and the present.

The eighth is the liberation of displaying, within a single exceedingly subtle dust-mote, the teaching of Dharma to sentient beings by buddhas of the past, the future, and the present.

The ninth is the liberation of displaying, within a single exceedingly subtle dust-mote, the major marks of all buddhas of the past, the future, and the present.

O child of the Victors, these are the ten liberations of the Blessed One, the buddhas.

This completes the twenty-ninth chapter, the chapter on the inconceivable qualities of the buddhas, from the hundred-thousand-section sūtra known as the Great Extensive Ornament of the Ear.

Translator's notes

The phrase rendered "ten-fold conduct free from attachment and free from obstruction" translates a somewhat compressed Tibetan construction. The "ten" likely refers to a set of ten kinds of unattached, unobstructed conduct associated with advanced bodhisattva practice, though the precise enumeration is not spelled out here in the text.

The expression "knowing exclusively, one after another, the lineage of all the buddhas" renders a terse phrase about recognizing the buddha-lineage in unbroken succession; the exact doctrinal referent may depend on the broader sūtra context.

The name Utpalahṛdaya means "Heart of the Utpala Lotus" and Padmahṛdaya means "Heart of the Lotus"; both are kept in Sanskrit reconstruction as proper names of bodhisattvas addressed in this sūtra. The term rendered "sense base of the nose" translates a compound referring to the nasal āyatana. The claim that this yields "supreme dominion over all buddhas" is unusual and may reflect a specialized tantric or Avataṃsaka-register meaning in which the unbounded olfactory faculty symbolizes a penetrating, all-encompassing sovereignty; the Tibetan reads straightforwardly and no emendation has been made. The phrase rendered "the ripening — the karmic maturation of perfectly virtuous ripening" reflects a doubled form in the Tibetan ("yang dag pa'i dge ba'i rnam par smin pa'i rnam par smin pa") that appears redundant.
