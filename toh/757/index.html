About this text

Toh 757 in the Kangyur is a Mahāyāna sūtra known in English as "The Noble Great Strength," in Sanskrit as the Ārya-Mahābala-nāma-mahāyānasūtra, and in Tibetan as Phags pa stobs po che zhes bya ba theg pa chen po'i mdo. It belongs to the general class of sūtra literature and centers on the bodhisattva Great Strength, a powerful four-armed figure associated with Vajrapāṇi, presenting teachings delivered by the Buddha on Gṛdhrakūṭa mountain in the presence of this great being. The text combines narrative and ritual elements, including mantras, and sits within the broader Kangyur collection of translated scriptures regarded as buddhavacana, or the word of the Buddha.

Thus did I hear. At one time, the Blessed One was teaching the Dharma on the great Gṛdhrakūṭa mountain. Then the bodhisattva, the great being Vajrapāṇi — praised, lauded, honored, and delighted by all buddhas — was also present there.

Then, with his hand, the Blessed One gave ease to sentient beings, and took his seat upon a lotus throne wreathed in a garland of flames blazing on all sides, in the eastern direction.
