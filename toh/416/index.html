About this text

The Tantra Called Glorious Vajra Mahākāla, the Wrathful Protector, Source of Secret Accomplishment, known in Sanskrit as the Śrī Vajra Mahākāla Krodhanātha Rahasya Siddhi Bhava Tantra and in Tibetan as Dpal rdo rje nag po chen po khros pa'i mgon po gsang ba dngos grub 'byung ba, is a tantric scripture centered on Mahākāla, the fierce protector deity of the Buddhist teachings. It opens with homage to Heruka and presents the Blessed One, absorbed in the meditative state of the great bliss of all the tathāgatas, teaching a "wheel of command" meant to destroy the maṇḍala of Māra. The work frames this wrathful protection as an expression of compassion: for as long as the Buddha remains in the world and does not pass into nirvāṇa, he acts tirelessly and with loving diligence for the benefit of all beings. It is Toh 416 in the Kangyur, where it belongs to the collection of tantras, the class of esoteric scriptures concerned with ritual, deity practice, and the attainment of spiritual accomplishments.

Translation

In the language of India: śrī vajra mahākāla krodhanātha rahasya siddhi bhava tantra nāma In the language of Tibet: The Tantra Called Glorious Vajra Mahākāla, the Wrathful Protector, Source of Secret Accomplishment.

Homage to glorious Heruka!

Then, through the direct knowing
of the wisdom of the all-knowing Blessed One,
I shall explain the wheel of command
that destroys the maṇḍala of Māra.

Having entered the meditative absorption called the unobstructed and unfailing great bliss of all the tathāgatas, he spoke these words:

As long as the all-knowing Sugata
remains in the abode of saṃsāra
and does not pass into nirvāṇa,
for that long, for the incomparable benefit of sentient beings,
unceasingly, for all beings,
unafraid of the noose of existence,
with the diligence of a loving heart,
he always works for the benefit of sentient beings.
To the very limit of the realm of space
he purifies his aspirations,
and with a mind that strives for their good through compassion,
the hero purifies existence.

Like a jewel that takes on various forms,
this, throughout all the tantras,
is hidden in the language of barbarians (mlecchas).
It is the destroyer of the suffering of saṃsāra.
The great being, the vajra master
who has become equal to Vajrasattva,
swiftly grants accomplishment
and instantly brings true conviction.
With whatever intentions
people act in accord with their minds,
he emanates forms suited to each,
clothes himself in various guises, and dwells in bliss.
Therefore, with many efforts,
one should accord with them in various ways.

Oneself is the buddha Heruka,
with the hosts of heroes and yoginīs.
Through thorough union with one's own deity,
one will accomplish the supreme wisdom.
By this, the ocean of victors,
the deities of the maṇḍala and the hosts of field protectors (kṣetrapālas),
the charnel grounds established from the beginning,
the lord of the ḍākas, the Great Protector,
the hosts of body, speech, mind and activity,
the deities of the families of wisdom and qualities,
Mahākāla together with his retinue,
the Great Goddess together with her servants:
by the vajra holder, at all times,
through the illusion-like meditative absorption,
the Great Protector, outer, inner and secret,
through the play of great bliss,
the inconceivable secret wisdom,
will be accomplished in this very life.
So spoke the Blessed One who holds the vajra.

All things have the characteristic of space,
yet space has no characteristic.
The three realms without exception,
all beings, are like illusions.
Beings, the stable and the moving,
cannot be apprehended as objects;
seeing, hearing and the rest
all appear like illusions.
That by which people are bound,
when fully understood, is itself liberation.
Through this union of the mudrā,
the three worlds are transformed into great bliss.
Without afflictions there is no wisdom;
this is far from the paths of awakening of the śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas.
The aggregates, the elements, the sense sources,
the four great elements and the rest,
all of them, through the blessing
of the great wisdom pure from the beginning,
appear clearly as many things.
Without wisdom they do not arise;
they are impelled by the wisdom of the buddhas.
Therefore, in this unsurpassed tantra
one should strive to generate faith.

The secret vow is unsurpassed.
First, by obtaining the sacred commitments of the guru,
cause and result arise.
They are the nature of body, speech and mind.
For the steadfast they are enjoyment;
the one who is truly steadfast passes beyond sorrow.
Abiding in the sacred commitments of yoga,
the secret practice of signs
and empowerment grant accomplishment.
The guru, together with the tathāgatas,
the victors and the bodhisattvas,
is the unsurpassed lord of the maṇḍala.
Whatever mantra practitioner enters this secret
should rely on a genuine guru.
Here, with the mind of awakening,
one should know the distinctions of approach, close approach,
accomplishment and great accomplishment,
their stages, signs and times,
and how they are applied to the rites.
When, through the practice of the rites,
the Protector and his retinue are pleased,
the rites and the various accomplishments
will swiftly be achieved, without doubt.

This was the first chapter, on secret manifest awakening, from the Tantra of the Source of Accomplishment of the Glorious Great Protector.

Then all the blessed tathāgatas and all the bodhisattvas heard the speech of the Blessed One, Vajradhara, as though in a dream, and these words of vajra speech were spoken:

The vajra holder who, like an illusion or an echo,
in delightful and solitary places
practices the accomplishment that gathers all siddhis:
of all the protectors without exception
who have appeared in this Fortunate Eon,
the one who has become the heart of them all
is the secret field protector, the Great Protector.
This protector of all the buddhas
guards the teaching.

So it was spoken. Then the bodhisattva mahāsattvas Samantabhadra and the others, together with Vajradhara and the others, addressed the Blessed One with these words:

Where is the great Vajra Vehicle,
the true path that gathers the secrets,
the essence that guards the teaching of the Buddha,
which takes the result as the path?
In the age of the five degenerations,
the Guide guards the teaching of wisdom.
What is this very secret practice?
Please teach it here in the Vajra Vehicle.

When they had made this request, the Blessed One, Vajradhara, spoke:

The meditative absorption in which
the union of wisdom and compassion is cultivated
is the teaching of the unsurpassed Jewel;
it turns back evil adversaries.
For the teaching of the supreme Sage
there are three: upholding, maintaining and protecting.
In the degenerate time of the five hundred years,
it casts off the grasping at self.
Endowed with the three bodies and the five wisdoms,
one should accomplish the single secret.

So he spoke.

The Blessed One, glorious Heruka Vajra, abiding fully in the expanse of secret great bliss, in the center of the great maṇḍala of the host of yoginīs of self-awareness, entered the meditative absorption of the indestructible vajra, the essence of the supremely victorious vajra, unsurpassed and self-arisen, the secret abode in which all the buddhas are spontaneously present. He then taught the inexhaustible Dharma called "True Arising," known as that which accomplishes the wisdom siddhi of the vajra essence of body, speech and mind and is fully victorious in the battle against Māra.

The part that rests upon the vajra ground
holds treasure vases with open mouths.
The luminous king of time,
with clouds over but half a quarter of the sky,
pervading sound and what lies beyond sound,
is a realm that abides as no multiplicity.
From the source of the secret samaya
comes the Victor, lord of blazing jewels,
self-arisen, free of direction and time,
in the maṇḍala palace of the source of phenomena.
Endowed with the qualities of the family,
an ocean of sky that bears its fruit,
its perfections spontaneously present,
is the indivisible, coemergent Victor.
The palace is the secret celestial mansion,
a maṇḍala of jewels with swirling light.
In the center of a lotus with its anthers,
encircling, round on every side,
adorned with garlands beyond measure,
are measureless arrays, tier on tier.
Supreme Mount Meru rises there,
and all around, encircling it,
are the oceans and the mountains,
ringing it, perfectly even and alike.
These stand firm in their due order:
in particular the peerless great red lake,
and in turn the ocean, the abode of milk.
On the mountain of bones, the aggregate
of the human body gathered from the elements,
which has a dwelling where outer and inner are equal,
all this is shown to be spontaneously present.
From that, through a variety of things,
appearing distinctly, the form of wisdom,
self-arisen great bliss itself,
arises everywhere, without equal.
The source of the tathāgatas of the three times,
the vajra seat of the essence,
from the indivisible vajra posture,
whose nature has no beginning and no end,
of the pure family of the great secret,
possesses the body of the vajra of desire.
In the south, at Kukkuṭapāda Mountain,
and facing Śrīparvata,
at Dhānakaṭa Parvata,
in the center of a lotus with spreading anthers,
the source of phenomena ever abides,
the glorious, many-storied house of joy,
the realm traversed by all the victors
of the ocean of secret mantra,
the great secret dharmadhātu.
In the glorious maṇḍala of the constellations,
together with the peoples of the barbarian lands,
with gods, asuras and gandharvas,
in the palace of self-arisen wisdom
of the fully perfected great secret,
he placed Brahmā, Indra, Viṣṇu and the lord Īśvara (Śiva)
at the four gates of the maṇḍala,
and then, to tame beings who are full of attachment,
he spoke the supreme essence.
The buddhas dwelling in the ten directions,
the hosts of bodhisattvas,
and the hosts of heroes and lords of yogis,
as many as the sands of the river Ganges,
myriads equal to the atoms of Mount Meru, were present.
To the Tathāgata, king of sages,
the great Vajradhara,
seated on a lion throne in their midst,
the great being Vajragarbha
draped his upper robe over one shoulder,
set his knee upon the ground,
joined his palms, and asked about this:
"The spontaneously present great mudrā
of the exalted great secret,
the path of the unchanging Vajra Vehicle;
the time, the place and the person,
the distinctions in teaching the domain of practice,
accomplishment, practice and the host of activities,
the methods, the powers and the entry,
with the causes that make the stages manifest:
Blessed One, please explain this very thing."
Thus he made his request.

Then the Blessed One Vajradhara declared:
"Great being, delighted, pleased and filled with faith
in the teaching of the guru, the Buddha,
you have asked about the secret meaning:
excellent, excellent!
In order to tame all who are hard to tame,
I shall truly teach you
the most wondrous treasury
of the wisdom of the mind of awakening.
Now, that which fully bestows
the accomplishment of every siddhi,
the essence of all the victors,
is the very sequence of the secret rites.
Among the field protectors who guard
the teaching of all the buddhas of the three times,
who abide by their command and hold the samaya,
the Protector is the chief, the lord of the ten.
Glorious Mahākāla,
who accomplishes the supreme activities
for all the glorious vidyādharas,
is the one who brings victory
in the battle against the assembled asuras.
By all the eight classes of gods and demons without exception
he is held as the chief one worthy of offerings.
With the glorious Great Goddess,
who became the black Remati,
as brother and sister they guard the teaching.
The secret of body, speech and mind,
the meaning of suchness alone, outer and inner:
as lord of qualities and activities,
the samaya Protector, the field protector,
is chief of the twenty-four,
and moreover brings the three under his power."

From the Tantra of the Source of Accomplishment of the Glorious Great Protector, this is the second chapter, on the intent concerning the secret in general.

Then the secret is to be explained.
This very unsurpassed accomplisher,
having seen the suffering of beings
fallen into the ocean of saṃsāra,
teaches each and every method
by which they may be freed.
As for the time: in the Fortunate Eon,
after countless ages have passed
since the end of the Age of Perfection,
from the Age of Two and the Age of Three
on through the sequence to the Age of Strife,
in the time of the Blessed One, when life lasts a hundred years,
at the end of the period of five hundred,
the degenerate time of that very teaching,
in the age of the unruly,
exceedingly savage and harsh:
at that time the Protector's teaching arises.
Persons, too, are explained as twofold:
the person who is to be liberated,
and the practitioner who liberates.
The first is one who destroys the teaching,
who reviles the Three Jewels,
who hates the guru and reviles me:
these and their like are declared the ones to be dealt with.
The practitioner is of three kinds:
one of Heruka and the glorious yoginī,
purified and trained, with clear samādhi,
who has attained the levels of the stages;
in common, in general and in particular,
the vajra holder who abides in samaya.
As for the distinctions of place:
knowing the times of approach and of accomplishment,
and the times for applying the rites,
pleasant and secluded places, charnel grounds and the like,
where meditative concentration grows, are the special ones.

In the domains of objects and the sense faculties,
whatever is seen and whatever is heard,
whatever is touched and whatever is recalled,
the appearances of the maṇḍala deities,
and every thing whatsoever that arises,
should be known as blissful.

Condensed from all the teachings,
going even beyond the bodhisattva path,
secret, taking the result as the path:
this is the Vajra Vehicle itself.
Holding, sustaining and guarding the teaching, these three:
this is the special protection.

Accomplished through the samādhi of approach
and the samādhi of close approach,
the glorious Blood-Drinker, great primordial wisdom,
perfecting all the pure characteristics,
abiding as a continuous stream of the merit of bliss,
makes pure strength increase.

At the time of approach, abiding
as Heruka, one's own deity,
one should meditate in front of oneself on the four-armed Protector,
servant of the command, bound by samaya.
Then light radiates from one's own mantra at the heart;
of the circling recitation,
one should especially do the recitation
aloud and very slowly.

At the time of close approach,
from oneself as Heruka
a hūṃ is sent out, and by the light radiating in front
one generates him, and at the end gathers him back.

At the time of accomplishment, Heruka
seals the crown of the Protector's head;
imagine him, together with the secret mantra,
as the threefold stack of beings.

The secret great king of the field protectors,
the way to accomplish him through three rites:
he, lord of the worldly and the world-transcending,
for one who abides wholly in great bliss,
dwelling in the three times and the three wheels,
is attained by one who keeps the observances.
The unsurpassed great accomplishment
is the great Wisdom Protector himself.
The eight goddesses, Crow-Faced and the rest,
surround him in their order.
In the center of the maṇḍala palace,
in the charnel ground, the Protector sports.
Hard to find in the three worlds,
the nature of samaya conduct
is always to keep one's own mantra hidden;
strive greatly to keep it secret.

As for the special activities,
know the sequence for applying them to rites:
paralyzing, expelling,
stupefying, causing to faint,
bewildering, making mute,
seizing with poison, making deaf and blind,
making one devour one's own flesh,
vomiting foul matter, poison and blood:
these, which bring untimely death,
are fully taught as the special rites.
Bringing down rain, scattering clouds,
bringing down lightning and hail,
turning these back from one's own side and protecting it,
repelling sorcery, binding speech,
destroying power, and magical displays:
these are the secrets of the minor rites.

The method is the sequence of the rite.
A certain person who is a fit support
should first please the guru,
second, obtain the four empowerments,
and third, having been blessed,
in accordance with the authorization of the entrusted command,
know the unerring order of engagement.
One should know the particulars of the support.

Power arises from knowing
all phenomena to be like illusions,
and, through the power of genuine truth,
realizing them to be without intrinsic nature.

The uncommon engagement is that of one
who, endowed with loving-kindness, compassion, the mind of awakening
and the four immeasurables,
keeps in mind the welfare of beings.
Actualizing is the close accomplishment:
time, number and signs.
At the beginning, the middle and the end,
for the principal deity and the retinue,
whatever is accomplished, as best, middling or least,
the divisions of the signs are taught.

The cause is unobstructed
dependent origination;
when it manifests directly,
no one is able to stop it.
From that the perfect result
arises like a ripened kernel.
Therein the meaning of the inexhaustible
essence of the unobstructed vajra,
the illuminator of genuine primordial wisdom,
is attained through the kindness of the guru.

This is the third chapter, which teaches the stages of the secret, from the Tantra of the Source of Accomplishment of the Glorious Great Protector.

Then the genuine practice of the Mighty One
who conquers the māras is fully explained.
The secret Great Protector,
together with his hidden consort
and the four secret ḍākinīs,
sports in delight.
With one face and four arms,
the hero who tames those hard to tame,
with one face and three eyes,
a wrathful dwarf the color of a blue lotus (utpala),
hair standing on end, his body terrifying,
is seated on a corpse seat,
adorned with eight nāgas as ornaments.
Wrathful, like a black rain cloud,
with three eyes like heaps of sparks
he gazes with a fierce stare.
Powerful, with the bearing of an elephant,
wearing a hanging necklace of entrails,
draped with a garland of freshly severed human heads,
utterly untamed, with a charming form,
great tamer of the māras, god of gods,
full of the moods, perfect in splendor:
the mood of charm that dispels the darkness of ignorance;
marvelous, proclaiming the Dharma, displaying laughter;
heroic, engaged in the conduct of the observances;
displaying terror as he tames the vicious;
the mood of ferocity that casts off wrong views;
wrathful in compassion as he cuts down the vicious;
wondrous as he conquers the afflictions;
his mind peaceful, unmoved by anger and desire.
His first right hand holds a curved knife,
his first left a skull full of blood,
his second right brandishes a sword,
and the left holds a khaṭvāṅga staff.
Proclaiming with his voice "ha ha,"
he crushes wrong views.
Wearing a skirt of tiger skin,
upon a multicolored lotus and a sun,
he stands in a self-arisen posture of play.
In front of him is the fierce lady,
the goddess with one face and two arms,
the color of darkness, one-faced,
two-armed, three-eyed, of wrathful manner,
holding a curved knife and a skull cup.
The father is adorned with the six bone ornaments,
the mother sealed with the five;
they abide embracing, inseparable in body,
encircled by a garland of firelight.
The guardian ladies of the retinue of the ocean of wisdom:
in the east, the ḍākinī Hārītī;
in the south, the one called Elephant-Nose;
in the west, the Matted-Haired Rākṣasī;
and in the north is Crown of Time.

Where the ocean and the riverbanks are,
With a body like black water,
Mahākāla, crusher of the vicious:
With effort one generates compassion,
And then the one to be slain is named.
Without compassion it will not succeed,
And therefore compassion should be generated.
Those who commit the five deeds of immediate retribution,
And those who throw the Buddha's teaching into turmoil,
Should, through the samādhi of the family of anger,
Be slain, merely for the time being.
Ever sporting with a fearsome face,
Bearing the force of utter ferocity,
Sporting from land to land,
And dwelling in the trees,
They protect the fields of the maṇḍala.
Encircled by the charnel grounds,
They pervade the abodes of the mātṛkās and the rest.
The field protectors worshipped by the nāgas,
Encircled by a powder of fire,
Are distinguished by gates and eyes.
Embodied by the mantra of the principal deity,
He dwells in the body of the practitioner.
These are taught in sequence, with their times.
For the mantrin who keeps the samaya,
Among the special substances for practice,
A torma of black grain is most auspicious.
Adorned with the great flesh,
The feast substances and pūjā should be offered with devotion.
From the distinctions of outer, inner and secret,
In the manner that has been taught, from the activities,
The particular, the continuous and the occasional
Become manifest through accomplishment and practice.
At no time whatsoever, out of anger,
And in no way whatsoever, should one destroy.
If one slays one who is not a fit object,
Because the yogin has broken the samaya,
Accomplishment can never come about.
Especially, the unsurpassed wisdom,
This samaya of practicing the samaya Protector,
Is greatly wondrous;
It joins one to the path of the sugatas.
This moon of the awakening mind
Abides through the waxing and the waning fortnights.
In the waxing it causes increase,
In the waning it causes decrease.
On the fifteenth of the waxing fortnight,
The ḍākinīs of the awakening mind too
Gather in great bliss.
This is taught as the definitive meaning.
The maṇḍala and its deities are the body itself.
Elsewhere, deity and maṇḍala,
Though sought, will never be found:
So the heroic Guide has always taught.
In the secret of body, speech and mind,
He who has become the life of all the wrathful ones,
Lord of qualities and activities,
Is the master of outer, inner and secret.
From the intrinsic nature in which the Teacher abides,
The wheel is summoned and dissolved without duality.
From the essence and the dregs come cause and effect,
And the particular modes of abiding, dependent origination,
Delusion and non-delusion.
Because he holds power over all the three realms,
The distinction between the Protector of worldly action
And the Protector of wisdom becomes manifest.
He who pervades designations and conventions
Is taught as the common one.
From these actions and these beings,
The obscurations, the common, the portions of the deity,
Declared directly and indirectly,
Life and what is not life:
Through cause, condition and exhortation,
The secret mantra recitation is revealed.
This is the manifestation.

This is the fourth chapter, the Manifestation of the Secret, from the Tantra of the Source of Accomplishment of the Glorious Great Protector.

Next, what is to be truly explained:
Heruka, the Glorious Protector himself,
And his secret emanation, Mahākāla:
I shall explain the stages of his practice.
This supreme method of secret practice
Should be given to the fortunate.
In the place where one practices,
Having abandoned unfavorable distractions,
One should then practice the Black One.
Gazing upon all beings,
With the mind of immeasurable awakening,
Having generated great compassion,
Seated upon a comfortable seat,
Facing south, hair loosened, naked,
First, in a single instant,
Generate oneself as the deity; at the heart,
From a a moon disk
Cool and radiant should be imagined.
Above it, from raṃ, a sun disk,
With seven horses and hot rays,
Radiating the nature of fire, should be imagined.
In its center, the black letter hūṃ,
Having the nature of the five wisdoms.
From the illumination of its light,
Imagine boundless rays of four colors.
From their radiating to the natural abode,
The gurus and the special deities,
Together with the maṇḍala and the wheel,
And Mahākāla with his retinue,
Dwell in the sky before you; to them
Go for refuge and present offerings,
Confess wrongdoing and so forth.
Having asked forbearance, request them to depart.
After that, all things whatsoever,
Without remainder, are dissolved into emptiness,
And one should recite:
oṃ svabhāva śuddhāḥ sarva dharmmāḥ svabhāva śuddho'haṃ
oṃ vajra śuddhāḥ sarva dharmmāḥ vajra śuddho'haṃ
oṃ yoga śuddhāḥ sarva dharmmā'yoga śuddho'haṃ
All outer and inner things
Should be imagined in the state of primordial purity.
That itself, together with the hūṃ,
Abides as mere nāda;
And from that sky-like state
One should pervade with the four abodes of Brahmā.
From that, these seed syllables
Should be imagined in order, as now explained.
From yaṃ, the wind maṇḍala;
From raṃ, the fire maṇḍala;
White khaṃ, the water maṇḍala;
From laṃ, the golden ground.
Upon it, from bruṃ, Mount Meru arises.
These are the essential nature of outer and inner.
On the upper portion of them,
In the center, from paṃ, a lotus,
A vast, multicolored lotus.
At its center, with pericarp and anthers,
From ha, a crossed vajra,
Imagined as possessing five colors.
In its center, from raṃ, a sun;
Arising from the seed syllable hūṃ,
The whole of the ground becomes vajra.

Translator's notes

The verse beginning "The vajra holder who, like an illusion or an echo" has no explicit main clause for its first four lines; it is rendered as a subject phrase leading into the statement about the Great Protector. The speaker of these "words of vajra speech" is not explicitly named, so a passive is used. "The five hundred years" refers to the last of the five-hundred-year periods of the teaching's decline. Several lines in the opening cosmological verses are obscure, notably "treasure vases with open mouths" and "clouds over but half a quarter of the sky"; these are rendered literally. The mountain names are identified as Kukkuṭapāda (Tibetan "Bird-Foot Mountain") and Śrīparvata; the latter reads "dpal gyi ril" in the text, taken as "dpal gyi ri la". "Brahmā, Indra" interprets the compressed "tshangs dbang". "Reviles me" takes "bdag" as the speaker; "reviles oneself" is also possible. "Lord of the ten" reads "mgon pos bcu'i" as "mgon po bcu'i". "Foul matter" renders གཤིས་དན, whose sense is unclear; it may be a corrupt form. The names of the southern, western and northern retinue goddesses (Elephant-Nose, Matted-Haired Rākṣasī, Crown of Time) could not be confidently identified with Sanskrit names and are translated literally; the northern name, written བྱང་དུས་ཡི་གཙུག, is especially uncertain in its reading. The "threefold stack of beings" refers to the nested samayasattva, jñānasattva and samādhisattva. The six and five "mudrās" of father and mother are taken as the bone ornaments. The line rendered "Ever sporting with a fearsome face" is uncertain; the Tibetan reads an obscure word (perhaps a corruption of a word for "face" with bared fangs), and the line is also a syllable short. "Powder of fire" and "gates and eyes" are rendered literally; they likely refer to features of the drawn maṇḍala. The "four abodes of Brahmā" are the four immeasurables (brahmavihāras).
