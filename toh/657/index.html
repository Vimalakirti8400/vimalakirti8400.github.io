About this text

Toh 657 in the Kangyur is known in Sanskrit as the Ārya Mahāmegha and in Tibetan as the Phags pa sprin chen po, rendered in English as The Noble Great Cloud. It is a Mahāyāna sūtra set dramatically in the palace of the nāga king Nanda, where the Buddha addresses a vast assembly of monks, bodhisattvas, and nāga kings, suggesting that its concerns include the nāga world, the powers associated with rain and clouds, and the spiritual blessings that flow from these forces. The text belongs to the general class of sūtra literature within the Kangyur, the canonical collection of the Buddha's translated words, and its elaborate opening assembly scene — replete with named nāga lords and a jeweled cloud maṇḍala mansion — signals the rich cosmological and protective dimensions characteristic of this genre.

In the language of India: Ārya Mahāmegha. In the language of Tibet: The Noble Great Cloud.

Homage to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas.

Thus did I hear at one time. The Blessed One was dwelling in the palace of the nāga king Nanda, in the pleasure grove of the nāga king Nanda and Upananda, within the great cloud maṇḍala mansion whose essence is the glory of precious jewels. He was accompanied by a great Saṅgha of monks, a great Saṅgha of bodhisattvas, and a great assembly of nāga kings, namely: the nāga king Nanda, Upananda, Sāgara, Anavatapta, Tejin, Varuṇa, Sthira, Dhṛtarāṣṭra, Dhanada's son, the nāga king Takṣaka, Pṛthivīpāla's son, Mudgara, Śrīteja, Śrībhadra, Vidyullatā, Maṇiśiraḥ, Maṇidhara, Prabhāśikhara, Śikhāvinda, and the nāga king Indradhvaja, Indrapūjita, Jambūdhvaja, Maṅgalin, Mahāmaṇḍala, Mahāhasta, Nakṣatravat, Candrateja, Śrīmati, Candradarśin, the nāga king Sudarśana, Sthirātman, Kaṇṭhamaṇi, Meghaja, Dhārādhara, Prabhañjana, Viprabhañjana, Tejodbhava, Mahāgarvita, Gambhīraghoṣa, and the nāga king Mahāghoṣa, Vimalaghona, Utpala, Mahābala, Ārakadhara, Nānasena, Mahāpāśa, and the nāga king Elāpattra, Sarvābhāsa, Karkaśakarṇa, Śaṅkha, Dardara, the nāga king Upadardara, Sukhaṅkara, Hastatāra, Mahāhastatāra, Mahāmala, Udḍīna, Niśśalya, Lambaka, Kṛmisthāna, Kṛṣṇa, and the nāga king Indrasena, Kardama, Upakardama, Śaṅkhadhara, Kheḍin, Suprabha, Hastin, Tīkṣṇa, Pītaśyāma, Vidyulpradīpa, Mahāvidyulprabha, Balādhideva, Udakabala, and the nāga king Uttamaprāvṛta, Amṛtin, Tīrthika, Vaiḍūryaraśmi, Suvarṇaghoṣa, Suvarṇaprabha, Candradhvajaraśmi, Sūryaraśmi, Udaya, Vatsaśirā, and the nāga king Śveta, Kṛṣṇa, Yama, Śīlavat, Maṇḍūka, Haṃsateja, Valayindhara, Dhāraṇidhara, Sahasraśirā, Cūḍāmaṇi, and the nāga king Arthādarśin, Mahāmegha, Susena, Bhūpāla, Manuṣyada, Sudānta, Prītyutpāda, Kumbhīramukha, Viṣadhara, and the nāga king Kṣipratīkṣṇa, Padmaka, Pucchin, Prasārin, Bhayankaraḥ, Tejomanorama, Pañcaśirā, Vallīra, Syandanajita, and the nāga king Guru, the nāga king Dīrghapucchin, Mahāśirā, Pimbika, Vitara, Kṣipragāmin, and the nāga king Triśirā, Nāgasena, Mahāteja, Kardamadatta, and the nāga king Trāsin, Prabhādīpta, Saptaśirā, Mahāsahasra, Priyadarśana, Mahāpratāpa, Nirmalaśrī, Nayanaśobhana, Mahākrodha, Prabhāskandha, and the nāga king Pracaṇḍa, Krodhavegaśālin, Ratnadāna, Ratnadānamahāmegha, and Indratejas —

At that time those nāga kings, together with their retinues, rose from their seats, arranged their robes over one shoulder, placed their right knees on the ground, and, turning with palms joined toward the Blessed One, offered him profound reverence and deep prostrations. They presented to the Blessed One flowers, incense, perfume, garlands, ointments, powders, garments, parasols, victory banners, flags, silk streamers, the sound of music, cymbals, the beating of drums, song and melody, jeweled flowers, jeweled streamers, pearl garlands, nāga flowers, and supremely beautiful nets of pearl — offerings various, immeasurable, and beyond counting. Having presented these, they circumambulated him and sat down to one side.

Seated to one side, they then offered aspiration prayers:

May bodies as vast as oceans, equal in number to the finest atoms of all world-system oceans, be present within each and every maṇḍala-ocean of the assemblies of buddhas and bodhisattvas, within all the boundlessly vast oceans of world-systems, and within each finest atom of all earth, water, fire, and wind, and within each finest atom of all that appears as form. And from each and every one of those finest atoms, may there arise clouds of body-oceans — perfectly transcending all countable oceans of bodies — numberless, immeasurable, inconceivable, incomparable, beyond measure, beyond expression, utterly transcendent. And from each such body, may there stream forth immeasurable and numberless clouds of hand-oceans, blessed and flowing in continuous torrents in every direction, so that from each and every atom, distinct, non-intermingled clouds of oceans of all offerings, spreading to fill the ground of every direction, may make offerings to all buddhas and bodhisattvas. Such is our aspiration: that immeasurable, numberless, inconceivable, beyond measure, without limit, and beyond expression distinct non-intermingled cloud-oceans of bodies of universal conduct, blessed so as to cover all of space, and clouds of bodhisattva body-oceans — even as they truly are — together with the dense colors and light-rays of all precious jewels, bodies like all suns and moons, and clouds of oceans of retinue-maṇḍalas of bodies —

Having made aspirational prayers of this kind, those nāga kings then circumambulated the Blessed One three times, prostrated at his feet, and, the Blessed One having given his permission, each took their seat.

At that time, the great lord of nāgas of the great trichiliocosm, endowed with the power of irreversible aspiration, arrayed with an ocean-cloud retinue of infinite attendants, the one called King Like a Parasol of the Majestic Maṇḍala Arraying an Ocean-Cloud of Infinite Attendants, had come to this Jambudvīpa in order to make offering to, pay homage to, honor, and hear the Dharma from the Tathāgata.

Then the nāga lord, King Like a Parasol of the Majestic Maṇḍala Arraying an Ocean-Cloud of Infinite Attendants, rose from his seat, arranged his robe over one shoulder, placed his right knee on the ground, and, joining his palms and bowing in the direction where the Blessed One was, addressed the Blessed One in these words: "If the Blessed One would grant me the opportunity to ask and to receive a prophetic declaration in answer to my request, I would ask the Blessed One, the Tathāgata, the arhat, the perfectly and completely awakened buddha, something on my behalf."

When this was said, the Blessed One declared to the nāga lord, King Like a Parasol of the Majestic Maṇḍala Arraying an Ocean-Cloud of Infinite Attendants: "Nāga lord, ask whatever you wish, and I shall gladden your heart by prophetically declaring the answer to whatever you ask."

When this was declared, the nāga lord, King Like a Parasol of the Majestic Maṇḍala Arraying an Ocean-Cloud of Infinite Attendants, addressed the Blessed One in these words: "Blessed One, how may the stream of all suffering of all nāgas be cut off? How may they come to be endowed with every happiness and become thoroughly joyful? How may the rains fall in regular streams here in Jambudvīpa at the proper times? How may all grasses, trees, herbs, and forests grow? How may all crops arise? How may all vital essences arise rightly? And how may the people of Jambudvīpa come to be endowed with happiness?"

When this was said, the Blessed One declared to the nāga lord, King Like a Parasol of the Majestic Maṇḍala Arraying an Ocean-Cloud of Infinite Attendants: "Nāga lord, it is excellent, excellent that you, having entered for the benefit and happiness of all sentient beings, intend to ask the Tathāgata about this matter. Therefore, nāga lord, listen well, take it to heart,

and I shall explain it to you. Nāga lord, if one is endowed with a single quality, all the nāga sufferings of all nāgas will be cut off without remainder, and they will come to be endowed with happiness. What is that one quality? It is this: loving-kindness (maitrī).

Nāga lord, gods and humans who abide in loving-kindness will not be burned by fire, will not be overcome by weapons, will not be swept away by water, will not be overcome by poison, and will not be overpowered by the armies of enemies. They will lie down in happiness and will awaken in happiness. They will be fully protected by their own merit. By great merit they will be resplendent with majesty. They will not be looked down upon by the world with its gods. They will be beautiful and pleasing to behold. They will be unobstructed in all their paths of travel. All their sufferings will be cut off without remainder, and they will be supremely joyful. They will be endowed with all happiness, and having realized the highest human Dharma, after the dissolution of the body they will be reborn in the Brahma world. Nāga lord, these are the benefits for gods and humans who abide in loving-kindness.

Nāga lord, therefore one should abide by the bodily actions of loving-kindness, the verbal actions of loving-kindness, and the mental actions of loving-kindness.

Nāga lord, furthermore, the dhāraṇī called Bestower of All Happiness is to be recited. It cuts off without remainder all the nāga sufferings of all nāgas, and it bestows all happiness. It causes the rains to fall in their proper seasons upon this Jambudvīpa, and it causes all grasses, creeping plants, herbs, forests, and crops to grow. Nāga lord, what is the dhāraṇī called Bestower of All Happiness? It is this:

tadyathā dhāraṇī dhāraṇī uttāraṇi saṃpratiṣṭhite vijayavarṇa satyapratiṃjñāsaraha jñānavati uddepādani vināmani abhiṣecani abhivyāhāra śubhavati ajimatu ehi kumabalaṇi bahahārakleśana dhunapāpaṃ śodhaya mārgaṇa niratika dharmatāśuddha lokavitimirarajasa duḥkhaśamana sarvabuddha avalokana adhiṣṭhiti prajñājñānavisvāhā.

Nāga lord, this dhāraṇī, which accomplishes all the meanings of the dhāraṇī's words, which enters into the dharmakāya, and which has been blessed by all buddhas — this Bestower of All Happiness — is to be held and to be recited.

Nāga lord, furthermore, the blessed and arranged majestic essence arisen from the class of clouds, which makes itself appear as an emanation, which is the glorious cloud-maṇḍala of the stream of waters that is the crest of wisdom, which illuminates with golden rays — the names of the bones arisen from the tips of a single hair and of the Tathāgatas of those lineages are to be grasped and kept in mind. These cut off without remainder all the nāga sufferings of all nāgas, all nāga lineages, all nāga clans, all nāga families, all nāga elements, all nāga birth-places, all nāga kings, all nāga daughters, and all nāga attendants, and they accomplish all the requisites of happiness.

This is the sixty-fourth chapter, called "The Maṇḍala of the Wind Circle that Brings Down Rain," from the noble Great Cloud Sūtra of the Great Vehicle, in one hundred thousand verses.

The ritual for one who wishes to bring down heavy rain is as follows. In a clean outdoor place with unobstructed sky, one sets out a blue cushion and hangs up a blue banner. The Dharma-teacher bathes, remains near the octagonal bathing pool, dresses in clean blue garments, anoints himself with sweet incense, eats the three white foods, and then sits upon a blue seat. Facing east, he makes supplication to all the Tathāgatas, and reads this chapter of the Maṇḍala of the Great Cloud continuously, without interruption, day and night. He places four vases filled with blue water that have been consecrated. He also prepares, to the best of his ability, torma offerings, incense, and flowers.

The Dharma-teacher then draws with black ink in the four directions as follows. To the east, at a distance of about three arm-lengths, he draws a three-headed nāga king together with a retinue of various nāga attendants. To the south, at a distance of about five arm-lengths, he draws a five-headed nāga king together with a retinue of various nāga attendants. To the west, at a distance of about seven arm-lengths, he draws a seven-headed nāga king together with a retinue of various nāga attendants.

To the north, at a distance of about nine arm-lengths, he draws a nine-headed nāga king together with a retinue of various nāga attendants.

The Dharma-teacher then guards his body and abides in loving-kindness, settles his mind in compassion toward all sentient beings, makes supplication to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas, and shares the roots of virtue with the nāgas. This is the ritual procedure.

Whoever reads this chapter on the wind-maṇḍala of the Great Cloud when rain is not falling will, within one day, two days, or at most seven days, without fail see rain descend. The ocean may at times fall out of season, yet this rain ritual from the sacred scriptures of the Great Sage shall never be otherwise. Nevertheless, one must abide in moral discipline and virtue, thoroughly abandoning corrupt conduct, and completely relinquishing meat, fish, and the like. One should eat the three white dairy foods — curds, yogurt, and milk — along with large rice sweetened with molasses and the three sweets, and recite while doing so. Practicing in this way, success will come. As the King of Sages has declared, one should have no doubt whatsoever.

This concludes the sixty-fourth chapter of the noble Mahāyāna sūtra the Great Cloud — the Chapter on the Wind-Maṇḍala for Bringing Down Rain — together with its ritual procedure.

Translator's notes

The bulk of this passage is a lengthy enumeration of nāga kings in the opening assembly list of the Ārya Mahāmegha Sūtra (Noble Great Cloud Sūtra). The Tibetan names are phonetic renderings of Sanskrit originals, many of which are partially opaque or attested in variant forms across manuscripts. The Sanskrit reconstructions given here follow the most plausible etymological equivalents based on standard nāga-name conventions in this genre of sūtra literature, but readers should be aware that several names are uncertain and some may reflect Tibetan calques rather than direct Sanskrit transliterations. In particular, names such as Mudgara, Vitara, Pimbika, Vallīra, Kheḍin, and a handful of others are tentative. The name rendered "Kumbhīramukha" (crocodile-faced) follows the Tibetan literally. No single critical edition of the Sanskrit original of this text is currently available to confirm all reconstructions. The passage is a long list of nāga names followed by a liturgical aspiration prayer of the Avataṃsaka-sūtra type, built on the characteristic Gaṇḍavyūha style of stacked cosmological hyperbole. The nāga names are reconstructed from the Tibetan by standard back-translation conventions; the Sanskrit originals are not preserved in this portion of the extant Sanskrit text, so the reconstructions are approximations based on the Tibetan semantic equivalents. The aspiration prayer section that closes the passage is deliberately left as an open construction in the Tibetan, flowing into the next section; the translation preserves this syntactic openness as faithfully as English allows. The passage is a dense, liturgically formulaic series of offering-cloud accumulations typical of the Gaṇḍavyūha section of the Avataṃsaka Sūtra. The recurring refrain of homage, reverence, praise, and offering ("bsti stang du bya'o, bla mar bya'o, ri mor bya'o, mchod par bya'o") is rendered consistently as "render homage, act as supreme objects of reverence, offer praise, and make offerings." The term "ri mo" here carries the sense of offering praise or adoration in the form of imagery or artistic veneration; this is a defensible and conventional reading in this liturgical context. The phrase rendered "as half-body of a bodhisattva" (བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའི་ལུས་ཕྱེད) is unusual. It may describe sandalwood powder covering the lower half of a bodhisattva's body, or the term ཕྱེད may be functioning adverbially to mean "throughout the body." The precise referent is obscure and may depend on a longer textual context.

The term ཨནྡ་རྙིལ represents the Sanskrit Anavatapta, the legendary cool lake in the Himalayas, source of the four great rivers; it is kept in transliterated form for consistency.

The passage ends mid-sentence, consistent with it being one segment of a longer continuous offering-cloud enumeration. The term rendered "indranīla" refers to a blue gemstone, likely sapphire or a blue lotus variety, transliterated from the Sanskrit indranīla. The Tibetan "ཨནྡ་རྙིལ" is a phonetic rendering of this Sanskrit term and is kept in transliteration accordingly.

The phrase "clouds of vast ocean-clouds of buddhas and bodhisattvas" reflects a somewhat redundant Tibetan construction; the text literally reads "buddha and bodhisattva gaṅ-chen-mtsho-sprin rgya-mtsho," meaning "great ocean-cloud ocean," and appears to be an intensive formulaic expression rather than an error. The phrase rendered at the close as "all the ocean-clouds of buddhas and bodhisattvas throughout the vast ocean of clouds of the great ocean" translates a somewhat redundant accumulation of honorific ocean-and-cloud imagery typical of this liturgical text. The expression "gang chen mtsho'i sprin gyi rgya mtsho" literally means "the ocean of clouds of the great lake (or vast ocean)," an intensifying formula; the rendering preserves its rhetorical force without further unpacking. The name of the nāga king is a long descriptive title rendered consistently throughout: "King Like a Parasol of the Majestic Maṇḍala Arraying an Ocean-Cloud of Infinite Attendants." The Tibetan compound is formulaic and slightly varied in spelling across occurrences but refers to the same figure throughout; the rendering is kept uniform. The dhāraṇī syllables are rendered as they appear in the Tibetan phonetic transcription of Sanskrit, without normalization beyond what the source permits. The passage preserves the standard Mahāyāna dhāraṇī formula and is not a continuous grammatical Sanskrit text; transliteration rather than translation is the correct approach here.
