About this text

Toh 734 in the Kangyur is a dhāraṇī text known in Sanskrit as the Ārya-vijayavatī-nāma-pratyaṅgirā and in Tibetan as Phyir zlog pa 'phags pa rnam par rgyal ba can, rendered in English as the Repelling Dhāraṇī Called the Noble Victorious One. It belongs to the dhāraṇī and mantra literature of the Kangyur, a class of texts concerned with the recitation of sacred formulas believed to confer protection and avert harm. The text is devoted to a powerful repelling dhāraṇī said to subdue harmful sorcery, dispel curses, pacify inauspicious forces, and ward off a wide range of fears and dangers, making it a practical ritual text oriented toward the protection of all beings. It opens with a series of homages to the buddhas, bodhisattvas, knowledge-mantra holders, the Three Jewels, and in particular the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara, situating it firmly within the Mahāyāna devotional and protective tradition.

Translator's notes

The terms translated as "zombie" render the Tibetan ro langs, literally "risen corpse," a recognized category of undead being in the Indo-Tibetan ritual tradition.

The phrase rendered "vase-spirits" translates grul bum, a class of harmful entities associated with ritual vessels; the exact nature varies by commentary.

The phrase "shadow-oppressors" translates grib gnon, spirits or forces that bring ritual pollution or shadow-borne oppression. "Amnesia-spirits" translates brjed byed, literally "that which causes forgetting," a class of harmful entity that induces forgetfulness or mental dissociation.

The mantra syllables throughout are transliterated as they appear and are not semantically translated, as is standard practice for dhāraṇī texts.

The Sanskrit title in the colophon, Ārya-vijayavatī-nāma-pratyaṅgirā, identifies this as a pratyaṅgirā-type dhāraṇī — a class of texts specifically designed to repel and reverse harmful spells directed at the practitioner. The passage contains several sequences of colored-hair epithets in Sanskrit, likely invoking a multi-hued goddess: nīlakeśe (blue-haired), pītakeśe (yellow-haired), lohitakeśe (red-haired), avadātakeśe (white-haired). These are left in transliterated Sanskrit as they function as dhāraṇī-style invocations within the ritual text.

The final verse section shifts register entirely, functioning as a praise-and-promise stanza common to dhāraṇī texts, assuring the wearer of the text's protective efficacy. "Victorious one who repels all" renders ཕྱིར་ཟློག་རྣམ་པར་རྒྱལ་བ་ཅན and "the arising of victors" renders རྒྱལ་བ་འབྱུང་བ་གསུངས་པ; the latter phrase is somewhat cryptic and may refer to the teaching concerning the coming of buddhas or the proclamation of their arising, a common dhāraṇī motif.
