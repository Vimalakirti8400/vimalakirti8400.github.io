About this text

The Noble Great Vehicle Sūtra called The Irreversible Wheel, known in Sanskrit as the Āryāvaivartacakra and in Tibetan as Phakpa Chir Mi Dokpé Khorlo, is a Mahāyāna sūtra preserved as Toh 240 in the Kangyur, where it belongs to the General Sūtra section. It opens at Śrāvastī, in the Jeta Grove, where the Buddha, the youthful Mañjuśrī, and the bodhisattva Maitreya each enter deep meditative absorption in the last watch of the night, setting the scene for a teaching that gathers disciples such as Śāriputra and Ānanda. The discourse concerns irreversibility on the path to awakening, the state from which a practitioner can no longer turn back, and it presents its teaching as the Irreversible Wheel of great wisdom. In its closing section the Buddha explains how faith in this teaching completes all six perfections and shortens one's time in cyclic existence, before the assembly rejoices amid wondrous signs. According to its colophon, it was translated into Tibetan by the Indian preceptors Jinamitra, Dānaśīla, and Munivarma together with the Tibetan editor-translator Yeshé Dé.

Translation

In the language of India: aryā vaivarta cakra nāma mahāyāna sūtra. In the language of Tibet: The Noble Great Vehicle Sūtra called "The Irreversible Wheel." The first section.

Homage to all buddhas and bodhisattvas.

Thus did I hear at one time. The Blessed One (the Buddha) was staying at Śrāvastī, in the Jeta Grove of Prince Jeta, the park of Anāthapiṇḍada, together with a great saṅgha of monks.

Then, in the last watch of the night, the Blessed One entered into the meditative absorption called Stainless Light. Mañjuśrī, the youthful, entered into the meditative absorption called All-Illuminating. The bodhisattva great being Maitreya entered into the meditative absorption called Universal Light.

Then, in the last watch of the night, at daybreak, the venerable Śāriputra came out of his dwelling and went to where the dwelling of Mañjuśrī the youthful was. As the venerable Śāriputra thought to enter the dwelling of Mañjuśrī the youthful, he saw ten thousand lotuses rising up all around the Blessed One's residence, and he also heard a great sound of song. He saw that light came forth from those lotuses as well. He saw that the light lit up the whole of the Jeta Grove of Prince Jeta. He saw that the entire great city of Śrāvastī and the whole of the great trichiliocosm, the world system of a billion worlds, became illuminated as well.

Then, as the venerable Śāriputra was thinking that he would not go into the dwelling of Mañjuśrī the youthful, he found that he had entered it, and knew that he himself was sitting in front of Mañjuśrī the youthful. He saw Mañjuśrī the youthful sitting with his legs crossed. Seeing him, he snapped his fingers, but Mañjuśrī did not rise. Then he cleared his throat, but still Mañjuśrī did not rise. Then, having seen the miraculous display of Mañjuśrī the youthful, he felt as though he were dwelling in the midst of the great ocean, and he saw Mañjuśrī the youthful, seated with his legs crossed, travelling eastward together with the dwelling.

Then the venerable Śāriputra sat cross-legged in front of Mañjuśrī the youthful, gazing at Mañjuśrī the youthful without blinking, and he saw Mañjuśrī the youthful pass eastward beyond as many world systems as there are grains of sand in the river Ganges, and arrive at a world system called the Realm of Irreversibility.

Then he saw the tathāgata called Body of a Fully Blossomed Lotus Covered with Rays of Light. He saw lotuses come forth from the pores of that tathāgata, and those lotuses filled ten thousand leagues. He saw that those lotuses were in full bloom, each with a thousand petals. He saw, seated on the centers of those lotuses, bodhisattvas endowed with the thirty-two major marks, their bodies the color of gold. Among them was one great lotus that had become empty, the Blessed One having risen from it. He saw Mañjuśrī the youthful sit upon that great lotus and rise up into the sky together with it; having risen up, circumambulate that Blessed One three times; bow his head at the feet of that Blessed One; and then sit cross-legged on that same great lotus.

Then the tathāgata Body of a Fully Blossomed Lotus Covered with Rays of Light, although he knew, said this to Mañjuśrī the youthful: "Mañjuśrī the youthful, where have you just come from?"

When he had said this, Mañjuśrī the youthful said this to the tathāgata Body of a Fully Blossomed Lotus Covered with Rays of Light: "Blessed One, I have come from the Sahā world system."

Then that Blessed One's attendant, the bodhisattva called Sweet Sound, rose from his lotus seat, draped his upper robe over one shoulder, bowed his head at the feet of that Blessed One, placed his right knee on the center of the lotus, bowed toward the Blessed One with his palms joined, and said this to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, how far away is that Sahā world system?"

When he had asked this, the tathāgata Body of a Fully Blossomed Lotus Covered with Rays of Light said this to the bodhisattva great being Sweet Sound: "Son of noble family, it lies to the west of this world system, beyond as many world systems as there are grains of sand in the river Ganges."

He asked, "Blessed One, which blessed one teaches the Dharma in that world system?"

That Blessed One said, "He is called the Tathāgata, the arhat, the perfectly complete Buddha Śākyamuni."

He asked, "Blessed One, what kind of Dharma does he teach?"

He said, "He teaches on the basis of three vehicles."

He asked, "Blessed One, what are those three vehicles?"

He said, "The vehicle of the śrāvakas, the vehicle of the pratyekabuddhas, and the Great Vehicle. It is on the basis of those three vehicles that the Blessed One, the Tathāgata, the arhat, the perfectly complete Buddha Śākyamuni teaches the Dharma."

He asked, "Blessed One, are not the Dharma teachings of all the buddhas, the blessed ones, in agreement?"

He said, "Son of noble family, the Dharma teachings of the buddhas, the blessed ones, are in agreement."

He asked, "To what extent are the Dharma teachings of the buddhas, the blessed ones, in agreement?"

He said, "The Dharma teachings of the buddhas, the blessed ones, agree in the irreversible wheel of Dharma."

He asked, "Blessed One, why then does that Blessed One teach the Dharma on the basis of three vehicles?"

He said, "Son of noble family, those beings are inclined toward what is lesser. Because they do not enter the one vehicle, he guides them in that way through skill in means. Son of noble family, the buddhas, the blessed ones, possess great skill in means. That Blessed One has appeared in a time of the five degenerations, and those beings have no faith in the one vehicle."

He asked, "Blessed One, is it really so hard to teach the Dharma there?"

He said, "Son of noble family, it is so."

He asked, "Blessed One, it is truly my good fortune that I was not born there."

He said, "Son of noble family, do not say that."

He asked, "Blessed One, why is that?"

He said, "Son of noble family, compared with someone who accomplishes roots of virtue here for twenty thousand years, someone there accomplishes far more merit in a single morning. And why? Because that world system is so utterly afflicted."

He asked, "Blessed One, how utterly afflicted has that world system become?"

He said, "Son of noble family, as for how utterly afflicted that world system has become: were you to listen to the utter affliction of that world system, your whole lifespan would run out."

Then the bodhisattva great being Sweet Sound took lotuses made of the seven precious substances and scattered them in the direction of the Blessed One, the Tathāgata, the arhat, the perfectly complete Buddha Śākyamuni. Having scattered them, he said again and again, "I shall behold that Tathāgata, the arhat, the perfectly complete Buddha."

He said, "Son of noble family, do you wish to see that Tathāgata, the arhat, the perfectly complete Buddha?"

He replied, "Blessed One, I wish to see him."

He said, "Then go together with this Mañjuśrī the youthful."

Then the bodhisattva Sweet Sound said this to Mañjuśrī the youthful: "Mañjuśrī, I shall go to behold that Tathāgata, the arhat, the perfectly complete Buddha."

Mañjuśrī said, "Son of noble family, for the moment there are many buddhas, blessed ones, to whom I must pay homage and offer veneration."

He said, "Then I too shall pay homage and offer veneration together with you."

Then Mañjuśrī the youthful paid homage to the Tathāgata, the arhat, the perfectly complete Buddha Body of a Fully Blossomed Lotus Covered with Rays of Light and circumambulated him three times. Then, in the time it takes a strong man to stretch out his bent arm or bend his outstretched arm, Mañjuśrī the youthful vanished from that world system, and he appeared in the presence of the buddhas, the blessed ones, in as many world systems to the east as there are grains of sand in the river Ganges, and in every one of them this very Dharma teaching was taking place. So it was too in the south, the west, the north, the zenith, the nadir and the intermediate directions. Yet here in the Sahā world system, in Jambudvīpa, night had not yet given way to dawn.

Then the venerable Ānanda, while still sitting in his dwelling, saw light coming in through the gaps of the window. Thinking that he would go out of his dwelling, he rose from his seat, and he saw that before the sun had risen the whole of the Jeta Grove of Prince Jeta had become illuminated. He saw that the whole of the Jeta Grove of Prince Jeta was filled with clear, limpid, blue water, and that there were neither trees nor temples there. Seeing this, he thought, "Since these portents, which have never occurred before, are appearing, a great Dharma will surely be taught today."

Then the venerable Ānanda thought, "I shall set my foot on the ground." When the venerable Ānanda then set his foot on the ground, it was not wet, nor was it affected in any way, and joy and happiness arose in him. He went to where the Blessed One's residence was, and on arriving he saw ten thousand lotuses all around the Blessed One's residence, and he heard a great sound of music and a great sound of song. He saw that light came forth from those lotuses as well, and he saw that this light illuminated the whole of the great trichiliocosm, the world system of a billion worlds. Joy and gladness arose in him, and he placed his right knee on the ground, joined his palms, and paid homage to the Blessed One. As he paid homage, night turned to dawn and the sun rose.

From among those lotuses, one went to the center of the Jeta Grove of Prince Jeta and remained there. Then the venerable Ānanda saw that lotus remaining in the center of the Jeta Grove of Prince Jeta. Then the venerable Ānanda thought, "Portents of a Dharma teaching have occurred. I shall go and prepare the Blessed One's seat." As soon as the Blessed One's seat was prepared, the great earth quaked in six ways. The whole of the Jeta Grove of Prince Jeta was covered all over with flowers. Trees laden with flowers and with fruit appeared as well.

All the monks too thought to come out of their dwellings, but seeing the whole of the Jeta Grove of Prince Jeta filled with clear, blue water, they were able neither to go out nor to turn back. They saw no trees or temples there, but they saw a great radiance. While still remaining in those very dwellings, they thought, "Since these portents are appearing, a great Dharma will surely be taught today."

Then the Blessed One arose from that meditative absorption and sat down on the seat that had been prepared. As soon as the Blessed One was seated, Mañjuśrī the youthful emerged from the depths of the earth together with countless tens of millions of bodhisattvas. Having emerged, he bowed his head at the feet of the Blessed One, circumambulated the Blessed One three times, and sat down before the Blessed One together with those bodhisattva great beings.

Then the Blessed One said to the venerable Ānanda, "Ānanda, go, and summon all the monks, nuns, laymen, and laywomen, however many are staying in Prince Jeta's Grove in Śrāvastī."

"Excellent, Blessed One," replied the venerable Ānanda, and heeding the Blessed One, he went about from monastery to monastery and said to the monks, "Venerable ones, the Teacher is calling you."

They replied, "We too have recognized the portents, yet we are unable to go. Why? Because we see that the whole of Prince Jeta's Grove is filled with clear blue water."

Then the venerable Ānanda, having heard what those monks said, went to where the Blessed One was, bowed his head at the feet of the Blessed One, and said to the Blessed One, "Blessed One, the monks are not able to come. Why? Because they see that the whole of Prince Jeta's Grove is brimming with clear blue water."

The Blessed One declared, "Ānanda, those monks perceive as water what is not water. Just as those monks perceive as water what is not water, in the same way they perceive as the fruit of stream-entry what is not the fruit of stream-entry; they perceive as the fruit of once-returning what is not the fruit of once-returning; they perceive as the fruit of non-returning what is not the fruit of non-returning; they perceive as the fruit of arhatship what is not the fruit of arhatship; they perceive as a pratyekabuddha what is not pratyekabuddhahood; they perceive as form what is not form; and so with what is not feeling, what is not perception, what is not formations, and what is not consciousness, they perceive it as consciousness. Ānanda, go and summon them, and they will come."

Then the venerable Ānanda, heeding the Blessed One, entered the great city of Śrāvastī in order to summon those monks once more, and he announced to the monks, the nuns, the laymen, and the laywomen, "Venerable ones, the Teacher is calling you."

Then the venerable Ānanda, having announced this to the four assemblies, went to where the Blessed One was, bowed his head at the feet of the Blessed One, and said to the Blessed One, "Blessed One, I have announced it to the four assemblies."

Then the Blessed One declared to the venerable Mahāmaudgalyāyana, "Maudgalyāyana, go, and summon all who are present in the great trichiliocosm, the world system of a billion worlds: the bodhisattva great beings who have donned the great armor, and the monks, nuns, laymen, laywomen, gods, nāgas, yakṣas, gandharvas, asuras, garuḍas, kinnaras, mahoragas, humans, and nonhumans, however many there are."

"Excellent, Blessed One," replied the venerable Mahāmaudgalyāyana, and heeding the Blessed One, in an instant, a moment, a split second, just as a strong man might stretch out his bent arm or bend his outstretched arm, Mahāmaudgalyāyana announced it throughout the great trichiliocosm to the bodhisattva great beings, the monks, the nuns, the laymen, the laywomen, the gods, the nāgas, the yakṣas, the gandharvas, the asuras, the garuḍas, the kinnaras, the mahoragas, the humans, and the nonhumans. Then, through the might of the Buddha and the power of his own miraculous abilities, he sat down before the Blessed One and said to the Blessed One, "Blessed One, I have announced it to the four assemblies."

Then those four assemblies came to fill an area a thousand leagues (yojanas) in circumference, and the gods filled the sky above to a height of some fifty thousand leagues.

Then Mañjuśrī the youthful said to the Blessed One, "Blessed One, now that these four assemblies have gathered, and the gods too have filled the sky, may the Blessed One, the Tathāgata, the Arhat, the perfectly complete Buddha, please teach the Dharma concerning the Irreversible Wheel. Blessed One, in this assembly there are many monks, nuns, laymen, and laywomen, and many hundreds of thousands of gods. Since there are many others besides who perceive the fruit of stream-entry, who perceive the fruit of once-returning, who perceive the fruit of non-returning, who perceive the fruit of arhatship, and who perceive pratyekabuddhahood, then in order to free them from their perceptions, may the Blessed One please explain with what intention the Blessed One taught the fruit of stream-entry, the fruit of once-returning, the fruit of non-returning, and the fruit of arhatship, and with what intention the Blessed One taught pratyekabuddhahood."

Then the Blessed One gave his consent by remaining silent. Then the venerable Śāriputra said this to the Blessed One:

"Blessed One, last night, in the latter part of the night, I rose, came out of my dwelling, and went to where the dwelling of Mañjuśrī the youthful was. When I had arrived there and was about to enter the dwelling of Mañjuśrī the youthful, I saw the Blessed One's residence, and all around the Blessed One's residence I saw ten thousand lotuses. I heard a great sound of music and a great sound of song. I also saw light coming forth from those lotuses, and I saw that light come to pervade the whole of Prince Jeta's Grove, the whole city of Śrāvastī, and the entire great trichiliocosm. Blessed One, of what is this a portent?"

When he had said this, the Blessed One spoke these words to the venerable Śāriputra: "Śāriputra, this is the portent of the teaching of this Dharma, which Mañjuśrī has urged me to give."

Then the venerable Ānanda said this to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, last night, during the last watch of the night, while I was inside my dwelling, I saw light coming in through the gaps of the window. Thinking, 'I shall go out of my dwelling,' I rose from my seat. Before the sun had risen, the whole of Prince Jeta's Grove had become pervaded by it, and the whole of Prince Jeta's Grove was brimming with clear blue water. I saw that no one was going in here, and that there was no monastery to be seen either. Blessed One, of what is this a portent?"

The Blessed One declared, "Ānanda, this is the portent of the teaching of this untainted Dharma, which Mañjuśrī the youthful has urged me to give."

Then, at that time, the Blessed One spoke to the venerable Ānanda in verse:

"Fearless Mañjughoṣa
has asked about this:
this wisdom is untainted,
the unsurpassed wisdom of the buddhas.

Fearless Mañjughoṣa,
endowed with wisdom, has asked about it:
this wisdom is free of concepts,
free of elaboration, and free of defilement.

In it no thing is established,
and likewise nothing is unestablished.
The wisdom that has abandoned everything
is what Mañjuśrī has fully asked about.

In it no fruit is established;
it is declared absent in every way.
This is the protector of the worlds,
the veiled speech of the saviors,

for whose sake fearless Mañjughoṣa
has fully asked.
In it there is no sound;
all sounds have become equal."

When he had spoken thus, the venerable Ānanda said this to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, what is this? Is Mañjuśrī the youthful making his request for the sake of the Irreversible Wheel?"

He declared, "Ānanda, it is so. Ānanda, the buddhas, the blessed ones, are those who turn the Irreversible Wheel."

Ānanda asked, "Blessed One, with what intention did the Tathāgata teach the follower by faith, and teach the follower of the Dharma? Likewise, with what intention did the Blessed One, the Tathāgata, the Arhat, the perfectly complete Buddha, teach the eighth-ranked one, the stream-enterer, the once-returner, the non-returner, the śrāvaka arhat, and the pratyekabuddha? When the Blessed One, the Tathāgata, with some intention, fully taught the follower by faith, and likewise, with some intention, fully taught the follower of the Dharma, the eighth-ranked one, the stream-enterer, the once-returner, the non-returner, the śrāvaka arhat, and the pratyekabuddha, did the Blessed One, the Tathāgata, thereby fully teach that these too are the very Dharma of the bodhisattva? What is the case? Please, Blessed One, explain it."

He declared, "Ānanda, it is so. The Tathāgata fully taught in this way that these very things are the Dharma of the bodhisattva. Why is that? Ānanda, the beings born in the time of the five degenerations are inclined toward the lesser. Since they are not inclined toward the wisdom of the buddhas, Ānanda, the buddhas, the blessed ones, who are skilled in means, teach the Dharma to them in that way through skill in means. Ānanda, the beings who are inclined toward the vast are few. Ānanda, it is for the sake of those beings who are inclined toward the lesser that the Tathāgata teaches the Dharma through such skill in means. By this means he teaches the Dharma of the Buddha."

Then the Blessed One, having spoken thus, fell silent.

Then the venerable Ānanda said this to Mañjuśrī the youthful: "Mañjuśrī, why does the Tathāgata let this pass in silence?"

Mañjuśrī said, "Venerable Ānanda, the beings who are inclined toward a teaching of the Dharma such as this are few. These arhats who have the thought, 'I am an arhat,' are very many. Venerable Ānanda, the beings who fully understand what the Tathāgata said with an intention, that is, with what intention the Tathāgata, the Arhat, the perfectly complete Buddha taught the Dharma, are few. To me this is directly evident. Those four assemblies have fallen into doubt, wondering how it is that the Tathāgata now establishes the follower by faith, and likewise, at length, up to how he establishes the pratyekabuddha. Because these many tens of millions of bodhisattvas have been pondering with what intention the Tathāgata fully taught the follower by faith, and so on up to with what intention the Tathāgata fully taught the pratyekabuddha, all the waters have been held back and do not flow, and the birds too do not fly in the sky.

Why is that? This teaching of the Dharma is hard to be inclined toward, and so the Tathāgata lets it pass in silence."

Then those ten thousand lotuses moved all around the residence of the Tathāgata, and from them came forth a sound such as this: "Since we have heard this teaching of the Dharma, in this very place, from ninety-nine koṭīniyutas of buddhas, make this request of the Tathāgata: 'Please, may the Blessed One, the Tathāgata, the Arhat, the perfectly complete Buddha, teach the Dharma concerning the teaching of the Dharma of the Irreversible Wheel.'"

Then the venerable Śāriputra made this request to the Blessed One: "Last night, during the last watch of the night, together with Mañjuśrī the youthful, I heard this very teaching of the Dharma from countless buddhas, blessed ones, in the east, and likewise I heard this very teaching of the Dharma from countless buddhas, blessed ones, in the south, the west, the north, below, above, and in the intermediate directions. Therefore, Blessed One, please explain the teaching of the Dharma of the Irreversible Wheel."

Then eighty-five koṭīniyutas of gods dwelling in the sky made this request to the Tathāgata: "Since we have heard this teaching of the Dharma, in this very place, from ninety-two koṭīniyutas of buddhas, may the Blessed One, the Tathāgata, the Arhat, the perfectly complete Buddha, please explain the teaching of the Dharma of the Irreversible Wheel."

Then the venerable Ānanda said this to the Blessed One: "May the Blessed One, the Tathāgata, the Arhat, the perfectly complete Buddha, please teach with what intention the Tathāgata fully taught the follower by faith, fully taught the follower of the Dharma, and likewise, at length, fully taught the eighth-ranked one, the stream-enterer, the once-returner, the non-returner, the śrāvaka arhat, and the pratyekabuddha. Blessed One, these four assemblies remain silent, not even making the sound of clearing their throats, and, Blessed One, many hundreds of thousands of living beings have fallen into doubt and uncertainty. Please dispel the doubt of those who wonder with what intention the Tathāgata, the Arhat, the perfectly complete Buddha fully taught the follower by faith, and likewise, at length, up to the pratyekabuddha. These many have become witnesses."

Then the Blessed One spoke these words to the venerable Ānanda: "Ānanda, it is so. The Tathāgata, the Arhat, the perfectly complete Buddha teaches the Dharma with witnesses."

Ānanda asked, "Blessed One, what is the witness of the tathāgatas, the arhats, the perfectly complete buddhas?"

The Blessed One declared, "Ānanda, the witness of the tathāgatas, the arhats, the perfectly complete buddhas is the Dharma. The tathāgatas, the arhats, the perfectly complete buddhas possess the power of the Dharma, and they teach those dharmas just as they have directly realized them. Therefore, Ānanda, listen well and closely, and keep it properly in mind, and I shall explain to you how the Tathāgata, the Arhat, the perfectly complete Buddha fully taught the bodhisattva great being as a follower by faith, and likewise as a follower of the Dharma, as the eighth-ranked one, as a stream-enterer, as a once-returner, as a non-returner, as a śrāvaka arhat, and as a pratyekabuddha."

"Excellent, Blessed One," replied the venerable Ānanda, and as he listened to the Blessed One, the Blessed One spoke these words: "Ānanda, here a bodhisattva great being has faith in order to lead countless, immeasurable beings to see the Buddha without attachment, and yet does not regard even those qualities of the Buddha as real. He is not attached to form, and likewise he is not attached to feeling, perception, formations, or consciousness. This, Ānanda, is called a bodhisattva great being who is a follower by faith.

"Moreover, Ānanda, if a bodhisattva great being has conviction that all these dharmas were declared empty by the Tathāgata, the Arhat, the perfectly complete Buddha, then, Ānanda, a bodhisattva great being so inclined is called a follower by faith.

"Moreover, Ānanda, if a bodhisattva great being has conviction in the wisdom of the Buddha and gives rise to longing for it, thinking, 'When shall I come to possess wisdom such as this?', yet does not regard even that wisdom as real, then, Ānanda, that bodhisattva great being obtains the name 'follower by faith.'

"Moreover, Ānanda, if a bodhisattva great being has no faith in the five qualities of sensual pleasure, and attains the generation of the power of faith, then, Ānanda, that bodhisattva great being should be known as a follower by faith.

"Again, if he thinks, 'Oh, that I might someday become one who gives the inconceivable gift of the Dharma, just as the Tathāgata has given it,' and has conviction in that inconceivable gift of the Dharma, then, Ānanda, that bodhisattva great being too is called a follower by faith.

"Moreover, Ānanda, a bodhisattva great being, with an undaunted mind, gives away completely all that is to be given. Though he gives away even his body, he does not think that he is purified by giving, and he is without avarice toward anything at all. He dedicates all of it to awakening, and yet does not regard even that awakening as real. Such not regarding as real is called a follower by faith.

"Moreover, Ānanda, the bodhisattva great being is perfect in faith in the Buddha. His mind is untainted. He has conviction in the untainted Dharma. He does not reject the six sense sources, nor does he pursue them. He is inclined through faith. Those beings who lack faith he introduces to faith, and he causes them to have faith in the Dharma of the Buddha. Having come to possess faith, he gives rise to the mind set on awakening, and yet he does not find that mind. Having faith, he knows sameness, and he knows the dharmadhātu, the expanse of phenomena, as sameness. How does he know it? It is spoken of conventionally as 'the dharmadhātu,' yet he does not find that expanse either. Such a one is called a follower by faith.

Ānanda, if you ask how the Tathāgata, the arhat, the perfectly complete Buddha, teaches the bodhisattva, the great being, as a non-returner: Ānanda, here the bodhisattva, the great being, has wholly passed beyond partial conduct and has attained the conduct of the Buddha. He has given up all conduct and completely abandoned it. He does not abide in any dharma; he is without abiding and without support. Therefore he does not return. Through the nature of reality he does not truly see any coming or going. He has completely passed beyond ordinary beings, has completely abandoned the perception of ordinary beings, and has demolished even the Buddha; and he possesses the dharma that is without support. Why is that? Because he has attained the element of peace, and in it he observes neither ordinary beings nor the Buddha, the guide of the world.

He has passed beyond the lower realms, and desire is wholly cut off. In order to destroy completely all that has become view, he attains the awakening that is free of nourishment. Because he has no clinging to the sixty-two views, he is free of entity and nonentity. He is like nirvāṇa. In order to reverse the perception of nirvāṇa and not to engage in any perception, he has purified all dharmas, the lower realms, and the stains. He has vanquished Māra, the Evil One. He is free of intoxication. He has pulled out the thorn of ignorance. He has dried up existence. He has cleared away the desire of delight. He has cleared away the afflictions. He has expelled all perceptions. He has pulled out the thorn of sorrow. He is free of pride, and even of pride in that. He has attained the light of fully understanding the aggregates. Therefore he attains, in the wisdom of the Buddha, the inconceivable sameness of wisdom.

Therefore that bodhisattva, the great being, unattached to the swamp of the desire realm, attains the coming forth of the buddhas of the past. He attains the treasure of the buddhas of the past, the treasure laid down by the buddhas of the past, from which nothing is taken away and to which nothing is added. He attains the vehicle of the Buddha, which has room for all beings, chief and unsurpassed. In an unobstructed way, in the way in which all signs are signless, he attains the element of peace in which all doubts are cut off. Therefore, again, he does not come here.

Moreover, Ānanda, thinking, "I shall establish in awakening those beings whom I have taken up," he established them. How did he establish them in awakening? He made them understand awakening without beings, and raised them out of the perception of beings. Why is that? He knows the element of beings to be empty. What is empty is inconceivable, and is free of the perception of beings. Why is that? Because he knows the element of beings to be empty like the element of space, free of beings, and yet not free of beings. That element of space too does not exist as the element of space; it does not abide. That element of space is empty of space itself, and all signs of beings are vanquished.

He attains signless awakening, and is free of the perception of beings and of space. He realizes the dharma element to be like the element of space. Why is that? It is like this: all dharmas and all beings are by their intrinsic nature the dharma element. Why is that? Because he does not observe them, and since he does not observe them, he does not come here again. Therefore he is counted among those called "non-returners." He is not counted among all dharmas, yet he has entered the count of the buddhas, and so he is called "a non-returner."

The third fascicle.

Ānanda, if you ask how the Tathāgata, the arhat, the perfectly complete Buddha, teaches the bodhisattva, the great being, as an arhat: Ānanda, here the bodhisattva, the great being, has destroyed every practice of limited conduct, and is worthy to accomplish the conduct that liberates all beings. He has destroyed all afflictions, and he is worthy to free from those afflictions the beings who suffer, yet he observes neither those beings nor those afflictions. In this way he is called "an arhat."

He has destroyed every apprehension of form. He abides in the nonobservation of the emptiness of all dharmas and of the peace of all dharmas, and therefore he knows all dharmas to be empty. He realizes them as signless. He is free of all signs. He has destroyed all perception of the lower realms. He realizes the dharma in which there are no beings. He fully knows all dharmas to be empty. He is worthy to attain awakening and worthy to accomplish the inconceivable awakening of the Buddha; therefore he is called "an arhat."

He is worthy to teach the Dharma. He is worthy to teach the Dharma free of elaboration and free of turbidity, as the perfectly complete buddhas of the past taught it, as those of the present teach it, and as those yet to come will teach it; therefore he is called "an arhat."

He is worthy to establish beings in awakening, so that they may understand all dharmas, be free of elaboration, and be without grasping. He is worthy to accomplish the loving-kindness of the Buddha, the loving-kindness of a love that has no clinging to beings. He is worthy to establish beings in that loving-kindness too. In the loving-kindness that does not observe beings, he does not observe those beings, and he is worthy to establish them as well; therefore he is called "an arhat."

In order to uphold all dharmas, he is worthy to teach the Dharma to beings, and he has no perception of the Dharma as Dharma. Because he is worthy in this way, he is called "an arhat."

He is worthy to teach living creatures the faculties, the powers, and the branches of awakening; he has attained nonattachment even to these, and he has no clinging to any thing whatsoever. Therefore he is called "an arhat."

He is worthy of all the honors that are to be found in the world, however many they may be, yet he is not stained by them and not attached to them. And so that others too may be unstained by honor and unattached to it, he is worthy to teach the Dharma to sentient beings. Therefore he is called "an arhat."

In the manner of not going, he is worthy to go to the buddha fields, and there too he looks upon the buddhas, the blessed ones, in the manner of not seeing. Just as a buddha beholds the buddhas, so is he worthy to look upon them. Therefore he is called "an arhat."

He is worthy to accomplish fields such as these, in the manner in which no dharma is accomplished: an inconceivable field, a field without obscurations, a field where the māras are vanquished, a field equal to the unequaled, a field free of elaboration, a field without turbidity, an empty field, an irreversible field, a field without women, a field without afflictions, a field of the Buddha's eloquence, a field without opponents, and a field of utter, complete nirvāṇa. Because he is worthy to accomplish such fields, he is called "an arhat."

He is not attached even to the things that give rise to attachment, and he is not disturbed even by the things that give rise to disturbance. Therefore he is called "an arhat."

He knows the vehicle that is equal to cessation, he realizes the vehicle of the buddhas, and he is worthy to set forth in detail the awakening of the buddhas, the blessed ones, which he has discerned. Because beings are unmoving, that awakening is inconceivable; and in order to make them unmoving, he is worthy to establish many tens of millions of beings in awakening. For those who abide in sameness, neither all beings nor awakening are conceptualized. He is worthy to lead beings directly into sameness. Just as in sameness there is no sameness, so too all dharmas are equal to that. As sameness is, so too is awakening: it abides equally in nonconceptuality, and the arhat too is equal to it. He is worthy in this way as well: having understood it thus, he is worthy to teach the Dharma to beings in just that way. He mixes no dharma with any other; possessing the Dharma that is taught without mixing, he is worthy to teach the Dharma in that way, and yet not to express it even in words.

He frees beings from perceiving them as beings to be observed. He frees beings from permanence and annihilation. He moves beings away from the view of the perishing collection, and turns them utterly away from the pride that thinks "I am." He frees them from the nonarising, the noncessation, and the nonformation of all dharmas. He frees them from the nondisintegration of form, and from the nondisintegration of feeling, perception, formations, and consciousness. He frees them from the immovability of the qualities of ordinary beings, and from not setting down and from setting forth the qualities of a buddha. He frees them from perceiving the fruit of stream entry, and from perceiving the fruits of once-returner, non-returner, and arhat. He frees them from perceiving a pratyekabuddha, and from clinging to the perception of a bodhisattva. He frees them from clinging to generosity undertaken for the sake of awakening, and from clinging to moral discipline, patience, diligence, meditative concentration, and wisdom undertaken for the sake of awakening. He frees them from clinging to the superknowledges, and from clinging to the accomplishment of calm abiding and insight. He frees them from clinging to birth as a śrāvaka. He frees them from clinging to brothers, sisters, relatives, and ties of blood. He frees them from attachment to not seeing the self, and from clinging to fondness, to the perishing collection, and to busy company. He frees them from clinging to gain and honor. He frees them from clinging to seeing people as householders and as renunciants. He frees them from clinging to seeing dharmas as inferior and supreme. He frees them from clinging to rejecting the qualities of ordinary beings and fixing special attention on the qualities of a buddha. He frees them from clinging to seeing inferior and excellent dharmas. He frees them from clinging to the accomplishment of the perfectly complete marks, and from clinging to accomplishing it all in the manner of purifying a buddha field.

He frees them from clinging to the arising of a mind that divides things in two, thinking: "These beings are of a nature to pass into complete nirvāṇa; these beings are not of a nature to pass into complete nirvāṇa. These have the good fortune to attain it; these lack the good fortune to attain it. These practice for the sake of awakening; these do not practice for the sake of awakening. These are of bad character; these possess moral discipline. These have faulty wisdom; these possess wisdom." He frees them from clinging to a mind that divides things in two in this way, thinking: "These are worthy recipients of giving; these are not worthy recipients of giving. These possess diligence; these lack diligence. These are the qualities of fools; these are the qualities of the wise. These are the qualities of men; these are the qualities of women. These are the qualities of the noble ones; these are not the qualities of the noble ones." He frees them from the conceptual thought of a mind arising in this way, thinking: "These are irreversible from awakening; these turn back from awakening. These have received a prophecy; these have not received a prophecy. These are near; these are not near." And he frees them from clinging to passing into nirvāṇa without appropriation once awakening has been attained.

By freeing them in these ways, he is worthy to free many beings. By teaching that beings have no intrinsic nature, he is worthy to free beings in this way, and so he is called "an arhat."

Ānanda, if you ask how the Tathāgata, the Arhat, the Perfectly Complete Buddha has fully taught that bodhisattva great beings are śrāvakas: Ānanda, here the bodhisattva great being proclaims the qualities of a buddha to immeasurable beings, proclaiming, "Awakening is peace, free of elaboration, free of turbidity." Because he proclaims in this way, he is called a śrāvaka.

He proclaims the bliss of nirvāṇa, saying, "There is no other bliss like this"; he proclaims, "Accomplish the faculties, the powers, the branches of awakening, and the applications of mindfulness!"; and he proclaims, "This vehicle is supreme." Because he proclaims in this way, the bodhisattva himself is called a śrāvaka.

He proclaims, "This body is empty; no essence is found in it; it is what deludes fools. Therefore take the body apart!" And he proclaims, "See this eye sense source as seeing falsely! Accomplish the wholly inconceivable eye of a buddha! Accomplish, in the manner of nonaccomplishment, that eye by which one is never deluded anywhere!" Proclaim it thus! Because he proclaims all dharmas in this way, he is called a śrāvaka.

He proclaims that all sounds are like echoes: "Do not cling to sounds! Here there is nothing heard. Here there is nothing spoken." Therefore he is called a śrāvaka.

"Do not perceive odors as odors! Here there is nothing smelled. It is like this: a man asleep may smell many kinds of odors in a dream, yet because there is no real thing, there is no odor at all there; rather, he smells through error. In the same way, the nose is erroneous; because it is like a dream, know that there is no essence in it!" So he proclaims; therefore he is called a śrāvaka.

He proclaims the tongue sense source thus: "This is a lump of flesh; it does not taste flavors. The lump of flesh, too, is like a mass of foam, like a dream. The example, too, is without substance." So he proclaims; therefore he is called a śrāvaka.

"Take apart the body sense source! This body is empty of intrinsic nature. What is empty of intrinsic nature is a nonthing. What is a nonthing is unborn and does not arise. What is unborn and does not arise is itself awakening." So he proclaims; and because he proclaims this to many beings, he is called a śrāvaka.

He proclaims, "The mind is without intrinsic nature, a nonthing, of the nature of an illusion, without arising, without cessation." Because he proclaims this, he is called a śrāvaka.

"Give the gift of the Dharma! This gift of the Dharma is the path to awakening. That awakening is inconceivable; through the inconceivable one accomplishes the inconceivable. Why is that? As the seed is, so is the fruit; and it is spoken of in words as being without fruit." So he proclaims.

"Give material gifts! This material gift is small; compared with it, the gift of the Dharma is foremost.

Do not be stingy in this! Do not perceive giving as giving! Do not perceive it as an essence! Give gifts with a nonconceptual mind! Just as an illusory man gives a gift to an illusory man with a nonconceptual mind, in the same way give gifts with a nonconceptual mind! If you give in this way, awakening will come to full ripeness." So he proclaims, and because he proclaims in this way, he is called a śrāvaka.

Why is that? Because he has fully accomplished nondual wisdom, the very essence of wisdom; and just as he has accomplished it, so he proclaims the qualities of a buddha as nondual and undivided. Therefore he is called a śrāvaka.

Since he proclaims direct perception in this way,
Therefore he is called a śrāvaka.

"This body, by its essential nature,
Is empty": thus he takes it apart.
What is empty by essential nature
Is unborn and does not arise;
What is unborn and does not arise,
That, here, is awakening itself.
Thus he proclaims to beings;
Therefore he is called a śrāvaka.

"This mind, by its essential nature,
Is without intrinsic nature, a nonthing;
It is not a being, not a thing":
This is what he proclaims.

"The gift of the Dharma is inconceivable;
This is the path to awakening;
Through it awakening will be accomplished;
So give gifts!" he proclaims.

"Give material gifts of various kinds,
Many kinds, and pure;
If there is no stinginess in them,
That too is the path to awakening."

Because he has cut off the afflictions,
Is wholly free of the defilements,
And has utterly passed beyond all attachment,
Therefore he is called a śrāvaka.

The wise one proclaims
The unsurpassed qualities of a buddha;
All sounds are sublime and at peace;
Through this the sublime essence is accomplished.
He directly knows every essence,
Yet abides in no essence at all;
Nondual, undivided into two,
He proclaims the sublime proclamation.

Just as the teachings of the Dharma
Of many buddhas are proclaimed,
So the gentle one, having set out for awakening,
Here sets them forth in this way.

Where the buddhas, protectors of the world,
Dwell, unsurpassed,
In those buddha fields, the most excellent,
And in those closed off as well, he proclaims.

He proclaims to this trichiliocosm,
Which rests in space:
"Beings are just like space,
Forever equal to nirvāṇa.
The four elements that are spoken of,
From which beings are imagined,
Are like space,
Beyond concept and beyond thought.
The elements are like this;
See that there is no essence in them!
Here there is no birth and no death,
No nirvāṇa and no affliction;
Here nothing is established as a thing;
Here there are no beings.
Contemplate that element
Which no one ever sees!"
Day and night, without slackening,
He proclaims this to beings.

"I am proclaiming something":
No such conceit is in him at all.
Whoever proclaims to others in this way,
Know him to be a śrāvaka.
It is not by saying so that he is a śrāvaka;
He has been fully taught as a śrāvaka.

The steadfast one, whose mind is never lowly,
Teaches living creatures the Dharma
Through which all dharmas
Come to be known just as they are.

Freed from attachment and undefiled,
To free them from all attachment
He teaches the Dharma to living creatures;
He is called an arhat.

He also teaches the sphere
Free of elaboration, without turbidity, without attachment;
Having seen all the qualities of a buddha
Without exception, he teaches them.

What the buddhas have spoken
He does not conceive as far from the Dharma,
Nor does he conceive it as near to the Dharma;
Therefore in him there is no mixing.
Though he proclaims, there is no mixing,
And he speaks to countless beings,
Yet he does not see those beings;
Therefore he is called a śrāvaka.

Ānanda, with this in mind
I have fully taught the śrāvaka.
The bodhisattva who does not abide:
Know the śrāvaka to be such!

Ānanda, if you ask how the Tathāgata, the Arhat, the Perfectly Complete Buddha has fully taught that bodhisattva great beings are pratyekabuddhas: Ānanda, here the bodhisattva great being has direct perception of all dharmas.

If one asks how he has direct perception: because he superimposes nothing on any dharma, he is without affliction; and in the manner of denying no dharma, he realizes and comprehends that they do not disintegrate. Therefore he is called a pratyekabuddha.

He realizes that all dharmas are beyond thought, and he knows that all beings, since they will not be born in the future, are like nirvāṇa; that they are unborn and unceasing; that the limit of reality is the limit of nirvāṇa; that the limit of beings is the limit of a mirage; that they are without substance and without entity; that the limit of all dharmas is inexpressible; that the limit of the inexpressible is the limit of the designation of names; that it does not abide in words; and that it is not expressed by words.

Why is that? Because words are hollow and empty; words do not know the limit of the self or the limit of beings. He has realized that limit, and since that limit has become directly perceived for him, he is called a pratyekabuddha.

That this form aggregate is expressed by sound; that this form aggregate is soundless and free of sound; that it is expressed in words as "this is always the form aggregate"; and that in it there is neither self nor anything belonging to a self: in this way the form aggregate has become directly perceived for him. Why is that? The words by which the form aggregate is expressed are hollow and empty, unborn and unceasing. In reality, words themselves are always nonexistent, so how could there be words that express the form aggregate? In this way the form aggregate has become directly perceived.

That this feeling aggregate is expressed by sound; that this feeling aggregate is soundless and free of sound; that it is expressed in words as "this is always the feeling aggregate"; and that in it there is neither self nor anything belonging to a self: in this way the feeling aggregate has become directly perceived. Why is that? The words by which the feeling aggregate is expressed are hollow and empty, unborn and unceasing. In reality, words themselves are always nonexistent, so how could there be words that express the feeling aggregate? In this way the feeling aggregate has become directly perceived.

That this perception aggregate is expressed by sound; that this perception aggregate is soundless and free of sound; that it is expressed in words as "this is always the perception aggregate"; and that in it there is neither self nor anything belonging to a self: in this way the perception aggregate has become directly perceived for him. Why is that? The words by which the perception aggregate is expressed are hollow and empty, unborn and unceasing. In reality, words themselves are always nonexistent. How could there be words that express the perception aggregate? In this way the perception aggregate has become directly perceived.

That this formations aggregate is expressed by sound; that this formations aggregate is soundless and free of sound; that it is expressed in words as "this is always the formations aggregate"; and that in it there is neither self nor anything belonging to a self: in this way the formations aggregate has become directly perceived for him. Why is that? The words by which the formations aggregate is expressed are hollow and empty, unborn and unceasing. In reality, words are always nonexistent, so how could there be words that express the formations aggregate? In this way the formations aggregate has become directly perceived.

That this consciousness aggregate is expressed by sound; that this consciousness aggregate is soundless and free of sound; that it is expressed in words as "this is always the consciousness aggregate"; and that in it there is neither self nor anything belonging to a self: in this way the consciousness aggregate has become directly perceived for him. Why is that? The words by which it is expressed as the consciousness aggregate are hollow and empty, unborn and unceasing. In reality, words are always nonexistent, so how could there be words that express the consciousness aggregate? In this way the consciousness aggregate is directly known.

Thus the five aggregates are soundless, and whatever words express them, they have become directly perceived for him. Therefore he is called a pratyekabuddha. Why is that? Because he has comprehended that words are soundless, that the conditionality of the aggregates is not conditionality, and that the conditionality of words is a conditionality that cannot be expressed. Therefore he is called a pratyekabuddha.

Then, through the power of the Buddha, the venerable Ānanda knew with his mind the thoughts in the minds of those many hundreds of thousands of living beings, and he said to Mañjuśrī the youthful, "Mañjuśrī, having heard what those elders said, these many hundreds of thousands of living beings have fallen into doubt about what these elders had in mind when they spoke, and the Blessed One, too, remains silent. He said, "Mañjuśrī, are these elders, too, irreversible from awakening?"

He said, "Ānanda, it is so."

Ānanda said, "Mañjuśrī, since the Blessed One remains silent about what these elders had in mind when they spoke these words, please speak about it."

Mañjuśrī said, "Venerable Ānanda, 'mother' refers to the ignorance that gives birth to saṃsāra, and they have utterly destroyed that ignorance. Venerable Ānanda, 'father' refers to improper attention accompanied by the passion of delight. They are free of that improper attention, and the passion of delight, too, they have utterly destroyed. 'Arhat' refers to the qualities of ordinary beings, and they have demolished and destroyed them by way of not demolishing. Whatever perception of the qualities of ordinary beings they had, that too they have split apart by way of not dividing any phenomena. Whatever perception they had that had become a perception of the Tathāgata, that perception too has left them by way of nonarising. Venerable Ānanda, it was with this in mind that they said, 'Today we are fully endowed with the five deeds of immediate retribution.' Why? There is nothing removed and nothing taken up; therefore they are said to be endowed with the five deeds of immediate retribution.

"If you ask what they had in mind when they said, 'Today we are fully endowed with the five qualities of sense desire,' venerable Ānanda, they know these five qualities of sense desire to be like a dream, like an illusion, and like a mirage. In them that knowledge is complete, and they have made the five qualities of sense desire neither incomplete nor complete. Why? Because these are utterly nonexistent, and in that way they fully know them. In that way they are endowed with them. In that way their knowledge is complete, and therefore they are said to be fully endowed with the five qualities of sense desire. Ānanda, it was with this in mind that they said, 'Today we are fully endowed with the five qualities of sense desire.'

"Venerable Ānanda, as for their saying, 'Today we are fully endowed with wrong views; we are devoid of right view,' venerable Ānanda, they see all phenomena as wrong. Venerable Ānanda, 'wrong' means false: all phenomena are false. They are joined to nothing at all; like space, they are without dejection, without attachment, and without support. Why? All phenomena have as their essential nature the absence of entity, and they know them in that way. In reality all phenomena are equal, and that equality is right view. They are free of that perception of equality. Why? Awakening is free of all perceptions, and without apprehending even the subtlest particle of any phenomenon, they have attained and realized those qualities of a buddha. Venerable Ānanda, it was with this in mind that they said, 'Today we are fully endowed with wrong views; we are devoid of right view.'

"Venerable Ānanda, as for what they had in mind when they spoke the words, 'Today we have taken the lives of many hundreds of thousands of living beings,' venerable Ānanda, having heard this hidden speech of theirs, many hundreds of thousands of living beings and many hundreds of thousands of gods have known and realized all phenomena to be like an illusion, like a dream, and like an echo. Thereby, free of the perception of a being and the perception of a life, they have produced roots of virtue for awakening, in the manner in which no phenomenon is produced. Having heard this, other monks, nuns, laymen, and laywomen, different from them, also became free of the perception of a being and the perception of a life, and therefore they too will not die. Why? If there is a perception of a being and a perception of a life, there will be death; but they become free of that perception through the nature of reality that is utterly unborn. Venerable Ānanda, it was with this in mind that they spoke the words, 'Today we have taken the lives of many hundreds of thousands of living beings.'

"Venerable Ānanda, as for what they had in mind when they spoke the words, 'Today we have attained awakening; today we have passed completely into nirvāṇa in the element of nirvāṇa without remainder of the aggregates,' venerable Ānanda, many hundreds of living beings and many hundreds of thousands of living beings within this assembly have today understood the afflictions to be equal to the awakening of a buddha and have generated acceptance of the unborn nature of phenomena. Therefore, having today triumphed over the afflictions for the sake of awakening, they said, 'Today we have attained awakening.' There is no abandoning of afflictions, and there is no teaching of the qualities of a buddha. In them there is not even the slightest affliction. Venerable Ānanda, it was with this in mind that they spoke the words, 'Today we have attained awakening; today we have passed completely into nirvāṇa in the element of nirvāṇa without remainder of the aggregates.'

"'Today' means that this very day is called 'today.' Why? Venerable Ānanda, any son or daughter of good family who has generated the mind set on awakening has illuminated all phenomena. That being's generation of the mind set on an awakening not yet attained is a rising together with all phenomena. When he becomes free of the perception of awakening, then he sets together with all phenomena in the element of nirvāṇa without remainder of the aggregates.

"Venerable Ānanda, for those of the bodhisattva vehicle, it is day by the perception of the noble ones. For the childish, it is day by the perception of the sun. Why? If day had truly become something with an essence, then days would become a heap. If they had truly become a heap with an essence, then nights too would become a heap, and the past days and nights would be mixed together. Day would become night, and night would become day.

"Venerable Ānanda, for the childish it is day by the perception of day, and night by the perception of night. For one of the bodhisattva vehicle who is cared for by a spiritual friend, there is no perception of day in the day and no perception of night in the night. Why? The wisdom of a buddha is free of all perceptions, and therefore it aspires to nothing as directly present."

Then, at that time, Mañjuśrī the youthful spoke these verses:

"Ignorance, which gives birth to saṃsāra,
Is what is called 'mother';
They have pulled it out, root and all;
Therefore it is said they have killed their mother.

Improper attention,
The passion of delight, is called 'father';
Just as it truly is,
They know it as forever without entity.

Truly without entity,
It is uprooted and not engendered again;
Unapprehended and without abode,
Therefore it is said to be killed.

What are spoken of as the qualities of an arhat,
And the separate qualities of the childish:
Since they have demolished craving,
It is said they have killed an arhat.

Whatever perception of the Saṅgha arose,
They know its nature;
That all phenomena are indivisible
Is taught with certainty by the word 'splitting.'

Whatever perception of a perfect Buddha
They had formerly conceived,
As unborn and unceasing,
That they have pulled out, root and all.

This perception of unbroken continuity,
As empty, and empty of middle and end,
They have expressed in words;
They have understood it as equality.

The five qualities of sense desire that are spoken of,
By whatever names they are designated,
Since they have realized perception as an illusion,
They are therefore free of perception.

These sense desires the monks
Have left undiminished, just as before;
And so, in the presence of the Protector of the World,
They spoke those words.

The phenomena of desire, like a dream,
They know to be empty;
Their knowledge of them as utterly unborn
Is perfectly complete.

As hollow, empty, and without core,
They fully know phenomena to be wrong;
And 'wrong' is said to mean false;
Their perception in this is perfectly complete.

All phenomena are false;
Nowhere do they truly mingle;
Like five fingers in space,
Since they do not exist, they do not truly mingle.

Whatever is spoken of as right,
That view they know;
With whatever the equality of phenomena is equal,
Their knowledge of the view is equal to that.

'Because beings are perceived, there is dying':
So those of childish mind imagine.
If beings are not apprehended,
Death cannot come to be.

The deeply baneful perception of a life,
Through which so very many people
Do not cast off the suffering they have taken on,
That perception they have removed.

They are free of the perception of beings;
They have demolished the perception of a life;
Therefore it is said that they
Have cut off the lives of many beings.

They have triumphed over all afflictions;
They have realized the nature of reality free of affliction;
They know awakening to be free of superimposition,
Unchanging, and utterly stainless.

They have defeated all the māras without exception;
They have purified the qualities of a buddha;
Because all phenomena are without discord,
Through nonarising they have passed into nirvāṇa."

As soon as Mañjuśrī the youthful had spoken these verses, those very many hundreds of thousands of living beings became free of doubt and uncertainty and also gained light on all phenomena. Each and every one of them offered his delight to Mañjuśrī the youthful and said, "Just as Mañjuśrī the youthful has now taught, so shall we too speak the Dharma, and so shall we teach it to beings."

Then the Blessed One said to Mañjuśrī the youthful, "Mañjuśrī, your fine explanation of those things that were said in hidden speech is excellent, excellent!"

Then the Blessed One said to the venerable Ānanda, "Ānanda, those many hundreds of thousands of living beings who had fallen into doubt and uncertainty, and who have now been released from doubt and uncertainty, Ānanda, have been brought to maturity by Mañjuśrī the youthful, who caused them to take up awakening. They fully understand the Dharma he has taught."

Ānanda asked, "Blessed One, how is it? Are they irreversible from awakening?"

The Blessed One said, "Ānanda, it is so. They are irreversible from awakening. Why? Because they have been taken in hand by the spiritual friend Mañjuśrī the youthful."

Ānanda asked, "Blessed One, how is it? Are all those monks, too, who rose up from the perceptions of the faith-follower, the Dharma-follower, and likewise the eighth, the stream-enterer, the once-returner, the non-returner, the arhat, the śrāvaka, and the pratyekabuddha, irreversible from awakening?"

The Blessed One said, "Ānanda, this is hard to believe. Those who are inclined toward the inferior, the lazy, those who indulge in sensual things, those who delight in busyness, those whose mindfulness lapses, those without vigilance, those who are not in meditative equipoise, those whose minds are confused, those gripped by conceit, those who guard their bodies, those who guard their lives, those who hold views about body and life, the stingy, those of bad character, the malicious, those without faith in the qualities of a buddha, those of corrupt wisdom, those who have fallen into the hands of bad friends, those who are not cared for by a spiritual friend, those who have abandoned the Perfection of Wisdom, those who hold objectifying views, those intent on gain, those intent on honor, those attached to alms bowls and robes, those who do not strive at yoga without sleeping in the first and last watches of the night, those who speak falsehood, those who use divisive words, those who use harsh words, those who engage in idle chatter, the covetous, those full of malice, those with wrong views, those busied with any sort of entanglement, those who go about on errands as messengers, those who engage with signs, and those without faith or inclination toward all the phenomena that are emptiness, signlessness, wishlessness, nonformation, nonarising, nonceasing, and imperishability: Ānanda, for those possessed of such qualities, it is hard to have faith in this matter.

Then the Blessed One, having spoken thus, fell silent. Then, through the power of the Buddha, the venerable Ānanda said to Mañjuśrī the youthful, "Mañjuśrī, why does the Blessed One let the time pass in silence?"

Mañjuśrī said, "Venerable Ānanda, in the future, in the last five hundred years of that later time, most beings will be possessed of such qualities, and they will not have faith. That is why the Blessed One lets the time pass in silence."

He said, "Mañjuśrī, who are those who will have faith in this?"

Mañjuśrī said, "Venerable Ānanda, those who will have faith are few. Venerable Ānanda, it is like this: for example, the beings who are skilled in the meaning are very few, while those who are unskilled are exceedingly many. Why is that? Because they do not practice it, and therefore they are not skilled in it. Venerable Ānanda, in the same way, the beings who have faith in this teaching of the Dharma are very few. And those who do have faith will not always be held in honor; rather, they will be utterly cast out by villages, cities, towns, provinces, and the circles of royal palaces. Why is that? Because in the past they committed deeds, the obscuration of their own karma, that cause them to be deprived of the Dharma."

He said, "Mañjuśrī, please speak for the sake of those very ones whose faith will be slight."

Mañjuśrī said, "Venerable Ānanda, ask the Blessed One, and the Blessed One will answer you."

Then the venerable Ānanda said to the Blessed One, "Blessed One, please explain it for the sake of those sons and daughters of good family whose faith will be slight. When they have heard it, they will rejoice."

Then the Blessed One looked out in the four directions and covered the great trichiliocosm with his tongue. From that tongue he sent forth light, and that light pervaded world systems as numerous as the sands of the river Ganges, illuminating them with a great radiance. Then, through the power of the Buddha, that fourfold assembly saw, in world systems in the east as numerous as the sands of the river Ganges, the buddha blessed ones teaching this very teaching of the Dharma, and they heard that teaching of the Dharma as well. In the same way, in all ten directions, they heard this very teaching of the Dharma, with nothing added and nothing missing. Having heard it, they gathered together and made this request to the Blessed One: "Just as the Blessed One has taught, so we see these countless, innumerable buddha blessed ones also teaching this very teaching of the Dharma, with nothing added and nothing missing. Therefore, may the Blessed One please expound this very teaching of the Dharma and this very talk on the Dharma without interruption." Such was their request.

Then the Blessed One drew back his tongue and said to the venerable Ānanda, "Ānanda, does a tongue such as this speak falsehood?"

He replied, "No, Blessed One, it does not. Therefore, may the Blessed One please explain it for the sake of those sons or daughters of good family whose faith will be slight. Then even those who lack faith will be brought to faith by those who have been won over."

The Blessed One said, "Ānanda, this entire fourfold assembly now gathered here, and all the gods, nāgas, yakṣas, and gandharvas who have heard this teaching of the Dharma, are irreversible from unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening. When they too have fully awakened to unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening, they will teach this Dharma discourse in this very place, with nothing added and nothing missing, just as I teach it now."

Then that fourfold assembly and the gods, nāgas, yakṣas, and gandharvas were satisfied, delighted, and overjoyed; filled with great gladness, joy and happiness of mind arose in them. Some scattered garments over the Tathāgata, and some scattered flowers, garlands, and ointments. The women took off all the ornaments from their bodies and scattered them in the direction where the Blessed One was. Having scattered them, they all gathered together and spoke these words: "The tathāgatas, the arhats, the perfectly complete buddhas, speak without duplicity."

Then the Blessed One again said to the venerable Ānanda, "Ānanda, it is just as these women have said. The tathāgatas, the arhats, the perfectly complete buddhas, speak without duplicity; they are without attachment, without aversion, without delusion, and without stain. Therefore, hearing the buddha blessed ones is never fruitless."

He asked, "Blessed One, what is this that is called hearing the buddha blessed ones?"

The Blessed One said, "Ānanda, do you not know?"

He replied, "Blessed One, I do not know."

The Blessed One said, "Then listen, Ānanda, and I shall explain."

He said, "May the Blessed One please explain."

The Blessed One said, "Ānanda, all those who hear my name, Śākyamuni, will become irreversible from awakening. Why is that? Ānanda, since awakening is without attachment, without aversion, and without delusion, and so is never fruitless, what need is there even to mention those who offer a single flower to the Tathāgata, or those who scatter a single flower upon the relic stūpas of the Tathāgata who has passed into parinirvāṇa?"

He asked, "Blessed One, will beings born in the animal realm who hear it also become irreversible from awakening?"

The Blessed One said, "Ānanda, for any who hear the name Śākyamuni, that very hearing will become for them a seed of awakening. Ānanda, if hearing the tathāgatas, the arhats, the perfectly complete buddhas, were not fruitful, then the tathāgatas would not be ones who speak without duplicity."

"Ānanda, what do you think of this? How big is the seed of a nyagrodha tree in whose shade a hundred, or two hundred, or three hundred, or four hundred, or five hundred living creatures may sit?"

He replied, "Blessed One, its seed is small."

The Blessed One said, "Ānanda, does that nyagrodha tree, once it has sprung from so small a seed, gradually grow, thrive, spread out, and become broad?"

He replied, "Blessed One, that is so."

He said, "Ānanda, just as that nyagrodha tree, once it has sprung from a small seed, gradually grows, thrives, spreads out, and becomes broad, in the same way, Ānanda, the seed of hearing the sound 'Śākyamuni' will also gradually bring about unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening. It will not go to waste, it will not utterly go to waste, and it will not rot. Why is that? Ānanda, this seed of all phenomena is without any abiding, and therefore it will not utterly go to waste. Ānanda, this seed of all phenomena is imperishable, and therefore it will not go to waste. Ānanda, this seed of all phenomena is without clinging, and therefore it will not rot."

When he had said this, the venerable Ānanda said to the Blessed One, "Blessed One, what is this? Is it the Blessed One's former aspiration, or is it the very nature of things that hearing the buddha blessed ones is fruitful?"

The Blessed One said, "Ānanda, I did make this aspiration: 'May whoever hears my name become irreversible from unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening.' And moreover, this is also the nature of reality of the buddha blessed ones. Why is that? Ānanda, all the buddha blessed ones are equal and the same in all qualities."

He asked, "Blessed One, if the buddha blessed ones are equal and the same in the nature of reality, why then do they make aspirations?"

He said, "Bodhisattvas who have not heard this sublime Dharma make aspirations, and through that aspiration they will quickly come to hear this teaching of the Dharma."

He said, "Blessed One, those who possess such qualities as these act for the benefit of many beings. How wondrous it is, the extent to which the buddha blessed ones possess such vast qualities!"

The Blessed One said, "Ānanda, so it is. That is to say, for the sake of buddhahood I acted for the benefit of many beings. Ānanda, my buddhafield is not one possessed of the five degenerations. Ānanda, in the past I attended upon and served the buddha blessed ones, had no regard for body or life, gave away everything I owned, and was free of avarice; and, having undertaken great diligence, without abiding in any phenomenon, I benefited all beings; and because I declared no phenomenon at all, I truly accomplished awakening."

When he had said this, the venerable Ānanda said to the Blessed One, "Blessed One, it is wondrous that Māra, the Evil One, makes no obstacle to this teaching of the Dharma."

He said, "Ānanda, he has not heard it. Why is that? Because Mañjuśrī the youthful blessed it beforehand, and so he makes no obstacle."

Then Mañjuśrī the youthful withdrew that blessing. The very moment Mañjuśrī the youthful withdrew the blessing with regard to Māra, the Evil One, then, in no more time than a dream, Māra, the Evil One, heard the sound of the Irreversible Wheel, and he also heard the name of Śākyamuni. Then Māra, the Evil One, frightened, terrified, and panic-stricken, his hair standing on end, cried out, "All my victories are becoming no victories! All my domains are becoming no domains!" And he grieved and wailed. Through his grieving and wailing he took on an aged and decrepit form: just as a man who has reached a hundred years grows old and decrepit, so his form grew old.

Then Māra, the Evil One, with his fourfold army, together with all the gods of Māra's class there are in the great trichiliocosm, went to where the Blessed One was, in an array just like the one at the time when the Blessed One attained awakening. When Māra, the Evil One, came into the presence of the Blessed One, he appeared aged, decrepit, and worn out: just as a man who has reached a hundred years walks with his body bent forward, leaning on a staff, so it was that Māra, the Evil One, came before the Blessed One. When he had arrived, the gods stationed in the sky proclaimed to his fourfold army a sound such as this: that by hearing the sound of the Irreversible Wheel and the name of Śākyamuni, one attains irreversibility. And having heard it, all those fourfold armies were checked and fell into doubt, and they no longer followed Māra, the Evil One.

Then Māra, the Evil One, alone and without a companion, went before the Blessed One and said to the Blessed One, "Blessed One, I am alone and without a companion, aged, decrepit, and worn out, with no one even to lift me up by the hand. All my victories have become no victories, and all my domains have become no domains. Since the Tathāgata, the arhat, the perfectly complete Buddha, is loving toward all beings, may the Blessed One please show love to me. Since there is no one apart from the Blessed One himself who will grant me protection, please have compassion for me."

When he had made this plea, the Blessed One said to Māra, the Evil One, "Evil One, the realm of beings is vast. Evil One, even if buddha blessed ones as numerous as the sands of the river Ganges were to appear in the world every single day, still, Evil One, they could not exhaust the realm of beings."

When he had said this, Māra, the Evil One, said to the Blessed One:

"Blessed One, though the realm of beings is vast, I have become alone and without a companion, and there is no one at all to lift me up by the hand. Blessed One, since I go wandering along the road leaning on a stick, Blessed One, please give me relief. Blessed One, please give me relief in such a way that I may be joyful, that I may quickly become wholly joyful, and that I may have a large following."

When he had made this plea, the Blessed One said to Māra, the Evil One, "Evil One, go with an easy heart. Evil One, all those, however many they may be, who do not come to trust this Dharma discourse will become your supporters and companions."

Then Māra, the Evil One, was satisfied, delighted, and overjoyed; filled with great gladness, joy and happiness of mind arose in him, and he spoke these words: "I shall bless beings thoroughly in such a way that they have no faith in this Dharma discourse and fall into doubt. If they come to doubt, they will come under my power."

Then Māra, the Evil One, again said to the Blessed One, "Blessed One, please give me further relief, so that I may be more joyful still. Let the Blessed One not proclaim aloud what he has declared, namely, 'All those, however many, who hear my name, Śākyamuni, will become irreversible.' Blessed One, please remain silent. Please give me relief in such a way that beings will not undertake diligence to accomplish the vehicle of the buddhas, and will not abide in awakening."

When he had made this plea, the Blessed One said to Māra, the Evil One, "Evil One, go with an easy heart. I shall act in such a way that no being whatsoever is established in awakening. I shall not move any being whatsoever from the realm of beings: I shall act in such a way that no being whatsoever is moved from form, and likewise from feeling, perception, formations, and consciousness.

"Evil One, go with an easy heart. I shall act in such a way that no being whatsoever moves from the view of the perishable collection, that no being whatsoever moves from apprehending form, that no being whatsoever moves from the sixty-two views, that no being whatsoever moves from perceiving the past, or from perceiving the future, or from perceiving the present, and that no being whatsoever moves from killing, from taking what is not given, from sexual misconduct, from lying, from divisive speech, from harsh speech, from idle chatter, from covetousness, from malice, or from wrong view.

"Evil One, go with an easy heart. I shall not let any being whatsoever take up generosity. I shall not let them take up moral discipline, patience, diligence, meditative concentration, or wisdom.

"Evil One, go with an easy heart. I shall not move any being whatsoever from grasping at beings as beings, or from clinging to beings as beings. I shall not move them from perceiving a father, from perceiving a mother, from perceiving a brother, from perceiving a sister, from perceiving a wife, from perceiving a son, from perceiving a daughter, from perceiving day, from perceiving night, from perceiving a month, or from perceiving a half month.

"Evil One, go with an easy heart. I shall not move any being whatsoever from the perception of generosity, the perception of moral discipline, the perception of patience, the perception of diligence, the perception of meditative concentration, the perception of wisdom, the perception of the awakening mind, the perception of the powers and the fearlessnesses, the perception of the faculties, the strengths, and the factors of awakening, the perception of the path, the perception of the Buddha, the perception of the Dharma, the perception of the Saṅgha, the perception of the Saṅgha of the Buddha, or the perception of the Buddha as omniscient.

"Evil One, go with an easy heart. I shall act in such a way that no being whatsoever is moved from any phenomenon, and that no being whatsoever is let take up any phenomenon."

Then Māra, the Evil One, was delighted and overjoyed. Joy and happiness of mind arose in him, he was freed from the thorn of sorrow, and on that very spot he became youthful again. Then he scattered flowers over the Blessed One, circumambulated the Blessed One three times, and spoke this verse:

"The Tathāgata, supreme in the world,
The perfect Buddha, does not speak otherwise;
He does not speak words that turn out otherwise.
Today joy has arisen in me."

Then Māra, the Evil One, having spoken this verse, went away from the Blessed One's presence with an easy heart. Then, when Māra, the Evil One, had reached his own abode, he was endowed with the five qualities of divine sense pleasure and gained mastery over them, and he amused himself, delighted, and enjoyed himself fully; and he no longer gave rise to any thought of harm.

When this teaching on the sending back of Māra was being explained, the great earth shook in six ways.

The fifth fascicle.

Then the venerable Ānanda said to the Blessed One, "Blessed One, why did this great earth shake in six ways?"

The Blessed One said, "Ānanda, it is because, when this teaching on the sending back of Māra was being explained, six million four hundred thousand living beings attained acceptance of the nonarising of phenomena."

He asked, "Blessed One, are there perhaps some here who have fallen into doubt about it?"

The Blessed One said, "Ānanda, a hundred million living beings have fallen into doubt and uncertainty, thinking, 'What is this? What sort of thing is this? Have we not heard otherwise elsewhere?' And thinking, 'How could we ever be happy? How are we to abide? From where have we come?', they do not even know which direction is which, and they stare at one another."

Then the venerable Ānanda said to the Blessed One, "So that there may be no doubt about what was spoken and with what intention, may the Blessed One please quickly make it clear to these beings. The Blessed One said, 'Evil One, go with an easy heart. I shall act in such a way that no being whatsoever is established in awakening. I shall act in such a way that no being whatsoever is moved from the realm of beings,' and so on, applied in full as before, up to, 'Evil One, go with an easy heart. I shall act in such a way that no being whatsoever is moved from any phenomenon, and that no being whatsoever is let take up any phenomenon.' With what intention did the Blessed One say this? Blessed One, please quickly make it clear to these beings. Blessed One, so that light may be shed for the people of later times, for the sake of those who will take up this Dharma discourse, master it, recite it, and keep it, please explain with what intention the Blessed One spoke as he did."

Then, at that time, the Blessed One spoke these verses:

"Awakening has no abiding;
Awakening is nowhere cut off.
Therefore I said that I
Would not establish beings in awakening.

"All beings and awakening,
And likewise I myself, have no abiding.
With this in mind I said
That beings have no abiding in awakening.

"From the realm of beings, in any way at all,
No being is to be moved,
For any existing thing therein
Is never apprehended at all.

"This realm of beings, by its very nature,
Is empty and inconceivable.
Even those endowed with omniscience
Cannot move it from this.

"A being is nothing but the aggregates;
The aggregates and nirvāṇa are not two.
With this in mind
I said that no being whatsoever is moved.

"That which is grasped and so set moving:
In it no grasped thing is established.
By its essential nature it does not move:
Understand the aggregates in this way.

"The aggregates are unmoving and single,
Without a being, without intrinsic nature,
Forever wholly without support;
Know them to be without abiding.

"The aggregates are just like space.
As for this domain of the aggregates,
Where there is no domain, as their domain
The aggregates are taught to be like space.

"What is spoken of as the element of space
Is unborn; it is not born.
In truth the aggregates are equal to it;
They cannot be moved.

"The perishable collection, or any knowable phenomenon,
Is not truly apprehended at all.
'It is not space, and it is not moved':
That is what I said in words.

"The mind of beings, as something firmly abiding,
Is never at all apprehended.
Both the object and minds
Are by essential nature without movement.

"Whatever the sixty-two views may be,
Whatever phenomena seem distinct,
They are, in just this way,
Like reflections in water.
Know the sixty-two views always
To be like an optical illusion.
Because they are selfless and insubstantial,
By essential nature they do not move.

"Perception of the past and the future,
And likewise of what arises in the present:
This perception is insubstantial,
Like the water of a mirage.
This perception is selfless;
In it there is no being.
Where there is no being,
That cannot be moved.

"Beings who have taken life,
Who have entered the great wilderness:
To bring them wholly to nirvāṇa,
There is no power that can move them from this.
If they were apprehended anywhere at all,
Then they could be moved in that way;
Because there are no beings,
It is said they are not to be moved.

"Awakening is spoken of as 'not given':
Never is it given to anyone at all.
Therefore whoever strives in this
Cannot be moved.

"What embodied beings have not given
Is the gift, the inconceivable gift of Dharma.
Therefore whoever strives in this
Cannot be moved.

"Whoever, knowing that in what is called sexual misconduct
There is in truth nothing wrong at all,
Strives in this way,
Cannot be moved.

"The foremost one who undertakes diligence
So that people who persist in speaking false words
May be liberated,
Cannot be moved.

"Whatever is divisive or harsh in speech,
And whatever is idle chatter,
All of it is like an illusion,
Hollow, forever without substance.
Its ground or its abiding place
Never exists at all.
Know sound to be like an echo;
It, too, has no abiding.

"Whatever covetous thought has arisen in him,
Marked by attachment to self,
That self he has fully understood;
Therefore he cannot be moved.

"Whoever knows that malice by its nature
Has no defining characteristic, and that awakening too
Has just that as its characteristic,
Cannot be moved.

"Whoever, fully understanding wrong view,
Has discerned clearly through right view,
And has passed beyond all reckoning,
Cannot be moved.

"The wise do not have young women
Given away as gifts;
Giving away the young is deeply wrong;
This is no illumination.

"The moral discipline that, through wrong view,
Casts aside the noble path:
One seeking the noble path here
Does not lead anyone into that.

"The many kinds of patience that exist
Among the non-Buddhist outsiders (tīrthikas) beyond this teaching
Are held to be neither patience that follows the path
Nor a means of joining with nirvāṇa.
The endurance of striving in the five fires
And the like among heterodox tīrthikas
Does not bring the attainment of awakening;
Therefore the wise do not strive in it.

"Here, the outsiders who hold to signs,
And all their domains of concentration,
Are censured by the buddhas;
Therefore he does not lead anyone into them.

"Whatever wisdom is bound up with desire,
He does not lead anyone into it,
For that knowledge does not accomplish
The inconceivable wisdom of the buddhas.

"The fearless bodhisattva,
The one who gathers beings,
That intelligent one cannot be moved
From gathering beings.

"Because of clinging to the perception of beings,
The awakening mind is spoken of here.
That awakening is without clinging;
It cannot be moved.

"Father and mother and brothers,
Sisters, sons, and relatives:
All are said to be like illusions;
They cannot be moved.

"All those various things:
The perception of them all is insubstantial.
A phenomenon that has no substance
Cannot be moved.

"Whatever perception there is of day and night,
Whatever of a month or of a half month,
All those perceptions are without attachment,
Just like the water of a mirage.

"Whatever perception has arisen
Of generosity, moral discipline, patience, diligence,
Meditative concentration, wisdom, the awakening mind,
And of the powers and the fearlessnesses,
And whatever has arisen concerning the faculties,
The factors of awakening, and the paths,
Knowing that it has arisen from unknowing,
The wise do not set it in motion.

"Whatever perception of the Buddha, perception of the Dharma,
Or perception of the Saṅgha has arisen,
One who abides firmly, free of all diversity,
Is not to be moved from it.

"Those who have no perception of the Buddha or the Saṅgha,
Those who have no perception of the Omniscient One,
Make the awakening of the buddhas,
Inconceivable awakening, far distant.
So that awakening might not become distant,
Here, therefore, before the Evil One,
I spoke these words:
'They will not be moved from this.'

"All phenomena and all beings,
Every one of them, just as they are,
I set forth plainly; but the Evil One,
Through his evil understanding, did not realize it."

As soon as the Blessed One had explained this teaching on the sending back of Māra, those hundred million living beings were freed from doubt and gave up uncertainty; they attained illumination regarding all phenomena and attained acceptance of the nonarising of phenomena. The moment they attained that acceptance, they all gathered together and spoke these verses:

"The path of the Buddha is inconceivable;
We clearly understand the supreme path.
The Guide who cuts through uncertainty,
The Blessed One, is our Teacher.

"We have attained illumination everywhere;
We abide well on the path of the Buddha.
All the directions have become illumined,
And we see koṭis of buddhas.

"The nature of reality, which never
Abides in form, we too come to see.
Thus have our eyes
Been purified by the Guide, the Protector of the world."

Then those hundred million living beings, having spoken these verses, each scattered garments as an act of offering to the Blessed One and to this Dharma discourse, and spoke these words: "May this Dharma discourse resound in the ears of all beings!"

Then the venerable Ānanda said to the Blessed One, "Blessed One, those who, as soon as they hear this Dharma discourse that teaches the sending back of Māra, have faith and trust in it and give rise to no doubt: Blessed One, how much merit will they generate?"

The Blessed One said, "Ānanda, suppose a son or daughter of good family were to respect, honor, venerate, and make offerings to a hundred buddhas in the morning, a hundred buddhas at midday, a hundred buddhas in the evening, a hundred buddhas in the first watch of the night, a hundred buddhas at midnight, and a hundred buddhas in the last watch of the night, serving them with every comfort and with everything that lets them dwell in ease, and were to serve them in this way for twenty thousand years. Ānanda, what do you think of this? Would that son or daughter of good family generate much merit on that basis?"

He replied, "Much, Blessed One. Much, Sugata. Blessed One, it is not easy even to give an example of it."

The Blessed One said, "Ānanda, a son or daughter of good family who, as soon as they hear this teaching on the sending back of Māra, has faith and trust in it and gives rise to no doubt, will generate much merit."

Then three sons of good family of the bodhisattva vehicle came through the sky, through the open air, bearing many hundreds of thousands of koṭis of māndārava flowers, towering high like Mount Meru. The venerable Ānanda and the entire assembly saw those sons of good family of the bodhisattva vehicle coming from far away, and when they saw them, they were filled with wonder.

Then the venerable Ānanda said to the Blessed One, "Blessed One, where have these bodhisattvas come from?"

The Blessed One said, "Ānanda, eastward from this buddhafield, beyond as many world systems as there are sands in the river Ganges, there is a world system called Lotus Towering Like Meru. There they heard this Dharma discourse, and for that reason they have come here from that world system."

Then those three sons of good family of the bodhisattva vehicle took their places before the Blessed One and scattered and strewed those māndārava flowers over the Blessed One. Having scattered and strewed them, they spoke these words: "Blessed One, we have faith and trust in this Dharma discourse, and we have no doubt. Why is that? Because, just like the tathāgatas, the arhats, the perfectly complete buddhas, we too have no uncertainty whatsoever."

Then one of those sons of good family of the bodhisattva vehicle said to the Blessed One, "Blessed One, if those who speak truly were to speak of a 'Tathāgata,' that Tathāgata is I, for I have no uncertainty about this Dharma discourse and entertain no doubt."

The second son of good family of the bodhisattva vehicle said to the Blessed One, "Blessed One, if those who speak truly were to speak of a 'Blessed One,' that Blessed One is I, for I have no uncertainty about this Dharma discourse and entertain no doubt."

The third son of good family of the bodhisattva vehicle said to the Blessed One, "Blessed One, if those who speak truly were to speak of a 'Buddha,' Blessed One, that Buddha is I, for I have no uncertainty about this Dharma discourse and entertain no doubt."

Then many hundreds of thousands of living beings in that assembly thought, "Since it is impossible for two buddhas ever to arise in the world at the same time, why do these speak like this?" They became distracted and grew uneasy on their seats.

Then, through the power of the Buddha, the venerable Ānanda said to the Blessed One:

"Blessed One, how is this? Are those really the names of these three, that they proclaim things in this way, as though roaring a lion's roar?"

The Blessed One said, "Ānanda, the name of the first of these is Rightly Engaged by Uttering the Word 'Tathāgata.' The name of the second is Rightly Engaged by Uttering the Word 'Blessed One.' The name of the third is Rightly Engaged by Uttering the Word 'Buddha.' Ānanda, that is how it is. There is a reason why these speak in this way, and those, moreover, are their names."

Ānanda said, "Blessed One, may those who became distracted come to understand that these three spoke as they did after reflecting on this, and may their roots of virtue become even more brightly luminous. For example, Blessed One, if a little boy, a youth whose complexion is perfectly pure, were also bathed in red sandalwood water, his body would become even more fragrant, perfectly pure in complexion, and radiant. In the same way, Blessed One, so that the roots of virtue of those bodhisattvas who are skilled in veiled speech may become even more brightly luminous, may the Blessed One please explain to the many hundreds of thousands of living beings who became distracted what these three have said in veiled speech."

Then the Blessed One spoke these verses:

"Past, present, and future
Are like suchness itself.
Because he sees all phenomena in that way,
He is called 'Tathāgata.'

Whatever phenomena are present, past,
And yet to come,
They are not two, indivisible into two,
Forever utterly without characteristics.

Just as the buddhas of the past
Gave inconceivable gifts,
So these have given gifts in that same way;
Having reflected on that, he said this.

He has entered awakening without attachment;
Just as the tathāgatas of the past did,
So he has entered awakening.
Therefore he is called 'Tathāgata.'

Having entered into all phenomena,
He seeks peaceful awakening,
Yet he does not apprehend even that awakening.
Therefore he is called 'Tathāgata.'

He speaks just in the manner of those of the past;
So too of those yet to come;
And as those of the present attain, so does he.
Therefore he is called 'Tathāgata.'

Just as the steadfast ones, lords of compassion for living beings,
Showed patience in the past,
So he is steadfast in patience
And abides at the farthest shore of patience.

Just as I exerted myself in diligence
In the past, when I was seeking awakening,
So he has become diligent.
Therefore he is called 'Tathāgata.'

The sameness of all phenomena that I touched,
That sameness he has touched,
And in him there is no conceit whatsoever.
Therefore he is called 'Tathāgata.'

All phenomena are free of conceit;
By their essential nature they are always the same,
Without conceptualization, nothing at all.
He knows that sameness.

Like the tathāgatas of the past,
He too has realized likewise the meditative absorption
That is nonconceptual and peaceful.
Therefore he is called 'Tathāgata.'

The intrinsic nature of all phenomena,
Which I have fully proclaimed,
Is forever utterly without entity.
That intrinsic nature he knows.

Through wisdom he has gained the defining mark.
By fully knowing the emptiness of phenomena,
He fully knows all phenomena
And has reached the far shore of wisdom.

Just as the inconceivable wise ones of the past
Reached the far shore of wisdom,
Yet did not even reach its end,
And, not having arrived, did not pass into nirvāṇa,

Just as those who reached the end of suchness
Did not arrive at its end,
So he too has not found it.
Therefore he is called 'Tathāgata.'

He does not apprehend awakening
In the way that others imagine it.
Because he does not apprehend any phenomena,
He is called 'Tathāgata.'

Just as awakening is free of attachment,
So he has attained nonattachment.
Being unattached to all phenomena,
He realizes the awakening that is free of attachment.

The path that none of the childish have practiced,
Whose essential nature they do not know,
Just as the protectors, lords of the world,
Cultivated it in the past,
So he cultivates
That undefiled, supreme path.

That beginningless path
He fully knows to be empty.
The nature of reality of the noble path,
Which is sameness and without beginning,
With that he makes himself the same.
Therefore he is called 'Tathāgata.'

As the path is, so is awakening;
And just as awakening is,
So he fully knows the body to abide.
Thus he is called 'Tathāgata.'

The word 'Tathāgata' itself,
In brief, is a name for the Dharma.
Those who have no doubt about this,
Those wise ones have entered awakening.

Ānanda, having reflected on this,
He proclaimed it in words.
What he said is just so;
This is the domain of the wise.

The irreversible bodhisattva,
Who is called wise, is fearless.
Whatever has been fully proclaimed,
All of it is his domain.

This explanation, Ānanda,
Of why that fearless, intelligent one
Is called 'Tathāgata'
Is taught to bodhisattvas."

Ānanda asked:

"Blessed One, what is the explanation
By which you have said
That the intelligent one is fearless?
And what are those dharmas like?"

The Blessed One said:

"The inconceivable awakening of the buddhas,
Which they accomplished
Over hundreds of koṭis of eons
And over many koṭis of eons:
Just so, for the sake of beings,
He accomplishes awakening and abides well,
And in this he has no fear.
Therefore he is called 'Blessed One.'

He has no fear of saṃsāra;
He abides firmly even within saṃsāra
And awakens beings from it.
Therefore he is called 'Blessed One.'

How is it that he has no fear there?
How does he abide firmly in saṃsāra?
O unsurpassed protector of the world,
How does he release beings?

He does not make phenomena increase,
Nor does he destroy even the slightest thing.
Because there is no increase and no destruction,
He releases beings from suffering.

Thus he has no fear of saṃsāra,
Thus he abides firmly in existence,
Thus he liberates beings.
Therefore he is called 'Blessed One.'

Whatever the dharmas of a buddha are,
He fears none of them, without exception.
Those dharmas, infinite and boundless,
He proclaims to beings.

"Knowing all the dharmas of a buddha, without exception,
To be dharmas empty of essential nature,
He accomplishes those dharmas,
Yet sees nothing in them.

Holding in reverence, in this way,
That phenomena are empty of essential nature, he meditates.
Knowing them to be empty in every respect,
He accomplishes them without fear.

Fully knowing all phenomena
To be false, he is not daunted.
Having become fearless,
He teaches the true characteristic of phenomena.

He has utterly passed beyond all fears
And has completely abandoned all the lower realms.
Without fear or terror,
He draws embodied beings out of the lower realms.

From saṃsāra, with its great terrors,
He frees countless beings.
He does not move beings out of it,
And yet he frees them from it.

Having freed them, though he establishes them
On the plain of nirvāṇa, supreme peace,
He does not apprehend those beings.
Therefore he is called 'Blessed One.'

Though he teaches embodied beings
Dharmas that are like space,
He has no fear in this.
Therefore he is called 'Blessed One.'

All the manifold dharmas
He proclaims without distinction.
They are equal through the equality of awakening,
And he does not apprehend even that awakening.

So that people may accomplish awakening,
He leads others into this.
Though he teaches dharmas in this way,
He has no fear of anything at all.

Through teachings of many kinds
He sets these embodied beings free.
He is free of fear and without grasping.
Therefore he is called 'Blessed One.'

He has truly attained the cessation of perception;
The bodhisattva is without afflictions.
Therefore he has gained this name
And is renowned as 'Blessed One.'

One beyond reproach who is seeking,
Since he has already fully known
That phenomena are the same as cessation,
He too is called 'Blessed One.'

The intelligent one does not cling to names;
He has no attachment to names.
In order to free them from attachment to names,
He teaches the Dharma to embodied beings.

The awakening that the wise have entered
Is free of names.
Beings who cling to names
Are very far from awakening.

Sounds, like echoes,
Perish in every way.
Knowing sounds to be nonexistent,
He is not attached even to 'they are equal.'

He is unattached to all sounds
And does not dwell in expressions.
Because he is free of elaboration for the sake of awakening,
He is called 'Blessed One.'

Such dharmas as these,
Of which the word 'Blessed One' is spoken,
The bodhisattva has not found.
Therefore he is called 'Blessed One.'

The steadfast one who has entered awakening,
Free of attachment and undefiled,
Fully knows this explanation
And is not attached to it in any way.

This explanation spoken in the word 'Blessed One,'
And the others too,
Knowing these, he says,
'I am a buddha, a blessed one.'

Ānanda, having reflected on this,
He said, 'Whoever speaks truly of the Blessed One
Speaks of me in so speaking.'
That is why he spoke as he did."

Ānanda asked:

"Blessed One, having fully understood which teachings
To bodhisattvas does this steadfast one
Say, 'I am a buddha'?
What is that explanation?"

The Blessed One said:

"Because he has realized all phenomena, without exception,
To be utterly nonexistent,
And because he has realized phenomena to be pure,
He is called 'Buddha.'

One who knows the afflictions to be false,
Does not come under their power,
And is unattached to all afflictions
Is called 'Buddha.'

When he knows the body to be empty,
This is not mine, nor anyone's;
In truth, in this body,
No core can be apprehended.

The childish, unskilled regarding the body,
Take what has no core to be a core.
He realizes it exactly as it is.
Therefore he is called 'Buddha.'

Whatever has become
The perception 'I' is utterly without characteristics.
Knowing it to be without characteristics,
He does not come under its power.

He knows form to be unborn at the prior limit
And never to have truly arisen.
It is imagined by the childish;
There is nothing established in it.

He realizes feeling to be rootless;
He does not apprehend a root in it.
Since he does not abide in any phenomena,
For him there is no feeling.

Having realized that perception is like a mirage,
Without substance and without entity,
He therefore does not engage
With any phenomena in any way.

All bodies are without a core;
Nothing is grasped by formations.
Having realized that the body is empty of formations,
He is not attached to the body.

The formations and the body as well
Are like the core of a plantain trunk.
He realizes this exactly as it is;
Thus he is called 'Buddha.'

In truth, consciousness
Does not abide inside the body,
Nor is consciousness possible
Outside this body.

In all phenomena, in every way,
Consciousness is not possible.
Its abode, its lack of abode,
And its transference are not apprehended.

He thus understands consciousness
To be forever utterly without entity.
Like a magical emanation or an illusion,
No one ever knows it.

No being whatever, in any way,
Sees consciousness as it is.
In truth, no one at all
Has come to know it.

All phenomena are without entity;
In truth there is no knowing.
Here neither phenomena nor beings,
As they are imagined, exist even within.

All phenomena are without entity,
Unchanging and free of elaboration.
He realizes them to be like emanations.
Therefore he is called 'Buddha.'

Having realized all these phenomena,
He abides as the buddhas abide.
He does not abide in any phenomena.
Therefore he is called 'Buddha.'

Just as phenomena abide,
So he understands them as suchness.
Buddha and awakening are alike,
Unapprehendable and unconditioned.

As for the generation of the mind
By which one has set out for the sake of awakening,
Since not even the subtlest phenomenon is apprehended,
That mind does not become an object of apprehension.

"Since he has generated that mind
For the sake of awakening,
He realizes that awakening and mind are the same,
Like something made by illusion.

Ānanda, having reflected on this,
He proclaimed in words,
'The one who is called Buddha,
The buddha, the guide, is I.'

The dharmas taught by the word 'Buddha'
Are like these.
Those who abide in this Dharma
Have set out for supreme awakening.

Those who know dharmas in this way
Are close to supreme awakening.
They have no doubt whatsoever
About any phenomena.

Those who come to know this Dharma,
Spoken after reflecting in this way,
Because they have abandoned uncertainty,
Will become the best in the world."

As soon as the Blessed One had given this answer, teaching the three called Tathāgata, Blessed One, and Buddha, those many hundreds of thousands of living beings said to the Blessed One, "Blessed One, concerning what the Blessed One intended in calling the bodhisattvas 'Tathāgata,' 'Blessed One,' and 'Buddha,' we are now without doubt and entertain no uncertainty. Blessed One, we understand those dharmas completely. By way of not apprehending any phenomena, we have regained our mindfulness here. The Tathāgata, the arhat, the perfectly complete Buddha has done for us what a father and mother do. The Blessed One has stretched out his hand to us. We will not again give rise to a wavering mind; we have attained the Dharma that is without wavering. Blessed One, just as, for example, no one can make unwavering space waver, in the same way, Blessed One, we too are without wavering with regard to all phenomena. Why is that? Because we realize that all phenomena are without wavering, equal to and like space, and unborn."

Then those many hundreds of thousands of living beings bowed their heads at the Blessed One's feet, circumambulated the Blessed One three times, and sat down not far from the Blessed One.

Then the bodhisattva great being Smiling Serene Faculties spoke these verses at that time:

"Many who perceived results
You have freed from perceiving results.
You who have attained the perfect result,
Protector of the world, I bow to you.

You speak of the results,
And you also proclaim sameness.
Buddha, perfect in result,
Wise one of the world, I bow to you.

There is no being who desires a result.
Perfect Buddha, who frees those
Whose domain is the various results,
Leader of the world, I bow to you.

You have fully entered sameness
And you also teach all dharmas.
You know all beings to be the same;
In sameness, I bow to you.

Beings who fix on results as objects
You have fully freed from fixing on objects.
You who release from every object of fixation,
Renowned in the world, I bow to you.

Perfect in the result that is utter peace,
Having wholly abandoned the various results,
Endowed with every virtue without exception,
Knower of the world, I bow to you."

Then, having praised the Blessed One with these verses, the bodhisattva great being Smiling Serene Faculties bowed his head at the Blessed One's feet, circumambulated the Blessed One three times, withdrew from the Blessed One's presence, and sat down a short distance away. There he remained, gazing at the Blessed One without blinking, with joy and supreme delight.

Then the bodhisattva great being called Padmaśrīgarbha rose from his seat, scattered lotuses over the Blessed One, and praised the Blessed One with these verses:

"Many beings who dwell in the perception of things
You have set free.
Free of timidity, without grasping,
Supreme sage, I bow to you.

You teach the Dharma
That pacifies existence and is free of existence.
Hero, you who are free of existence,
Supreme sage, I bow to you.

You know things to be empty,
Forever without essential nature.
Perfect Buddha who has abandoned existence,
Supreme sage, I bow to you.

You have truly cut off what leads to existence;
Without afflictions, you abide nowhere.
Fully liberated from fear and terror,
Supreme sage, I bow to you.

Without timidity, free of diffidence,
You proclaim the sublime speech.
You have passed beyond all sounds.
Supreme sage, I bow to you.

Free of sorrow, you have attained fearlessness,
And you fully pull out the thorn of sorrow.
You have abandoned Māra and are without faults.
Supreme sage, I bow to you."

Then, having praised the Blessed One with these verses, the bodhisattva great being Padmaśrīgarbha said this:

"In the later time, in the final age,
Those wise ones who are not frightened
By such a sūtra as this,
Those beings are worthy of homage."

Then the bodhisattva great being called Dustless Intellect said to the Blessed One:

"This sūtra teaches with certainty
The vast Dharma of the buddhas.
Those who have no doubt
About it will be few.

Through clinging to the perishable collection,
Perceiving as body what is not body,
The childish, wholly bound to perceptions,
Speak ill of it when it is explained.

Beings whose devotion Māra has turned back,
And those under Māra's sway,
Those who have no devotion
To such a sūtra as this, are unwise."

Then the bodhisattva great being Utpalanetra said to the Blessed One:

"Those gentle ones who have no doubt
About such a sūtra as this
Are equal to the eye of the world,
And they also bestow the eye."

Then the bodhisattva great being Devoted to Stūpas said to the Blessed One:

"Those gentle ones who have devotion
For such a discourse as this
Are like stūpas for the world;
They will become supreme among humans."

Then the bodhisattva great being Ever Longing said to the Blessed One:

"Those who uphold this
Turn their backs on this body;
No desire arises in them, they are unattached
To any phenomena, and free of doubt."

Then the bodhisattva great being Devoted to Garments said to the Blessed One:

"Those who are free of doubt about this way,
Those beings will be wrapped all around
With incense, with many koṭis
Of garments, and with flowers."

Then the bodhisattva great being Giver of Food said to the Blessed One:

"To those who are free of doubt about this way
I shall give, without exception,
The finest divine foods,
As many as there are, with a hundred flavors."

Then the bodhisattva great being Rightly Engaged in Compassion on Seeing Beings said to the Blessed One:

"Those base ones who doubt
This unsurpassed sūtra,
Those beings are objects of compassion;
Hereafter they are Māra's due.

Those who doubt such a sūtra as this
Even for a single moment,
Those beings will go to the hells
And will remain long in the hells.

Those who do not understand this way
Are companions of evil friends.
They are unskilled in this profound teaching,
Wholly covered by the net of delusion.

Those who, with poor understanding, look for faults,
Or, through attachment to gain and honor,
Reject this teaching,
Do so because of their bad character.

Those with no devotion to awakening,
Lazy, who have abandoned diligence,
Devoted to the lesser, with corrupt wisdom,
Will not have devotion to this.

Those who always delight in saṃsāra,
Who are under the sway of eternalist views,
And who cling to the three worlds,
Will not have devotion to this.

Those who babble, foolish and of poor understanding,
Greedy for sensual pleasures and unskilled,
Who always delight in bustle,
Are the ones who reject it.

Attached to robes and alms bowls,
Always greedy for food,
With a meager share of the white dharmas,
These are the ones who reject it.

Beings who desire results
And cling to results
Find it hard to understand here
The intent of the savior, the protector of the world."

Then the bodhisattva great being Wholly Abandoning Evil Friends said to the Blessed One:

"Those who reject this sūtra,
Which frees from the perception of results,
Those beings are to be shunned
Like excrement, urine, and utter rot.

Those who reject this Dharma
Are to be shunned in this way:
Like a stinking corpse,
They are to be utterly abandoned.

For example, when a dull-witted man hears
That robbers, raiders of villages,
Are lurking in the wilderness,
He flees, thinking, 'They will come into view.'

Just so, those who reject this sūtra,
Bewildered by delusion,
Utterly wicked and of poor understanding,
Hearing of it, flee, thinking, 'We will come to see it.'"

Then the venerable Ānanda said to the Blessed One, "Blessed One, it is amazing how utterly refined in understanding these sons of good family are. Blessed One, how is this? Do they speak these words through having generated the power of meditative absorption, or do they speak in this way through the power of the Buddha?"

The Blessed One said, "Ānanda, they speak in this way through the power of the Buddha. These sons of good family have also attained the generation of the power of meditative absorption with respect to this Dharma discourse, and they have also attained nonattachment. Why is that? Ānanda, just as these sons of good family have now heard it from me, so they heard this Dharma discourse, exactly this much, with nothing added and nothing missing, from sixty koṭis of buddhas. Recollecting it, they speak in this way through generating the power of meditative absorption and through the power of the Buddha. Why is that? Ānanda, what these have said is just so; it is directly evident to them."

When he had said this, the venerable Ānanda said to the Blessed One, "Blessed One, how much merit will a son of good family or a daughter of good family generate who, as soon as they hear this Dharma discourse, has devotion to it and entertains no doubt?"

The Blessed One said, "Ānanda, suppose a son of good family or a daughter of good family who has rightly set out for unsurpassed, perfectly complete awakening were to fill this Jambudvīpa with jewels and present them as an offering to the tathāgatas, the arhats, the perfectly complete buddhas. One who, as soon as they hear this Dharma discourse, has devotion, faith, and conviction and does not doubt it will generate far more merit than that. Ānanda, leave aside Jambudvīpa filled with jewels. Ānanda, suppose someone were to fill as many world realms as there are grains of sand in the river Ganges with jewels and present them as an offering to the tathāgatas, the arhats, the perfectly complete buddhas. One who hears this Dharma discourse and, having heard it, as soon as they hear it, has devotion, faith, and conviction and does not doubt it will generate far more merit than that."

Then the Blessed One spoke these verses at that time:

"Compared with one who fills this Jambudvīpa
With jewels and makes an offering
To the tathāgatas, the saviors,
The buddhas, the protectors of the world,

The wise one who hears this sūtra
And has exceptional devotion to it,
That one endowed with devotion
Will generate more merit than that.

Compared with one who fills with jewels
Fields as many as the Ganges' sands
And offers them to the buddhas,
This is more excellent than that."

The sixth fascicle, the last.

When he had said this, the venerable Ānanda said to the Blessed One:

"Blessed One, how much merit will a son of good family or a daughter of good family generate who, having devotion to this Dharma discourse and understanding it, takes it up, bears it in mind, reads it, masters it, or recites it?"

The Blessed One said, "Ānanda, suppose a son of good family or a daughter of good family who has rightly set out for unsurpassed, perfectly complete awakening, apart from this Dharma discourse, were to give gifts to the tathāgatas, the arhats, the perfectly complete buddhas for a hundred eons; apart from this Dharma discourse, were to guard moral discipline for a hundred eons; apart from this Dharma discourse, were to practice patience for a hundred eons; apart from this Dharma discourse, were to exert diligence for a hundred eons; apart from this Dharma discourse, were to enter into the attainments of the meditative concentrations for a hundred eons; and apart from this Dharma discourse, were to cultivate wisdom for a hundred eons and become skilled in the affairs of the world. Ānanda, that person would not have shown honor to the Tathāgata."

Then the Blessed One spoke these verses at that time:

"One who for a full hundred eons
Serves the Tathāgata
With food and everything else
Does not thereby show him reverence.

One who upholds this sūtra,
Which destroys fixation on results,
Has shown honor to the buddhas
And has shown honor to the Dharma.

The perfect buddhas are the Dharma body,
Born of the Dharma, bearing the banner of the Dharma;
This is honor to the buddhas,
And so he has honored the buddhas.

Those who for a full hundred eons
Make offerings of robes and the like
To the tathāgatas, the buddhas,
Have not thereby honored them.

But if one upholds this way,
Then one honors the buddhas.
This is the supreme among honors;
Giving many robes is not like this.

Those who for a full hundred eons
Scatter incense and flowers
Before the buddhas, protectors of the world,
Have not thereby honored them.

One who upholds this sūtra
Of the profound and supreme way
Shows the sublime honor
To the saviors, the protectors of the world.

Though one were to build a stūpa
For the protector of the world from precious materials,
Making it like Mount Meru,
One would not thereby have honored the Buddha.

One who upholds this sūtra
And does not even regard himself as upholding it
Offers the supreme service,
Supreme among all offerings.

A person who for a full hundred eons
Guards moral discipline
But does not uphold this sūtra
Has no supreme discipline.

One who upholds this sūtra
And has no conceit about upholding it,
That discipline is praised;
That discipline is unsurpassed.

Because he has fully upheld this way,
The wise one seeks that discipline
Which is great discipline,
Inconceivable and immeasurable.

For one who trains in this sūtra
As I have taught it, seeing sameness,
There is neither moral discipline
Nor bad conduct.

One who does not train in this sūtra
Does not train for awakening.
Awakening and training are the same;
No one has ever trained in it.

One who upholds this sūtra,
The discipline taught in this sūtra,
Such discipline of theirs
Will become perfect discipline.

A person endowed with patience
Who here, for a full hundred eons,
Endures from all beings
Harsh words and abuse,

Whose mind does not change
Even when his hands and feet are cut off,
Who nowhere gives rise
To hostility or anger,

That person who speaks of patience,
Even should he spend koṭis of eons
In patience of that kind,
That patience is not praised.

One who hears this sūtra
And upholds it with devotion,
That is the supreme patience;
That is patience most excellent.

One who upholds this sūtra
And is attached to nothing at all,
That patience is especially exalted;
That patience is unsurpassed.

One who desires the supreme wisdom of the buddhas,
The wisdom free of attachment,
Should swiftly take up this sūtra
Without attachment to it.

Compared with one who for a full hundred eons,
Endowed with diligence, never sits down,
Not overcome by sleep, self-possessed,
Always passing his time this way,

If a wise one applies himself to this sūtra
In order to receive its transmission,
That diligence is superior to the other;
This diligence is especially exalted.

One who for a full hundred eons
Becomes a sage with the five superknowledges
But does not listen to this sūtra
Is not called supreme in superknowledge.

One who, knowing the meaning, without abiding anywhere,
Upholds this sūtra
Has the supreme superknowledge
Among all superknowledges.

A person who for a full hundred eons
Is endowed with wisdom,
Endowed with worldly wisdom,
Certain in the affairs of the world,

If he does not train in this way,
Does not know the way of wisdom.
One who upholds this way,
That hero truly has wisdom.

One who hears this sublime sūtra
And has devotion to it and understands it,
That steadfast one has wisdom;
He is learned in wisdom.

Whoever wishes in any way
To know all phenomena through wisdom
Should receive the transmission of this sūtra;
He will attain this wisdom.

What is fully taught in this sūtra
Is the domain of wisdom.
Therefore exert yourselves with diligence
To take up this sūtra."

Then the venerable Ānanda said to the Blessed One:

"Running a full league, or even a hundred leagues,
One should listen to this sūtra,
Which dispels the manifold fruits of karma.

Crossing a full league
Heaped to the brim with burning embers,
The wise should swiftly go
To wherever this sūtra abides.

Whatever meditator seeks meditative concentration
In the highest of all concentrations
Should receive the transmission of this sūtra,
Which dispels all afflictions.

Whoever wishes to go
To the world realm renowned as Abhirati,
That realm of surpassing beauty,
Should expound this sūtra.

Whoever wishes to see the Buddha Akṣobhya,
The unsurpassed Sage,
Should receive the transmission of this sūtra,
Read it often, and uphold it.

Whoever wishes to go to Sukhāvatī,
The abode of bodhisattvas,
If he receives the transmission of this sūtra,
Will go there very swiftly.

By reciting this sūtra
Spoken by all the buddhas,
One will see the perfect Buddha Amitābha,
The inconceivable.

Bodhisattvas beyond reproach,
Who long for these qualities,
Should uphold this sūtra
Spoken by all the buddhas."

When he had said this, the Blessed One said to the venerable Ānanda: "Excellent, Ānanda, excellent! Ānanda, it is just as you have said. Ānanda, whenever a son of good family or a daughter of good family reads or recites this sūtra with a mind free of all mental distraction and all signs, at that time they will see whatever buddha they wish to see. But if, being distracted, they do not read it or do not recite it, then at the time of death they will not see the buddhas, the blessed ones, before their eyes. Why is that? Ānanda, the son of good family or daughter of good family who receives the transmission of this Dharma discourse, or upholds it, or recites it, or reads it, will be embraced by all the buddhas."

Then the maiden Siṃhā, together with five hundred maidens, joined her palms toward where the Blessed One was, bowed, and said to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, if one who has become a woman receives the transmission of this sūtra, or upholds it, or recites it, or reads it, what qualities will come of it?"

The Blessed One said: "Siṃhā, if a woman who has truly set out for unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening receives the transmission of this Dharma discourse, or upholds it, or recites it, or reads it, or bears it, then her very state of being a woman will be her last. Why is that? Because by receiving the transmission of this Dharma discourse, upholding it, reciting it, bearing it and reading it, the female afflictions, those afflictions through which a woman takes on the state of a woman, will not arise in that woman."

When he had said this, the maiden Siṃhā said to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, what are those female afflictions, possessing which a woman takes on the state of a woman?"

The Blessed One said: "Siṃhā, here a woman sees another woman who is fair of form, beautiful, lovely to behold, decked with ornaments, and at ease, and she generates desire for that. If she does not consider, 'This is a lump of filth painted with colors, a seducer of the childish, foul-smelling, smeared with filth,' then, not considering it in this way, she sees it as attractive, and by generating desire for it the woman takes on the state of a woman.

"Moreover, Siṃhā, here a woman becomes jealous, miserly and deceitful. She says one thing in words while her mind is carried off elsewhere; she says one thing openly in words while secretly thinking another; she goes to a monk for the sake of enjoyment and not for the sake of the Dharma; she is overcome by anger, by dullness and by much sleep; she delights in busy company; she does not seek to receive the transmission of a Dharma discourse such as this; and she spends her days and nights with an afflicted mind rather than a purified mind. Through these afflictions that woman takes on the state of a woman and does not bring it to an end.

"Therefore, Siṃhā, in brief, so that none of her afflictions may arise or occur, a woman should receive the transmission of this Dharma discourse, should recite it, should master it, should uphold it and should read it. Why is that? Because this Dharma discourse is free of the female afflictions."

The maiden Siṃhā said: "Blessed One, if a woman does not wish to be transformed from the state of a woman, yet receives the transmission of this Dharma discourse, upholds it, recites it, bears it, reads it and masters it, how should that be regarded?"

The Blessed One said: "Siṃhā, even though she may not wish to be transformed from the state of a woman, if she receives the transmission of this Dharma discourse or reflects on it, it should be known that this is her last state as a woman, except when it is a display of miraculous power.

"Siṃhā, suppose, for example, that some man or woman were to hurl their own body into a great mass of fire, spreading everywhere, blazing and full of flames, and were to say these words: 'May my body not be burned! May the color of my skin not change!' Siṃhā, what do you think? By saying those words, would that man or woman obtain that wish?"

She said: "No indeed, Blessed One. Blessed One, a great mass of fire has the very nature of burning, and it destroys the color of the skin."

The Blessed One said: "Siṃhā, in just the same way, this Dharma discourse dispels all afflictions."

"Therefore, Siṃhā, a woman who does not want the state of a woman, who wishes swiftly to accomplish the qualities of a buddha, who wishes to see countless buddhas, and who wishes to attain unobstructed eloquence, should uphold this very Dharma discourse, should master it, should recite it, should read it, should bear it, and should learn it by heart."

Then the maiden Siṃhā, together with the five hundred maidens, joined her palms and said to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, we received this Dharma discourse from the Tathāgata, the Arhat, the perfectly and completely awakened Buddha Dīpaṃkara, and ever since then we have given it to many hundreds of thousands of koṭis of niyutas of living beings to uphold."

Then the venerable Ānanda said to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, how is this? Has the maiden Siṃhā not been transformed from the state of a woman?"

The Blessed One said: "Ānanda, do you think that the maiden Siṃhā is a woman?"

He said: "Blessed One, that is so."

The Blessed One said: "Ānanda, do not say that. Why is that? Ānanda, this is a miraculous display of the maiden Siṃhā and these five hundred maidens. Having seen the people of later times, the maiden Siṃhā has shown this miraculous display out of compassion for women, and in order to free women from the state of a woman. Why is that? These women enter all the households which men do not enter and have no right to enter, and there they train women by means of miraculous display. Ānanda, in the maiden Siṃhā there is no quality of a woman and no quality of a man. Why is that? Because no phenomenon whatever is apprehended, she does not apprehend a woman and does not apprehend a man. The maiden Siṃhā has fully realized and understood this Dharma discourse; she has attained illumination. Therefore, Ānanda, a woman who wishes to follow the training of the maiden Siṃhā should uphold this Dharma discourse, should master it, should recite it, should bear it, should read it, and should learn it by heart."

Then five thousand nuns rose from their seats, joined their palms toward where the Blessed One was, bowed, and said to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, from this day onward we will uphold this Dharma discourse, master it, recite it, bear it, read it, and learn it by heart. Why is that? Blessed One, we do not seek the state of a woman. Blessed One, we despise the state of a woman. Blessed One, from this day onward, until we have mastered this Dharma discourse, we will not lie down to rest, and we will not let ourselves be overcome by dullness and sleep."

When they had said this, the Blessed One said to those nuns: "Sisters, you have spoken these words well. You have put on the great armor, and in order that you should in no way seek the state of a woman but should seek the qualities of a buddha, you have undertaken this great diligence. Excellent, excellent! Therefore, exert yourselves with even greater diligence to uphold this Dharma discourse."

When those nuns heard this, they were satisfied, elated and delighted; they rejoiced greatly, and joy and gladness arose in them. Each of them spread her upper robe over the feet of the Blessed One, and having spread them, they spoke this verse:

"For the sake of the state of a man,
Today we have found relief.
Since the Victor does not speak otherwise,
We shall become supreme in the world."

Then five hundred wives of householders, hearing this verse of those nuns, rose from their seats, joined their palms toward where the Blessed One was, bowed, and said to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, from this day onward we too will uphold this Dharma discourse, master it, recite it, bear it, read it, and learn it by heart. From now on, may we never again fall under the power of others, never have to look to the faces of others, and never be made to keep company with them. May we never again carry a child in the womb for nine or ten months. Why is that? Blessed One, once one has become a woman, even if she is born into a royal household, she falls under the power of others and must wait upon a husband for as long as she lives. Blessed One, from this day onward, until we die, we will exert ourselves with diligence. We will undertake to eat only a single meal a day. We will not give up our resolve. Until we have made this Dharma discourse dwell within our very bodies, we will not allow even our husbands to take us by the hand."

The Blessed One said: "Daughters of good family, you have spoken these words well. Excellent, excellent! From now on you will not fall under the power of others. You will not have to look to the faces of others. You will not carry a child in the womb for nine or ten months. You will be born in a buddha field where there are no women."

Then the venerable Ānanda said to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, what is the name of the world realm in which these sisters will be born?"

The Blessed One said: "Ānanda, the world realm in which they will be born is called Appearing Like a Precious Lotus."

Then the venerable Ānanda said to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, what is the name of the tathāgata who dwells in that world realm?"

The Blessed One said: "Ānanda, there dwells the tathāgata called Appearing to Scatter Precious Jewels for All Beings, and he teaches the Dharma. Even as these wives of householders recite this Dharma discourse, they will see him."

When those laywomen, the wives of householders, heard this, they were satisfied, elated and delighted; they rejoiced greatly, and joy and gladness arose in them. They untied from their necks ten thousand pearl necklaces adorned with precious jewels and scattered them over the Tathāgata, strewing them all about. Having scattered them, they spoke these verses:

"In the transformation of the state of a woman,
Today we have found relief.
The Buddha does not speak otherwise;
He has declared it in words of truth.

This body of afflictions, so very evil,
This body of a woman, we shall abandon.
Not knowing things truly as they are,
The childish, and even the learned, are deluded.

From now on, for us, the dwelling in the womb
Will no longer be a great terror.
Since we have obtained supreme virtue,
We are free of the dwelling in the womb."

When those wives of householders had spoken these verses, they gazed at the Blessed One with unblinking eyes and bowed to him.

Then Śakra, lord of the gods, scattered divine flowers over the circle of the Blessed One's assembly and said to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, I have taken up this Dharma discourse. Blessed One, I have mastered this Dharma discourse."

The Blessed One said: "Kauśika, because of that, from now on you will not have to enter the battlefront of the gods and the asuras."

Then, in order to bring to fulfillment the roots of virtue of many hundreds of thousands of living beings, Mañjuśrī the youthful said to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, for as long as I had not set this Dharma discourse turning, the Tathāgata had not set his mind on awakening."

The Blessed One said: "Mañjuśrī, because of that you appear throughout the ten directions and have become supreme among many koṭis of niyutas of bodhisattvas, just like the disk of the sun."

Then, at that moment, this great earth shook in six ways, and the whole circle of the assembly was covered with divine flowers knee-deep.

Then the venerable Ānanda said to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, what is the cause, and what is the condition, for this shaking of the great earth that has occurred in the world? And who has scattered these flowers?"

The Blessed One said: "Ānanda, many koṭis of niyutas of gods heard what Mañjuśrī the youthful explained, and with joyful minds they scattered these flowers. Having scattered them, they spoke these words: 'We too uphold this Dharma discourse. We master it. We recite it. We bear it. May we too become just like Mañjuśrī the youthful!' No sooner had they spoken these words than this Dharma discourse became manifest to them, and all of them, satisfied, elated and delighted, bowed at my feet and at the feet of Mañjuśrī the youthful. Then this shaking of the earth occurred."

When he had said this, the venerable Ānanda said to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, this Dharma discourse possesses great qualities. Blessed One, this Dharma discourse possesses marvelous and wondrous qualities. Blessed One, those beings within whose hearing this Dharma discourse resounds are not possessed of meager roots of virtue."

The Blessed One said: "Ānanda, so it is; so it is. Those sons of good family or daughters of good family who listen to this Dharma discourse and, having heard it, are devoted to it, have faith in it, uphold it, master it, recite it, and bear it, have attended upon many buddhas. They are freed from every fall into the lower realms and bad destinies, and from every birth in the unfavorable states. They have conquered Māra, the Evil One. They have raised aloft the banner of the Dharma. They have set the lamp of the Dharma fully ablaze. They have dispelled the blinding darkness. They have reached the seat of awakening. Ānanda, those who, as soon as they hear this Dharma discourse, are devoted to it, uphold it, master it, recite it, and bear it, are close to me. Ānanda, those who uphold this Dharma discourse, master it, recite it, bear it, and read it are my children, born from the Dharma body. Each of them wishes to eat the food of the Tathāgata. Each of them wishes to sit at the foot of the tree of awakening, just as I now sit there. Each of them wishes to teach the Dharma, just as I now teach it."

When he had said this, the venerable Ānanda said to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, in later times, in a later age, will there be some who are devoted to this Dharma discourse, who uphold it, master it, recite it, bear it, retain it, and read it?"

The Blessed One said: "Ānanda, those who are devoted to it now will be devoted to it in later times. Those who uphold it now will uphold it in later times. Those who read it now will read it in later times. Ānanda, that someone who has not heard this Dharma discourse now should hear it in later times, this I do not see anywhere in the world with its gods, its māras and its Brahmās, among the generations of beings with their ascetics and brahmins; that is not possible. Ānanda, those whose wealth this is now, it will also be their wealth in later times.

"Ānanda, suppose, for example, that here some householder or householder's son were rich, of great wealth, of great means, with much property and many possessions, and that he buried treasures as a hoard and went away to another country. Ānanda, what do you think? As soon as he comes back, will he find what he buried as a hoard?"

He said: "Blessed One, he will find it. Why is that? Because that wealth is his very own."

The Blessed One said: "Ānanda, in just the same way, those whose wealth this is now, it will also be their wealth in the future, and just as I now distribute it, they too will distribute this unsurpassed wealth of the Dharma to others."

"Ānanda, those who in later times will be devoted to this Dharma discourse, who will uphold it, master it, recite it, bear it, and read it, I see with the eye of a buddha just as though they were present now. Those who will reject it I also see."

When he had said this, the venerable Ānanda said to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, what kind of destiny will befall those who are not devoted to it, who reject it, who speak ill of it, and who disparage it?"

The Blessed One said: "Ānanda, what is the use of your asking this? Be silent!"

The venerable Ānanda said: "Blessed One, please explain. Then even those who are not devoted and have no faith will become devoted and will understand."

The Blessed One said: "Ānanda, whatever is the destiny of one who has committed the five acts of immediate retribution, that will be their destiny. Ānanda, what do you think? If someone were to take the lives of all the beings in a great trichiliocosm, what kind of destiny would befall him?"

He said: "Blessed One, he is a wicked one."

The Blessed One said: "It should be known that that is the destiny of such a person. Ānanda, if someone were to destroy the stupas of buddhas, blessed ones who have passed into complete nirvāṇa, as many as the grains of sand of the river Ganges, and were also to burn them with fire, would he, on that basis, generate much demerit?"

He said: "Much, Blessed One! Much, Sugata! On that basis he would generate much demerit; it cannot be equaled, it cannot even be contemplated."

The Blessed One said: "It should be known that that is the destiny of such a person. Ānanda, if someone were to bring about the cessation of the way of the Dharma of the buddhas, the blessed ones, of the past, the future and the present, what kind of destiny would befall him?"

He said: "Blessed One, he is a wicked one; it is not easy to put into words."

He said: "It should be known that that is the destiny of such a person."

The venerable Ānanda said: "Blessed One, further, if someone turns other beings away from it, what kind of destiny should be understood for him?"

The Blessed One said: "Ānanda, if someone were to gouge out the eyes of all the beings in a great trichiliocosm who possess the ten virtuous paths of action and have truly set out for unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening, what kind of destiny would befall him?"

He said: "For many eons he would be born blind, and he would also experience feelings of suffering, burned by the fire of the hell beings."

The Blessed One said: "Ānanda, I instruct you, and you should understand this: whoever turns even a single being away from this Dharma discourse, it should be known that that is his destiny."

The venerable Ānanda said: "Blessed One, if a bodhisattva who has set out for unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening has doubts about this Dharma discourse but does not reject it, how will it be with him?"

The Blessed One said: "Ānanda, for as many thoughts of doubt as he gives rise to, for so long he will fail to please the buddhas. For that many eons he puts awakening far off."

He said: "Blessed One, further, if someone has been devoted to it and has understood it, and then comes to have doubts, how large will be the body with which he experiences suffering?"

The Blessed One said: "Ānanda, what is the use of your asking about his body? Be silent!"

He said: "Blessed One, these four assemblies will be terrified, and the people of later times, when they hear of it, will also be terrified; therefore please explain."

The Blessed One said: "The body with which he experiences suffering will measure ten thousand leagues."

The venerable Ānanda said: "And the one who did not guard his words, what will his tongue be like?"

The Blessed One said: "Ānanda, his tongue will measure a thousand leagues, and it will be plowed by a hundred million plows. A hundred million iron balls, blazing and fiercely ablaze, will burn upon it. Why is that? Because he did not in any way guard his words."

Then those four assemblies were terrified and their hair stood on end. With tears streaming down their faces and with weeping countenances, they fell to the ground then and there. Having fallen, they all spoke with one voice: "Blessed One, we are filled with remorse for the sake of any son of good family or daughter of good family who, by speaking such words, will experience such a bodily form."

Then other beings there, tears streaming down their faces, wept and spoke these words: "Blessed One, whatever doubts we have given rise to in this life and in other births, Blessed One, calling as witnesses also all the other tathāgatas we have seen, we wish to confess that karmic obscuration individually in the presence of the Tathāgata. We wish to renounce it individually. Since we confess the transgression as a transgression and see the transgression as a transgression, may the Tathāgata, the Arhat, the perfectly and completely awakened Buddha, accept our transgression."

The Blessed One said: "Sons of good family, it is excellent, excellent, that you see the wrong as a wrong, and confess it and renounce it. Sons of good family, this confession made after seeing a wrong as a wrong brings growth in the Dharma and Vinaya of the noble ones."

Then the venerable Ānanda said to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, will those in this assembly who have given rise to doubt also, Blessed One, go to such a hell?"

The Blessed One said: "Ānanda, those here who have given rise to doubt, since they have confessed and renounced it, will find that what they must experience becomes slight."

The venerable Ānanda said: "Blessed One, how slight will what they must experience become?"

The Blessed One said: "Ānanda, at the time of their death they will experience the sufferings of the hell beings. From each and every pore of their skin countless, immeasurable feelings will arise; and this is said only in brief. Why is that? Because they have in some measure confessed individually before me and before countless other buddhas, blessed ones.

"Therefore, Ānanda, a son of good family or a daughter of good family should generate devotion to this Dharma discourse, thinking, 'My body too might come to be like this; I too might come to experience feelings like these.' And having become devoted, they should be free of hesitation and free of doubt. One who has not forsaken the Tathāgata, who has not forsaken the tathāgatas of the past, the future and the present, who has not forsaken the Dharma, who has not forsaken the Saṅgha, and who has not forsaken the way of the Dharma of the buddhas, the blessed ones, of the past, the future and the present, should be devoted to this Dharma discourse, should uphold it, should master it, should recite it, should bear it, and should read it."

When he had said this, the venerable Ānanda said to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, when the buddhas, the blessed ones, give the teaching of the Dharma of the irreversible wheel, with what in mind did the Blessed One speak of 'one who has not forsaken the Saṅgha'? What is it that the Tathāgata has designated as the Saṅgha?"

The Blessed One said: "Ānanda, the Tathāgata has designated the irreversible as the Saṅgha."

He said: "Blessed One, yet those irreversible bodhisattvas are not themselves inclined toward the irreversible Saṅgha, nor are they counted among it."

The Blessed One said: "Ānanda, all those who from the depths of their hearts have given rise to the mind set on awakening, and who do not subsequently perceive even that mind, are truly inclined toward the irreversible Saṅgha. All of them are counted among the irreversible Saṅgha."

The venerable Ānanda said: "Blessed One, how greatly the buddhas, the blessed ones, are endowed with skill in means! It is wonderful how, through skill in means, they have fully taught this meaning."

Then Śakra, lord of the gods, scattered divine māndārava flowers over the Blessed One, and having scattered them, he said to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, may all beings too teach the Dharma with this skill in means!"

The Blessed One said: "Kauśika, all those who become devoted to this Dharma discourse will teach the Dharma with this skill in means, just as I do now."

Then many thousands of gods scattered divine flowers over the Blessed One and spoke these words: "Blessed One, may all beings too obtain this Dharma discourse!"

Then the venerable Ānanda said to the Blessed One: "For the sake of the people of later times, may the Blessed One please bless this Dharma discourse."

The Blessed One said: "Ānanda, even if a son of good family or a daughter of good family who has gone out into the middle of the ocean should seek to listen to this Dharma discourse, they will without doubt come to hear it. Why is that? Ānanda, this Dharma discourse has been blessed by the buddhas of the past."

He said: "Blessed One, although this Dharma discourse has been blessed by the buddhas of the past, may the Blessed One also bless it now."

Then the great earth shook in six ways: it shook, and it shook violently. Before the Blessed One there appeared a great lotus of Jambu River gold with many hundreds of thousands of koṭis of petals, illuminating all the ten directions with its light. And that light illuminated world realms as many as the grains of sand of the river Ganges. Then those four assemblies saw that before every one of the buddhas, the blessed ones, as many as the grains of sand of the river Ganges, in those world realms, there had appeared a great lotus of Jambu River gold with many hundreds of thousands of petals, illuminating all the ten directions with its light.

Then Śakra, lord of the gods, in the guise of a householder, gave flowers of many different kinds to those four assemblies and spoke these words: "Scatter them as an offering to those tathāgatas, arhats, perfectly and completely awakened buddhas, and to this Dharma discourse! Scatter them!"

Then those four assemblies took the flowers and scattered them over those tathāgatas, arhats, perfectly and completely awakened buddhas, and they saw the flowers remain as parasols directly above the heads of those tathāgatas.

Then those four assemblies said to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, this shaking of the great earth that has occurred, and this great lotus that has appeared before the Blessed One and before these tathāgatas, of whom are they the portent?"

The Blessed One said: "Friends, this is the sign of the blessing of this Dharma discourse. Know by this sign that this Dharma discourse is blessed."

The venerable Ānanda said: "Blessed One, this Dharma discourse has been blessed."
