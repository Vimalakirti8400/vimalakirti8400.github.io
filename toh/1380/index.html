About this text

The Summary Verses on the Glorious Kālacakra, known in Tibetan as Pal Dükyi Khorlo Domtsik, is a short verse digest composed by the great Indian paṇḍita Abhayākara and translated into Tibetan by the paṇḍita Jowo Ripa and the translator Kheu Gé Khorlo Drak. It condenses the whole scope of the Kālacakra system into a compact outline, dividing it into what is expressed and the words that express it, and setting out the expressed under the two truths: the conventional, treated as the outer world with its formation, abiding and destruction, the inner world of the body, and the "other" Kālacakra of empowerment and practice, and the ultimate, treated as view, path and result, culminating in the Great Seal (Mahāmudrā) and the six-limbed yoga. It closes with a brief account of the structure of the Kālacakra tantra and its commentaries, serving as a mnemonic map of the tradition for students. The work is Toh 1380 in the Tengyur, where it belongs among the commentaries on the tantras.

Translation

Homage to the holy ones.

Kālacakra, the truth without division,
is called twofold from the side of what is to be known:
the expressed and the expressing.
The expressed is the two truths.
The conventional is threefold: outer, inner and other.
The outer is formation, abiding and destruction.
Formation is the support and the supported.
The support is cause, sequence, configuration,
nature, color, name,
letters, number and measure.
The cause is held to be that, from what karma has compounded,
it arises out of particles.
The sequence is space, wind and so on in order.
The configuration is the bow and so on; the nature,
motion and so on; the color, blue and so on.
The names are wind and so on, and mountains and so on.
The letters are ya ra la and so on.
The number runs from wind up to the oceans.
The measure is height and circumference.
The supported is called sentient beings:
their cause, the classification of their names,
their respective abodes, their lifespans,
and their seed letters are to be well understood.
The cause is karma and affliction.
The names run from Avīci
up to the thirty-one levels of existence.
The abodes run from wind up to the Peak of Existence.
The lifespans are counted in years and in eons.
The seed letters are ka and so on.
Thus formation is to be understood in summary.
Abiding is of two kinds:
the periods of the teaching, and astronomical calculation.
In the first, through the four ages and so on,
upon the regions of the earth
the wheel-turning kings, in embodied form,
make an end of the misleaders (vināyakas) and uphold what is good.
Calculation is gathered into five limbs:
the planets, the lunar days, the lunar mansions,
and those that dwell in the houses of the zodiac.
The lunar days are to be known through the vowels,
through dependent origination, and through the Fierce One and the rest.
Destruction is the earth and the rest dissolving in sequence,
until at last it becomes empty.
The inner is birth, abiding and death.
Birth is inner and outer.
The inner is its time, its causes,
its nature, its conditions, and the time of its completion.
The time is the stirring of wisdom.
The causes are semen, the life force and the blood of the womb.
The nature is the aggregates and elements as its intrinsic nature.
The condition is the power of the tastes the mother eats.
As to its aspects, through semen, blood, channels and winds
the eight principal ones are produced.
The time of completion is the twelfth or thirteenth month.
The outer is taught as shape, measure
and enumerations.
The shape is the body, the limbs and so on.
The measure runs from the finger's breadth up to the cubit.
The divisions are the aggregates and elements,
the sense faculties and the faculties of action,
the cakras, the wheels of activity,
and the wheels of action.
Moreover, through channels, winds and mind
the nature of the body is summed up.
Abiding is activity and protection.
Activity is the outer itself applied to the inner,
so that it becomes the wheel of time.
Protection is destroying the evils of the mind,
pacifying illness, and the elixir that rejuvenates the body.
Death is timely and untimely.
The other is the means of accomplishment, and
the worldly empowerment: its nature,
its etymology, its divisions and its rite.
Its nature is that it is to be received from another.
Its etymology is the washing away of stains and the decisive destruction of them.
Its divisions, as common and special,
are to be known as seven and three.
Its rite is the preparation and the main part.
Accomplishment is path and result.
The path is the action seal and the wisdom seal.
The action seal has its nature, its divisions,
and the rite of putting it into practice.
Its nature is that she is endowed with capacity and the right name.
Its divisions are by the other's family and so on.
The practice is approach and accomplishment.
The nature of the wisdom seal
is that she is imagined by one's own mind.
Its divisions are the three maṇḍalas.
Its rite is the preliminary, the main part and the conclusion.
The main part is generation and meditation.
Meditation is of two kinds: with clinging to things,
and the meditation on purity free of clinging.
Afterward, rising from meditative equipoise,
appearances, sounds, phenomena and so on
are joined, as outer, to the inner aspects,
and this is done with body, speech and mind.
The result is attaining the culmination
of the desire realm and the form realm.
What rests on ultimate truth
is view, path and result.
The nature of reality, in its discordant side,
teaches the wrong conceptions of the outer and the inner.
In its concordant side, it establishes nonduality,
to be known as its nature, its divisions, what illustrates it,
and its examples.
Its nature is nonconceptual wisdom,
which is moreover the opposite of the inert.
Its divisions, those of the bearers of qualities,
are to be known as the five emptinesses, the six drops,
what is to be abandoned and so on.
What illustrates it is sound and sign:
e vaṃ a and so on,
and the shapes that symbolize them.
Its examples are the pra ti se na and so on,
which transcend worldly examples.
The bearers of qualities are what make known
the abiding nature of the channels, winds and mind.
The path is empowerment and the means of accomplishment.
The empowerment is transcendent.
Its nature is recognizing one's own
mind of awakening through the condition of the guru.
Its etymology is the same as above.
Its divisions are the actual and the designated.
The first is at the time of buddhahood;
the designated is now, at the time of the cause.
Its rite, although it is free of activity,
is for the time being, through examples and symbolic words,
a conceptual construction of the outer.
The accomplishment is the Great Seal (Mahāmudrā);
its nature is nonconceptual wisdom.
Its divisions are threefold in character.
Its practice is taught by way of
its cause, its nature and its divisions.
The cause is the three conditions; the nature
is focusing on the nonconceptual.
The divisions are the six limbs.
Through what is to be abandoned, what abandons, and what is to be attained,
the path is gathered into those limbs.
Instructions, characteristics, signs, results,
individual divisions and all the rest
are meditated in the manner of the four accomplishments.
The result is the perfection of abandonment and attainment:
the four bodies of the Buddha and so on,
through the paths of the doors of liberation and so on,
by way of what disciples are to purify.
That is the expressed, in summary.
The words that express it
are summed up as the extent of the text, the numbers, and the manner of explanation.
The extent of the text is the root and the commentary.
The numbers are the numbers of the chapters of the tantra:
the abridged tantra and the summary of its meaning,
explained at length by the commentary.
As for the five chapters of the tantra,
their fixed number and their fixed order:
the number is that of the three tantras,
the sequence of bringing others in and of the actual things;
the order is yoga and absorption, coarse and subtle,
inferior and supreme.
As for the three commentaries explained,
their fixed number is the channels in the body,
half of those, and the center of the center;
the major and minor marks, and samādhi;
their order is the respective detailed commentaries.
The method of explanation has the characteristics of the six alternatives,
which moreover are the provisional and definitive meanings.
The summary of meaning in the three tantras
is summed up in general and individually,
as actual objects and as ordinary objects;
it is to be known in each respective limb.
Through the kindness of the holy ones, by this holy path
made clear with certainty, may sentient beings,
in this very human birth,
swiftly and certainly attain awakening.

The Summary Verses on the Glorious Kālacakra, composed by the great paṇḍita Abhayākara, are complete. They were translated by the paṇḍita Jowo Ripa and the translator Kheu Gé Khorlo Drak.

Translator's notes

The text reads ལས་བརྒྱ ("hundred actions") where the outline of the path as "action and wisdom" and the following treatment of the wisdom seal plainly require ལས་རྒྱ, the action seal (karmamudrā); translated accordingly. Likewise ཆོས་ཅན་ཅི་རླུང་སེམས is read as ཆོས་ཅན་རྩ་རླུང་སེམས, "channels, winds and mind." The word rendered "configuration" (dbyings) refers here to the shapes of the elemental maṇḍalas (the wind maṇḍala being bow-shaped), and may stand for dbyibs, "shape." Following the house glossary, the beings the wheel-turning kings overcome are given as "misleaders (vināyakas)"; in the Kālacakra setting the reference is probably to the false teachers and their doctrine overcome by the Kalkī kings. The phrase "the Fierce One and the rest" (drag mo) names a class of lunar days in the calendrical system and is translated literally. The lines on the fixed number and order of the five chapters and of the three commentaries are highly compressed, and their precise reference (for example "bringing others in," likely the empowerment chapter) is uncertain. The pra ti se na is the divinatory mirror image, used as an example of empty form.
