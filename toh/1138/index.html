About this text

In Praise of the Praiseworthy, a Praise of the Buddha, the Blessed One, known in Sanskrit as the Varṇārhavarṇa Bhagavato Buddhasya Stotra and in Tibetan as Sangs rgyas bcom ldan 'das la bstod pa bsngags par 'os pa bsngags pa, is a devotional hymn in verse composed by the Indian master Maticitra, better known as Mātṛceṭa, one of the most celebrated poets of early Indian Buddhism. Across thirteen chapters it extols the Buddha from many angles: his omniscience, his powers and fearlessness, the purity and unassailable truth of his speech, his kindness toward beings that can never be repaid, the beauty of his body, and, in its closing chapters, the weariness with existence his passing should arouse and a series of vivid poetic comparisons drawn from nature. The poet opens by confessing that he once squandered his words praising the unworthy, and offers this hymn as a way of washing his speech clean. The work is Toh 1138 in the Tengyur, where it belongs to the Praises (stotra) section, and its colophons record a translation by Sarvajñādeva and Paltsek, with a further translation credited to Padmākara and Rinchen Zangpo.

Homage to the Three Jewels.

Not knowing what is a worthy field and what is not,
I followed mere sounds,
and so I erred; and in the past
whatever praise I spoke of those not worthy of praise,

that which is soiled by the mud of speech,
O Sage, at the bathing ford from which merit flows,
relying on you, the one who has gone to bliss,
I now begin to wash thoroughly clean.

This is the cleanser that removes
the faults of whatever wrong
I, blinded by delusion,
committed against the supreme assembly.

Until I pass into nirvāṇa,
may I never fall away from the two paths of words:
your own speech,
and the telling of your qualities.

Having made just this talk my very essence,
since I have no other aim than this,
if I speak, may I speak only of this;
or else may I not speak at all.

Your qualities belong to you alone,
and your equal is found only in yourself;
therefore all other qualities
cannot stand in comparison with you.

If one reflects upon
the qualities of a buddha,
all other things that have reached perfection
become like mere impurity.

When there is no one at all like you,
what need is there to speak of one superior?
There is not even anyone
who falls only slightly short of you.

Since there is no example
by which anyone could describe you, Guide,
you have left no opening for example;
homage to you, the Incomparable.

Because the qualities of a buddha are beyond measure,
other persons than you,
even those who have attained a similar liberation,
differ from you very greatly.

Because they are alike merely in being empty,
who, not knowing the difference,
would compare the empty hollow of a hair pore
with the vast sky?

Who could count the countless?
Who could measure the measureless?
Your qualities, in number
and in power, cannot be estimated.

Like one who wanders
within a vast and boundless grove of wish-fulfilling trees,
I, among your good deeds,
am never sated and never find the end.

O ocean of the qualities of those worthy of offerings,
were your qualities as a tathāgata
to be taught in full,
not even the sky could contain them.

The one who served you in your very presence,
the general of the Buddha's Dharma,
the crown of all the hearers,
who proclaimed the profound after you,

when the Blessed One questioned him
on a matter belonging to omniscience,
replied, "I do not know";
thus the profundity of the Buddha's qualities is shown to be beyond even his knowing.

If even those foremost ones
find their powers exhausted before you,
how could a worm of impure existence like me
have any room to conceive of you?

You are a great sāla tree,
whose branches of qualities, bent low by compassion,
this person has merely
taken hold of a little.

When great masses of rain clouds
have let fall their streams of rain,
even if a swallow scorched by the summer heat drinks,
what does it take away?

Though four great rivers
flow down from Lake Anavatapta,
they cannot make it run dry;
what need to speak of other streams that flow from it?

The mines of jewels
are not exhausted by enriching the world;
Protector, the telling of your qualities too
I consider to be just like that.

With diligence, from your own teaching,
I have gathered the flowers of your qualities,
and as a tree with its own blossoms,
I offer flowers to you.

As a mine is honored with its own wealth,
as a mountain with its own pigments,
as sandalwood with its own fragrance,
as a lake with its own water,

as a gold-producing jewel
with the very gold that comes from itself,
so with qualities gathered from your own teaching
I worship you.

From the center of the sky the moonlight spreads in every direction
and all of it comes streaming down;
when the moonlight falling upon the earth opens them,
the groves of kumuda flowers do not fail to bloom.
Just so, in the endless ocean of the Victor's praises,
whose water is his immeasurable qualities,
by speaking even one portion of it, the person of faith
is not left without the fruit he longs for.

The words that speak the Buddha's praise bear fruit:
they are provisions for the journey through births, a path to happiness without obstacle,
they become the sublime vehicle, the water channel for the roots
of qualities such as faith, and much else besides.

Those whose faculties are wholly given over to faith
and who strive in the work of praising the Buddha,
the stream of Dharma that arises for them in each instant
no one but the omniscient can grasp even in part.

Our strength itself is subject to impermanence,
and this leisure and endowment is extremely hard to win;
those who do not strive in this work,
who could be more deceived than they?

From In Praise of the Praiseworthy, a Praise of the Buddha, the Blessed One, this is the first chapter, called Praise of Him Who Cannot Be Praised.

Blessed One, when you were present in the world,
I was wholly given over to the power of faith,
because you gazed upon me
with a buddha's eyes moistened by compassion.

Calling to mind
that very compassion of yours,
to you who are worthy of homage
I bow, as though you were before me.

How could anyone who is not omniscient
understand you, the Omniscient One?
Great and vast in your very being,
known by yourself alone, homage to you.

To say of you, "It is this, it is like this,"
is not within the range of one like me;
what you are and what you are like I cannot declare;
homage to one such as you.

Protector, you are like yourself alone;
as the victors are, so are you;
there is no one else at all;
homage to one such as you.

As you know yourself,
as you are known by those like you,
as others do not know you,
homage to one such as you.

As you yourself have made yourself known
through your qualities,
compassion, the ten powers and the rest,
homage to one such as you.

From this day onward,
having gathered the very essence
of the sources of your qualities from those qualities themselves,
as far as I am able, I bow to you.

Homage and reverence to you, the Arhat;
homage and reverence to you, worthy of offerings;
homage and reverence to you, the Wise One;
to you I bow down and pay homage.

The perfect Buddha is a jewel;
the jewel is the very nature of the Dharma;
the Saṅgha is a source of jewels;
to you, the gathering of the three jewels, I bow.

You, worthy of offerings, are a jewel;
the intent of your mind too is a jewel;
you are the source of the jewel of the true Dharma;
to you, a jewel in every way, I bow.

Having become supreme among all jewels,
you outshine every jewel;
to you, the source of all jewels,
the true jewel, I bow.

Homage to you, whose mind is a jewel;
homage to you, whose Dharma is a jewel;
homage to you, a jewel in every respect.

Having made familiar the meditation that ends existence,
you have become supreme among all sages;
to you, the arhat worthy of homage,
I bow with reverence.

You who have taken the way contrary to the world,
who abide at the very limit of reality,
who have reached the culmination of the ascetic's life,
unsurpassed one, homage to you.

Perfect in every aspect,
you outshine all other beauty,
and at all times bring delight;
to you, the lotus among buddhas, I bow.

Of lovely color and sweet fragrance,
beautiful to behold and unhurried,
perfect Buddha, white lotus
in full bloom, homage to you.

You who have thoroughly refined the very essence of bliss,
who have attained every touch you wish for,
the ascetic who brings forth happiness,
ever youthful one, homage to you.

Since your toil has borne its fruit,
you have attained the culmination entire;
ascetic who vowed to speak the sublime,
homage to you.

Relying on pure conduct, you know the Vedas
and all the limbs of the Vedas;
purified by knowledge and conduct,
true brahmin, homage to you.

The noble qualities were never taken from you;
your noble deeds are outstanding;
you carry out the work of the world's teacher;
Noble One, homage to you.

Your mind you made pure
by rightly taking up austerities and vows;
you have washed away all wrongdoing;
you who have bathed, homage to you.

You have attained supreme sovereignty
and outshine all beings;
you sound the lion's roar;
Lion, homage to you.

Leaving a wide and trampled track through speculative talk,
you keep to the high ground of the true Dharma,
the destroyer of all other doctrines;
Great Elephant, homage to you.

The path of the nature of reality is exceedingly hard;
none but you can bear it;
you made it clear and walked it,
and so became the path of the world; homage to you.

Gentle, thoroughly tamed and patient,
endowed with vigor, fine color and strength,
possessing a body complete in all its limbs,
thoroughbred one, homage to you.

You know the directions, the watering places and the pastures;
steady, you have no crooked gait;
you have attained the bull's own place;
Bull among men, homage to you.

Skilled in every means
of taming, free of laziness,
you hold the reins of persons to be tamed;
most excellent one, homage to you.

For if your teaching did not exist,
even the fortunate would find no purpose;
therefore to you, who have become
the pure conduct of all, homage.

Possessing every excellent quality
in utterly complete perfection,
you have brought to an end all striving
after virtuous qualities; homage to you.

Your own aim is completely fulfilled,
and you are wholly intent on the aims of others;
bestower of the nectar of the true Dharma,
you who bring satisfaction, I bow to you.

Afraid of many fears,
you left behind the shore that is not the far shore,
crossed well the great river,
and went to the far shore; I bow to you.

You dispel every disease of the afflictions
and draw out every thorn;
holder of spells, the accomplished one,
chief of physicians, I bow to you.

You know the path that is best and the path that is not,
and show the direction of peace;
caravan leader who brings forth
accomplishment and happiness, homage to you.

You always dispel the darkness,
you always open the eyes,
you always possess the quality of never forgetting;
fully awakened one, I bow to you.

Having surely washed away the mud of stains
with the stainless water of the true Dharma,
you are supremely purified;
utterly pure one, I bow to you.

You have abandoned both wrongdoing and merit;
your being is imbued with emptiness;
toward those who help and those who harm you are even;
utterly free of attachment, I bow to you.

Having surely cleared away all afflictions
and all habitual tendencies, your mind is stainless,
your liberation free of obscuration;
utterly liberated one, I bow to you.

You possess a form one never tires of beholding;
your body is radiantly beautiful with light;
among all things that may be seen
you are the loveliest to see; I bow to you.

Endowed with the thirty-two
major marks of a great being,
together with the host of excellent minor marks,
you who are worthy to be seen, I bow to you.

The unsurpassed qualities of a buddha,
the fearlessnesses, the powers and the rest,
you proclaimed aloud among beings;
you who are worthy of delight, I bow to you.

With the unsurpassed, pure, noble qualities
that are to be cultivated,
both your bodies are imbued;
you who are to be meditated upon, I bow to you.

Because the activities of your body, speech and mind
are thoroughly purified,
you have gone beyond the mere keeping of moral discipline;
you who are pure and clean, I bow to you.

Having abandoned the speech of the afflictions,
intent on the culmination of peaceful action,
you have accepted silence;
Sage at peace, I bow to you.

Your mind is not carried away
by pleasure or by pain; your mind is steady;
you outshine all and have become
the supreme essence of beings; I bow to you.

Toward all, in every way,
your steady understanding and your intentions
are well established, and so you possess
the quality of never forgetting; homage to you.

Rich in qualities beyond the world,
destroyer of the world's distractions,
you who bring light to the world,
Protector of the World, I bow to you.

Even toward friend, enemy and stranger,
intent on the benefit of all beings,
you who are ever loving,
Great Compassion, I bow to you.

Complete in every perception of the Dharma,
Protector endowed with the great superknowledges,
knowing what has been done and what has not been done,
Omniscient One, I bow to you.

Having abandoned every opening for attack,
having cut every bond,
you who make wisdom shine,

dispeller of craving, I bow to you.

Worthy to be pleased, worthy of homage,
not to be transgressed, without fault,
worthy to be worshipped with every effort,
possessor of the three knowledges, I bow to you.

Skilled in removing what does no good,
wholly intent on bringing forth benefit,
devoted single-mindedly to love,
sublime spiritual friend, I bow to you.

For the beings who have entered wrong paths,
you always strive
to turn them toward the sublime path;
sublime kinsman, I bow to you.

Foremost of those who know the true station,
you have attained the supreme station
and become the highest of two-footed beings;
there is no station higher than that.

Having attained the unsurpassed station,
you dispel all suffering;
free of attachment and without grasping,
highest of two-footed beings, I bow to you.

Without relying on your teaching
no one sees reality;
for the world, which is like the blind,
you have become the eye; I bow to you.

If there were no sun upon the earth,
though there were fire and lamps as well,
and though constellations, gems and the moon
were present, the light would not be complete.

Though that light, and others,
and lights far more excellent than that
were present, if you did not abide,
beings would have no light.

Thus for those in the world whose minds
are veiled by the thick cataract
of the black darkness of ignorance, and have grown dim,
you have become the lamp; I bow to you.

Because their understanding of the Dharma is unrefined,
they do not know benefit as benefit,
nor do they know harm as harm;
refinement too comes about by relying on you.

Childish beings for the most part
wish to cast off harm, yet they abandon benefit;
though they seek benefit, they do not know it,
and so they resort to one harm after another.

Because you show them the method,
by relying on you they come to understand
both of these correctly, just as they are;
you have become the physician; I bow to you.

Lord, the one above whom you stand
is himself the highest in the world;
therefore, above even the highest,
to you, the Highest, I bow.

Even those whom the world's wise worship
worship you, the very embodiment of trust;
to be worshipped by those worthy of worship,
sublime one worthy of worship, I bow to you.

Whoever is worthy of praise and is praised,
that person too praises you;
to you, who are to be praised
even by those worthy of praise, I bow.

The gods too bow to you;
the sages too bow to you;
to you, to whom even those worthy of homage
must bow, I bow.

What person with a mind,
paying homage to you, worthy of homage,
an immeasurable field of merit,
would ever be sated?

Therefore I pay homage and bow to you;
to you, worthy of bowing, I bow;
never breaking the stream of homage,
always I pay homage to you.

Ever practicing the sublime conduct, endowed with the path,
knowing what has been done and what has not, and what is attained, possessed of perfect discernment,
possessor of great offerings, worthy of homage, without equal,
arhat worthy of reverence, homage to you.

Homage to you, who possess an incomparable, delightful body.
Homage to you, who proclaim supremely melodious speech.
Homage to you, who possess a stainless, perfectly pure mind.
Homage to you, whose very being is a cultivated host of qualities.

To bow to the Buddha with the thought that he is a field,
stainless, is something not even a fraction of which
all auspicious things can match, nor all offerings and gifts,
nor austerities and vows, nor all the ritual baths.

To bow to the Lord of Sages
brings renown and makes one glorious;
it tears out all wrongdoing by the roots
and makes one's splendor increase;
it dispels all enemies;
for people in the world
it makes their continuum certain of the happy realms,
and in the end it ripens into the fruit of peace.

Thus, reflecting on its many qualities,
and reflecting too that the body is by nature perishing,
what wise person would undertake
any bodily act other than bowing to the Buddha?

When this teaching, which dispels
the Lord of Death, has faded away,
and everything is covered by darkness,
to whom will one show reverence?
Therefore hasten to perform
worship without equal;
the fears of the future
have already arrived, right now.

From In Praise of the Praiseworthy, a Praise of the Buddha, the Blessed One, this is the second chapter, called Homage.

Kinsman of the Sun, whose light
is far more luminous than the sun's,
foremost and supreme of all to whom homage is due,
worthy of homage, I pay homage and bow to you.

Of great strength, a great hero,
of great superknowledge and great power,
you who loose great arrows on the field of battle,
with a great festival I worship you.

Worthy of the world's worship, supreme among the worshipful,
worshipped with the worship of the world,
unsurpassed vessel of worship,
you who are worthy of worship, I worship you.

Through worship and knowledge you attained realization;
your mind has not a trace of pride;
to you, who are worthy of honor, I set aside
all prideful thoughts and give you honor.

Even toward those who revere you and those who do not,
you whom the world worships with reverence,
Indra, devoted to honoring you,
reverently pays you honor.

There is no one at all like you;
none superior has arisen, nor ever will;
therefore toward all beings you are
the unequalled one, even toward foe and friend.

Born from the unmixed noble lineage,
equal in fortune to all the buddhas,
with the radiance of your complexion and your wisdom
you were born, illuminating every direction.

Endowed with the thirty-two marks,
with rays of light shining a fathom around,
with the eighty excellent minor marks that accord with the marks,
blazing with splendor,

your body possessing the glory of a great king
outshines the beauty of all;
illuminating the world,
you abide like a second sun.

Blessed One, the dharmas understood in all times,
all the sources of every kind,
like a myrobalan fruit in the palm of the hand,
have become the domain of your mind.

Toward dharmas, the moving and the unmoving,
the one and the manifold, each in its own way,
as the wind moves through space,
your mind meets no obstruction.

Just as for you, who accomplish what is to be done,
by the mere arising of a thought
everything is accomplished as you wish,
so have you fully comprehended the dharmadhātu.

For you, who have reached the culmination of virtue,
there is no virtue born of effort;
whatever you may wish,
it depends on the wishing alone.

The being of dharmas as dharmas,
the nature of reality, and the power of dharmas,
each abiding together with its causes,
together with its conditions and its results,
with their elements and their various divisions,
with their arising and their ceasing, in all their breadth:
all of this, each one, in every aspect,
you, the Omniscient One, know, and no other.

To you even the deeply hidden is manifest;
even the very far is near;
even the most tangled is plain and clear;
even the very high is low;
even the deepest dark is filled with light;
even where great doubt remains, it is evident;
even the most profound of things to be known
lies plain before you.

Just as for great Indra's elephant
a girth made of gold is no burden,
so even the most profound nature of reality
does not weary you.

As freedom from attachment is among dharmas,
as straightness is among the ways of deliverance,
as a buddha is among the excellent,
so you are supreme among all beings.

As the unshakable is among liberations,
as the nature of reality is among reasonings,
as omniscience is among the kinds of wisdom,
so you are supreme among all embodied beings.

As liberation is among purities,
as emptiness is among the truths,
as patience is among austerities,
so you are supreme among all living creatures.

As your body is among forms,
as your mind is among minds,
as your Dharma is among teachings,
so you are supreme among all living creatures.

From In Praise of the Praiseworthy, a Praise of the Buddha, the Blessed One, this is the third chapter, called The Establishment of Omniscience.

Sugata, you alone,
the one Dharma that illuminates all dharmas,
possess the knowledge of all aspects;
it is yours and no one else's.

By your wisdom alone
all that can be known is pervaded;
for all others besides you,
the knowable alone remains beyond them.

"From this cause and condition comes this result;
from this, that does not come":
all such certainty of what is possible and what is impossible
you know, and no other.

The ripening of actions rightly undertaken,
in all its times,
and all the realizations of every level,
in every aspect you know, and no other.

All the levels of yoga without exception,
the establishment of the afflicted
and of the purified, together with their profound depths,
you alone have fully understood.

Faculties that are keen and faculties that are not,
the lesser, the middling and the great,
with their endless distinctions of every part,
none but you has understood.

Whatever comes into accord
and whatever comes into discord,
all the varied inclinations of people,
you alone have fully understood.

This world has various elements;
all the various elements without exception,
the divisions of the various elements, you alone
know, and no other.

The various accomplishments
that arise from various causes and conditions,
all these, through the power that knows the paths leading everywhere,
you have fully understood.

What has arisen in beginningless existence,
and what will arise without end,
to all this your wisdom
gains entry as if seeing it directly.

The former limit and the later limit,
the coming into being of the world,
and likewise the linking of rebirth,
you see as though they were between the limits.

Whether it be near or far,
just as it is directly evident to you,
so too have you abandoned the obscurations
together with their habitual tendencies.

"This thing is not fully known,
and of this affliction the habitual tendency
still remains": that one cannot say this,
that is the station you have attained.

Whatever you declared to be obstacles
are surely obstacles;
whatever path you have taught,
that alone surely leads to deliverance.

"That cannot obstruct,"
or "That does not lead to deliverance":
not even a fragment of such objection from others
could possibly apply to it.

There is nothing you were to conquer that you have not conquered;
there is nothing knowable you have not brought to light;
you, the Lord, are the one who knows all
and has conquered all, and no other.

Since you possess the powers and are not given to fear,
it is fitting that you are never daunted;
but that, though possessing such might,
you hold no one in contempt, that is a marvel.

That you have freed from their arrogance
some who were full of false pride
was only a means of taming them;
in it there was no pride in your strength.

What in the world is said of the powerful,
that they are "victorious over all,"
is said of others by way of praise;
that station is fully realized in you.

Guide, no one is able
to challenge you in accordance with the Dharma;
you are not one in whom faults may be sought;
you have found the ease of being faultless.

Being without fault, you need not
guard the four from anyone;
because these four are wholly complete,
no one can find an opening against you.

Your understanding is unimpeded toward all;
your attention is ever closely present;
your compassion is equal toward all;
your mind is attached to nothing.

You have crossed the whole ocean of the knowable;
you have abandoned all habitual tendencies for good;
you know the distinctions of every obstacle;
you are skilled in every way of deliverance.

You know the dispositions of all;
you have gone to the far shore of the means of taming;
and in teaching the antidotes to all the various afflictions
you are never daunted.

Those childish talkers who, though relying on you,
have not gained confidence,
that is the fearsome power
of the thicket of the darkness of ignorance.

Whatever, even relying on your word,
has not been removed,
each such thing is called an obscuration,
and those obscurations are not yet exhausted.

From In Praise of the Praiseworthy, a Praise of the Buddha, the Blessed One, this is the fourth chapter, called Praise of the Powers and Fearlessnesses.

Hearing your word, O Sage,
which tells of the faults of existence, beings grow weary of it;
but though they blaze on every side
in the fire of saṃsāra's unbearable suffering,
beings clad in the armor of unknowing
do not grow weary in that way.

Thus, Protector, your word
makes its meaning utterly clear, even to direct experience;
and since by its explanation the profound
and deep-bottomed nature of reality becomes plain,
your word performs
the miracles of reversal as well.
