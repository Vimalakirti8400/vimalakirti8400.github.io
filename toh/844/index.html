About this text

The Root Tantra of Accomplishment through Worship and Praise of the World, known in Tibetan as Jikten Chötö Drubpa Tsawé Gyü and given a Sanskrit title that the Tibetan transmits in an irregular form beginning Lokastotrapūja Tantra, is Toh 844 in the Kangyur, where it belongs to the collection of Ancient, or Nyingma, Tantras. It is a tantra spoken by the Blessed One Vajradhara, the great secret embodying the body, speech and mind of all the tathāgatas, who in the palace of Akaniṣṭha tames the haughty and powerful chieftains of the world, such as the māras, yamas, nāgas, yakṣas, king spirits and tsen, and binds them to protect the teaching. Across eleven chapters it sets out how these worldly spirits are empowered, entrusted with their duties, and propitiated through offerings and praise, describing the signs of successful practice in experience and dreams, the substances and supports proper to each class, and the rites they are charged to carry out. It closes by reminding its audience that these beings are not truly separate from awakened mind, and that the practice culminates in the dharmakāya beyond all dualistic grasping.

In the Tibetan language: The Root Tantra of the Practice of Worldly Offering and Praise

Homage to the Blessed One Vajradhara.

Thus did I hear at one time. The Blessed One Vajradhara was dwelling in the palace of the king of the gods in the abode of Akaniṣṭha. He is the great secret, the embodiment of the body, speech, and mind of all the tathāgatas. He perfectly accomplishes every last enlightened activity of the body, speech, and mind of each and every buddha of the three times. He strikes terror through his might, strength, fearlessness, mastery, and form, and he swiftly tames the ferocious, the violent, the arrogant, the powerful, and those who are hard to withstand. He was drawn forth from suchness, the heart essence of the mind of all the sugatas. He is the chief bodhisattva great being, who has fully entered the great ground of final culmination; the great lord of the mighty families of gods belonging to the desire realm and the form realm; and the great leader of the yakṣas of the abode of Aḍakavatī and the abode of Mount Meru.

Various light rays, the magical display of the body, speech, and mind of the buddhas who abide throughout the three times, were sent forth, and he was exhorted with these words:

O Lord of Secrets, Vajradhara!
You were empowered by all the buddhas
To guard the sacred teaching
As lord of might and strength.
Look to that great pledge (samaya),
And from the secret of body, speech, and mind,
With the supreme form that terrifies the terrifying,
Together with fierce speech,
Unleash your force with a ferocious mind;
And, to tame all who are hard to tame, without exception,
Through emanations, magical displays, and enlightened activities,
Please enact the play of your former vow!

As soon as he had been exhorted in this way, the Blessed One knew that the time had come to perform the activity of the buddhas. Although his own body, speech, and mind did not stir from the peaceful nature of reality just as it is, he looked upon the violent ones who were the objects of his taming, and throughout every world system of the ten directions, reaching as far as the limits of space, there appeared inconceivable magical displays of his body, the form of a great wrathful one, as follows.

His body is dark blue, and he has nine faces. The faces on the right are dark brown, baring their fangs; the faces on the left are dark maroon, their features drawn tight; the central faces are dark blue, wrathful and fearsome. With the blazing vajra in his right hand he stirs the skull cup of blood in his left, and drinks. In his eight lower right hands he holds the severed heads of the great gods he has liberated, and in his eight lower left hands he holds the heads of the eight great nāgas; these too are complete with their flayed hides, the bases of fortune. His nine right eyes are rolled upward, his nine left eyes gaze sideways, and his nine central eyes glare downward in anger. Fierce wrathful furrows are gathered on his brow. From his nostrils the fierce wind of existence storms forth; from his mouths blazes the fire of the eon's end. His red fangs are bared up into the realm of space. His mustache and beard gather like clouds. The dark brown locks of his hair blaze and coil up to the peak of existence. From his ears comes a great and vicious roar. His limbs are wound about with clusters of snakes. He wears as his garments the liberated māras and rudras and the hide of an elephant, and a tiger skin is wrapped around his lower body. He is adorned with dots and streaks of blood and grease, and his body is smeared with charnel ash. He is adorned with garlands of skulls, dry and fresh, dripping with flesh and blood. With his eight feet he tramples Brahmā, Viṣṇu, Maheśvara (Śiva), and Śakra (Indra), together with their consorts. He is in union with a great consort, supremely ferocious and terrifying, whose form matches his own.

Countless and beyond all telling, tens of millions of these came to be, like a full sesame pod, and all of them uttered the fierce sound of speech oṃ vajra caṇḍa sarva duṣṭāntaka hana daha paca hūṃ phaṭ. From each one of them, too, he caused inconceivable emanations, and emanations of those emanations, to appear.

The arrogant ones of the world systems, namely the hosts of gods, the māras, yamas, rākṣasas, yakṣas, planetary māras, nāgas, king spirits, ging spirits, the great mātṛkās, the hosts of ḍākinīs, the rudras, the tīrthikas, the lords of life, the lords of wealth, the lords of longevity, the lords of mantra, and the lords of existence; and furthermore the arrogant ones of the world who dwell in all the world systems, in the great charnel grounds, in the great lands, in the borderlands of Jambudvīpa, on the kings of mountains and on the small hills, in the oceans and the streams, in the pārijāta trees, in the great forests, and in inconceivably many minor places, abiding in male and female forms, chiefs and retinues and especially those who roam, together with their hosts of servants and attendants, came forth from their own abodes in haste, alarmed, with minds full of fear and dread, and with minds subdued. Like tufts of cotton tossed by the force of a fierce wind, they arrived helplessly before the Blessed One.

The arrogant ones dwelling throughout all the world systems of the ten directions, and in particular in the Sahā world system, in the four continents, in the great ocean together with the nāga abodes of the pleasure waters, on great Mount Meru, among the hosts of yakṣas of the seven mountains, in the abodes of the gods in the desire realm, together with the form realm and the formless realm, in the hells, among the hungry ghosts, in the birthplaces of animals, and on the lands of Jambudvīpa, each saw, wherever he was, a great fierce form of immeasurable splendor, which others could not approach, present before him. All of them, each separately, went for refuge to their own great chieftains. The great chieftains, together with their retinues, went for refuge to that Lord of Secrets in his form of great terror. All of them displayed, without exception, their own forms, wealth, retinues, ornaments, mounts, abodes, comforts, and particular activities, and, bringing forth the heart essences of their names, they spoke:

"mahādeva ejaḥ umadeva ejaḥ dhudra maduphrag jaḥ dunti madu jaḥ." With these words the great lord of the world, Maheśvara (Śiva), with his consort, together with the male and female māras, each offered his own name.

Then the leader of the yamas, together with his own host, offered each his name together with his life, saying: "hrīḥ ṣṭi kriṣṇa yama preta jaḥ mama yamuntri bhitri jaḥ."

Then the chieftain of the rākṣasas offered the heart essence of the names of himself and his retinue: "rakṣa raśaramati rakṣa maśuti jaḥ."

Then the chieftain of the yakṣas offered the heart essence of the names and lives of himself and his retinue: "yakṣa kuberakṣa tame yakṣi maśa jaḥ."

Then the chieftain of the worldly yakṣa gods, the Lord of the Snowy Mountains (Himavat), offered his own name: "gaṃ bhabhatra dheva taruti jaḥ."

Then the leader of the minor kings of the world, the lord of the thirteen divine the'u spirits, offered his name: "gaṃ tapaśali bartri saṃkaraśa jaḥ."

Then the chieftain of the general māras, together with his own retinue, spoke the heart essence of their names, offering: "du spraśaṃ ja akaru kaṃ ca jaḥ."

Then the mistress of the hosts accomplished from wisdom and from karma and of the samaya-bound, She Who Gathers Existence under Her Power, together with Ekajaṭī and all the hosts of ḍākinīs, offered each the heart essence of her name: "mama trag mutra raca ekajvaṭi ruru ro tiṣṭa bhatto retu mati kha muntra paca lameśa jaḥ."

Then the mighty gods of the world and the great leaders of the rudra class offered each the heart essence of his name: "deva abhiśutri rutra niji haṃ śaṃ tri jaḥ."

Then the king of the obstructors of the world, the tīrthikas, and the hosts of māras and mu spirits offered each the heart essence of his name: "vināyaka tirthi taka du mun mun paḥ jaḥ."

Then the great chieftains of the hosts of the lord of life of the world, of Vajrin, lord of the yakṣas and nāgas, of the great king of the vital soul, and of Mahākāla, lord among the gods, offered each the heart essence of his name: "giṅkara tri pale rāja bajra buru lu ja bajra la rutri ja mahākāla yakṣi jaḥ."

Then the great chieftains of the nāgas and of the planets and stars of the world brought forth the life essence of their names and offered: "nāga cita huya nāga me kṣetra hula ghuhe paśa mike hūṃ jaḥ."

Moreover, the haughty and savage lords of the world realms, together with their retinues, offered each their bodies together with their lives: "e anri jaḥ samaya srog breng breng jaḥ jaḥ."

Then the Blessed One revived those haughty ones and their retinues, granted them fearlessness, and spoke these words:

"Alas, you hosts of the world's renowned ones,
Do not be afraid of me.
I am the lord of the enlightened activity
Of the body, speech, and mind of all the buddhas.
Through the compassion and the wisdom
Of the great peace that is the nature of reality,
The three times, the conditioned and the unconditioned,
Are vividly manifest in a single instant.
For the sake of beings yet to come,
I display the secret of various bodies.
You chieftains of the haughty ones as well,
From beginningless time until now,
Have fallen under the power of affliction and delusion;
Through the habitual tendencies of your negative states,
You act unvirtuously with body, speech, and mind.
The suffering of saṃsāra flows on unbroken,
And, having fallen into the great hells,
You may never find a time of liberation.
Therefore, at this supreme present time,
As I and all the buddhas
Bring body, speech, and mind, qualities,
And the activities of our deeds to full fruition,
Listen to the words of our command.
Make your karma and your existence meaningful,
And cast off your unvirtuous habitual tendencies.
Take up the foundations of the training, give rise to the mind of awakening,
And with your might, power, ability, and ferocity,
Act in accordance with the Buddha's teaching.
Protect, as the vidyādharas command.
You who, as kings and lords,
Or bound in body in the manner of servants and attendants,
Are asked for accomplishments: carry out the activities.
If you do not act in this way,
And transgress the secret word,
The continuum of your body, mind, and life
Will be destroyed this very moment."

Having declared this, he bound them by a sacred oath and established them in it.

This is the first chapter, the introduction, on fully taming the hosts of worldly chieftains.

Fierce in activity, who liberate the ten fields,
To the hosts of the thirty chieftains I bow.

Next I shall explain this thoroughly.
All these hosts of the haughty ones and their retinues
Are in essence the self-aware mind of awakening,
Never stirring from the unborn expanse.

It is divided into outer, inner, and secret.
All the maṇḍalas of appearance and existence
Together with the Lord of Secrets himself:

The assembly of one's own appearances gathered together,

Which make the other side appear as nondual,
Giving pleasure in their own state,
And the definitive display of the marks and signs of each:
This is the inner.
The assembly of mind and what is displayed to mind
Is all, from the very beginning, the great maṇḍala,
An unfabricated nature, spontaneously present.
As all the scriptures without exception teach,
There is no other dharma: the object itself
And the self of appearance that grasps it as object
Are realized and understood as nondual.
This is the utterly secret offering and praise.
Emanating and accomplishing from the body is the body;
The wheel of the sound of the ten syllables is the speech;
The self-liberated, unfabricated essence is the mind.
Gods, nāgas, yakṣas, tsen spirits, and māras,
