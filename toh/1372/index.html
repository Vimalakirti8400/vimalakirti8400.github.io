About this text

The Instruction on the Six-Branch Yoga, known in Tibetan as Jorwa Yenlak Drukgi Mengak and probably corresponding to a Sanskrit Ṣaḍaṅgayoga-upadeśa, is a short verse manual of completion-stage practice in the Kālacakra tradition. Opening with homage to the holy gurus and to Kālacakra, it sets out for initiated practitioners the six branches of withdrawal, meditative concentration, breath control, retention, recollection, and samādhi, giving for each its object, time, posture, gaze, signs, and results, from the appearance of empty forms to the attainment of immutable bliss and awakening, and it closes with brief instructions for the intermediate state and for practitioners of passion. Its colophon attributes it to Kālacakrapāda as an oral instruction of the whispered lineage, translated into Tibetan by the Indian paṇḍita Somanātha and the translator Dro Sherab Drak. It is Toh 1372 in the Tengyur, where it belongs among the commentaries and practical instructions on the tantras.

Translation

Herein is the Instruction on the Six-Branch Yoga.

Homage to the glorious holy gurus and to glorious Kālacakra.

Having paid homage to Kālacakra,
I shall explain the six-branch yoga.
Withdrawal and meditative concentration,
Breath control, likewise retention,
Recollection and samādhi:
As the six branches of yoga
These the supreme Buddha has constantly taught.
In each of them, six points are to be known.
The best realize it through the first;
The middling, through breath control and the rest;
The least cultivate all six in sequence.

One who has first received entering as a child,
The vase, the secret, and the wisdom,
And then has received the fourth empowerment,
Who possesses the root and branch pledges,
Such a one should dwell in solitude,
And cultivate withdrawal in this way.

The cultivation: by cultivating emptiness;
The cessation: one makes the winds cease;
The arising: bliss arises of itself;
The emergence: the signs of the path actually emerge.
Its nature is emptiness,
Gathering in all individual conceptions.
Its divisions are taught to be ten.
It is held to be sense direct perception.
Its aggregate is that of gnosis, and it is wisdom.

Doing it on a soft cushion:
Straighten the body and sit in the vajra posture;
Turn back the tongue, draw in the belly, turn the eyes upward;
This is the gaze of turning toward the crown protuberance.
Bind the two hands in the vajra fist;
By this the winds are bound.
Placing the life wind in the drop at the forehead,
Meditate at the time of the right-hand wind.
At night, in a place with a roof,
Focus on space and meditate on emptiness;
Not at dusk, at dawn, or at midnight.
By day, in a place without a roof,
Gaze into the sky, in the morning and the afternoon,
Not into the sun's rays, nor at midday.
By meditating thus, objects and the rest are blocked;
Inwardly, with the divine eye and the rest,
One sees empty forms, together with their supports.
As qualities, the ten perfections abide;
As deities, they are called the ten goddesses.
As signs on the path, ten are seen:
Smoke, mirage, appearances in space,
And a lamp appear, as the winds of the elements dissolve;
The last is the cloudless sky:
The perfection of wisdom bearing signs.
Blaze, moon, sun, lightning, Rāhu,
And the drop: these six are the latter.
All of these are seen as causes and bear signs:
The five elements, the five wisdoms, the five aggregates.
From the Guhyasamāja and the Nāmasaṃgīti, half and half.
Emanation body and enjoyment body are without conception.
The bliss of the rising of the vajra occurs.
Through meditating, at the time, a black line;
Seen within it are empty forms.
By this one comes to realize empty form.
Having entered absorption in withdrawal,
One blesses all mantras,
And accomplishes truth of speech;
One becomes free of grasped objects.

The second, the branch of meditative concentration,
Is done in order to stabilize empty form.
Its nature and its object are empty form;
Its time is when the space wind flows.
Its divisions are said to be five.
The gaze is the same; the mudrā
Is that of equipoise; it is mental direct perception.
The cultivation: on the forms arisen by the force of the former,
One rests the mind, gazing into space.
As the elements dissolve upward in sequence,
Whatever is seen, earth and the rest, is like an illusion;
One sees no difference between equipoise and post-meditation.
The bliss of attachment to concentration arises;
Urged on by the goddesses, one obtains prophecy.
The mind's entry into emptiness is wisdom;
The mind's holding to things is examination;
Engaging in holding that is analysis;
Resting the whole mind is joy;
Bliss is perfect, unwavering bliss.
It has the nature of space, and is consciousness.
If one enters absorption in concentration,
One attains the higher knowledges.
These two are virtuous at the beginning;
Since they realize empty form, they are called approach.

Then, having seen empty form,
In order to generate immutable bliss,
One should cultivate the branch of breath control.
Pressing one foot with the other,
Tuck the thumbs inside and clench the fists.
Its object is the wind of the three tips;
Its aggregate is formations; its time, when the wind flows;
The gaze is that of Amṛtakuṇḍalin.
First, expelling the wind outward,
Then inwardly, with the swift, the gathering,
The stiffening, and the joining with mantra recitation,
With the vase breath and the finest particle,
Having fully drawn the two winds into the center,
One should fully pacify the ten winds.
The bliss of the life wind entering the vajra fire
Will certainly arise.
By striving for six months,
Each day ten winds come to cease,
And twenty-one thousand six hundred cease.
By this the two winds meet at the navel.
Through the blazing of the wisdom caṇḍālī,
The ten buddha goddesses awaken,
And the bliss accompanied by the seminal fluid arises.
Above and below, the two places grow warm.
Without eating there is no hunger; even if one eats, it digests.
The downward-clearing wind does not leak; bliss trickles down.
The great magical display of the three vajras;
Lightness of body and the rest, nonconceptual bliss:
This is called mental direct perception.
Through life force and exertion, certainly,
One is beheld by the buddhas and bodhisattvas.

In order fully to stabilize wind and mind,
One should cultivate the branch of retention.
Its object is the wind of bliss itself;
It is retention because it holds firmly.
Its aggregate is feeling; it abides in the central channel.
The gaze and the rest are as before;
The two hands are bound; the time is that of space.
As soon as the wind enters the dhūtī,
Not letting it travel along the channels, place it in the drop.
Into the four drops, of the body and the rest,
Gradually and all at once, wind and mind
Are surely inserted and made unmoving.
When they become unmoving and stable there,
The bliss of attachment to the drop arises.
Even if one enters union with any woman,
The drop will not fall.
The body shows bliss arising, and the body becomes stable.
That too is mental direct perception.
Through the power of constant retention,
Vajrasattva truly enters.
One cannot be overcome by the Lord of Death and the rest.
Going and coming are brought to an end,
And the afflictions and the rest are brought to an end.
One enters into the ten powers:
Long life, freedom from illness, balanced elements.
These two are virtuous in the middle;
They are explained as near accomplishment,
And taught as the conduct of fierce austerity.

Then, in order to increase bliss,
One practices the branch of recollection.
Its object is bliss itself:
It is the recollection of bliss in one's continuum.
Its time is when the wisdom wind flows.
The gaze is the gaze of the deity;
Its aggregate is perception; the little finger is placed.
Its divisions are three: by the force of meditation,
By the hand, and by union with a woman.
Circling, one brings down the awakening mind from the place at the forehead,
And holds it firmly at the vajra jewel,
Understanding this through the example of mercury.
From above to below, a single vajra;
By directing wind and mind in this way,
Sixteen arise within the four joys.
This is the waxing fortnight, the mark of great passion.

The examples are cotton, the tiger, and the bé tree:
With elaboration, without elaboration, and utterly without.
By abandoning the habitual tendencies of emission,
The three obscurations are purified.
Passion should be thoroughly applied.
By meditating on its meaning, great bliss,
The innate, will arise.
The six higher knowledges also greatly increase,
One sees the two kinds of form body directly,
And will certainly obtain prophecy.
It is held to be reflexive direct perception.
For one absorbed in recollection,
A maṇḍala of light will appear.
Moreover, it comes from the wisdom body.
It is called accomplishment.

Then, in order to accomplish immutable bliss,
One should certainly cultivate samādhi.
Its nature is the nonduality of bliss and emptiness;
It is dwelling one-pointedly in that.
Its aggregate is that of the sphere of form, and so on;
Its time is when the wisdom wind flows.
The gaze is the peaceful gaze of the buddhas;
The hands are in the mudrā of samādhi.
The cultivation: empty form with support,
And bliss without support, being of one taste,
The mind abides unmoving and stable.
All things dawn as bliss.
Glorious immutable bliss arises,
And one truly sees the buddhas directly.
For one who has become stable in that,
The drop is reversed upward and goes to the crown.
Having attained the essential nature of the vajra jewel,
The dharmakāya, the saṃbhogakāya,
And the nirmāṇakāya are attained at the four places.
The four vajras, the liberations,
And the four awakenings are also realized.
From the secret place to the crown, the winds,
Through the cessation of one thousand eight hundred,
The twelve grounds are attained in sequence,
And the sixteen bodies are purified.
The drop at the crown, the particle at the jewel:
This is called nonabiding nirvāṇa.
All qualities are perfected.
Merely through mastery of samādhi,
One becomes free of all obscurations,
And becomes pure, without stain.
It is taught as the aggregate of form.
It is explained as great accomplishment,
And is held to be yogic direct perception.
These two are held to be virtuous at the end.

Thus, through the six branches,
In three years and three fortnights,
One becomes like a buddha;
This is also held to be the grounds and their attainment.
By purifying the six elements, the six wisdoms;
The six families and the six perfections are held;
The six higher knowledges and the six recollections;
The six winds and supreme wisdom are taught.
With the key points of the body raised, the mudrā is clear.
Then, by performing the three conducts,
The four pure bodies and wisdom are accomplished,
And the powers and all the other qualities, complete,
Will certainly come in this very life.

This is the instruction for awakening in the intermediate state:
Drive the lower wind out through the upper aperture.
Those who have the instruction of the six yogas,
Even if they do not meditate, accomplish it in seven lives.
If the signs of withdrawal have appeared,
And one should then die,
One will meet with the sublime path,
And after that will attain accomplishment.

The instruction of the six yogas for the passionate:
Bringing down, holding, and reversing,
Conveying to the places, fixing the focus on the channels,
And mixing the winds: these six yogas are to be practiced.
The practice of withdrawal
Should be done intensively and in due measure.
In concentration, meditate intensively on empty form.
Holding the wind through forceful yoga,
Do it to the point short of the head aching.
Hold wind and mind intensively in the drop.
With passion, unite with many action seals,
And make great bliss of one taste.
"Without this, the supreme is not accomplished,"
So it is taught; therefore one must surely accomplish this.
Conceptual meditation is taught to be worldly cycling:
What use are the generation stage and the like?
So taught Kālacakrapāda.

The Instruction on the Six-Branch Yoga, the oral instruction of the whispered lineage composed by Kālacakrapāda, is complete. It was translated by the paṇḍita Somanātha and the translator Dro Sherab Drak.

Translator's notes

The line "From the Guhyasamāja and the Nāmasaṃgīti, half and half" is terse and its reference is unclear; it is rendered literally. In the list of the five limbs of concentration, spyod pa ("conduct") is read as dpyod pa, "analysis," as the series wisdom, examination, analysis, joy, and bliss requires. The obscure word gda' in the list of breathing methods has been taken as mantra recitation; the meaning is uncertain. The opening line naming cotton, the tiger, and the bé tree as examples for the three kinds of conduct is obscure, and the identity of the bé tree is uncertain. "Three years and three fortnights" reads zla phyed gsum as three half-months, in keeping with the Kālacakra tradition; read as a numeral it could mean two and a half months. Rigs drug is rendered "six families" because the context is the purified correlates of the six elements, not the six classes of beings. Kun rdzob ("conventional") is read as kun rdzogs, "complete." The line "With the key points of the body raised, the mudrā is clear" is terse and its exact sense is uncertain.
