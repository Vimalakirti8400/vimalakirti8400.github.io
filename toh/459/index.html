About this text

Toh 459 in the Kangyur is a tantric scripture known in Sanskrit as the Śrī Vajra Caṇḍa Citta Guhya Tantra Uttara and in Tibetan as the Dpal rdo rje gtum po thugs gsang ba'i rgyud phyi ma, rendered in English as the Glorious Subsequent Secret Mind Tantra of Vajra Caṇḍa. It belongs to the class of tantric texts and takes the form of a revelation unfolding in response to a request by Śakra, lord of the gods, who asks the Buddha to disclose the supreme secret of body, speech, and mind. The text centers on the fierce deity Vajra Caṇḍa, a wrathful tantric figure depicted as a vast, dark-blue goddess of terrifying aspect, and it unfolds teachings on empowerment and the hidden dimensions of awakened mind. As a "subsequent" or "uttara" tantra, it is understood to be a continuation of an earlier tantra associated with the same deity, situating it within a broader cycle of Vajra Caṇḍa practice literature preserved among the tantric texts of the Kangyur.

Translation

In the language of India: Śrī Vajra Caṇḍa Citta Guhya Tantra Uttara. In the language of Tibet: The Glorious Subsequent Secret Mind Tantra of Vajra Caṇḍa. Homage to the Glorious Vajra Caṇḍa.

Then Śakra (Indra), lord of the gods, made this request: O Blessed One, please be so gracious as to reveal the supreme secret of the empowerment that is the supreme teaching, as it truly is, of the secret body, speech, and mind of the Blessed One himself.

When this request had been made, from within the state of the perfectly purified dharmadhātu came the utterance: Mahā Caṇḍa Krodhī Hūṃ. And thereupon, to the left, the great wrathful goddess Caṇḍa, vast and dark blue, bearing a terrifying aspect, with hair wild and upswept, her red eyes wide open and glaring, gnashing her fangs, furrowing her brows in fury, possessed of three eyes, her right hand clasping a vajra bell to the principal consort in an embrace, her left hand pressing a skull-bowl of blood to the principal consort's breast, both feet entwined around the principal in the manner of delight, with the sounds of Hī Hī in delight and Hā La, causing all to tremble and be overwhelmed, adorned with charnel-ground ornaments within a mass of fire, she abides in the manner of being entwined around the body.

Then the Blessed One in great bliss, resting in the meditative absorption of bliss, with the Hūṃ and Oṃ present in his own sky, together with a jewel and a vajra, the Phaṭ into the hollow at the tip of the vajra, the mother's secret place endowed with the patra, together with a lotus and a pistil — from the mother arises the solar maṇḍala.

This is the first chapter of the Subsequent Secret Mind Tantra of the Glorious Vajra Caṇḍa, called The Magical Display of the Mother and the Chapter on the Thorough Endowment of the Secret Sky.

Then the lord of the gods made this request: Blessed One, please explain what the perfection of bliss is. When this was asked, the Blessed One entered into the meditative absorption of great bliss-perfection and declared what is called the wheel of bliss-perfection:

Hear, O supreme lord of the gods!
The thirty-two wheels that sustain bliss
abide like conch-shell and jasmine flower.
The indestructible Hūṃ gazes downward.
At the great throat, the enjoyment, with sixteen petals;
in the center, a syllable Maṃ like liquid gold.
Ya, Ba, Ra, La in four colors,
dwelling on the four petals in the four directions.
The Dharma wheel of eight petals —
Hūṃ and Muṃ in the center, at the intermediate directions
Oṃ Krodhī Ha Māṃ Lāṃ Pāṃ Tāṃ.
The emanation wheel of sixty-four,
with Oṃ white in the center, and likewise the rest.
The fire wheel, triangular, Raṃ;
the wind, bow-like, bearing a banner,
endowed with Ja, Nya, Phaṭ, and Raṃ.

Through the union of descent and circulation,
for the supreme vessel of highest capacity:
draw up with the tongue and ring-finger, guide with the breath.
The wisdom consort consecrated by blessing,
entrusted to one's own body, endowed with method —
bliss, and great bliss,
vast great bliss, and supreme bliss.

This is the second chapter of the Subsequent Secret Mind Tantra of the Glorious Vajra Caṇḍa, called the chapter on the Wheel of Bliss-Perfection.

Then the lord of the gods made this request: Blessed One, please grant teachings on the power of blessing and the power of mantras for the benefit of sentient beings.

When this was asked, the Blessed One declared: Hear, O lord of the gods! In the past, all the Tathāgatas granted me empowerment as the holder of the secret treasury. Now I shall explain it to you. This is what destroys all nāgas:

Nī Ma Sa Slā La Naṃ. Nāga Kuna Zhi. Citta Muliṅ Naṃ. Cast it back! Nāga Shig! Nāga Zlog! Nāga Byer! Nāga Daha Daha Māraya Phaṭ! Nāga Hana Hana! Māraya Phaṭ!

This mantra, of the secret holder of the secret treasury, destroys the nāgas. The substances to be employed are as follows: the root and the protector herb together, black broom-grass, grease, two-headed idrā and magadha earth, the fragrance of earth, and the five varieties — these destroy every poison. Pure fat, great grease, antler-shavings, great fragrance, scented water, and peacock brains are for anointing and for fumigation. Muzī, costus, great fragrance, refined great substance, and scented water combined: poured over the torma, all spirits are spontaneously pacified. This protects oneself.

Nāga śatidun jib. Nāga citanaṃ. Nāga nirarbu. Śāna tiṅśa durtsub. Nāga araliśam. Nāga dundun. Gyen gyen. Nāga śaṃti. Ya ya. Tshub tshub dusharbu.

This is the third chapter of the Latter Tantra of Glorious Vajra the Fierce, the Secret Mind Treasury, entitled "Destroying All Nāgas."

Then the Blessed One entered into the meditative absorption called "Binding the Three Worlds" and proclaimed the mantra that binds the world:

Om ekayama hekayama hrimayama sarva citta gzerthum vighnaṃ.

The instant he spoke these words, from the pinnacle of existence down to the lowest hell, all minds were utterly seized, and bodies were bound motionless as carvings.

Then he entered into the meditative absorption called "Repelling Armies" and proclaimed the mantra that repels armies:

Om ekayama hedun rakṣa heduna hūṃ jaḥ hāṃ. Yamacittaśama. Śatūṃ stambhayanaṃ dhrika rbad.

When this was spoken, all the world-systems were thrown into turmoil, and all the proud and arrogant ones reeled as if spinning like a potter's wheel, rendered utterly intoxicated.

This is the fourth chapter of the Latter Tantra of Glorious Vajra the Fierce, the Secret Mind Treasury, entitled "Binding and Repelling the World."

Then the Blessed One entered into the meditative absorption called "Garuḍa Fully Roused" and proclaimed the secret mantra called "Piercing with Garuḍa's Activity":

Graṃti tigtig. Grata bragśita tale hedu hūṃ phaṭ.

Then again he entered into the extraordinary meditative absorption of slaying enemies alone and proclaimed the mantra called "Fierce Vajra, Descending Thunderbolt":

Om mārana sod sod. Mārana byiṃ byiṃ. Mārana rmugs rmugs. Mārana hal hal. Mārana dundun. Myogs myogs. Byiṃ byiṃ. Thurili li rbad ja ja. Apply this to nāgas and all enemies — śatrūṃ.

This is the fifth chapter of the Latter Tantra of Glorious Vajra the Fierce, the Secret Mind Treasury, entitled "The Chapter of the Secret Mantra."

Then once again the Lord of Gods (Śakra, that is, Indra) made this request: "O Blessed One, please bestow the special rite for utterly defeating the malicious ones." In response, the Blessed One entered into the meditative absorption called "The Secret of Garuḍa's Liberation" and proclaimed the supremely secret mantra:

Om tadyathā. Om ṭakicati. Gurya. Ghyulimiliśa śa ghyughyu. Śadti biriti. Hūṃya hūṃya. Hūṃtuti svāhā.

Rice and barley along with black sesame, and chebulic myrobalan — grind these fine. Prepare nine or eleven portions. Encircle a garuḍa egg with mantra. Set it to one side and perform the rite.

Mārana sod. Mārana byiṃ byiṃ. Mārana mugs mugs. Mārana hana hana. Mārana dundun. Hala hala. Myos myos. Byiṃ byiṃ. Thuri lili ja ja.

Then offer the torma:

Baliṃta khāhi khāhi. Gṛhṇa hṛhṇa. Mahārakṣi pato. Samāya svāhā.

Make supplication and entrust the enlightened activities.

Spikenard, all-great flesh, black tongue-plant, and śarīra, together with musk-rat and tiger's claws — bundle these into cloth and bind them to the body.

Om nyimas lānāṃ. Ghuni źircita ghu bināṃ. Citramutrazlogbyer. Citaghulināṃ. Yakṣa nāga zlog. Nāga śig. Nāgamgolānāṃ. Hapasakhre.

Always make offering with a stainless, spread-out torma. Through pacification, draw out poison and also release the sorcery-curse. In the four directions of the root maṇḍala, place painted nāga forms marked as poisonous. The one bound there, imagined as a colored-thread snake, is pierced and split open by a flaming razor. In the four directions of great Mount Meru, the nāgas of the four classes arising from the four poisons, the lords of earth, water, fire, and wind — of the four poisons — in order to release what is bound and draw out the poison, in order to imagine a garuḍa on the wing of a poison-neutralizing bird —

send each poison away to its own place,
proclaim the truth and make the meditative absorption clear.

This is the sixth chapter, the chapter of profound secrecy, from the Later Tantra of the Secret Mind of Glorious Vajra the Fierce.

Then the Blessed One entered into the meditative absorption called "Source of Nectar," and declared the supreme nectar. In a properly measured precious vase, smear it with the king of fragrances and four others, and fumigate it with dark incense smoke. Milk rich with herbs, rice and barley, white sesame and āruṛa, barūra and skyu-ru-ra, the three sweets, various nāga medicines, and water blended with sandalwood and other fragrances — fill it with these, so that it is a vessel with a ladle that has milk. Adorn the mouth of the vase, bind its neck with blue silk, and place it upon a pure maṇḍala. One endowed with divine pride inserts the tip of an iron tube into it and blows steadily without letting the breath escape. Consecrate it also with a khadira-wood kīla of eight finger-widths and with the supreme mantra-arrow. Recite the mantra one hundred thousand times for the vase, then wash it upon the object of compassion. If it is taken internally, liberation will occur.

This is the seventh chapter, the chapter on the nectar-vase ablution, from the Later Tantra of the Secret Mind of Glorious Vajra the Fierce.

Then again, the achievement of the nectar vase as a powder was taught. Listen, O chief lord of the gods — Śakra (Indra), king of the gods: The secret meditative absorption is to be accomplished thus. From nutmeg and barley, blue in color, rice and the herb māgadhī — accomplish it by soaking and then compounding in meditative absorption. When a sign appears in it, accomplish it as a powder. Smear it on, then combine it with fragrant water as a sour compound. Combine it with great molten butter and take it internally. With the mantra of the iron tube applied to the vase water, make offerings and exert oneself in meditative absorption. Wash and bathe with the vase water and anoint with āḍegū. Take the sour compound and great molten butter internally.

This is the eighth chapter, on the powder of the crumbled vase, the āḍegū ointment, and the internal dose, from the Later Tantra of the Secret Mind of Glorious Vajra the Fierce.

Then again the Blessed One declared to the lord of the gods: Listen, O lords of the gods — though the forms of malevolent poison are many, when gathered they are of two kinds: manifest poison and covert poison. Covert poison is difficult to repel. The poisoner is endowed with the three afflictions. Because delusion is greatest, they belong to the animal class. Because attachment is greatest, it is hard to eradicate. And because aversion is very great as well, they introduce poison by covert means, and when it is repelled they apply it again. Because jealousy is greatest, when another threatens their own support, they harm others. The characteristic of covert poison is this: though they loosen even a fierce and malevolent poison, they cause it to rise up again at intervals and opportune moments. To repel it is extremely difficult. Whoever doubts the supreme method I teach for repelling it will never accomplish it, and even if they do, they will become negligent. Therefore, O lords of the gods, be absolutely certain and free of doubt. With red sandalwood as the principal, take nāgakeśara, the herb māgadhī, oil of China, ocean foam, and the flesh of wild boar — or alternatively, oil of China, māgadhī, combined with the flesh of wild boar. Pierce with iron inside an iron vessel. After doing the approach practice three hundred thousand times, during the accomplishment practice rely on nothing other than the three whites. Wash the body with milk and water from melting snow. Apply dots to six places on the fish-shaped body: the two armpits, the forehead, the heart, the back, and both palms — apply a dot the size of a sesame seed to each place. On the navel, knees, and secret place, apply māgadhī alone. Let each dot be twenty-one applications. Do this for a full year, like the sun upon a snowy mountain.

This is the ninth chapter, the chapter on repelling covert poison, from the Later Tantra of the Secret Mind of Glorious Vajra the Fierce.

Then the lord of the gods asked: Kyema! O wrathful lord, holder of the vajra — what are the characteristics of fierce and malevolent poison?

What are the characteristics of that which can be repelled and that which cannot? May the great wrathful lord explain.

Upon this request, the Blessed One declared:

With joy, listen well, O lord of the gods, you who are Śakra (Indra, king of the gods).
Understand the signs of approach and harm from the practice of service and accomplishment.
Iron, copper, a person, a naked figure, a vase being carried,
cattle and fish being eaten as food,
a great fire blazing, a bridge being constructed,
a tree being planted, and the sun rising —
know these to be signs of auspiciousness.

The sun setting, a shooting star falling,
a person being imprisoned and loaded with a burden too heavy to carry,
an adept departing for another land — these are signs of inauspiciousness.
Water appearing from dry land,
fish, snakes, and ground-rats being killed or devoured,
a goiter and a wasting body sitting upon a throne,
a naked person bound at the head, a gathering of sentient beings making offerings in view,
frightening springs drying up, strings of jewels constantly appearing,
frogs and snakes being eaten as food, dogs and jackals being killed and so forth —
know these to be signs of auspiciousness.

Small creatures entering the body together with water and frogs and snakes,
these pursuing one from behind on the outside,
insects with long proboscises and the like eating the body,
being chased and beaten by a crowd of cattle and buffalo,
being bitten by packs of dogs and jackals and sinking into swamp,
encountering bears, lion cubs, and the like in an inauspicious display —
as signs of harm: entering a deep pit, narrow and constricting,
as though one cannot pass through, and unable to turn back;
steep rocky cliffs, a narrow valley and a dark cave,
a ruined temple and a dilapidated dwelling where one cannot pass through,
entering through a doorway and again unable to exit;
foul water, a lake, mucky water, fish, snakes, and the like,
pigs, oxen, buffalo and the like,
displaying many deeply disagreeable forms,
unable to escape a sheer cliff, chased by packs of jackals —
these are signs of harm.

As for the bodily experience of those signs of harm:
one becomes languid and inclined toward alcohol,
afflicted with illness, the limbs heavy,
the flesh twitching and trembling, the skin mottled,
the face peeling, the flesh stiffening, pain arising intermittently in the joints —
know these to be signs of harm.

This is the tenth chapter, the chapter setting forth the omens of service and accomplishment, and the signs of harm, from the Later Tantra of the Secret Mind of Glorious Vajra the Fierce.

Then the lord of the gods supplicated: Kyema! O Blessed One, may you please explain the characteristics by which a fierce and malevolent poison arrives at the body of a sentient being.

Upon this supplication, the Blessed One declared:

A poison that has reached as far as the flesh and muscle —
the heart is always displeased,
the muscle-roots are gnawed, with twitching and trembling;
there is constant craving to pull and grasp,
the flesh appears as though wiped with milk,
and the color of the flesh resembles a tongue of flame.
At times, when the flesh and muscle are afflicted,
one feels as though cold water is being poured upon them,
the skin peels, wool-like fuzz appears, and the flesh becomes mottled.

When fierce poison reaches the flesh:
twitching and trembling, as though devoured by maggots,
the flesh becomes dark, reddish, and numb,
as though smoke has entered the eyes —
the corners of the eyes fill with fluid,
the eyes become swollen and the brows knit,
the brows twitch and the eyes also tremble,
the fingers become like a rat's tail.

When it enters the channels and the fluids:
a foul and fetid odor arises from the body,
fluid blisters and swellings appear —
these are the blossoms of the channels.
The color of the yellow fluid is like soot,
and a yellow fluid reeking of rotten leather and the stench of rotting hide
is what arises.
The upper body is uncomfortable and the mind is agitated as well.
These are the general characteristics,
and there are those that will additionally arise:
the sensation of heat like the light of fire comes,
the sensation of being pierced by thorns comes,
the body becomes hot and blisters arise,
when the skin is peeled it oozes,
wounds and sores do not heal,
when the skin is peeled there is an absence of feeling,
oozing appears without pain,
there is shivering in the heat,
the body's vitality and strength diminish,
swellings appear and the flesh becomes mottled,
the flesh stiffens and the eyes of the flesh resemble frog's eyes,
throughout all the flesh-hollows there is twitching and trembling.

When poison has reached the bones —

the outer flesh all withers and dries,
the colour turns blue-grey and the flesh's lustre is lost,
even the lustre of the skin fades away;
yet though recovery may not occur,
through mantra, medicinal substances, and meditative absorption (samādhi)
life is prolonged, the voice kept clear, and the body made comfortable.

Thus it was declared. This concludes the eleventh chapter of the Subsequent Tantra of the Secret Mind of Glorious Vajra the Wrathful, the chapter setting forth the characteristics of malignant poison that has reached the bones.

Then Śakra (Indra), lord of the gods, petitioned: "Alas, O Blessed One! For the benefit of beings in future times, please set forth the particular medicinal substances." Upon this petition, the Blessed One declared:

Hear me, O Śakra, lord of the gods, chief among devas!
When sentient beings of future times
are endangered by malignant poison,
all such beneficial remedies as these
are to be accomplished together with mantra.

When it resembles the light of fire, apply wolf's-bane.
For that which resembles the piercing of thorns,
apply ra-poison and iron-hook herb.
When blisters arise from heat,
apply madder and tang-spas.

When the flesh is peeling and oozing,
apply thick broth of ra, together with sre and long-tol.
When wounds and sores do not heal,
apply magnet prepared through the inner method;
tame that very condition with fierce mantra.

When the peeling flesh has no sensation,
apply tiger flesh with golden flower,
mixed with rock salt, and apply it.

Nourish the pulverized flesh with nectar-essence.
Mix nutmeg, la-dong broth,
and scented water together and anoint.

For oozing that is without pain,
anoint with donkey-pill, ginger, and white butter.
Mix fever-cooling tongpa with horn
together with heat and great resin.

When the body's vitality and strength are diminished,
take vase-water and magnet,
apply and wash, then drink it.
Apply scented water and great resin.

When the flesh is mottled with saffron and pig's fat,
and when something resembling the stiffening of an ox appears,
apply golden flower with nutmeg
and strive earnestly at the action of mantra.

When the flesh stiffens and frog's eyes appear,
and when all the flesh-hollows twitch and tremble,
apply musk, nutmeg, and dark tongpa.

For poisons in the mid-range of the flesh,
apply earth-fragrance and magnet,
pure white-bark, tongpa, costus root,
black bdellium with its fragrant resin,
the flavour of flavour-substance, savage-poison,
the essence of a woman who has given birth to one child, and pig-essence.
Or alternatively: savage-poison with tongpa,
la-dong broth, and earth-fragrance,
combined with great resin and scented water, and anoint.

Take bdellium, pure white-bark, and all-tree-bark,
spikenard, savage-poison, and the flavour of flavour-substance;
bind with a large bone and gather with resin.
Form into pills, burn them,
and fumigate through the nostrils again and again.

As for great-poison nāgas and the malignant spirits (Skt. graha) that arise from them,
and whatever spirit-forces there may be that harm sentient beings:
when they sense the smell of this great malignancy,
the heads of those malignant ones utterly burst apart
as darkness is dispersed by the light of the sun.

For malignant poisons that have entered the flesh:
anoint the anointing substances on from outside,
and fumigate continually with fumigating substances.
Apply magnet with cold scented water,
wash it well and administer it internally in a continuous course.
The ailments will be purified and hair and the like will grow back.

For poison residing in the channels and blood-vessels:
the anointing and fumigation, like the current of a river,
along with the internally administered substances, are as follows.
Purify magnet with fragrant water
and mix it with chebulic myrobalan without interruption.
In the seat of the channels it separates the clear from the turbid within the ailments.
The ailments whose clear and turbid portions have been separated emerge outwardly,
like the sun on a snow mountain.

As for poisons that have spread to the bones,
administer the internally administered substances continuously.

Thus it was declared. This concludes the twelfth chapter of the Subsequent Tantra of the Secret Mind of Glorious Vajra the Wrathful, the chapter on taming malignant poison through medicinal substances.

Then again Śakra, lord of the gods, petitioned: "O Blessed One! Please set forth the method for completely eradicating the utterly malignant." Upon this petition, the Blessed One declared:

Hear me, O god, great lord of Śakra!
To completely eradicate what is utterly malignant,
one must strive with great diligence in the practice of burning.

The fire pit is triangular, eight finger-widths across. Smear it with blood and charnel-ground charcoal. Draw a vajra with three points in the center. Place three stūpas at the corners. Upon this set the wrathful vessel, and inside it, with the triangular point facing south, pile up the wood of a noxious tree at the three corners. This is to be done at the southeast of the great maṇḍala. On strips of bark, write the enemy's name one hundred and eight times, along with various poisons, the three hot substances, and donkey urine. Pile in also the tail-feather of a female crow. With the samādhi of the deity, with offering and mantra, make the samādhi vivid and present it to the deity. The mantra of burning is this: Sarva vighnān māra ya jvalaraṃ. With one thousand and eight recitations performed many times over, cast the ashes into a great river. Take also the ashes from the bottom of the fire pit, mix them with strong-smelling dog musk and the three hot substances, form them into the shape of an enemy figure, and with the words Nāga stambhana press it down at the center of a crossroads.

Thus it was declared. This concludes the thirteenth chapter of the Subsequent Tantra of the Secret Mind of Glorious Vajra the Wrathful, the chapter on the fierce burning that utterly eradicates the arrogant.

Then again Śakra, lord of the gods, petitioned: "O Blessed One! For the work of suppression, how is one to completely eradicate the utterly malignant?" Upon this petition, the Blessed One declared:

Hear me, O great mighty lord of gods!
To tame with suppression what is utterly malignant,
join two turtle shells face-to-face.
Fill them with ocean foam, the wild khre bird,
a full handful of nāga kesara,
the three whites, great meat, and śarīra relics,
with magita as the chief ingredient,
combined with costus root, a sheath from the ro nye plant,
tamped down with a stick, and bound with blue and red thread.
Recite the mantra three thousand times over the arrow.
Nāga stambhanaṃ.
At the full-moon ray of the twenty-third day,
at a western crossroads or a triple junction,
or else at the site of the harm,
dig one cubit deep with a cypress stake.
Place the filled and bound turtle shells inside,
and upon them set the suppressing Mount Meru.
While performing these acts, avoid inferior foods;
let the diet be the three whites and sweet things.
With rotted flesh, clotted blood, and tallow,
and with the mantra arrow endowed with mantra,
cut off the enemy's support from his home base.
By the power of mantra it will be as swift as a fierce lightning bolt.
Not even for a single moment will harm come to oneself.

This concludes the fourteenth chapter of the Subsequent Tantra of the Secret Mind of Glorious Vajra the Wrathful, the chapter on irreversible suppression.

Then Śakra, lord of the gods, petitioned again: "O Blessed One! These malignant ones on their own wreak harm upon beings, and they also overcome the power of various others. If by the power of others they cause beings to go astray, how is one to thoroughly remove that?" When he had spoken thus, the Blessed One declared:

Hear me, O great mighty lord among gods!
In the final age, when a single malignant poison
has entered into a sentient being
and these emissaries cause disruption,
when fierce spirits, kings, gongpo demons,
and all those who harm accomplishments
are known for what they truly are,
they will destroy themselves by their own hand.

Rāja citta bhaḍ nan. Cahur thuṃ jaḥ mrāya bhaḍ nan. Oṃ tri citta bhaḍ nan. Tri citta bhaḍ nan. Hab śam tri citta bhaḍ nan. Ru tri citta bhaḍ nan. Rāja trig nan. Cahur thuṃ ja. Māra ya bhaḍ.
