About this text

The Pith Instructions on Kālacakra, known in Sanskrit as the Kālacakra Upadeśa and in Tibetan as Dü kyi Khorlö Men-ngak, is a brief tantric commentary attributed to the master Kālacakrapāda the Great and translated into Tibetan by the Indian preceptor Buddhākarabhadra with the translator Gyijo Dawé Özer. It sets out a series of verses on the completion-stage practice of the Kālacakra system and then explains them line by line: the night and day yogas of gazing into the sky, the ten signs that appear from emptiness, beginning with smoke and mirage and ending with the drop, the vision of the Buddha's body within that drop, the restraint of the life-wind through the vase practice, the progress through the grounds as the count of breaths is overcome, and the cultivation of imperishable bliss. Its verse commentary draws on the Chanting of the Names of Mañjuśrī to illustrate the stages of the day yoga. It is Toh 1366 in the Tengyur, where it belongs to the section of commentaries on the tantras.

Translation

In the language of India: kālacakra upadeśa. In the language of Tibet: Pith Instructions on Kālacakra.

I pay homage to the Blessed One, the glorious Vajrasattva.

With a mind wholly intent on the sky, with unblinking eyes, having entered the vajra path, from emptiness one sees smoke, mirage, the stainless, clear sky-light, and a lamp, a blaze, the moon, the sun, the vajra, the great supreme one, and the drop. In its center is the body of the Buddha, free of objects, the manifold enjoyment body. The yogī gazing steadily at a sky free of clouds should make it appear, until one sees, in the channel of time, a black line radiating stainless light. There the body of the Omniscient One, of many colors, is like the sun in limpid water. In all its aspects it is one's own mind, wholly free of objects; it is not at all another's mind. Seeing the body, at the time of each day one blocks the life-wind, until one sees one's own body encircled by rays of light, as by a turning wheel. By this, for those who strive in restraint, forgoing touch for six months, the mind of the path goes to the bliss of peace. In the instant at the end of passion and the beginning of dispassion, the all-pervading lord increases the count of breaths. Within the net of the head are the joy of cessation and the innate; gnosis and consciousness are in the center. Whatever bliss of the union of the two arises through vajra and lotus at the time of sleep, that which increases it increases by the power of the guru's word; in this there is no flaw. For the excellent yogī, this union enters the Lord of Victors and vanquishes the māras and the afflictions.

Now, in order to enter the luminous mind, the passage beginning kha, that is, "sky," was spoken. Here one should train in the two kinds of yoga, in the manner of mantra and in the manner of the Perfection Vehicle: one should become habituated to yoga in the sky and in a place without a roof. Whoever trains in the yoga of the sky sees, at night in the darkness, with a mind wholly intent on the sky, the signs such as smoke.

"With unblinking eyes, having entered the vajra path" means that the life-wind has entered the central channel. "From emptiness" means that, through a mind wholly intent on the sky, through unblinking eyes, and through having entered the vajra path, one sees from emptiness smoke, mirage, the stainless, clear sky-light, and likewise a lamp; these are seen through the night yoga. Then one sees a cloudless sky.

Then, again, the day yoga in the sky is taught in the words:

Arisen from space, self-arisen,
the great fire of wisdom and gnosis.

This is seeing a blaze in the cloudless sky.

Vairocana, the great light,
the radiance of gnosis, brilliant,

this is its appearing as the moon. "Lamp of beings" is its appearing as the sun. "Lamp of gnosis" is its appearing as Rāhu.

Great splendor, luminous,

this is lightning, that is, the supreme part.

King of knowledge, foremost lord of mantras,

this is seeing it as the drop. Thus the Blessed One taught the night yoga in such places as the gathering of the ten signs, and taught the day yoga in the Chanting of the Names (Nāmasaṃgīti). "King of secret mantras, accomplishing the great aim" is seeing all things, vases, cloth and the rest, in every form, as the body.

"In its center" means that in the center of the drop one sees many enjoyment bodies of the Buddha, free of objects, without substance, and of the nature of imagination. Then, through the yoga of the body, one hears the very sound of crackling fire. Thus the emanation body appears as form; the meaning is that the enjoyment body appears as sound.

Here, the day yoga should be made to appear by the yogī who gazes steadily at a sky free of clouds; in the morning and in the afternoon one should keep one's back to the sun. Otherwise the rays of the sun will cause blurred vision, because they harm the eyes. In this way one should train each day, and it appears, until one sees, in the center of the drop that has entered within the channel of time, the avadhūtī, a black line the size of a hair radiating stainless rays of light.

"There the body of the Omniscient One" means this: like the sun in limpid water, the three realms without remainder, of many colors, are all one's own mind, wholly free of objects; and that is not another's mind, because one does not know the minds of others. Here, first, the appearance of one's own mind is truly seen with the flesh eye of the Tathāgata. With the divine eye and the others, one sees and knows the minds of others. The Blessed One taught these five eyes in the Dharmasaṃgīti. Thus, in order, the flesh eye, the divine eye, the buddha eye, the wisdom eye and the gnosis eye arise through the power of meditation. Then one becomes omniscient, for whom there is nothing whatever unseen.

Therefore, having seen the body, at the time of each day one should perform the vase practice that blocks the life-wind. "Until one sees one's own body encircled by rays of light, as by a turning wheel" refers to seeing within the body described before. "Those who strive in restraint" are beginners who practice celibacy. "Forgoing touch for six months" means abandoning the union of the two organs. "The mind of the path" is the mind of the body of emptiness. "Goes to the bliss of peace" means attaining imperishable bliss.

"Passion" is the white fortnight. "Its end" is the defining mark of the sixteenth digit. Then "dispassion" is the dark fortnight. "Its beginning" means not yet having entered the dark fortnight. "Instant" is the instant that is the mark of complete awakening in a single instant. "Increases the count of breaths" means that the twenty-one thousand six hundred breaths increase from the tip of the jewel of the secret vajra. When it is certain that, of the thirty-six hundred breaths, the counts of nine hundred and nine hundred have been blocked and abandoned, one attains the grounds two at a time. Thus at the navel, the heart, the throat and the forehead one attains the grounds by four, by six, by eight and by ten. At the crown protuberance, through the exhaustion of all the life-wind, one attains the twelve grounds.

Then, what comes from within the net of the head to the secret place is the central channel, by way of joy and the innate. This is so at the time of sleep. At the heart is the central channel of gnosis. At the throat it is by way of the nature of the drops of body, speech, mind and gnosis.

What comes from the forehead is the bliss of the union of the two. As for that bliss, by the power of the guru's word one makes the bliss of the seminal fluid go upward. That it increases up to the point of being free of the blocking of the life-wind described before is nothing to marvel at.

Thus, having vanquished the afflictions and the māras, it enters the Lord of Victors: that is, the excellent yogī, abiding in the avadhūtī, through a year's practice slays the life-winds that had entered the left and the right, and enters the Lord of Victors, the body of emptiness. Therefore, through the vase practice one generates the instant of great bliss, and it is certain that the bliss increases.

The Pith Instructions on Kālacakra, composed by the master Kālacakrapāda the Great, are complete.

It was translated by the Indian preceptor Buddhākarabhadra and the translator Gyijo Dawé Özer.

Translator's notes

The verse lists the ten signs of the Kālacakra night and day yogas; "sky-light" renders the Tibetan for the sign elsewhere called fireflies, and "the vajra" and "the great supreme one" are glossed by the commentary as Rāhu and lightning (the supreme part). In the verse, "channel of time" reads the commentary's rtsa (channel) for the verse's rtsal (skill), an evident slip. In the Nāmasaṃgīti quotation, "foremost lord of mantras" reads gtso (lord) for mtsho (lake), matching the known verse. In the breath-count sentence the written "nine, eight, nine hundred" is read as "nine hundred and nine hundred" (1,800 breaths per ground, two grounds per 3,600), which fits the arithmetic of 21,600 breaths over twelve grounds. The phrase rendered "of the nature of imagination" for the enjoyment bodies is uncertain; the Tibetan may intend "free of conceptuality." "The gathering of the ten signs" may refer to a passage or text not securely identifiable. Where the root verse says "in this there is no flaw," the commentary here says the increase of bliss is "nothing to marvel at"; the two readings are kept as each passage gives them.
