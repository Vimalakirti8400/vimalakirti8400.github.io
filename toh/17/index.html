About this text

Toh 17 in the Kangyur is a Perfection of Wisdom sūtra known in English as "The Noble One Hundred and Fifty Modes of the Perfection of Wisdom," in Sanskrit as the Āryaprajñāpāramitānayaśatapañcāśatikā, and in Tibetan as the Phags pa shes rab kyi pha rol tu phyin pa'i tshul brgya lnga bcu pa. It belongs to the Prajñāpāramitā class of canonical Buddhist texts, which expound the doctrine of the perfection of wisdom and the nature of emptiness as the foundation of the bodhisattva path. The text opens in the traditional sūtra style with the Buddha as its central figure, described in richly elaborated terms as the sovereign of great yogins, the mother of all Tathāgatas, and the embodiment of omniscient primordial wisdom. As one of the shorter Perfection of Wisdom sūtras preserved in the Kangyur, it presents the teaching of transcendent wisdom through a structured enumeration of its modes or aspects, situating it within the broader tradition of texts that systematically analyze the nature and practice of prajñāpāramitā.

Translation

In the language of India: Āryaprajñāpāramitānayaśatapañcāśatikā. In the language of Tibet: The Noble One Hundred and Fifty Modes of the Perfection of Wisdom.

Homage to the Blessed One, the Perfection of Wisdom.

Thus did I hear. At one time, the Blessed One — who possesses the distinctive varieties of knowledge of all the Tathāgatas; who, by the crown adorned with the jewels of all the precious Tathāgatas, holds perfect sovereignty over the ten powers throughout the three realms; who is the mother of all Tathāgatas and is endowed with omniscient primordial wisdom; who is the sovereign lord of the great yogins; who, by the mudrā of all the Tathāgatas, has realized in his heart the sameness of all phenomena; who performs the most varied activities; who performs the enlightened activity of completely fulfilling all the hopes of every last sentient being throughout the entire realm of beings without exception; who is great compassion, the great Vairocana, ever abiding in the sameness of the three realms; whose body, speech, and mind are all the vajra Tathāgata; who abides in the domain of Paranirmitavaśavartin (the King of the Gods who wields power over the creations of others), throughout the entire desire realm — this Blessed One, residing in a place beautifully adorned with the finest great precious jewels, with multicolored pennants hung with tinkling bells set in motion by the wind, with garlands of flowers, with tassels, with ornamental festoons, with hanging swags, and made beautiful as the moon, surrounded by and attended upon by hundreds of millions of bodhisattvas, with all auspicious praises raised — taught the Dharma together with the great bodhisattvas, among whom were the bodhisattva great beings Vajrapāṇi, Avalokiteśvara, Ākāśagarbha, Vajramuṣṭi, Mañjuśrī, the one who, the very moment of generating the awakening mind, turns the wheel of Dharma, Gaganagañja, and the bodhisattva great being who utterly vanquishes all the powers of Māra — these and others, hundreds of millions of bodhisattvas assembled together, surrounding him and facing him.

He taught the Dharma correctly, setting forth what is called the gateway to the complete purity of the essential nature of all phenomena — the mode of the Perfection of Wisdom — which is good at the beginning, good in the middle, and good at the end, excellent in meaning, excellent in word, unmixed, perfectly complete, perfectly pure, and perfectly clarified.

This, then, is as follows: the abode of the perfectly pure state of supreme joy is none other than the abode of the bodhisattva. The abode of the perfectly pure state of the extreme of attachment is none other than the abode of the bodhisattva. The abode of the perfectly pure state of contact is none other than the abode of the bodhisattva. The abode of the perfectly pure state of the bonds of the beloved is none other than the abode of the bodhisattva. The abode of the perfectly pure state of sovereignty over all and of power is none other than the abode of the bodhisattva. The abode of the perfectly pure state of views is none other than the abode of the bodhisattva. The abode of the perfectly pure state of joy is none other than the abode of the bodhisattva. The abode of the perfectly pure state of craving is none other than the abode of the bodhisattva. The abode of the perfectly pure state of pride is none other than the abode of the bodhisattva. The abode of the perfectly pure state of adornment is none other than the abode of the bodhisattva. The abode of the perfectly pure state of that which gives satisfaction is none other than the abode of the bodhisattva. The abode of the perfectly pure state of radiance is none other than the abode of the bodhisattva. The abode of the perfectly pure state of bodily ease is none other than the abode of the bodhisattva. The abode of the perfectly pure state of form is none other than the abode of the bodhisattva.

The abode of the perfectly pure state of sound is none other than the abode of the bodhisattva. The abode of the perfectly pure state of smell is none other than the abode of the bodhisattva. The abode of the perfectly pure state of taste is none other than the abode of the bodhisattva. The abode of the perfectly pure state of touch is none other than the abode of the bodhisattva.

And why is this so? It is because all phenomena are perfectly pure by their essential nature, and since all phenomena are empty of essential nature, the Perfection of Wisdom is utterly pure.

Vajrapāṇi, whoever hears even once this method of the Perfection of Wisdom — which accomplishes the gate of the perfectly pure essential nature of all phenomena — will, until reaching the heart of awakening, be fully severed from all obscurations: afflictive obscurations, Dharmic obscurations, and great karmic obscurations. Even if they have committed wrongdoing, they will never be reborn in the lower destinies such as the hells. And whatever evil has been done will be purified free of suffering the very moment it is committed. Whoever retains, reads daily, recites, and properly contemplates this teaching will, in this very life, attain the vajra meditative absorption of the equality of all phenomena, and thereby gain mastery over all phenomena. They will experience joy, delight, and supreme bliss in regard to all phenomena. And in sixteen lifetimes of a great bodhisattva, they will become a Tathāgata or attain the vajra-holder's realization.

Then the Blessed One — who is Vajrapāṇi, who has completely realized all Tathāgatas, who encompasses all maṇḍalas, the supreme being of all vajra-holders, victorious over all three worlds in all three realms without remainder, who tames the entire realm of sentient beings without exception, who accomplishes every aim, who holds the great sacred pledge, and who is attended by the many great beings of vajra secrecy — taught the meaning of this method of the Perfection of Wisdom. Smiling with a radiant face, he held the vajra with his left hand, took up the primordial great vajra, pressed it to his own heart in a gesture of praise, and spoke this essence of his own suchness, called the sacred pledge of the vajra-meaning of great bliss: HŪṂ.

Then the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Vairocana, in turn taught this method of the Perfection of Wisdom known as "Accomplishing the Complete and Perfect Awakening to the Peaceful Nature of Reality of All Tathāgatas." Because the vajra is by nature indestructible, the complete and perfect awakening to vajra equality is great awakening. Because all aims are one, the complete and perfect awakening to equality of meaning is great awakening. Because all is perfectly pure by essential nature, the complete and perfect awakening to equality of Dharma is great awakening. Because there is non-conceptuality with respect to all things, the complete and perfect awakening to universal equality is great awakening.

Vajrapāṇi, whoever hears, retains, reads, and meditates upon these four complete accomplishments of phenomena — even if they engage in all manner of wrongdoing — will be completely liberated from all lower destinies until they are seated at the heart of awakening. And they will swiftly attain the complete and perfect awakening of unsurpassed and perfect enlightenment.

Then, having so declared, the Blessed One grasped the fist of wisdom, extensively showed forth this very meaning of the real, smiled with a radiant face, and proclaimed this essence of the equality of all phenomena: A.

Then the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Śākyamuni, the tamer of all the untameable, likewise taught this Perfection of Wisdom — this mode of practice called "The Gathering of Victories through the Equality of All Phenomena":

Through the absence of conceptual elaboration of attachment, there is the absence of conceptual elaboration of aversion. Through the absence of conceptual elaboration of aversion, there is the absence of conceptual elaboration of delusion. Through the absence of conceptual elaboration of delusion, all phenomena are without conceptual elaboration. Through all phenomena being without conceptual elaboration, one should know the Perfection of Wisdom itself to be without conceptual elaboration.

Vajrapāṇi, whoever hears, retains, reads, and meditates upon this mode of the Perfection of Wisdom — even if they were to slay every sentient being born in the three realms — will not fall into the lower destinies, for the power of taming is present in them. And they will swiftly attain the complete and perfect awakening of unsurpassed and perfect enlightenment.

Then the Blessed One showed forth the meaning of this nature of reality extensively to Vajrapāṇi, and, seizing the mudrā called "The Vajra Victorious over the Three Worlds," with brow furrowed and eyebrows raised, his lotus face both wrathful and laughing, his fangs bared, standing in the posture of the left leg extended — abiding thus, he spoke this essence called the Vajra Hūṃ:

HŪṂ.

Then the Blessed One, the Tathāgata, having attained the nature of reality that is utterly pure in its essential nature, likewise taught this mode of the Perfection of Wisdom called "The Wisdom Mudrā of Avalokiteśvara — the Equality of All Phenomena":

Through the complete purification of all attachment, all aversion in the world will be completely purified. Through the complete purification of all defilement, all evil in the world will be completely purified. Through the complete purification of all phenomena, all sentient beings in the world will be completely purified. Through the complete purification of all knowing, the Perfection of Wisdom in the world will be completely purified.

Vajrapāṇi, whoever hears, retains, reads, and meditates upon this mode of the Perfection of Wisdom — even while dwelling amid all attachment and every defilement — will not be stained by the faults of attachment, by defilements, or by adventitious afflictions, just like a lotus. And they will swiftly attain the complete and perfect awakening of unsurpassed and perfect enlightenment.

Then the great bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara showed forth this very meaning extensively and, his face breaking into a radiant smile, gazing upon the opening petals of the lotus — unstained by the defilements of attachment and the rest — and for the sake of all beings, he spoke this essence called "The Lotus of Manifold Forms":

HRĪḤ.

Then the Blessed One, the Tathāgata, the sovereign of all three realms, likewise taught this mode of the Perfection of Wisdom called "The Essence of Wisdom Born from the Empowerment of All Tathāgatas":

Granting empowerment brings attainment of sovereignty over all three realms. Granting wealth brings complete fulfillment of every aspiration. Granting Dharma brings attainment of the equality of all phenomena. Granting material things brings attainment of all well-being of body, speech, and mind.

Then the great bodhisattva Ākāśagarbha broke into a radiant smile, bound upon his own head the garland-crown of precious vajra empowerment, showed forth this very meaning extensively, and spoke this essence called "The Precious Pledge of All Empowerments":

TRĀṂ.

Then the Blessed One, the Tathāgata who has received the wisdom mudrā of all Tathāgatas, who holds the fist of all Tathāgatas — the ever-abiding Tathāgata — again set forth this mode of the Perfection of Wisdom called "The Vajra of the Place of the Wisdom Mudrā of All Tathāgatas":

Perfectly holding the body mudrā of all Tathāgatas brings one to become all Tathāgatas themselves. Perfectly holding the speech mudrā of all Tathāgatas brings attainment of all phenomena. Perfectly holding the mind mudrā of all Tathāgatas brings attainment of all meditative absorptions. Perfectly holding the vajra mudrā of all Tathāgatas brings all body, speech, and mind to become vajra, and brings attainment of the supreme vajra accomplishment.

Whoever hears, retains, reads, recites, and meditates on this mode of the Perfection of Wisdom will attain all accomplishments, all excellence, all knowledge, all aims, will have all body, speech, and mind become vajra, will attain the supreme vajra accomplishment, and will swiftly come to perfect and complete awakening in unsurpassed, genuine, and fully perfected enlightenment.

Then the great bodhisattva, holding the great pledge vajra mudrā of the great vajra fist, showed forth this very meaning extensively, and likewise breaking into a radiant smile, spoke this essence called "The Pledge of the Iron Vajra Mudrā, the Pledge of All Accomplishments":

AḤ.

Then the Blessed One, the Tathāgata in whom all phenomena are without conceptual elaboration, again set forth this mode of the Perfection of Wisdom called "Revolving the Letters like a Wheel":

By way of the mode of being without essential nature, all phenomena are emptiness. By way of the mode of being without characteristics, all phenomena are without characteristics. By way of the mode of being without aspiration, all phenomena are without aspiration. Through the perfect purity of the Perfection of Wisdom, all phenomena are luminous by their own intrinsic nature.

Then the Blessed One, the youthful Mañjuśrī, showing this very meaning extensively, and while breaking into a radiant smile, with his own sword striking all Tathāgatas, spoke this supreme essence of the Perfection of Wisdom:

A.

Then the Blessed One, the Tathāgata gathered together with the retinue of all Tathāgatas, set forth this mode of the Perfection of Wisdom called "Entering the Great Wheel":

Entering into the equality of the vajra brings entry into the wheel of all Tathāgatas. Entering into the equality of meaning brings entry into the wheel of the great bodhisattvas. Entering into the equality of all phenomena brings entry into the wheel of the sacred Dharma. Entering into the equality of all brings entry into all wheels.

Then the bodhisattva, immediately upon arousing the mind of awakening, showed this very meaning of the dharmatā extensively — turning the wheel of Dharma — and, drawing back a smile, spinning the vajra wheel, spoke this essence called "Spinning the Vajra Wheel and Entering into All Vajra Wheels":

HŪṂ.

Then the Blessed One set forth this mode of the Perfection of Wisdom called "The Supreme of All Offerings," presenting all offerings to the Tathāgata with a vessel extensive in the ritual of all offerings:

Arousing the awakening mind is the extensive ritual of offering to all Tathāgatas.

Completely protecting the realm of all sentient beings is the extensive ritual of offering to all Tathāgatas. Completely upholding the sacred Dharma is the extensive ritual of offering to all Tathāgatas. Writing down the Perfection of Wisdom in letters, engaging in the writing of it in letters, retaining it, reading it, reciting it, teaching it, listening to it, reflecting on it, meditating on it, and venerating it — all of this is the extensive ritual of offering to all Tathāgatas.

Then the great bodhisattva Ākāśagarbha (Treasury of Space), showing that very meaning extensively, drew back a smile and spoke this essence called "The Pledge of All Meaningful Actions":

OṂ.

Then the Blessed One, the Tathāgata who is capable of taming all sentient beings, set forth this mode of the Perfection of Wisdom called "The Essence of Knowing How to Tame All Sentient Beings, Completely Holding the Fist of Wisdom":

Because all sentient beings are of the nature of equanimity, the wrathful one is of the nature of equanimity. Because all sentient beings are of the nature of being tamed, the wrathful one is of the nature of being tamed. Because all sentient beings are of the nature of reality, the wrathful one is of the nature of reality. Because all sentient beings are of the nature of vajra, the wrathful one is of the nature of vajra. And why is this? It is because the taming of all sentient beings is awakening.

Then the bodhisattva who utterly vanquishes all the powers of māra, showing that very meaning extensively, drew back a smile and, with the mudrā of the vajra-tusk and the form of Vajrayakṣa, spoke this essence called "The Vajra-Wrathful Laughter":

HA.

Then the Blessed One, abiding in the equanimity of all phenomena, set forth this mode of the Perfection of Wisdom called "The Supreme of All Phenomena":

Because all is of the nature of equanimity, the Perfection of Wisdom is equanimity itself. Because all is of the nature of meaning, the Perfection of Wisdom is meaning itself. Because all is of the nature of reality, the Perfection of Wisdom is reality itself. Because all is of the nature of action, the Perfection of Wisdom should be understood as action itself.

Then the Blessed One Vajrapāṇi, the Tathāgata, entered into meditative absorption called "Blessing the Maṇḍala of the Pledge of All-Meaningful Action of All Tathāgatas and All Bodhisattvas," and spoke this essence called "The Pledge of All That Is Meaningful":

HŪṂ.

Then the Blessed One, the all-encompassing Tathāgata Vairocana, set forth this mode of the Perfection of Wisdom called "Blessing All Sentient Beings":

Because the great bodhisattva Samantabhadra is the intrinsic nature of all things, all sentient beings possess the tathāgatagarbha (buddha-nature). Because all are empowered by the vajra-essence, all sentient beings possess the vajra-essence. Because all speech arises from going forth, all sentient beings possess the Dharma-essence. Because all are yoked to performing the activities of all sentient beings, all sentient beings possess the karma-essence.

Then the outer vajra clans gave forth a great clamor of sounds, showing the meaning of the nature of reality extensively, and offered their essences. Then the great vajra-sovereign offered this essence of his own suchness:

DRĪ.

Then all the mātṛkās (Mother-goddesses) prostrated to the Blessed One and, in harmonious speech, offered this essence of their own suchness called "The Pledge of Drawing in All, Seizing, Destroying, and Accomplishing":

BHYOḤ.

Then the three lords, beginning with the Honey-maker and the others, prostrated to the Blessed One and, in harmonious speech, offered this essence of their own suchness:

SVĀ.

Then the four sisters prostrated to the Blessed One and —

offered this essence of their own suchness:

HĀ.

Then the Blessed One, the Tathāgata — endowed with qualities that are boundless, infinite, and ultimate — in order to bless this very teaching to its ultimate culmination, spoke this manner of the Perfection of Wisdom called "The Vajra Blessed by the Ultimacy of the Sameness of All Phenomena," consecrated by the ultimate sameness of all phenomena:

Because the Perfection of Wisdom is itself boundless, all Tathāgatas are boundless. Because the Perfection of Wisdom is itself infinite, all vajras are infinite. Because the Perfection of Wisdom is itself manifold, all phenomena are manifold. Because the Perfection of Wisdom is itself without culmination, all phenomena are without culmination.

Whoever hears this manner of the Perfection of Wisdom, holds it, retains it, reads it, teaches it, recites it, and meditates upon it — that person will swiftly attain the buddha, having fully traversed the boundless conduct of the bodhisattvas, having exhausted all obscurations, and will quickly become a Tathāgata or a vajra-holder.

Then all the Tathāgatas assembled, and in order that this doctrinal category might be accomplished swiftly, with meaning and without obstruction, they bestowed upon Vajrapāṇi the word "Excellent!" saying:

O great sentient being — Excellent! Well done!
O great bliss — Excellent! Well done!
O Great Vehicle — Excellent! Well done!
O great intelligence — Excellent! Well done!
This rite has been well proclaimed.
By all the buddhas and vajra-holders,
this supreme Vinaya of fruitful result,
this vajra sūtra teaching, consecrated,
this unsurpassed king of all rites —
whoever holds these teachings,
māras and all other such forces
can neither endure nor withstand them.
Buddhas and bodhisattvas alike,
and all supreme accomplishments —
just as proclaimed by all the buddhas,
these shall be attained without long delay.

Having spoken thus, in order to accomplish the secret, all those who are the Jewel of the Buddha, together with the wise, praised what the vajra-bearing one had taught.

Then the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Vairocana — having attained the secret dharmatā of all Tathāgatas — since all phenomena are free of conceptual elaboration, proclaimed this supreme pledge called "The Supremely Meaningful Vajra of Great Bliss": this most excellent gateway of the Perfection of Wisdom, the equality of phenomena endowed with vajra-meaning, the noble teaching having no beginning, no end, and no middle.

For the great bodhisattvas, the supreme accomplishment of great desire will become the supreme accomplishment of great bliss for all Tathāgatas. For the great bodhisattvas, the supreme accomplishment of great bliss will become the supreme accomplishment of the great awakening of all Tathāgatas. For the great bodhisattvas, the supreme accomplishment of the great awakening of all Tathāgatas will become the supreme accomplishment of utterly vanquishing all the powers of the great māra. For the great bodhisattvas, the supreme accomplishment of utterly vanquishing all the powers of the great māra will become the supreme accomplishment of supreme sovereignty over all three realms.

For the great bodhisattvas, the supreme accomplishment of supreme sovereignty over all three realms will become the supreme accomplishment of protecting, without remainder, every class of sentient being throughout every world-realm, and of bringing benefit, happiness, and supreme and utterly great bliss to all sentient beings — this by virtue of being the cause for wishing to completely purify, without remainder, every class of sentient being throughout every world-realm, belonging as it does to those great bodhisattvas who are deeply devoted to saṃsāra and of great diligence, in that they wish to wholly purify it.

Why is this so? The verses say:

For as long as the supremely wise abide
within the realm of saṃsāra,
for that long they are able to accomplish
the matchless welfare of beings, without passing into nirvāṇa.

Through accomplishing their practice in the abode
of the Perfection of Wisdom, skillful means, and primordial wisdom,
because all phenomena are utterly purified,
saṃsāra itself becomes completely pure.

If one disciplines through attachment and the rest,
then throughout saṃsāra, constantly and without end,
in order to cleanse the world's wrongdoing,
one disciplines oneself within the span of saṃsāra.

Just as a deep-red lotus
is unstained by the flaw of its coloring dye,
so likewise those who benefit beings
are unstained by the flaw of abiding within it.

With great desire thoroughly purified,
with great bliss and great wealth,
by attaining sovereignty over the three realms,
one makes the welfare of sentient beings stable.

Vajrapāṇi, whoever arises in the morning and daily recites, hears, holds, reads, and meditates upon these Dharma categories belonging to the gateway of the mode of the Perfection of Wisdom will attain ease of mind and the attainment of all happiness. They will also attain in this very life the supreme accomplishment of the great bliss samaya of the vajra that fulfills all purposes. The supreme secret of all Tathāgatas will likewise be accomplished, and they will become a great Vajradhara of all, or a Tathāgata.

Then the Blessed One declared to Vajrapāṇi, Lord of Secrets: "Lord of Secrets, these are the twenty-five mantra syllables of the gateway of the mode of the Perfection of Wisdom:

Oṃ — the vajra of awakening mind. Oṃ — the conduct of Samantabhadra. Oṃ — the wish-fulfilling jewel. Oṃ — the unobstructed. Oṃ — the reversal of birth. Oṃ — all consciousnesses. Oṃ — the great nature of reality free from attachment. Oṃ — the armor of diligence. Oṃ — the all-pervading. Oṃ — the vajra-firm mind, Hūṃ Phaṭ. Oṃ — all Tathāgatas. Oṃ — the essential nature utterly purified. Oṃ — the knowledge of the nature of reality utterly purified. Oṃ — the thus-action that thoroughly purifies. Hūṃ. Oṃ — assuredly destructive vajra-bearer, Phaṭ. Oṃ — desiring what is desired. Oṃ — the one bearing the vajra of renunciation. Oṃ Hūṃ — the taming of all. Oṃ Hrīḥ Oṃ A — the gateway called thus. Oṃ — the Perfection of Wisdom. Āṃ Bhaṃ Hūṃ A. Oṃ. Oṃ — the supreme body of all Tathāgatas. Oṃ — the speech of all Tathāgatas, utterly pure. Oṃ — the vajra mind of all Tathāgatas, Āḥ. Oṃ A — may all the bonds of evil be shattered! May you be liberated from the continuum of all lower rebirths! Oṃ — the vajra of all samayas. Hūṃ Tra Ṭ.

This is the mudrā and mantra for destroying evil. One will become a great Vajradhara of the supreme secret accomplishment of all Tathāgatas, and a Tathāgata.

Vajrapāṇi, this will not be heard in the ear-passage of sentient beings who have not generated roots of virtue. Sentient beings who have not generated roots of virtue are unable to hear it, write it, have it written, read it, retain it, meditate upon it, show it reverence, have it depicted, or offer worship to it."

Those who hear even a single syllable from this categorized teaching of the Dharma in the manner of the Perfection of Wisdom — those sentient beings will have generated roots of virtue before many buddhas in other lives. What need is there to speak of those who have fulfilled them completely?

Vajrapāṇi, there are those who have not shown respect, not held as their teacher, not paid homage to, and not made offerings to buddhas numbering in the tens of millions of koṭis, as many as the grains of sand in eighty Ganges rivers. Those persons are unable to hear this categorized teaching of the Dharma, this gateway to the manner of the Perfection of Wisdom.

Whatever place this categorized teaching of the Dharma resides in, that place shall become a stūpa. Whatever person carries this categorized teaching of the Dharma on their body, or writes it in a book and makes use of it, that person shall become a worthy object of veneration. They shall remember their births across many tens of millions of eons. The evil māra shall not be able to create obstacles for them. The four great kings and the other gods shall constantly follow close behind that person in order to protect, guard, and shelter them. They shall not die an untimely death before their time has come. All buddhas and bodhisattvas shall hold them in their regard. In brief, they shall be reborn in whatever buddha-field they desire.

Thus, although this categorized teaching of the Dharma, this gateway to the manner of the Perfection of Wisdom, has many benefits, I have set forth only a portion of them.

Having spoken these words, the Blessed One finished. The bodhisattva mahāsattva Vajrapāṇi, those bodhisattvas, that entire assembled retinue, together with the gods, humans, asuras, and gandharvas — the whole world rejoiced and praised what the Blessed One had declared.

Translator's notes

The text is the opening of the Āryaprajñāpāramitānayaśatapañcāśatikā, a short Perfection of Wisdom sūtra. The long epithetic description of the Blessed One is structurally a single sentence in Tibetan and is rendered here as such in compressed form.

The phrase rendered "the one who, the very moment of generating the awakening mind, turns the wheel of Dharma" is a functional description of a bodhisattva whose name in this list is given descriptively rather than as a proper noun in the Tibetan; it corresponds to a known epithet in this sūtra's tradition.

The series of "perfectly pure abodes" beginning with "supreme joy" and continuing through the list is a characteristic doctrinal feature of this text, in which samsaric states and qualities are recast as purified stations of the bodhisattva path. The term rendered "extreme of attachment" for attachment-extreme follows the Tibetan closely and signals the purification of desire at its outermost reach rather than mere ordinary attachment. The phrase rendered "who is Vajrapāṇi" reflects the Tibetan's appositive construction identifying the Blessed One in this tantra as Vajrapāṇi himself, a standard tantric identification.
