About this text

The Noble Dhāraṇī Called the King of Detailed Rites, the Secret of the Supreme Secret, the Well-Established Palace of the Vast Great Jewel, known in Sanskrit as the Ārya Mahāmaṇi Vipula Vimāna Supratiṣṭhita Guhya Parama Rahasya Kalparāja Nāma Dhāraṇī, is a lengthy dhāraṇī scripture, with its Tibetan title rendering these same epithets. It opens at Rājagṛha, where the Blessed One, having defeated ten million māras and subdued the Mīmāṃsakas, wandering ascetics, naked ascetics, and other non-Buddhist teachers, has crossed to the far shore of saṃsāra. The vast armies of Māra then fill Jambudvīpa, setting the stage for the Buddha's teaching of protective incantations and the detailed ritual procedures that accompany them. Organized in more than one volume, the work belongs to the class of dhāraṇī and ritual texts that combine narrative framing with practical rites. It is Toh 885 in the Kangyur, where it is found among the collected dhāraṇīs.

Translation

In the language of India: ārya mahā maṇi vipula vimāna supratiṣṭhita guhya parama hasya kalā rājā nāma dhāraṇī

In the language of Tibet: The Noble Dhāraṇī Called the King of Detailed Rites, the Secret of the Supreme Secret, the Well-Established Palace of the Vast Great Jewel.

The first volume.

Homage to all buddhas and bodhisattvas.

Then, at the first assembly in the great city of Rājagṛha, the Blessed One defeated ten million māras. He also subdued and defeated the Mīmāṃsakas, the wandering ascetics, all the naked ascetics, and the other non-Buddhists (tīrthikas). Then he went beyond the faults of saṃsāra, to the far shore of the river of saṃsāra.

At that time the armies of Māra, many hundreds of thousands of koṭis of niyutas of them, filled Jambudvīpa with their servant hosts of māras in countless multitudes. Then the Blessed One blessed the ground so that it became vajra. Those beings did not even hear the frightening and terrifying sounds, and every rain of weapons fell as a shower of various flowers.

In the middle of a crossroads of the city of Rājagṛha, the ground split open. A radiant lotus of the seven precious substances, with a thousand petals, rose from it. Its stem was beryl, its center was gold from the Jambū river, its anthers were emerald, and its height reached as far as the world of Brahmā. Once the lotus had appeared, it lit up all ten directions.

Then from that lotus came this sound, an exhortation by means of the dhāraṇī called Endowed with the Supreme Jewel Vajra:

"Homage to all the tathāgatas who dwell in the ten directions!

oṃ maṇi vajra hṛdaya vajra māra sainya vidravani hana hana vajra garbhe trāsaya trāsaya sarva māra bhuvanāni hūṃ hūṃ sandhara sandhara buddha maitri sarva tathāgata vajre kalpa adhiṣṭhite svāhā"

Again the sound "Excellent!" rang out from the lotus, filling this great trichiliocosm with sound: "Śākyamuni has crossed the wilderness of existence. He has vanquished Māra. He has cast off the dust of the afflictions. He has broken open the eggshell of ignorance. He has lit the great lamp of the Dharma. Through the power of this dhāraṇī he blessed the ground so that it became vajra and vanquished Māra. Excellent, excellent!"

Then the Lord of Secrets, Vajrapāṇi, his hair standing on end and his body trembling, touched the two feet of the Blessed One and said to him, "Blessed One, why has this great vidyā-mantra appeared in Jambudvīpa at the first assembly? Blessed One, I have never heard that this vidyā-mantra appeared in the world before."

Then the Blessed One said to the Lord of Secrets, Vajrapāṇi, "Vajrapāṇi, there is a dhāraṇī called the Secret of the Well-Established Palace of the Vast Great Jewel. Through its power this great trichiliocosm has become vajra by nature, and the rain of weapons falling here has turned into various flowers as it falls. Through the power of this dhāraṇī the retinue of Māra was vanquished and ten million living beings were tamed. Through its power this lotus appeared in the middle of the crossroads.

"Vajrapāṇi, without this dhāraṇī one cannot attain complete and perfect awakening. One does not defeat ten million māras, and one does not dry up the ocean of the afflictions. One cannot light the lamp of the Dharma.

"Vajrapāṇi, though I practiced austerities for many hundreds of thousands of koṭis of niyutas, I could not attain buddhahood until I had heard this dhāraṇī and made diligent effort with intense striving. Only afterward did I attain buddhahood.

"Vajrapāṇi, this dhāraṇī has great power and great benefit. Vajrapāṇi, it is the nature of reality, the suchness of the buddhas, the blessed ones, and it fully accomplishes the body of the perfectly awakened. Vajrapāṇi, by merely holding its name, one will have held the names of all the tathāgatas. By merely uttering and reciting it, one will have made offerings to all the tathāgatas, honored them, and bowed at their feet."

Then the bodhisattva mahāsattva Vajrapāṇi made offerings to the Blessed One with various flowers, scents, and various kinds of incense. He circled the Blessed One three times, touched the Blessed One's feet, and said to him, "Blessed One, please teach what special qualities are found in that Sahā world. Blessed One, beings do not build up their accumulations with meager roots of virtue other than these foundations of the true Dharma, the body of the Tathāgata."

Then the Blessed One said to the Lord of Secrets, Vajrapāṇi, "Vajrapāṇi, those beings are lazy and weak in diligence. They are bewildered by delusion and by the arrogance of desire. They are hard to convince. They do not honor their fathers, their mothers, ascetics, or brahmins, and they do not serve the head of their family. This dhāraṇī will not come into their hands. Those of little merit, little fortune, and weak intellect will not hear it and will not explain it. They will not develop faith in this dhāraṇī, taught by the Tathāgata, which destroys all wrongdoing."

Then the Blessed One said to the great yakṣa general Vajrapāṇi, "Son of noble family, east of where I am, past immeasurable buddha fields, past hundreds of thousands of koṭis of niyutas of buddha fields as many as the grains of sand in the Ganges river, there is a world called Jewel Lamp. Its city is surrounded on every side by a wall twelve yojanas long, made of the seven precious substances. It is prosperous, happy, and delightful, with good harvests, and it is full of people, of many human beings. Its men, women, boys, and girls, all its crowds of people, appear adorned with every kind of ornament, their heads decked with various head ornaments of blazing jewels and gold. They have the forms of gods. They possess great might and great diligence, and they have every kind of friend. They have mastered the fields of knowledge, and they go about radiant with light."

"In that world-system a king called Various Jewels holds sovereignty. He is surrounded by eight hundred million ministers, and he has a chief queen called Illuminator, who has a retinue of twenty thousand women and whose body rivals that of every daughter of the gods. In that world-system, too, all the flowering trees, fruit trees, juice trees, and fragrant trees are made of the seven precious substances. All the flowers that grow in water are made of the seven precious substances. All the flowers that grow on dry land are made of Jambū river gold. The lifespan of its people lasts eighty thousand eons. All of them follow the ten virtuous courses of action, and they have deep faith in the Buddha, the Dharma, and the Saṅgha. That king, moreover, rules his kingdom in accordance with the Dharma, not contrary to the Dharma, for he has the intention to benefit all sentient beings.

"In that world-system, too, there is a tathāgata, an arhat, a perfectly complete buddha called Great Jewel, Brilliantly Variegated, Well Established and Pure. Surrounded by a great host of bodhisattvas and by many tens of millions of vidyā-mantra holders, he performs the deeds of a buddha. That Tathāgata has a golden-colored body with the thirty-two major marks and the eighty excellent minor marks. He is encircled by a halo a fathom wide, and his light is as resplendent as the seven precious substances. All those bodhisattvas, too, have golden-colored bodies radiating light, endowed with the marks, and they sit on lotus seats made of the seven precious substances. All of them have inexhaustible eloquence, and unobscured wisdom is their domain. Having mastered the fields of knowledge, they contemplate the dhāraṇī called the Secret of the Vast Palace of the Great Jewel, Well Established, and through the power of that very dhāraṇī these special qualities have come about. That Tathāgata, too, teaches this dhāraṇī to all sentient beings. Through the power of this dhāraṇī those beings experience happiness, and all of them have been completely freed from the hells, from birth as animals, from the world of Yama, and from the realms of the asuras. All the doors to the lower realms have been closed. The doors to the higher realms have been opened. All the doors to the happy destinies have been opened, and all the sentient beings of that world-system have truly entered upon unsurpassed, perfectly complete awakening. In that world-system all sentient beings abide with minds of loving-kindness, just as water and milk become one.

"When that Tathāgata was formerly a bodhisattva, while practicing this Secret of the Vast Palace of the Great Jewel, Well Established, he made this aspiration: 'May all sentient beings who are born in my buddha field be certain never to turn back from unsurpassed, perfectly complete awakening.' Such was the aspiration he made. Those who hear this dhāraṇī, hold it, read it, master it, meditate on it, recite it, contemplate it, and practice it; or, at the very least, who hear its name, touch it with their hands, wear it on their bodies, merely see it with their eyes, or see it written in a book, written on cloth, or written on a wall; even those who have committed the five acts of immediate retribution, who are headed for the Avīci hell, who have rejected the true Dharma, or who have slandered the noble ones; butchers, caṇḍālas, hunters, fowlers, the blind, the deaf, lepers, the destitute, the one-eyed, hunchbacks, the lowly, those with obscurations projected by past karma, those whose karma is not yet fixed to be experienced, those bound by the noose of Māra, those fallen into wrong views, those seized by vināyakas, and those harmed by the lunar mansions and the planets: all of them will certainly never turn back from unsurpassed, perfectly complete awakening. Even those born as animals, namely wild beasts, birds, mosquitoes, flies, rats, ants and beetles, and every other kind of living creature, whether born from an egg, born from a womb, born from warmth and moisture, or born miraculously, all of them will never turn back from unsurpassed, perfectly complete awakening. One should have no doubt or hesitation about this."

Then, as soon as the Blessed One had spoken these words, that whole assembly rejoiced, and the pores of their skin tingled with delight. A rain of divine flowers fell: utpalas, lotuses, kumudas, white lotuses, māndāravas, and great māndāravas. Divine cymbals resounded. Rays of light came forth from the bodies of everyone in that circle of the assembly.

Before the Blessed One there also appeared a many-storied mansion of the seven precious substances, brilliant, lovely to behold, perfectly proportioned, and delightful. It stood on four pillars, had four projecting doorways and four stairways, and blazed with light. At its four corners four great precious jewels blazed with light like the sun. Many nets of jewels, precious stones, and pearls hung from it. It was beautified with many bells, adorned all over with many silk banners, and beautified with clusters of many kinds of flowers.

This earth, too, shook in six ways: it quaked and trembled, resounded, and rose and sank. All the abodes of the gods were illuminated by light, and all who dwell in the abodes of the gods were roused. The Four Great Kings were roused as well. All the abodes of the vidyā-mantra holders were roused too. All the abodes of the gods blazed, blazed up on every side, and became a single mass of flame, and all the obstructing vināyakas were terrified; crying out in distress, they scattered in the ten directions.

Then from the Blessed One's ūrṇā, the curl of hair between his brows, rays of light spread out. They lit up every world realm in the ten directions and illuminated every buddha field. Having roused those buddhas, the blessed ones, the light returned and dissolved into the crown of his head.

Then those buddhas, the blessed ones, offered their approval to the Tathāgata, the arhat, the perfectly complete Buddha Śākyamuni: "Blessed One, excellent, excellent! Blessed One, the time has come for you to go and see the Tathāgata Maṇibhadra, Resplendent, Purely Established, to pay homage to him and to attend upon him. So go, and listen in full to the dhāraṇī called the Secret of the Well-Established Palace of the Vast Great Jewel. Blessed One, that dhāraṇī has great power and great benefit. That dhāraṇī was blessed by the tathāgatas of the past. Whoever hears it, or at the least hears its name, or even touches it with their hand, will become irreversible and will awaken fully to unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening."

Then the Blessed One understood that the buddhas, the blessed ones who had come from all the buddha fields of the ten directions, had made this request. Once again he sent out a hundred thousand koṭis of niyutas of light rays, and he summoned the retinue that had gathered and spoke these words: "Children of good family, come here. This is the sign of the Tathāgata."

Then the Blessed One himself rose from his seat, and in the middle of the multistoried palace a vajra lion throne appeared. On that throne appeared a jewel lotus, brilliant, beautiful to behold and delightful, with a stem of gold and a core of red pearls, and on it the Blessed One sat. The moment the Blessed One sat down, this great earth shook violently. And the Blessed One entered into the meditative absorption called the Pure Great Jewel.

The whole retinue took heart, and in the middle of the sky above, the entire maṇḍala of the retinue was arrayed. With a great retinue and a vast array, with the great blessing of the Buddha and his emanations, with a retinue of many bodhisattvas, with gods, nāgas, yakṣas, gandharvas, asuras, garuḍas, kinnaras, mahoragas, humans and many non-humans, with a retinue of many millions of vidyā-mantra holders, together with Vajrapāṇi, the Lord of Secrets, with Śakra (Indra), Brahmā, all the world protectors and the Four Great Kings, they set out.

All of them were seen to go off toward the east, passing beyond a hundred thousand koṭis of niyutas of buddha fields, as many as the sands of measureless Ganges rivers, and arriving in the world realm called Jewel Lamp. By the power of the Blessed One's miraculous ability they reached that world realm in an instant, a moment, a split second. As soon as they arrived in that world realm, they descended from the sky and went before the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Maṇibhadra, Resplendent, Purely Established. Having come there, he said, "Blessed One, are you free from harm? Do you move about with ease? Do you dwell in comfort?" and he offered a thousand-petaled lotus made of the seven precious substances.

Then, as the Blessed One was seated with his retinue in the divine palace, that Tathāgata stretched out his golden-colored hand. Profoundly and deeply he gladdened the Tathāgata, the arhat, the perfectly complete Buddha Śākyamuni. Seated in that very multistoried palace, that Blessed One in turn asked: "Blessed One, have you turned the wheel of Dharma well? Have you vanquished the armies of Māra together with their mounts? Have you lit the lamp of Dharma? Have you raised the banner of Dharma? Have you beaten the drum of Dharma? Have you sounded the conch of Dharma? Blessed One, appearing in the world as a buddha, have you come to know unsurpassed omniscience? Blessed One, today, in Jambudvīpa, please turn the wheel of Dharma, the treasury of Dharma, a second time."

The moment the Blessed One spoke these words, that entire buddha field and world realm shook in six ways. It shook, shook strongly and shook strongly everywhere. It moved, moved strongly and moved strongly everywhere. It quaked, quaked strongly and quaked strongly everywhere. It rumbled, rumbled strongly and rumbled strongly everywhere. It resounded, resounded strongly and resounded strongly everywhere.

A great rain of flowers also poured down, and a great display of miraculous power appeared. Musical instruments gave forth sound without being played. In the sky above, the young gods played delightful divine instruments of fivefold music. All the great nāgas there sent down a rain of various jewels. They also sent down a rain of various fragrant powders and various sandalwood waters. They also sent down a rain of various garments, ornaments and streamers. They also sent down a rain of divine red pearls, agate, the great precious jewel Vairocana, the great precious jewel Sūryagarbha, water crystal, fire crystal and Śrīgarbha jewels. They also sent down a rain of divine flowers: blue lotuses, lotuses, white water lilies, white lotuses, māndārava flowers and great māndārava flowers. They also sent down a rain of divine flowers made of gold from the Jambū river and flowers made of divine silver. They also sent down a rain of divine flower garlands.

The young gods dwelling in the midst of the sky uttered the sweet sound "alala," proclaimed it in melody, and gave their approval: "The Blessed Śākyamuni has turned the wheel of Dharma a second time in the world, and has set up in Jambudvīpa the great precious wish-fulfilling jewel, the banner of Dharma, and namely

and has caused the great detailed rite, the King of the Secret of the Well-Established Palace of the Vast Great Jewel, to be widely practiced in Jambudvīpa. Excellent, excellent!"

Then the Tathāgata, the Arhat, the Perfectly Awakened Buddha Maṇibhadra the Radiant, Pure and Well Established sent forth rays of light of many kinds from the ūrṇā hair between his brows. Those rays of light roused and illuminated all the buddhas, the blessed ones, in all the buddha fields of every world system in the ten directions. They also illuminated this great trichiliocosm, the world system of a billion worlds. They roused and illuminated all the abodes of the gods, all the abodes of the nāgas, the hells, the animal realms, the world of Yama, and the realms of the asuras as well. Then the light turned back, circled the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Śākyamuni, three times, and entered once again into the uṣṇīṣa.

Then all those buddhas, the blessed ones, of all the buddha fields of the ten directions performed miraculous displays of magical power from their own buddha fields, and blessed buddhas as numerous as the sands of countless Ganges rivers came to that buddha field. By the miracle of each one's magical power they emanated multistoried houses made of the seven precious substances. Inside those multistoried houses appeared lion thrones made of gold from the Jambū river, and all those buddhas, the blessed ones, entered the multistoried houses together with their own retinues.

Then the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Maṇibhadra the Radiant, Pure and Well Established, completely delighted all those tathāgatas and made fine offerings to them by a great miracle of magical power, and then they sat upon their own lion thrones.

Then the bodhisattva, the great being Maṇigarbha, went to where the Tathāgata Maṇibhadra the Radiant, Pure and Well Established was. On arriving, he bowed his head at the feet of the Blessed One and said this to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, a great assembly of buddhas is fully present. A great assembly of bodhisattvas, and all the great assemblies of gods, nāgas, yakṣas, asuras, garuḍas, kinnaras, mahoragas, humans and nonhumans are also fully present. A great gathering of the hosts of vidyā-mantra holders has also arrived; that is to say, the host of great bodhisattvas such as Vajrapāṇi has also arrived. Blessed One, since a great miracle of magical power has also taken place, Blessed One, this is the time to teach the Secret of the Well-Established Palace of the Vast Great Jewel. This is the moment." Thus he entreated a second and a third time.

Then the Blessed One, the Tathāgata, the Arhat, the Perfectly Awakened Buddha Maṇibhadra the Radiant, Pure and Well Established, said to the bodhisattva, the great being Maṇigarbha: "Son of noble family, go and make your request to the Tathāgata, the Arhat, the Perfectly Awakened Buddha Śākyamuni, and he will teach it to you."

Then the bodhisattva, the great being Maṇigarbha, went to where the Blessed One, the Tathāgata, the Arhat, the Perfectly Awakened Buddha Śākyamuni was. On arriving, he circled the Blessed One three times and sat before him. Then the bodhisattva, the great being Maṇigarbha, said this to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, out of compassion for all sentient beings, please teach this Secret of the Well-Established Palace of the Vast Great Jewel."

Then the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Śākyamuni, understanding that the request had been made, said to the Lord of Secrets, Vajrapāṇi: "Lord of Secrets, go toward the center of this great assembly and strike the ground with your vajra!"

Then the Lord of Secrets, Vajrapāṇi, having heard what the Blessed One said, went to where the center of the great maṇḍala of the assembly's ground was and struck that spot with his vajra. The moment he struck the ground, a square became visible there. This great trichiliocosm, the world system of a billion worlds, also quaked in six ways. The moment it quaked, there appeared on that spot a multistoried house of the seven precious substances: square, set on four pillars, with four doors opening out, even, delightful, well proportioned, beautifully furnished, radiant, lovely to behold, blazing with light, with four flights of steps, three yojanas in height and five yojanas in width.

In the center of that multistoried house there also appeared a stupa made of gold from the Jambū river, adorned with many nets of jewels, precious gems and pearls, covered with a net of bells and tinkling bells made of the seven precious substances, hung with many bells, beautified with many silk tassels, and richly adorned with many garlands of flowers. In the center of that stupa resided the bodies of three tathāgatas.

Then all those buddhas, the blessed ones, made offerings to that multistoried house. The retinue of bodhisattvas also made offerings to that multistoried house and to the stupa, and paid them honor, with flowers, incense, perfumes, garlands, ointments, parasols, victory banners, flags and music, joining their palms and bowing.

Then the many assemblies of gods, nāgas, yakṣas, gandharvas, asuras, garuḍas, kinnaras, mahoragas, humans and nonhumans were struck with wonder and amazement. Overjoyed, they raised a loud clamor and shouts of laughter, crying, "Why have this multistoried house and this stupa appeared?" They circled the stupa and the multistoried house, made offerings, paid them honor, and made offerings with flowers, incense, perfumes, garlands, ointments, cymbals of various kinds and the beating of drums. All those bodhisattvas and the whole circle of the assembly also sat with joined palms and bowed.

Then a voice came from the multistoried structure: "O great assembly, look at what appears in the sky! Look!"

Then the whole great assembly gathered there looked up into the sky. The moment they looked, a great mass of clouds appeared, formed of beryl and of great precious jewels. Then, upon that mass of clouds, they saw this dhāraṇī called the Secret of the Well-Established Palace of the Vast Great Jewel written in golden letters. From the very middle of the sky a voice came: "All buddhas, blessed ones, please recite the dhāraṇī called the Secret of the Well-Established Palace of the Vast Great Jewel! Please recite it!"

The moment the voice came, the writing appeared on the mass of clouds before each tathāgata, and these words were also proclaimed in song: "Homage to the Blessed One Śākyamuni! Homage! Open the door of this supreme stūpa! In the middle of that stūpa reside the bodies of three tathāgatas. By their power this great and special display of miraculous emanation appears, and by that same power the magical display of all the tathāgatas also appears. The maṇḍala, the rite, the vidyā-mantras of the dhāraṇī of the Secret of the Well-Established Palace of the Vast Great Jewel, together with all the requisites of the detailed rite, will be well and thoroughly explained in the midst of the assembly, complete and in full."

Then all those buddhas, blessed ones, who had gathered from the ten directions spoke these words to the Tathāgata, the Arhat, the Perfectly Complete Buddha Śākyamuni: "Blessed One, please open this precious stūpa and reveal those who reside within it, namely the tathāgatas, arhats, perfectly complete buddhas called Jewel King Flower Banner, Radiant Jewel, and Noble Vajra King."

Then the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Śākyamuni, by a great miracle of magical power went to where the multistoried stūpa stood. Extending his golden-colored right hand, adorned with the marks born of a hundred merits, he opened the leaves of the door of that supreme stūpa and revealed the bodies of the tathāgatas.

Then those tathāgatas gave their approval to the Tathāgata Śākyamuni, saying: "Excellent, Śākyamuni! Excellent! Today you have turned the wheel of Dharma in Jambudvīpa for a second time. Blessed One, sit upon this very lion throne!" Then the Tathāgata Śākyamuni sat upon that very throne within the stūpa.

Then the bodhisattva, the great being Vajrapāṇi, said to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, why have these tathāgatas appeared within the multistoried stūpa? And from where have they come?"

The Blessed One said: "Vajrapāṇi, in the past, inconceivable, measureless, immeasurable, unfathomable, incalculable, boundless and utterly countless eons ago, at a time when Jambudvīpa was prosperous, flourishing, with good harvests, delightful, and filled with many people and folk, when sāli rice grew without being plowed or sown, when nothing was claimed as one's own and nothing was possessed, and the whole world was endowed with happiness, at that time there was no buddha in Jambudvīpa.

"At that time three sages dwelt on the king of mountains called Jewel Mountain. The name of the first was Jewel Crest, the second was Golden Crest, and the third was called Vajra Crest. They thought, 'How shall we attain buddhahood itself, perfectly complete awakening unsurpassed in the world, in order to liberate all sentient beings?' And single-mindedly they kept the Buddha in mind, kept the Dharma in mind, and kept the Saṅgha in mind.

"Then, having thought this, those sages fell silent for a moment, and as they contemplated, they attained the samādhi called the Palace of Radiant Loving-Kindness That Delights All Beings. The divine eye arose in them, and when they looked upward, a god of the realm of the Pure Abodes called out from the sky: 'Excellent, holy beings! Excellent! This generating of the mind toward unsurpassed, perfectly complete awakening that you have done is excellent!'"

"O good men, the Secret of the Well-Established Palace of the Vast Great Jewel was spoken by the tathāgatas of the past for the benefit of all sentient beings. Merely by hearing it, one will attain everything from irreversibility up to unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening. All dharmas will become manifest. All the gateways of dhāraṇī will become manifest. All māras will be stunned. The lamp of all dharmas will be lit. All roots of virtue will become manifest. The six perfections will be brought to completion. One will be completely freed from the hells, from birth as an animal, from the world of Yama, from the realm of the hungry ghosts, and from the states of the asuras. One will gain complete freedom from birth, aging, sickness, death, sorrow, lamentation, suffering, unhappiness and agitation.

"In later times, in a future age, there will appear in this Jambudvīpa beings who do not honor their fathers, do not honor their mothers, do not honor śramaṇas and do not honor brahmins. There will be the nonvirtuous, those who reject the true Dharma, those who disparage the noble ones, those bound for the Avīci hell, those who deceive buddhas and bodhisattvas, those who kill arhats, those who commit the five deeds of immediate retribution, those who kill brahmins, those who kill cattle, those who commit robbery, the crooked, those who lie in wait at toll posts, those who speak falsehood, those who take what is not given, those who engage in sexual misconduct, those who speak divisively, those who speak idle words, those who cheat in measuring, those who cheat in the methods of measurement, those who cheat with the scales, those who seize the wealth of others, those who embezzle what was entrusted to them, those who betray their friends, and those gripped by the obscurations of their former karma.

"All of these, through the power of this dhāraṇī, whether they merely hear it, or read it, or master it fully, or wear it on the body, or write it down and carry it, or fasten it to the top of a banner, or make it into a book, or write it on cloth, or write it on silk, or write it on a wall, or write it on a board, or at the very least hear its sound, or touch it with the hand, or are touched by its shadow, or even if they are touched by another being who has done so, all of them without doubt will become irreversible and will awaken into unsurpassed, perfect and complete buddhahood.

"In this very life, too, they will gain boundless hundreds of thousands of good qualities. They will be free of all evil. They will possess all roots of virtue. They will put an end to all māras. The whole world will be pleased with them. They will be worthy of the offerings of all beings. Kings, princes, ministers and queens, together with their retinues, will all be pleased with them. All śramaṇas and brahmins will be pleased with them. Their words will be worthy of acceptance. Their hands and feet will always be soft and tender. Their speech will be gentle and pleasant. They will not be poor, and will not experience worldly suffering.

"Their bodies will not be harmed by poison, nor by weapons, nor by fire, nor by water. They will not be harmed by the danger of savage wild beasts, nor by the danger of brigands, nor by the danger of robbers, nor by the danger of barbarians, nor by the danger of roads and losing the way, nor by the danger of yakṣas, nor by the danger of bhūtas, nor by the danger of rākṣasas, nor by the danger of piśācas, nor by the danger of ḍākinīs, and no danger from snakes will arise.

"Their bodies will not be harmed by fever: neither one-day fever, nor two-day fever, nor three-day fever, nor four-day fever, nor chronic fever; no fever of any kind will harm their bodies.

"No disease of the eyes will arise, nor disease of the ears, nor disease of the nose, nor disease of the tongue, nor disease of the mouth, nor disease of the teeth, nor disease of the lips, nor disease of the throat, nor earache, nor disease of the hands, nor disease of the back, nor disease of the bladder, nor hemorrhoids, nor urinary disease, nor diarrhea, nor swellings, nor disease of the thighs, nor disease of the calves, nor disease of the feet. No disease whatsoever will arise. That is to say, abscesses, sores, erysipelas, stomach pain, itching, blisters, mange, leprosy, ulcers of the feet and the like will not arise in any way at all.

"They will not suffer pain in the brain, nor paralysis of one side. They will not become blind, nor become one-eyed, nor be hunchbacked. They will not die through the power of others. They will not become deaf, nor become blotched with skin disease, nor live in contempt. These distinctive qualities will arise for them in accordance with the Dharma.

"Even at the time of death their confidence will be inexhaustible. They will not die with a disturbed mind. All the tathāgatas will show their faces to them directly and give them comfort. Their bodies will not be harmed by sorcery or by spells that cause distraction. Their bodies will not be harmed by the application of mantras. They will fall asleep in happiness and wake in happiness. Even in their dreams they will see hundreds of thousands of buddhas. They will see all the tathāgatas surrounded by hosts of bodhisattvas. Through the power of the secret dhāraṇī, such distinctive qualities arise."

This is what they saw being proclaimed.

Then the joy of those sages touched their hearts, and becoming elated, their bodies dissolved like melting lumps of butter, and the melted liquid sank into the earth. When ten months had passed, those bamboo shoots split open, and from within them emerged three boys, handsome and lovely, beautiful to behold, endowed with the finest radiant complexion. Then those boys sat down cross-legged in that very place, beside them, and remained in meditation. When seven days had passed, on that day they awakened into perfect and complete buddhahood. Their bodies became golden in color, adorned with the thirty-two major marks of a great being and the eighty excellent minor marks, and wholly adorned with a halo of light a fathom wide. And those bamboo shoots became multistoried mansions.

This dhāraṇī, the Secret of the Well-Established Palace of the Vast Great Jewel, also appeared in the sky in letters of gold. The multistoried mansions of the bamboo shoots, too, are these. The gathered assembly of bodhisattvas, too, is this one. Vajrapāṇi, should you think that the one called Pure Abode who at that time, on that occasion, showed honor and service to those sages and to those Tathāgatas was someone else, do not see it that way. Why is that? This Tathāgata, the arhat, the perfectly complete Buddha, the Radiant and Well-Established Maṇibhadra, when he was the attendant called Pure Abode, was prophesied by them to become a tathāgata.

This secret dhāraṇī of all the Tathāgatas is the mother of all the Tathāgatas, the secret essence of all the Tathāgatas, the turning of the wheel of Dharma of all the Tathāgatas, the secret of all the Tathāgatas going to the seat of awakening, the secret of all the Tathāgatas going to the vajra lion throne, the secret of the miraculous powers and magical displays of all the Tathāgatas, the secret of all the Tathāgatas' complete fulfillment of the six perfections, the secret of the intent of the Perfection of Wisdom of all the Tathāgatas, the secret of the fields of knowledge of all the Tathāgatas, the secret of the maṇḍala of all the Tathāgatas, the secret of all the Tathāgatas' true holding of the mudrās, the secret of all the Tathāgatas' emitting of ten million rays of light, the secret of the true limit of all the Tathāgatas, the secret of the blessing of the miraculous display of the meditative absorption of all the Tathāgatas, the secret of the ornament of the awakening mind of all the Tathāgatas, the secret by which the buddhas establish all bodhisattvas in everything, the secret of fully realizing and comprehending the meditative absorptions of all the Tathāgatas, the secret that destroys all obscurations. Excellent, excellent, that you have spoken this!"

Then the Tathāgata, the arhat, the perfectly complete Buddha Śākyamuni, with deliberate intent and full knowledge, looked upon all the Tathāgatas and emitted hundreds of thousands of billions of light rays of various colors, namely blue, yellow, red, white, crimson, crystal, and silver. They went forth into the endless, boundless world systems and illuminated all the buddha fields. Having exhorted all the Tathāgatas, they turned back, circled the Blessed One three times, and entered into the crown protuberance on his head. The rite of the root vidyā-mantra depends neither on the lunar day nor on the constellation, and fasting is not required either. Before an image of the Tathāgata, or before a stūpa containing relics, one should do just ten thousand recitations. Then, on the fifteenth day, the day of the full moon, one should bathe thoroughly, put on clean clothes, and make offerings as one's means allow before the stūpa containing relics. One should offer four lamps. One should also offer scents, flowers, and so on. One should eat the three white foods. One should circumambulate the stūpa one hundred and eight times. If, having recited the vidyā-mantra one hundred and eight times, one sleeps right there before it, one will see the Tathāgata before dawn breaks. One will also see noble Vajrapāṇi. All one's wishes will be completely fulfilled. Whatever one desires, one will obtain. Even if one is someone who has committed the five deeds of immediate retribution, if one practices at the three times of day, one will without doubt see them. One should not give rise to doubt or hesitation.

If one rises in the morning and does one hundred and eight recitations, all one's aims will be accomplished. Poison will not affect his body, nor will harmful concoctions, nor water, nor fire. Robbers will not rob him. Disease will not afflict his body. Enemies will not frighten him. No illness will arise in his body: no eye disease, no nose disease, no tongue disease, no ear disease, no tooth disease, no brain disease, no disease of the limbs or the lesser limbs, and no fever. Fevers that recur every day, every second day, every third day, or every fourth day will not afflict his body. He will not be troubled by fear of snakes or by fear of ferocious beasts, and sorcery will not affect his body.

This dhāraṇī is endowed with such qualities as these. It utterly vanquishes all fears. It destroys all wrongdoing. It accomplishes all good qualities. It brings the six perfections to completion. It accomplishes the suchness of the tathāgatas. This dhāraṇī is accomplished simply by reading it, and it carries out every ritual action.

If one climbs to a mountaintop and recites it, wherever one's eyes look, all the wrongdoing of the beings there will be destroyed. Beings will be freed from all the hells. All those who have gone to birth among animals will also be freed. If one enters a temple and recites it, all the gods will do whatever one commands. If one enters an abode of nāgas and recites it, the nāgas will come under one's power. If one recites it facing the sun, the sun will actually descend, remain before one, and grant whatever boon one asks. If one recites it before Vajrapāṇi, he will grant every boon, and one will also see him.

If one recites the mantra one thousand and eight times over sweet flag, eats it, and then enters the royal palace, the queen and her retinue will be delighted, and she will grant whatever one asks. If one recites seven times over peppercorns and puts them in one's mouth, people will grant whatever one asks and will come under one's power. If one recites one thousand and eight times over white mustard seed and scatters it into the sky, all winds and hail will be stopped. If one recites one hundred and eight times over salt and gives it to brahmins, each and every one who eats the salt will come under one's power. If one wishes to bring kṣatriyas under one's power, one should perform a fire offering of white mustard seed. If one recites the mantra one thousand and eight times over guggulu and uses it to fumigate anyone possessed by a graha, that very graha will come down. By fumigating with it, one will be freed from all grahas.

Four incense bowls should be set out.

He will become irreversible, he will remember his births, and he will be held in the minds of all the tathāgatas. If one climbs to the summit of a mountain and recites one hundred thousand times, one will become the lord of the whole world and will do whatever one wishes; all the people of Jambudvīpa will do whatever one commands. If one enters a lake and recites one thousand and eight times, one will see all the nāgas. If one takes white mustard seeds, recites the mantra one thousand and eight times, and scatters them into the sky, a great rain will fall, and all the nāgas will come under one's power. If one recites constantly, one will attain great glory.

One fills a vase with water and pours into it together habshang tseu, palāśa wood, white mustard seed, saffron, and sandalwood, and recites ten thousand times. If one washes with it a person stricken by a grave illness, all illness will vanish and he will be freed from all illness. All his sins too will be destroyed, all sorcery will be destroyed, and he will obtain every kind of glory. If one washes a person stricken by leprosy, he will be completely freed from leprosy. If one also washes a person stricken by the spotted-skin disease, he will be completely freed from the spotted-skin disease. If women who wish for a son bathe with it, they will obtain a son. If one who lacks accomplishment bathes with it, he will attain great accomplishment. And so on: the things to be achieved as worldly accomplishments will also be accomplished, ever higher. One will do whatever one wishes.

Now come the actions of the near-heart mantra. If one recites it one hundred thousand times, all obstructors and vināyakas will touch their heads to one's feet and say, "O vidyādhara, protect me! Do not destroy our lives, and whatever you command, we shall do." If one recites it twenty-one thousand times, all the gods and all the nāgas will come under one's power, and one will become the lord of all vidyā-mantras. They will do whatever one commands. If one recites it thirty thousand times, all the bhūtas, yakṣas, and the rest will come under one's power.

If one performs fifty thousand recitations, whatever one summons will be summoned: a god, a nāga, a yakṣa, a gandharva, a garuḍa, a kinnara, an asura, a vidyādhara, a woman, a brahmin, a kṣatriya, a ruler of the land, a minister, a queen, a head of the town market, a royal chaplain (purohita), and various other sentient beings as well will be summoned. If one performs sixty thousand fire offerings of guggulu and white mustard seed, one will attain the stainless samādhi. With seventy thousand, one will become a universal monarch among the vidyādharas. With eighty thousand, Vajrapāṇi together with the host of his retinue will remain before one. With ninety thousand, all the bodhisattvas will give one encouragement. With a full hundred thousand, one will see all the tathāgatas, and they will declare: "Son of good family, you have accomplished all secret mantras and all treatises; go wherever you wish. Blessed by all the tathāgatas, you will be irreversible and will fully awaken into unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening. With the permission of the tathāgatas, perform all actions as you wish, for other purposes as well, worldly or transcendent."

Next I shall explain the rite of the maṇḍala. First, one should examine thoroughly beforehand, and afterward begin the work. With powders of five colors poured into clean vessels for scents, one should draw a maṇḍala with four gates. Then the center of the maṇḍala should be anointed with sandalwood and honored with saffron. The size of the central maṇḍala is two cubits. The outer maṇḍala is four cubits and has the form of an enclosure. In the middle of the heart of the maṇḍala one should make a celestial palace of the seven precious substances and a buddha's lotus seat, variegated and of seven colors. Upon the heart of the lotus is a golden wheel with a thousand spokes, complete with rim and hub, wreathed in a garland of flames blazing all around; its shaft is of beryl. The heart of the maṇḍala should be furnished with a covering all around. The gates, beginning with the east, should be adorned with parasols about a cubit in size, with victory banners, and with pairs of Chinese silk cloths of five colors.

To the left is Vajrapāṇi in threatening form, holding a yak-tail whisk and a weapon, seated. To the right is the bodhisattva Maṇivajra, adorned with all ornaments, holding a jewel and a yak-tail whisk in his right hand, seated. At the four corners the Four Great Kings should be drawn. They should be drawn in fierce form, wearing armor and coats of mail, holding weapons in their hands, with all ornaments and crowns of matted hair.

At the four intermediate directions four golden vessels filled with scented water should be placed. If there are no golden vessels, one should use earthen jars of no small size, covered with gold. Such vessels, which must not leak, should be filled with milk and placed in the four directions.

On the right side of the maṇḍala is the goddess Mahāśrī, adorned with all ornaments. On the left, draw the great goddess Śaṅkhinī, seated. In the middle of the door, draw the great goddess Vajradūtī, eight-armed, holding various weapons in her hands, adorned with all ornaments, seated.

In the four directions of the heart of the maṇḍala, hanging blue silk and various nourishing essences should be placed. Various arrangements should be set out. Four full vases, thirty-two lamps, and various fruits and flowers should be placed. For the Buddha, the Blessed One, a vase made of gold is placed. Turuṣka should be burned for each. In the heart of the maṇḍala a vase made of silver is placed. For the Vajra Holder there is incense. For the Jewel Holder there is incense of turuṣka, sandalwood, mahārājña, kunduru, kākavija, and white incense. For Śrīdevī there is sandalwood. For Śaṅkhinī there is guggulu. For the Messenger there is śala incense. Each should be burned separately. Torma is also offered. This is the work for the heart of the maṇḍala.

As for the outer maṇḍala, its four doors should be opened wide, beautifully proportioned, and four auspicious doors should be set up. At the eastern door is Viṣṇu with his retinue. In the south is Maheśvara (Śiva). In the west is the yakṣiṇī called Puṣpadantī. In the north one should make the goddess called Bhīmā together with Modesty, along with seven maidens.

Then thirty-two pegs should be driven in, and the pegs are bound with thread of five colors. The four corners are adorned evenly with banners of five colors. The various flowers that are strewn, the torma, and the various flowers should be made even and not disordered. Seven vessels for the various nourishing essences are placed. Thirty-two earthen vessels, thirty-two vessels of flour, thirty-two incense bowls, and one hundred and eight lamps should be placed. One hundred and eight garlands and various vessels filled with perfume should be placed. Incense and various kinds of kunduru are burned.

Then, for entry through the door, two vases filled with perfume are set down. Having offered whatever torma can be obtained, one carries out the rite of bringing in the disciple just as it should be done. Then:

Next I shall explain the rite of the image that accomplishes all aims. On a cloth whose edges have not been cut and which is free of hairs, one cubit or two cubits square, a painter who has kept a fast, using a new vessel and paints without glue, should paint the Blessed One (the Buddha) adorned with all ornaments, seated upon a lotus and teaching the Dharma, surrounded on all sides by a many-storied pavilion.

To the right, Vajrapāṇi should be made twelve-armed, the color of the autumn reed from which arrows are made, holding various weapons in his hands, and four-faced. His first face is peaceful, the second is wrathful, the third bares its fangs, and the fourth is a wrathful, frowning face looking upward. He wears his matted hair in a topknot, is adorned with all ornaments, and should be seated upon a lotus in the half cross-legged posture.

On the left side, the bodhisattva called Maṇivajra should be made four-faced and sixteen-armed. One right hand holds a wish-fulfilling jewel, offering it to the Tathāgata; one left hand holds a lotus; one hand grants reassurance; and two hands should be joined palm to palm. The other hands should hold various implements: a trident, a wheel, a vajra, a sword, a spray of flowers, a rosary, a spouted water pitcher, a sword, a book, a stupa, and Mount Meru. His first face is peaceful. The right face resembles Mahākāla. The left face has the form of Narasiṃha and is the color of the palāśa tree. The upper face has a transformed expression. He should be seated on a lotus seat in the half cross-legged posture.

Below him draw Śaṅkhinī, eight-armed, kneeling, intent upon the act of making offerings to the Blessed One. Below Vajrapāṇi, the great goddess Śrī, kneeling, should be offering a vessel of various precious jewels to the Blessed One, the Tathāgata. Behind the great goddess Śrī there should be the Messenger, with a smiling face, four-armed, adorned with various ornaments, holding various weapons. Behind Śaṅkhinī there should be Puṣpadantī, wearing white garments, holding flowers and gazing up at the Blessed One.

Before the Buddha, the Blessed One, there should be an eight-petaled lotus having the colors of the seven precious substances, its stem made of beryl and its center of gold. Upon it draw a wheel of gold from the Jambū river, fully spread out, complete with its hub, blazing all around and exceedingly brilliant. Near that lotus draw the Four Great Kings wearing helmets, armor, and coats of mail, adorned with all ornaments. Near that lotus there should be a pond adorned with various jewels. On the bank of that pond there should be a vidyādhara holding in one hand a censer and a bunch of flowers, and in the other hand a rosary, kneeling and gazing up at the Tathāgata. Beside the many-storied pavilion, in the midst of the sky, Brahmā, Viṣṇu, Maheśvara (Śiva), and others should be making offerings of various flowers to the Blessed One. These and other rites of the image should be performed.

Then the vidyādhara, wearing white clothes and eating the three white foods, should complete a hundred thousand recitations before the image, from the eighth day of the month until the fifteenth, the full moon. Then he will see the image move and see himself blazing. He will obtain the unobscured eye. He will obtain the samādhi called Pure Jewel Conduct. He will become the universal monarch of all vidyādharas. He will see all the tathāgatas. The very moment he has recited, he will be freed from birth in the hells and among animals. He will abandon attachment, aversion, delusion, and all the rest. He will be free from the stain of stinginess. He will possess all merit. He will possess all happiness. He will take up all roots of virtue. He will be blessed by all the tathāgatas. He will be reassured by all the buddhas and bodhisattvas. He will be upheld by all the gods. He will also be protected by yakṣas, rākṣasas, pretas, piśācas, asuras, garuḍas, kinnaras, mahoragas, humans, and all nonhumans. He will be worthy of veneration in all royal palaces. He will be empowered over the whole world. He will take up all good qualities and perfections. He will obtain these and other distinctive qualities.

If merely by seeing it, merely by reciting it, or merely by reading it one takes up these qualities and benefits, what need is there to speak of doing a great deal more? Any being who reads it, recites it, applies himself diligently to its repetition, practices it, accomplishes the set of mudrās, accomplishes the vidyā-mantra, and accomplishes the pair of cloths, should be called none other than a tathāgata. He becomes worthy of offerings. He becomes worthy of homage from the world with its gods, humans, and asuras. That being should be regarded and known as equal to a tathāgata. Because all the tathāgatas grant him their prophecy, that being, having become irreversible, is clearly prophesied to unsurpassed, perfect, complete awakening. From then on he will not be born in a mother's womb. It should be understood that in whatever world realm he goes to, there he will be born miraculously from a lotus. From then on he will not be parted from the tathāgatas, nor will he be parted from the bodhisattvas. Wherever he is born, there he will meet the tathāgatas everywhere and meet the bodhisattvas, and he will bring the path to completion up to the seat of awakening.

This is the chapter of the great and extensive image, the fifth rite.

Then the Blessed One said to Vajrapāṇi, the Lord of Secrets: "Vajrapāṇi, this is the king of detailed rites. It possesses great and special qualities. This is the essence of all the tathāgatas, the mother who gives birth to all the buddhas. This is the turning of the wheel of Dharma of all the tathāgatas. This is the arrival of all the tathāgatas at the seat of awakening. This is the raising of the victory banner of Dharma of all the tathāgatas. This is the sounding of the Dharma conch of all the tathāgatas. This is the vajra seat of all the tathāgatas. This is that by which all the tathāgatas vanquish and crush. This is the sublime secret of all the tathāgatas. This is the great secret of all the tathāgatas.

"Vajrapāṇi, this tames the afflictions of all the beings of Jambudvīpa. This is the destroyer of evil belonging to all the tathāgatas. This dries up all the hells of all the beings of Jambudvīpa, turns back the world of the hungry ghosts, and frees them from rebirth as animals. This dries up the birth, aging, sickness, death, sorrow, lamentation, suffering, unhappiness and turmoil of all the beings of Jambudvīpa.

"Vajrapāṇi, moreover, the mass of merit arising from it cannot be fully told, even if one looks with the divine eye or even with the mind of a tathāgata. So vast and so profound are its special qualities, supreme, excellent and so on, that ever higher and higher it excels in great miraculous display.

"This king of detailed rites of the dhāraṇī of the Secret of the Well-Established Palace of the Vast Great Jewel will not come into the hands of beings of little fortune. They will not hear it, will not honor it, and will not hold it."

How then could this supreme secret ever come into the hands of one who has not seen tathāgatas as many as the sands of the Ganges River, or be heard, honored, or held by such a one?

Vajrapāṇi, this secret of the Tathāgata is exceedingly hard to fathom. Vajrapāṇi, suppose someone, for a hundred thousand eons, were to offer each day to the buddhas, the blessed ones, as many as the sands of a hundred thousand koṭis of niyutas of Ganges Rivers, the requisites of comfort: robes, alms food, bedding, medicines as remedies for sickness, foods, snacks, meals, drinks, mounts, and palaces. Suppose he likewise worshipped all the tathāgatas with flowers, incense, perfumes, garlands, ointments, parasols, victory banners, and pennants. And suppose someone filled a great trichiliocosm world system to the brim with the seven precious substances and offered it each day to all the buddhas. That would not produce a great heap of merit. But, Vajrapāṇi, if someone recites this secret of the Tathāgata even once, he will come to possess a far greater heap of merit, which even all the tathāgatas could not finish describing.

If someone recalls this king of dhāraṇīs, the secret of the tathāgatas, even once, then, through the proclamation of the distinctive qualities explained above, he will have worshipped all the tathāgatas. With every most excellent requisite there is, he will thus be worshipping the secrets of the tathāgatas every day.

Then that whole gathered assembly, gods, nāgas, yakṣas, rākṣasas, asuras, garuḍas, kinnaras, mahoragas, and all humans and nonhumans, raised a great clamor of "Alala!" and sounds of laughter, touched the feet of the Blessed One, and said to the Blessed One, "How wonderful! The heart of the supreme secret, as supremely hard to find as the appearance of a buddha, has been placed in Jambudvīpa!"
