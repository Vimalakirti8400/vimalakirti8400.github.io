About this text

Toh 363 in the Kangyur is a tantric text known in Sanskrit as the Śrī Kālacakra Tantrōttara Tantra Hṛdaya, and in Tibetan as the Dpal Dus Kyi 'Khor Lo'i Rgyud Phyi Ma Rgyud Kyi Snying Po, rendered in English as "The Heart of the Subsequent Tantra of the Glorious Kālacakra." It belongs to the Kālacakra cycle, one of the most elaborate and celebrated systems within the Vajrayāna tradition, and serves as a condensed or essential extraction drawn from the Subsequent Tantra of the Kālacakra. The text is set within the legendary kingdom of Śambhala, where, in the palace of Kalāpa, the teachings on the king of tantras are proclaimed to assembled sages, weaving together cosmological, astrological, and soteriological themes that are characteristic of the Kālacakra system. As part of the Kangyur, it is classified among the tantric scriptures and opens with a homage to Mañjuśrī, situating it within a tradition that regards this wisdom deity as a presiding presence over the revelation of these profound teachings.

Homage to Mañjuśrī, the Gentle Melodious One.

Now, to the stainless body of the maṇḍala of the dharmadhātu, Mañjuśrī, through virtue and non-virtue — what one hears said: that the buddhas, the non-Buddhists (tīrthikas), the gods, and the hells themselves arise from these — the supreme king, in the renowned city of Kalāpa in the palace of Śambhala, the Lord of Speech, the Lion, seated upon his throne, proclaimed again to the sages the king of tantras, clearly revealing it.

That this indeed came to be is true: the supreme god, the guru, Viṣṇu, the lord of the gods, the moon, the sun, Rāhu, the fire god — on earth, in the celestial realms, in space — from merit and from sinful deeds arise. Human beings, in the next existence, as hungry ghosts and in the hells — what is prohibited: the bond of the oath of flesh. The sinful deeds of the tantra of the Vedists and the skull-bearers — those are false.

Wherever, through the power of virtue and non-virtue over the seven great planets and so forth, even brahmins take birth there — having abandoned their former body, they will certainly take hold of another body in a different place. From the words of the sage's progeny, killing living beings, eating — the culminating eight come to pass.

From eating flesh, even brahmins fall from the celestial realm down to this earth — and that is true. The excellent Brahmā, Īśvara, Viṣṇu — their Veda, the peaceful dharma, supreme Viṣṇu — have prohibited flesh as food. For those bound by the oath of flesh, the tantra of the yoginī and the yogi, by the fully awakened buddha, with fifteen and so forth — for those bound by the Sugata's vow, buddha-awakening: the buddhas entirely do not eat flesh, and should not be protected by one's own ego.

By approaching living beings with the mind intent on killing, perceiving them as sentient beings to be slaughtered — one falls here. By five limbs one will commit the killing of life, but space alone is not the agent of killing for living beings. The killing of life is accomplished by three: the causes of body, speech, and mind — and one who enjoys it and produces it, the very sovereign of the mind, will destroy sentient beings through the karma of enjoyment.

Lions, serpents, cats, tigers, and crows — by five limbs of birth, the killing of life comes to pass. Kings and common folk bring it about by doing, having others do it, and rejoicing — these three. A monk without compassion who tastes the flavour of flesh on his tongue and seeks out taste after taste — by such faults as killing oneself and so forth, monks and novices alike go to the hells.

When a physician prescribes flesh for a sick monk, even that which is killed by hawks and falcons, or killed for one's father's sake, or dead from a sacrificial fire, or from fire, water, unsolicited causes — if these conditions are absent, what was said to be permissible to eat at that time: the flesh that was taught to monks by the one of ten powers was for the sake of overcoming illness, not as a deception about death.

If one eats flesh and so forth, one will certainly suffer in the great bodies beneath the earth. Sentient beings, hungry ghosts, and so forth — the births difficult of access; ascetics, householders, kings — certainly in their own homes, the homes of others, the enjoyment of wives, daughters, companions and retinues, and their own homes. The bodies of the dead, whatever they are — wives, sons, daughters, companions and retinues, whoever — here, upon the stone one should see clearly form, sound, smell, taste, and tactile sensation themselves.

A hammer has awareness; from the state of being free of the aspects of self and other, a stone hammer becomes an aggregate. In the body of living beings on the earth whose awareness has declined, it is also present. The vital breath moving through living beings — through awakening well from the state of being free of aspects of self and other, one knows it. The chariot of the sun of Śambhala and so forth — the aggregates (skandhas), the elements (dhātus), the sense faculties (āyatanas), the destinies...

All the aggregates of the form element and so forth belonging to wandering beings are transformations of subtle particles — mere awareness that experiences, free of self and other, without conceptual thought. By that which awakens living beings from sleep, causing the light that dispels darkness to arise in those who do not experience through such means, the moon, the sun, Rāhu, the fire of time, and darkness all appear as one. Earth and the rest, fragrance and the rest, the eye and the rest, the rainbow, rain, lightning, and wind, and a sixth pair — these are the six aggregates in the body, and the object, the subject, and their sixfold activity, a sixth pair. These are what experiencing living beings, whose aggregates have fallen into sleep, are roused by means of the illuminator. From the outer self, the inner self, the third which is supreme self, and these, the fourth arises, transcendent. The nirmāṇakāya, the saṃbhogakāya, and likewise the dharmakāya — these four arise, transcendently issued forth. Not being empty within, supremely arisen from the elements, merely transformed, the three and so forth dissolve. These four, and the three and so forth, dissolve definitively, as the supreme one, into the dharmadhātu, the basic space of phenomena. The aggregates, elements, and the limbs of the sense faculties, the sense bases, the dense darkness, the appearance of a luminous hue — the suchness of consciousness and of the vital breath, the boundless Dharma of awakening, understood as wandering through the three realms of existence. Fragrances, like flowers in the sky, do not exist; residing in the dharmadhātu, the space of mere particles, these phenomena have no more existence than space itself. Because these wandering beings, along with their self, their third and so on, and the three bodies of a buddha and so forth — from within their limbs, through affliction and ignorance, again come grasping and becoming — karma and formations become existence, and here these three together with their pair constitute causative phenomena, suffering. Consciousness and the rest become the fivefold mode; likewise come the eleventh and the twelfth. From the three comes the pair, and from the pair come the seven resultant Dharma-limbs of immeasurable existences. As from a tree come flowers, and from flowers come fruits in turn — so from the afflictions of wandering beings come karmic actions, and from these come suffering, and from suffering come afflictions: the entire wheel, without remainder, arises from causes. Phenomena have no cessation; arising from what is no more than space, they have no arising either, and for that reason: living beings, particles of life, endowed with aggregates, passing into other destinies, are neither permanent nor impermanent. Generosity and so forth, whatever is endowed with the ten virtues — the emptiness of that, and the emptiness of the side of awakening. Whatever becomes the agent of those actions — the supreme Victor together with his children, and the classes of māras among sentient beings — from the aggregates, and from those back again, there is no transfer to another existence: the joining of the continuum of the aggregates is like a lamp, recitation of a text, a mirror, undefiled water, sound, the crystal of the sun, a seed, and sour-tasting things. Wherever it is true to say that the dharmakāya of the Victors arises from the white collection of Dharmas — from Dharma the saṃbhogakāya, complete with major marks and minor features, the entire assembly of sounds — the aggregates become supreme in the nirmāṇakāya. The unchanging, supreme bliss is not the dharmakāya, and therefore — whatever the defining characteristic of the body, the teacher among those who have become buddhas, the nāga, declared here: "That which is neither one nor many, the great and excellent support of benefit to self and other, neither existent nor non-existent, like space, with equal taste, difficult to analyze, its very nature — uncolored, utterly unchanging, peaceful, incomparable, equal, all-pervading, free of conceptual elaboration, peerless, to be known each by themselves" — to the dharmakāya of the Victors, I bow down. That which, having transcended the world, inconceivable, is itself the fruit of wholesome conduct, supremely excellent in itself, becomes the cause of delight to those of great intelligence, and in the midst of the retinue speaks variously —

the vast and holy Dharma that pervades all worlds without ceasing, proclaimed by the buddhas —
to that which abides as the great Dharma-King, the perfect enjoyment body (saṃbhogakāya), here I bow down.

That which becomes the cause of ripening for sentient beings, appearing brightly to some like fire, and again to others — fully awakened, turning the wheel of Dharma in utter peace — is perceived. Entering through the skillful means of form in its many aspects, it vanquishes the terrors of the three existences. Following in the ten directions, accomplishing great purpose — to the emanation bodies (nirmāṇakāyas) of the mighty Victors, I bow down.

Liberated from conduct within the three realms, become equal to space, the very intrinsic nature of all things,
pure and peaceful, supremely solitary and tranquil, its nature to be realized by yoga itself —
difficult to comprehend, difficult to analyze, peaceful, incomparably equal yet all-pervading, without marks,
unequalled, blissful, non-conceptual — the single body: to the body of the Victors, I bow down.

In buddha fields as countless as atoms, without exception, throughout all world-systems, from the birth-places of moon and sun and so forth, the divine realms, the surface of the earth, the sky, to the limit of the vajra queen — in female birth-places, in the dharmadhātu, in the realms of good rebirth and ill, at all times it fully abides. A perfect buddha, by the power of the aggregate of living beings endowed with the qualities of the sense-objects, through the force of merit and sin,

seminal fluid and blood, entering the lotus of the womb of a vidyā-consort, together with the vital wind and the striving wind — that then becomes possessed of the intrinsic nature of the five wisdoms (jñāna), with the vidyā fully present. Born from the qualities of the sense-object, that form of one who enjoys the saṃbhogakāya — not that, but supremely relying on the elements — man, woman, neither-sex, god, nāga, Viṣṇu, Victor, Sage — arising from its own power.

The very suchness of that, the fivefold suchness, and the three families' rank, united in one secret: a hundred faces, the lord of awareness-holders, the moon, the earth and so forth — object and subject — the master, the dharmadhātu. That hundred-faced one: from its cause, born of two — in the limbs of arms, legs, fingers and their joints — from that, the three feelings and so forth come to cessation, moving swiftly like sound.

That which is fivefold and threefold — from which object and subject, scent, body and so forth arise — the five elements and more: moon, sun, darkness, whereby the elements are sealed. From the one into the many elements, born of two — within whatever constitutes the assembly of fingers and toes of arms and legs — all from the moon's cause, the supreme family of a hundred fully unfolded — males and females.

The aggregate of the support's elements, intrinsic nature and qualities, is an indestructible mass of particles.
The cause of life — vowels and syllables and so forth by nature — is likewise the indestructible life-element.
The support of semen is the moon and sun, and those particles are the aggregate of living beings.
Awakened by that method, through the force of merit and sin, the fruit in another ground —

one's own five aggregates of karma are enjoyed, and again in another and yet again resumed.
The white meditative concentration in praise of that, the definite hundredfold — the karma-aggregate is not exhausted.
The naked ones: the karma-aggregate is utterly destroyed by fire alone that burns.
The white meditative concentration, the arhat's mantra, the definite hundredfold praise — from mind, body and speech,

the lotus-pervading quintessence, lord of the seed-syllable, the arhat's pure drop (bindu) is meditated upon. In the center, the body, hidden, blazing with a hundred peaks, causing clarity on all sides — the secret treasury of the gods, this supreme rank — worthy of offering by Śakra (Indra), the king of a hundred sacrifices. These qualities — by burning, offering, and consuming — are seen to burn without remainder the entire karma-aggregate.

Whoever enters into harmful meditative concentration — for that one, day and night, demerit increases. Whoever turns back from harmful meditative concentration — for that one, day and night, demerit diminishes. Whoever enters into fierce meditative concentration — for that one, day and night, demerit increases. Whoever turns back from fierce meditative concentration — for that one, day and night, demerit diminishes. Whoever enters into Dharmic meditative concentration — for that one, day and night, demerit diminishes. Whoever turns back from Dharmic meditative concentration — for that one, day and night, demerit increases. Whoever enters into white meditative concentration — for that one, day and night, demerit diminishes. Whoever turns back from white meditative concentration — for that one, day and night, demerit increases. Whoever, day and night, enters into harming — for those, demerit increases. Whoever, day and night, turns back from harming — for those, merit increases. Likewise by flesh, liquor, and seizing another's wealth, and by acting wrongly through desire, through attachment, aversion, and delusion, and through definite anger, pride, and craving — so it comes to be. Whoever enters into generosity — for that one, day and night, merit increases. Whoever turns back from generosity — for that one, day and night, demerit increases. Likewise moral discipline and patience, day and night, and vajra-like diligence in the same way, and wisdom, skillful means, aspirational prayer, the application of power, and the vajras of emptiness and gnosis — doer obscured by the existent and non-existent, and one who seizes space, and the supreme self — when space is absent the doer has no abode: this is the word of those who speak of emptiness. Without making oneself the agent of sentient beings, merit and demerit increase fully at all times in every way. The Victors endowed with the ten powers, those possessed of gnosis, and the gods — without having enjoyed them, these do not perish. The six, beginning with individual withdrawal, endowed with the power of the ten powers: thus it is taught to meditate on emptiness. By the application of smoke and the like at night, and again by day by the sky, fire, and the like, through individual withdrawal the yogin sees the things of the three times arrived in the center of the sky. By meditative concentration, having made the mind very stable in that, one abandons outer phenomena entirely. By breath-control, the vital wind courses free from the paths of sun and moon, moving in darkness. Free from the going out and coming in of breath, and again by the limb of retention, one holds the drop. By recollection, from one's own body, as from the moon, light radiates into the sky in abundance. By meditative absorption, the semen, beginning from the vajra, ascending to the crown, becomes utterly filled with stainless bliss. Gazing into the sky, delusion is utterly destroyed, for one sees the emptiness of the manifold. Gazing inward at one's own nature, attachment is destroyed, for the vital winds are restrained. Gazing without fixation on limbs, aversion is destroyed — by the stainless bliss of the semen. Gazing at the tip of the nose, pride is destroyed — this is from the inviolable speech of Brahmā. By restraining the vital force and exertion, the maṇḍala of a buddha's reflection, perfectly pure and stainless: at the secret center, the navel, the heart, the throat, and the crown — the vajra places — each pair of stages in sequence, the semen ascending upward and the blood descending, from the inviolable melody of Brahmā — the vajras of body, speech, and mind, free from the three stains: the one who reveals this is a buddha. The eye of the flesh of dogs and so on, and the eye of the gods and the like, through the power of intrinsic qualities — the eye that knows the minds of others as understood by pratyekabuddhas and śrāvakas — that is the buddha's eye: these are the three. For the bodhisattvas, the fourth, pure and stainless, becomes the eye of wisdom.

The fifth, free from the three stains, is the wisdom eye — the eye that sees all things, belonging to the buddhas.

Concordant in cause with bliss, semen is itself the vajra; because one holds it, one is assuredly Vajradhara. The semen is the seed of the body; when it moves and one holds the wheel, what is the bliss of the navel wheel — the signified — while one is in the act of signifying it, with sound at the heart: the Protector destroys saṃsāra. Whoever at the throat knows that, holding the motionless bliss, is the one who perceives and holds the jewel. Whoever holds the moon at the crown knows the bliss of semen, stainless — the holder of wisdom. At the uṣṇīṣa, in the Brahmā aperture, unchanging, supreme bliss fills the sixteen joys completely. Whoever knows wisdom to be without intrinsic nature — the supreme moon, the extent of the sixteen phases fulfilled — that one is immeasurable; whoever knows this, at the crown, with the vajra, is the sixth: Vajrasattva. Illumination becomes the element of wisdom; whoever at the crown is the unchanging vajra-being, the stainless earthly drop at the crown — that very person, together with water and the dimming of the sun, Rāhu, fire, karmic maturation, the heart together with sound, bliss, wind, and the navel — is far-reaching. At the secret wheel, the bliss endowed with darkness is overcome — the void is the element of void darkness. Whoever has the six objects beginning with the eye: whatever comes from the goddesses — earth and heart and the rest — and whatever is the aggregate of form and the rest, and the six objects — those are conceived as deities. Among those with pure faculties and the rest, the six who possess objects — Krodharāja and the like — those in whom semen does not move: the six objects are the blue and so on. Whoever is the wrathful Yamāntaka together with the moon, conquering the vital wind completely; together with the sun, conquering the vital wind completely — whoever is the wrathful Padmāntaka; together with Rāhu, conquering the vital wind completely — whoever is the wrathful Vighnāntaka; together with the far-reaching, definitively conquering the vital wind — whoever is the wrathful Prajñāntaka. When the vital wind is stopped, the two-armed Hevajra in its peaceful aspect — here Vajrasattva conquers pride. When the vital wind is arrested, the four-armed Hevajra — Vajrabhairava who destroys aversion — becomes bodhicitta. Hevajra who destroys attachment, three-faced and six-armed, becomes the great bodhisattva Maheśvara. Eight-faced and four-footed, the one who destroys delusion — Brahmā together with bliss — becomes Samayasattva. When the vital wind is arrested at the navel, free from the three stains, Vajrasattva himself is a buddha. When it is arrested at the heart, free from the three stains, the bodhisattva pratyekabuddha is himself a buddha. At the throat, just as the sun — free from the three stains — the great bodhisattva becomes a śrāvaka. When the wind faculty is arrested at the forehead, the samaya being together with bliss is the Teacher's son. At the navel wheel, the supreme unchanging bliss — together with the four root channels of the faculty of the wrathful one and the thirty-two — is transformed taste by taste: the three wheels of Śrī Cakrasaṃvara. The eight segments of the bones of arms and legs, the palm, the finger joints, and their segments — the channels of the six wheels here, doubled: these are the yoginīs of great Śrī Cakrasaṃvara. At the four channels of the uṣṇīṣa, the transmigration is liberation; likewise the eight wheels of Dharma. At the throat, the thirty-two; at the navel wheel, sixty-four. Whoever is filled with lunar parts — the white crown — here the white part is the secret. The channels of the wheel, moreover, become the supreme unchanging bliss: the Kālacakra, the deity of the higher [realms].

The time is three hundred and sixty: the junctures too are the vajra body. The shaft of the lunar mansions, the sky, the hand, the king — that too is declared by the primordial buddha as space. The thirty portions of the moon, the sky, the sky-suspended, the lunar channel — this is the auspicious moon. Whatever channels and body-hairs, without remainder, abide in the body, are named the vajra sprout. The vajra water, urine, blood — these, through the emptiness of the body, shall be lapped up by the tongue. By the teeth of Maitreya, the flesh of face, hands, and feet is eaten; therefore one is fierce. By the great mouth of the five superknowledges, the herds of the land's livestock are devoured; therefore one is a lion. Merit, wisdom, and perfect moral discipline — free of the three defilements — Kālacakra, the three-eyed one. In Glorious Guhyasamāja, whatever bodies arise from object, subject, and the power of the karma of living beings — there, some of those elements are eternally made manifest among the maras as gods. Channels, flesh, bone, semen, and blood, assembled from atoms, are the abode of the gods. Each sentient being — buddha, god-realm, sky, earth's surface — abides as the principal in its own respective abode. In brief, through a succession of illusions, there is transformation; the vajra recitation accomplishes object and subject. By self-consecration, the vital wind becomes the hare-bearing one — the moon — and here, in this fourth, one desires manifest awakening. Here the fifth, the supreme union, the perfected sequence — even in Glorious Guhyasamāja — the guru of the three existences has taught the generation stage of the gods in order to truly bind the maras. Whatever bodhisattva is born from the path of the ten virtues, from generosity, moral discipline, and so forth — such a one will desire to be reborn, and it is not mere thought alone; I too am the examined and the self. These words of the maras one takes to one's own mind, and by such a pervasive nature, there is no master whatsoever of meditation; in the god-realm and on earth, merit and moral discipline are utterly inferior. Endowed with the supreme ten powers, the perfect gods dwell rightly in the dharmadhātu. One should employ the hook, the vajra, the arrow, the bow, the bell, the sword, and the lasso to accomplish. Having been summoned and seated here, and having been struck with supreme terror, with hands bound — through this meditation, virtue is fully abandoned, and the maras, upon dying, proceed to the lower realms. The divine ones who have become buddhas, free of the three defilements, who accomplish the welfare of all sentient beings — these should be served with intensely sweet-smelling flowers, incense, lamps, divine food-offerings, mantra recitation, and definite praise a hundredfold; and the accomplished siddha, greatly gladdened, bestows the vidyādhara attainment in the subterranean world, the world of the gods, on earth's surface, and in the sky. The vital mantra that, blocking the channels of moon and sun at the time of day and night — reciting for one month undivided: space, sky, the suspended, the course through the chest, the time-flavor, darkness — by grasping upper and lower, the vital wind freed from the three paths, carried along by the winds, is filled. One achieves the attainment of grasping the hare — the moon — and the sky-farer holds the vidyādhara. For the sake of merit, the bodhisattva, through compassion, gives even the kingdom's queen — even one's own flesh. For the sake of wisdom, one meditates with an unmoved, stainless awareness on the reflection of emptiness — the entities of the three times. For the sake of moral discipline, one carries the vajra drop — the semen — to the crown of the head and from the head to the brahma aperture. Merit, wisdom, and moral discipline — purely free of defilement — transform one into Vajrasattva. A person of great fault who gives a kingdom in mind — who gives it — by receiving it one does not enjoy it.

A wife endowed with every quality — given in mind — is not enjoyed by whoever receives her. Pure flesh given in mind — it is not enjoyed by whoever receives it, bound as they are by the samaya of flesh. By this reasoning, if a bodhisattva were then to possess the ten powers, how could one of low birth not become so? Maitreya's supreme austerity is this: at dawn, for the sake of beer, he goes to the beer-seller's house. At midday, through supreme compassion, for the sake of fish, he goes to the fishermen's market. At dusk, for the sake of engaging with women, he goes with joy to the houses of servant-girls. Through beer, fish, and women — the three defilements — how is the avadhūtī not of common birth? Those who claim that the many things have become one are making a commitment to oneness. Those who claim that what has no object to be proved is established as something having both stable and moving beings — they too make a commitment. When the logical reason becomes equivalent to the object to be proved, the prover does not establish the object. If the object to be proved is one thing and the logical reason is another, then their commitment is undermined. If sounds are committed to as permanent — established as like space, bodiless — then likewise, if sounds are committed to as impermanent — treated as like a pot — because they arise through great effort, then even though permanence is established from space, from your own mouth it is definitively established that sounds are impermanent. Like the domains of form, touch, smell, and taste, why is sound, the fifth, not the same? That by which one first perceives — from that, on the earth and in the intermediate space of the sky, that sound again by the ear comes to be fully apprehended: that is what makes sounds permanent. That by which one first apprehends the meaning — from that, on the earth and in the intermediate space, again in that manner by the ear the meanings come to be definitively apprehended — does this then make forms permanent? A single permanent thing to be illuminated — a sound possessing every quality by its own nature — dwells in the cavity of the ear. The collection of sounds that illuminates, spoken from the throat, has not in turn become permanent. Does not the sound-tumult that one speaker utters come to fill the ear-cavity that listens? That which is to be permanently and clearly illuminated can indeed become so — but it cannot do so by depending on something else. The cause that acts as the agent of an object, being elsewhere, illuminates the darkness. Whatever is permanently and clearly illuminated — having been produced here by a cause — is something made. What is made and whatever else is established through incompatible properties does not become established. Because of that incompatibility, made things exist, while the other is absent. That which has become the cause of what is to be illuminated — that which illuminates it — why is it not a cause? Those things that have dissolved into darkness and are illuminated by it — why are they not its fruit? If sound and meaning were to become one, then the deterioration of the meaning would become the deterioration of the sound. The universal and the particular would likewise become the same — being mere space, and being otherwise, and being one. If emptiness were to possess sound, then in all three worlds there would always be no sound. That by which space is established as existent — by that, mobile and immobile beings are established as nonexistent. If space and the causes of the collection of elements were established from incompatible properties, then from that cause, for the prover, it would definitively be established that sound is inferior to non-sound. In space and on the earth: planets and stars, animals and humans, gods and rākṣasas, hungry ghosts and asuras and nāgas bound by the samaya of their own kind — through the power of the qualities intrinsic to the syllables of mantra, having the nature of shape and color — through the sounds of one or many meanings, and also in some cases through a single sound, many meanings are expressed.

From the cause of non-existence and emptiness, stable and moving things are produced from their causes. Living creatures, having abandoned their former body, will again take up another body through the power of intention. The other form of living creatures, like a vast garland of lightning, vast in form and having a body — having passed through the clouds, they travel through the sky in the directions and the intermediate directions with swift force. Living creatures, together with the life-force, separate from the inert body, fully possessing sensation and the rest — through the connection of karma they proceed alone into the thirty-one realms of existence according to virtue and non-virtue. Not singular, the five aspects, separated from objects, the inner self and the supreme self — they do not transcend space, are not obscure, are not without result, and are not of diminished capacity. From the mind, through desire, a person does not arise by intrinsic nature; and without the life-wind there is no mind. Living creatures, together with mind, through the connection of karma, proceed into higher and lower destinies. Because of mutual dependence, the conscious and the inert, cause and effect as such; and because of mutual dependence, when the assembled collection of elements has fully become what it is, arising from that — even what is permanent is itself subject to change, and therefore the collection of elements is definitively subject to change. Whatever is permanent and unchanging in intrinsic nature — though it may change, what is truly permanent is not subject to change. The supreme lord has spoken of the beings of superior permanence and the inferior of permanence as establishing existence. Because of mutual dependence, reasoning and species too become cause and effect. Because of mutual dependence, the uncreated and the created become cause and effect. Because of mutual dependence, the created and the created become cause and effect. All is mere consciousness — the elements of earth, water, fire, wind, and space; form, tangibles, smell, and sound together with their pleasures — these do not become a collection of atoms. From consciousness, resultant things do not arise by themselves, nor are they led by non-existence, nor by a leader. Like a sprout and so on arising from a seed — it proceeds thus, but not from itself alone, and not without a cause. Legs and bones, rivers, flesh and sap, blood, fat, skin — these are not a collection of atoms. Having abandoned mere consciousness, one goes again to another existence as mere consciousness. There, taking up a body, going again to another existence — that too is that very mere consciousness. If migrating beings were mere consciousness, then here another object would be mere consciousness. If that were to occur — if migrating beings were mere consciousness — they would not be non-dual, because of those two. At the outset, an aggregate of subtle atoms has no part belonging to a migrating being — in what space do those particles exist? From the migration that is the gathering of subtle atoms, a migration may occur, yet such a migration is not itself an aggregate of particles. Separated from particles and abiding in space, the dissolution of other migrating beings is not different from that. If a body were to become an aggregate of particles, then at that time whose body would be dissolved? An examined element being non-existent, apart from that there is no separation of particles; whatever particle exists is also a particle — and through being free of contraction and production, it abides in the midst of space, whether accompanied by wind or not. Subtle atoms have six parts, and those six parts in turn also have six parts — and again, the six parts of those: why is it not established that these very atoms, through subtler parts, fill the expanse of space? That particles of mercury and gems have or do not have parts is not to be established by valid cognitions. If they have parts, mercury cannot penetrate copper, nor does it penetrate stone; yet an aggregate of particles without parts is likewise impossible. Mercury does not touch copper; born from two, a gem turns stone into a divine precious jewel. But consciousness that is impaired by the six conceptual discriminations is not a modification of an aggregate of particles. An external blue form that is an aggregate of particles is not established by the valid cognition of that aggregate. From the habitual tendencies of consciousness, what appears in the expanse of space similar to a dream is renowned as the seen.
