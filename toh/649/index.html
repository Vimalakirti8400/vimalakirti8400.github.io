About this text

Toh 649 in the Kangyur is a dhāraṇī text known in Tibetan as "Jur-geg" (འཇུར་འགེགས་), a short ritual scripture belonging to the dhāraṇī section of the canon. The text presents a consecrated mantra — the Heart of the Wisdom Star — together with precise instructions for its performance: the practitioner is directed to sit beside flowing water, recite the essence-mantra seven times over water held in the palm, and cast it into the stream, thereby liberating hungry ghosts from their suffering and granting them divine nourishment. Rooted in the compassionate concern for beings in the lower realms that characterizes much of the dhāraṇī literature, the text reflects a broader tradition of ritual water-offering practices aimed at relieving the torments of the deceased. It was translated into Tibetan and established in its authoritative form by the great translator Yeshe Dé, one of the most prolific and eminent translators of the early Tibetan canonical period.

Translation

They offered praise to what the Blessed One had declared. The dhāraṇī called "Jur-geg" is complete.

Translated and established by the great translator, the venerable Yeshe Dé.

Homage to the Three Jewels.

Oṃ jñānāvalokite samantaspharaṇa raśmi bhava samaya mahāmaṇi duru duru hṛdaya jalani hūṃ.

Sit beside a flowing stream or the shore of a lake. Pour water into the cupped palm of your right hand. Recite this essence-mantra, the Heart of the Wisdom Star, seven times. Then cast those seven palmfuls of water into the flowing stream or into the water. All the hungry ghosts who are seen by a buddha's eyes, however many there may be, will come to possess the flavor of divine food and the finest divine drink. Each and every one of those hungry ghosts will be fully satisfied by seven measures of the measure of Magadha, in both food and drink. And merely by drinking that water, every one of them will be freed from the realm of rebirth as hungry ghosts and will be reborn in a happy destiny, in a heavenly realm of higher existence.

Whether they are men or women, boys or girls, or likewise those belonging to the realm of animal rebirth — whether deer or birds,

Translator's notes

The title "Jur-geg" is a transliteration of a Tibetan name whose Sanskrit original is not recoverable from this passage alone; it likely renders something like "Jūrgega" or is itself a Tibetan adaptation. The term is left as found.

The mantra line is rendered in its received Sanskrit phonetic form as it appears in the Tibetan transliteration; no normalization has been imposed.

The "measure of Magadha" refers to the drona, a unit of dry and liquid measure associated with the Magadha region, used here to convey an enormously generous and satisfying quantity of food and drink.
