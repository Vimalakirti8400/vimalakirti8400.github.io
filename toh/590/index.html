About this text

Toh 590 in the Kangyur is the Noble Great Queen of Mantra-Vidyā Who Emerged from the Uṣṇīṣa of All the Tathāgatas, Bearing the White Parasol, Unconquerable, the Averter of All, known in Sanskrit as the Ārya-sarva-tathāgatoṣṇīṣa-sitātapatrā-aparājitā-pratyaṅgirā-mahāvidyārājñī, and in Tibetan as the Phakpa Dezhin Shekpa Thamché kyi Tsuktor né jungwa Dukkar Pochen zhéjawa Zhengyi mi tubma Chirzokpé Riksngakma Chenmo. It is a dhāraṇī sūtra belonging to the Tantra section of the Kangyur, centering on the powerful and protective spell that radiates from the uṣṇīṣa — the crown protrusion — of all the buddhas, personified as a goddess bearing a white parasol and renowned for her capacity to avert all harm and liberate beings from lower rebirths. The text takes the form of a canonical discourse delivered by the Buddha while dwelling among the Gods of the Thirty-Three, where he enters a special meditative absorption and from his uṣṇīṣa emanates the great mantra-vidyā at the heart of the teaching. The Sitātapatrā tradition became one of the most widely venerated dhāraṇī cycles across the Buddhist world, prized for its purificatory and protective power.

Homage to the Three Jewels. Homage to all buddhas and bodhisattvas.

Thus did I hear. At one time the Blessed One was dwelling in the divine realm of the Gods of the Thirty-Three (Skt. trāyastriṃśa), in the assembly hall of the god Sudharmā, together with a great Saṅgha of monks, a great Saṅgha of bodhisattvas, and with Śakra (Indra), lord of the gods. There the Blessed One, seated upon a prepared throne, entered into the meditative absorption (samādhi) called Viewing the Uṣṇīṣa. The very moment he entered that absorption, from the midst of the Blessed One's uṣṇīṣa there arose these words of secret mantra:

Namo bhagavate uṣṇīṣāya śuddhe viraje vimale svāhā.
