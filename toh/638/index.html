About this text

The Noble One Hundred and Eight Names of Vajrapāṇi, Together with Dhāraṇī and Mantra, known in Sanskrit as the Ārya-Vajrapāṇi-aṣṭottaraśataka-nāma-dhāraṇī-mantra-sahita and in Tibetan as Pakpa Lakna Dorjé Tsen Gyatsa Gyepa, is a dhāraṇī text devoted to the bodhisattva Vajrapāṇi, the powerful wielder of the thunderbolt who serves as a protector of the Buddhist teaching. The text presents one hundred and eight names of Vajrapāṇi together with his heart-essence mantra and dhāraṇī, the recitation of which is described as causing the earth to tremble and striking fear into all obstructors and malevolent forces. It belongs to a series of such name-collections and is identified as the eighth in that root series. This work is Toh 638 in the Kangyur, where it falls within the category of dhāraṇī literature.

In the language of India: Ārya-Vajrapāṇi-aṣṭottaraśataka-nāma-dhāraṇī-mantra-sahita. In the language of Tibet: The Noble One Hundred and Eight Names of Vajrapāṇi, Together with dhāraṇī and mantra.

Homage to the bodhisattva, the great being Vajrapāṇi.

Then the bodhisattva Vajrapāṇi uttered his own heart essence: Om vajra caṇḍa sarva duṣṭān taka, hana daha, paca hūṃ phaṭ.

No sooner had this dhāraṇī and mantra been proclaimed than the great earth trembled. All obstructors and misleaders cried out in fear, uttering sounds of distress and anguish. All those assembled in the great maṇḍala raised their voices in exclamations of wonder. The great gods and the great lords of vidyā-mantras caused a great rain of ornaments to fall. The Blessed One, his face wreathed in smiles, gazed upon the Vajra-Holder and declared: "Dispeller of hostility, do not make this assembly overly frightened."

The Vajra-Holder then addressed the Blessed One: "Blessed One, this dhāraṇī of mine is supremely secret. It is the highest of all vidyā-mantras. It turns back all obstructors. It dispels all hostility. It bestows the excellent. It bestows prosperity. It causes enemies to become pleased. Hearing it is meaningful. Reciting it is meaningful. Venerating it is meaningful. It generates power. It makes the form beautiful. It generates confidence. It causes lordship to be attained. It makes one endowed with wealth. It confers mastery. It destroys hostility. It makes hostility collapse. It removes poison. It causes victory. It causes joy. It causes supreme joy. It causes clarity of mind. It destroys pride. It defeats adversaries. It pacifies evil. It purifies transgressions that have been committed. It pacifies the afflictions of attachment and the rest. It turns back the boundless hells. It pacifies evil dreams. It pacifies inauspiciousness. It generates splendor. It makes good fortune arise. It causes all sentient beings to rejoice. It confers mastery over others. It bestows whatever is desired. It makes all qualities flourish abundantly. When there is too much rain and when rain does not fall, it thoroughly pacifies harm caused by the seasons. In times of great war, great plague, great conflict, fears of the non-human and the like, hostility, sorcery and harmful spells, great zombie conjurations, banishment, and times when life is threatened, when great terrors arise — rising at dawn, bathing the body, putting on white clothing, facing east and gazing upon the face of the Blessed One, the Vajra-Holder, and reciting this seven times — by that mere recitation one will be freed from all fears, will also attain great accomplishment, and will be victorious in all directions. There is nothing higher than this great dhāraṇī for the protection, refuge, and shelter of sentient beings."

Then Vajrāmogha spoke: This, the root text of the vajra names, adorned with vajra secrets, with its one hundred and eight vajra names, bears the title Vajrāmogha. It averts all hatred. Whoever holds these one hundred and eight names with faith will obtain all attainments and will be completely freed from all faults. The mighty Vajrapāṇi will always follow close behind as a constant attendant. The great qualities of Vajrapāṇi are such that whoever rises early in the morning and recites them will be freed from all wrongdoing and will be liberated from all lower realms. Whoever rises early and recites this supreme secret mantra, spoken at the outset, will cause kings, ministers, townspeople, and relatives alike to rejoice. Whoever recites this supreme praise will gain sovereignty over the entire world, all wrongdoing will be exhausted, and life and merit will increase. One will thereby obtain whatever excellent happiness one desires. Whoever rises early and recites these names in the presence of Vajrapāṇi will without any doubt come to have whatever one wishes. When one recites the one hundred and eight names, even obstructing spirits, misleading demons, and all terrifying kings will be unable to overpower one. Whatever wise person recites the supreme one hundred and eight names will obtain a beautiful form, the arising of strength, and the increase of happiness. Whoever steadfastly and purely recites these one hundred and eight names will, in brief, obtain the happiness of vast qualities.

Translator's notes

The phrase "མཚམས་མ་མཆིས་པ་ཟློག་པ" rendered as "turns back the boundless hells" could also mean "averts the actions of immediate retribution" (the five heinous acts, ānantarya). The context of purification strongly favors the ānantarya reading, but since either is possible and the doctrinal stakes differ, it is worth noting.

The epithets in the closing verse section present genuine challenges. Many are descriptive bynames of Vajrapāṇi in his wrathful form — "protuberance" renders "མཐོན་ཀ" which likely refers to a cranial protuberance or topknot-like crest (uṣṇīṣa-like feature). The term "ཆགས་པའི་རྡོ་རྗེ" is translated as "Vajra of attachment" in the sense of a grappling or clinging attribute rather than the affliction of desire; it refers to Vajrapāṇi's power to bind and seize. Ti-se is the Tibetan name for Mount Kailash, rendered here in its classical Tibetan form since it functions as a proper name in context. The phrase "great mountain" renders lhun po, which also carries the sense of Sumeru or Mount Meru; the passage appears to identify Ti-se with a sacred cosmic peak rather than making a Meru equation explicit.

The term rendered "protuberance" throughout this and the preceding passage represents a topknot or cranial protuberance (uṣṇīṣa, Skt. uṣṇīṣa), a major mark of a great being, here applied to a wrathful deity in a tantric context.

"Rākṣasa" translates srin po, a class of fierce supernatural beings; "guardian" translates srung po. Both appear as epithets of the deity praised.

The line rendered "of pure fare, son among sons" is somewhat compressed in the Tibetan; the rendering reflects the most natural doctrinal reading without over-interpretation. The text ends mid-phrase with "The noble Vajrapāṇi's" indicating the passage continues beyond what was provided. The epithet Vajrāmogha rendered as a name for the speaker corresponds to "Vajra of Amogha" or "Infallible Vajra," consistent with the text's title and the identification of the speaker as Vajrāmogha (the personification of the text's title). The phrase rendered "rises early in the morning" translates a formulaic expression meaning to recite at dawn, a standard prescriptive context in dhāraṇī literature.
