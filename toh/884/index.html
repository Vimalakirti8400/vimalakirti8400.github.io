About this text

The Dhāraṇī Called "The Stūpa Accomplished as Bringing Everything to Perfection through Knowledge," known in Tibetan as Shepé Tamché Tartar Chinpar Druppé Chörten Zhejawé Zung, is a short scriptural incantation whose opening gives only a Tibetan title, with no Sanskrit title preserved. It begins with homages to the Three Jewels, to the Blessed One Vajradhara, to the buddhas of the three times, to Akṣobhya, Amitābha, and Avalokiteśvara, and to the three buddha families. It then presents a teaching set on a great Mount Meru made of five substances, whose very base is described as having the nature of a stūpa and on which stands a jeweled mansion. The text centers on the stūpa as a sacred embodiment of awakened knowledge and on a dhāraṇī connected with it. It is Toh 884 in the Kangyur, where it belongs to the class of dhāraṇī texts, the collected spells and incantations of the canon.

Translation

In the Tibetan language: The Dhāraṇī Called "The Stūpa Accomplished as Bringing Everything to Perfection through Knowledge."

Homage to the Three Jewels. Homage to the Blessed One Vajradhara. Homage to the Blessed One, the Tathāgata, the arhat, the perfectly complete Buddha, the King of Splendor of the way of all dharmas, of discipline, and of miraculous display.

Homage to the buddhas of the three times;
Homage to the virtuous Three Jewels;
To Akṣobhya and Amitābha
And Avalokiteśvara I pay homage.
Homage also to the three families.

Thus did I hear at one time. The Blessed One, the great Vajradhara, was dwelling on the great Mount Meru, made of five kinds of substance, whose very base abides as the intrinsic nature of a stūpa. Upon it was a mansion of various jewels and four immeasurable stūpas, and because it is the abode of the great stūpa of intrinsic nature, even the shape of its summit had become a stūpa. There he dwelt together with the bodhisattvas of the ten grounds, such as Vajrapāṇi, Mañjuśrī, Avalokiteśvara, and Maitreya, and with the lords of the pure realms, such as Brahmā, Śakra (Indra), and the Four Great Kings.

Then the Blessed One sent forth from the uṣṇīṣa on the crown of his head, from the śrīvatsa at his heart, and from other places, some sixty hundred thousand koṭis of niyutas of light rays of many different colors. With the great illumination of these light rays he filled and pervaded all the countless, inconceivable, and inexpressible world systems of the great trichiliocosm, as a pod is filled with sesame seeds. Through this, among all the infinite sentient beings, the blind saw forms, the deaf heard sounds, the mute were able to speak, the lame and the crippled walked and their limbs were made whole, the sick were freed from illness, and the hell beings had their lives shortened and were gradually freed from suffering. Moreover, the beings of the destinies gradually regained their mindfulness. Having done this, he filled and expanded the whole element of space with the dharmakāya, the stūpa of the mind. Those light rays then gathered back into the crown of the Blessed One's head, whereupon they became an image of the dharmakāya.

Then the retinue, Vajrapāṇi and the others, were filled with wonder, and they made offerings to the Blessed One of canopies, victory banners, pennants, parasols, yak-tail whisks, bells, hanging silk streamers, incense, flowers, garlands, ointments, lamps, food offerings, music, and so on. Having made these offerings, they said this to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, protector of beings, endowed with great compassion, please explain the rite for consecrating a stūpa, the great vidyā, the Vimaloṣṇīṣa dhāraṇī."

The Blessed One declared: "If any son of good family or daughter of good family holds, keeps, writes, reads, or listens to the name of this rite for consecrating a stūpa, the great vidyā, the Vimaloṣṇīṣa dhāraṇī, then the five acts of immediate retribution, the ten nonvirtues, and whatever evil deeds that person has done will all be purified without remainder. This is the rite for consecrating a stūpa with the great vidyā of Vimaloṣṇīṣa:

oṃ traiyadhā sarva tathāgata hṛdaya garbhe jvala dharmadhātu garbhe saṃharaṇa āyuḥ saṃśodhaya pābaṃ sarva tathāgatasamanta uṣṇīṣa vimale viśuddhe svāhā"

As soon as this great vidyā was spoken, all the world systems became of the intrinsic nature of a stūpa.

If one writes this great vidyā on paper, cloth, or birch bark and fastens it to the top of a victory banner, it becomes worthy of the offerings, prostrations, and honor of all gods, humans, and others. If one wears this great vidyā on the body or at the neck, gods, humans, non-humans, and others will regard that person as the Buddha himself.

When one goes to a mountain crag, a grove, a hermitage, or any place at all, if one recites this great vidyā, all the mountains and places will become like stūpas. If one recites

oṃ traiyadhva sarva tathāgata hṛdya garbhe jvala dharmadhātu garbhe saṃharaṇa āyuḥ saṃśodhaya pāpaṃ sarva tathāgata samanta uṣṇīṣa vimale viśuddhe mahāvirajeragaṃ virasi dharmadhātu garbhe vivisāra a alaṃ svāhā

then all the ground as well will become of the intrinsic nature of a stūpa.

When one crosses any water, river, lake, ocean, or the like, if one consecrates it with this great vidyā mantra, all that water too will become of the intrinsic nature of a stūpa. If one recites

oṃ traiyadhe sarva tathāgatā hṛdaya garbhe jvala dharmadhātu garbhe saṃharaṇa āyuḥ saṃśodhaya pāpaṃ sarva tathāgata samanta vimala uṣṇīṣa viśuddhe mahāvirajeragaṃ virasi dharmadhātu vivisāra amamaki svāhā

then all water too will become of the intrinsic nature of a stūpa.

Furthermore, if one applies this great vidyā mantra so that it pervades space as far as space extends, all of space too will become of the intrinsic nature of a stūpa. If one recites

oṃ traiyadhve sarva tathāgatā hṛdaya garbhe jvala dharmadhātu garbhe saṃharaṇa ayuḥ saṃśodhaya pāpaṃ sarva tathāgatā samanta vimala uṣṇīṣa viśuddhe mahāvirajeragaṃ virasi dharmadhātu vivisāra a a ekhaṃya svāhā

then the whole element of space will become of the intrinsic nature of a stūpa, and it will become worthy of prostration, circumambulation, and offering.

If, reciting this Vimaloṣṇīṣa dhāraṇī, one establishes a stūpa upon any of the five great elements, all afflictions and obscurations will be purified.

Furthermore, if one consecrates with this vidyā mantra any material at all, whether earth, wood, stone, clay, or rubble, and builds a stūpa, it will become the cause of a stūpa of various jewels. If, having recited oṃ ratna caitye bañcaye vivisāra svāhā, one smears it with cow dung and the like, all of it will become the cause of a jewel stūpa.

If this great vidyā mantra is not set in, then even if one has a stūpa built of the seven precious substances, such as gold, silver, and beryl, it will be no different from stone, wood, and clods of earth. Why is that? Because the essence and the blessing of the secret mantra do not reside in the intrinsic nature of these materials themselves.

If one sets it in after consecrating it with this great vidyā, then on whatever great element one establishes the stūpa, the stains of obscuration of all countless eons will be purified. One will be born into a householder family that is like a great sāla tree, and will attain the irreversible ground.

If one consecrates with this great vidyā and establishes a stūpa on stone, wooden planks, and the like, one will without doubt be born into a brahmin family that is like a great sāla tree. One will traverse the ten grounds in succession and attain the eleventh, Samantaprabhā, Universal Light.

If one consecrates with this great vidyā and establishes a stūpa on gold, silver, beryl, and the like, one will without doubt be born into a royal family that is like a great sāla tree, and will attain the ground of Vajradhara.

Furthermore, if a stūpa is set up with this great vidyā mantra, then even setting up a single stūpa will be the same as setting up ten million. If it is not set up with this great vidyā, then even setting up ten million will be the same as setting up one. Why is that? It is as explained above.

The mantra of that great vidyā is this:
tadyathā sūkṣme same samaye śānte dānte nirkule yaśo tejo sarva buddha adhiṣṇāna adhiṣṭhite anumide svāhā

Its rite is this: one recites it twenty-one times over a ball of clay. If one then sets up a single stūpa, it will be the same as setting up ten million. The number and the benefits of setting up a hundred cannot be told even by the noble śrāvaka arhats. The number and the benefits of setting up a thousand cannot be told even by the bodhisattvas. The number and the benefits of setting up ten thousand cannot be told even by the tathāgatas. The number and the benefits of setting up a hundred thousand cannot be told even by the tathāgatas of the ten directions and the three times.

Therefore, the offering rite to be performed for setting up five or fewer supports for offering is as follows. First, at the place where the stūpa is to be built, or at the place of the stūpa's clay, or at the place from which the stūpa's clay is taken, one should bless the lord of the ground and set a boundary against the grahas and obstructors who dwell in the ground with evil intent and who create hindrances to the stūpa and to virtue. One consecrates the marking cord with this great vidyā mantra, reciting oṃ vajra sūtra hūṃ phaṭ. Then, visualizing that very cord as a vajra cord, one marks out with the line as much ground as is suitable for the stūpa, and as much ground as is suitable for the clay. When the ground has been marked out in this way, that site itself will become like the cause of a stūpa of many kinds of precious substances.

Then, on that same ground, one should set this great vidyā mantra into pegs of khadira wood eight finger-widths long. Reciting oṃ vajra kīli kīlaya sarva duṣṭaṃ hūṃ phaṭ seven times and visualizing them as vajra pegs, one plants them at the center and at the four corners. When they are planted in this way, one will be protected from humans and nonhumans, except for the karmic maturation of former actions. The khadira pegs are to be left in place and not pulled out until as much earth for the stūpa's clay as is needed has been taken.

Then one should recite this great vidyā mantra over sesame and white mustard seed, reciting oṃ amṛte hana hana hūṃ phaṭ seven times. By scattering them over the area, that very area will become countless abodes of the tathāgatas. Having thus blessed the ground as an abode of the tathāgatas and set a boundary against the evil intentions of obstructors, one makes offerings with incense, flowers, torma, and the like to the gods, nāgas, asuras, and others who dwell in that area, and dedicates them. If one then recites this Vimaloṣṇīṣa dhāraṇī, that area will become exceedingly auspicious and filled with blessing.

Then, when digging the earth for the stūpa's clay, one should dig while reciting this great vidyā mantra. Reciting oṃ vajra khana khana hūṃ phaṭ and visualizing that very ground as having turned into the five precious substances, if one digs, it will become the cause of the completely pure body of the Tathāgata.

Then the clean earth that has been taken is placed in a single clean spot. The rite for pounding the earth is this: one presses the mallet with the mudrā of Amṛtakuṇḍalin, the three-pronged vajra, consecrates it with this great vidyā mantra, and pounds. Reciting vajra muṅgara ākoṭaya ākoṭaya hūṃ phaṭ seven times, visualizing a vajra mallet, if one pounds, all afflictions and habitual tendencies without exception will be purified and destroyed.

Then, for kneading the clay, one touches scented water with the mudrā of Amṛtakuṇḍalin. Within that scented water one visualizes a white letter a, the syllable of the nature of reality, and from it a radiant moon disc. The great rays of light of that moon disc pervade the scented water, and one visualizes the scented water itself as having become the nature of amṛta. Then one should recite this great vidyā mantra, oṃ amṛte hūṃ phaṭ, seven times. Through this consecration, that water will become like a stream of great amṛta.

Then one should recite this great mantra for kneading the clay, oṃ vajra bramardanāya hūṃ phaṭ, seven times, and that very clay will become the body of the Tathāgata.

Then, to bless that very clay as the inconceivable body of the Tathāgata, one binds the mudrā of Amṛtakuṇḍalin over the clay and should recite this great vidyā dhāraṇī: namo ratnatrayāya tadyathā sūkṣme sūkṣme śānte śānte dānte dānte nirākule yaśo yaśovati tejo śuddhe viśuddhe sarva tathāgatā adhiṣṭhāna adhiṣṭhite ānamotehite svāhā One recites this great vidyā oneself three times seven, that is, twenty-one times. When it is consecrated with this great vidyā mantra, five kinds of rays of wisdom light will radiate from the clay itself into the ten directions.

And having radiated in the ten directions, they accomplish the benefit of all sentient beings, each according to how they are to be trained. When those rays gather back again, the clay itself takes on the nature of the five precious substances, and it comes to possess the causes and conditions of the inconceivable body of the Tathāgata. One visualizes this in the mind as well.

Then, when forming the clay balls, one should recite this great vidyā: oṃ vajra āyuṣe svāhā. When it has been recited seven times and the clay is blessed with it, from the clay ball itself clouds of rays of wisdom radiate and pervade the infinite world systems of the ten directions, and they work for the benefit of many beings. When those massed clouds of rays gather back into the clay ball, it becomes of the nature of the jewel of wisdom, utterly pure like a crystal. One visualizes this in the mind as well.

Then, inside the clay ball, one places the heart mantra of Vimaloṣṇīṣa, either made into a stamped seal or written as mantra on birch bark, cloth, paper, or tree bark. Within that, the heart syllables of each of the five families, oṃ hūṃ trāṃ hrīḥ aḥ, are joined together. Whether they are made into a stamped seal or written as letters, either way, if they are placed inside, the clay comes to possess the wisdom mind of the Tathāgata. When placing them inside, one should recite this vidyā mantra. When one recites oṃ vajra garbhe svāhā, the wisdom mind of all the tathāgatas gathers and enters into it. One visualizes this in the mind as well.

Then one should recite this great vidyā mantra for smearing the inside of the caitya mold with white mustard seed or sesame oil: oṃ araje biraje svāhā. When it has been recited three times and the mold is blessed, through the union of skillful means and wisdom it becomes a stupa of the very dharmadhātu. One visualizes this in the mind as well.

Then, when pressing the stupa, one should recite this great vidyā: oṃ vajra mutgara ākoṭaya ākoṭaya hūṃ phaṭ. When this is recited and the stupa is pressed, through the nondual union of the realm and wisdom it becomes a stupa of the dharmakāya. Then, if one also presses the stupa with the heart mantra of Vimaloṣṇīṣa given above, through the nondual union of the realm and wisdom it becomes a boundless stupa of the dharmakāya.

Then one should recite this great vidyā mantra for removing the stupa from the mold, and remove it: oṃ dharmadhātuye svāhā dharmadhātugarbhe svāhā. When this has been recited seven times and the stupa is removed, through the nondual union of skillful means and wisdom there arises a stupa, a boundless dharmakāya, which perfectly accomplishes the benefit of all wandering beings for as long as saṃsāra is not emptied. One visualizes this in the mind as well.

Then, for the stupa that has been removed from the vessel, one should recite this great vidyā mantra for inserting the wheel: oṃ pramaṇaye svāhā. When this is recited and the wheel is set up, the stupa is adorned with jewel parasols, jewel finials, jewel victory banners, jewel tassels, silk streamers and so on; it is adorned with the manifold qualities of nirvāṇa, and it fulfills the benefit of beings just as they wish. One visualizes this in the mind as well.

Then, when clothing the stupa in its robe, one recites this great vidyā and offers it: oṃ dharmadhātu svabhāva biśuddhe dharmate svāhā. When this has been recited many times and the robe is applied, the stupa comes to possess the meaning of the utterly pure dharmadhātu.

Then, as for the maṇḍala of the pressed stupas: if five stupas have been pressed, the maṇḍala should be drawn as five, in the four directions and the center. Likewise, whatever number of stupas has been pressed, whether a hundred or a thousand, a stone platform is built as a maṇḍala grid for that number, of a size to hold the stupas. On earth, stone, wood or the like, the maṇḍala is made square, and the throne beneath is made a platform in the form of a lion throne, a jewel throne, or a lotus throne. On top of that, one sprinkles cow dung and scented water. In the center and the four directions, at every spot where a stupa is to sit, one smears circles with saffron and sandalwood water, snaps lines with a cord, and draws the maṇḍala. As for drawing the maṇḍala: if five stupas have been pressed, it should be drawn as five, in the four directions of the maṇḍala and the center. Likewise, whatever number of stupas has been pressed, whether a hundred or a thousand, the ground should be divided by vajra-lattice lines into a maṇḍala grid for that number, of a size to hold the stupas. Every spot where a stupa is to be placed should be drawn as an eight-petaled lotus.

Then, when the stupas are to be seated in the maṇḍala, one recites this great vidyā mantra and invites them to be seated: oṃ supratiṣṭha stupe svāhā. When this has been recited three times and the stupas are placed, one comes to enjoy the perfect accomplishment of the benefit of oneself and others.

Then the sequence for setting out the stupas is this: first they are set in the center, then in the east, then in the south, then in the west, and then up to the north, in due order. When setting them out, one should recite this great vidyā mantra: oṃ vajra padmā saya ye svāhā. Having recited it three times, one says, "Please be seated on the vajra and lotus seat," and one visualizes in the mind as well that the whole earth, the realm of space, and the depths of the ocean are filled with stupas.

Furthermore, after each stupa is pressed, it is seated straightaway upon the maṇḍala, worshipped with the five kinds of offerings such as incense and flowers, and invited to remain. As for painting the stupas with colors and so forth, one envisions white for pacifying, yellow for enriching, red for magnetizing, and green for protection, and the robes too are offered in those same colors. One visualizes this in the mind in the same way.

Then the maṇḍala of the stupas should be blessed. Upon the maṇḍala stands a red syllable raṃ, and one should recite this great vidyā mantra: hūṃ raṃ svāhā. By reciting it three times, that syllable blazes up as a great mass of wisdom fire and completely purifies the conceptions attached to those places, and the very ground of the maṇḍala becomes a maṇḍala made of the five precious substances. One visualizes this in the mind as well.

Then, in the centers of the maṇḍala sections that have been drawn, whether five, a hundred, a thousand, or however many, a white letter A abides. From it one should recite this great vidyā mantra: oṃ a candre maṇḍal hūṃ phaṭ. When it has been recited as many times as is fitting, each becomes a moon-disc seat for a stūpa. Likewise, one also visualizes this with the mind.

Then, when performing the rite of the stūpa, face east or west, whichever is comfortable. Through samādhi, transform oneself into the body of Vajradhara in the great mudrā. At the heart, upon a moon disc, establish a five-pronged vajra radiating a mass of wisdom light. From the body and from all the pores, rays of wisdom light, together with maṇḍalas of buddhas, radiate boundlessly to the very limits like massed clouds. They bless all the realms of sentient beings and act for their benefit, and then dissolve back into the body. Likewise, one also visualizes this with the mind.

Then, the generation of the stūpa's celestial palace. For the stūpa placed in the maṇḍala, bind the mudrā of Vajradhātvīśvarī and touch the top of the stūpa. Then one should recite this great vidyā mantra: oṃ vajra dhātu adhitiṣṭha hūṃ. By reciting it and blessing in this way, the stūpa comes to abide within a measureless palace of various jewels. It rests upon a jeweled ground, upon a maṇḍala of the dharmadhātu, at the center of a moon seat. Likewise, one also visualizes this with the mind.

When each stūpa has thus been erected and set in place, one makes offerings to it with the various kinds of offerings. These are lion banners, rice, and various presents, arranged in the same manner, together with canopies, pennants, parasols, garlands, incense, lamps, and fragrances. Then one should recite this great vidyā that thoroughly purifies the kinds of offerings: oṃ sarva pūja puṣpe svāhā. By reciting it and blessing in this way, perfect massed clouds of Samantabhadra's offerings gather thickly and fall like a stream of rain. Likewise, one also visualizes this with the mind.

One makes offerings to the stūpa with each portion of the five kinds of offerings. As a gift one offers one each of small silk streamers, canopies such as pennants, or small parasols, whichever is suitable.

When it has thus been erected according to the rite, the clarifying of the samādhi of contemplation is as follows. In the four directions and the center of the stūpa, five places in all, one should properly contemplate and visualize the buddhas of the five families with their body colors, ornaments, attire, and perfect retinues.

That stūpa, made of the five wisdoms, is by nature completely pure and clear. In the center of its vases, visualize the heart essence of the Vimaloṣṇīṣa dhāraṇī and the individual heart syllables of the five families, one syllable each, clear and shining like refined gold. From them various rays of wisdom light radiate boundlessly into the ten directions of the five families. From that, clouds of tathāgata bodies pervade to the limits of space, adorning and accomplishing everything to the very ends of the dharmadhātu. They bless all world realms, accomplish the benefit of all beings, and fulfill their hopes. Then they gather back and strike the crown of the head and the heart of that very one. The obscurations of activity and the rest are purified. Visualize that body, speech, and mind have become vajra itself.

In a conch bowl, one offers an arghya mixed with scented water and various fragrances. Bind the arghya mudrā and place the conch bowl upon it. In the scented water, a white A becomes a moon disc, and its rays of light transform the scented water into the nature of the nectar of wisdom. Contemplate and visualize this, and then one should recite this great vidyā for presenting offerings: oṃ a arghaṃ pratīccha svāhā. After reciting it, waft the arghya and raise it three times to the crown of the head, then offer it. By this, all the noble ones become pleased. One also says in words, "May all the noble ones be pleased!"

Then visualize that clouds of an ocean of mudrās spread out at the crown of the head and gather into the wisdom body, conferring all the perfect empowerments. Then one should read this Vimaloṣṇīṣa dhāraṇī and recite it as many times as possible, and make extensive dedications and aspiration prayers.

If it is not convenient to complete this rite as given, then, through the samādhi of transforming into the body of one's own deity, one should make one's contemplation even. One contemplates the stūpa, the offering articles, and all the rest as by nature the completely pure dharmadhātu. Then, even if one erects the stūpa in this way, it is like performing the rite extensively, and there is no conflict.

Even if it does not turn out like that, if one erects a stūpa while reading, holding, writing down, and keeping this Vimaloṣṇīṣa dhāraṇī, that itself is the extensive performance of the rite. That stūpa is transformed into the body of a tathāgata and brings about the perfect benefit of oneself and others. If this Vimaloṣṇīṣa dhāraṇī is not recited, then even though offerings are made to the stūpa and its enthronement is performed, it does not become enthroned.

The scripture, too, states this:

Because the very nature of the mantra
Of all vidyā mantras and secret mantras is not clear,
And the samādhi of contemplation is not clear,
Stūpas and other sacred images,
Though erected, will not come to completion.

So it is stated. As for the qualities of erecting stūpas and images by one endowed with secret mantra and contemplation, it is said:

This sublime rite of the stūpa:
The distinction of the merit it brings
Is that, with a stūpa in which this dhāraṇī is recited,
To erect one is like erecting ten million.
Of saṃsāra from ages long past,
The five deeds of immediate retribution and the heaps of evil,
However many there are, are cleansed without remainder.
One comes to have a long and perfect life.
When born in other lives,
One is born into a supreme and noble family,
Fully endowed with the finest wealth,
And is born a wheel-turning king.
One comes to remember former and later lives.
In all the worlds of the three times
One is never separated from the Three Jewels,
And obstructors and harms cannot shake one.
One's wishes are completely fulfilled.
In the end, one attains manifest awakening.

The Vimaloṣṇīṣa dhāraṇī is complete.

Translator's notes
