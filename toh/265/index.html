About this text

Toh 265 in the Kangyur is a sūtra excerpt known in English as "The Ritual of Homage from the Noble Great Realization Fully Expansive," drawn from the larger Tibetan canonical work sometimes identified in Sanskrit as the Mahābodhi-vistara-sūtra and in Tibetan as the Phags pa rtogs pa chen po yongs su rgyas pa'i mdo. It is a devotional and liturgical text that brings together several thematic chapters: a ritual of homage, the names and praises of the Tathāgatas of the three times, the twelve divisions of scripture, homage to bodhisattvas by name, and a chapter on confession. The text is addressed by bodhisattvas assembled from the ten directions to the Buddha and takes the form of reverent address, praise, and purification practice. As the opening makes clear, what survives here is a condensed and edited translation of select portions from that larger sūtra, rather than the complete work.

Homage to all buddhas and bodhisattvas.

From the sūtra called "The Noble Great Realization Fully Expansive": the chapter on the ritual of homage; the chapter on the occasion for speaking of the virtues proclaimed through the names; the names of the Tathāgatas of the three times; the twelve divisions of scripture; the chapter on praising the bodhisattvas by name and paying homage to them; and the chapter on confession — these have appeared in a brief form, corrected and translated.

Then the bodhisattvas who had come from the ten directions joined their palms together and addressed the Blessed One (the Buddha):

"We have come to this world-realm through the power and blessing of those buddhas of the ten directions, and having seen the face of the Blessed One and heard the Dharma of the Great Vehicle, we request permission for ourselves, for gods and humans, to receive, hold, and bear this scriptural collection of sūtras. We ask that it be granted. For after the Blessed One has passed beyond sorrow into nirvāṇa, we will teach it and spread it without interruption, widely and ceaselessly, in this land and region, and in other directions — in mountain retreats, in forests, and at places beneath trees where mantras are held; in villages, in towns and settlements, in dwellings, and in hermitage residences; in charnel grounds, beneath trees, in the open without shelter; at stūpas, in temples, in the courtyards of Saṅgha monasteries; in places where many people gather to hear Dharma taught; in the residences of ordinary people; and to all who have become human.

Why is this? So that the sacred Dharma may abide for a long time, and so that the lower destinies may be permanently closed off.

How is that? Having heard that the teaching of the Blessed One shuts the doors of the hells, if even a single word from this scriptural collection is recited aloud, the gods too will rejoice and be delighted, and will constantly attend and serve, and will themselves engage in virtuous conduct.

Moreover, some people, upon hearing this scriptural collection fully expansive with great realization, will rejoice and be delighted, have faith and reverence, will write it, recite it and read it aloud, retain and bear it, and will pay homage. Even if such a person merely calls by name some Tathāgata or some bodhisattva taught in this scriptural collection, that person, in this present life, will be happy and at ease, no sin or non-virtue will arise, and at the time of death we bodhisattvas and the gods will lead the way before them, and they will see pure buddha-fields and, with faith, will take birth together in one place.

Why is this? Because they have received and fully held this scriptural collection.

To receive and fully hold this sūtra collection is to fully hold the body of the Buddha. One who fully holds the body of the Tathāgata is a bodhisattva. Therefore we share one and the same training. For this reason, by fully holding this scriptural collection, we will be born together in one place, will not abandon one another, will not be separated, and our aspiration is one."

Then, from among those assembled, the king of the gods and demigods (asuras), the great king of Brahmā, the gods of the Heaven of the Thirty-Three, the four great kings who guard the four directions of the world, the attendant vajra-bearers, the great commanders and others, the nāga kings Nanda and Upananda, the king of the asuras, the king of the garuḍas, the king of the kinnaras, the gods of the nine-fold cluster, the gods of the mountains, the gods of the trees, the gods of rivers and streams, the gods of the lakes, the gods of the earth, the gods of fire, the gods of the wind, and many other such kings — those and their like, measureless and countless in number — together with the great gods, rose from their seats.

They bowed with their faces and crowns to the ground, joined their palms together, and with reverence and devotion addressed the Blessed One: "Blessed One, from this time forward, we and all those with us will constantly uphold this scriptural text of the Dharma, fully permeated by great realization, just as the Blessed One has taught it. Wherever this sūtra abides, we — the kings of the gods — will always be present before it. We will keep that place clean and pure. Whether it is inside a stūpa, a dwelling among ordinary people, a secluded area, or any place whatsoever, even if one holds this sūtra with a limb that is not entirely clean, we will protect that person so that no improper harm can come upon them. They will constantly behold virtuous qualities, be reborn among the gods in a future life, and by the merit thus gained, they will encounter the arising of buddhas and never fall away from the Great Vehicle."

Then the Blessed One spoke to those great bodhisattvas and to the kings of the gods, asuras, and rākṣasas and the rest: "So it is. So it is. It is just as you have said. If it is difficult even to hear a scriptural text of the Dharma such as this, what need is there to speak of comprehending it? For one who takes up, holds, recites, and reads this scriptural text of the Dharma — having cleansed and purified oneself with washing and bathing, having generated faith, put on clean robes, made the place clean, adorned and beautified the interior of the place with silken canopies and victory-banners and the like, anointed it with excellent fragrant sandalwood powder and other powders, burned many kinds of incense, and prostrated — doing so three times by day and three times by night, for seven days, reading and reciting this scriptural text of the Dharma with undistracted mind and attention, with unforgetting mindfulness, with single-pointed steadiness, with straightforward intention, with mindfulness established, with correct and unwavering reception and holding of the text, with stable conduct, with faultless recitation and chanting — in such a manner, reciting and reading by name the Tathāgatas taught in this sūtra, along with the twelve categories of scripture and the bodhisattvas, and venerating, prostrating to, and upholding them with faith and devotion — all heavy misdeeds whatsoever will be thoroughly removed, just as this sūtra explains. Why is that? This scriptural text of the Dharma, fully permeated by great realization, has been practiced, trained in, blessed, and upheld by all the buddhas of the ten directions. It is the mother of all buddhas, the king of all sūtras, the treasury of the highest teaching, the path of all bodhisattvas, and the very heart-essence of the deepest scriptural texts of the Dharma fully permeated by great realization.

"By way of a single illustration, it is comparable to, and inconceivable like, six great things in the world — namely the four great ones and more. What are the six? The great earth, the great water, the great fire, the great wind — these four great ones — and then the sun, and space. So it is: this sūtra, by way of comparison, is like the great earth in that it supports all things, pure and impure, good and bad, great and small. Again, this sūtra is like the great water in that it washes clean all the filthy mire of impurity and every stain. Again, this sūtra is like the great fire in that it burns away all the sparks and embers of the unclean defilements of karma and afflictions. Again, this sūtra is like the great wind in that it scatters and blows away all the unclean dust-rags of the defilements of karma and afflictions.

Again, this sūtra is like the sun in that it illuminates throughout every darkness of ignorance. Again, this sūtra is like empty space in that it opens up and holds within itself all things without exception.

This sūtra, vast and complete, thoroughly pervaded by the great realization of the Mahāyāna sūtras, is without equal in its breadth. It holds within itself all beings who reach as far as bodhisattvas, śrāvakas, and those of the desire realm, the form realm, and the formless realm.

Therefore, you should receive this sūtra and hold it completely, spread it widely, and let it pervade everywhere. Devote yourselves to it with faith and respect. You, and all the great bodhisattvas with you, should constantly assimilate the wisdom of the buddhas and come to behold the essential nature of the Tathāgata.

O you kings of the gods, those who take up and fully uphold this sūtra should always behold me and all the buddhas of the past who have manifested complete and perfect awakening and turned the great wheel of Dharma.

Then from within the great assembled retinue, a bodhisattva named Steadfast Faith rose from his seat, arranged his robe properly, bowed his head to the feet of the Blessed One, and addressed the Blessed One in these words: "Blessed One, we beseech you to grant us the occasion to ask, for the sake of the healing and happiness of the many."

Then the merchant's son Water-Pourer recited three times the name of the Blessed One Ratnakūṭa, and the fish heard it; immediately upon their death, they were reborn as gods in the Heaven of the Thirty-Three. I also beseech you to teach it so that sentient beings whose ethical foundation has deteriorated and who are confused by afflictions may be fully liberated."

Then the Blessed One said to the bodhisattva Steadfast Faith: "Son of good family, if I were to fully set forth the names of all the buddhas of the ten directions, even teaching for a hundred million aeons, there would be no end to their completeness."

One could count the number of drops in all the waters, but one could never recount and know all the names of every buddha. All the mountains of Mount Meru could be weighed and measured, but one could never declare and know all the names of every Blessed One, every buddha. The extent of the realm of space could be known, but one could never declare and know all the names of every Blessed One, every buddha.

Therefore I shall now set out for you a mere summary of the names of the Blessed Ones, the buddhas of the three times. If any person hears these names even once, that person, the very moment they pass from this life, will be born in a divine realm. If, having heard them, one arouses faith and reverence, writes them down, recites them by name, and pays homage, then the heavy sins of immeasurable cycles of existence will be purified, and one will gain merit beyond measure. When that person's time is spent, they will be born in whichever buddha-field of the ten directions they aspire to in their mind. They will see me, and they will also come to see the buddhas of the fortunate aeons yet to come.

Furthermore, Blessed One, we and all beings, since time without beginning, through wrong views have described what is coarse as if it were the four weighty acts, and the four weighty acts as if they were coarse; or have called what is destroyed "not destroyed," and what is not destroyed "destroyed"; or have called what is heavy "light" and what is light "heavy"; or have seen what is pure as impure and what is impure as pure — such wrong views and the like. We have praised and extolled the learning of the world, while showing no reverence for the teaching of the Buddha; we have taken up worldly aims rooted in non-virtue, pondering them and holding to them — maintaining the eight kinds of uninvestigated wrong view. We have called the authentic words of the Tathāgata the words of Māra, and the words of Māra we have called the authentic words of the Tathāgata. At times we have placed our faith in and clung to the expositions of the six non-Buddhist teachers. We have fallen into the confused thought that, because the Tathāgata has passed fully into nirvāṇa, the Three Jewels are impermanent — and other wrong views of this kind, beyond counting. Therefore the fear and terror we now feel, the shame and remorse, are likewise inconceivable. We take refuge in the Three Jewels. The Blessed One, the buddhas, are the father and mother who regard all with perfect equanimity, possessing compassion and loving-kindness. The bodhisattvas are our spiritual friends. We pray that through our regret and confession purification may be granted to us and all sentient beings, and that from this moment forward we may never again commit such acts.

For we have heard the declaration of the Blessed One: if one does not confess and acknowledge the four root downfalls, or the five acts of immediate consequence, or the act of denigrating the true Dharma, one will assuredly fall into the hells. And in that case neither the Tathāgatas, nor the bodhisattvas, nor the śrāvakas, nor the pratyekabuddhas will be capable of offering refuge to such a person. Neither the Saṅgha, nor one's own father and mother, nor the gods, nor any person in the world will be able to provide refuge. Hearing this, the fear and terror, the shame and remorse that arise in us are truly inconceivable. We take refuge in the Three Jewels.

The many precious buddhas of the past, the thousand buddhas yet to come, and the buddhas of the present time — the emanation Blessed One, Śākyamuni, and all the Blessed One buddhas — are the lords of compassion and loving-kindness, the father and mother who regard all with perfect equanimity. The bodhisattvas are our spiritual friends. We pray that, through our regret and confession, purification may be granted to us and all sentient beings — that all the heavy misdeeds accumulated since countless aeons past, whether obscurations of affliction, obscurations of karma, or obscurations of karmic maturation, together with the four weighty acts, the five acts of immediate consequence, the ten non-virtues, and all other grave wrongs, may be cleared away.

Furthermore, Blessed One, may all the heavy misdeeds accumulated by us and all beings since countless aeons past, throughout the succession of lives in cyclic existence, be cleared away, and may we never commit them again from this moment forward.

Furthermore, Blessed One, from countless aeons past until this very present life, we and all beings have made improper use of the general common property of the Saṅgha, the common property of the Saṅgha of all ten directions, the property of the present Saṅgha, the property of the five Saṅgha communities, the property belonging to all monks collectively, and the goods of all donors. Moreover, we have falsely claimed to have attained the true Dharma, the four meditative concentrations, and the four fruits, when in fact we had not. Through the power of such evil acts as causes, we became sources of thorough affliction. And having gone forth from home life into homelessness, we broke the four root vows; we transgressed the eight weighty rules, the six rules, the thirteen cases requiring a communal meeting of the Saṅgha,

the two indeterminate rules, the thirty rules entailing expiation with forfeiture, the ninety-one rules entailing simple expiation, the four rules requiring individual confession, the many rules of training, and the seven rules of serious weight — transgressing these and committing each of them in turn; and further, going so far as to commit the four root defeats and the five acts of immediate retribution. Moreover, having performed such acts as denigrating the true nature of reality and the profound sūtras, and having carried out deeds born of great craving — concealing and hiding all of these so that they grew and multiplied day by day — all of this and more, we now confess and acknowledge with regret before the buddhas of the ten directions, for the sake of benefiting the many beings of this world.

All ordinary beings, from time without measure, have failed to repay the kindness of the buddhas and of their fathers and mothers, have not sought the Dharma of complete purification, and have performed all manner of non-virtuous actions. Or, puffed up and arrogant through pride in their own lineage, they performed non-virtuous acts with full deliberate intent, and yet, not seeing them as transgressions, they acted from foolish stupidity, as though they were children. And having drawn near to companions who are friends in non-virtue, their minds deranged by afflictions, they sought no satisfaction with the five qualities of sensual desire and performed non-virtuous acts. Or they were deceived by attachment to good rebirths. Their minds troubled and agitated by the knots of attachment, aversion, and delusion — accumulating many sins gathered together through body, speech, and mind — all of these we now confess with remorse, holding nothing back.

Furthermore, having failed to serve and honour the buddhas, the sacred Dharma, and the noble Saṅgha with proper reverence — all those many sins of such a kind we confess, disclose, and acknowledge individually.

And having failed to attend with proper reverence upon the bodhisattvas and the solitary buddhas (pratyekabuddhas) — all those many sins of such a kind we confess, disclose, and acknowledge individually.

Obscured and concealed by delusion, and having committed non-virtues through arrogance, laziness, and self-deprecation — all those many sins of such a kind we confess, disclose, and acknowledge individually.

Now, having made offerings to all the buddhas of the ten directions, may we cause all sentient beings without exception to be established upon the ten grounds, and may each and every one of them come to fully realize the authentic truth of the Tathāgata.

Then the Blessed One spoke to Prince Sudarśana: "Excellent! Excellent! O great noble one of the royal lineage, it is supremely excellent! Great noble one of the royal lineage, you yourself are skilled in discerning virtuous and non-virtuous actions; and beyond confessing and openly disclosing your own sinful acts with great fear and terror and remorse, you have further confessed and acknowledged them for the sake of all sentient beings. Therefore, you are truly a great noble one of the royal lineage. Moreover, beyond freeing yourself from sin and non-virtue, you have worked to free all sentient beings from sin as well; and rather than making aspirational prayers for your own sake alone, you made aspirational prayers for the sake of all sentient beings from the very start, and dedicated the merit toward their fully realizing the authentic truth of the Tathāgata's word. Therefore, you are truly and authentically a great noble one of the royal lineage."

Then, once Prince Sudarśana and the three thousand men had finished their confession and disclosure, they offered praise to the Blessed One in verse:

O Blessed One, singularly supreme above all,
ornament of the three realms, you who are father and mother,
yours is the wisdom that is equal toward all,
beyond compare in that equality toward every being —
I bow before the buddha, so rare to find.

The childish employ wisdom for their own sake alone,
and give wealth and goods expecting something in return;
a buddha of great loving-kindness is not like that.
To him whose great diligence makes equal both enemy and friend,
I go for refuge.

Among all two-footed beings, the buddha is supreme,
endowed with the eye of wisdom, whose knowing and seeing pervades all.
The gods have not attained that wisdom —

To all the buddhas of the three times,
we all bow down with reverence and respect.
Likewise to the buddhas I go for refuge —
it is as though one had made offerings to all the buddhas of the three times.

Then the Tathāgatas of all ten directions spoke in verse to Prince Sudarśana and the three thousand people:

If you desire to gather the Dharma and qualities of a buddha,
do not follow after people of much learning alone.
Honor those who are skilled in virtue as you would an abbot,
and you will swiftly attain buddhahood.

If one keeps and guards moral discipline in perfect purity,
that person's name is truly a monk (bhikṣu).
If one is able to contemplate emptiness,
that person's name is one who practices yoga.
If one is able to accomplish deeds with diligence,
that person's name is one who walks the noble path.
If one accomplishes nirvāṇa,
that person's name is one of true and perfect bliss.

Novices bound by the vow of moral discipline,
and monks who hold their precepts in pure virtue,
should perform the restoration ceremony every half-month.
Let the Dharma robes be inspected and then distributed;
the almsbowl, staff, and other implements should be received with respect,
and with purity one should collect alms in a clean manner.

All the gods were filled with joy,
and bowing with palms joined in respectful homage, they said:
"O gods, we shall ensure that these people here
accomplish that which has been entrusted to them.

Just as the other gods of the Heaven of the Thirty-Three
render respectful homage and service to Śakra, Indra, the lord of gods,
so let them do likewise in this very life and attain excellence,
and in other lives to come the benefit is beyond measure.
By going forth into renunciation and guarding the ground of moral discipline,
one repays the kindness of the Buddha."

Prince Sudarśana, hear this now as well:
Apart from myself and awakening,
let all people of the world
generate a mind of pure faith and devotion,
and directly know that the buddhas of the ten directions dwell evermore
and do not pass into nirvāṇa.
Likewise with the Dharma and the Saṅgha.
People who are such as this
repay the kindness of the Blessed One.

And again, Prince Sudarśana, hear this:
Without making any distinction or discrimination among all people of the world,
one directly knows the Dharma of the single vehicle,
and understands with certainty that in the ten directions — in the past,
in the present, and in the time to come —
the buddhas teach only the single Great Vehicle,
with no such thing as a second or a third.
Without making any distinction or discrimination:
if one can generate a mind of such faith and devotion,
people who are such as this
repay the kindness of the Buddha.

Prince Sudarśana, hear this now as well:
After I have passed away and departed,
if among humanity there be those
who continuously generate a mind of faith and devotion,
holding that buddha-nature itself is present
in all sentient beings without exception —
people who are such as this
repay the kindness of the Blessed One.

If someone practices virtue even for a moment
without seeking a result within existence,
and dedicates it toward the unsurpassed fruit,
that person repays the kindness of the Buddha.

Hear this yet again, Prince Sudarśana:
After I have passed into nirvāṇa,
if among humanity there are those
who, with reverence and devotion toward the Three Jewels,
recite this sūtra even for a moment,
and offer a teaching-fee to the preceptor —
people who are such as this
repay the kindness of the Sugata (the Well-Gone One, an epithet of the Buddha).

If others give various goods and wealth,
clothing, food, and possessions,
and distribute them broadly to all —
that person repays the kindness of the Blessed One.

Prince Sudarśana, hear this now as well:
In the final age of this my Dharma,
if some among the people take counsel together
and listen to this sūtra even for a moment,
that is of greater benefit
than making offerings to a hundred thousand bodhisattvas.

Because a supreme sūtra of the highest vehicle such as this
is the mother of the buddhas' awakening,
if one has faith and reflects upon the awakening of the buddhas,
and generates reverence and devotion toward this sūtra,
and with non-dual mind brings one's thoughts to evenness toward all —
that person repays the kindness of the Buddha.

Prince Sudarśana, hear this now as well:
If in the final age of this Dharma
someone thinks to repay the kindness of the Buddha
and takes even a single session of sitting practice before a buddha image,
that is of greater benefit
than giving life to all sentient beings dwelling in the three realms.

If one disturbs and slanders the meditative concentration of the Saṅgha,
it exceeds the harm done by all the people who fill the three realms —

it is equal to killing all sentient beings.
If one disturbs and slanders the meditative concentration of the Saṅgha,
that sin is likewise the same.

If, upon seeing someone else practice virtue,
one disparages and slanders it and remains without faith,
one cuts the very lineage of the Three Jewels
and does not repay the kindness of the Blessed One.

Then the Blessed One bestowed upon Prince Sudarśana and the three thousand people, having finished teaching the Dharma, the joy of gladness arose in them, and they received the feet of the Blessed One upon the crowns of their heads.

From the noble sūtra of the Great Realization Fully Expanded, the chapter on the rites of prostration and the chapter of repentance is now complete.

The opening phrase "Two and one hundred and twelve verses" renders a colophon-style numerical notation indicating the text contains one hundred and twelve verses, with "two" likely referring to a chapter or section number, though this structural marker is somewhat elliptical in the original.

The nāga kings "Nanda and Upananda" correspond to the Tibetan "Dga' bo" (Nanda, "joyful") and "Bsnyen dga' bo" (Upananda, "closely joyful"), a well-known pair of nāga brothers in Buddhist cosmological literature.

The phrase rendered "gods of the nine-fold cluster" translates "Bu dgu ma'i lha," a somewhat unusual grouping; it may refer to a specific class of astral or planetary deities, and the exact referent is not entirely certain. The phrase rendered "twelve categories of scripture" translates མདོ་སྡེ་བཅུ་གཉིས, literally "twelve sūtra-collections," which in Buddhist canonical tradition refers to the twelvefold classification of scriptural texts (dvādaśāṅga-pravacana). This is a well-established technical term and the rendering is standard.

The simile of the six great elements concludes here mid-passage; the comparisons to the sun and space would naturally follow in the next section of the text. The bodhisattva's name, rendered "Steadfast Faith," translates the Tibetan མོས་པའི་མཚན (mos pa'i mtshan), literally "one whose mark or name is aspiration/devotion." The term mos pa (adhimukti) carries the sense of confident aspiration or resolute faith; "Steadfast Faith" or alternatively "Aspiration" would both be defensible.

The king's name, ལྷའི་དབང་ཕྱུག་འོད (lha'i dbang phyug 'od), has been rendered "Indra of the Gods, called Light" — the title lha'i dbang phyug is a standard epithet for Indra (Śakra), and འོད (Light) appears to be a proper name distinguishing this king, likely a human ruler with a divine epithet rather than the deity Indra himself.

The Heaven of the Thirty-Three (སུམ་ཅུ་རྩ་གསུམ་གནམ་གྱི་ལྷ) is the Trāyastriṃśa heaven, the realm of Śakra, standard in Buddhist cosmology. The passage closes mid-sentence, breaking off after "By reciting the names of the buddhas of the ten directions and the three times, I shall now..." This is because the Tibetan source itself ends here; the continuation belongs to the next passage.

The epithet rendered "King of Flame, Sovereign of Mount Meru" translates the compound name literally; it may refer to a specific buddha whose name combines the imagery of a lamp-flame and Mount Meru as "the king of mountains," functioning as a single proper name. The phrase "the seed may yet be scorched of its own accord" renders "sa bon tshig par rang 'byung" and conveys the idea that merely hearing these teachings will cauterize the seeds of negative karma even without full comprehension, a standard Mahāyāna notion of the power of buddha-name recitation and hearing the Dharma.

The thirty thousand Tathāgatas called Dīpaṃkara: the Tibetan reads "mar me mdzad stong phrag sum cu," literally "thirty thousand Lamp-Makers," which is a standard epithet of the buddha Dīpaṃkara. The text appears to refer to a group of buddhas sharing this name, which is attested in certain sūtra traditions.

The name "Sixteen Royal Sons" rendered as a buddha name is unusual and likely refers to a group of buddhas identified with the sixteen princes of the buddha Mahābhijñājñānābhibhū, well known from the Lotus Sūtra tradition.
