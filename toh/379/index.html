About this text

Toh 379 in the Kangyur is a tantric scripture known in Tibetan as "Dur khrod bdag mo'i gsol pa" and associated with the deity Vārāhī, the sow-headed vajrayāna goddess also known in Sanskrit as Vārāhī. It belongs to the class of Highest Yoga Tantra texts and takes the form of a dialogue in which the Mistress of the Charnel Ground petitions the hero Heruka with questions concerning the body colors of the assembled goddesses, the meaning of the name Vārāhī, the significance of the seed syllables, and the nature of the generation and completion stages. Heruka's responses unfold as a teaching on the five wisdoms, the qualities of pacification, and liberation within the unchanging basic space of one's own mind, placing the text firmly within the Cakrasaṃvara or Heruka cycle of tantric literature. As Toh 379 in the Kangyur, it sits among the tantra section of the canonical collection of Buddha's translated word preserved in the Tibetan tradition.

Translation

The Mistress of the Charnel Ground makes her request. "What are the body colors of the goddesses, and how, by knowing them, does one become liberated? To what does the name Vārāhī apply? Upon what is the meaning of the syllables bestowed? To what does the essence of the generation and completion stages apply?"

The hero Heruka then declared: "Listen, O Vārāhī of the charnel ground! Undefiled by the taint of faults, white and free of conceptual elaboration, she who pacifies — undefiled by the taint of non-attachment, red and resounding with fierce cry, the Mother. The sovereign freedom of mind-itself, without any single antidote, like the wrathful one whose complexion is the color of the sky — liberation in the unchanging basic space of one's own mind. Vast in the five wisdoms (pañcajñāna), yellow, the Faultless Accomplisher, immovable in the royal essential nature — undefiled by the taint of the variegated, she who is adorned with the luster of the deep blue sapphire (indranīla), gathering the variegated into a single essence, in no aspect whatsoever undergoing change, not changing from the dharmadhātu, the basic space of phenomena, undefiled by any conceptual thought.

The syllable Phag means freedom from conceptuality. Since the basic space itself is free from the conceptual elaboration of discursive thought, the syllable Mo means dissolving into the basic space. When these are analyzed, three aspects emerge.

The sacred Dharma Vārāhī of non-arising: because she is without arising or cessation, she is the dharmakāya, the truth body. The Vārāhī of expressible sound: Phag is Kāli, and is said to be skillful means, while Mo is Āli, and is held to be the saṃbhogakāya, the enjoyment body. The Vārāhī who symbolizes the basic space: the body color indicates emptiness, the face of Phag is compassion free of conceptuality, and for the Vārāhī of skillful means and wisdom, the nakedness that is bliss-and-emptiness free of fault — because it is beyond conceptuality, she is the nirmāṇakāya, the emanation body.

The letters are vivid yet non-conceptual; even when they dissolve, they remain completely open, appearance-and-emptiness free of extremes. Because the essence of the generation and completion stages is inseparable, what is indicated is the luminosity of appearance that is empty, and that very luminosity is the essential nature of appearance — non-dual union, free from assertions, in which each seals the other. There is no doubt that liberation comes through this." Thus he spoke.

This concludes the first chapter — teaching the generation stage of the Latter Tantra — of the Tantra of the Manifest Arising of Vārāhī, the inconceivable primordial wisdom of the non-dual mind of all ḍākinīs.

Then the Mistress of the Charnel Ground, smiling, made her request to the great hero: "What are the mantras among the secret mantras? What are the syllables to be recited? How are the four maṇḍala-wheels to be arranged?"

Heruka declared: "Listen carefully, Mistress of the Charnel Ground. Among the syllables of the secret mantras, the syllable OṂ performs all things. The syllable of the blissful body is Vajra Vārāhī. The KRODHAKĀLI of speech gives rise to power. HARIŚ is the mind of realization. ĀVEŚAYA gives rise to qualities. EVAṂ MAYĀ accomplishes enlightened activities. Beyond those, there is furthermore this: in the very center of the five maṇḍala-wheels, having sealed with the fourth syllable, the recitation is performed in the three modes — outer, inner, and secret.

For the outer recitation of pacification, the syllables are white in color. White rays radiate from them, and by sending these rays through the pores of the hair so that they touch all sentient beings, one should visualize that all obscurations — afflictive and cognitive — are purified. By having the rays touch the Tathāgatas as well, one offers a pleasing offering to them. The rays dissolve back into oneself, and pacification is accomplished.

For enrichment, with the radiation and gathering of yellow rays, one should know how to radiate and gather as before. For magnetization, radiate and gather red rays. For wrath, radiate and gather black rays. By radiating the variegated rays, all siddhis and accomplishments of the four activities are fulfilled. For the inner and secret recitation as well, one should know how to radiate and gather as before."

Vārāhī of the charnel ground again asked: "What is the syllable that exhausts and increases? What is the ultimate syllable?" The great hero further declared: "A is the syllable that exhausts delusion. HA exhausts aversion. RI is the syllable that exhausts pride.

NI exhausts attachment. SA is the syllable that exhausts envy. A increases the wisdom of the dharmadhātu. HA increases the mirror-like wisdom. RI increases the wisdom of equality. NI increases the wisdom of discernment. SA increases the wisdom of accomplished activity. A appears as the buddha ḍākinī. HA appears as the vajra ḍākinī. RI appears as the jewel ḍākinī. NI appears as the lotus ḍākinī. SA appears as the karma ḍākinī. Through A, the Tathāgata family is obtained. Through HA, the great vajra family is obtained. RI obtains the jewel family. NI obtains the lotus family. SA obtains the great karma family. A is the ultimate syllable of the dharmakāya. HA is the syllable of the form of the saṃbhogakāya. RI is the nirmāṇakāya. NI is the syllable of the essential-nature body. SA is the syllable of the vajra body.

So he declared.

Vārāhī of the charnel ground again asked: "Through the gradation of superior, middling, and lesser faculties, how does one come to know and be liberated?" The great hero further declared: "There are three grades of faculties — superior, middling, and lesser. To a person of superior faculties, instructions are given in six instants. By the wisdom of the first instant, conceptuality regarding what is apprehended is severed. By the wisdom of the second instant, conceptuality regarding the apprehender is severed. By the wisdom of the third instant, conceptuality of both apprehended and apprehender is severed. By the wisdom of the fourth instant, conceptuality clinging to meditative experiences is severed. By the wisdom of the fifth instant, conceptuality regarding pleasant meditative experience is severed. By the wisdom of the sixth instant, conceptuality of mental engagement is severed. That such a person will be liberated is beyond doubt.

To a person of middling faculties, the instructions given concern the six winds. Familiarization with the co-emergent wind clears the obscuration of intrinsic nature. Familiarization with the directly arising wind clears obscurations like those of nighttime. Familiarization with the self-originated wind clears the obscuration of resolution. Familiarization with the relinquishing wind clears the obscuration of what goes before. Familiarization with the purifying wind clears the cognitive obscuration regarding knowable objects. Familiarization with the completing wind progressively overcomes the grounds one by one. All the wisdoms arise at one and the same time, and all knowable objects are resolved at one and the same time. These are not six winds but wisdom itself. Because darkness obscures, wisdom arises gradually and the obscurations are cleared gradually. That such a person will be liberated admits of no doubt.

To a person of lesser faculties, the object is the pure buddha-field, the body is the pure maṇḍala, one's own mind in its purity is the body of Vārāhī, pure speech is declared to be mantra, and the purity of view and meditation is siddhi. Whatever is non-dual between the illustrative example and the ultimate meaning — without that, siddhi will not arise for one who lacks the fortune for it. If one meditates for six years, there is no doubt."

So he declared.

This concludes the subsequent tantra of the Tantra of the Inconceivable Non-Dual Wisdom of the Mind of All the Ḍākinīs, the Arising of the Manifest Vārāhī.

Translator's notes

The passage contains several Tantric seed syllables and deity-name mantras rendered in transliteration. The specific identifications of KRODHAKĀLI and HARIŚ follow standard Cakrasaṃvara-related Vārāhī traditions; the precise sandhi and reading of "ཧ་རི་ས" as HARIŚ involves minor uncertainty but the identification is conventional in this corpus.

The phrase "ཡིག་འབྲུ་བཞི་པས་རྒྱས་བཏབ་ནས" rendered as "having sealed with the fourth syllable" could alternatively mean "sealed with the four syllables"; the singular reading is preferred here given the context of a culminating syllable, but this is genuinely ambiguous. The five wisdoms associated with the syllables A, HA, RI, NI, SA follow the standard Vajrayāna pentad: dharmadhātu wisdom, mirror-like wisdom, wisdom of equality, all-discerning wisdom, and all-accomplishing wisdom, here mapped onto the buddha, vajra, jewel, lotus, and karma families respectively — a standard tantric correlation.

The phrase rendered "essential-nature body" translates the Tibetan term for the svābhāvikakāya, the fourth body added in some tantric and Yogācāra-Madhyamaka schemes alongside the three traditional kāyas; its inclusion alongside the "vajra body" here reflects a five-kāya schema.

The six winds described for the middling faculty practitioner are a technical tantric teaching specific to this cycle; the rendering follows the literal sense of the Tibetan and the obscurations they clear are left as described in the text, without harmonizing them to any external commentary.
