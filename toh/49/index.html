About this text

The Noble Great Vehicle Sūtra called The Display of Amitābha, known in Sanskrit as the Ārya Amitābhavyūha Nāma Mahāyāna Sūtra and in Tibetan as Pakpa Ö Pakmé Kyi Köpa, is a Mahāyāna sūtra devoted to the buddha Amitābha and the splendor of his realm. It opens with the Buddha at Vulture Peak Mountain near Rājagṛha, surrounded by a great assembly of thirty-two thousand monks who are all arhats. It then describes Amitābha's buddhafield, the pure land of Sukhāvatī, and the aspirations through which it came to be, and it is closely related to the larger Sukhāvatīvyūha tradition so influential in East Asian Pure Land Buddhism. It is Toh 49 in the Kangyur, where it appears as the fifth chapter of the Heap of Jewels, or Ratnakūṭa, the great collection of Mahāyāna sūtras.

From the Dharma discourse of the noble Great Heap of Jewels in a hundred thousand chapters, the fifth chapter: The Display of Amitābha. The first fascicle.

In the language of India: ārya amitābha byuha nāma mahāyāna sūtra. In the language of Tibet: The Noble Great Vehicle Sūtra called The Display of Amitābha.

Homage to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas.

Thus did I hear at one time. The Blessed One was staying at Rājagṛha, on Vulture Peak Mountain, together with a great Saṅgha of thirty-two thousand monks. All of them were arhats. Their defilements were exhausted, and they were free of afflictions. They were steadfast in their abiding. Their minds were thoroughly liberated by perfect knowledge, and their fetters to existence were completely exhausted. They had attained their own aim, they were victorious, and they had reached the supreme state of discipline and peace. Their minds were thoroughly liberated, and their wisdom was thoroughly liberated. They were thoroughbreds and great elephants. They possessed the six superknowledges and had gained mastery. They dwelt in meditation on the eight liberations, had attained the powers, and had superknowledge. All of them were great elder śrāvakas, namely:

the venerable Ājñātakauṇḍinya, the venerable Aśvajit, the venerable Vāṣpa, the venerable Mahānāman, the venerable Bhadrajit, the venerable Yaśodeva, the venerable Vimala, the venerable Subāhu, the venerable Pūrṇa, the venerable Gavāṃpati, the venerable Uruvilvākāśyapa, the venerable Nadīkāśyapa, the venerable Gayākāśyapa, the venerable Kumārakāśyapa, the venerable Mahākāśyapa, the venerable Śāriputra, the venerable Mahāmaudgalyāyana, the venerable Mahākauṣṭhila, the venerable Mahākapphiṇa, the venerable Mahācunda, the venerable Aniruddha, the venerable Accomplished One, the venerable Nandika, the venerable Kimpila, the venerable Subhūti, the venerable Revata, the venerable Khadiravanika, the venerable Vakkula, the venerable Svāgata, the venerable Amogharāja, the venerable Pāragāmika, the venerable Panthaka, the venerable Cūḍapanthaka, the venerable Rāhula, and the venerable Nanda.

These and others besides were all great elder śrāvakas endowed with superknowledge, with the exception of one person, namely the venerable Ānanda, who still had further work to do on the path of training. He was also accompanied by many bodhisattva great beings, Maitreya and others.

Then the venerable Ānanda rose from his seat, draped his upper robe over one shoulder, and knelt with his right knee on the ground. Bowing with palms joined toward the Blessed One, he said to the Blessed One:

"Blessed One, your faculties are utterly serene, the color of your face is completely pure, and the hue of your skin is perfectly clear and golden. Just as, for example, a pale yellow autumn cloth that is completely pure and thoroughly cleansed looks golden, in the same way the faculties of the Blessed One are utterly serene, the color of his face is completely pure, and the hue of his skin is perfectly clear and golden.

"Venerable Blessed One, it is just as when gold from the Jambū River, refined a hundredfold, is placed in a crucible by a skilled goldsmith or a goldsmith's apprentice, thoroughly refined, and then laid upon a yellow woolen cloth: it becomes utterly pure, perfectly clear, and golden. In the same way the faculties of the Blessed One are utterly serene, the color of his face is completely pure, and the hue of his skin is perfectly clear and golden.

"Blessed One, never before, not even in times long past, have I seen the Tathāgata's faculties so serene, the color of his face so completely pure, and the hue of his skin so perfectly clear and golden. Blessed One, I therefore think this: today the Tathāgata is keeping in mind the tathāgatas, arhats, perfectly complete buddhas of the past, the future, and the present. Today the Tathāgata dwells in the abiding of a buddha, in the abiding of a victor, in the abiding of omniscience, and in the abiding of a great elephant. That is what I think."

When he had said this, the Blessed One said to the venerable Ānanda, "Excellent, Ānanda, excellent! Did the gods tell you this, or the buddhas, the blessed ones? Or do you know it in this way through your own discerning knowledge?"

When the Blessed One had said this, the venerable Ānanda said to the Blessed One, "Blessed One, the gods did not tell me this, nor did the buddhas, the blessed ones. Rather, Blessed One, through my own discernment I thought: today the Tathāgata is keeping in mind the tathāgatas, arhats, perfectly complete buddhas of the past, the future, and the present, and today the Tathāgata dwells in the abiding of a buddha, in the abiding of a victor, in the abiding of omniscience, and in the abiding of a great elephant."

When he had said this, the Blessed One said to the venerable Ānanda, "Ānanda, your reflection is vast, your discernment is excellent, and your eloquence is virtuous. Ānanda, you have set out for the benefit of many people and the happiness of many people, out of compassion for the world, and for the welfare, benefit, and happiness of the great multitude of beings, of gods and humans. That you have thought to ask the Tathāgata about this is excellent, excellent!

"Ānanda, it is just so. Even when the wisdom and vision of immeasurable, countless tathāgatas, arhats, perfectly complete buddhas are gathered together, the wisdom and vision of the Tathāgata suffers no impairment."

"Why is that? Ānanda, it is because the wisdom and vision of the Tathāgata are unobstructed. Ānanda, if the Tathāgata wished, he could remain for an eon, or a hundred eons, or a thousand eons, or a hundred thousand eons, or even longer than that, on a single meal of alms, and still the faculties of the Tathāgata would not decline. The color of his face would not change. The color of his skin would not be impaired. Why is that? Because, Ānanda, the Tathāgata has gone to the far shore of the bliss of meditative absorption.

"Ānanda, it is extremely rare for perfectly complete buddhas to appear in the world. For example, just as the udumbara flower rarely appears in the world, in the same way, Ānanda, it is extremely rare for tathāgatas, arhats, perfectly complete buddhas to appear in the world: those who seek the welfare of all sentient beings, who seek their benefit, who are loving, and who have entered upon great compassion. Ānanda, you wish to bring the courageous teachers of the whole world to appear in the world, and for the sake of the bodhisattva great beings you have thought to ask the Tathāgata about this matter. That is the power of the Tathāgata. Therefore, Ānanda, listen well and thoroughly, and keep it in mind, and I shall explain it to you."

Ānanda replied, "So be it, Blessed One," and listened as the Blessed One had instructed. The Blessed One then spoke these words:

Ānanda, even earlier than the time before the Tathāgata Siṃhamati, there appeared in the world the Tathāgata, the arhat, the perfectly complete Buddha, endowed with knowledge and conduct, the Sugata, the knower of the world, the unsurpassed guide who tames beings, the teacher of gods and humans, the Buddha, the Blessed One called Lokeśvararāja.

Ānanda, in the teaching of that Tathāgata, the arhat, the perfectly complete Buddha Lokeśvararāja, there was a monk called Dharmākara. He was greatly endowed with mindfulness, endowed with intelligence, endowed with realization, endowed with wisdom, and endowed with diligence, and he was devoted to what is vast.

Ānanda, then the monk Dharmākara rose from his seat, draped his upper robe over one shoulder, and placed his right knee on the ground. He bowed with joined palms toward where the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Lokeśvararāja, was, paid homage to the Blessed One, and then praised him to his face with these verses:

Immeasurable is your light, boundless and matchless your mind;
no other light whatsoever shines beside it.
The lights of the sun, the moon, and hosts of jewels
do not blaze forth in any world.

O essence of beings, your form is without limit;
likewise, Buddha, your speech has boundless melody.
In moral discipline, concentration, wisdom, and diligence,
there is no other in this world like you.

You have found the Dharma, profound and most vast;
the mind of the supreme Buddha is like an ocean.
Yet in teaching you have no conceit;
having cast off what is base and hateful, you carry beings to the farther shore.

Just as the splendor of the supreme Buddha is boundless,
and the majesty of the Lord of Men blazes in all directions,
so I too shall become a buddha, a king of Dharma,
and set beings fully free from aging and death.

Generosity, self-control, moral discipline, patience, diligence,
meditative concentration, samādhi, and likewise supreme wisdom:
these disciplines I have truly taken up.
I shall become a buddha who protects all sentient beings.

Seeking awakening, the supreme peace beyond compare,
I shall worship all those protectors,
many hundreds of thousands of millions of buddhas,
as boundless as the sands of the Ganges.

World-systems as many as the Ganges' grains,
and the boundless fields beyond them:
to all of these I shall send forth light.
Such a power of diligence I shall undertake.

Having adorned a vast field, foremost, sublime, and supreme,
here in this conditioned realm,
the bliss of the matchless realm of nirvāṇa,
I shall purify it so that nothing equals it.

Beings who come there from the ten directions,
once arrived, will swiftly delight in happiness.
May the Buddha, the valid authority, be my witness here:
I wish to generate an unerring power of diligence.

The wise ones of the worlds of the ten directions,
whose knowledge is unobstructed, always know my mind.
Though I should go to the hell of ceaseless torment and dwell there always,
I shall never turn back from the power of my aspiration.

Ānanda, then the monk Dharmākara, having thus praised the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Lokeśvararāja, to his face with these verses, addressed him as follows: "Blessed One, I wish to fully awaken into unsurpassed, perfectly complete awakening. As I again and again generate the mind set on unsurpassed, perfectly complete awakening and make dedication, I request the Blessed One to teach me well the Dharma by which I may swiftly and fully awaken into unsurpassed, perfectly complete awakening and become in the world a tathāgata equal to the unequaled. I also request the Blessed One to explain fully to me the ways by which I shall take up the perfect array of the qualities and ornaments of a buddha field."

Ānanda, when he had made this request, the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Lokeśvararāja, spoke to the monk Dharmākara as follows: "Therefore, monk, you yourself take up the perfect array of the qualities and ornaments of a buddha field."

He replied, "Blessed One, I am not equal to that. I request the Blessed One to explain the perfect arrays of the qualities and ornaments of the buddha fields of other tathāgatas, so that by hearing of them all my own arrays may become complete."

Ānanda, then the Tathāgata, the arhat, the perfectly complete Buddha Lokeśvararāja, knowing the monk's intention, wishing for his aim and wishing for his benefit, compassionate, took up compassion. So that the lineage of the buddhas would not be cut off, he generated great compassion for sentient beings, and for a full ten million years he thoroughly explained the perfect arrays of the qualities and ornaments of the buddha fields of eighty-one hundred thousand koṭis of niyutas of buddhas, together with their aspects, together with their locations, and together with the grounds from which they arose.

Then the venerable Ānanda said to the Blessed One, "Blessed One, how long was the lifespan of that Blessed One Lokeśvararāja?"

When he had asked this, the Blessed One said to the venerable Ānanda:

"Ānanda, the lifespan of that Tathāgata Lokeśvararāja was a full forty hundred thousand koṭis of niyutas of eons.

Then, Ānanda, the monk Dharmākara took up all the perfect arrays of qualities and ornaments of the buddhafields of those eighty-one hundred thousand koṭis of niyutas of buddhas, gathered into a single buddhafield. He bowed his head at the feet of the Blessed One (the Buddha), the Tathāgata Lokeśvararāja, circumambulated him, and departed from the presence of that Blessed One. For five eons he took up a perfect array of the qualities and ornaments of a buddhafield, exceedingly vast and exceedingly excellent, such as had never before arisen in any of the world systems of the ten directions. He also made exceedingly vast aspirations.

Thus, Ānanda, he took up a perfect buddhafield far more vast, far more excellent, and far more immeasurable even than the perfect fields of those eighty-one hundred thousand koṭis of niyutas of buddhas that the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Lokeśvararāja, had described. Then he went to where the Blessed One Lokeśvararāja was, and when he arrived he bowed his head at the feet of that Blessed One and said: 'Blessed One, I have taken up the perfect array of the qualities and ornaments of my buddhafield.'

Ānanda, when he had said this, the Tathāgata Lokeśvararāja spoke to the monk Dharmākara: 'Then speak, monk, for the Tathāgata too will rejoice. This is the time. Gladden the assembly! Bring forth delight! Roar the lion's roar! When they hear it, bodhisattva great beings, now and in the future, will take up such a perfect buddhafield as this, a ground for aspirations, and will bring it to completion.'

Then, Ānanda, the monk Dharmākara said to that Blessed One: 'Therefore, may the Blessed One please listen to my special aspirations, through which, when I have fully awakened to unsurpassed, perfect, complete awakening, that buddhafield will come to possess an inconceivable array of qualities and ornaments.

Blessed One, if in that buddhafield of mine there should be hells, the animal realm, the domain of hungry ghosts, or the realm of the asuras, then for as long as that is so, I shall not fully awaken to unsurpassed, perfect, complete awakening.

Blessed One, unless the beings born in that buddhafield of mine never fall, after dying and passing on from there, into the hells, the animal realm, the domain of hungry ghosts, or the realm of the asuras, until then I shall not fully awaken to unsurpassed, perfect, complete awakening.

Blessed One, unless all the beings born in that buddhafield of mine are of a single color, the color of gold, until then I shall not fully awaken to unsurpassed, perfect, complete awakening.

Blessed One, if in that buddhafield of mine gods and humans should be distinct, rather than being reckoned as "gods and humans" only by name and designation, as a mere expression of conventional usage, then for as long as that is so, I shall not fully awaken to unsurpassed, perfect, complete awakening.

"Blessed One, if, when I have attained awakening, the countless, immeasurable buddhas, the blessed ones, in immeasurable buddhafields do not proclaim my name, do not speak my praise, do not speak my eulogy, and do not speak of my renown, then for as long as that is so, I shall not fully awaken to unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening.

"Blessed One, if, when I have attained awakening, there are beings in other world systems who have generated the mind set on unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening, who hear my name, whose minds become filled with faith, and who keep me in mind, and if, when the time of their death draws near, I do not stand before them, surrounded and preceded by a saṅgha of monks, so that they abide with undistracted minds, then for as long as that is so, I shall not fully awaken to unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening.

Do not prove to be this distinguished and exalted,

The lion among humans, the very heart of beings.

The second section.

Ānanda, the monk Dharmākara, the bodhisattva great being, was endowed with such excellent aspirations. Ānanda, bodhisattvas endowed with excellent aspirations such as these are few. Aspirations of this kind rarely appear in the world. They are few, but not wholly absent.

Ānanda, the monk Dharmākara set forth these special aspirations before the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Lokeśvararāja, in the presence of the world with its gods, māras, and Brahmās, its population of ascetics and brahmins, its gods, humans, and asuras. He then came to abide in practice exactly as it truly is, and he accomplished such purity of a buddha field, such greatness of a buddha field, and such vastness of a buddha field. He practiced the conduct of a bodhisattva for countless hundreds of thousands of koṭis of niyutas of years, innumerable, inconceivable, incomparable, immeasurable, limitless, inexpressible. Throughout that time he never once engaged in conceptual thoughts of desire, malice, or harm. Never once did he give rise to perceptions of desire, malice, or harm. Never once did he give rise to perceptions of forms, sounds, smells, tastes, or tangible objects.

That youth was gentle and pleasant to be with. He was accepting by nature, easy to satisfy, and easy to support. He had few desires and was content, fully secluded, free of aversion, and free of bewilderment. He was upright and never base, without guile and without deceit, of good disposition, gentle, and pleasant in speech. He was ever diligent and never gave up his diligence in seeking out the pure qualities. He accomplished great aspirations for the sake of all sentient beings.

He was reverent toward the Buddha, the Dharma, and the Saṅgha, toward his teachers, his preceptors, and his spiritual friends. He was ever armored for the conduct of a bodhisattva. Upright, gentle, without pretense or flattery, and endowed with good qualities, he led the way in bringing all sentient beings to take up every virtuous quality. He dwelt in the abiding of emptiness, signlessness, wishlessness, non-formation, non-arising, and non-cessation. He was without pride and kept his speech well guarded.

While he was practicing the conduct of a bodhisattva, he gave up every kind of verbal action that would harm himself, others, or both, and he devoted himself to those verbal actions that would benefit himself, others, and both. Being vigilant in this way, whenever he entered villages, towns, market towns, provinces, or royal palaces, he never felt attachment or aversion toward forms, sounds, smells, tastes, tangible objects, or mental phenomena. While practicing the conduct of a bodhisattva, he himself practiced the perfection of generosity, and he also led others to take it up properly. He himself practiced the perfections of moral discipline, patience, diligence, meditative concentration, and wisdom, and he also led others to take them up properly. He accomplished roots of virtue of such a kind that, because he possessed them, wherever he was born, many hundreds of thousands of billions of trillions of treasures would spring forth from the ground.

While practicing the conduct of a bodhisattva, he established in unsurpassed, perfect, and complete awakening countless, immeasurable hundreds of thousands of billions of trillions of sentient beings, so many that it would not be easy to finish recounting them in words. He revered, honored, and served just as many countless, immeasurable blessed buddhas, and made offerings to them of robes, food, bedding, medicines for the sick, and other requisites, together with everything that makes for living in comfort and ease. The sentient beings he brought to maturity, and those he established as merchants, householders, and ministers, and in royal and brahmin families as stately as great sāla trees, could likewise not easily be finished by telling them in words. In the same way, he established beings in lordship over Jambudvīpa, in the status of a universal monarch (cakravartin), of a world guardian, of Śakra (Indra), of Suyāma, of Santuṣita, of Sunirmita, of Vaśavartin, of a king of the gods, and of Mahābrahmā. The countless, immeasurable blessed buddhas whom he revered, honored, served, and worshipped, and then requested to turn the wheel of Dharma, also could not easily be finished by describing them in words.

While he was practicing the conduct of a bodhisattva, for immeasurable, countless, inconceivable, incomparable, unfathomable, limitless, and inexpressible hundreds of thousands of billions of trillions of eons, a most delightful fragrance of sandalwood, far surpassing that of the gods, came from his mouth, and the fragrance of utpala flowers came from every pore of his body. In all the worlds he was handsome, beautiful, and pleasing to behold, possessed of a supreme, excellent, and radiant complexion, his body well adorned with the excellent major and minor marks. All jewel ornaments, all manifested garments and robes, all manifested flowers, incense, fumigants, garlands, ointments, parasols, victory banners, and pennants, and all manifested instrumental music and song came forth from every pore of his body and from his two hands. Such was the virtue that he accomplished. Ānanda, in this way the monk Dharmākara, formerly, while practicing the conduct of a bodhisattva, reached the perfection of mastery over all requisites.

From the tip of every ray there come thirty-six thousand koṭis of buddha bodies, golden in color and bearing the thirty-two marks of a great being. They go to countless, immeasurable world systems in the east and teach the Dharma to sentient beings. In the same way they go to countless, immeasurable, distinct world systems in the south, the west, the north, below, above, and in the intermediate directions, and teach the Dharma to sentient beings.

"Ānanda, in that buddhafield there are no black mountains at all. All its mountains are nothing but mountains of jewels. There is no Sumeru, king of mountains, no Cakravāḍa range, no Mahācakravāḍa, king of mountains, and there are no great oceans. That buddhafield is level everywhere, delightful and even as the palm of the hand, and its ground is adorned with jewels of every kind."

When he had said this, the venerable Ānanda said to the Blessed One, "Blessed One, the gods of the realm of the Four Great Kings dwell on the slopes of Sumeru, and the gods of the Heaven of the Thirty-Three dwell on its summit. Where, then, do they dwell there?"

The Blessed One said, "Ānanda, what do you think? Here, where do the gods above Sumeru, king of mountains, dwell: the Yāma gods, the Tuṣita, the Nirmāṇarati, the Paranirmitavaśavartin, the Brahmakāyika, the Brahmapurohita, and the gods from Mahābrahmā up to Akaniṣṭha?"

Ānanda replied, "Blessed One, the ripening of karma and the formation of karma are inconceivable."

The Blessed One said, "Ānanda, the inconceivable ripening of karma and formation of karma can be fathomed. But the inconceivable virtue, miraculous power, and blessing of the buddhas, the blessed ones, no one can fathom, measure, or conceive. In other words, it is classed as the inconceivable domain of the buddhas. Thus the inconceivable merit of the merit that beings have made and the roots of virtue they have generated, and their wisdom, miraculous power, and prosperity, arise in that buddhafield."

Ānanda said, "Blessed One, I have no doubt, hesitation, or uncertainty about this. I ask the Tathāgata about this matter in order to dispel the doubts, hesitation, and uncertainty of sentient beings in the future."

The Blessed One said, "Excellent, Ānanda, excellent! That is just what you should do. Ānanda, in that world system Sukhāvatī, rivers of many kinds flow. There are great rivers there a yojana wide, and some ten, twenty, thirty, forty, up to fifty yojanas wide, and some as much as a hundred thousand yojanas wide, and twelve yojanas deep. All these rivers flow with water of many most fragrant scents, and they flow along with masses of scattered jewel flowers. They are also filled with many different sweet sounds.

"Ānanda, from these great rivers there comes a delightful sound surpassing that of music played by skilled musicians with heavenly songs of a hundred thousand koṭis of parts. The sound that comes from these great rivers is profound, comprehensible, intelligible, not false, pleasing to the ear, touching the heart, gladdening, sweet, delightful, never tiring to hear, pleasant, and harmonious, a sound that says, 'impermanent,' 'peace,' and 'no-self,' and is pleasant to hear. It resounds in the ear faculties of those beings.

"Ānanda, both banks of these great rivers are lined with jeweled fragrant trees, from which hang branches, leaves, flowers, and clusters of blossoms of many kinds. When those beings there who wish to enjoy untainted heavenly delight and play on the banks of these rivers enter the rivers, the water, if they wish, comes only up to their ankles.

If they wish, the water stays knee-deep; if they wish, waist-deep; if they wish, neck-deep; and divine delights also arise. Those beings there who wish, "May the water be cool," find it cool. Those who wish, "May it be warm," find it warm. Those who wish, "May it be both cool and warm," find that water both cool and warm, and it is pleasant for them.

Those great rivers flow full of water scented with the leaves of the divine tamāla tree, with aloeswood, with kālānusārin, with tagara, with uragasāra sandalwood, and with the finest incense. They are covered with divine flowers: blue lotuses, lotuses, white water lilies, white lotuses, and other flowers of exquisite fragrance. In their rippling waters are geese, swans, cranes, ruddy shelducks, kāraṇḍava ducks, parrots, mynas, cuckoos, kuṇāla birds, kalaviṅka birds, peacocks and others, all with pleasing voices. They are frequented by flocks of birds emanated by the Tathāgata and beautified by swans and kings of swans. Their fords are easy to enter, they are free of mud, and they are strewn with golden sand.

There, whenever those beings wish, "May such thoughts of ours be fulfilled," at that time such thoughts of theirs are fulfilled in accordance with the Dharma.

Ānanda, the pleasing sound that comes from that water makes itself understood to the entire assembly in that buddha field. Those beings seated on the riverbanks who wish, "May this sound not reach our ears," find that it does not reach their divine ear faculty. Whoever wishes to hear a sound of whatever kind hears a pleasing sound of just that kind: namely, the sound of the Buddha, the sound of the Dharma, the sound of the Saṅgha, the sound of the perfections, the sound of the grounds, the sound of the powers, the sound of the fearlessnesses, the sound of the unshared qualities of a buddha, the sound of the superknowledges, the sound of the specific knowledges; the sound of emptiness, of signlessness, of wishlessness, of the unconditioned, of the unborn, of the unarisen, of nonentity, and of noncessation; the sound of peace, of utter peace, and of complete pacification; the sound of great loving-kindness, great compassion, great joy, and great equanimity; and the sounds of acceptance that phenomena are unborn and of attaining the ground of empowerment. Having heard such sounds, one obtains joy and vast, supreme delight. There arise sounds connected with solitude, with freedom from attachment, with peace, with cessation, with the Dharma, and with the roots of virtue that bring awakening to completion.

Ānanda, in the world Sukhāvatī, the Land of Bliss, there is not even the name of non-virtue. There is no sound of obscurations at all. There is no sound of the lower realms, the bad destinies, or the states of downfall at all. There is no sound of suffering at all. Ānanda, since there is not even the sound of a feeling that is neither painful nor pleasant, how could there be suffering or the sound of suffering? Ānanda, this is, in brief, the account by which it is called the world Sukhāvatī, but it is not the full account. Ānanda, were it given at length, even if one were to recount the causes of happiness in the world Sukhāvatī for an eon or for more than an eon, until one was exhausted, one could not reach the end of those causes of happiness.

Ānanda, all the beings who have been born, are being born, and will be born in that world Sukhāvatī possess form, complexion, strength, power, stature, mastery, accumulated merit, and the superknowledges; such kinds of enjoyment of clothing, ornaments, parks, palaces, and multistoried houses; such kinds of enjoyment of forms, sounds, smells, tastes, and tangible objects; and all such kinds of wealth and enjoyment. They are like the gods of the Paranirmitavaśavartin heaven, the gods who control the emanations of others.

Ānanda, the beings of the world Sukhāvatī do not eat coarse food taken in morsels. Whatever kind of food they wish for, they perceive it as already present, and their bodies are satisfied and their forms are satisfied. Nor do they put it into their bodies. Once their bodies are satisfied, whatever kind of incense they wish for, the entire buddha field becomes perfumed with just such kinds of divine incense. There, for anyone who does not wish to smell that scent, not even the slightest perception of fragrance or its perfume arises. In the same way, whatever kinds of incense, garlands, ointments, powders, parasols, banners, flags, and music they wish for, the entire buddha field becomes pervaded by just such things. Whatever garments they wish for, of many hundreds of thousands of different colors, the entire buddha field becomes pervaded by just such precious garments, and they know themselves to be clothed in them.

As for ornaments for the head, ornaments for the ears, and ornaments for the neck, hands, and feet, namely crowns, earrings, bracelets, armlets, necklaces, half-necklaces, ear ornaments, finger rings, golden chains, belts, nets of gold, nets of pearls, and nets of little bells made of every kind of jewel, whatever ornaments they wish for, they see the buddha field filled with just such ornaments, adorned with many hundreds of thousands of jewels and hanging from jewel trees, and they know themselves to be adorned with those ornaments.

Whatever kind of palace they wish for, of whatever color, emblem, shape, height and breadth, adorned with hundreds of thousands of turrets of various jewels, with jeweled thrones spread with various divine cloths and furnished with multicolored cushions, just such palaces appear before them. Within those jeweled palaces that have appeared, each of them dwells surrounded and honored by seven thousand goddesses, and they play, delight, and enjoy themselves. In that world there is no difference between gods and humans, except that by conventional usage they are counted as "gods" and "humans."

Ānanda, it is like this. For example, in the presence of a universal monarch, a lowly person or a eunuch does not shine, does not glow, does not stand out, is not confident, and is not radiant. Likewise, in the presence of the gods of the Paranirmitavaśavartin heaven, even Śakra (Indra), lord of the gods, does not shine, does not glow, and does not stand out in his parks, palaces, clothing, ornaments, power, magical abilities, miracles, and sovereignty, except in the realization of the Dharma and the enjoyment of the Dharma. Ānanda, the people of the world Sukhāvatī should be regarded as being just like the gods of the Paranirmitavaśavartin heaven.

Ānanda, in that world Sukhāvatī, when the morning hour arrives, winds rise from all four directions, stirring everywhere and churning all around. They sway, sway all around, shake, and shake all around the jewel trees, which are bright, beautiful to behold, of distinct colors and of many colors, and perfumed with various exquisitely fragrant divine scents. From the trees, beautiful flowers of many kinds with delightful fragrances fall onto that great jeweled ground, and the whole of that buddhafield becomes strewn with those flowers to the depth of seven times a man's height. For example, when a man skilled in strewing flowers on the ground scatters them with both hands and lays them out evenly, they are very bright and beautiful to behold. In the same way, those flowers, possessed of fragrance and distinct colors, cover that entire buddhafield to the depth of about seven times a man's height. Those kinds of flowers are soft; to give just a comparison, they are pleasant to the touch like kācalindi cloth. When one sets down one's foot, they sink about four finger-widths, and when one lifts one's foot, they rise about four finger-widths. When the morning hour has passed, those flowers too disappear without remainder. After that, the buddhafield becomes clear, delightful, and clean, without any clutter from the earlier flowers. Then once more winds rise from all four directions and scatter fresh flowers that were not there before. As in the morning, so too at midday, in the afternoon, at night, at dusk, at midnight, and at dawn. When those winds, perfumed with various fragrances, rise and touch those beings, they come to possess a bliss like that of a monk who has entered the absorption of cessation.

Ānanda, in that buddhafield, except as the Tathāgata speaks of them, there is no designation at all by the names of fire, sun, moon, planets, constellations, the forms of stars, or darkness and pitch-black darkness. Nor is there any designation of day and night at all. Nor is there any notion at all of keeping a household.

Ānanda, in that world Sukhāvatī, from time to time rain falls from clouds of divine scented water. There falls a rain of divine flowers of every color, of the seven kinds of divine jewels, of divine sandalwood powder, and of divine parasols, victory banners, and flags. Divine canopies are held aloft, and divine jeweled parasols together with yak-tail fans are held in the sky. The melodious sounds of divine music resound, and goddesses dance.

Ānanda, all the beings who have been born and who will be born in that buddhafield are certain of rightness until they reach nirvāṇa. Why is that? Because there the two groups, the undetermined and those certain of wrongness, are neither established nor designated.

Ānanda, in brief, it is for this reason that that world is called Sukhāvatī, and this is not the full account. Ānanda, were it taught in full, even if one were to recount the causes of happiness of the world Sukhāvatī until the eon came to an end, one could not reach the end of those causes of happiness.

Then, at that time, the Blessed One spoke these verses:

Even if all sentient beings had become sugatas,
Skilled in the ultimate meaning, their wisdom utterly pure,
And for an eon, or for longer still,
Were to proclaim the praises of Sukhāvatī,
As they recited their garlands of praise,
Those ten million eons would be exhausted,
Yet the praise of Sukhāvatī would not reach its end,
Nor would their eloquence be exhausted.

Were someone to shatter worlds as many as the finest atoms,
Crushing and grinding them into dust,
And fill with jewels still more worlds than that,
And give them all away in generosity,
That would not come to a fraction of the merit
Of one who, hearing the name of Immeasurable Light, Amitābha,
And the surpassing qualities of Sukhāvatī,
Rejoices and joins his palms.

Therefore, having heard the qualities of that Tathāgata,
Be glad and joyful,
And so as to go to Sukhāvatī, that supreme world,
Give rise to fervent aspiration.

To the merit of one who hears
The very name of the world Sukhāvatī,
All those vast and supreme fields
Do not compare, even in part.

Those who hold the Victor's word and possess wisdom
Will gain still greater merit than that.
Faith is the root for attaining the ultimate;
Therefore, having heard, cast off your doubts.

Ānanda, the qualities and the praises of the world Sukhāvatī are thus immeasurable.

Ānanda, in each of the ten directions, in buddha fields as numerous as the sands of the river Ganges, blessed buddhas as numerous as the sands of the river Ganges proclaim the name of that Blessed One, the Tathāgata Amitābha. They speak his praises. They spread his renown far and wide. They declare his qualities. Why is that? Because all those sentient beings who hear the name of that Blessed One Amitābha, and who, having heard it, give rise from the depths of their hearts to faith and joy for even a single moment of thought, will not turn back from unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening.

Ānanda, those sentient beings who in various ways bring that Tathāgata to mind again and again, who generate many immeasurable roots of virtue, who dedicate their minds to awakening and make the aspiration to be born in that world, will find, when the time of their death draws near, that the Tathāgata, the arhat, the perfectly and completely awakened Buddha Amitābha stands before them, facing them, surrounded by many assemblies of monks. Having seen that Blessed One Amitābha, they will die and pass on with minds filled with clear faith, and will be born in that very world, Sukhāvatī.

Ānanda, any son or daughter of good family who thinks, "How wonderful it would be to see the Tathāgata Amitābha in this very life!" should give rise to the mind set on unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening. With the steady current of superior intention, they should wholly direct their minds toward being born in that buddha field, and they should generate roots of virtue and dedicate them.

Ānanda, those who bring that Tathāgata to mind, who continually generate many immeasurable roots of virtue, and who set their minds firmly on that buddha field, will find, when the time of their death draws near, that an emanation of the Buddha appears before them, just like the Tathāgata, the arhat, the perfectly and completely awakened Buddha Amitābha in color, in shape, in height and breadth, and in his retinue, the Saṅgha of monks. Filled with clear faith at seeing the Tathāgata, through that meditative absorption focused on him and through unforgetting mindfulness, they will die and pass on and be born in that very buddha field.

Ānanda, those sentient beings who recollect that Tathāgata even for a single moment of thought and who wish to be born in that buddha field; who, when the profound Dharma is being taught, find joy in it and are not disheartened, not dismayed and not daunted; and who bring that Tathāgata Amitābha to mind even for a single moment of thought and give rise to longing for him, will see that Tathāgata Amitābha in a dream and will be born in that world, Sukhāvatī. They will also become irreversible from unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening.

Ānanda, it is because those tathāgatas see this compelling benefit that, in countless, immeasurable worlds of the ten directions, they proclaim the name of that Tathāgata Amitābha, speak his praises and spread his eulogies.

Ānanda, from each of the ten directions, too, bodhisattvas as numerous as the sands of the river Ganges go to that buddha field in order to behold that Tathāgata Amitābha, to pay homage to him, to honor and serve him, and to question him, and in order to behold that assembly of bodhisattvas and those special and perfect arrays of the ornaments of qualities of that buddha field.

Then, at that time, the Blessed One, expounding this very point at length, spoke these verses:

To pay homage to the Buddha, the Guide Amitābha,
From the buddha fields of the east, as endless as the Ganges' sands,
Those perfected bodhisattvas came.
Bearing handfuls of many flowers of various colors,
Sweet-scented and delightful,
They scattered them over Amitāyus, worshipped by gods and humans,
The supreme Guide of humankind.

To pay homage to the Buddha, the Guide Amitābha,
Likewise from all the buddha fields of the south, the west
And the north, however many there are,
Those perfected bodhisattvas came.
Bearing handfuls of many incenses of various colors,
Sweet-scented and delightful,
They scattered them over Amitāyus, worshipped by gods and humans,
The supreme Guide of humankind.

Those many bodhisattvas
Made offerings, bowed at the feet of Amitābha,
Circumambulated him, and spoke these words:
"How wondrous and beautiful is this buddha field!"
With minds of joy and delight beyond measure,
They scattered handfuls of flowers,
And before that Guide they spoke these words:
"May our fields too become like this!"
The very moment they scattered those handfuls of flowers,
They became a parasol a hundred leagues across,
Finely adorned and beautiful with a shining handle,
Spread above, covering the Buddha's body entirely.
Those bodhisattvas likewise
Paid him honor and, rejoicing, spoke these words:
"Those beings who have heard the name of the Supreme Human
Have gained a fine gain indeed.
Since we have come to this buddha field,
What we gained before has been well gained.
Behold how this dreamlike field appears,
Which the Teacher accomplished over a thousand eons!
Boundless in light and boundless in splendor,
Boundless in life span, with a boundless Saṅgha,
Surrounded by bodhisattvas and made beautiful:
Behold the Buddha, the supreme heap of merit!"

Then Amitāyus smiled,
And thirty-six billion trillion rays of light
Came forth from the maṇḍala of his face
And pervaded thousands of millions of fields.
All those rays of light returned again
And dissolved into the crown of the Guide's head.
When the rays of light had dissolved,
Gods and humans felt great joy.
Then the Buddha's son of great renown,
Avalokiteśvara, rose and asked:
"For what reason does the Protector of the World smile?
Blessed One, what is its cause and its condition?
Compassionate one who benefits many beings by liberating them,
Expert in the ultimate, please explain this."

Hearing his supreme speech, pleasing and blissful,
beings come to have joyful minds.
Bodhisattvas from many world systems
who come here to Sukhāvatī to see the Buddha
hear those words and feel great joy,
and swiftly they come to behold this field.
Having arrived in this vast field,
they swiftly gain the divine eye and the divine ear,
recollection of former lives, supreme and skillful intelligence,
and the power of miraculous abilities as well.

The Buddha Amitāyus declared:
"In former times I made this aspiration:
If beings should hear my name,
may they always come to my field.
Because that excellent aspiration of mine has been fulfilled,
beings come from many world systems.
Once they have arrived in my field,
they become irreversible within a single birth.
Therefore any bodhisattva here who wishes,
'May my own field become like this,
and by my name, by my voice, and by the sight of me
may I set many beings free,'
should go swiftly, in great haste,
to the world system Sukhāvatī,
and, going into the presence of Amitābha,
make offerings to a thousand koṭis of buddhas."

By the power of miraculous abilities they go to many fields,
make offerings to many koṭis of buddhas,
and having made offerings to the Sugatas,
they return again to Sukhāvatī by the forenoon.

The third fascicle.

Ānanda, the bodhi tree of that Tathāgata, the arhat, the perfectly complete Buddha Amitābha, is sixteen hundred yojanas in height. Its branches, leaves, and petals hang down as far as eight hundred yojanas, and the thickness and girth of its root measure five hundred yojanas. It is always in leaf, always in flower, and always in fruit. It is of various colors, of many hundreds of thousands of colors, with various leaves, various flowers, and various fruits. It is adorned with all manner of ornaments, illuminated by moonlike precious jewels, decorated with the precious jewels worn by Śakra (Indra), filled with precious wish-fulfilling jewels, and exquisitely decorated with the supreme ocean jewels. Its color surpasses that of the gods. Golden threads hang from it, and it bears beautiful necklaces, clusters of jewels, garlands, single-strand necklaces, garlands of red pearls, garlands of blue pearls, and a multitude of girdles hanging from the mouths of lions. It is adorned with jewel threads, with every kind of jewel, and with conch shells. It is canopied with nets of gold, nets of pearls, nets of every kind of jewel, and nets of little bells. It is beautifully ornamented with makaras, auspicious emblems, swastikas, and crescent shapes, adorned with sparkling gems, with gold, and with every jewel ornament, and beautifully adorned in accordance with whatever beings perceive in their wishes.

Ānanda, when that bodhi tree is stirred by the wind, the sound that arises from it is made known throughout immeasurable world systems. Ānanda, know that those beings in whose ears the sound of that bodhi tree resounds will not be afflicted by disease of the ear until they reach awakening. Know that those immeasurable, countless, inconceivable, incomparable, unfathomable, limitless, and inexpressible beings before whose eyes that bodhi tree appears will not be afflicted by disease of the eye until they reach awakening. Ānanda, know that those beings who smell the fragrance of that bodhi tree will never be afflicted by disease of the nose until they reach awakening. Know that those beings who taste the flavor of the fruit of that bodhi tree will never be afflicted by disease of the tongue until they reach awakening. Know that those beings who are touched by the light of that bodhi tree will never be afflicted by disease of the body until they reach awakening. Ānanda, know that those beings who contemplate that bodhi tree with certainty as the nature of reality will, from this day on until they reach awakening, never have a distracted mind. All those beings, as soon as they see that bodhi tree, become irreversible from unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening.

Through the blessing of the former aspiration of that very Tathāgata Amitāyus, through the special service he rendered to the Victors of the past, and because his former aspiration and conduct were thoroughly complete, thoroughly cultivated, not meager, and without deficiency, they obtain the three kinds of patience, namely the patience that follows sound, the patience of conformity, and patience regarding unborn phenomena.

Ānanda, all those bodhisattvas who have been born, are being born, and will be born in that buddha field are bound to but one more birth, and will fully and perfectly awaken to unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening, except where the power of an aspiration decides otherwise. Those bodhisattvas, without exception, roar the great lion's roar, are clad in great armor, and strive to bring all beings to complete nirvāṇa.

Ānanda, the śrāvakas who dwell in that buddha field all have a radiance of one fathom. The bodhisattvas who dwell there all have a radiance of a hundred thousand koṭis of yojanas, except for two bodhisattvas whose light always, without interruption, pervades that world system with unceasing radiance.

Then the venerable Ānanda said to the Blessed One, "Blessed One, what are the names of those two bodhisattva great beings?"

The Blessed One said, "Ānanda, of those two, one is named the bodhisattva great being Avalokiteśvara, and the other is named Mahāsthāmaprāpta. Ānanda, these two passed away from this buddha field, transmigrated, and were born in that buddha field."

Ānanda, the bodhisattvas who are born in that buddhafield all possess the thirty-two marks of a great being. Their bodies are complete. They are skilled in meditative concentration and the superknowledges (abhijñā), and skilled in drawing distinctions through wisdom. Their faculties are sharp. Their faculties are restrained, and they possess the faculty of perfect knowledge. They are undaunted, their faculties are unmoving, and they have attained patience. Every one of them has endless, boundless good qualities.

Ānanda, the bodhisattvas who are born in that buddhafield are never apart from the sight of the buddhas, and every one of them is by nature unable to fall back until they reach awakening. From that point on, none of them will ever fail to remember their former lives; they will remember them. The only exceptions are those who, like me at present, made aspirations in the past to dwell in eons stirred up by the five degenerations, times such as these when buddhas, blessed ones, appear in the world.

Ānanda, the bodhisattvas who are born in that buddhafield all go to other world systems within a single morning and serve many millions of billions of buddhas, blessed ones. They think, "We shall make offerings with flowers, incense, lamps, perfumes, garlands, ointments, powders, garments, parasols, victory banners, pennants, cymbals, songs, and the sounds of music of such kinds as these." Whatever thoughts of this kind they form, and whatever they wish for, the moment they form the thought, all such offering articles appear in their hands through the power of the Buddha. With these, from the flowers through to the sounds of music, they make offerings to those buddhas, the blessed ones, and they also produce and gather many roots of virtue, immeasurable and countless.

And if they wish, "May clusters of flowers of such kinds as these appear in our hands," the moment they form the thought, clusters of divine flowers of various colors and various scents, with many fragrances, appear in their hands. They scatter those clustered flowers of such kinds over those buddhas, the blessed ones; they strew them and shower them down. Of all the clustered flowers they scatter, even the smallest among them becomes a parasol of flowers some ten yojanas in size in the sky above, and the earlier ones do not fall to the ground until the very last has been scattered. Some of the clustered flowers scattered there become parasols twenty yojanas in size in the sky above. Some are thirty or forty yojanas in size. Some even become parasols of flowers in the sky above as large as a hundred thousand yojanas.

Then they give rise to joy and to vast, supreme joy, attain vast gladness of mind, and produce countless and immeasurable roots of virtue. Having served many hundreds of thousands of millions of billions of buddhas, they return within that single morning and dwell in the world system of Sukhāvatī. Their aspirations are accomplished because they are upheld by the blessing of the past aspirations of that Blessed One, the Tathāgata Amitāyus, and because of the hearing of the Dharma they gave in the past, the roots of virtue they produced toward the Victors of the past, and their past aspirations, which were fulfilled and complete, not few, thoroughly analyzed, and thoroughly cultivated.

Ānanda, the bodhisattvas who have been born in that buddhafield all speak discourses on the Dharma that are endowed with omniscience. The beings in that buddhafield do not give rise to any perception of possessiveness at all. As they all walk about and roam through that buddhafield, they give rise neither to delight nor to displeasure. When they go, they go without attachment, never with attachment. That is to say, grasping at things, the thought of a fixed abode, and thoughts of that kind never occur in them at all.

Ānanda, the bodhisattvas who are born in that world realm Sukhāvatī have no perception of others, no perception of their own, and no perception of a self. They have no quarrels, no disputes, no discord, and no mind of inequality. Toward all sentient beings they have a mind of equality, a beneficial mind, a loving mind, a tender mind, a gentle mind, a virtuous mind, a deeply serene mind, a steadfast mind, an unobstructed mind, an undisturbed mind, an unclouded mind, and a mind that practices the conduct of the Perfection of Wisdom.

Their understanding has fully entered into the illusory nature of the mind-stream. They are equal to a great ocean of wisdom, and their intelligence is like Mount Meru. Having gathered many qualities, they delight in rightly advancing through the limbs of awakening and strive earnestly to advance rightly to buddhahood. Their fleshly eye discerns thoroughly. They accomplish the divine eye, they have fully realized the eye of wisdom, they have reached the far shore of the Dharma eye, and they accomplish the buddha eye. They explain and teach, teaching rightly and extensively, and they accomplish unattached wisdom. They strive for the sameness of the three realms. Their minds are tamed and their minds are at peace. They do not apprehend any phenomenon. They are skilled in the definitive explanations of origination and are possessed of the definitive explanations of the Dharma. They are skilled in what is clear and what is unclear, and skilled in what is proper and what is improper. They dwell without regard for worldly talk. Through supramundane discourse they realize the essence, and they are skilled in thoroughly investigating all phenomena. They dwell in the knowledge that all phenomena are by nature at peace. Their sphere of activity is without any object of reference. They possess nothing, grasp at nothing, have no intention, and have no aggregates. Being without grasping, they are utterly liberated. They are without afflictions. They abide without limit in the superknowledges and abide in what has no root. Their minds are without attachment and without dejection. They strive earnestly in the profound teachings and are never discouraged. They are exalted through entering the wisdom of the Buddha, which is so hard to fathom. They have attained the path that leads to one single goal and are free of doubt. They have passed beyond all hesitation. They do not depend on others for their knowledge, and they are without arrogance.

Exalted in wisdom, they are like Mount Meru. With their undisturbed understanding, they are like the ocean. Through the light of wisdom, through their minds that are white, utterly white, pure and virtuous, and through the supreme quality of their light, which shines with the color of refined gold, they far surpass the light of the sun and moon. Because they bear with the virtue and nonvirtue of all sentient beings, they are like the earth. Because they flow forth to wash away the stains of the afflictions, they are like water. Because they burn away all conceited notions about phenomena and burn up the afflictions, they are like the king of fire. Because they are unattached to all the world, they are like the wind. Because they have thoroughly penetrated all phenomena and because nothing whatsoever exists anywhere, they are like space. Because they are unstained by all the world, they are like the lotus. Because they proclaim what is Dharma and what is not, they are like the great thunder that comes in its season. Because they pour down the rain of the water of Dharma, they are like a great rainstorm. Because they outshine great assemblies, they are like a bull. Because their minds are supremely tamed, they are like a great elephant. Because they are thoroughly tamed, they are like a fine thoroughbred horse. Because through their prowess and fearlessness they know no fear, they are like the lion, king of beasts. Because they protect all sentient beings, they are like the banyan, king of trees. Because they are unmoved by all opponents, they are like Meru, king of mountains. Because they cultivate immeasurable loving-kindness, they are like space. Because they go at the forefront in mastery of all virtuous qualities, they are like the Great Brahmā. Because they hoard nothing in their dwellings, they are like birds. Because they vanquish all opponents, they are like the garuḍa, king of birds. Because they so rarely appear, they are like the udumbara flower.

Like elephants, they are well settled in meditative equipoise and so are undistracted. Their faculties are not dull. They are skilled in ascertaining the meaning, and they abound in patience and certainty. Because they wish for the good fortune of others, they are without jealousy. They are fearless in discussions of the Dharma and insatiable in seeking the Dharma. In moral discipline they are like beryl. In learning they are like a mine of jewels. Their voices are sweet with the great sound of the drum of Dharma. They beat the great drum of Dharma, blow the great conch of Dharma, raise the great victory banner of Dharma, hold aloft the great flag of Dharma, and set ablaze the great lamp of Dharma.

Seeing with wisdom, they are free of delusion. They are without aversion, their mental barrenness subdued. They are pure, without foulness, and without greed. They delight in giving out, they have given away what is to be given, and their hands are open in generosity. They delight in sharing both the Dharma and material things, and in giving they are without stinginess. They are unmixed with defilement, without fear in their minds, and without attachment. They are steadfast and heroic. They hold others dear, they are endowed with devotion, and they are endowed with a sense of shame. They have drawn out the thorn, they are without barriers, and they have attained the superknowledges. They are gentle and pleasant to be with. They work for others' benefit, they illuminate the world, and they are guides. They have abandoned the craving for pleasure, clinging, and anger. They are pure, free of sorrow, and stainless, having abandoned the three stains. They delight in the superknowledges. They have the power of their causes and the power of their aspirations. They are not inferior and they are without deceit. They have generated roots of virtue under many hundreds of thousands of millions of billions of buddhas. They have extracted the thorn of pride. They are free of attachment, aversion and delusion. They are pure and devoted to virtue. They are praised by the holy Victors, skilled in the ways of the world, and have fully accomplished trained wisdom. Lauded by the Victor, their minds are filled with joy. They are courageous and able to endure. They are unequaled, without mental barrenness, immeasurable, and free of dust. Their gatherings are vast, and they have become leaders like bulls. They speak the truth. They are endowed with understanding, endowed with mindfulness, endowed with intelligence, and endowed with realization. They wield the weapon of wisdom. They are endowed with merit and endowed with radiance. They are free of mental barrenness, stains and anger. Ever diligent in the Dharma,

All of them have learning in all dharmas as vast as the ocean.

Ānanda, in brief, the bodhisattvas of that buddhafield are all like this. If one were to describe them at length, even if one taught for hundreds of thousands of koṭis of niyutas of eons, living for the lifespan of a tathāgata, one could not reach the end of the qualities of those holy beings, and the Tathāgata's fearless confidence would never run out. Why is that? Ānanda, it is because the qualities of those bodhisattvas and the unsurpassed wisdom and eloquence of the Tathāgata are both inconceivable and beyond compare.

Ānanda, rise, turn to face the west, scatter flowers, join your palms, and bow toward that direction where the Blessed One, the Tathāgata, the Arhat, the perfectly complete Buddha Amitābha, stainless and utterly pure, dwells, lives, sustains himself, and teaches the Dharma. His name, too, is renowned without obstruction throughout the ten directions. In each direction, buddhas, blessed ones, as many as the grains of sand in the river Ganges, whose speech is free of attachment and free of hindrance, speak his praises and extol him, not merely a few times, and they speak of him melodiously.

When he had said this, the venerable Ānanda said to the Blessed One, "Blessed One, I wish to behold that Tathāgata, the Arhat, the perfectly complete Buddha Amitābha, the one of Immeasurable Life, and those bodhisattva great beings who have cultivated roots of virtue under many hundreds of thousands of koṭis of niyutas of buddhas."

The very moment the venerable Ānanda had spoken these words, at that instant, that Tathāgata, the Arhat, the perfectly complete Buddha, sent forth from the palm of his hand a light such that a hundred thousand koṭis of buddhafields became pervaded by its radiance. At that time, in all those hundred thousand koṭis of buddhafields, whatever black mountains, jewel mountains, Sumerus, great Sumerus, Mucilinda mountains, great Mucilinda mountains, Cakravāḍa ranges, great Cakravāḍa ranges, walls, pillars, dense trees, gardens, and palaces there were, and whatever gods and humans there were, all of them were overwhelmed and outshone by the light of that Tathāgata Amitābha.

Just as, for example, after the sun has risen, one person can see another person standing about a fathom away, so too at that time, through the power of the Buddha and because that light was utterly pure, the monks, nuns, laymen, and laywomen, the gods, nāgas, yakṣas, gandharvas, asuras, garuḍas, kiṃnaras, mahoragas, humans, and nonhumans, from this buddhafield beheld the Tathāgata, the Arhat, the perfectly complete Buddha Amitābha, towering above all buddhafields like Sumeru, the king of mountains, outshining every direction, blazing, radiant, and resplendent in his beauty, along with that great assembly of bodhisattvas and that saṅgha of monks.

For example, if this great earth were to become a single expanse of water, there would be no trees, no mountains, no islands, no grasses, shrubs, herbs, or forests, no rivers, and no chasms or precipices would appear; there would be nothing other than the great earth become a single ocean. In the same way, in that buddhafield there is no other sign or feature whatsoever apart from the light a fathom wide that issues from the bodies of the śrāvakas and the light of the bodhisattvas, which extends for hundreds of thousands of koṭis of yojanas. The Blessed One, the Tathāgata, the Arhat, the perfectly complete Buddha Amitābha, outshining that assembly of śrāvakas and that assembly of bodhisattvas, appears clearly in every direction.

At that time, to all the bodhisattvas, śrāvakas, and pratyekabuddhas, and to all those gods and humans in that world Sukhāvatī, this Sahā world became visible, and so did the Tathāgata Śākyamuni, surrounded by a great saṅgha of monks and teaching the Dharma.

Then the Blessed One said to the bodhisattva great being Ajita (Maitreya), "Ajita, do you see this perfect array of the ornaments of qualities of that buddhafield? Do you see, in the sky above, the delightful pleasure groves, the delightful forests, the delightful parks, the rivers, and the delightful ponds filled with jewel flowers, lotuses, kumudas, and white lotuses? Do you see how, from the ground below up to the realm of Akaniṣṭha, the expanse of the sky is strewn with flowers, adorned all over with garlands of flowers, filled with bright garlands of various jewels, and frequented by birds of many kinds emanated by the Tathāgata?"

He replied, "Blessed One, I see them."

The Blessed One said, "Ajita, do you see the other flocks of birds, which fill all buddhafields with their song, and through which those bodhisattvas are never parted from recollection of the Buddha?"

He replied, "Blessed One, I see them."

The Blessed One said, "Ajita, do you see those beings in this buddhafield who, having entered palaces a hundred thousand yojanas in size, travel through the sky without hindrance?"

He replied, "Blessed One, I see them."

The Blessed One said, "Ajita, what do you think? Is there any difference between the gods who wield power over others' emanations (Paranirmitavaśavartin) and the humans born in the world Sukhāvatī?"

He replied, "Blessed One, I do not see even a single difference. The humans of that world Sukhāvatī are equal to them in everything, up to and including great miraculous power."

The Blessed One said, "Ajita, do you see that in that world Sukhāvatī some people dwell within the wombs of great lotuses?"

He replied, "Blessed One, just as, for example, the gods of the Heaven of the Thirty-Three and the Yāma gods enter celestial palaces fifty leagues, a hundred leagues, or five hundred leagues in size, and there play, enjoy themselves and amuse themselves, in the same way, Blessed One, I see some people of that world Sukhāvatī dwelling within the wombs of great lotuses. Blessed One, there are also beings there who are born miraculously, appearing seated cross-legged on lotuses. Blessed One, what is the cause, and what is the condition, that some dwell within the wombs of lotuses while others are born miraculously, seated cross-legged on lotuses?"

The Blessed One said, "Ajita, those bodhisattvas who dwell in other buddha fields and, with minds fallen into doubt, generate roots of virtue in order to be born in the world Sukhāvatī, dwell there within wombs. Those who are without doubt and without hesitation, who generate roots of virtue in order to be born in the world Sukhāvatī, and who trust in, have faith in and are devoted to the unattached wisdom of the buddhas, the blessed ones, are born miraculously, seated cross-legged on lotuses.

"Ajita, those bodhisattva great beings dwelling in other buddha fields who give rise to the intention to see, to pay homage to and to attend upon the Tathāgata, the Arhat, the Perfectly Complete Buddha Amitābha, who give rise to no doubt, who have no hesitation about the unattached wisdom of the Buddha, and who accumulate their own roots of virtue, come to be born miraculously, seated cross-legged. Just as, for example, with other bodhisattvas not long born, in a single instant their bodies take on such a form.

"Ajita, look at the distinction in wisdom and the gradation of wisdom: namely, at the deficient wisdom and the meager wisdom of those who, by attending to perceptions fallen into doubt, are thus deprived for five hundred years of seeing the Buddha, and deprived of seeing bodhisattvas, of hearing the Dharma, of Dharma discourse, of virtuous conduct, and of all excellence of virtue.

"Ajita, suppose, for example, that a king, an anointed kṣatriya, had a chamber adorned with gold and every kind of beryl, hung with silks, garlands and clusters of tassels, with canopies of various colors stretched above, fine cloth spread out, various loose flowers strewn about, perfumed with abundant incense, adorned with mansions, cool pavilions, windows, parapets, porticoes and balconies of every kind of jewel, covered with nets of golden and jeweled bells and little bells, square and symmetrical, with four pillars set up, four doors fitted and four stairways. Suppose that a son of that king, having committed an offense, were put into that chamber and placed within an inner enclosure of Jambū River gold, and that a couch were arranged for him there, its seat spread with a woolen rug, with quilted covers and palalika cloths laid upon it, a covering of cloth from the land of Kaliṅga spread over it, silk spread on top of that, and red cushions placed on both the right and the left, bright and lovely to behold, so that he might sit or lie on it as he pleased; and suppose many different kinds of pure and delicious food and drink were set out for him. Ajita, what do you think of this? Is the enjoyment of that prince abundant?"

He replied, "Blessed One, it is abundant."

The Blessed One said, "Ajita, what do you think of this? Does he relish it? Does he swallow it? Does he find joy in it?"

He replied, "No, Blessed One. He longs to be freed from the chamber into which the king consigned him because of his offense, and he seeks out noblemen, young men of noble family, ministers, the women of the queen's retinue, chief merchants, householders, provincial kings, or anyone at all through whom he might be freed from that chamber. Blessed One, that prince has no joy in that prison, and he will not be freed until the king permits it."

The Blessed One said, "Ajita, in the same way, those bodhisattvas who generate roots of virtue while fallen into doubt, and who hesitate about the wisdom of the buddhas, the wisdom that is unequaled and equal to the unequaled, are born in that world Sukhāvatī through hearing the Buddha's name and through mere serene faith of mind; yet they are not born miraculously, seated cross-legged on lotuses, but rather experience dwelling within the wombs of lotuses. There they dwell, perceiving those wombs as parks and celestial palaces. There is no excrement or urine, no saliva, no mucus, and nothing disagreeable arises; yet for five hundred years they are separated from seeing the Buddha, hearing the Dharma, seeing bodhisattvas, ascertaining the related Dharma discourses, and all practice of virtuous dharmas. They take no delight there and find no satisfaction. When their former fault is exhausted, they emerge from the wombs of the lotuses; yet even when they have emerged, they do not know whether they have emerged above, below, or straight across.

"Ajita, look how, during those five hundred years, attending upon many hundreds of thousands of koṭis of niyutas of buddhas, generating many roots of virtue, countless, measureless and incalculable, and upholding the immeasurable qualities of a buddha, all of this failed to be accomplished because of the fault of doubt. Ajita, look how great a harm doubt becomes for bodhisattvas. Ajita, therefore bodhisattvas should completely abandon doubt and give rise to the mind of awakening, and, in order swiftly to attain the might that produces the power of benefit and happiness for all sentient beings, they should fully dedicate their roots of virtue to being born in the world Sukhāvatī, where the Blessed One, the Tathāgata, the Arhat, the Perfectly Complete Buddha Amitābha dwells."

When the Blessed One had spoken thus, the bodhisattva Ajita said this to the Blessed One:

"Blessed One, how many bodhisattvas who are fully accomplished will be born in the Sukhāvatī world realm from this buddha field, and from the presence of the blessed buddhas of other buddha fields?"

The Blessed One said, "Ajita (Maitreya), seventy-two trillion fully accomplished bodhisattvas will be born in the Sukhāvatī world realm from this buddha field. They have produced roots of virtue under many hundreds of thousands of trillions of buddhas, and so they are fully accomplished as irreversible. What need is there even to mention those whose roots of virtue are lesser than that?

"From the presence of the Tathāgata Duṣprasaha, eighteen trillion bodhisattvas will be born in the Sukhāvatī world realm. In the northeastern direction there dwells the Tathāgata called Ratnākara, and from his presence nine hundred million bodhisattvas will be born in the Sukhāvatī world realm. From the presence of the Tathāgata Jyotiṣprabha, two hundred and twenty million bodhisattvas will be born in the Sukhāvatī world realm. From the presence of the Tathāgata Amitasvara, two hundred and fifty million bodhisattvas will be born in the Sukhāvatī world realm. From the presence of the Tathāgata Lokapradīpa, six hundred million bodhisattvas will be born in the Sukhāvatī world realm. From the presence of the Tathāgata Nāgābhibhū, six hundred and forty million bodhisattvas will be born in the Sukhāvatī world realm. From the presence of the Tathāgata Virajaprabha, two hundred and fifty million bodhisattvas will be born in the Sukhāvatī world realm. From the presence of the Tathāgata Siṃha, eighteen thousand bodhisattvas will be born in the Sukhāvatī world realm. From the presence of the Tathāgata Śrīkūṭa, eight hundred and ten million bodhisattvas will be born in the Sukhāvatī world realm. From the presence of the Tathāgata Narendra, ten trillion bodhisattvas will be born in the Sukhāvatī world realm. From the presence of the Tathāgata Balābhijña, twenty-two thousand bodhisattvas will be born in the Sukhāvatī world realm. From the presence of the Tathāgata Puṣpadhvaja, two hundred and fifty million bodhisattvas will be born in the Sukhāvatī world realm: bodhisattvas who have attained diligence, who have entered fully by a single entry, and who in a single span of eight days have cast off the saṃsāra of ninety-nine hundred thousand trillion eons. From the presence of the Tathāgata Jvalanādhipati, one hundred and twenty million bodhisattvas will be born in the Sukhāvatī world realm. From the presence of the Tathāgata Vaiśāradyaprāpta, six hundred and ninety million bodhisattvas will be born in the Sukhāvatī world realm in order to see the Tathāgata Amitābha, to bow to him, to venerate him, to inquire of him, and to put questions to him.

"Ajita, in this way, even if one were to recite for a full trillion eons the names of those tathāgatas from whose presence those bodhisattvas go to the Sukhāvatī world realm in order to see, bow to, and venerate the Tathāgata Amitābha, one could not reach the end of them.

"Ajita, see how well those beings have gained whatever they have gained who hear the name of the Tathāgata, the Arhat, the Perfectly Complete Buddha Amitābha! Those beings who gain even a single thought of faith in the Tathāgata Amitābha, and whose faith in this Dharma discourse is unshakable, will not incline toward the lesser.

"Therefore, Ajita, you should have faith and you should understand. Before the world with its gods, its māras and its Brahmās, and its population of ascetics and brahmins, you should take up this Dharma discourse, keep it, read it, master it, teach it correctly and extensively to others as well, and delight in meditating on it. Ajita, even for a single day and night one should take up and keep this Dharma discourse, read it and master it, and from the depths of one's heart explain it correctly and extensively to others as well. At the very least one should write it out well in a volume and keep it, and one should also generate toward it the perception that it is the Teacher.

"Whoever wishes swiftly to establish immeasurable beings in irreversibility from unsurpassed, perfectly complete awakening, to see the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Amitāyus, and himself to take hold of the excellent, perfect, and superior array of the ornaments of the qualities of a buddha field, should cross over a great trichiliocosm world realm completely filled with fire in order to hear this Dharma discourse; he should hear this Dharma discourse. And having heard it, with a mind that does not waver, he should rejoice in it from the depths of his heart. He should undertake great and mighty diligence in order to take up this Dharma discourse, to master it, to keep it, to write it down, and to meditate on it. Even for as long as it takes to milk a cow once, he should recite it to others. And even after crossing that great trichiliocosm world realm completely filled with fire, he should not give rise to even a single thought of regret. Why is that? Ajita, it is because many trillions of bodhisattvas, by listening to this vast Dharma discourse of this kind, become irreversible from unsurpassed, perfectly complete awakening.

In times to come, until the true Dharma has utterly perished, there will be sentient beings in whose ears this kind of vast Dharma discourse resounds. It is thoroughly praised by all the buddhas, extolled by all the buddhas, and approved by all the buddhas, and it swiftly brings about the great wisdom of omniscience. Those beings have gained a gain that is excellently gained. They have produced roots of virtue, they have rendered special service to the Victors of the past, and they will be blessed by the blessing of the buddhas. Those who, having heard it, attain joy and vast supreme joy, who take it up, retain it, read it, master it, teach it extensively to others as well, and come to delight in meditating on it, and even those who merely write it down and make offerings to it, will also produce a great deal of merit, merit that is not easy to count.

Ajita, in this way I have done what a Tathāgata does. Now you too must strive, free of doubt. Do not entertain any doubt about the wisdom of the Buddha, which is unattached and unobstructed, lest you end up in a prison of jewels, even one endowed with the supreme of every kind. Ajita, the appearance of a buddha is hard to find. The teaching of the Dharma is hard to find. The perfect freedoms are hard to find. Ajita, I have explained to you the perfection of all roots of virtue; now you must apply yourself and accomplish it. Ajita, so that this Dharma discourse will not be wasted, I make a great entrustment of it. Strive so that the Buddha's Dharma does not decline. Do not distort the command of the Tathāgata, lest for a long time it lead to harm, to what is not beneficial, to unhappiness, to suffering, and to falling into ruin.

Then, at that time, the Blessed One spoke these verses:

Those who have not made merit
Will never hear such teachings as these.
Those heroes who have reached accomplishment
Will hear these words.

Those who have seen the perfect Buddha,
The Light-Maker, protector of the world,
And have heard the Dharma with reverence,
Will attain the supreme joy.

Those attached to views, the inferior, and the faint-hearted
Cannot find faith in the Buddha's Dharma.
Those who have made offerings to the buddhas of the past
Train in the conduct of the protectors of the world.

Just as a blind man in a dark cave
Does not even know the path, so how could he show it?
So too, all the śrāvakas do not understand
The wisdom of the Buddha; what need to speak of other beings?

Only buddhas know the qualities of a buddha;
Not gods, nāgas, asuras, yakṣas, or śrāvakas.
Even the path of the pratyekabuddhas
Is not the full explanation of the Buddha's wisdom.

Even if all sentient beings became sugatas,
Learned in pure wisdom and in the ultimate,
And for an eon or even longer
Proclaimed the qualities of a single buddha,
They would pass completely into nirvāṇa;
And though they proclaimed them for many koṭis of eons,
They would not find the measure of the Buddha's wisdom.
So marvelous is the wisdom of the Victor.

Therefore the wise person, whose nature is understanding,
Who places trust in my words,
Having mastered the heap of the Victor's wisdom,
Declares in words, "The Buddha alone knows."

Only rarely is a human body found,
Only rarely does a buddha appear,
Faith and wisdom are found only after a long time;
Therefore, to attain the goal, generate diligence.

Whoever hears such supreme Dharma as this
Will attain joy in recollecting the Sugata.
Those who generate devotion to the awakening of the buddhas
Were my friends in times past.

When this Dharma discourse was taught, for twelve koṭis of sentient beings the Dharma eye regarding phenomena became pure, free of dust and without stain. Twenty-four koṭis attained the fruit of non-returning. The minds of eight hundred monks were liberated from the defilements without grasping. Twenty-five koṭis of bodhisattvas attained acceptance of the non-arising of phenomena. Forty koṭis of a hundred thousand niyutas of koṭis of gods and humans, who had not generated the mind before, generated the mind set on unsurpassed, perfect, complete awakening, and dedicated their roots of virtue to rebirth in Sukhāvatī, in order to see, pay homage to, and attend upon the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Amitābha. All of them, after being born there, will gradually complete the conduct of a bodhisattva, and in other buddha fields will fully awaken into unsurpassed, perfect, complete awakening as tathāgatas called Mañjusvara. Eighty koṭis of niyutas who had attained acceptance from the Tathāgata Dīpaṃkara and are irreversible from unsurpassed, perfect, complete awakening, whom the Tathāgata Amitābha, while formerly practicing the conduct of a bodhisattva, had brought to maturity for unsurpassed, perfect, complete awakening, will also be born in the world realm of Sukhāvatī and will fulfill their former aspirations and conduct.

At that time this great trichiliocosm world realm shook in six ways, and miracles of every kind appeared everywhere. Mandārava flowers were strewn over the ground knee-deep. The music of gods and humans resounded. Even as far as the abode of Akaniṣṭha the sound of "We rejoice!" resounded.

When the Blessed One had spoken thus, the bodhisattva mahāsattva Ajita, the venerable Ānanda, that entire assembly, and the world with its gods, humans, asuras, and gandharvas rejoiced and praised what the Blessed One had said.

From the Dharma discourse of the noble Great Heap of Jewels in one hundred thousand chapters, this concludes the fifth chapter, called The Array of the Qualities of the Buddha Field of the Tathāgata Amitābha.

It was translated and edited by the Indian preceptors Jinamitra and Dānaśīla and the chief editor and translator, the venerable Yeshé Dé, and was then revised and finalized according to the new language standard.

Translator's notes

The elder rendered "the Accomplished One" translates Tibetan grub po, which I could not identify with confidence with a Sanskrit name in the standard list of this sūtra's audience; it is translated by meaning. The final sentence, in which the wisdom and vision of countless buddhas are gathered together, is terse in the Tibetan. The next piece explains it as showing that the Tathāgata's wisdom and vision are unobstructed. Apart from Dīpaṃkara, the names of the past tathāgatas are given in English translation of the Tibetan rather than reconstructed Sanskrit, since this Tibetan list does not match the extant Sanskrit lists closely enough for confident reconstruction. Several names recur in the list exactly as in the Tibetan, among them Glorious Heap, Sandalwood Fragrance and Voice of Brahmā. The rendering "Gainer of a Host" for sde brnyes is tentative. The line rendered "Though I should go to the hell of ceaseless torment and dwell there always" reads literally "I, being without interval (mtshams med), shall always dwell." I take mtshams med as referring to the Avīci hell, as in the Sanskrit parallel of this vow. It could also simply mean "ceaselessly." The recurring number "bye ba khrag khrig brgya stong" renders the Sanskrit koṭi-niyuta-śatasahasra, given here as "hundred thousand koṭis of niyutas." In the second vow the Tibetan's double negative ("as long as they would not not fall") is rendered by its plain sense: the vow is that beings born there never again fall into the lower realms. "Firmly settled in the true state" renders ཡང་དག་པ་ཉིད་ལ་ངེས་པ (Skt. samyaktvaniyata), the assurance of reaching nirvāṇa. In the eleventh vow, the Tibetan places the list of exceptions in the middle of the vow, before the main clause. The English gives the vow first and then the exceptions, which keeps the long list readable. "Bound to just one more birth" renders སྐྱེ་བ་གཅིག་གིས་ཐོགས་པ (ekajātipratibaddha). Several gem names are rendered by their usual equivalents: spug as agate, "stone essence" (Skt. aśmagarbha) as emerald, men shel as crystal and shel as quartz. The fifth vow here is phrased, as the Tibetan stands, as beings perceiving the diverse colors of the adornments, which differs somewhat from the Sanskrit parallel about the adornments being beyond measure. In this passage ཚད་མ་མཆིས་པ means "limitless, immeasurable," not "valid cognition."
