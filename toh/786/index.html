About this text

The Dhāraṇī That Completely Pacifies All Hatred (Tibetan: Sdang ba thams cad rab tu zhi bar byed pa'i gzungs) is a very short dhāraṇī text, Toh 786 in the Kangyur, where it is classed among the dhāraṇīs. Its colophon-style opening names it as a dhāraṇī of the Noble One, the one who completely pacifies all hatred, and adds that supplicating the Glorious Goddess will cause all people to become loving. After the customary homage to the Three Jewels and to the Tathāgata, the text consists chiefly of a mantra-like recitation invoking Mañjuśrī and asking to be rescued from all suffering. Its concern is the pacification of hatred and the cultivation of loving-kindness, and its Sanskrit title is not given in the lines quoted here, so it is described only by its Tibetan name and its English rendering.

Translation

If one makes supplication to the Glorious Goddess, all people will become loving. Thus concludes the dhāraṇī of the Noble One, the one who completely pacifies all hatred.

Homage to the Three Jewels.

Homage to the Tathāgata, the arhat, the perfectly and completely awakened buddha.

Oṃ kumāra rūpa dhāraṇi viśva sambhava, come, come. Laghu laghu. Bhrūṃ bhrūṃ. Hūṃ hūṃ. Jina jika, Mañjuśrīye. Tāraya māṃ. Sarva duḥkhebhyaḥ.

Translator's notes

The opening line appears to be a stray colophon-like or heading fragment of the manuscript tradition, followed by the title and colophon of the dhāraṇī, which here precede the text itself. I have kept the order as it stands in the Tibetan. The identification of "the Glorious One" (dpal) as a goddess is an interpretation; the term could also refer more generally to glory or auspiciousness, or to Śrī in a deity form, and the line is a ritual benefit statement rather than part of the dhāraṇī.

The mantra is given in its Sanskrit transliteration and should be recited rather than translated. A rough sense of the sections is as follows: "kumāra" means prince or youth, an epithet of Mañjuśrī; "rūpa-dhāraṇi" suggests holding or bearing the form; "viśva-sambhava" suggests the origin of all; "āgaccha" means come; "laghu" means swiftly; "jina" means victor; "Mañjuśrīye" is a dative or vocative form addressed to Mañjuśrī; "tāraya māṃ" means "deliver me"; and "sarva duḥkhebhyaḥ" means "from all sufferings." The syllables jika and bhrūṃ are not securely analyzable and are best treated as seed-syllable-like sounds. The overall sense is a petition to Mañjuśrī, the youthful prince, to come swiftly and deliver the reciter from all suffering.
