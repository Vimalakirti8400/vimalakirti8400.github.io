About this text

Toh 739 in the Kangyur is a canonical Buddhist sūtra known in Sanskrit as the Ārya Śrī Mahādevī Vyākaraṇa and in Tibetan as "The Noble Great Goddess Śrī: A Prophecy." As its title suggests, it belongs to the genre of vyākaraṇa, or prophetic revelation texts, in which the Buddha delivers a formal prophecy concerning a divine or bodhisattva figure — here the Great Goddess Śrī, a deity associated with auspiciousness and good fortune. The text opens with the Buddha residing in the pure land of Sukhāvatī in the company of a great assembly of bodhisattva mahāsattvas, including Avalokiteśvara, Vajrapāṇi, Kṣitigarbha, and others, setting the stage for a solemn declaration concerning the goddess. It belongs to the broader class of dhāraṇī and tantra-adjacent sūtras within the Kangyur, reflecting the tradition's interest in the protective and salvific powers of great feminine divine figures.

Translation

Qualities fathomless and without end. Namo Bhagavate Brahmani. Namaḥ Sarasvatyai Devi — siddhayantu mantra-pādaṃ Brahmānumantra svāhā. Here ends the Praise to the Glorious Goddess of Melodious Sound.

In the language of India: Ārya Śrī Mahādevī Vyākaraṇa. In the language of Tibet: The Noble Great Goddess Śrī: A Prophecy.

Homage to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas.

Thus did I hear. At one time the Blessed One was in Sukhāvatī, together with this great assembly of bodhisattvas — namely, the bodhisattva mahāsattva noble Avalokiteśvara, the bodhisattva mahāsattva Mahāsthāmaprāpta, the bodhisattva mahāsattva Sarvanīvaraṇaviṣkambhin, the bodhisattva mahāsattva Kṣitigarbha, the bodhisattva mahāsattva Samantabhadra, the bodhisattva mahāsattva Ākāśagarbha, the bodhisattva mahāsattva Vajrapāṇi, the bodhisattva mahāsattva Sarvabhayavigatī, and likewise the bodhisattva mahāsattva Śubhamaṅgaladhāriṇī, the bodhisattva mahāsattva Puṇyalakṣaṇadhāriṇī, the bodhisattva mahāsattva Sūryacandra, the bodhisattva mahāsattva Jagatpāla, the bodhisattva mahāsattva Tīrthikamaṅgaladhāriṇī, the bodhisattva mahāsattva Mañjuśrī Kumārabhūta, and all the other bodhisattva mahāsattvas of the Fortunate Aeon — and they were all assembled together.

Then the bodhisattva mahāsattva Avalokiteśvara approached the Blessed One, and having come before him, prostrated with his head at the feet of the Blessed One and sat down to one side.

The Great Goddess Śrī also approached the Blessed One, and having come before him, prostrated with her head at the feet of the Blessed One, circumambulated him one hundred thousand times, prostrated likewise with her head to those bodhisattva mahāsattvas dwelling in Sukhāvatī, and sat down to one side.

Then the Blessed One, adorned with countless hundreds of thousands of merits, fully surrounded by tens of millions of Tathāgatas, was praised and extolled by Śakra (Indra, lord of the gods), by Brahmā, and by all the world-guardians. The Blessed One looked upon the Great Goddess Śrī and, speaking in the voice of Great Brahmā, addressed the bodhisattva mahāsattva Avalokiteśvara in these words:

"Avalokiteśvara, whoever among kings, ministers, monks, nuns, laymen, laywomen, brahmins, the warrior caste (kṣatriyas), the merchant caste, or the common people bears this praise called The One Hundred and Eight Names of the Great Goddess Śrī, Free of Stain, so Renowned — in the realm of that king of the warrior caste, all fears for those sentient beings, all epidemic disease, and all harm shall be pacified; thieves, the deceitful, and all fears from humans and non-humans shall not arise; and all wealth, grain, treasuries, and granaries shall flourish abundantly. In the home of that king of the warrior caste, prosperity shall assuredly dwell."

Then those bodhisattva mahāsattvas spoke these words: "Blessed One, this that you have graciously proclaimed is excellent, excellent! It is excellent indeed. Such are the qualities and benefits that belong to those who hold the names of the Great Goddess Śrī and who, upon hearing them, strive to accomplish them."

Then the bodhisattva mahāsattva, the noble Avalokiteśvara, spoke these words to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, where did the Great Goddess Śrī plant her roots of virtue?"

The Blessed One declared: "The Great Goddess Śrī planted roots of virtue in as many Tathāgatas as there are grains of sand in the river Ganges. In a past age gone by, in a world system called Arisen from Jewels, two Tathāgatas appeared in the world: the Tathāgata named Ocean of Qualities of Jeweled Flowers, Vaiḍūrya and Golden Mountain, and the Tathāgata named Beautiful in Color, Splendor of Golden Light. The Great Goddess Śrī also planted roots of virtue in many other Tathāgatas besides these.

These names of the Tathāgatas, moreover, are what cause the roots of virtue of that Great Goddess Śrī to increase, to be accomplished, and to be constantly attended upon. Here in this present context, these names bring about the authentic recitation of the Great Goddess Śrī; they dispel all evil; they eliminate all misdeeds and transgressions; they purify the entire body from defilement; they gather and increase wealth and grain; they cut off poverty; they summon and attract all gods, nāgas, yakṣas, gandharvas, asuras, garuḍas, kiṃnaras, and great serpents; they pacify all epidemic disease, harm, conflict, discord, disturbance, and strife; and they accomplish the six perfections.

All the Tathāgatas then prophesied to the Great Goddess Śrī, saying: "Great Goddess Śrī, in a future time, in a world-realm called Greatly Magnificent, Adorned with Precious Jewels, you will be the Tathāgata, the Arhat, the perfectly and fully awakened Buddha known as Śrī Arisen from Precious Jewels. That world-realm will be adorned with a wealth of divine precious jewels of every kind. The Tathāgata himself will illuminate that world-realm, and the bodhisattvas dwelling in that world-realm will themselves radiate light and will have immeasurable life. From the sky itself will come the sounds of the buddha, the Dharma, and the Saṅgha. All the bodhisattvas who will be born in that buddha-field will be born from pure lotus hearts." This was the prophecy that was given.

Translator's notes

The Sanskrit dharaṇī phrases at the opening are transliterated mantra material and are rendered as-is per convention; their internal syntax is devotional-liturgical rather than propositional.

Several of the bodhisattva names in the assembly list appear in their Tibetan descriptive forms rather than as established Sanskrit proper names. "Mahāsthāmaprāpta" renders the Tibetan "mthу chen thob pa" (he who has attained great power), which is the standard Tibetan equivalent of that name. The remaining names — Sarvabhayavigatī, Śubhamaṅgaladhāriṇī, Puṇyalakṣaṇadhāriṇī, Jagatpāla, and Tīrthikamaṅgaladhāriṇī — are back-reconstructed from the Tibetan; they do not correspond to universally attested Sanskrit names in the canon, though the reconstructions are straightforward. The phrase rendered "Tīrthika Gates of the Ganges" for གང་གཱའི་མུ་སྟེགས is unusual: མུ་སྟེགས ordinarily means "tīrthika" (non-Buddhist adherent), but in the context of a Tathāgata name it likely refers to the sacred river fords or banks of the Ganges (the literal meaning of Sanskrit tīrtha, from which tīrthika derives), i.e., "auspicious splendor of all the sacred fords of the Ganges." Either reading is defensible; the ford interpretation is contextually more natural for a buddha epithet.

The name སྲེད་མེད་ཀྱི་བུའི་བརྟུལ་ཞུགས་ཀྱི་ཆོ་ག་ལྷུན་པོའི་དཔལ is rendered "Splendor of the Mountain of the Ascetic Rites of Akaniṣṭha." སྲེད་མེད་ཀྱི་བུ is Akaniṣṭha (the highest pure-abode heaven), and ལྷུན་པོ is "mountain / Sumeru." The compound is dense and the exact intended referent of the full name is uncertain. The passage containing the twelve continuous praises introduces a list of epithets that is clearly a litany in a quasi-formulaic register, blending Sanskrit loan-terms with Tibetan renderings. The phrase "བཅང་བར་བྱའོ། བཀླག་པར་བྱའོ།" means literally "should be held and should be read aloud," rendered as "memorized and recited" for natural English flow. The identification of "ཉི་མའི་བུ་མོ" as "Daughter of the Sun" is a recognized epithet of Śrī-Lakṣmī in the Indian tradition; the alternative gloss "born of sunlight" is possible but the genealogical reading is more likely here given the parallel maternal epithets throughout. The phrase "རིག་བྱེད་བཞི" ("the four vidyās, the mothers") may alternatively be read as "the four Vedas," since "rig byed" is a standard Tibetan rendering of Veda; given the Indic devotional context, both readings are defensible. The epithet "མུ་སྟེགས་ཐམས་ཅད་མ" meaning "Mother of all tīrthikas" is doctrinally striking and reflects Śrī's role as the primordial mother of all religious traditions in the Indic world, not an endorsement of non-Buddhist views. The passage is a dhāraṇī-text containing a mixture of Tibetan epithet-lists and Sanskrit mantra syllables transliterated into Tibetan script. The Sanskrit mantric syllables are rendered in their reconstructed Sanskrit forms rather than back-transliterated from Tibetan phonetics, as the Tibetan orthography reflects pronunciation conventions that can obscure the underlying Sanskrit. The phrase rendered "daughter of wisdom's companion" translates ཤེས་ཉེན་ཅན་གྱི་བུ་མོ, which is somewhat opaque; it may refer to one associated with or born of wisdom-intimacy, possibly an epithet connected to Sarasvatī or a similar figure. The term གྲུལ་བུམ is rendered "kumbhāṇḍa," a class of spirits; some editions read this differently. The closing phrase "these and" reflects that the Tibetan passage ends mid-sentence, broken off by the passage boundary.
