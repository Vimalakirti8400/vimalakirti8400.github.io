About this text

Toh 722 in the Kangyur is a devotional prayer text known in English as "Aspirations Arising from the Tantra of the Wish-Fulfilling Jewel of the Noble Avalokiteśvara," corresponding to the Sanskrit name associated with Avalokiteśvara's cintāmaṇi (wish-fulfilling jewel) cycle of teachings, and preserved in Tibetan as the text whose title begins "Phags pa spyan ras gzigs dbang phyug yid bzhin gyi nor bu'i rtog pa las smon lam 'byung ba." It is a supplicatory aspiration text, or prayer, drawn from or connected to a tantra centered on the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara in his wish-fulfilling jewel form, and its contents focus on invoking Avalokiteśvara by name so that the suffering, fears, harms, and adversaries of the practitioner and all sentient beings may be pacified. The text opens with reverential salutations to the buddha Amitābha, the Three Jewels, and Avalokiteśvara himself, reflecting its roots in the Pure Land and compassion-oriented devotional traditions. Situated within the Kangyur, the collection of Buddha's word in Tibetan translation, it belongs broadly to the category of tantric or dhāraṇī-related literature associated with Avalokiteśvara's vast cycle of compassionate practice.

Aspirations Arising from the Tantra of the Wish-Fulfilling Jewel of the Noble Avalokiteśvara

I bow before the Tathāgata, the arhat, the fully and perfectly awakened buddha Amitābha. I bow before the Three Jewels. I bow before the noble Avalokiteśvara. I bow before the bodhisattva, the great being endowed with great compassion, who has attained great power.

O venerable Avalokiteśvara, endowed with great compassion, please take heed. By my recollecting and invoking by name the noble Lord of Compassion, may all unbearable suffering of myself and of all sentient beings be pacified and brought to an end.

I bow before the venerable Avalokiteśvara, endowed with great compassion. By my recollecting and invoking by name the noble Lord of Compassion, may all fears, harms, and adversaries of myself and of all sentient beings be brought to an end.

I bow before the venerable Avalokiteśvara, endowed with great compassion. By my recollecting and invoking by name the noble Lord of Compassion, may all the karma and mental afflictions of myself and of all sentient beings, all unwholesome phenomena, and everything born of the works of māra be purified and released.

I bow before the venerable Avalokiteśvara, endowed with great compassion. By my recollecting and invoking by name the noble Lord of Compassion, may myself and all sentient beings behold the face of the venerable Avalokiteśvara at all times, and may every aspiration we have expressed be swiftly fulfilled.

I bow before the venerable Avalokiteśvara, endowed with great compassion. By my recollecting and invoking by name the noble Avalokiteśvara, Lord of Compassion, may myself and all sentient beings behold the faces of the noble ones directly and may every supreme petition we make be likewise accomplished.

I bow before the venerable Avalokiteśvara, endowed with great compassion. By my recollecting and invoking by name the noble Lord of Compassion, may great power be attained to bring benefit and happiness to all who are destitute and wretched in the worlds.

I bow before the venerable Avalokiteśvara, endowed with great compassion. By my recollecting and invoking by name the noble Lord of Compassion, may the great wilderness of famine and whatever harms of fire, flood, wind, and the like that arise and reduce many beings to distress in the worlds be pacified and cease to be.

I bow before the venerable Avalokiteśvara, endowed with great compassion. By my recollecting and invoking by name the noble Lord of Compassion, even when myself and any sentient beings are beset by the great terror of death and the passage from this world to the next, may they behold your face directly, O noble Lord of Compassion, and may you grant them great relief.

And when myself and other sentient beings pass through death, may we do so undisturbed by afflictions, with mindfulness of the selflessness of phenomena in Dharma, with bodhicitta firm and immeasurable, with mind unshrunken, with courage unobstructed, with all feelings of suffering completely calmed, and with mindfulness steadily established as we make that passage.

In every lifetime may we be born in the presence of the noble Avalokiteśvara, and with memory of our past lives,

may we be endowed with the full excellence of bodhicitta, pleasing the noble Lord of Compassion and all the others, and may we be empowered to accomplish everything just as it has been taught.

Just as the Lord of Compassion effortlessly perfects the gates of liberation through great compassion, and becomes of one taste with the compassion of all buddhas, so too may I and all sentient beings accomplish the same.

Just as the Lord of Compassion fully ripens sentient beings, utterly purifies buddha-fields, and perfectly completes all buddha qualities, so too may I and all sentient beings fully ripen all sentient beings, utterly purify all buddha-fields, and perfectly complete all the qualities of those buddha-fields.

Just as the noble Avalokiteśvara — who is the very embodiment of the compassion of all the victors of the three times and their bodhisattva heirs — is recalled and invoked by name, so too may we invoke by name and pay homage to all the victors of the three times together with their heirs.

Just as by recalling and invoking by name the noble Avalokiteśvara — who is the compassionate nature of all the victors of the three times together with their heirs — and by making offerings to him, an unbroken stream of the practice of great compassion arises toward all sentient beings who are the objects of that great compassion, so may that same unbroken stream arise for us.

For as long as I remain in saṃsāra,
for that many births as I take in a body,
for that long may I worship the Protector
and with this body gather the accumulations.

For as long as I remain in saṃsāra,
for as many births as my voice arises,
for that long may I recite the Protector's name
and gather the accumulations through speech.

For as long as I remain in saṃsāra,
for as many moments as my mind arises,
for that long may I keep the Protector in mind
and gather the accumulations through mentation.

Thus, having made the Lord of Compassion my refuge
through body, speech, and mind in this way,
may body, speech, and mind be free of fault
and may I come to possess the full excellence of qualities.

Thus, having continually engaged
body, speech, and mind with the body, speech, and mind
of the Lord of Compassion,
may I become like Avalokiteśvara, the Lord of Compassion —
the sole object of hope for the three realms of existence,
whose qualities are beyond all imagining —
and may I fulfill the every hope of the three realms.

Homage to the Three Jewels. Homage to the noble Avalokiteśvara, the bodhisattva, the great being, the greatly compassionate one. Thus: Om chakravarti cintāmaṇi mahāpadme ruru tiṣṭha jvala ākarṣaya hūṃ phaṭ svāhā. This completes the root mantra. Om padme cintāmaṇi jvala hūṃ. This is the heart mantra. Om bṭara ta padme hūṃ. This is the near-heart mantra. Om maṇi vajre ṇi hūṃ.

Translator's notes

The phrase rendered "great wilderness of famine" translates mu ge'i dgon pa chen po, literally "great wilderness or desolation of famine." The term dgon pa here carries the meaning of a vast, desolate wilderness or wasteland, not a monastery; this classical usage is attested in descriptions of catastrophic scarcity.

The closing sentence is syntactically incomplete in the passage provided, ending mid-phrase with "and with memory of our past lives." The Tibetan text breaks off here, and the continuation would have completed the aspiration. The root mantra and its associated shorter mantras are rendered as transliterated Sanskrit following standard Tibetan phonetic conventions. The syllable "bṭara" in the near-heart mantra is an unusual cluster that may represent a Tibetan phonetic approximation of a Sanskrit form not easily reconstructed with certainty; it is left as transliterated.

The phrase rendered "for as many moments as my mind arises" translates a line about the arising of mentation across births and moments, paralleling the body and speech verses; the Tibetan here uses "ཡིད་འབྱུང་བ" which could also be read as referring to the arising of mental events, but the parallel structure of the three verses supports a reading consistent with the continuous stream of mindful recollection.
