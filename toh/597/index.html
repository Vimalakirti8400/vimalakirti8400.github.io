About this text

Toh 597 in the Kangyur is a dhāraṇī text known in Sanskrit as the Āryasarvadurgatipariśodhana Uṣṇīṣavijayā and in Tibetan as "The Noble Victorious Crown Protrusion that Thoroughly Purifies All Lower Realms." It belongs to the dhāraṇī section of the Kangyur and centers on the powerful mantra of Uṣṇīṣavijayā, one of the most celebrated dhāraṇīs in the Buddhist tradition, renowned for its capacity to purify negative karma and liberate beings from lower rebirths. The text opens with a narrative frame set among the gods of the Thirty-Three, where a deity named Supratiṣṭhita receives a prophetic warning about his impending death and unfavorable rebirths, prompting the intervention of the Buddha, who reveals this dhāraṇī as a means of purification and protection. The inclusion of a detailed explanation alongside the dhāraṇī itself reflects the text's character as both a ritual and a doctrinal work, combining practical instruction with the soteriological promise of liberation from the lower realms.

Homage to all buddhas and bodhisattvas.

Then there was a certain son of the gods named Supratiṣṭhita who dwelt among the gods of the Thirty-Three in the divine assembly hall of Sudharmā. In a great celestial palace, surrounded by a vast retinue of divine maidens and amid great divine pleasures, he was rejoicing and sporting in delight. While he was thus enjoying those divine pleasures, in the night he heard a voice say: "The son of the gods Supratiṣṭhita will meet his death in seven days' time. Having died, he will be reborn in Jambudvīpa, and there too he will pass through seven destinies. After passing through those seven destinies, he will be born in the hells. And if at some point in a hundred births he is born as a human, even then he will be poor and blind."

When he heard this, the son of the gods was struck with fear and terror and dread, his hair standing on end. In great distress he went to where Śakra (Indra, lord of the gods) was, and arriving there, he prostrated at Śakra's feet. Trembling and weeping and crying out "Alas! Alas!" he said these words to Śakra: "Lord of the gods, please listen. Lord of the gods, today, while I was surrounded by a retinue of divine maidens and experiencing the joy of divine pleasures in bliss and delight, I heard a voice say: 'The son of the gods Supratiṣṭhita will meet his death in seven days. He will be reborn in Jambudvīpa, and there too he will pass through seven destinies and be born in the hells. And if at some point in a hundred births he is born as a human, even then he will be poor and blind.' Having heard this, Lord of the gods, how am I to be saved?"

Then Śakra, upon hearing these words about the son of the gods Supratiṣṭhita, was filled with great wonder. Thinking about what seven destinies this son of the gods would pass through, he sat in silence. The seven destinies were: birth as a pig, a dog, a fox, a monkey, a venomous snake, a vulture, and a crow — these seven destinies, in which one would experience the eating of exceedingly impure things.

Then Śakra, having seen those seven destinies, thought: "Alas! This son of the gods is to experience immense and unbearable suffering. Who, other than a Tathāgata, an arhat, a perfectly and fully awakened buddha, could become his refuge, his support, his ally?"

That very evening Śakra took incense, flowers, various fragrant offerings, garments, various ornaments, and the like, and went to where the Blessed One was. Arriving there, he prostrated with his head at the feet of the Blessed One, circumambulated him seven times, made a great offering, and then sat before the Blessed One. He presented in detail to the Blessed One the matter of the seven destinies of the son of the gods Supratiṣṭhita.

No sooner had Śakra finished presenting those seven destinies than from the crown protrusion of the Blessed One there issued forth rays of light of many colors.

Those rays illuminated all the realms and world-systems of the ten directions, and then, returning, they entered through the mouth of the Blessed One. Thereupon the Blessed One smiled and spoke thus to Śakra, lord of the gods: "Śakra, there is this dhāraṇī called the Uṣṇīṣavijaya — the Victorious Crown Protrusion — which the Tathāgata confers as an empowerment upon the crown of the head. It completely purifies all lower destinies. It destroys all obscurations, all the sufferings of birth in miserable states. It completely purifies all the realms of hell-beings, the birthplaces of animals, and the world of Yama, lord of the dead. It establishes beings on the path to the higher destinies. Therefore, Śakra, the moment one hears this dhāraṇī called the Uṣṇīṣavijaya — which completely purifies all lower destinies — it destroys the many and varied sufferings that by their very nature lead to obscuration. One will attain a stream of existence purified through birth. Throughout all one's lifetimes one will remember those lives. One will pass from buddha-field to buddha-field. One will move from divine world to divine world, traversing all thirty-two divine abodes. Śakra, the very moment this is spoken, one who has accumulated karma that would otherwise cause one's life to be cut short will instead come to have one's life restored. Abiding in the experience of ease, one will dwell having accumulated pure actions of body, speech, and mind. All the Tathāgatas will look upon that person with care. They will always, without interruption, guard, protect, and shelter that person. All the bodhisattvas too will hold that person in mind. By the mere utterance of this, all the hells, the birthplaces of animals, the world of Yama, and all the realms of the hungry ghosts will be dried up, utterly destroyed, scattered, cleared away, and emptied. In this way, for that person, the gates of all buddha-fields, all divine abodes, and all the dwelling places of the bodhisattvas will be thrown open. Wherever that person wishes to go, they will be able to enter."
