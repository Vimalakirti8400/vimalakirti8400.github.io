About this text

Toh 481 in the Kangyur is a tantric scripture known in English as "The King of Tantras Called 'The All-Secret,'" in Sanskrit as the Sarva-rahasya-nāma-tantra-rāja, and in Tibetan as Thamché Sangwa Zhejawa Gyüdkyi Gyalpo. It belongs to the class of Buddhist tantras and is addressed to the Wisdom Bodhisattva, opening with verses on the nature of mind, the five elements as expressions of essential reality, and the non-dual wisdom of the buddhas as the supreme secret underlying all phenomena. The text presents itself as a "king of tantras," suggesting a text of particular authority within the tantric corpus, and its themes of self-knowing awareness, the unborn nature of mind, and the path to buddhahood through meditative cultivation place it within the broader tradition of highest yoga tantra literature preserved in the Kangyur.

Translation

In the language of India: Sarva-rahasya-nāma-tantra-rāja. In the language of Tibet: The King of Tantras Called "The All-Secret."

Homage to the Blessed One, the Wisdom Bodhisattva.

The five elements are themselves the essence of the five.
What is clearly seen in human bodies —
if one thoroughly cultivates one's own mind,
by meditating on that very thing, one will assuredly become a buddha.

The supreme secret of self-knowing
is the unsurpassed wisdom of the buddhas.
It has the character of non-duality within duality,
and I bow always before the buddhas.

Whatever arises from selflessness,
whatever arises from non-dual wisdom —
free of pleasant and unpleasant alike —
to that I make no bow at all.

Genuinely giving birth to the unborn,
then, in the wake of that birth, always
meditating on the buddha body —
I bow before one's own supreme wisdom.

For yogins and all sentient beings alike,
genuinely giving birth to the unborn,
then, in the wake of that birth, always —
afflictions and their habitual tendencies wear them down.
Those with bodies become entangled
in discrimination between the pleasant and the unpleasant.
That very mind is itself suffering,
and it is from that mind that the bodies of sentient beings arise.
It is through the continuum of that very mind
that the suffering of beings is pressed ever down.

Vairocana, the Victor who protects,
the sole gathering of all buddhas,
who has realized all buddhas,
and liberates buddhas from saṃsāra —
for the benefit of all sentient beings, each and every one,
he is genuinely endowed with the quality of compassion.
Through union with the suchness of the cause,
he also reveals the suchnesses of the fruit.
Entering into the suchness of the fruit,
he does not desire the suchness of the cause.
Wherever desire arises,
that is the habitual tendency of saṃsāra.

Through the rites of the wise,
meditation is thoroughly examined.
Whatever has once again become one,
the yoga of the yogin has arisen.
Recollecting and taking the buddhas as focus,
and taking the Dharma as focus in recollection,
through the joining of the two accumulations,
one becomes the supreme two-footed being.

Placing the liṅga at the secret center (bhaga),
meditate fully on the buddha body.
O yogin of buddha-recollection,
meditate on the cloud of buddhas.

Placing the liṅga at the secret center,
meditate fully on the vajra Dharma.
O yogin of Dharma-recollection,
let the rain pour down from the cloud of Dharma.

The liṅga is explained as the characteristic sign,
the four characteristics of the mudrā.
The bhaga is explained as "the destroyer" —
it is the supreme destroyer of afflictions.
For all sentient beings and all their things,
it is the Dharma of one's own mind gathered together.
When one is slain through the practice of the selflessness of phenomena,
one will oneself die.
One's own mind itself is the fully awakened buddha.
That which is to be realized is mind itself.
One's own mind itself is what is to be slain,
and it is mind itself alone that must be slain.

Joined and truly connected with the practice,
there is no arising of existence whatsoever.
At the time of one's buddha-awakening,
meditate on the five practices as "I am the buddha."
Arising from meditative absorption and wisdom —
since it is very buddhahood, in brief —
without buddha yoga,
a yogin will not awaken.

Just as one sees subtle things clearly,
so too with objects of beauty.
The distinction between substantial things and non-substantial things
is what meditation, in every instance, examines.
Meditating on a thing is not the thing itself.
What is to be meditated upon is not the meditation.
Through having meditated on things,
one's own mind is thoroughly purified.

The buddha is not the domain of a buddha.
Nor does it become the domain of sentient beings.
Rather, by realizing one's own mind, one is a buddha —
not by a buddha realizing a buddha.

In this non-dual practice as well,
wisdom is examined through meditative absorption.
Just as the suchness of the cause is seen,
so is the suchness of the fruit.
The suchness of the cause is not at all present.

The suchness of the fruit is just so.
That suchness — suchness itself — is known.
That suchness is the conduct of all yogins.
The cause does not make the cause into a thing.
The fruit does not make the fruit into a thing.
Yet that cause is fit to become the thing that is the fruit.
One's own mind appears distinctly and individually.
That gnosis is the very substance of the cause.
The secret — that is the substance of the fruit.
Through the non-dual practice of the secret,
by means of that very substance, it is supreme.

Having realized suchness upon suchness,
that secret is the yogin of the secret practice.
To know and understand that secret —
the supreme is the yogin of the supreme.

What is described as having the essential nature of the beautiful,
the ugly too is its very substance.
Whatever form is beautiful or ugly,
not seeing it — that is the Tathāgata.

Without mere consciousness being annihilated,
yoga does not arise from yoga.
Just as gnosis is supremely pure,
so too, in just that way, purity is the Tathāgata.

The four aspects of consciousness,
through the ritual of the four mudrās,
and by means of the non-dual engagement of the secret,
body, speech, and mind arose as they truly are.
The mind is the great mudrā (mahāmudrā).
The samaya mudrā pervades all.
The Dharma mudrā is that of speech.
The action mudrā belongs to the body.

The substance sealed by the seal —
the lord of secrets is the yogin of the secret practice.
Through the essential nature of self-knowing awareness,
meditative absorption and gnosis are examined.
The activity analyzed as body, speech, and mind,
and the action analyzed as tantra —
the secret is explained as three aspects,
in the manner of the diverse vajra-actions.

The identity of the five buddhas
is the supreme secret gnosis.
Through those four mudrās of the secret,
the yogin accomplishes the activities.
The four great elements
are likewise called the four mudrās.
The union practice of the four mudrās
is joined perfectly with the four goddesses.

Earth is called Locanā.
Water is called Māmakī.
What are known as fire and wind —
those are Pāṇḍaravāsinī and Tārā.
The great lord is the element of earth.
The element of water is the water maṇḍala.
Fire is the element of fire.
Wind is the maṇḍala of wind.

The great lord's color is yellow,
and is square in shape, expanded.
The water maṇḍala is round,
white in color, and belongs to pacification.
The fire maṇḍala is red in color,
triangular, and belongs to activities of subjugation.
The wind maṇḍala is bow-shaped,
and belongs to fierce, wrathful activities.

Accomplished through the great elements themselves,
the four qualities of karmic attainment,
and the supreme karmic attainment — the highest attainment —
and the four kinds of wheel-turning sovereigns:

For pacification, there is the aspect of Locanā.
For enrichment, one bearing the lotus-vajra.
For subjugation, Vairocana is present.
For wrathful action, there is the fierce vajra.

Pacification is to be performed in the evening watch.
Likewise, enrichment in the morning.
Fierce activities at midday.
Activities of subjugation are to be performed at midnight.

For pacification, white is to be applied.
For enrichment, the appearance is yellow.
For activities of subjugation, red.
For the fierce, black is thoroughly applied.

That which arises from meditative absorption and gnosis —
the accomplishment of action is supreme.
The pure Blessed Ones are to be meditated upon.
One becomes unmoved from all the buddhas.

In the midst of the bhaga, the lord of secrets —
the vajra-space is the Tathāgata.
The identity that has become the lord of the five —
when meditated upon, one becomes without movement.

The great goddess dwelling in the heart
generates the yoga of the yogin.
The mother who generates all buddhas
is described as the sovereign of vajra-space.

Attachment, aversion, and delusion —
these are the three poisons of the world.
The buddhas, the Blessed Ones, are without poison.
Vajra-space is the Tathāgata.

By meditating upon attachment, aversion, and bewilderment,
one becomes the lord of the three realms —
a buddha, a Blessed One —
and through the truth of the buddhas, the poisons are overcome.

By individually realizing one's own mind,
the mind of delusion is the Tathāgata.

Delusion itself becomes the world —
one must look upon all sentient beings.
The mind of aversion is the Tathāgata,
and generates the wisdom of subtlety perfectly.
Aversion, transformed, becomes vajra itself;
all sentient beings are to be liberated.

The mind of attachment is the Tathāgata,
and generates the awakening mind perfectly.
Attachment, transformed, becomes goodness itself;
all buddhas are to be embraced with love.

The mind of loving-kindness is the Tathāgata.
Having perfectly generated pride,
one becomes beloved and becomes all —
embrace all buddhas.

Through the view of attachment, loving-kindness;
speaking the Dharma of compassion;
granting fearlessness to all;
and pleasing all buddhas —

from generosity in yoga, supreme joy arises;
from moral discipline, one becomes stainless;
from patience, one becomes a source of light;
from diligence, one comes to possess radiance;
from meditative concentration, what is hard to purify is refined;
through wisdom, one sees the manifest;
through great skillful means, one goes far;
from possessing power, one becomes immovable;
through aspirational prayer, one comes to possess excellent intelligence;
from possessing primordial wisdom, a cloud of Dharma.

The Victor, the Tathāgata,
becomes eleven through self-knowing selfhood:
the ten perfections (pāramitās),
powers and mastery perfected,
having gone beyond the ten grounds (bhūmis),
and marks that have become stable — from that,
vajra-water is the fully awakened buddha.

Extracting the nectar of Dharma —
the boundless expanse of all buddhas —
is abandoning apprehended and apprehender.
Extract it with the essence of Dharma's nectar.
By purely drinking the vajra-water,
the vajra master, the yogin,
becomes primordially pure at that very moment.
Having come to abide as primordially pure,
one then becomes the awakening mind,
comes to possess the awakening mind as one's own,
and becomes a bodhisattva Tathāgata,
clothed in the garment of complete liberation.
The awakening mind is unsurpassed.

The yogin who has perfectly generated it —
Vajrasattva dwells in the heart.
Holding the commitment of the limitless realm of sentient beings,
if one practices with the vajra commitment,
one will always abide in the commitment.

All that is called "woman" along the path of the three realms,
delightful and experiential —
engage with all of them in accordance with the ritual,
and do not disparage the vajra of mind.
Abide in the bodies of the three vajras.
All commitments and mudrās that exist —
perform them with the three vajra commitments.
Do not disparage the vajra of speech.

All the mudrās arising from Vajrasattva
are good women.
The yogin who relies upon them
comes to master all mudrās.

Throughout all world-realms,
however many are called "woman" —
they are the practice of the Great Seal (mahāmudrā).
Engage closely with all of them.

The yogin of the supreme secret
possesses sacred samādhi and primordial wisdom.

If one desires the supreme spiritual attainment (siddhi),
no pride, no hūṃ,
no vidyā, no mantra-knowledge,
and one does not place hand mudrās.
Do not perform the activities of a stūpa.
Do not read books either.
Do not perform maṇḍalas.
Do not seek the salutation of the three supreme vajras.
Do not disparage the master —
the guru is equal to all buddhas.

You are to slay living beings.
You are to speak words of falsehood.
You are to take what is not given.
You are to rely upon another's woman.

Not fire offering, nor meditative concentration —
not dividing body, speech, and mind,
but acting with a mind unlike any other:
this is the supreme secret.
All mantras are fully accomplished,
and all yoga in every aspect —
therefore, with all effort,
one must meditate upon the Dharma of the buddhas.

The eighteen unmixed qualities
are described as the Dharma of the buddhas.
Always meditating upon them —

the Dharma of the buddhas is the means of accomplishment.
The four meditative concentrations and the four formless absorptions,
attained without interruption,
together with the absorption of cessation —
by these one fully accomplishes the awakening of a buddha.

The thirty-seven factors of awakening (bodhipakṣa),
whatever has been rightly taught as Dharma —
by meditating upon them one is purified,
and the bodhisattva benefits all beings.

The mind that delights in beings,
firm and ardently cultivating the essence,
arising from the suchness of emptiness —
this is called vajra wisdom.

Arising rightly from the selflessness of phenomena,
it is the supreme non-dual wisdom.
The wisdom that is the highest nature of reality
is rightly called the dharmadhātu, the basic space of phenomena.

This is the wisdom of the dharmadhātu,
born from vajra-like meditative absorption.
The mind whose nature is vajra wisdom —
that is declared to be the vajra realm.

Through the union born of the mind's delight,
the union of the subtler wisdom and so forth is engaged.
The wisdom of all the Tathāgatas
is declared to be Vairocana.

Through the union born of loving-kindness' delight,
the supreme one who engages in the practice of union —
the mind that has grown firm, without defilement —
is declared to be Akṣobhya (the Imperturbable).

Through the manner of compassion's delight,
the one who bestows benefit upon sentient beings,
whose mind is truly endowed with the welfare of beings —
is declared to be Ratnasambhava (the Jewel-Born).

Through the union born of joy's delight,
the supreme Great Vehicle, stainless —
the mind that is luminosity, pure —
is declared to be Amitābha.

By the force of equanimity's delight,
the one who causes all sentient beings to awaken,
supremely meaningful and unsurpassed —
is declared to be Amoghasiddhi (the One of Accomplished Meaning).

The supreme wisdom that is the nature of awakening —
this is here declared to be vajra.
Arising from vajra wisdom —
that is declared to be Vajrasattva.

The one who summons all buddhas —
is declared to be Vajrāṅkuśa (the Vajra Hook).
The sovereign of all members of the vajra family —
is declared to be Vajrarāja (the Vajra King).

That by which all buddhas are made to be attracted,
the one who gives pleasure —
the great attachment of Māra's union —
is declared to be Vajrarāga (Vajra Passion).

That which pleases all the buddhas,
beginning with the exclamation "Excellent!" and so forth,
the one who brings joy to the bodhisattvas —
is declared to be Vajrasādhu (Vajra Excellence).

Whatever generosity of the bodhisattva
arises from the vajra equal to space —
the great meaningful jewel —
is declared to be Vajraratna (the Vajra Jewel).

The wisdom free of ignorance,
arising definitely from pure accumulations,
the great splendor that is the illumination of liberation —
is declared to be Vajrasūrya (the Vajra Sun).

The wisdom that is real in all aspects,
which grants entry and attainment on the path of liberation,
the great victory-banner crest of Dharma —
is declared to be Vajraketu (the Vajra Banner).

Vajrahāsa (Vajra Laughter), the great yoga —
the wondrous one from whom all buddhas arise —
this is the great bodhisattva
declared to be Vajrahāsa.

The great yoga of all the buddhas,
the one who realizes the Dharma of the buddhas,
the pure Dharma of the Tathāgatas —
is declared to be Vajradharma (Vajra Dharma).

The Great Vehicle with its sharp wisdom,
the one who decisively cuts through all afflictions,
the one who severs the cognitive obscurations —
is declared to be Vajratīkṣṇa (the Sharp Vajra).

The musical wheel of the buddhas,
vigorously turning the wheel of Dharma —
the great being of the Great Vehicle —
is declared to be Vajrahetu (the Vajra Cause).

Whose nature is wisdom and gnosis, utterly pure,
who has relinquished all sounds of voice,
who has become the embodiment of gentle speech —
is declared to be Vajrabhāṣa (the Vajra Speaker).

The foot of all perfections,
the one who realizes all phenomena,
the emanator for the benefit of all sentient beings —
is declared to be Vajrakarma (Vajra Action).

The one in whom recollection of the buddhas is perfectly established,
who has fully abandoned the Lesser Vehicle,
who has entered fully into the awakening mind —
is declared to be Vajrarakṣa (the Vajra Protector).

The one who exhausts all evil,
who likewise burns away all afflictions,
who appropriates the exhaustion of afflictions —

is declared to be Vajrayakṣa (the Vajra Yakṣa).

The vajras of body, speech, and mind,
having become perfectly unified as a single binding,
and that very suchness, that very suchness as gnosis —
is declared to be Vajramuṣṭi (the Vajra Fist).

All pledges (samaya) and mudrās
arise perfectly from the vajra-fist binding.
The two vajra fists joined together —
is declared to be the karma mudrā (action seal).

Without pride and without appearance,
without marks and without clinging,
the gnosis of selflessness and equality —
is declared to be Vajrasattva (the Vajra Being).

In this union of the secret,
the virtue of the Perfection of Wisdom
arose perfectly in twelve aspects —
the light of the vajra perfections.

Through the thorough cultivation of the awakening mind,
the gnosis that attains the perfection reaching the other shore of buddhahood —
is declared to be Vajracitta (the Vajra Mind).

The four bases of gathering sentient beings,
the vajra of the Perfection of Wisdom,
and the four dharmas unified as one —
is declared to be Vajraratna (the Vajra Jewel).

The secret gnosis, the great gnosis,
that very virtue of the perfection,
arising from the great perfection —
is to be known as Vajradharma (the Vajra Dharma).

The virtue of the perfections,
excellently taught as the means of engagement —
is to be known as Vajrakarma (Vajra Action),
for it is the pleasant sport of its nature.

The coquette is the perfection of generosity.
The virtue of the perfection of moral discipline
is the pleasing limb that adorns.
The songstress is the perfection of patience,
the one who proclaims the Dharma through set speech.
The dancer is the perfection of diligence,
the one who swiftly bestows buddhahood.
The incense is the perfection of wisdom,
since it spreads to pervade everything.
The flower is the perfection of meditative concentration,
unstained by the mire of desire.
The lamp is the perfection of aspiration,
illuminating all directions.
The fragrance is the perfection of skillful means,
the virtue that dispels foul odors.

The great skillful means of all buddhas
abides in four distinct divisions.
The great skillful means fulfilling all wishes —
is declared to be Vajrāṅkuśa (the Vajra Hook).
The one who brings about the conduct of awakening —
is declared to be Vajrapāśa (the Vajra Lasso).
Whatever brings stability to this vehicle —
is declared to be Vajrasphota (the Vajra Chain).
Since all phenomena are unborn
and primordially at peace, equal to space,
the one who perfectly enters into such speech —
is declared to be Vajraghaṇṭā (the Vajra Bell).

Through the engagement of the two accumulations,
the mind that reveals the benefit of all sentient beings,
luminous by its very nature —
is declared to be the lunar maṇḍala.

The realization of the true vajra
is itself the hook, being the engagement of grasping.
The one that pierces subtly is called the arrow.
Delight is called Excellent!
What is adorned is declared to be the jewel.
Splendor is called the sun.
What is raised up is declared to be the pinnacle.
What differentiates is declared to be laughter.
The purification of attachment is the lotus.
The vanquishing of afflictions is the sword.
The engagement of the maṇḍala is the wheel.
Whatever proclaims through its declaration is declared to speak.
The totality of variety is the vajra.
The engagement that is hard to destroy is the armor.
The fearsome conduct is the fang.
The engagement of the mudrā is the binding.

What is taught within the maṇḍala —
the mind itself is the supreme maṇḍala.
The palace is gnosis,
the raising up is the stacking of the mind.
Whatever forms the outer square,
having become equal through measure —
whatever mind there is of loving-kindness and so forth
is declared to be the four lines.

The recollection of Dharma engagement:
that is declared to be the vajra line.
What is perfectly freed from all views
is declared to be the gnosis line.
The excellence of the assembly of moral discipline
is declared to be the ornament.
Whatever mind there is of the faculty of faith and so forth
arises perfectly as the five enclosures.
The four liberations
are perfectly taught as the gateways.
The four right abandonments
arise as the pillars.
The four foundations of mindfulness
are to be known as the four cloisters.
The four bases of miraculous power
are the four threshold lintels.
The seven limbs of awakening —

adorned with garlands of flower clusters —
the eight limbs of the noble path
are taught to be the eight pillars.

Freed from the two obscurations
and examined by meditative absorption and gnosis,
the distinctive excellences of the wisdom of the path
are perfectly taught as the fan.

Cutting through all afflictions,
by the seizure of afflictions — the net:
the very act of perfectly cutting and abandoning,
abiding in the abandonment that clears away,
is the union of the two accumulations.
The half-seizure of afflictions
is taught to be the half-net.

That which is luminosity by intrinsic nature
is the unsurpassed awakening mind (bodhicitta).
Mind, which produces every meaning and aim,
is taught to be the lunar maṇḍala.

The purified intrinsic nature of wisdom and gnosis,
arisen as vajra-like meditative absorption —
the brilliant radiance of the light of liberation —
is taught to be the solar maṇḍala.

Perfectly taught as the five pigments,
they are the flowers of the five views.
Their union with the five wisdoms
is perfectly taught as the five buddhas.

The consciousness that is selfless as space
is the supreme gnosis.
The ornament of that gnosis, as it is taught,
is said to be the mind of attachment.

From detachment arises awakening;
it is other than the mind of attachment.
Therefore, both attachment and detachment
are perfectly taught as the awakening mind (bodhicitta).

The world is tamed through detachment,
yet the attainments of the mudrā arise from attachment.
The meditations on skillful means therein
bestow all attainments.

However the world is to be tamed,
in just that way the yogin should practice.
However the beings to be tamed have become,
every kind of clinging shall be worshipped.

Whatever is perfectly taught as the defining characteristic
will be indicated by that characteristic.
The gnosis sealed with the mudrā of body, speech, and mind
is taught to be gnosis sealed by those.

By meditating on the mudrā, the yogin
perpetually becomes a possessor of the mudrā.
Understanding the entity that has been sealed,
if one meditates, one becomes sealed by the mudrā.

Knowing the connection between the marks and the defining characteristics,
the yogin engages in authentic practice.
Just as the defining characteristic is perfectly established,
so too purity is swiftly accomplished.

By the marks of the right side,
the one endowed with wisdom is perfectly indicated.
By the marks of the left side, as they are seen,
compassion is indicated thereby.

From the union of both hands together,
the two meditative absorptions are taught.
By the marks of the two feet,
the two accumulations are taught.

From the union of the five fingers,
it is adorned by the five maṇḍalas.
The manifest awakening of the five buddhas
is to be taught in sequential order.

Whoever displays the middle finger
indicates what has arisen from space.
Whoever displays the index finger —
that is the maṇḍala of the great lord.
Whoever displays the ring finger
is said to be the water maṇḍala.
Whoever displays the thumb —
that indicates fire.
Whoever displays the little finger —
that becomes the wind maṇḍala.

Whoever displays the navel
is taught to be the lord of the vajra-space.
Whoever displays the heart
is to be understood as Vajrasattva.
Whoever displays the brow-centre
is called Vajraratna.
Whoever displays the throat
is to be known as Vajradharma.
Whoever displays the crown of the head
is to be known as Vajrakarma.

Whether awakened to manifest awakening in the five awakenings,
or endowed with the five wisdoms,
or perfectly possessing the five buddhas —
the maṇḍala of awakening is thereby taught.

What was taught as the sound in front
is perfectly indicated there as the first.
What was taught as the sound behind
is perfectly indicated thereafter as the latter.

Whatever arises as the first sound
is taught there as the sound of cause.
Whatever arises as the last sound
is here perfectly taught as result.

There are not two blessed buddhas.
There is no having both cause and result.
Because there is no first and no last,
therefore it is taught as permanent.

By realizing one's own mind, one becomes a buddha,
and that which is to be realized is mind itself.
What is spoken of as "a buddha-field"
should be understood as bodhicitta.

Sentient beings whose nature is pure
are perfectly explained as universally aware.
The pure nature of sentient beings
should be understood as bodhicitta.

Whatever arose first, strike it to the heart.
One becomes stable in that very itself.
Grasping the hair at the crown of the head,
in Amitābha one rests in meditative equipoise.
