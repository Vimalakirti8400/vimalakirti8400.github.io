About this text

The Means of Accomplishment Called Glorious Ekavīra, in Sanskrit Śrī Ekavīra Sādhana and in Tibetan Pal Pawo Chikpa zhe jawé Drubpé Tap, is a short tantric meditation manual, or sādhana, on Hevajra visualized as the "sole hero" without a surrounding retinue, joined with his consort Nairātmyā. Composed, according to its colophon, by the Indian master Vajraghaṇṭa and translated into Tibetan by the Indian preceptor Gayādhara and the monk Shākya Yeshé, it condenses the practice for busy or less learned practitioners, leading from preliminary offerings, confession, refuge and the four immeasurables, through meditation on emptiness and the generation of the protective circle, celestial palace and the eight-faced, sixteen-armed deity, to the empowerment by the tathāgatas and the inner yoga of caṇḍālī. It closes with verses explaining the symbolism of the deity's faces, arms and limbs and the purity of the aggregates, elements and senses as goddesses and deities. It is Toh 1226 in the Tengyur, belonging to the section of tantra commentaries and ritual manuals connected with the Hevajra cycle.

Translation

In the language of India: śrī ekapīrasādhanaṃ nāma. In the language of Tibet: The Means of Accomplishment Called Glorious Ekavīra.

Homage to Hevajra.

To Heruka and to Nairātmyā, the very nature of compassion and wisdom inseparable,
and to the lotus feet of the vajra master,
I pay homage, and I write the Ekavīra.
For those who are busy, aged, and of feeble understanding,
it is thoroughly condensed, so that they may gain accomplishment.

First of all, the yogin, having trained his mind well under an authentic vajra master by the stages taught in the Hevajra, should dwell in bliss together with a wisdom consort (prajñā) who is delightful and well trained in the teaching as it has been given, and with the enjoyments of desire.

Then come the outer and inner ablutions, the offerings, and the assembled tormas. At one's own heart, upon a sun, is a white hūṃ with five rays of light, and by the radiance of its light the guru and the buddhas become vividly clear in the sky. In the hands of eight goddesses are the five nectars, the essence of flavors, the hand drum, and embraces, and with these one makes offerings to them. Then, in their presence, one should confess one's sins, vow to restrain from doing them again, rejoice, dedicate, go for refuge to the Three Jewels, generate the mind of awakening, and meditate on the four abodes of Brahmā.

Then, with the light rays from the heart, one burns up the whole world and all the beings within it, and reciting oṃ śūnyatājñānavajrasvabhāva ātmako'haṃ, one meditates without any object of reference.

Then, by the power of one's former aspiration, in the sky, at the center of a lotus and a sun disc, a hūṃ transforms and becomes a crossed vajra. From it light radiates, and from its gathering back one meditates in full on the vajra fence, the tent, the ground and the rest. In its center one meditates on the wisdom source, shaped like the letter e. There, from yaṃ, raṃ, vaṃ and laṃ, arise the maṇḍalas of wind, fire, water and earth, crescent, triangular, round and square, and from their becoming one, one meditates, in the midst of the eight charnel grounds, on a square celestial palace with four porticoes and eight pillars, adorned with half-lattices, suns and moons, banners of many kinds of silk, and the five qualities of desire such as flowers.

In its center, upon a red lotus and in the middle of a sun, are Brahmā, Indra, Viṣṇu and Rudra. At the heart, from the transformation of the vowels and the consonants, arise a moon and a sun, and in their center, from aṃ and hūṃ, a curved knife and a skull cup, marked with aṃ and hūṃ. One imagines them as having the character of emanating and gathering back.

From all of these becoming one, oneself is glorious Hevajra, joined in bliss with Nairātmyā: eight-faced, sixteen-armed and four-legged, his body the color of the moon and of crystal. His root face is like his body, his right face blue and his left red, and the others are black as bees and bare their fangs. In the eight skull cups of his right hands he holds, in order, an elephant, a horse, a donkey, an ox, a camel, a human, a lion and a cat. Likewise in the eight of his left hands he holds earth, water, wind, fire, the moon, the sun, Yama and a yakṣa. He has a crown of skulls, a necklace, the five mudrā ornaments, a tiger-skin loincloth, ashes and a brahmin's thread. His yellow hair blazes upward, marked with a crossed vajra. He is rich with twenty-four eyes, and in the half-crossed posture he sports upon a māra. Nairātmyā is black, with one face and two arms, her yellow hair blazing upward. She is adorned with a wheel, earrings, a necklace, bracelets and a girdle, and wears a tiger-skin skirt. With her hands holding a curved knife and a skull cup she embraces the principal deity about the neck. One should abide by the instruction on nonduality.

Then, from the seed syllable at the heart, light rays in the form of hooks invite the wisdom being. One makes offerings and praises, and dissolves it in so that the two are not two.

oṃ hūṃ traṃ khaṃ hūṃ. In the eyes, the ears, the nose, the tongue, the body and the mind one should meditate on Mohavajra and the others, white, blue, yellow, red, green and black, holding a curved knife, a skull cup and a khaṭvāṅga.

Then, with the hooks of light rays from the heart, one invites the tathāgatas and makes offerings, and one makes the request abhiṣiñcatu māṃ sarva tathāgata. They then take the form of Herukas and confer empowerment with vases of nectar, and one imagines that the crown of the head is marked by the lord of the family.

Then, by meditating on the yoga of caṇḍālī according to the guru's instruction, one attains accomplishment through the sequence of the signs that are examined and so forth.

Delighting in the generation stage,
having extracted and recited the root mantra and the other secret mantras,
give torma to the great bhūtas,
and perform the rites of the maṇḍala and the rest.
Sing songs with Gaurī and enjoy the pleasures of desire;
practise no austerities, but accomplish through bliss;
always partake of the five nectars.

The eight faces are the eight liberations;
the sixteen arms are emptiness;
the four legs are the four means of attraction;
the three eyes are the three bodies;
the eyes of compassion are red;
the black limbs are the mind of loving-kindness;
the purified curving forms are the five buddhas;
wrathful, he is the tamer of the vicious.
To put it briefly, the five aggregates
are the five goddesses, Vajrā and the rest;
the four elements, purified, are four goddesses;
the eyes and the rest are Mohavajra and the rest;
the objects and the rest, purified, are six goddesses.
In reality they have no intrinsic nature.
Having rendered them free of poison through purity,
one should rely on these things that are to be relied upon.
Whatever conduct one engages in,
if one constantly keeps reality in mind,
one will attain accomplishment; of this there is no doubt.

From the ocean of Hevajra, the heart of the wisdom tantras,
I have made clear this means of accomplishing Ekavīra, which condenses its very essence;
by whatever virtue there is in this,
may beings without exception attain accomplishment.

The Means of Accomplishment Called Glorious Ekavīra, composed by the master Vajraghaṇṭa, is complete. It was translated and finalized by the Indian preceptor Gayādhara and the monk Shākya Yeshé.

Translator's notes

The wisdom (ཤེས་རབ) "well trained in the teaching" with whom the yogin dwells is taken in its tantric sense of a consort (prajñā), which the context of the enjoyments of desire supports. The opening lines on "those who are busy, aged, and of feeble understanding" could also be read as the author's self-description. The line གུག་སྐྱེད་རྣམ་དག་སངས་རྒྱས་ལྔ is obscure; "the purified curving forms" renders it literally, and it may refer to some feature of the deity's form or posture, possibly the crown of five skulls. "Sing songs with Gaurī" is a literal rendering; Gaurī may name the goddess or a consort. The deity's body color is given here as moon and crystal, as the text says.
