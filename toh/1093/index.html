About this text

The Noble Great Sūtra on Entering the City of Vaiśālī, known in Sanskrit as the Āryavaiśālīpraveśamahāsūtra and in Tibetan as Pakpa Yangpé Drongkhyer du Jukpé Do Chenpo, is a short discourse set during the Buddha's travels through the land of the Vṛjis. After staying at a boatmen's lodging among the reeds, the Buddha comes to Āmrapālī's grove outside Vaiśālī and sends the venerable Ānanda into the city, instructing him to stand at the threshold of its gate and speak words of truth and blessing for the welfare of its inhabitants. The text belongs to the tradition of protective scriptures, whose recitation is meant to ward off harm and bring peace and safety to a community. It is catalogued as Toh 1093 in the Kangyur, where it is placed among the collections of dhāraṇī and protective texts.

Translator's notes

"The great deity, god of the gods" is here an epithet of the Buddha himself, so it is not glossed as Śiva. The command "Enter into the body as well!" renders the Tibetan literally. Its exact sense is unclear; it may be an invitation to the benevolent spirits to join in protecting the people. In the seventh stanza, the phrase rendered "Siddhārtha" can be read as a plain description ("who accomplished his aim"). It is taken here as the Buddha's birth name, which the Tibetan form standardly renders, in keeping with the stanzas' sequence through his life story. In the third stanza, "those who run astray" renders a word that literally means "those who run"; the exact sense is uncertain. Here "the great god, god even of the gods" is an epithet of the Buddha himself, not a reference to Śiva, so no Śiva gloss is given. In the seventh verse, "accomplished every aim" plays on the Buddha's name Siddhārtha, "he whose aim is accomplished."
