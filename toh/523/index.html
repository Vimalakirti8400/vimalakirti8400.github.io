About this text

Toh 523 in the Kangyur is a dhāraṇī text known in Sanskrit as the Āryamahāmahāindra-nāma-dhāraṇī and in Tibetan as "The Noble Great Lord of the Earth" (འཕགས་པ་སའི་དབང་པོ་ཆེན་པོ་ཞེས་བྱ་བའི་གཟུངས). It belongs to the dhāraṇī class of canonical literature, presenting a sacred incantation framed within a discourse involving the buddha Sarvaprabha and a brahmin figure called Great Lord of the Earth. The text is concerned with the purification of all beings, as reflected in the closing colophon of the preceding work, which describes the dhāraṇī's power to completely purify all sentient beings. It sits within the Kangyur's collection of dhāraṇī scriptures, a broad grouping of texts that preserve mantric and ritual formulas regarded as the speech of enlightened beings.

The dhāraṇī called Paye Shéyé Tala La, which completely purifies all beings, is complete.

In the language of India: Āryamahāmahāndranāma-dhāraṇī. In the Tibetan language: The dhāraṇī called The Noble Great Lord of the Earth.

Homage to all buddhas and bodhisattvas.

Thus did I hear. At one time, the Blessed One, the perfectly and completely awakened buddha Sarvaprabha, was dwelling in a place graced with gods, in the company of the brahmin Great Lord of the Earth who was like a great sāla tree. Then the brahmin Great Lord of the Earth, who was like a great sāla tree, went to where the Blessed One, the perfectly and completely awakened buddha Sarvaprabha, was, and having arrived, he prostrated with his head at the feet of the Blessed One, the perfectly and completely awakened buddha Sarvaprabha, and took his seat to one side. Seated to one side, the brahmin Great Lord of the Earth, who was like a great sāla tree, addressed the Blessed One, the perfectly and completely awakened buddha Sarvaprabha, with these words:

Blessed One, I have made an aspiration toward unsurpassed, perfect, complete awakening. By what means shall I be able to recall my past lives? Please, Blessed One, explain to me clearly the method by which I will not forget the mind of unsurpassed, perfect, complete awakening.

When this had been said, the Blessed One, the perfectly and completely awakened buddha Sarvaprabha, spoke to the brahmin Great Lord of the Earth who was like a great sāla tree in these words:

Therefore, son, if you have made such an aspiration, take up this heart-essence of the vidyā mantra of Sarvaprabha. Memorize it. Retain it. Make it fully complete within you. What then is the heart-essence of the vidyā mantra of Sarvaprabha? It is this:

Translator's notes

The text opens with a colophon closing a preceding dhāraṇī text ("Paye Shéyé Tala La … is complete"), and then immediately opens the new text, the Āryamahāmahāndra-nāmadhāraṇī. The Sanskrit title reconstructs as Āryamahāmahendranāmadhāraṇī, meaning "The Noble Dhāraṇī called the Great Great Indra of the Earth," which the Tibetan renders as "The Noble Great Lord of the Earth." The buddha name "Sarvaprabha" (Tibetan: Kun tu 'od, "Universal Radiance / Omniluminous") is rendered in translation rather than transliterated, as it is a descriptive epithet-name not a standard canonical proper name requiring retention. The mantra syllables are transcribed as closely as the Tibetan phonetic rendering allows; some Sanskrit forms are uncertain due to the limitations of Tibetan phonetic transcription of Sanskrit, and the mantra text breaks off mid-sentence in the source passage as given. The Tibetan term གྲུལ་བུམ (rendered here as kumbhāṇḍa) appears twice, once in masculine and once in feminine form, and the same is true for ལུས་སྲུལ་པོ (skeleton or revenant). The preceding passage already established the pattern of paired male-female supernatural beings, and these follow that same pattern. The Tibetan name རྒྱལ་བ་འོད་སྲུང is rendered as "Victor, Kāśyapagotra" — འོད་སྲུང (Kāśyapa) here appears to designate a lineage or gotra affiliation, not a personal name, so "Kāśyapagotra" renders the sense accurately.
