About this text

Advice on the Uncommon Meaning for Yogins and Yoginīs, known in Tibetan as Rnal 'byor pha dang rnal 'byor ma rnams la thun mong ma yin pa'i don la gdams pa, and given in its Sanskrit title line in a garbled form that points to a "Yoga-yoginīnām asādhāraṇa-upadeśa," is a short tantric instruction attributed to the Indian master Ḍombi Heruka and compiled by the alms-going yogin Prajñāśrījñāna. It opens with a brief story set in a charnel ground, where a brahmin is expelled from a feast gathering for leaving without the master's leave, and then gives Ḍombi Heruka's advice to the assembled yogins on the proper conduct of the feast gathering, or gaṇacakra: where it may and may not be held, how participants are to enter, sit, eat and behave, which kinds of talk and attitude to avoid, and why its practices must be kept secret. It is Toh 1230 in the Tengyur, where it is placed among the tantra commentaries and practical instructions of the Heruka cycle.

Translation

In the language of India: yoga yogīnināṃ āsadarna alopedeśa nāma. In the language of Tibet: Advice on the Uncommon Meaning for Yogins and Yoginīs. Homage to glorious Heruka.

At the charnel ground called Aṭṭahāsa, with the king called Samuñjaya acting as patron, the master Ḍombi Heruka and a countless host of yogins gathered and turned the feast gathering. A brahmin called Mubakhada rose from his seat without asking the master and went outside. The yogins, recognizing that he lacked conscientiousness, performed the act of expulsion and did not let him into the assembly hall.

Then the master Ḍombi Heruka advised the yogins: "O you who have entered this uncommon path of the Great Vehicle, do not act contrary to the commitments and the disciplines. For as it is said in the Yoginīsaṃcāra Tantra:

Having obtained empowerment, do not let the commitments decline.
Turn the feast gathering in harmony, in the proper places.
In all deeds, one has the nature of Heruka;
Those who act improperly are cut down.

How wonderful! That yogins, even though the age of strife has come, have the enjoyment of these feast gatherings, which are the supreme means for attaining the accomplishment of mahāmudrā in a single lifetime, comes about through the power of uncommon merit. Therefore, O vajra holders, rejoice!

These sacred circles, moreover, should not be turned in ordinary places: marketplaces should be avoided. Towns full of distraction, the homes of brahmins, royal palaces, the homes of the merchant class, the monastic parks of the Saṅgha, and heedless barbarians should be avoided. The towns of non-Buddhists (tīrthikas), remote nomad camps, and places where savage beasts of prey roam should also be avoided. In places where ḍākinīs roam, at the edge of deserted towns, in hermitages, temples, charnel grounds, empty caves, at the foot of trees, and in forest clearings, one should build a feast house with three tiers and hold the feast gathering, or the heroes' banquet.

Those who do not have the signs should not gather there. Do not give the signs without the master's command. Do not open the door without making a request. Do not go inside without performing the purification and the torma. Do not converse before the offering water has been presented. Do not sit on the ground without offering flowers to the master. Do not sit in the row without being directed by the male and female attendants of the rite. Do not settle in equipoise in meditative absorption without making a request. Do not worship the yogins without placing the tilaka. Do not consecrate the food without sprinkling it with nectar.

Enjoy it without fear, doubt, or shame. Do not crave the food. Do not become attached to the male and female yogins. Do not remain in the garb of a naked ascetic. Do not wear the charnel ground ornaments without consecrating them. If one eats with conceptual thought and fastidiousness, one is born in the Blister Hell. Therefore even monks and others who are given to conceptual thought should not indulge in conceptual thought here. Sit cross-legged. Sit in the rows in order of seniority. Do not enter into union before the master has entered into union. Serve the food with both hands and receive it with both hands. Do not sing, dance, or behave in an unrestrained way without asking the master. Do not set food down in a place that has no base beneath it, such as a tree and the like. Those of small understanding, such as householders, should not show disrespect or disparage it either. The tantras say that if they disparage it, they will be born among the beings of the Avīci hell.

How wonderful! Yogins, do not look for the faults of others. At all times avoid exposing others' hidden faults, quarreling, and pride. Do not undertake any activity without asking the guru. Do not be frightened by the disciplined conduct of the yogins; if one is frightened, one will be born in the Vajra Hell. Heroines without a hero and heroes without a heroine should not turn the feast gathering. Nor should one hold the heroes' banquets without a maṇḍala.

Oh, yogins, do not eat out of greed for food, out of attachment, for the sake of the body, or to gain strength; if one eats in that way, one will be born as a hungry ghost with knotted entrails. Oh, yogins, do not abide in attachment, aversion, delusion, pride, and jealousy. Do not talk of conjurers, musicians, kings, war, trade, medicine, crafts, meaningless treatises, the trainings of the śrāvakas, low castes, prostitutes, and the like. Do not sit without restraining the body, in postures such as with the legs stretched out. In short, do not remain without restraining body, speech, and mind. As for beginners, even those who are free of conceptual thought should be kept close by those of excellent conduct. Do not lose faith in any form of conduct. Eat plenty of food. Do not find fault with the food. Do not pick at the food. Drink whatever drink is found. Do everything the guru commands. Do not leave while the body is not yet satisfied with food. Do not leave while the torma has not yet been cast. Do not lie down without asking leave, without making the dedication, and without making aspirations.

Oh, yogins, understand this as having great meaning. Be joyful, enthusiastic, and high-spirited. Do not speak of these ways elsewhere; if you do, the ḍākinīs will split your head and destroy your accomplishments as well. Therefore, do not fall away from these." So he spoke this instruction.

The instruction on the uncommon meaning spoken by the master Ḍombi Heruka is complete. It was compiled by the alms-going yogin Prajñāśrījñāna.

Translator's notes

"Aṭṭahāsa" renders dur khrod ha ha sgrogs pa, "the charnel ground resounding with ha ha," taken as the well-known charnel ground of that name. The brahmin's name, Mubakhada, is given as the Tibetan spells it; it could not be identified. "Before the offering water has been presented" reads mchod yon ma drangs par for the printed ma grangs par ("not counted"), which makes no sense here. The two lines about leaving read literally "do not leave because the body has not been satisfied by food" and "because the torma has not been cast"; they are taken as forbidding departure before these are complete. The sentence on beginners is terse and its exact sense is uncertain.
