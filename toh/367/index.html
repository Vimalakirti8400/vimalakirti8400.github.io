About this text

Toh 367 in the Kangyur is a tantric text known in Sanskrit as the Sarvakalpasamuccaya-nāma-sarvabuddhasamayoga-ḍākinījāla-saṃvara-uttarottara-tantra, rendered in Tibetan as the Uttara-Uttara Tantra of the Illusory Bliss-Supreme Ḍākinī-Net Union with All Buddhas, Called the Compendium of All Conceptuality. It belongs to the class of highest yoga tantras and concerns the union of all buddhas through the ḍākinī-net, centering on Vajrasattva as the embodiment of great bliss and the essence of all awakened beings. The text opens with homage to Vajrasattva and proceeds in verse, celebrating the non-conceptual mind in which suchness abides, the supreme secret of great attachment and great bliss, and the all-encompassing nature of tantric realization. As the closing colophon of the preceding chapter makes clear, this scripture is organized around the metaphor of the great wheel-turning of all conceptuality, and it sits within the tantra section of the Kangyur as a scripture of the ḍākinī-net or Saṃvara cycle.

The fierce vajra that liberates,
and Vajrapāṇi — what are they like?
How is the primordial family to be generated?
What are the forms of liberation through desire?
How are the three kinds of protector sought?
What is the nature of everything as suchness?
How does the heart-essence first arise?
What is the proximate heart-essence like?
Great bliss, the great vajra —
what is the nature of the meaningful samaya (sacred pledge)?
How is the maṇḍala taught?
What is its karmic ripening like?
With the measurement of lines, and likewise through conceptuality,
how are the spiritual attainments described?
The five secrets and the great suchness —
what are they, and what are they like?
The chief lords of the maṇḍalas,
and the families — what are they like?
The suchness of universal empowerment,
and the emblems of the suchness assembly,
the universal summoning and the great offering,
and the accomplishment of one's own blessing and the rest —
all of these: the great secret of suchness,
the holy and the ultimate,
for the sake of the accomplished meaning of the holy syllable —
is it not unsuitable to speak all of this?

In what way is this a tantra of the meaning of conceptuality? From non-conceptual pure phenomena, one fully conceptualizes for the sake of sentient beings. Therefore it is correctly called conceptuality, because conceptuality is thoroughly pure.

In what way is this a subsequent tantra? A tantra is explained as something thoroughly connected; the subsequent is that which was drawn forth earlier. Those very great secrets and secrets, too, are held to be the subsequent tantra.

In what way is this the wisdom of primordial gnosis? Completely abiding throughout all of space, the supreme essence of all vajras, that great bliss and great joy — the palace of the holy syllable. Thus declared the Blessed One, the glorious Vajrasattva.

This is what is known as the buddha-image of all buddhas.

From that, all beings, moving and unmoving, arise completely and perfectly. Since it arises from space, because it is a defining mark, it is supreme — without beginning and without end.

Translator's notes

The title as given in Tibetan transliteration of Sanskrit presents the text as an "uttara-uttara" tantra, meaning the "subsequent subsequent" or "post-postscript" tantra — a claim of being an explanatory continuation beyond the root tantra. This is a standard genre designation in the Sarma tantric canon.

The closing section describing syllable sequences refers to the Sanskrit alphabet arranged in the traditional Indian enumeration: ka through dha as the first series of consonants, followed by a second series through ṣa, with the vowel a placed in the center within a moon disc. This is a standard tantric description of maṇḍala-syllable visualization and is not in doubt, though the specific ritual application would require a commentary to fully specify.

The request-verse section is a formulaic list of questions posed to the teacher prior to his response — a standard dramatic device in tantra literature marking chapter divisions and indicating the topics to be covered.
