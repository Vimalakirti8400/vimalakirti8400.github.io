About this text

Toh 532 in the Kangyur is a dhāraṇī text known in Sanskrit as the Buddha Bhagavān Aṣṭaśatanāma Dhāraṇī and in Tibetan as Sangs rgyas bcom ldan 'das kyi mtshan brgya rtsa brgyad pa gzungs sngags dang bcas pa, rendered in English as "The Dhāraṇī Together with Mantras of the One Hundred and Eight Names of the Buddha, the Blessed One." As its title indicates, the text belongs to the dhāraṇī genre and is devoted to enumerating one hundred and eight names and epithets of the Buddha, each reflecting an aspect of his enlightened qualities — his compassion, his physical majesty, his freedom from wrongdoing, and his perfection of wisdom — embedded within a framework of dhāraṇī and mantra recitation. Such name-dhāraṇī texts were widely used in Indian and Tibetan Buddhist practice as a means of generating merit, invoking the Buddha's blessings, and internalizing his qualities through liturgical recitation. It is classified among the dhāraṇī and mantra collections of the Kangyur, the Tibetan canon of translated Buddhist scriptures.

In the language of India: Buddha Bhagavān Aṣṭaśatanāma Dhāraṇī. In the language of Tibet: The Dhāraṇī Together with Mantras of the One Hundred and Eight Names of the Buddha, the Blessed One.

Homage to the Omniscient One.

Homage to the Blessed One, whose body is anointed with the fragrant sandalwood incense of compassion, whose lotus feet are touched by the jeweled crowns of gods and asuras (demigods), who is the self-arisen dispeller of darkness, the teacher who brings benefit to all the world — the Teacher, the Tathāgata, the Sage, free from all wrongdoing, whose very nature is peace, beautiful of form, resplendent with the brilliance of his clearly manifest major marks, adorned with the minor and major marks, possessed of all qualities in perfect completeness.

His face blooms like an opening lotus. His teeth are white, even, and closely set, abundantly full, luminous and beautiful as a flawless jewel, radiant and clear. Bright and gleaming, he blazes at the center of a vast radiance of innumerable rays, like a thousand-rayed sun blazing with light, its maṇḍala overwhelming all with its splendor. Homage to the supreme Sage, endowed with the ten powers.

I bow down. I prostrate fully. I offer praise.

Oṃ bho hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ. Ā ā. Hī hī. Śiva śiva. Śānti śānti. Śama śama. Śamāpaya. Tathāgatānugata. Svabhāva. Abhāva. Samuttāya. Asamabala. Abīralabala. Amitābala. Atibala. Hūṃ ghuṃ ghuṃ. Dhro dhro. Bhrau bhrau. Bhredu bhredu. Hī hī. Bhagavān. Bhavānta kasurata bho ga mahāśāntikara. Pāpa kilpiṣa nāśaka svāhā.

Then, resplendent with the minor and major marks,
adorned in every limb,
brilliant as the sun,
possessing the ornament of the blazing maṇḍala,
renowned, utterly free from stain,
with a mind of Dharma, unstained and without fault,
the all-pervading great sovereign endowed with the ten powers,
glorious and free from dust —

upon the great jeweled ground,
at the summit palace, the vajra pinnacle,
where the sons of the Victor abide,
with many jewels radiantly gleaming,
and pure rays of light arrayed in clarity,
within the vajra maṇḍala palace,
upon the great field of the Sage's enjoyment,
he abides upon a vast and spacious lion throne.

Upon that very ground of jewel and substance,
upon that same vast and bright great throne,
the Tathāgata, with great joy,
is adorned and arrayed in perfect splendor —

the Sage, radiant with the light of fire, moon, and jewel,
brilliant with light that surpasses the rays of the sun,
in the manner of supreme ease and auspiciousness,
seated in the posture of luminous equipoise,
possessing a maṇḍala of blazing bodily light.

Through the expansion of clear light, the sovereign Sage
spreads forth immaculate rays of light —
this is called the Immovable One Possessed of the Vajra.

Having entered into the great maṇḍala,
those possessing the vajra, supremely disciplined,
having thoroughly purified all of that,
circumambulate the immaculate maṇḍala.

Having perfectly and completely purified
all the stains and impairments of the lower realms,
across the vast maṇḍala of the three existences,
shining clearly in many forms,
encompassing without exception all who move and all who do not move,
the great blissful Vajra-holder —

bearing the vajra sword,
enters within, crown unmoved.
Then, with the sound of a fearsome roar,
filling all, his eyes rolling as if reversed,
he who proclaims the awareness mantra Hūṃ,
the vajra terrifier, the one who strikes fear —
his beautiful face adorned with a smile,
his eyes, sinless, rolling wildly,
rises from his seat, prostrates,
makes excellent offerings, and declares:

"How wondrous! Destroyer of the extremes of birth,
dispeller of the stains of countless eons,
accomplisher of the qualities of awareness mantra —
this has been supremely proclaimed by the supreme Sages.

Beholding this, one obtains immeasurable virtue;
the wisdom of the buddhas becomes vast and expansive.
Even a bodhisattva of great wisdom,
practicing for a single eon,
could not finish rejoicing upon merely hearing
these qualities — fragrant with a hundredfold virtue —
from which all buddhas arise in an unbroken stream
and from which arise the many ways of practice.

Possessed of a vast ocean of enjoyment,
in the maṇḍala of the supreme awakening mind,
empowered by the supreme victors —"

Endowed with the qualities of non-regression, those who hear this and practice the conduct of the buddhas will, without exception, completely purify all the misdeeds of every sentient being. Their great minds will become entirely stainless. It pacifies, purifies, and bestows auspiciousness; it adorns with freedom from dust and brings peace. It is wholesome and dispels all sorrow; it definitively destroys all suffering. This supreme secret mantra that bestows power dispels all misdeeds upon the earth; it unfailingly grants the fruit that is beyond reproach; it is a teaching that tames aversion; it dispels transgressions that have been committed.

Whoever hears this dhāraṇī — even the attainment of buddhahood, difficult as it is to obtain, will be reached without long delay. All obscurations are completely pacified; it is the all-pervading lord that clears away every affliction. Whoever keeps this dhāraṇī — the one that pacifies afflictions — will attain Samantabhadra himself and likewise Vajrapāṇi himself. The lord of vidyā-mantra holders too will be attained by hearing this.

Brilliant throughout all worlds, the Blessed One Vairocana himself — the supreme and fathomless maṇḍala — until this very teaching is heard, the self-arisen rank that tames and utterly pacifies afflictions has not yet been gained. But once it is heard for the sake of complete purification, one is purified the very moment of hearing it. Therefore, those who wish for excellence and what is good should recite this sūtra of words. This supreme dhāraṇī proclaimed by the Victor, for the purpose of exhausting afflictions — whoever hears it and thereby abandons their misdeeds, committing evil no more, hating what is wholesome no longer, utterly turning away from the nature of evil — to them I shall appear in direct vision. Let no further evil be committed, and let all former misdeeds be confessed. Through the power and strength of this supreme dhāraṇī, all misdeeds without exception will be purified. Whatever non-virtuous and unwholesome actions were committed in a thousand other lifetimes, or even in ten billion lifetimes, will be purified — of this there is no doubt.

On the eighth day of the waxing moon and likewise on the fifteenth, one should diligently undertake the observance of the precepts and make offerings to the buddhas according to the proper rite. Thereafter, free from confusion, one will decisively vanquish the enemy of afflictions; and by reciting this incomparable and supremely difficult-to-maintain dhāraṇī that repels transgressions, one will assuredly behold the radiance of the moon from the Teacher's own face blazing forth. Then, having completed the two accumulations, one will become a true son of the Victors, gloriously adorned with the boundless qualities belonging to the domain of Amitābha, and will attain what is unfathomable even across ten billion eons.

Then, upon hearing the words of Vajrapāṇi, the beings dwelling in the maṇḍalas rose up with a great stir, their bodies adorned with jeweled shoulder ornaments, earrings, and all manner of ornaments swaying and flashing, and they presented the Blessed One with a great variety of offerings.
