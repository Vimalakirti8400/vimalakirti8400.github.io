About this text

The Noble Nīlakaṇṭha Dhāraṇī, known in Sanskrit as the Ārya-Nīlakaṇṭha-nāma-dhāraṇī and in Tibetan as the Phags pa Nī la kaṇ ṭha zhes bya ba'i gzungs, is a dhāraṇī text centered on the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara in his form as Nīlakaṇṭha, the "blue-throated one," a name evoking both his connection to Tantric iconography and his boundless compassion for sentient beings. The text opens with a series of reverent homages — to the Three Jewels, to the buddha Amitābha, and above all to Avalokiteśvara as Lord of the World — framing the dhāraṇī within a devotional context oriented toward the cultivation of wisdom, compassion, and skillful means. As a dhāraṇī scripture, it belongs to the broader genre of ritual and mantra literature preserved in the Kangyur, where such texts are prized for their protective and spiritually transformative power. It is catalogued as Toh 697 in the Kangyur, situated among the dhāraṇī collections of that canon.

Translator's notes

The passage is the opening section of the Nīlakaṇṭha Dhāraṇī, one of the most widely disseminated dhāraṇīs associated with Avalokiteśvara. The Sanskrit mantra portion from "Namo ratnatrayāya" onward is rendered directly in transliteration rather than translated, as is standard practice for dhāraṇī and mantra literature, where the sonic integrity of the text is primary. Minor scribal variations in the Tibetan transliteration of Sanskrit syllables are conventional and do not affect the meaning. The phrase "bodhisattva who has attained great power" renders the epithet Mahāsthāmaprāpta by its functional description as given in the Tibetan, rather than supplying the proper name, since the Tibetan text itself does not use the name explicitly. The image of "ascending the mountain of nirvāṇa" and "dwelling in the house of the unconditioned" are standard Mahāyāna soteriological metaphors preserved as given. This passage is a continuation of the Nīlakaṇṭha Dhāraṇī (the dhāraṇī of Avalokiteśvara in his blue-throated form), consisting almost entirely of transliterated Sanskrit mantra syllables rendered phonetically into Tibetan script. The translation preserves them as reconstructed Sanskrit, following the convention established in the preceding passage. The closing line in Tibetan — rendering "This concludes the dhāraṇī of the noble Nīlakaṇṭha" — is the only non-mantra Tibetan in the passage and is translated directly. Several mantra phrases within this section contain stock epithets drawn from Śaiva and Vaiṣṇava iconography applied to Avalokiteśvara: "Nārāyaṇa" (a name of Viṣṇu), "Vārāhamukha" (boar-faced, another Vaiṣṇava form), "Narasiṃhamukha" (man-lion-faced), and "Nīlakaṇṭha" (blue-throated, a Śaiva epithet) — all assimilated here into the bodhisattva's retinue of manifestations.
