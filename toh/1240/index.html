About this text

The Sādhana of the Maṇḍala Rite Called Thoroughly Well Grasped, known in Sanskrit as the Suparigraha maṇḍala rite and in Tibetan as Dkyil 'khor gyi cho ga'i sgrub thabs bzang po yongs su gzung ba, is a tantric ritual manual composed by the Indian scholar Durjayacandra. Opening with homage to the glorious Heruka and to the compassionate Buddha, it sets out in verse the procedures for a maṇḍala rite, from its preparations and mantras to the closing fire offering, with its reading of omens in the flames and its dismissal of the fire god. Its author presents it modestly, as a practical guide that follows established convention rather than an exhaustive account of every tantra's rites. The work is Toh 1240 in the Tengyur, where it is preserved among the commentaries on the tantras.

Homage to the Blessed One, glorious Heruka.

Beings have fallen into the spreading light of saṃsāra's destroying fire, its garland of sickness and sorrow,
and are blistered by a disease that turns and is hard to turn back; to bring peace to these afflicted beings,
a river of loving-kindness for beings, filled with the water of a rain of the nectar of compassion,
flows down everywhere: to that Buddha, the Blessed One, I bow.

No one knows in full
the rites for the meaning of all the tantras;
no one is able to accomplish
the rites for the meaning of all the tantras.
Therefore, following mere convention,
I do not elaborate;
I compose this,
a good compendium of the maṇḍala rite.

A master not carried off by gain and honor,
skilled in the activities, who has seen the maṇḍala,
a learned one well praised
for accomplishment and emanation, should perform it.
For peace, increase, consecration and the rest,
able to bring about the aims desired,
with up to two or three disciples,
one lays things out clearly in order to draw the maṇḍala.

To increase the accumulations and pacify obstructors,
one who knows the path of omniscience
should first perform the approach;
without the approach there is no accomplishment.
One who knows the distinctions among places
should at once remain in solitude
and meditate on the circle of deities.
When weary, he recites the secret mantra:
he recites the mantra of the Lord of the Circle a hundred thousand times,
a hundred thousand for his own special deity,
and ten thousand for each of the others.
Having obtained the supreme signs of accomplishment,
he gathers the maṇḍala into his own body.
This is called the preliminary approach.
That was the preliminary approach.

Having destroyed the hosts of obstructors through the approach,
one should take possession of the ground:
a mountain, a village or a city,
a region where the mind delights.
Where there are bones, ashes, thorns, stones,
or uncleanness from excrement, go elsewhere.
Ground with udumbara, nyagrodha, aśvattha,
palāśa, bakula or arjuna trees,
or shaped like the back of a turtle:
these six kinds of ground should be avoided.
Ground where water turns to the right,
sloping to the east and the north,
four-cornered and whitish:
dig there, refill the hole with that earth, and some is left over.

The mantrin holding vajra and bell,
with a mind full of compassion,
standing in the posture with the left leg extended,
should clear away all obstructors.
Dig half a cubit deep and fill it with water;
walk a hundred paces,
come back quickly and look:
if it stays just so, that is especially praised.
There, give torma to the lords of the earth,
the guardians of the field, and the rest.
Then, with mantra
and mudrā, one should request the ground.
Bind the two vajra fists,
and hold both at the edge of the boundary;
request it with the words, "Grant me this good ground!"
oṃ hūṃ jraṃ jraṃ svāhā.
That is the mantra and mudrā for requesting the ground.

Signs of good and ill appear;
if the next day they are favorable,
one should perform a pacifying fire offering,
or else subdue the obstructors.
With a mind steady as a mountain,
guard the ground well with the circle of protection.
One should dig to the depth of the knee, the navel or the waist,
according to the accomplishment sought.
Beginning from the winter months and so on,
for three months at a time,
the mahoraga has its head
to the south, the west, the north and the east,
and lies on its left side.
Knowing where its belly is, dig.
The faults of digging at its head, its back and so on
are to be learned from the extensive works.
That was the rite of digging the ground for the maṇḍala and so on.

Throw out husks, stones, gravel,
charcoal and the like;
place jewels, grains and medicines within,
and pound it until very firm.
If one is not able to remove
all the flaws of thorns,
press it with the hand and recite the secret mantra;
it then becomes powerful and pure.
oṃ hana hana krota hūṃ phaṭ.
That is the mantra for purifying the ground.

Sprinkling it with urine, take possession of this pure ground. For five nights without interruption the vajra master should do this. the ritual daggers in wrathful form,
in forward and in reverse order,

and going up to twenty, make them in even numbers.
Take vajras of three kinds,
made of gold, red bronze and the like:
three-pronged, five-pronged
and nine-pronged, these are the three kinds.
By distinguishing peaceful and not peaceful,

The fourth is the crossed vajra itself.
The five-pronged has four faces.
The middle is the place where it is held;
the outer prongs are of that same measure.
The central prong is shaped like a barley grain.
Not going beyond their portions of the directions,
the outer prongs are tips of makara fangs,
emerging from the mouths of makaras:
thick at the root, growing thinner toward the tip,
sharp, their heads gradually curving.
On the inner side of the prongs,

At the roots of the eight prongs,

With beautiful eight-petaled lotuses,

is the east; likewise Ratnasambhava is the south;
on the western and northern prongs
are Amitābha and Amoghasiddhi.
The eight limbs of the noble path, made pure,
are on the eight petals of the lotus:
they are the eight bodhisattvas,

Padmapāṇi, Maitreya,
Gaganagañja, Samantabhadra,

The central one abides as the ground of all:
the essence of the buddhas, the casket.
Vajrāṅkuśī, Pāśī,
Vajrasphoṭā, and Tārā.
In the intermediate directions, the Urger, Jewel Lightning,
the Chain, and the Invincible
are declared to be the lotus ḍākinīs.
Their proper places on the prongs are declared:
in the east, Vajraḍākinī;
in the north, the Terrifying Ḍākinī;
in the west, know her as Vetālī;
in the south, call Caṇḍālī to mind;
in the center, Jñānaḍākinī.
This is held to be the suchness of the vajra.

Of the bell, in six parts overall,
four parts of it are to be made
of bell metal unmixed;
the rest should be examined from other sources.
The space within that bell
is equal in width and in height.
A garland, an utpala, eight marks,
above and in the center, in their order;
by the rite it is marked with eight petals.
On one portion above the utpala
rest three garlands, and above them
a vase made of gold or copper,
silver or brass.
Above that, of the same measure,
make the face of Prajñā, looking to the east.
Above the face is a half vajra,
like half of the vajra described before.
Thus the bell of eight finger-widths and so on
should be made to match the vajra.
Its mouth is like a lotus slightly opened,
or else like the lip of an ox.
This is the description of the bell.

Facing the eight directions and intermediate directions,
meditate on the seeds of the deities.
In the east is the goddess Tārā;
Pāṇḍaravāsinī is in the north;
in the west know her as Māmakī;
in the south is Buddhalocanā.
The Fair-Colored, the Red, the Radiant One,
and Vajra Garland are in the intermediate directions.
The space residing within
is the formless realm, made pure.
Of this form realm, completely pure,
the utpala at the base, in bloom,
abides as the nature of the nectar of wisdom,
the ground of the perfection of wisdom.
The lotus and vajra above
are to be imagined as a vajra.
The handle in the center is wisdom,
gathering in and sending out all things.
With five kinds of lotus-turning gestures,
it should be rung at the eight places.
This is the suchness of the bell.

From gold and other substances, from the wood of the sacrificial tree,
or else from nimba wood,
the filling ladle is two cubits long.
Its divisions shall now be explained.
On a width of eight finger-widths,
carve a hollow equal to half of that.
Carve a vajra equal to the hollow.
The mouth is like a lotus petal.
The rim is square,
four finger-widths all around.
In that two-finger carving, of that measure,
is a three-pronged vajra inside.
The hole from which its essence drips
is the size of the little finger.
Below the waistband, of that same measure,
is the place of beautiful petals,
like a datura flower;
make this at the top of the handle.
Below the handle, a jewel of two finger-widths,
and at the end, a vajra of six finger-widths.
This is the description of the filling ladle.

The pouring ladle is a cubit and a half.
Its mouth is six finger-widths,
shaped like the petal of a lotus.
In its center, in width and depth,
carve three finger-widths, of that measure,
adorned with a five-pronged vajra.
Two finger-widths are the place of adornment;
know that the jewel is one finger-width.
The handle of the filling ladle
is just long enough to grip in the fist and pour.
The pouring ladle is somewhat thinner,
and its vajra is three finger-widths.
This is the description of the pouring ladle.

Vessels of gold, silver, mother-of-pearl, conch,
stone, wood, and copper:
these are the vessels for the welcoming water;
at the least, use a vessel of leaves.
That teaches the vessels for the welcoming water.

Scented water, sweet milk,
pure water, and white flowers,
as they are found, are placed in the vessel;
then fumigate with incense and recite the secret mantra.
This is the rite of preparing the welcoming water.

White, yellow, red and green:
in accomplishing peace, increase and the rest,
prepare the scents, the water and the rest
in keeping with the action.
This is the rite of the varieties of welcoming water.

Flowers, incense, lamps,
the conch of scented water and the rest are set on the right;
on the left, the mantra's welcoming water first;
and a vessel is also placed in front.
This is the arrangement of the vessels for the welcoming water and so on.

The three-syllable or five-syllable
mantra, as taught in the tantra,
in keeping with the action,
is made the mantra for the welcoming water and the rest.
For peace it ends with vaśaṃ;
for increase the ending is vauṣaṭ;
for killing, hūṃ phaṭ comes at the end;
for summoning and subjugating, jaḥ and hriḥ.
These are the mantras for the welcoming water and the rest.

First, scented water should be offered;
second, sweet milk;
third, pure water;
fourth, flowers are added.
The scented water from the mouth of the conch
should be offered at the feet.
oṃ niriti hūṃ khaṃ: this is the mantra of the water for the feet.

For the water for rinsing, it is milk
and sweetness. The purifying sprinkling
is pure water alone; with a bundle
of kuśa grass, recite the mantra of the welcoming water.
oṃ sarbba saṃśodhani hūṃ phaṭ: this is the mantra of the water for the mouth and of the purifying sprinkling.

Having taken the flowers with the white wisdom,
one distinguishes the borders and the rest in sequence.
For the water for the feet, the water for the mouth and the basin:
copper bowls, or vessels of iron,
or vessels made of other substances
such as gold and silver, are set on stands.
This is the classification of the water for the feet and the rest.

The water for the feet, the water for the mouth, the purifying sprinkling and the rest
should be offered in their proper order.
Because all of these are limbs of the welcoming water,
they are spoken of by the word "welcoming water."

The sequence of the rite of offering the welcoming water:
binding the two fists evenly together,
extend the two middle fingers and join their tips;
this is shown as the great argham.
oṃ pratīccha arghaṃ hūṃ svāhā: this is the mantra and the mudrā for offering the welcoming water.

Recite the mantra of Amṛtakuṇḍalin over white mustard seed.
With the two fists, around the chosen deity,
circling with one's own circling,
one should purify, two by two, in the spaces between.
With both fists together at once,
one also circles to the right.
As before, the white mustard seed
should be put into the fire vessel in order to purify.
oṃ sarbba pāpaṃ dahana bajrāya bajra satvasya sarbba pāpaṃ daha svāhā: this is the rite of purification.

Having drawn the wisdom consciousness inward,

put it into the fire, just as with the white mustard seed.
oṃ hūṃ taṭi phaṭ: this is the rite of ritual touching.

puṣparāga topaz, the red gem,

lapis lazuli and coral:

When taking possession of the ground,

In order to accomplish all the actions,
the master, the vajra holder, should perform them.

arising from Vajrasattva. Within it

Worship properly the one perfect in wisdom,

"In the ways of conduct and the special qualities
of all the buddhas, the protectors,
and in the grounds and the perfections,
goddess, you have mastery.
In order to attain all the accomplishments,
I shall draw the maṇḍala."
Saying this three times, one requests Pṛthivī, the earth goddess, to depart.

Bathe the body and anoint it with scent,
adorn it with as many shining ornaments as there are,
and wearing yellow garments,
enter in the afternoon.
Seated in the center of the maṇḍala,
strewn with scent and flowers,
meditating on oneself as the chosen deity,
and joyfully holding incense,
bowing deeply with all the limbs,
one should petition the Victors:
"Buddhas dwelling in all directions
without exception, please consider me.
Great Vajradhara, I, so-and-so,
wish to draw the maṇḍala.
Buddhas and all the rest, please come,
and grant this accomplishment."
This is the petition to the buddhas.

To the master, who is like Vajrasattva,
and is equal to the Permanent One and the other Victors,
the disciples who hold vajra and bell
should make petition:
"Peace of all the Tathāgatas,
abode of all the Tathāgatas,
supreme selflessness of all phenomena,
please explain the sacred maṇḍala.
All the marks perfectly complete,
all that are not marks entirely abandoned,
Samantabhadra, supreme body,
please explain the sacred maṇḍala.
Arisen from the supreme Dharma of peace,
purifying the conduct of wisdom,
Samantabhadra, supreme speech,
please explain the sacred maṇḍala.
Great mind intent on the welfare of beings,
pure by nature and without stain,
Samantabhadra, supreme mind, the guide,
please explain the sacred maṇḍala."
Or else this single verse
should be recited by the four with one voice:
"Peace of all the Tathāgatas,
abode of all the Tathāgatas,
guide, please explain the maṇḍala
that arises from Vajrasattva."

Then, rising, and bowing
to the buddhas who dwell in the sky,
one should generate the pride of being
oneself the union of all the buddhas:
"I am the Buddha Vajrasattva;
I am Vajra Sun;
I am Amoghavajra, the Dharma;
I am all the buddhas."
With the mantra hūṃ bajrotiṣṭha,
one's own manifested wheel
is raised into the sky,
and with the vajra dance one circles all around it.
With the mantra explained earlier,
make the base into the nature of vajra.
The fence, the net, the canopy
and the rest are done as before.

a peg eighteen finger-widths long,
or else twelve finger-widths,
six or four finger-widths in thickness,
made of khadira wood, of bone or of iron.
It should be anointed with red sandalwood,
and beautified with garlands and a red crest.
It is to be held in the left fist.
The hammer, placed on the head of the peg
with the other hand,
strikes it with the earlier mantra,
and the pegs are driven in at the east and the other places.
Or else, with mantra and mudrā,
make the pegs, the fence and the net.
Showing the palm of the hand upward,
turn it over and strike the ground.
oṃ bajra hūṃ phaṭ: this is the mantra and mudrā of the vajra peg.
The two hands, with fingers interlinked like a chain,
are stretched upward and turned outward.
oṃ hūṃ phaṭ: this is the mantra and mudrā of the net.
Making both hands into fists,
extend the wisdom and the lotus.
Join the earth parasol and the eight consciousnesses;
join wisdom and consciousness;
joining the earth parasol and the lotus,
circle through the eight directions and intermediate directions.
oṃ jaṃbhani hūṃ phaṭ: this is the mantra and mudrā of the fence.

The one endowed with yoga
should consecrate the vase.
Vases free from black bases and other faults
are to be set out in front:
long-necked and big-bellied,
their mouths beautified with fine branches,
filled with milk, urine and the rest,
and their necks wrapped with cloth.
Having made the matching ones firm,
they are to be placed in the maṇḍala house.
In order to fulfill the aims of the eight accomplishments,
the recitation is done up to seven times.
oṃ tapte tapte maha tapte svāhā: this is the mantra for summoning the water of the Gaṅgā.
Joining the earth parasol and consciousness,
and extending all the rest,
touching the vase, with this secret mantra
one recites up to seven times:
oṃ suṃ kṣuṃ yuṃ hūṃ phaṭ svāhā. This is the consecration of the door vases.

With the stainless lines laid down,
at the four doors of the ritual wheel,
beginning from the eastern door,
the vases are to be set out.
Those called Victory and Auspiciousness
are on the two sides of the eastern door;
in the south are declared Accomplishment and Protection;
in the west, Victorious and Peace;
in the north, Accomplishment and Miraculous Power.
This is the offering of the precious accomplishments.

Whatever here is the abode of the deities,
wherever the maṇḍala is fixed,
that maṇḍala should be anointed
with the five scents, or else with others.
By emanating the syllables with their own mantras,
one should set out the wheel of the deities.
Then, by the practice of summoning,
the wheel of wisdom deities is summoned there.
Then with the five kinds of offerings
one worships the Victors properly, with faith,
and petitions them for blessing
until the maṇḍala is complete:
"Buddhas, whose intention is loving and beneficial,
please consider me.
Having come together with the mantras,
you take possession of the ground.
Great Vajradhara, I, of this name,
in order to create the maṇḍala of so-and-so,
with whatever offerings I am able,
shall draw the maṇḍala at dawn tomorrow.
In order to care for the disciples
and also to make offerings to you,
it is fitting that you, moved by loving compassion,
draw near and remain."
Saying this three times is the consecration of the deities of the maṇḍala.

Then the disciple, having bathed
and put on clean garments,
holding flowers in cupped hands and prostrating,
should petition the guru:
"You alone draw us out
from the ocean of existence in which we sink,
fearful with its sea monsters
of birth, aging, sickness and death.
Great Bliss, since you are my Teacher,
master, please consider me.
I seek you, great protector,
who show the way of great awakening.
Grant me that very pledge;
grant me also the mind of awakening;
grant me also the three refuges,
the Buddha, the Dharma and the Saṅgha.
Protector, please lead me
into the supreme city of great liberation."
This is the disciple's petition for the pledge.

"Since you are a vessel of the great way,
come here, my son! The rite
and the manner of conduct of the secret mantra
of the Great Vehicle I shall rightly explain to you.
The perfect buddhas who have passed,
likewise those who have not yet come,
and the protectors who have now appeared
and remain for the benefit of beings:
having known this excellent rite
of the secret mantra of them all,
the Hero, at the foot of the tree of awakening,
attained omniscience, free of signs.
Through the unequalled application of secret mantra,
the protector Śākyasiṃha
destroyed the most terrible hosts of Māra,
and their great armies as well.
Therefore, in order to attain omniscience,
my son, make this your resolve!"

Having thus advised the disciple,
one performs confession, rejoicing and the rest.
First one has him go for refuge to the three,
and then he should generate the mind of awakening:
"Just as the protectors of the three times
made certain of awakening,
so I shall generate the unsurpassed,
sacred mind of awakening.
The training of moral discipline,
the gathering of virtuous qualities,
and the working for the welfare of beings: these three moral disciplines
I shall firmly uphold.
The Buddha, the Dharma and the Saṅgha,
the unsurpassed Three Jewels:
the vow that arises from the yoga of the buddhas
I take first, beginning from now.
For the great supreme vajra family,
the vajra, the bell and the mudrā,
these very things I shall rightly uphold,
and I shall uphold the masters as well.
For the delightful pledge
connected with the jewel family,
every day at the six times
I shall always give the four kinds of giving.
For the great pure lotus family,
arisen from great awakening,
the outer, the inner and the secret, the three vehicles,
the sacred Dharma, I shall uphold each one.
For the great supreme action family,
I shall rightly uphold
being endowed with all the vows,
and I shall perform the acts of offering as best I can.
Having generated the unsurpassed,
sacred mind of awakening,
for the welfare of all beings
I take the vows without exception.
Those who have not crossed over I shall carry across;
those not liberated I shall liberate;
those not relieved I shall relieve;
and I shall establish beings in nirvāṇa."
Saying this three times, the vows are taken.

Then the disciple, set before one, is sprinkled with scent, and at his heart one holds a five-pronged vajra. On the crown of his head one imagines a vajra marked with various colors, and at the place of the throat a lotus. Then, having made offerings with flowers, incense and the rest, set him in front. A tooth stick twelve finger-widths long is washed with scented water and milk, its tip bound with flowers; and scented water recited over seven times is also given to purify the mouth. oṃ ajitva saṃbartani hūṃ phaṭ: this is the mantra recited over the tooth stick. oṃ hrīḥ biśuddha dharmmā sarbbā pāpaṃ niśca saṃśodhaya sarbba bikalpa apanaya hūṃ: this is the mantra for giving the water.

The disciple, facing north,
holding the tooth stick without splitting it,
throws it onto the ground,
onto an area the size of a cowhide.
If it falls with its upper end facing toward him,
he will be most fortunate.
If it falls with its outer end facing away,
he will be without good fortune.

For protection, over a thread
recite the mantra of Amṛtakuṇḍalin;
then make three knots in it
and tie it on the disciple's right upper arm.

Then, so that they will not turn back,
one should arouse enthusiasm in the disciples,
and so that they turn away from other vehicles,
proclaim the greatness of the Great Vehicle:
"You great beings
today have gained an unequalled gain,
because all the great Victors
together with their heirs
have taken all of you
into this teaching, and you are being born in it.
Therefore tomorrow you
will be truly born into the Great Vehicle.
This supreme path, from which the Great Vehicle
greatly arises, is glorious.
Since you are going there,
you self-arisen ones of great fortune,
through knowing all the world
you will become Tathāgatas.
Beyond existence and nonexistence,
stainless like space,
profound, wholly beyond the conceptions
of all reasoners, without any abode,
free of all elaboration,
yet elaborated through elaborations,
free of response, of action and the rest,
abiding in the two truths:
this supreme among excellent vehicles
is attained if one abides in its way.
My son, tomorrow the maṇḍala
will be drawn. Now sleep!"

Having said this, one enters, makes offerings properly,
and requests the maṇḍala deities to depart.
Having bestowed the yoga of the chosen deity,
one then brings the disciple
into the celestial mansion and protects him well.
One should carefully examine the lion's sleeping posture.
Shoots of kuśa grass with unbroken tips
should be imagined as arisen from dhi.
The first is placed as a pillow,
and the others are spread as a mat.
"With a virtuous mind, sleep,
and tell me the dream you see, just as it is."
Saying this, one has the disciple lie down.
One should ask what dream he saw.
Having heard it, one should explain without doubt
whether it is good or not good.
Through a virtuous dream the three kinds of accomplishment are achieved;
an unvirtuous one is to be pacified by fire offering and so forth.
This is the consecration of the disciple.

On a good planetary day, lunar mansion and the like,
and also on the fourth day,
the guru, having performed the fire offering,
together with the disciple begins the maṇḍala.
The six months, the four planets,
whichever are the fourteen lunar mansions,
and the number of seven lunar dates
are praised by the Victors for consecration and the rest.
The middle and last months of spring, the last of summer,
the autumn month, the middle and last of winter;
the planetary days of Mercury, Jupiter, Venus,
and the day of the moon: these are good.
The second day and the third day,
the fifth, the seventh, the tenth,
the thirteenth, and the full moon:
these seven days are good.
Puṣya, Hasta, Anurādhā, Uttaraphalgunī,
Rohiṇī, Abhijit, Mṛgaśiras and Ārdrā,
Jyeṣṭhā, Mūla, Pūrvāṣāḍhā and Uttarāṣāḍhā,
Pūrvabhādrapadā and Uttarabhādrapadā: these are the lunar mansions.
Under Uttaraphalgunī one should perform consecration;
through it wisdom will increase.
Whatever accomplishment abides in the mind
will be accomplished under Hasta.
Under Mūla and Abhijit
the teachings will be fully understood.
Ārdrā brings good fortune.
Under Rohiṇī wealth will arise.
Mṛgaśiras is declared to be for peace.
Pūrvāṣāḍhā and Uttarāṣāḍhā bring increase of sons.
Pūrvabhādrapadā and Uttarabhādrapadā are known for increase.
Under Puṣya one is victorious in everything.
Jyeṣṭhā rightly bestows happiness.

Anurādhā bestows the supreme.
Relying on the right time and good planets,
this activity is the consecration.

do not transgress the word of the Sugata,
and never speak
in disparagement of your vajra brothers and sisters.
Do not be lacking in love for sentient beings;
do not speak of suchness to those without the fortune;
do not abandon fortunate beings;
never disparage your own body.
Do not set limits on the sublime Dharma,
nor scorn the teachings of others.
Until you reach the heart of awakening,
do not abandon the Three Jewels.
Always guard the sacred commitments;
hold the vajra and the bell;
always keep the crown knot;
always perform the fire offering;
always erect maṇḍalas;
always consecrate the Sugatas;
give torma to the great bhūtas."
With these words and the like, the consolation should be given at length.

Having conferred the wisdom empowerment,
one should then speak of the fourth:
"Vajra and bell: by the sleep of that melody
the eyes are sealed;
realizing, and wholly free of concepts,
peace comes clearly forth.
This gnosis is exceedingly subtle,
the vajra essence, equal to space,
stainless, bestowing liberation, peace itself;
and you yourself are its father."
From the lips of the holy guru
the fourth is to be known.

After the disciple's rites are complete,
having come out of the maṇḍala,
one should perform the fire offering to complete the rite.
Then, having entered, ask the Victors' forbearance;
having made offerings and praises,
ring the bell with the left hand,
move the vajra with the right and offer incense,

grant the accomplishments that accord with them.
Though you depart to the buddha realm,
please come again."
This is the mantra requesting departure.

and then cast them into a river.

In the center of the element of space one should visualize an eight-petaled lotus. On the eastern petal imagine Indra; in the south place Antaka (Yama); on the western petal the lord of waters, Varuṇa; in the north, likewise, the yakṣa lord. In the fire quarter, the southeast, the great Agni; in the southwest, Nairṛti, lord of the rākṣasas; in the wind quarter imagine the Homeless One, the wind god; in the northeast, Īśāna, lord of the bhūtas. The sun, the moon, Brahmā and the rest, the holy Brahmakāyika gods, the Brahmapurohita gods, those dwelling in the Joyless and the Boundless, the Truly Arisen, the Great Kings, the Paranirmitavaśavartin gods, the gods of Nirmāṇarati, those dwelling in the Pure Abodes, those dwelling in Sukhāvatī, those dwelling in Akaniṣṭha, the Radiant world realm, and those dwelling in the Heaven of the Thirty-Three: all of these are to be placed above. The gods above are brilliant white, Indra and the rest are black, Karkoṭa and the rest are deep red. Since consecration and the fire offering
are two limbs of the maṇḍala,
after the maṇḍala I shall teach
both the consecration and the fire offering.

A master of good birth and intelligence,
who performs all his actions without conflict with the world,
and who knows the ten principles,
is fit to perform the consecration.
At a captivating place of consecration,
anointed with the five well-thickened substances,
adorned with flowers,
beautified with a canopy, white parasols, pennants
and much else besides,
and fragrant with burning incense,
on a day of good planets and constellations, or on another,
the master, properly dressed, proceeds.
He should wear a necklace, anklets, armlets,
earrings and bracelets;
if he has none, at least a finger ring,
of no less than a māṣa in weight.
The images, painted scrolls and books there
are adorned with as many ornaments as there are.
Joining himself with the special deity,
he places them before him and performs the preliminary consecration.

First of all, the vidyādhara
diligently rings the bell,
brandishes the vajra in his right hand,
and gives the torma according to the rite.
With the five products of the cow, the five barks,

he washes what bears the form of the deity,
and offers water for the face and the rest as before.
Again, through that same yoga,
he washes it and blesses it.
Knowing the place and the time,
he performs the preliminary consecration in the evening.
He sprinkles cleansing water from the conch.

upon the images and the rest set there,
the form accomplished through the six limbs,
he visualizes it as the very deity;
with verses he invites the forms of the deities
into the sky and properly worships them.

"Just as the perfect buddhas
abide in Tuṣita,
and as they entered the womb of Queen Māyā,
so likewise please abide in this form.
Protectors who always dwell here,
for the full increase of the awakening mind,
please accept with loving compassion
these flowers, incense and the rest."
He properly worships, and then praises;
after that he purifies,
and then fully protects.
This is the preliminary consecration.

After the consecration and the rest,
whatever was forgotten, could not be done,
or left the rite incomplete
should be completed by the fire offering.
Through the variety of aims to be accomplished
the fire offering becomes of many kinds;
but since a lengthy text is daunting,
I shall teach here only a brief summary.
First, with mantra and mudrā,
one should purify the ground:
extending the right hand,
visualizing it as a vajra, one strikes the ground.
oṃ hana hana krodha hūṃ phaṭ
This is the mudrā and mantra for purifying the ground.
The hearth is a cubit across
and about half that in depth.
Then come the base and the rim,
and outside, a vajra garland.
The base has anthers,
the rim has lotus flowers;
each is four finger-widths,

Wheel, jewel, lotus or sword,

in the central field, one finger-width,

For pacifying, the colored powder is white;
for increasing, it is made yellow;
for subjugating, red; for the others,
black: these are the five kinds.
The directions differ according to the kind of rite,
and the times too are to be determined;
the wise examine the direction and the time
for each such rite.
Facing north, the mantrin
properly undertakes the rite of pacifying;
increasing is always facing east;
for fierce rites it is facing south;
for subjugating, west is taught,
and likewise for summoning and for expelling.
Pacifying is performed in the evening,
increasing in the morning,
fierce rites at midday,
and subjugating and the like at midnight.
Twigs of the sacrificial tree,
their tips unbroken,

for increasing, fourteen finger-widths;
for pacifying, twelve;
for subjugating, ten; for hostility

A fire like Indra's bow, the rainbow, is good,
