About this text

Toh 692 in the Kangyur is a dhāraṇī text known in English as "The Heart of the Wish-Fulfilling Wheel of the Secret Treasury of the Noble Avalokiteśvara, the Unobstructed," with the Tibetan title Pakpa Chenrezik Wangchuk gi Sangwé Dzö Thokpa Mepé Yizhin Kyi Khorlo Nyingpo. It belongs to the dhāraṇī genre of canonical literature, presenting a sacred formula and its surrounding narrative as spoken by the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara, the great being of compassion, before the Buddha on the Sage's Peak. The text concerns the transmission of a powerful wish-fulfilling dhāraṇī — described as the "Sandalwood Heart of the Great Many-Petalled Lotus Wheel" — capable of fulfilling every aspiration of those who practice it. As a dhāraṇī sūtra attributed to Avalokiteśvara, it falls within the broader class of incantation texts found throughout the Kangyur, sitting alongside other works dedicated to this beloved bodhisattva and his compassionate activity.

Translation

In the Tibetan language: The dhāraṇī called "The Heart of the Wish-Fulfilling Wheel of the Secret Treasury of the Noble Avalokiteśvara, the Unobstructed."

Homage to all buddhas and bodhisattvas.

Thus did I hear. At one time the Blessed One was dwelling together with a great multitude of bodhisattvas, great beings, on the Sage's Peak. Then the bodhisattva, the great being Avalokiteśvara rose from his seat, arranged his robes, placed his right knee on the ground, joined his palms, and addressed the Blessed One in these words:

"Blessed One, I possess a great dhāraṇī called 'The Sandalwood Heart of the Great Many-Petalled Lotus Wheel,' which fulfils every wish and aspiration, bringing them fully to completion. If the Tathāgata, in his great compassion, would grant me leave to teach it, I wish to teach it, by the power of the Blessed One's blessing, for the benefit and healing of all sentient beings. Why is that? Blessed One, because this dhāraṇī, endowed with the power of great splendor, completely fulfils all aspirations like a great wish-fulfilling jewel and like a wish-granting tree."

Then the Blessed One praised the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara, saying "Excellent! Excellent!" and declared: "Because you have entered upon this course out of loving compassion for the sake of sentient beings, you are granted leave to teach this unobstructed dhāraṇī."

The bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara, having been given this permission, rose, joined his palms, made obeisance, and sat again upon his seat. He gazed upon the assembled retinue with one-pointed mind, and then, with a mind endowed with great compassion, he spoke this mantra:

Namo ratnatrayāya. Namo āryāvalokiteśvarāya. Bodhisattvāya. Mahāsattvāya. Mahākāruṇikāya. Tadyathā: Oṃ cakravarti cintāmaṇi mahāpadme ruru tiṣṭhate. Vajra bhara. Ākarāya hūṃ svāhā.

This is the mantra of the body.

Oṃ padme. Cintāmaṇi. Mahāvajra bhara hūṃ.

This is the mantra of the heart.

Oṃ bhara te padma hūṃ.

This is the mantra of the heart of the heart.

At the moment when the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara spoke this dhāraṇī of the wish-fulfilling wheel, the great earth shook and trembled in six ways. The palaces of gods (devas), nāgas, yakṣas, gandharvas, asuras, garuḍas, kiṃnaras, and great serpents also trembled greatly. The king of the māras and his retinue were seized with extreme terror and great fear arose in them. The palace of the king of the māras blazed fiercely. Yakṣas, demons, malevolent spirits of every kind were struck with dread and fright, covered their faces, and fell to the ground. All the gates of the hell realms of sentient beings were thrown open, and the sentient beings suffering there were liberated and experienced the supreme bliss of the gods. At that time the gods rained down showers of jeweled flowers and various jeweled ornaments of every kind. Divine music resounded with manifold sounds in the sky, making offering to the Tathāgata.

Then the Blessed One, in the voice of Brahmā, spoke these verses to the bodhisattva, the great being Avalokiteśvara:

"Excellent! Excellent!" he exclaimed in praise. "Noble son, because you have taught this dhāraṇī out of compassion for sentient beings, you have thereby obtained great benefit for them."

Then the Blessed One once again spoke to the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara, saying: "Noble son, for the sake of sentient beings, explain the ritual procedure for this dhāraṇī, the Great King of Miraculous Powers."

Then the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara addressed the Blessed One in these words:

"Whatever noble son or noble daughter, monk or nun, layman or laywoman, young boy or young girl wishes to experience karmic maturation in this very life — if they apply themselves to this dhāraṇī of the canopy with single-pointed, earnest diligence and recite it without interruption, whether in an auspicious time or an inauspicious time, whether in a state of purity or impurity, it will render all their undertakings meaningful. Whatever the purpose for which one desires and seeks an aim, if one recites it one hundred and eight times, many hundreds and thousands of aims will be accomplished. There is no other mantra or dhāraṇī of the wish-fulfilling wheel that can compare with this one. Why is that? Because it utterly destroys evil karma of the past and present, and heavy obscurations.

"For any person who recites this dhāraṇī, even those who would otherwise fall into the Avīci hell will be liberated, and even the sins of the five acts without interval and others of their kind will be purified — how much more needless to say regarding evil karma, malevolent spirits and obstructors, and all other fevers and ailments.

"Those afflicted with fevers lasting one day, two days, three days, or four days, or those suffering fever day and night, or suffering from disorders of wind, bile, phlegm, or the like — if this vidyā mantra is recited for them, all will be purified and they will recover.

"Whoever has been stricken by poison or sorcery, or who suffers from swelling, itching, leprosy, cataracts, wind disease, madness, or fever of the brain — or who has illness in the ears, the nose, the lips, the tongue, the throat, the mouth, the face, the crown of the head, the brain, the chest, the ribs, the heart, the abdomen, the waist, the back, the legs, the hands, or anywhere else throughout the body — for all of these this dhāraṇī will bring healing. Whatever fevers and ailments exist in the body, all will be healed.

"Yakṣas, rākṣasas, vināyakas, evil māras, and malevolent spirits will not be able to cause harm. There will be no fear from weapons, armies, floods, fire, wind, rain, or violent hail. Enemies, thieves, malicious kings, and vicious robbers will not be able to cause harm. There will be no sudden untimely death. No evil dreams will be dreamt. Scorpions, snakes, vermin, poisonous insects, centipedes, vicious savage beasts, lions, tigers, and wolves will not be able to cause harm.

"In battle, one will be victorious in every instance. If disputes arise with one's superiors, reconciliation will come about."

If this dhāraṇī is recited even once, all the benefits set forth above will be accomplished just as one wishes.

If anyone recites this dhāraṇī one hundred and eight times in a single day, the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara will declare to that person: "Child of noble lineage, have no fear. Whatever you aspire to and whatever you seek, all of it shall be given to you." The Tathāgata Amitāyus will also appear in person. The array of magnificent qualities of the world-realm of Sukhāvatī, just as described in the sūtras, will become visible. The assemblies of bodhisattvas of the world-realm of Sukhāvatī will also become visible. All the buddhas of the ten directions will become visible. The abode of the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara, Mount Potalaka, will also become visible. One's own body will become utterly purified, and kings and ministers will always show honor and respect. One will be loved and revered by many people. In all future births, one will not be born from a womb but will take birth upon a lotus. One will be endowed with supreme splendor. In all future births one will remember one's previous lives. From this moment until the attainment of perfect and complete awakening, one will never fall into the lower destinies, and will always be reborn in the presence of a buddha. This is called the dhāraṇī that thoroughly destroys all evil actions.

This concludes the first chapter, the dhāraṇī that thoroughly destroys all evil actions.

Then, out of compassion for sentient beings, the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara again taught the secret ritual of the dhāraṇī that is the essence of the wish-fulfilling wheel — a teaching suited to anyone, one that accomplishes cherished aims, so that all things are spontaneously pacified. This teaching encompasses two kinds of wealth: worldly wealth and transcendent wealth. Worldly wealth consists of gold, silver, and the various precious substances. Transcendent wealth is twofold in its array of merit and primordial wisdom: it is the supreme ease of body and mind; being loved and revered by many people; the complete protection of all sentient beings who are suffering; the expansion of the mind of loving-kindness and the conferring of happiness upon those endowed with intelligence; and the constant increase of the necessities of life together with lasting power and strength. The domain of this secret treasury is not to be spoken of to others.

If anyone desires to obtain the supreme attainment of my wish-fulfilling wheel dhāraṇī, let them recite it with one-pointed mind, at all times and in all places, whether clean or unclean — for there is no fault in doing so — and let them complete the full count of recitations. Furthermore, let them call aloud the name of the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara, the name of the wish-fulfilling wheel dhāraṇī, and the name of the person concerned. If one wishes to bring about closeness with a king, a prince, a queen, a royal son, a princess, a brahmin, a member of the warrior caste, a commoner, an outcaste, a man, a woman, a boy, a young girl, or adherents of the various non-Buddhist schools, let one call their names and always, at dawn, complete the count of recitations.

If one seeks the supreme attainment and wishes to come under the protection of a king, one should recite it one thousand and eight times every morning at dawn for seven consecutive days, and one will come to meet with that king. If one wishes to come under the protection of a queen, one should recite it nine hundred times.

If one wishes to come under the protection of a royal son, one should recite it eight hundred times. If one wishes to see one's own children, one should recite it seven hundred times. If one wishes to come under the protection of a princess, one should recite it six hundred times. If one wishes to come under the protection of a brahmin, one should recite it five hundred times. If one wishes to come under the protection of the warrior caste, one should recite it four hundred times. For a commoner, three hundred times. For an outcaste, two hundred times. For a monk or a nun, one hundred times. For a layman or a laywoman, ninety times. For a boy or a young girl, sixty times.

This is called the rite of seeking support and intimacy, and it bestows abundance of wealth, servants, horses, and all the requisites of well-being. If one desires to meet with someone dear and beloved who dwells far away, one will travel to them swiftly as the wind, exactly as one has imagined in one's mind. Whatever one wishes — when the required count of recitations is completed, all those aims will be accomplished.

If one wishes to behold the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara himself in his true form, one should recite the mantra one thousand and eight times, and one will see his very body in its true reality, and every aspiration will be fulfilled. If one wishes to behold Vajrapāṇi, one should recite the mantra ten thousand times, and Vajrapāṇi will manifest in plain sight, will show loving care, and like a father's love for his son will grant all the wishes of one's heart. If one wishes to see a buddha surrounded by a great assembly of retinue, one should recite the mantra thirteen thousand times, and one will see just that. If one recites without interruption for the duration of seven parasols, the holders of vidyā-mantras will manifest in person, and each from his own place will grant to that person whatever power his respective mantra holds and will completely protect him. On the seventh day, the lords of the great trichiliocosm — the world rulers and Śakra (Indra), the lord of the gods — will come with their retinues and grant all his wishes. If one performs the recitation rite exactly as set forth above, all aspirations will be perfectly fulfilled. This is called the rite from the secret treasury of the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara by which all come to be loving and devoted.

This concludes the second chapter, the rite by which all come to be loving and devoted, from the secret treasury of the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara.

Then the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara, moved by compassion for sentient beings, further taught a rite of compounding a medicine — one that, when worn upon the body, causes those who see one to feel love and delight, brings supreme accomplishment, and fulfills all aspirations exactly as one wishes. One should take equal portions of the following: costus root, white sandalwood, saffron, camphor, musk, mace, clove, kakola, lotus, blue utpala, and gold dust. These are to be pounded and blended with white honey. After reciting the foregoing mantra one thousand and eight times, one should fumigate the body with it, or fumigate one's garments with it, or anoint the eyes with it, or apply a dot to the forehead, or rub it upon the body. When this is done, kings, queens, ministers, servants, men, women, and all others will feel love and delight; they will honor the Dharma of the path, generate the awakening mind, and without the slightest reluctance give away their bodily comforts and wealth and render homage — this is beyond all telling.

Just as the sun and moon are beheld with delight by all, so shall the aims one is pursuing be fulfilled and accomplished. Whoever comes to carry this medicine upon their person will be purified of all the obscurations arising from misdeeds and will be freed from every obstacle.

One will be freed from forcible seizure by a king's power, from being swept away by water, from being scorched by fire, from weapons of every kind, from poison, and from all the afflictions of bondage.

If this rite of preparing the medicine called Akata is performed with sincere and heartfelt intention, all will feel love and delight toward that person.

After fumigating with this medicine, one should apply it to the mouth in a fine mist. This is what is called the rite of the medicine for inspiring love and delight in others, as taught by Avalokiteśvara.

This was the third chapter of the rite of preparing the medicine Akata for inspiring love and delight in others, from the dhāraṇī of the wish-fulfilling wheel of the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara.

Furthermore, moved by compassion for all sentient beings, the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara taught the rite of the mouth-anointing medicine for making one pleasing and delightful to all. Take camphor, musk, and saffron, ground very fine, and blend them with ox gallstone. Recite each of the three mantras given above one thousand and eight times, then prepare the mixture with pure water into pellets of varying sizes, roughly the size of a hollow tree's fruit. Next, recite the three mantras above one hundred and eight times, then dry the pellets in the shade, sheltered from parasols and wind. Each pellet should then be consecrated by reciting each of the three mantras above seven times. Place one pellet in the mouth, and then, whether one addresses a king, a minister, a trusted ally, or a great assembly of people, they will show respect and, without withholding wealth or precious things, will give whatever is asked of them. Whatever one speaks, all will trust and heed it. Even if there is a dispute, all will listen and act accordingly.

While holding the medicine in the mouth, one should continuously recite the mantra, and Avalokiteśvara will cause all aspirations to be fulfilled.

Before an image of the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara, draw a square maṇḍala of incense-water, one fathom in extent, with a single circumambulatory path. Within the maṇḍala, scatter various flowers from fragrant trees as many as are available, burn white sandalwood, and then take the medicine pellets prepared earlier and place them within the maṇḍala. Set up four victory banners, spread curtains and white cloth, and draw four white lotuses atop the maṇḍala. After making offerings to the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara, recite the heart mantra and the mantra of the heart of the heart one hundred and eight times. Recite also the mantra of the body one hundred and eight times, burn the white sandalwood, and scatter flowers. At that moment all aspirations will be fulfilled. Take the medicine that is within the maṇḍala, fasten it upon the body, and wherever one travels, whatever one seeks will come to accomplishment; whatever one speaks, all will trust and heed it; every aim one undertakes will be fulfilled — except when the mind has not been made one-pointed.

This is called the rite of the mouth-anointing medicine from the wish-fulfilling wheel.

This was the fourth chapter of the rite of the mouth-anointing medicine, from the wish-fulfilling wheel of the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara.

Then the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara, out of loving care for sentient beings, taught the rite by which one becomes beloved and pleasing to the sight of all the many people of the world.

Take equal parts of the following: māṃśī (spikenard), lotus petals, blue lotus, ocean-water foam, the bones of a black carp, castor oil, kakunbanya, musk, saffron, Chinese saffron, long pepper, naleśam, and cāsaka. Pound these together and strain them to a fine consistency. For every tola-weight of this previously prepared medicine, add half a tola-weight of musk and camphor, grind them finely together, and prepare this compound in the presence of an image of Avalokiteśvara. Then recite each of the three mantras given earlier one thousand and eight times each, generate the mind of loving-kindness and compassion toward all sentient beings, and place the medicine at the feet of the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara. Afterward, touch the medicine with the tip of an ivory stylus and apply it to the eyes. This will cure all conditions of eye discharge and brain-heat, all diseases caused by cataracts, white film, weeping eyes, red film, blindness, and all forms of brain-heat.

If this medicine is applied to the eyes once each day, like the application of a collyrium, all eye diseases will be cured. If it is applied twice, all feverish diseases of the body will be cured. If it is applied three times, all eighty-four kinds of heat-disorders of the mind will be cured. If it is applied four times, one will not be harmed by any inner or outer obscurations. If it is applied five times, one will be victorious in every battle against enemies. If it is applied six times, all sinful actions, all afflictions, the four grave offenses, the five acts of immediate retribution, evil dreams, and all harmful enchantments will be utterly destroyed, and one will never again fall into the lower destinies.

If it is applied seven times, kings, great ministers, and all the multitudes of people will follow, honor, trust, and love one. If it is applied within two weeks, great independence and mastery will be attained. If it is applied within three weeks, one will become intimate with kings and great ministers. If it is applied within four weeks, yakṣas together with their retinues will render service. If it is applied within five weeks, asuras, nāgas, yakṣas, and rākṣasas will become one's servants. If it is applied within six weeks, garuḍas and rākṣasas of great power will guard one fully until the attainment of perfect and complete awakening, and neither harmful enchantments nor spirits and demons will be able to harm one.

If it is applied within seven weeks, the Great Black God and the eight classes of spirits and rākṣasas will draw near, follow, and serve one. If it is applied within eight weeks, one will become an antarā. If it is applied within nine weeks, all treasures will be revealed to one's sight. If it is applied within ten weeks, the doors of the dwellings of the asuras will open of themselves, and one will see everything within them and will pass in and out without obstruction.

If it is applied within eleven weeks, whatever number of blazing medicinal ingredients one has taken in will all manifest as direct experience and bring delight, and one will attain long life. Or if one wishes to attain great power, that too will be accomplished in the same way.

If it is applied within twelve weeks, many sages (ṛṣis) will open up treasuries of jewels, reveal them nearby, and one will enjoy them at will.

If it is applied within thirteen weeks, the doors of the palace-halls of the nāgas will open of themselves, and one's sight will pass through them without obstruction.

If it is applied within fourteen weeks, the doors of the palace-halls of the gods of the desire realm will open of themselves, and one will see everything within them.

If it is applied within fifteen weeks, even in the darkness of night all will become as clear as if lit by an umbrella of light.

If it is applied within sixteen weeks, one will see even the vajra ground beneath the earth and the maṇḍala of wind below it.

If it is applied within seventeen weeks, one will see all the hell-beings, however many there are, throughout the four continents, and by the power of this, all those sentient beings who are experiencing suffering will be liberated.

If it is applied within eighteen weeks, that person will attain a power like that of the sun.

If it is applied within nineteen weeks, one will behold the true body of Vajrapāṇi, and all one's aspirations will be completely fulfilled.

If it is applied within twenty weeks, one will behold Avalokiteśvara, the sovereign endowed with great compassion, and all one's aspirations will be completely fulfilled.

If it is applied within twenty-one weeks, one will attain the feet of miraculous power. The doors of the palace-halls of the gods of the form realm will open of themselves and one will see everything within them. One will also behold the buddhas and bodhisattvas of the ten directions and all the perfectly pure buddha-fields.

If it is applied within one year, one will attain the five perfectly pure eyes.

Whoever wishes to accomplish this ritual of practice should have very deep faith in this teaching, should cherish compassion for sentient beings, and if there is no doubt, everything will be accomplished just as taught above. If, however, doubt should arise, the rituals will be difficult to accomplish.

This is the Eye-Medicine Ritual from the Jewel Wheel of Avalokiteśvara's Sandalwood. This concludes the fifth chapter, the chapter on the eye-medicine, from the Mind Wheel of the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara.

Thereupon, the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara, out of compassion for sentient beings, further taught a fire-offering ritual of auspicious oblation that accomplishes all purposes of attainment, that utterly destroys all afflictions, the obscurations of moral fault, and all evil karma, that subdues all enemies, that pacifies the angry mind of the vināyakas so that they no longer harm the multitude of beings but instead give rise to a mind of loving-kindness.

One digs a pit and makes a square fire-pit one cubit in size. One mixes equal parts of the flower mālentsa, white mustard, curd, honey, and buttermilk, and cuts pieces of aloe-wood, sandalwood, and fragrant wood separately into pieces twelve finger-breadths in length and one finger-breadth in width. One burns these in the fire-pit, takes a small amount of medicine, consecrates it once with the mantra, and casts it into the fire-pit. When this has been done a full one thousand and eight times, all karmic obscurations will be utterly destroyed and one will be able to live for one hundred and twenty years.

If the fire-offering ritual is performed continuously without interruption for seven days, one will be able to live for one thousand years and the body will become entirely pure.

If the ritual is performed for two weeks, kings, princes, and great ministers will become benefactors and friends.

And ordinary people too will come to take refuge and offer devoted service.

If the ritual is performed according to the proper procedure for a full three weeks, the thirty-three gods with their retinues, the gods of the sun and moon, and the four great kings with their retinues will draw near and bestow complete protection. Vajrapāṇi will grant the great spiritual attainments. And the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara will fully accomplish all of one's great aspirations.

If rain becomes scarce in a region, one consecrates white mustard seed and butter with this mantra and burns them inside the fire-pit for the duration of three parasols — three sessions — whereupon rain will fall.

If the rain is excessive and will not cease, one takes ash from that same fire-pit, consecrates it with one hundred and eight recitations of the mantra, and scatters it into the sky, whereupon the rain will cease.

If harmful hail falls or fierce winds suddenly arise, one likewise takes that ash, consecrates it with one hundred and eight recitations of the mantra, and scatters it in the direction of the clouds, whereupon all will be pacified.

If one recites this mantra continuously, one's power and strength will become comparable to that of a great and mighty lord. Upon relinquishing this body, one will be born in the world-system of Sukhāvatī. And in every single lifetime until one attains complete and perfect awakening, one will obtain the recollection of past lives.

Then the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara addressed the Blessed One in these words:

Blessed One, just as I have taught this dhāraṇī of the Wish-fulfilling Wheel of Sandalwood Essence — whoever among monks or nuns, male or female lay practitioners, men or women, holds it even once will without fail attain accomplishment. I shall not give rise to any thought of doubt. Since all aims contemplated in the mind will be obtained, I have the very deepest confidence, and I shall not raise any doubt.

Then the Blessed One praised the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara, saying: Excellent! Excellent! With great compassion you have fully explained this wondrous ritual of the dhāraṇī of the Wish-fulfilling Wheel, bringing benefit and happiness to all sentient beings of Jambudvīpa.

Whoever arouses the aspiration and recites it with the mouth will obtain the spiritual attainment. Even so, in accordance with what I have taught, for the sake of sentient beings you should again and again instruct them with earnest diligence, and — in order that they may obtain the attainment — present the practice in embodied form. Do not contradict my words, and I too rejoice in this.

Then the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara addressed the Blessed One in these words:

Blessed One, since time without measure I have, with a mind of loving-kindness and tenderness, been guiding sentient beings, perpetually protecting them and granting them attainments — this the Blessed One himself knows and has authority over. It is for the sake of sentient beings that this dhāraṇī of the Wish-fulfilling Wheel has been taught. Therefore, whoever receives and holds it, and who completes the continuous count of personal recitation, will have their aspirations fulfilled. In just this way I, through the power of the Blessed One's blessing, deliver all suffering sentient beings completely from their suffering.

At that moment, when the bodhisattva, the great being Avalokiteśvara, had spoken this dhāraṇī of the Wish-fulfilling Wheel, the entire vast assembled retinue was filled with great joy, and with deep confidence they received it fully and put it into practice.

This is the sixth chapter, on the ritual procedure of fire offering (homa) and the compounding of medicinal substances, from the dhāraṇī of the Wish-fulfilling Wheel of the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara.

The dhāraṇī called "The Heart of the Wish-fulfilling Wheel, Unobstructed, the Secret Treasury of the noble bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara" is complete.

Translated and edited from the Chinese exemplar by the great revision abbot and translator, the monk Chögrup, who followed the tradition of the Blessed One, and the text was thereby established and settled.

Translator's notes
