About this text

Toh 742 in the Kangyur is a short ritual text known in Sanskrit as the Agra-vidyā-mantra and in Tibetan as the Vidyā Mantra of Supreme Attainment, concerned with the invocation and praise of the Blessed Goddess Śaṅkhinī, a wrathful or protective feminine deity associated with worldly benefits such as wealth, longevity, power, and the pacification of enemies and obstacles. The text belongs to the category of dhāraṇī or mantra literature within the Kangyur and prescribes the recitation of the mantra one hundred and eight times in a spirit of loving-kindness, following veneration of the Three Jewels. It was translated from Sanskrit into Tibetan by the Indian preceptor Jinamitra together with the great revision translator and monk Yeshe De, and it appears in the collection immediately following a related text, the Twelve Names of the Great Glorious Goddess, with which it shares thematic concern for the veneration of powerful goddess figures in the tantric Buddhist tradition.

Translation

The Twelve Names of the Great Glorious Goddess is complete.

Translated by the Indian preceptor Jinamitra and the great revision translator, the monk Yeshe De, who reviewed and established the final text.

In the Indian language: Agra-vidyā-mantra. In the Tibetan language: The Vidyā Mantra of Supreme Attainment.

Homage to the Blessed Goddess Śaṅkhinī.

She who brings full ordination to completion. She who causes splendor to increase. She who causes enjoyment and wealth to grow. She who causes power and lordship to expand. She who causes one's retinue to increase. She who causes long life to grow. She who pacifies enemies. She who pacifies obstacles.

Having offered worship to the Three Jewels and dwelling in a mind of loving-kindness, one should recite this one hundred and eight times in a single session. It is as follows:

Namaḥ sarva-tathāgatānāṃ. Tadyathā: Oṃ śaddhini devitā āgaccha āgaccha tiṣṭha dhane dhana-jaye brī brī bṛddhi-kari dhṛ dhṛ dhṛti-kārīṇāna vividyā-veśabasta āyur-dharaṇi yuyu āyuṣpālanita tathāgata smara bodhi-citta matīlaṃ bodara dehi me varna śadbini svāhā.

By this very virtue, may I behold this very emanation body. May I attain patient acceptance of emptiness. May I see the meaning of emptiness. May emptiness accomplish its purpose for me. By reciting this, all obstacles and interruptions shall be entirely dispelled.

The noble text called The Vidyā Mantra of Supreme Attainment is complete.

Translator's notes

The mantra body is transliterated rather than translated, as is standard. The Tibetan transcription of the Sanskrit mantra syllables is irregular in places, reflecting the phonological conventions of the translation period; the reconstruction offered here follows the most plausible reading but some syllable boundaries remain uncertain, which is normal for dhāraṇī texts of this kind.

The phrase rendered "patient acceptance of emptiness" translates the technical term for tolerance or forbearance with regard to emptiness, which in Madhyamaka and Prajñāpāramitā literature refers specifically to the realization of emptiness at the level of the path of preparation or, in some contexts, the path of seeing — the capacity to rest without fear in the recognition that all phenomena lack inherent existence.
