About this text

Toh 193 in the Kangyur is a Mahāyāna sūtra known in Sanskrit as the Ārya-Śrī-Yamahādevī-vyākaraṇa and in Tibetan as the Pakpa Lhamo Chenmo Pal Lungtenpа, rendered in English as The Noble Prophecy of the Great Goddess Śrī. It belongs to the class of vyākaraṇa, or prophecy texts, in which a buddha foretells the future spiritual attainments or destiny of a particular being — in this case the great goddess Śrī, a prominent divine figure in both Hindu and Buddhist traditions. The sūtra is set in the realm of Bliss, the pure land of Amitābha, where the Blessed One addresses a vast assembly of celebrated bodhisattvas including Avalokiteśvara, Vajrapāṇi, Kṣitigarbha, and Samantabhadra, among others. As a canonical Mahāyāna sūtra, it sits within the Kangyur, the Tibetan Buddhist collection of texts regarded as the word of the Buddha.

Here ends the Sūtra of the Great Vehicle.

In the language of India: Ārya-Śrī-Yamahādevī-vyākaraṇa. In the language of Tibet: The Noble Prophecy of the Great Goddess Śrī.

Homage to all buddhas and bodhisattvas.

Thus did I hear. At one time, the Blessed One was in the realm of Bliss, together with a great assembly of bodhisattvas, namely: the great bodhisattva, the noble Avalokiteśvara; the great bodhisattva Mahāsthāmaprāpta, He Who Has Attained Great Power; the great bodhisattva Sarvanīvaraṇaviṣkambhin, He Who Removes All Obscurations; the great bodhisattva Kṣitigarbha; the great bodhisattva Samantabhadra; the great bodhisattva Ākāśagarbha, Essence of Space; the great bodhisattva Vajrapāṇi; the great bodhisattva He Who Dispels All Fears; likewise the great bodhisattva He Who Holds All the Auspiciousness of Joy; the great bodhisattva He Who Holds All the Signs of Merit; the great bodhisattva He Who Holds the Sun, the Moon, and the Three Worlds; the great bodhisattva He Who Holds All the Auspiciousness of the Non-Buddhists; the great bodhisattva Mañjuśrī, Ever Youthful; and together with all the other great bodhisattvas of the Fortunate Aeon — all of them were present there.

Then the great bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara went to where the Blessed One was, bowed at the feet of the Blessed One, touching his head to the ground, and sat to one side.

The Great Goddess Śrī also went before the Blessed One, bowed at his feet, touching her head to the ground, circumambulated him three times, bowed her head in homage to all the great bodhisattvas dwelling in the realm of Bliss, and sat to one side.

Then the Blessed One, adorned with many hundreds of thousands of merits and surrounded by as many Tathāgatas as there are ten millions, praised and extolled by Śakra (Indra, lord of the gods), Brahmā, and all the world-protectors, looked upon the Great Goddess Śrī and, in the voice of the Great Brahmā, spoke these words to the great bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara:

Avalokiteśvara, whoever among kings or ministers, monks or nuns, laymen or laywomen, brahmins, the warrior caste, the merchant caste, or the common people carries this hymn of praise called "The Hundred and Eight Unstained Renowned Names of the Great Goddess Śrī" — in the land of that king of the warrior caste, all terrors for those sentient beings, epidemic diseases, and every form of harm will be pacified. No dangers whatsoever from thieves, deceivers, or from humans or nonhumans will arise. Wealth, grain, storehouses, and all treasuries will increase and flourish. And in the household of that king of the warrior caste, good fortune will assuredly come to dwell.

Then those great bodhisattvas spoke these words: Blessed One, these words you have so well declared — Excellent! Excellent! Whatever qualities and benefits accrue to those who hold the names of the Great Goddess Śrī, who hear them and take them into practice, those benefits will come to be for them all.

Then the great bodhisattva, the noble Avalokiteśvara, spoke these words to the Blessed One: Blessed One, where did the Great Goddess Śrī plant her roots of virtue?

The Blessed One declared: The Great Goddess Śrī planted roots of virtue with as many Tathāgatas as there are grains of sand in the river Ganges. In a past era, in a world realm called Arisen from Jewels, there appeared in the world a Tathāgata named Jewel-Flower Ocean of Qualities, Splendor of Vaiḍūrya and Golden Mountain, Radiant with the Beauty of Golden Light. And with many other Tathāgatas beyond him, the Great Goddess Śrī likewise planted roots of virtue.

These names of Tathāgatas are themselves what cause the roots of virtue of the Great Goddess Śrī to increase and be fulfilled, and they perpetually follow in her wake. Here in this place they serve to: fully declare the Great Goddess Śrī's praises; dispel all evil; eliminate all faults and transgressions; render every body free from stain; gather and multiply wealth and grain; cut off poverty; summon and attract all gods, nāgas, yakṣas, gandharvas (fragrance-eaters), asuras, garuḍas, kiṃnaras (half-humans), and great mahoragas (great serpents); pacify all epidemic disease, harm, conflict, disturbance, dispersal, and dispute; and bring the six perfections (pāramitās) to completion.

The Great Goddess Śrī was also prophesied by all the Tathāgatas. They declared: "Great Goddess Śrī, in a future time, in a world-realm called Adorned with the Precious Splendor, you will become a Tathāgata, arhat, perfectly and fully awakened buddha named Splendor Arisen from the Precious Jewel. Translator's notes

The opening line "Here ends the Sūtra of the Great Vehicle" is a colophon marker that appears to belong to a preceding text, with the present sūtra title beginning immediately after. This is a standard scribal convention in Tibetan canonical collections where texts are copied sequentially.

The bodhisattva rendered "He Who Holds All the Auspiciousness of the Non-Buddhists" (mu stegs kyi bkra shis thams cad 'dzin pa) is a puzzling epithet. It likely refers to a bodhisattva who subsumes or encompasses all the blessings attributed to non-Buddhist traditions, asserting Buddhist supremacy over them; the exact identity of this figure is not established in standard enumerations. The epithet rendered "Tathāgata of the Shores of the Ganges's Fords" translates a name built around "gaṅgā-tīrtha," literally the fords or crossing-places of the Ganges, which in the Indian religious context denotes the sacred bathing and pilgrimage sites along the river; the full name suggests auspiciousness surpassing all such sacred sites of the non-Buddhists. The rendering "Son of Non-Craving" for "Sred med kyi bu" reflects the standard Tibetan epithet for the bodhisattva who later becomes the buddha of that name, and is retained as a proper name rather than a descriptive phrase. The epithet "Sovereign of Fearlessness, Avalokiteśvara" applied to the Great Goddess Śrī is doctrinally significant: this text identifies Śrī-devī as a manifestation or aspect of Avalokiteśvara, a standard Tibetan Buddhist identification. The rendering follows the Tibetan syntax directly.

The term "garuḍas" renders ནམ་མཁའ་ལྡིང (literally "sky-soaring"), the standard Tibetan equivalent of Skt. garuḍa; "kinnaras" renders མིའམ་ཅི (literally "is it a human?"), and "mahoragas" renders ལྟོ་འཕྱེ་ཆེན་པོ (literally "great belly-crawlers").

"Mama" in the dhāraṇī invocation is left as the Tibetan phonetic rendering of a Sanskrit seed-syllable or invocative term within the mantra sequence and is not translated, as it functions as a mantra element. The passage is a litany of epithets followed by mantric dhāraṇī syllables, then a closing eulogy. The Sanskrit mantra strings have been rendered in reconstructed normalized Sanskrit; the Tibetan phonetic transcription is at times irregular, and the normalization follows standard identification of the deities and terms involved. The term གྲུལ་བུམ (kumbhāṇḍa) denotes a class of spirits with gourd-shaped bodies. The closing sentence breaks off mid-clause at དཔལ་དང། — the passage ends incompletely here, continuing in the next segment.
