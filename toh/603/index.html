About this text

Toh 603 in the Kangyur is a dhāraṇī text known in English as "The Dhāraṇī Called Supreme Compassion," in Sanskrit as the Karuṇāgra-nāma-dhāraṇī, and in Tibetan as Snying rje mchog ces bya ba'i gzungs. It belongs to the class of dhāraṇī literature, presenting a sacred mantra together with its accompanying liturgical framework and the spiritual and healing benefits conferred by its recitation and ritual use. The text is devoted to Avalokiteśvara, the bodhisattva of compassion described as the Lotus-Bearer who rouses beings mired in suffering, and it promises benefits ranging from the recollection of past births to the curing of leprosy in all its forms. As a dhāraṇī sūtra, it sits within the broader collection of esoteric and quasi-esoteric scriptures preserved in the Kangyur, and it opens by paying homage to the omniscient one before proceeding to reveal what it calls the supreme secret mantra of the Tathāgata.

Homage to the omniscient one.

Homage to the Lotus-Bearer (Avalokiteśvara), who holds the lotus in his hand. Homage to the arising intelligence, who perfectly rouses those sentient beings who have sunk into the dense thicket of manifold sufferings. I shall now set forth the supreme secret mantra of the Tathāgata — the arising of the pinnacle of great compassion — the siddhi of unobstructed and purposeful activity that has come forth from the mouth of the Tathāgata itself, which pacifies the karmic obscurations of the entire maṇḍala of sentient beings and the manifold sufferings of the lower states of existence, which utterly pacifies all inauspiciousness, bad omens, evil dreams, ill portents, the many kinds of disease, and the many kinds of untimely death, and which thoroughly crushes the great thousandfold world-system of negativity. I begin by first paying homage to the Blessed One, whose body is adorned with countless tens of millions of billions of accumulations of merit.

Namo Bhagavate Vairocana-Tathāgatāya Arhate Samyaksambuddhāya, sarva ajñāna dhakāra śamayitre. Tadyathā: sādhani sādhani, siddhi viddhāri, bhagavate paraṃpāra, kleśa dhavati, ajñāna śamanī śamāya, sarva pāpaṃ viśuddha dharme nirmala jñāna gocare, asaṃga cāriṇī praśama mūrti sukha prate, sarva kleśa dhavati, sarva pāpaṃ viśodhani, vidhyamāni vināśāni, vināśāni, vināśāya, vināśāya, bhagavati sura-sa vimala buddhi, sara sara, visara visara, suru suru, sūra sū, prasara prasara, visara visara, ajvana-makoṭi-sahasra-sañcitāna karma āpāraṇi kṣapaya, kṣema-vati buddhapiṣāya, vaśavartini pāramitā jñāna gocare, viśeṣavati, aśeṣa mālinī, kāraṇa gocare, mahā jñāna abhiṣeka vati, su-viniścita dharme, he he bhagavati, mahākaruṇe kuru kuru viśodhaya māṃ, sarva satvaṃś ca, mahākaruṇodakena sasiñcāya, samavadhāya, sambhalāya, prarohe me, mahākaruṇa vijana pariśuddhāya, amogha me kuru jīvita rakṣa rakṣa māṃ, bhagavati mama mahā bodhicittaṃ sammūhyed ma santu śuddha viti mi karuṇi, buddha bodhi prate, naraka preta tiryag-gati, duḥkhena mūlāni, deva-manuṣya-gati, saṃvahana vigato padhakleśe, sarva-buddha-dharma-paripūraṇi svāhā.

This dhāraṇī, called "The Supreme Compassion of the Blessed One," issued from the uṣṇīṣa of the Blessed One. It is attended by an immeasurable thousandfold retinue of dhāraṇīs. It destroys all misdeeds, karma, and afflictions. It pacifies all inauspiciousness, evil dreams, ill portents, and all forms of untimely death. It grants long life, merit, good form, strength, happiness, confidence, and freedom from illness. It utterly vanquishes all bhūtas (elemental spirits), forgetfulness-demons, yakṣas, rākṣasas, garuḍas, asuras, and all kinnarī spirits. Merely by holding, hearing, or uttering the name of this dhāraṇī, the karmic obscurations accumulated over a hundred thousand billion lifetimes will be purified. All afflictions will be burned away. All states of existence in the lower realms will be cut off for that person entirely.

That person will be embraced and upheld by all buddhas and bodhisattvas. They will come to be endowed with the virtues of a bodhisattva. They will come to possess qualities that never decline. They will never fall away from compassion. All sentient beings will come to rejoice in them.

If one recites this seven times in each of the three periods of the day and seven times in each of the three periods of the night, one will be freed from all wrongdoing, and one will come to obtain immeasurable and countless thousands of millions of bodhisattva qualities.

At dawn, having seated oneself comfortably in the cross-legged posture and formed the triangular mudrā with the hands, one directs one's attention to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas. Bringing to mind with clarity the Tathāgata as the great embodiment of all qualities, one says, "I prostrate to the Tathāgata," and having made mental prostration, if one then recites this twenty-one times, every wish and intention one holds in mind will be completely fulfilled, and one will attain accomplishment exactly as desired.

On the eighth, fourteenth, and fifteenth days of the month, one makes offerings to the Blessed One, takes the eight-fold precepts of the purification fast, dresses in white robes, makes acts of generosity, takes up the great awakening mind, and, with hands extended in an unimpeded gesture, draws a maṇḍala with one's own hands. One makes offerings of flowers of three different colors and burns white sandalwood incense. Then, mentally contemplating the various offerings and great honors fitting for the Tathāgatas throughout boundless thousands of world-realms, one presents them in thought to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas. If one then recites this great blazing quintessential dhāraṇī of the Tathāgata — the great tathāgatagarbha of the Tathāgata — until a feeling of great joy arises, one will know oneself to be dwelling in the very presence of the Tathāgatas, and will attain the joy of the Tathāgatas' great spiritual richness of Dharma, which is free from all material attachment. From that point forward, one will be born irreversibly within the lineage of the Tathāgatas. And in future lives, one will attain the great bodhisattva grounds. One will also attain the sovereignty of the great wheel-turning holder of the knowledge-mantra. One will attain dhāraṇī. One will please all the Tathāgatas. One will attain undiminished higher knowledge. In this very life, all sentient beings will come to rejoice in that person. All the gods will protect them. All nāgas, asuras, garuḍas, kinnaras, yakṣas, and rākṣasas will also protect them, and whatever such a person speaks, those beings will act to accomplish. Whatever that person intends and sets in motion, the gods and all the rest will carry out. All that person's wishes will be completely fulfilled. No one will be able to obstruct their speech. The gods will increase that person's strength, physical beauty, merit, youthful vigor, health, and prosperity. No obstacles whatsoever will be able to hinder them.

In brief, this is for those who wish to enter the city of the Tathāgatas, those who wish to behold the great body of a Tathāgata buddha, those who wish to obtain every excellence of qualities, those who also wish to enter into the practice of training as a great bodhisattva, those who wish to obtain the samaya of the great bodhisattva's nature of reality, those who wish to continuously and uninterruptedly, without any obstacle, realize the suchness of the great tantra,

those who wish to adorn themselves with the ornament of the Tathāgata's qualities — for all such persons, in order to accumulate the great assembly of qualities, this is praised by the Tathāgatas and properly relied upon by the bodhisattvas, the great beings.
