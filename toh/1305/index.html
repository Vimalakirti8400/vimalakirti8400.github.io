About this text

The Sādhana of Nairātmyā Yoginī, in Sanskrit Nairātmyayoginīsādhana and in Tibetan Dakmé Naljorma Druptap, is a short tantric meditation manual attributed to the Indian adept Ḍombi Heruka. It belongs to the Hevajra cycle. It teaches the practitioner to visualize the goddess Nairātmyā, "she who is selflessness", at the center of a circle of fifteen yoginīs, beginning with offerings, refuge, the mind of awakening and the meditation on emptiness. It goes on to the generation of the maṇḍala, the sealing of the deities, the symbolism of their ornaments, and the inner yoga of the subtle channels and great bliss. It is preserved as Toh 1305 in the Tengyur, among the commentaries on the tantras. It was translated into Tibetan by the Indian preceptor Gayādhara and the Tibetan monk Śākya Yeshé.

Translation

In the language of India: nairātmayoginisādhanaṃ. In the language of Tibet: The Sādhana of Nairātmyā Yoginī.

Homage to Vajrasattva.

By the master Ḍombi Heruka.

From the Hevajra Yoginī Tantra,
which perfectly gathers all of reality,
the meditation on the circle of goddesses is here taught.

In the sky, the venerable guru,
and, in a form like that of Akṣobhya,
the sovereign lord of vajra power should be visualized;
and the wise one bows down and makes offerings.

In the heart, imagine one's own seed syllable,
and present clouds of offerings.
Constantly, through the eight goddesses,
by the preparation of the five nectars:
the five yoginīs, Gaurī and the rest,
are said to hold the five nectars.
Blue Caṇḍālī and Ḍombinī,
together with the corpse, are joined in embrace.

Constantly confess one's misdeeds,
rejoice in merit,
go for refuge to the Three Jewels,
and fully take up the mind of awakening.

First, one should meditate on loving-kindness;
second, meditate on compassion;
third, one should meditate on joy;
and at the end of all, equanimity.

Then recite the mantra of the Victor:
oṃ śūnyatā jñāna vajra svabhāva ātmako'haṃ.
Having meditated on everything as by nature emptiness,
the yogin should then recollect the former aspiration.

First, from ra, meditate on a sun.
On that sun, from hūṃ, arises a crossed vajra,
and by means of that very vajra, one should meditate
and firmly bind the fence and the canopy.

Wisdom, which has the form of the letter e,
the wise one should imagine as the element of space.
Within it, a square ground,
green in color, should be meditated upon.
Dwelling within the earth maṇḍala,
one should meditate on the water maṇḍala.
Dwelling in the center of the water maṇḍala,
one should meditate on the fire maṇḍala.
Upon that is the circle of the wind maṇḍala.
Dwelling within the wind maṇḍala,
one should imagine oneself as well.
In evaṃ itself, oneself
being mingled with it: that is the circle.

It is square, with four doors,
adorned with eight pillars,
complete with nets and half nets,
and embellished with vajra lines.
In that circle, fifteen lifeless ones, corpses,
should be meditated upon.
In the directions and intermediate directions of the yoginīs,
the seats are said to be fifteen.
Upon the seats are moons.
Upon the moons, the discerning wise one
should imagine their seeds entering within:
the root vowels and so forth.

The mirror-like wisdom and the wisdom of equality
are well known as the moon and the sun.
The curved knife arising from aṃ
is said to be discriminating wisdom.
These made into one, diligently,
are the perfected, the pure dharmadhātu.
The bodies of the yoginīs
are known to have the nature of the five wisdoms.
The sun and moon together with the curved knife
mingle as water mingles with water.
Having mingled once again, afterward,
Nairātmyā Yoginī is generated:
black in color, of great renown,
adorned with the five mudrās.
In her right hand is a curved knife, greatly terrifying;
in her left is a lotus vessel;
on the side that is not the right is a khaṭvāṅga;
her lower body is wrapped in a tiger skin.

a ā i ī u are declared to be those of Nairātmyā Yoginī and the rest. ū ṛ ṝ ḷ are those of Pukkasī and the rest. ḹ e ai o: these seeds are declared to be those of Gaurī and the rest. au aṃ: these seeds are declared to be those of the ones dwelling below and above.

With Mind and Benefit, the Lord of Jewels,
Amitāyus and Amoghasiddhi,
seal Nairātmyā and the rest;
the wisdom consorts abide in their ranks.
With Mind and Benefit, the Lord of Jewels,
and the Sugata Amitāyus,
seal Pukkasī and the rest in the intermediate directions,
following their order.
The four yoginīs, constantly meditated upon
in their very places,
are sealed with the Aversion, Delusion, Avarice
and Attachment Vajras.
Below and above, by sealing
with body and speech, it is complete.
By this sealing and constancy,
then the twenty mudrās are declared.

In Indra's quarter, Vajrā; in Yama's, Gaurī;
in Varuṇa's, Vāriyoginī;
in Kubera's, Vajraḍākinī;
in the center, Nairātmyā Yoginī.
In Īśāna's quarter, Pukkasī;
likewise in Agni's, Śabarī;
in Nairṛti's, Caṇḍālī;
and in Vāyu's, she is declared to be Ḍombī.
Further, at the eastern gate is Gaurī;
likewise in the south, Caurī;
at the western gate, Vetālī;
likewise in the north, Ghasmarī.
Below, she is explained as Bhūcarī,
and above, she is declared to be Khecarī.
Just as Nairātmyā Yoginī is,
so all of them are in supreme delight.

In the heart, imagine one's own seed syllable.
Emitting rays of light, then
invite the buddhas,
and the eight goddesses should make offerings.
Afterward, the supplication is made:
"Blessed Ones, great beings,
all you Blessed Tathāgatas,
grant me empowerment," and so forth, one requests.
With a vase filled with the five nectars,
the wise one is empowered by the buddhas.
Having been empowered by all the buddhas,
upon the crown ornament is the Lord of the Mind.

First, one should meditate on black;
second, one should meditate on red;
third, meditate on yellow;
likewise fourth, on green;
fifth, the color blue;
sixth, that which has the aspect of white.
Meditating on the six limbs, the yogin
afterward completely abandons the colors.

In the aggregate of form is Vajrā;
in feeling, she is declared to be Gaurī;
perception is Vāriyoginī;
formations, Vajraḍākinī;
and by the nature of the aggregate of consciousness,
there is Nairātmyā Yoginī.
The mistress of the maṇḍala abides in terrifying form,
fearsome and causing fear.
Earth is explained as Pukkasī;
likewise the water element, Śabarī;
fire should be known as Caṇḍālī;
wind is well known as Ḍombī.
For form, she is always explained as Gaurī;
for sound, Caurī is well known;
in the domain of smell, Vetālī;
for taste, Ghasmarī is declared;
the tangible is explained as Bhūcarī;
and for the dharmadhātu, Khecarī.
Because these are always completely pure,
the yogin accomplishes reality itself.

The eyes are Delusion Vajrā;
in the ears, Aversion Vajrā;
in the nose, she is explained as Avarice;
in the mouth, Attachment Vajrā;
in the body, Envy Vajrā;
in the mind, Nairātmyā Yoginī.
These armors, being utterly pure,
are applied to the sense faculties.

She is encircled by a garland of skulls.
The curved knife is that which cuts off views;
the skull cup rests
upon the garland of heads that are the afflictions.

In order to bow to the guru, the master, the chosen deity,
the wheel is worn.
So as not to hear words of slander
against the guru, the vajra holder,
earrings are worn for right listening.
For the recitation of mantra, the necklace;
the bracelets, for abandoning the killing of living beings;
for relying on the consort, the girdle.
With the seal of the five buddhas
one should constantly seal the body.
The mantra is:
oṃ a ā i ī u ū ṛ ṝ ḷ ḹ e ai o au āṃ svāhā.

The purity of the mudrās shall now be explained.
By the form of the wheel, Akṣobhya;
Amitābha is lord of the earrings;
of the necklace, the Lord of Jewels;
on the hands, Vairocana is declared;
on the girdle abides Amoghasiddhi.
These are the five mudrās upon them.
The holding of what utterly destroys faults
is fully taken up by the yoginī.

I shall explain the inner yoga.
In female form she brings the world under her power;
in wrathful form she burns.
Through the union of vowels and consonants,
the outer ones are covered two by two.
The mantra for stirring up a city is:
oṃ a ka ca ṭa ta pa ya śa svāhā.
The mantra of the torma for all bhūtas is:
oṃ ākaro mukhaṃ sarvadharmāṇāṃ ādyānutpannatvāta oṃ āḥ hūṃ phaṭ svāhā.

I shall explain the divisions of the vow
of the yoginī Nairātmyā.
At the navel abides the Great Seal;
constantly great bliss drips down.
Because it is sharp, it is in the form of fire;
this too the mātṛkās have examined.
First it has the nature of the vowels;
in the lungs, the buddhas have examined it.
Though it abides pervading the three worlds,
ordinary beings do not know it.
Nairātmyā abides within the body.
The indestructible one, face turned downward,
uninterruptedly holds the mind of awakening
constantly, through union.
Therefore three channels abide in the body:
lalanā, rasanā and avadhūtī.
In the form of body, speech and mind,
they are joined as the action seal itself.
Lalanā is of the nature of wisdom,
and rasanā abides as the limb of method.
Avadhūtī, abiding in the center,
has abandoned grasped and grasper;
she has the form that brings about great bliss.
Lalanā is the flow of Akṣobhya;
rasanā is the flow of blood;
avadhūtī is the very substance
of the constant abode of the protector Amitābha.
The action seal is endowed with bliss.
In one's own body are three channels.
Through vajra, jewel and lotus,
body, speech and mind drip down.
At the time of their becoming one,
by the holder of the vajra jewel, it is spring itself.
It is declared to be the nature of the buddha,
the perfect awakening of the protector Amitābha.
By night and also by day,
wisdom constantly arises.
The connate, the supreme joy,
the excellent bliss of the father, flows down.

For example, whoever, inside a house at night,
wishes to eat food and do everything with the body
looks into the thick darkness by a kindled lamp;
just so, in the darkness of delusion there is bliss.
Know it thus: the ocean of existence is poisoned water,
pervaded by attachment and the rest, which drink it in;
tormented by hunger, coiled and tangled in waves,
a lone desert with a great shore.
Since a person wise in awakening, even for a moment,
pursues his own aim alone,
casting off the garment that is the noose of delusion,
and yet does not go to the far shore, I find this very wondrous.

The one of excellent mind first meditates on the generation stage,
and should never be parted from mindfulness
of the mudrās and of what benefits,
of the mantras, the purities, the curved knife and all the armors.
Afterward, the one of excellent mind, delighted,
with mind continuously one-pointed on that reality, moment by moment,
meditating on the completion stage,
should constantly contemplate the connate that arises from existence.

In the place named Rādha, of enjoyments, a market of many kinds,
called the House of Kaṅkha, endowed with bees,
riding a tiger, adorned with many raised serpent hoods,
whoever is endowed with patience, whatever the conduct.
Then, from the wish to benefit others, for the sake of the world,
this sovereign among sādhanas that I have explained,
a gathering of the limbs of secret wisdom:
whatever I have explained, children of the Victors, please forbear.

The Sādhana of Nectar Light is complete. It was translated by the Indian preceptor Gayādhara and the Tibetan translator, the monk Śākya Yeshé.

Translator's notes

The text names two different goddesses Gaurī: one rendered by sound (among the four inner goddesses with Vajrā, Vāriyoginī and Vajraḍākinī) and one rendered by translation ("the White One", among the eight goddesses of the outer ring, with Caurī, Vetālī and Ghasmarī). Both are Gaurī in Sanskrit. The figures called "Mind" and "Benefit" in the sealing verses are translated literally because I could not identify them with confidence; they seem to be buddhas of the families, with the Lord of Jewels probably Ratnasambhava. The line about the wisdom consorts abiding "in their ranks" is uncertain. "Twenty mudrās" follows the Tibetan, although the list after it gives fifteen goddesses. The phrase "rang gis bon" in the second heart-syllable verse is read as a slip for "own seed syllable", and "khal" in the list of sense organs is read as "kha", "mouth". "Five mudrās" means the five bone ornaments. "Lalanā, rasanā and avadhūtī" follows the evident sense of a garbled line that reads literally "rkyang mor ma kun 'dar ma". The stanza beginning "In the place named Rādha" seems to describe where the author lived and his conduct, but its wording is obscure. The place name and "the House of Kaṅkha" are given tentatively. The verses on the ocean of existence and the similes before them are also difficult, and they are translated as literally as the sense allows. "The flow of Akṣobhya" in lalanā presumably refers to the white element, semen, in contrast with the blood of rasanā. "Nectar Light" is a literal rendering of the title in the closing line.
