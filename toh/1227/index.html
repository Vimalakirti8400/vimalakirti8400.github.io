About this text

The Means of Accomplishing Glorious Hevajra, in Sanskrit Śrīhevajrasādhana and in Tibetan Pal Kyé Dorjé shejawé drubpé tap, is a concise sādhana, a practice manual for meditating on the tantric deity Hevajra, condensed from the Hevajra Tantra. It guides the practitioner from choosing a suitable place and making preliminary offerings, refuge and the four immeasurables, through meditation on emptiness and the generation of the celestial palace, to the visualization of sixteen-armed, eight-faced Hevajra in union with Nairātmyā, surrounded by the eight goddesses beginning with Gaurī, followed by empowerment, the symbolic purity of each feature of the deity, and mantra recitation. Its colophon attributes it to the master Bodhigarbha and records its translation by an Indian preceptor and a Tibetan royal translator. It is Toh 1227 in the Tengyur, where it is found among the commentaries and practice texts of the tantra section.

Translation

In the language of India: śrī hevajra nāma sādhanaṃ. In the language of Tibet: The Means of Accomplishing Glorious Hevajra.

Homage to Hevajra.

Lord abiding as evaṃ itself, the four protectors,
Free from beginning, end and middle,
Vajradhara, homage to you.

Having bowed with faithful conduct at the feet of the guru, the Buddha, the Dharma and the elders of the Saṅgha, I write this.

First, the place is to be taught:

At a solitary tree or in a charnel ground,
Likewise in the houses of the mātṛkās,
Or else in a solitary place or at the outskirts:
Meditation there is said to be excellent.

Since this is stated, one sits in such a place, or in a place that delights one's own mind, and makes one's yoga even in the meditative absorption of one-pointed mind. At night, dwelling in one's own heart: from raṃ, a sun disk; upon it the syllable hūṃ, whose nature is method and wisdom. Seeing the gurus, the buddhas and the bodhisattvas in the sky, one emanates eight goddesses adorned with ornaments and makes offerings: Gaurī holding a moon vessel, Caurī holding a sun vessel, Vetālī holding a vessel of urine, Ghasmarī holding a vessel of medicine, Pukkasī holding a vajra, Śavarī holding a vessel of nectar, and Caṇḍālī holding a small drum. Offerings are made by them. Ḍombinī brings about great passion and subsequent passion, inseparable from what is touched.

Then one performs the outer offerings, the confession of misdeeds, going for refuge in the Three Jewels, and so on. Then one cultivates, first, loving-kindness; second, compassion; third, joy; and fourth, equanimity.

Then one makes the mind into emptiness: one should be free of grasping saṃsāra as a self. oṃ śūnyatājñāna vajra svabhāva ātmako 'haṃ.

Then, from the syllable raṃ, one first meditates on a sun disk. Upon it, from the syllable hūṃ, a vajra; at its center, hūṃ. Light rays radiate from that and become the canopy, the ground and the walls. In their center one meditates on the source of the expanse of space; it is the wind maṇḍala, the fire maṇḍala, the water maṇḍala and the earth maṇḍala. On top of these, from bhrūṃ, a wheel, with bhrūṃ at its hub; from bhrūṃ and the wheel, the two inseparable, comes Vairocana. He and the maṇḍalas of the four elements mingle into one and become a celestial palace in the center of the source of phenomena. The celestial palace is single-storied, has four doors, is beautified by four archways, and has the form of various flowers.

In its center, from āli, a moon disk, and from kāli, a sun disk. The moon is mirror-like wisdom; the sun is the wisdom of equality; in their middle, from hūṃ, a vajra, the discriminating wisdom. Their nature, mingled into one, is the all-accomplishing wisdom; its transformation into the form of Vajrasattva is the wisdom of the utterly pure dharmadhātu. Thus, from the mingled syllable hūṃ, the body of that Vajrasattva is generated through the five full awakenings.

His faces and hand emblems correspond, as of father and son. Vajrasattva has the form of a precious moon, with sixteen arms, eight faces and four legs; with his first two arms he embraces Nairātmyā, giving her delight. The awakening mind, the nonduality of method and wisdom, is the moon.

Gaurī abides in the direction of Śakra, Caurī in the direction of the pretas, Vetālī in the direction of the lord of waters, and Ghasmarī in the direction of the yakṣas, in the manner of conquering the four māras. With snarling forms they stand at the doors. Pukkasī, in a conquering form, abides in the corner of Īśāna, Śavarī in the corner of fire, Caṇḍālī in the corner of Nairṛti, and Ḍombinī in the corner of the wind. Once their bodies are completed from the seeds gaṃ caṃ baṃ ghaṃ baṃ saṃ laṃ ḍaṃ, one should send them forth.

Gaurī holds a curved knife in her right hand and a fish in her left. Caurī holds a small drum in her right and a boar in her left. Ghasmarī holds a snake in her right and a lotus vessel in her left. Pukkasī holds a lion in her right and an axe in her left. Śavarī holds a monk in her right and a staff in her left. Caṇḍālī holds a wheel in her right and a skull cup in her left. Ḍombinī holds a vajra in her right and makes the threatening gesture with her left.

Gaurī is black in color, Caurī red, Vetālī yellow, Ghasmarī green, Pukkasī blue, Śavarī white, Caṇḍālī the color of the sky, and Ḍombinī of many colors. All are three-eyed, with hair hanging loose, endowed with the four mudrās; all are standing, poised with one leg extended and one bent, and all are two-armed. Brahmā, Viṣṇu, Rudra (Śiva), Yama, the yakṣa, the rākṣasa and the asura are the seats of Gaurī and the others.

Then Vajrasattva together with his consort melts through great passion. Then he is roused by the four goddesses:

They worship him with various songs.
"O mind of compassion of Hevajra,
Protect me, Pukkasī!
Desiring union in great bliss,
Dispel your nature of emptiness and arise!"

"Having dispelled your nature of emptiness,
Grant accomplishment to Śavarī!
If you wish me to go on living,
Hevajra, I pray you, arise!"

"I, Caṇḍālī, not seeing you,
Make this request:
Why do you abide in emptiness?
As in your former vow, O hero, take your pleasure!"

"I am Ḍombī, without any sign;
Your mind is knowing.
To be without compassion is not right.
I pray you, arise like an illusion!"

First, the awakening mind of emptiness;
In the second, the seed arises;
In the third, the form is completed;
In the fourth, the syllables are set in place.
The lord and the lady of joy, the two seeds;
In between, the two terrifying blue ones.
From the light rays of the two, aṃ and hūṃ,
The great Vajra arises.
Radiating out in the forms of deities,
They completely fill the realm of space.
Gathered back into aṃ and hūṃ in the heart,
The yogin, as Vajradhara,
Takes on the nature of great aversion.
Pressing the earth with his four feet,
He terrifies gods and asuras.
Black in color, he terrifies.
He gives bliss to Nairātmyā,
And finds the bliss free of conceptual thought.
His form has the nature free of conceptual thought.
He tramples the four māras in turn with his feet;
He terrifies all that is terrifying.
His garland of heads should be made complete.
He dances upon the sun seat;
A crossed vajra rests upon his crown.
Black in color, he terrifies.
His first face is deep black;
His right face is white like the moon;
His left face is red and terrifying;
The blue face on top bares its fangs.
He is endowed with twenty-four eyes.
His other faces are black.

He utters the sound hūṃ,
And his body is smeared with ashes.
His eyebrows and streaming hair are yellow;
His body is adorned with the five mudrās.
His eyes gaze with a wrathful look;
In age he is about sixteen.
Elephant, horse and donkey,
Ox, camel and human,
Cat and lion:
In his right hands are eight skull cups,
And these are to be placed in order.
The earth goddess Pṛthivī and the water god Varuṇa,
The fire god Agni and the wind god Vāyu,
The moon god Candra and the sun god Sūrya,
The god Yama and Kubera:
In his left hands are eight skull bowls,
And these are to be set out in order.
Graceful, heroic, terrifying,
Laughing, fierce and frightening,
Compassionate, awe-inspiring, peaceful and the rest:
He is endowed with the flavors of the nine blazing dances.

śrī is nondual wisdom;
he is free of causes and the rest;
ru is free of aggregation;
ka is not abiding anywhere at all.

oṃ dharmadhātu svabhāva ātmako 'haṃ.

On the two eyes is the Vajra of Delusion; on the ears, the Vajra of Aversion; on the nose, the Vajra of Avarice; on the mouth, the Vajra of Attachment; on the sense of touch, the Vajra of Envy; on the mind, the yoginī Nairātmyā. The earth-goer is the Vajra Lady of Body; the sky-goer is the Vajra Lady of Speech; she who dwells in the center of the heart is Nairātmyā.

From the seed in one's own heart, black light like a hook radiates out, and with it one invites the buddhas of the three realms. Offerings are made to them by the eight goddesses. Then one should make supplication: oṃ abhiṣiñcatu māṃ sarva vajra tathāgata.

Then, all being complete as Heruka,
All the buddhas confer empowerment;
With water, the nature of all buddhas,
They empower with vases of nectar.

Then rains of flowers, of cloth, of sandalwood powder and of saffron fall, and the realm of space is filled with various sounds. Offerings are made with various forms, and Locanā and the other goddesses Also sing praise with vajra songs.

Once the empowerment has been conferred in this way, one should recollect: Akṣobhya has the form of the crown wheel; the earrings have the nature of Amitābha; at the neck is Ratnasambhava; the girdle is Amoghasiddhi; the bracelets have the nature of Vairocana.

The purity of the five mudrās is the five families. The purity of compassion is the red eyes. The purity of loving-kindness is the black color. The purity of the factors of awakening is the hide. The purity of the four noble truths is the bones. The purity of the mudrās is the five buddhas. His form is wrathful in order to tame vicious beings. The purity of flesh is Pukkasī; the purity of blood is Śavarī; the purity of the awakening essence is Caṇḍālī; the purity of marrow is Ḍombinī. The purity of the eight liberations is the eight faces. The purity of body, speech and mind is the three eyes. The purity of the sixteen emptinesses is the sixteen arms. The purity of the four māras is the four legs. The tathāgatas Akṣobhya, Vairocana, Ratnasambhava and Amitābha, and Gaurī and the rest, are the four mudrās. The purity of earth is Pukkasī; the purity of water is Śavarī; the purity of fire is Caṇḍālī; and the purity of wind is said to be Ḍombinī.

Having risen from abiding in the three times, one should assume the form of the deity. If there are no afflictions, one should do the mantra recitation: oṃ deva picu vajra hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ svāhā.

All activities are to be done in the form of Heruka; whatever accomplishment one desires, one should do it in the form of Heruka. If one remains so for six months, accomplishment will arise; if there is no deluded thought, one will attain the miraculous powers of a buddha.

Every movement is mudrā;
Every utterance is mantra;
The abiding of Heruka
Is the true knowing of consciousness.

This is the sādhana condensed from the tantra.

Since neither the learned nor the unlearned can fathom it,
This illuminates the profound way.
From the ocean of the milk of wisdom,
With the churning stick of instruction, in an instant,
Through the master's instruction on bliss,
It is condensed, like eating nectar:
This is the understanding of his mind.
This sādhana of Heruka,
Made well and clear,
Was composed from the Hevajra Tantra,
Following the sequence in which it is taught there.
By this merit of mine,
From setting out the stages of the glorious mantra,
By this virtue, until awakening is reached,
May sentient beings swiftly be liberated.
Gurus, parents,
And the people around me rejoice to hear it.
This, made by Bodhigarbha,
With the wisdom of what was recollected before,
By this expression of suchness,
May the glorious mind be found.

The Hevajra Sādhana composed by the master Bodhigarbha is complete. It was translated and finalized by the Indian preceptor Dīpaṃkaracandra and the Tibetan translator Lha Tsenpo Munirāja.

Translator's notes

"Corresponding, as of father and son" translates the Tibetan as written; it may be a slip for "father and mother," that is, the deity and his consort. In the list of hand emblems Vetālī is not mentioned, though she appears in the lists before and after it. "A staff" renders an obscure word for Śavarī's left-hand emblem; the Hevajra tradition elsewhere gives her a monk's ringing staff, and the reading is uncertain. "The lord and the lady of joy" and "the two terrifying blue ones" are rendered literally; their reference within the generation sequence is not fully clear. "Akṣobhya" reads the Tibetan "unborn" as an evident slip for the similar-looking name Akṣobhya, which fits the list of the five tathāgatas as ornaments. "Skull bowls" renders the loanword bandha, used in Tibetan for a skull vessel. "The awakening essence," in the list of bodily constituents, is bodhi in the sense of the seminal awakening mind. The line rendered "by this virtue, until awakening is reached" is obscure in the Tibetan and the rendering is tentative. The line "This, made by Bodhigarbha" also plays on the author's name, which means "heart of awakening." The Indian preceptor's name is spelled irregularly in the Tibetan and is given as Dīpaṃkaracandra.
