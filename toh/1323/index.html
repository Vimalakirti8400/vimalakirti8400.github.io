About this text

The Ten Realities, in Sanskrit Daśatattva and in Tibetan Dekhona Nyi Chupa, is a short practical manual of tantric ritual presenting in brief the ten realities, or ten essential procedures, that a vajra master must know according to the Hevajra tantra tradition. After homage to Mañjuśrī, Heruka and the guru, it explains in turn the protection wheel of the ten wrathful deities, the empowerments, the torma offering, mantra recitation, the fierce rite of separation, the averting rites performed through meditation and through a drawn protective wheel, the accomplishment of the maṇḍala, the rite of threatening, and the request for the deities to depart. Its colophon presents it as pith instructions of the Ḍākinī Vajrapañjara, composed by the great master Vimalakīrti and translated into Tibetan by Marpa Chökyi Lodrö. It is Toh 1323 in the Tengyur, where it belongs among the commentaries and ritual texts on the tantras.

Translation

In the language of India: da śa tattvā. In the language of Tibet: The Ten Realities.

Homage to the bodhisattva Mañjuśrī, who has become a youth.

Having bowed with reverence to Heruka, the glorious protector,
and to the guru,
I shall explain in brief the ten realities
taught in the Hevajra.
Protection, empowerment, torma, recitation and dance,
the rites of averting, the accomplishing of the maṇḍala,
the purifying of wrongdoing, and the request to depart:
these the buddhas have called the ten realities.

Their meaning is this. What is called the protection wheel is as follows. On a variegated lotus arisen from the yellow seed, the letter paṃ, is a sun arisen from the letter a, and on it the yellow seed bhrūṃ, which transforms completely into a ten-spoked wheel. On the sun disk resting on the hub at its center, blue hūṃ transforms completely, and from it comes Vajrahūṃkāra. Then, according to the rite, ten seed letters rest on the ten spokes, and from them the ten wrathful ones are generated.

On the eastern spoke is Yamāntaka, blue, holding a mallet. On the southern spoke is Prajñāntaka, holding a white club. On the western spoke is Padmāntaka, holding a red lotus. On the northern spoke is Vighnāntaka, holding a black vajra. On the spoke of Īśāna, the northeast, is Ṭakkirāja, black, holding a hook. On the spoke of Agni, the southeast, is Nīladaṇḍa, holding a black staff in his hand. On the spoke of Nairṛti, the southwest, is Mahābala, holding a trident in his hand. On the spoke of Vāyu, the northwest, is Acala, holding a black sword. On the upper spoke is Uṣṇīṣacakravartin, wearing a crown of Vairocana and holding a white wheel. On the lower spoke is Sumbharāja, wearing a crown of Akṣobhya and holding a black vajra in his hand. Vajrahūṃkāra has his head adorned with Akṣobhya and holds a black vajra.

The four wrathful ones of the east and the other cardinal directions wear crowns of Vairocana, Akṣobhya, Amitābha and Amoghasiddhi, in that order. In the same sequence, Ṭakkirāja of the northeast and the others, that is, Ṭakkirāja, Nīladaṇḍa, Mahābala and Acala, wear crowns of Akṣobhya, Vairocana, Amitābha and Amoghasiddhi.

All the wrathful ones have one face and two arms. The fingers of the left hand make the threatening gesture, and the right hand holds each one's own emblem. Their mouths gape in wrath, their round eyes are red, and their yellow hair stands on end. Their wrathful forms threaten the māras; they sit upon suns and shine with the light of a sun disk. Yamāntaka, Prajñāntaka, Padmāntaka and Vighnāntaka are stout, with bulging bellies, adorned with snakes, and their yellow eyebrows bristle upward, while the others have bodies of graceful color. This is the first reality.

Now the empowerment shall be described. In due order, one makes the maṇḍala in the maṇḍala house and accomplishes the rite of entering the maṇḍala and the maṇḍala itself. Then, to the disciple who has not the slightest intoxication, pride or arrogance, who looks on with deep faith, and who has been empowered with the seven, namely water, crown, vajra, lord, name and the rest, one should give the secret empowerment according to the rite.

After that, for the rite of the wisdom-gnosis empowerment: to one who has obtained the seven, who has obtained the secret empowerment, whose mind greatly delights in wisdom-gnosis, and who has been shown, by its sign, the bliss of union with the wisdom consort, one should entrust the wisdom consort, as taught in the scriptures, through the rite of rightly entrusting the wisdom consort. The sign of bliss that comes from receiving the right entrusting of wisdom-gnosis is the wisdom-gnosis empowerment. Then what is to be understood further on, through the teaching of the holy guru, is likewise empowerment. This is the second reality.

Now the third reality, the torma, shall be explained. By reciting the secret mantra hūṃ one takes on the form of glorious Heruka, and imagines in front the place that is guarded on every side, and the maṇḍala. One imagines a red lotus with eight petals set in the middle of three sources of phenomena, stacked one above the other. Upon it one invites Gaurī and the others, and, dwelling in countless worlds, Viṣṇu, the Great God (Śiva), Maheśvara, the sun, the moon and the others, all the classes of beings that bring ruin to the infinite worlds, every one of them together with their retinues. From the complete transformation of the seed letter aṃ they are two-armed, holding vajra and bell, white in color, their bodies embraced by their own consorts, seated with the right leg extended, blessed by the four branches, crowned with Vairocana, and served with welcoming water, drinks and the rest. One should meditate on them with a mind free of attachment to food.

In the middle, the guardians of the directions, placed on the petals that lie in their own directions, arise from the complete transformation of the seed letter hūṃ; Mahākāla wears a crown of Akṣobhya. Below are Karkoṭaka, Ananta, Vāsuki, Takṣaka, Kulika, Śaṅkhapāla, Padma and Mahāpadma, placed on the petals of their own directions, together with the asuras. From the complete transformation of the seed letter a they are red in color and crowned with Amitābha; the rest is as before. After that, the food and drink, the nectar and the rest, should be accomplished as nectar itself.

The sequence for this is as follows. From yaṃ comes a wind maṇḍala, smoke-colored and shaped like a bow. Above it is a red fire maṇḍala arisen from raṃ. Above that, arisen from the complete transformation of the letter a, is a fine lotus vessel filled with food and the rest, blessed by the five seed syllables, and melted by the blazing and the heat. Above it the seed letter oṃ transforms completely into a moon, which melts completely and is made into nectar. Together with perfume, flowers, incense, banners, parasols, garments and the rest, and drinks and the rest, the master, turned toward the ringing of the bell, should give the torma with oṃ inda yama jala yakṣa and the rest of that mantra. Then one makes offering through Gaurī and the others, who arise from the seed syllable at the heart of the wisdom being in one's own heart, holding various offerings in their hands, and so forth. After that one should praise them with the verses beginning "Akṣobhya, vajra wisdom."

Immediately after the praise, one should request them to depart with the mantra oṃ vajra satvasmaya manupālaya vajrasatva tveno patiṣṭha dṛḍho ma bheva su poṣdyo me bhava su toṣdyo me bhava anurakto me bhava sarva siddhimme prayaccha sarva karma su ca me cittaṃ śreyaḥ kuru hūṃ ha ha ha ha hoḥ bhagavān sarva tathāgata vajra mā me muñca vajrī bhava mahā samaya satva aḥ muḥ.

Now the torma will be taught in condensed form. The yogin of the deity, intent on ringing the bell, summons in due order, through Gaurī and the others, the reality of the deities mentioned above onto the red lotus in the center of the triangular source of phenomena, and in a single moment satisfies them with the torma accomplished earlier and with the mantras of Indra and the others; the rest is as before. This is the condensed torma. This is the third reality, the reality of the torma.

Now recitation will be explained. The yogin, possessing the yoga of his own deity preceded by the three meditative absorptions, immediately after meditating on the innate, recollects his own deity. In his own heart is the wisdom being, and at its heart, on a sun, rests the seed syllable hūṃ. Light rays arising from it remove the stains belonging to the body and speech of all sentient beings and then return and enter oneself; then one recites the mantra of one's own chosen deity. Again, one should view it in the same way. Having recited the secret mantra and made offerings and the rest, one should rise. This is the fourth reality, the reality of mantra recitation.

Separation will also be described. Suppose a yogin who has direct knowledge of the virtuous and nonvirtuous karma of others sees someone harming the teaching, deceiving the guru and the buddhas, or bent on killing his father and mother. With a mind endowed with compassion he earnestly tries to win that person over by giving, by drawing him close, and the like. Even though he himself does nothing evil, he should likewise strive again and again for the sake of the one who does such deeds. If that person does not heed his words, then, out of loving concern, he should perform the fierce rite taught in the glorious Hevajra.

The sequence for this is as follows. Between sessions, joined with the practice of meditative absorption, light radiates from the seed syllable at the heart of the wisdom being. One invites countless buddhas, summoning them from their natural abodes, and, before their eyes, looking at the target who does such deeds, one should proclaim it to the buddhas. After that one invites the deities of the maṇḍala and performs offering, praise and the tasting of nectar. With the light rays of the seed syllable at the heart of the wisdom being one invites the principal deity of one's own wheel, who enters the lotus by the vajra path, and one sees him emerge in the form of Gaurī holding a noose, a hook and a mallet. Gaurī emanates forms of her own light rays, which strip away the target's mantras, mudrās and the like, bind him with the noose, draw him with the hook, and summon him before the wheel. Then the yogin earnestly addresses that wisdom being:

"Through the power of my own nature,
and through attachment to the nature of Akṣobhya,
you must abandon this body,
the source of all contemptible deeds."

Having so addressed it,
Gaurī, with hook and noose,
draws out that wisdom being;
and with the hook in Gaurī's hand,
from the root of the target's navel,
all the entrails are drawn out.
Then at once the ascetic
should apply this to the fierce rite.

Again, one makes the form of an animal, naked and upright, vomiting blood from its mouth, trembling and without refuge, smeared with sesame oil, unclothed, its hair standing on end. One imagines a thing in the form of a fiery needle driven into its anus, imagines the seed syllable of fire at its heart, and at once one should liberate it. Then one accomplishes the wisdom being as Akṣobhya, performs offering, praise and the tasting of nectar according to the stages of the rite, and should see it in the buddha field of Akṣobhya. This is the fifth reality, the reality of separation.

Now averting through meditation will be taught. One imagines the principal deity Uṣṇīṣacakravartin, two-armed, with his own emblem and the threatening forefinger, wrathful, seated on a sun seat with the left leg extended, in wrathful form, threatening the hosts of enemies to empowerment and the rest, while the wheel spins to the left. Then one recites namaḥ samanta buddhānāṃ namaḥ samanta dharmānāṃ namaḥ samanta saṃghānāṃ oṃ sitātapatre oṃ vimala śaṃkara oṃ pratyadhira vajra uṣṇīṣa cakravarti sarva yantra manta mantā mūla karma bandhanaṃ tāḍanaṃ kīlanāṃ vā mama kṛte yena kena cita kṛtanta ta sarvāndu cchndi cchndi bhinda bhinda ciri ciri giri giri mara mara hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ phaṭ phaṭ. At the time of going to sleep one should recite it seven times and meditate. This is the sixth reality, the reality of averting through meditation.

On birch bark or the like one draws a ten-spoked wheel. On the first spoke one writes namaḥ samanta buddhebhyo namaḥ mudgara hastāya taṇḍa maṇḍal ā kṣepa karaya so-and-so rakṣa kuru hūṃ phaṭ. On the southern spoke: namaḥ samanta buddhebhyo mahā daṇḍa dhara pibi surāsura mardana bhūtaṃ nirmatha so-and-so rakṣa kuru hūṃ phaṭ. On the western spoke: namaḥ smanta buddhebhyo hulu hulu mahā padma amṛte kuru biṣaṃ so-and-so mama rakṣa kuru hūṃ phaṭ. On the northern spoke: namaḥ samanta buddhebhyo namo vajra krodhaya mahā daṃṣṭotkaṭa bhairabāya vajra santoha darbāya oṃ amṛta kuṇḍala so-and-so rakṣa kuru hūṃ phaṭ. On the spoke of Īśāna: namaḥ samanta buddhebhyo oṃ yama daṇḍāya nīla daṇḍāya kuru kuru muru muru sarva duṣṭān māraya māraya so-and-so rakṣa kuru hūṃ phaṭ. On the spoke of Agni: namaḥ samanta buddhebhyo namo mehākālāya ucchusma krodhāya ghadharacāya sarva nāga mahā hṛdāyāya so-and-so rakṣa kuru hūṃ phaṭ. On that of Nairṛti: namaḥ samanta buddhebhyo namo mocaya ākarṣaya cettaya motta gha gha ghātaya ghātaya so-and-so rakṣa kuru hūṃ phaṭ. In the direction of Vāyu: namaḥ samanta dharmāṇāṃ namaḥ samanta saṃghānāṃ amṛtata phaṭ traṃ oṃ bimala oṃ suṃbhani suṃbha hūṃ gṛhṇāpaya hūṃ ānaya go bhagavān bidyārāja so-and-so rakṣa kuru hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ phaṭ phaṭ.

On the rim of the wheel one should write this mantra: namaḥ samanta buddhāhāṃ namaḥ samanta dharmāṇāṃ namaḥ samanta saṃghānāṃ amṛtata phaṭ traṃ oṃ bimala oṃ śaṅkara oṃ pratyaṃgira oṃ uṣṇiṣ cakravarti sarva yanta mantra mūla karma bandha bandha taḍanaṃ kīlanaṃ bla bala kṛte yena kena cid kṛtaṃ tad sarva cchindha cchindha ciri ciri giri giri mara mara hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ phaṭ phaṭ.

This protective application destroys magical devices, mantras and the like. Once the protection wheel has been made, it should be tied to the upper arm, and one will not be overpowered by the three realms. This is averting through drawing, the seventh reality.

Immediately after this, the accomplishment of the maṇḍala shall be described. The mantra practitioner, facing west, should begin the accomplishment of the maṇḍala. With the light rays of the seed syllable at the heart of the wisdom being, one invites the maṇḍala that abides in the sky and imagines it melting into the lotus of one's own consort. From that melted state one should emanate, in their proper order, the principal deity of the wheel and the others into the maṇḍala of one's own seed syllable. After that one blesses the eyes and so forth, and performs offering, praise, the tasting of nectar and the rest. This is the accomplishment of the reality of the maṇḍala, the eighth.

Now the application of threatening shall be described. When the yogin's mind has become somewhat stable in meditation and some signs have been found, he should meditate for six months. If even so there is no accomplishment, then, by the rite described, preceded by the yoga of one's own chosen deity and preceded by a gathering in the sacred commitment, one should meditate for eighteen months. If even so there is no accomplishment, then between sessions one should undertake the application of threatening. Its stages are these.

The yogin endowed with the application of Akṣobhya and possessing the three meditative absorptions accomplishes and meditates on a black celestial palace surrounded by a black circle. After that he performs offering, praise and the tasting of nectar. With the light rays of the seed syllable at the heart of the wisdom being he invites the wheel of the Tathāgatas, and sees them fallen into the lotus maṇḍala, surrounded by a maṇḍala of fire, terrified and afraid, trembling violently. Then by the light rays of his seed syllable the host of Tathāgatas melt through passion for the consort, and what emerges by the vajra path transforms completely into two-armed Yamāntakas, who from the eight directions should strike them with ritual daggers, with oṃ a vajra cara a krona ma kīlaya hūṃ phaṭ. This is the rite of Akṣobhya. It is accomplished only when one is free of fixation on all of these, the one to be struck, the striker and the act of striking, and not otherwise. This is the accomplishment of threatening, the ninth reality.

After that the request to depart shall be described. Its sequence is this. One comes out of the maṇḍala house and performs a pacifying fire offering. Then one enters the maṇḍala house again and worships the maṇḍala with various offerings. One rings the bell with full elaboration:

hūṃ You have accomplished every aim of beings.
Grant us the corresponding accomplishments.
Having gone to the realm of the buddhas,
please come again. muḥ

That is the verse. After that one imagines the maṇḍala wheel of colored sand departing to the buddha field. With the rite ending in the three syllables one sweeps up the colored powder of the maṇḍala, puts it into a fragrant vessel, and should cast it into a river that flows into the ocean. One smears the ground of the maṇḍala with the five products of the cow, and with the rite ending in the three syllables one should give the torma. This is the tenth reality, the reality of the request to depart.

By whatever merit I have gathered
by setting out these ten realities,
may the cataracts of ignorance
of all beings be dispelled.

The pith instructions of the Ḍākinī Vajrapañjara called The Ten Realities, composed by the great master Vimalakīrti, are complete. Translated by Marpa Chökyi Lodrö.

Translator's notes

"Recitation and dance" renders bzlas dgar, read as bzlas pa and gar. "Agni, the southeast" reads me'i ("of fire") for the written ma'i. The eight nāga kings are identified from their standard Tibetan names; "Kulika" reads rigs ldan for the written rigs sbyin, and "Vāsuki" renders nor rgyas kyi bu. In the empowerment section, shes rab ("wisdom") refers to the consort (prajñā), and "wisdom-gnosis" renders shes rab ye shes (prajñājñāna). "Separation" renders dgar ba, here the fierce rite of separating the target's consciousness from the body and sending it to a pure land; the same word in the list of ten realities at the opening of the text is this rite, not "dance". "Liberate" renders bsgral ba in its ritual sense of killing while freeing the consciousness. The passage describes a visualized ritual performed on an effigy in animal form. "The reality of the deities" (lha'i de kho na nyid) in the condensed torma may simply mean the deities themselves. "So-and-so" renders the Tibetan che ge mo, the place in each mantra where the name of the person to be protected is inserted. The opening word, cut off at the start of this piece, is read as birch bark (gro ga), the usual writing surface for such protective wheels. "The five products of the cow" interprets sa ba'i rnam lnga as ba'i rnam lnga. "Three syllables" is not specified further in the text; it most likely refers to oṃ āḥ hūṃ.
