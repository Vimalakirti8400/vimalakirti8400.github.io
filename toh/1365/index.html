About this text

The Sādhana Called the Ornament of the Essence of Glorious Kālacakra, in Sanskrit Śrī Kālacakragarbhālaṃkārasādhana and in Tibetan Dpal dus kyi 'khor lo'i snying po'i rgyan zhes bya ba'i sgrub thabs, is a tantric practice manual devoted to Kālacakra, the "Wheel of Time," attributed in its colophon to the great scholar, the brahmin Piṇḍo. It opens with homage to Vajradhara and to Kālacakra as the union of the two truths and the three buddha bodies, and goes on to set out the meditative path. It explains how the awakening mind is drawn up through the body's centers, giving rise to the successive joys that correspond to the bodhisattva grounds, and describes complete awakening in a single instant and through five aspects, twenty aspects and the net of magical display. It closes with the pledges owed to the vajra master and the outer and inner pledges, supported by quotations from the Kālacakra, Hevajra, Cakrasaṃvara and Guhyasamāja tantras and the Nāmasaṃgīti. It is Toh 1365 in the Tengyur, where it belongs to the tantra commentaries, and it was translated into Tibetan by the Indian paṇḍita Bhadrabodhi and the Tibetan translator Gyijo Dawé Özer.

Translation

In the language of India: śrī kālacakra garbhālaṃkara sādhanaṃ nāma. In the language of Tibet: The Sādhana Called the Ornament of the Essence of Glorious Kālacakra.

Homage to the Blessed One, glorious great Vajradhara, lord of all the sugatas of the three times.

Kālacakra, the very self of the two truths,
The profound dharmakāya, the enjoyment body,
The emanation body for the faith and devotion of beings,
And sameness itself: to these I pay homage.

Kā is the unchanging, the peaceful;
La is that which has power over this;
Ca is the very movement of the mind;
Kra is the binding by the families.
To the three wisdoms I pay homage.

Realizing all things as a dream,
There is nothing you do not know; you know with full clarity;
Arising from the very dharmadhātu of the mind:
To the wisdom mind I pay homage.

The speech and mind of the dharmakāya,
The body, speech and mind of the enjoyment body,
The body, speech and mind of the emanation body:
To their union in a single wisdom I bow.

As for this yoga of glorious Kālacakra,
Beings of the age of strife,
Not knowing how wisdom is to be understood,
Say, "I am learned," and lead the childish astray.

The deluded and proud are puffed up from merely seeing books;
They despise the guru who is like Vajradhara,
And the moment they die they go to the Vajra Hell.
Therefore I write this Ornament of Kālacakra.

Here, first of all, is what abides within oneself. In the body of the yogin there are six elements, that is, six cakras, which abide together with the three channels: lalanā, rasanā, and the central channel, avadhūtī.

In the cakra of the crown protuberance, in the center of its five channel-petals, are the group of a ku ha, the consciousness aggregate, the space element, the ear faculty, mental phenomena, the bhaga, and seminal fluid; it generates the characteristics and the nature of these.

At the forehead, the cakra of great bliss has sixteen channel-petals, and in its center are the group of u pu va va, the perception aggregate, the water element, the tongue faculty, the object form, the faculty of the feet, and taking; it produces these.

In the thirty-two channel-petals of the cakra of enjoyment at the throat are the group of ṛ ṭu ra ṣa, the feeling aggregate, the fire element, the eye faculty, taste, the hands, and going; it produces these.

At the heart, in the eight channel-petals of the cakra of Dharma, are the group of i cu ya śa, the formations aggregate, the wind element, the nose faculty, the object touch, the faculty of speech, and the discharge of excrement; it produces these.

At the navel, in the sixty-four channel-petals of the cakra of emanation, are the group of ḷ tu sa la, the form aggregate, the earth element, the body faculty, smell, the faculty of the anus, and speaking; it produces these. This is what is called "the great emptiness, the five syllables."

The sixth: in the thirty-two channel-petals of the secret cakra are the wisdom aggregate, the wisdom element, the mental faculty, sound, the supreme faculty, and the discharge of urine; it produces these.

That in which these are of one taste, and in which what they generate is mingled into one, is the drop, emptiness, the essence of the sixth syllable. The five that bear the guise of e, the aspect of wisdom, are of the nature of vaṃ, the aspect of method. On this the Blessed One said in the root tantra:

e and lotus, the sphere of space,
bhaga, the source of phenomena, the water-born:
The seat of the syllable, entered by the one who holds the vajra,
Has been taught within my tantra.

Vajrin, vaṃ, Vajrasattva,
Vajrabhairava and Īśvara,
śrī heruka, Kālacakra,
The Ādibuddha and other names,
And the names of Viśvamātā:
That these have been taught as the characteristics
Of the abode, the support, and the supported,
Is in accord with the dispositions of sentient beings.
The Teacher, the omniscient Vajradhara,
The Buddha seated on the lion throne,
The teacher of the Kālacakra,
Was requested by you, Sucandra.

So it was said.

As for its being one: it is of the nature of a single mind whose very self is consciousness. On this, some hold that consciousness is eightfold, some hold that it is sixfold, some hold that it is threefold, and some hold that, together with a root consciousness, it is ninefold. In the definitive meaning, consciousness is one alone, not many.

This is evident through scripture and reasoning. As for scripture, the Laṅkāvatāra Sūtra says, "On the great ocean of the all-base consciousness, the engaging consciousnesses arise like waves from the current of objects." And the Three Baskets also say:

Traveling far and dwelling alone,
Bodiless, gone into the cave of the senses:
One who tames this mind so hard to tame,
Him I call a brahmin;
He will be freed from the bonds of Māra.

And the king of tantras itself says, "The agent and the doer are nothing but the all-base consciousness, just like a worm in its cocoon." And the glorious Buddhakapāla also says:

The one mind is like a stainless mirror;
Whatever is thought is a reflection.
Though one knows it to be like a dream,
Still it is grasped by conceptual thought.

And the Tantra of the Arising of Vajra Nectar also teaches the passage beginning, "Consciousness is one alone."

By reasoning, too, eight consciousnesses are untenable. Some say that of the four wisdoms, the mirror-like wisdom and the rest, which correspond to the all-base consciousness and the rest, some take the property-bearer (dharmin) as their object, while others say that they take both the property and the property-bearer as their object. But a wisdom that takes a property-bearer as its object is not the wisdom of a buddha, because it takes a property-bearer as its object, just like the mind of a sentient being.

Some say that the all-base consciousness is stable in its continuum, while the engaging consciousnesses are unstable. On this, one examines whether or not it undergoes momentary arising and ceasing. Many accept that the all-base is momentary. Momentary impermanence and the impermanence of a continuum are no different in being momentary and impermanent, because both are impermanent, just like the mental consciousness. Therefore consciousness is not many; it is one alone, without doubt.

On this, some may think as follows: since the perception aggregate, the feeling aggregate, the formations aggregate and the rest experience the divisions of self-awareness and so on, they are consciousness.

If one asks how the form aggregate is consciousness, the answer is this. The Abhidharma Sūtra too says:

Not made of atoms,
The all-base appears like the earth.
Just as, when milk is transformed,
The qualities of curd and buttermilk arise,
So too, through the transformation of consciousness,
Having been transformed, it appears as form.

And again, the Mahāyāna sūtra, the Laṅkāvatāra, also says:

External things do not exist
In the way the childish imagine them;
The mind, stained by habitual tendencies,
Arises in the form of objects.

And the Daśabhūmika Sūtra also says, "O children of the Victor, these three realms are mind only."

Therefore all these three realms are not established apart from consciousness, not even to the extent of the tip of a hair split a hundred times. Thus these three realms are one's own mind itself appearing in various aspects as earth, water, fire, wind, space, wisdom and so on. And they are one in being the very characteristic of wisdom.

This is taught clearly in all the kings of tantras. The king of tantras says:

All aspects are one's own mind itself;
Apart from objects, there is no mind of one's own.

And the glorious Guhyasamāja also teaches the passage beginning, "Generate the body like the mind!"

Then, by the power of the full ripening of the habitual tendencies of elaboration from beginningless time, through the distinctions of the conceptuality of apprehended and apprehender, object and mind appear as different, but they are not different in essence.

Then the king of tantras, the glorious saṃpuṭa, teaches the passage beginning, "Like the current of a river, and likewise like the ocean." Just as all the seventy-two thousand rivers arise from the ocean and enter the ocean again, so too form, feeling and all the rest arise from consciousness and enter consciousness again.

It is said: this is because consciousness itself, by the power of clinging to the habitual tendencies of elaboration from beginningless time, appears as apprehended and apprehender and appears as inert matter; that is, because consciousness itself appears as diverse. It is like white cloth dyed with colors, or like a pure crystal tinted by colors. Moreover, through the distinctions of objects, that very cognition appears differently as attachment, aversion and so on, and as virtue, non-virtue and so on, but it is not different in essence. Cognitions are like a dancer, a conjurer and a magician.

That itself is self-arisen wisdom, empty of apprehended and apprehender, free from the extremes of elaboration: the great Vajradhara. That itself is the great spontaneously present wisdom, because it has not arisen from anywhere. It is called nondual wisdom, because it is empty of the two consciousnesses of apprehended and apprehender, because emptiness and compassion are inseparable, and because the two truths are inseparable. The glorious Guhyasamāja also says:

Devoid of all entities,
Rid of aggregates, elements and sense sources,
And of apprehended and apprehender,
Equal in the selflessness of phenomena,
One's own mind, unborn from the very beginning,
Is the very nature of emptiness.

And the Nāmasaṃgīti also says:

The Buddha without beginning or end,
The Ādibuddha, without cause,
The single eye of wisdom, stainless,
Embodied in wisdom, the Tathāgata.

Therefore consciousness, or wisdom, is one alone. This is the stage of the common foundation, the first.

Now the nature of the deities will be made clear. The Guhyasamāja also says:

In brief, the five aggregates
Are renowned as the five buddhas;
The vajra sense sources too
Are the supreme maṇḍala of bodhisattvas.
Earth and water are Locanā and māmakī;
Fire and wind are known as Pāṇḍaravāsinī and Tārā.

Thus the aggregates, elements and sense sources are made clear as gods and goddesses.

As for the six families, the Extensive Commentary on Glorious Kālacakra also speaks of six aggregates, six elements, six faculties, six objects and six action faculties. To show this clearly: the form aggregate is Vairocana. The feeling aggregate is Ratnasambhava. The perception aggregate is Amitābha. The formations aggregate is Amoghasiddhi. The consciousness aggregate is Akṣobhya. The wisdom aggregate is Vajrasattva. Earth is Buddhalocanā. Water is mamākī. Fire is Pāṇḍaravāsinī. Wind is Tārā. Space is Vajradhātvīśvarī. The wisdom element is Viśvamātā. The two eyes are Mohavajra. The ears are Dveṣavajra. For the nose, Mātsaryavajra. For the mouth, Rāgavajra. For the body, Īrṣyāvajra. For the mind, Īśvaravajra. For form, Rūpavajrā. For sound, Śabdavajrā. For smell, Gandhavajrā. For taste, Rasavajrā. For touch, Sparśavajrā. For mental phenomena, Dharmavajrā. For the speech faculty and its utterance, Yamāntaka with consort. For the hands and taking, Prajñāntaka with consort. For the feet and going, Vighnāntaka with consort. For the anus and excrement, Padmāntaka with consort. For the bhaga and urine, Uṣṇīṣacakravartin with consort. For the supreme faculty and semen, Śumbha with consort. By this, the isolation of body is clearly taught.

These thirty-six deities abide in the six cakras. In the crown cakra, in the center of four channel-petals, abide those of the Akṣobhya family. In the forehead cakra of great bliss, in the center of sixteen channel-petals, abides the group of Amitābha. In the throat cakra of enjoyment, in the center of thirty-two channel-petals, abide those of the Ratnasambhava family. In the center of the eight channel-petals of the heart cakra of Dharma abides the group of Amoghasiddhi. In the center of the sixty-four channel-petals of the navel cakra of emanation abide those of the Vairocana family. In the center of the thirty-two channel-petals of the secret cakra abides the family of Vajrasattva.

Thus, by clearly teaching the aggregates, elements and sense sources as the maṇḍala of gods and goddesses, ordinary conceptuality is dispelled, and they become clear as that very maṇḍala.

Moreover, it is taught that there are six in each cakra. In the navel cakra of emanation, Amoghasiddhi abides in the east, Ratnasambhava abides in the south, Vairocana abides in the west, Amitābha abides in the north, Akṣobhya in the center above, and Vajrasattva below. The Nāmasaṃgīti says:

The great emptiness, the five syllables;
The drop, emptiness, the six syllables.

This is the stage of the isolation of body, the second.

Now the life-effort (prāṇāyāma) of purified speech will be explained. In the six cakras, a abides at the crown, u at the forehead, ṛ at the throat, i at the heart, ḷ at the navel, and aṃ at the secret place. At the crown it is a, empty. At the forehead, o, u and hā. At the throat, ar. At the heart, i and ya. At the navel, ta and al. At the secret place, aṃ.

Thus at each tip of the nose too, six each abide. From the east of the six cakras wind flows, from the south fire flows, from the west earth flows, from the north water flows, from the center above space, and from the center below wisdom flows; and in the same way they flow likewise from the five cakras. These flow in sequence through the lalanā, the rasanā and the avadhūtī. The five winds of the five limbs flow, the breaths of the five elements, whose very nature is to generate the five aggregates. As the Tantra of the Vajra Door says:

The life wind and the downward-clearing,
The one that abides together with fire,
The upward-moving and the pervading,
The moving and the fully moving,
And again the moving and fully moving,
The thoroughly moving and the definitely moving.

The glorious Kālacakra also says:

The single wind called life
Becomes tenfold:
Above and below and to the east.

When that very wind is released: above is the upward-moving wind, of the nature of space, green. Below is the downward-clearing wind, of the nature of wisdom, blue. In the east is the equalizing wind, of black rays. In the west is the life wind, yellow. In the south is the pervading wind, red. In the north is the Devadatta wind, white. Likewise there are Kūrma, Nāga, Kṛkara and Dhanañjaya: these are the ten winds.

Moreover, the wind-breaths, twenty-one thousand six hundred breaths, flow pervasively from the six cakras. From the lalanā flow ten thousand four hundred and sixty-two and a half. From the rasanā flow ten thousand four hundred and sixty-two and a half. From the avadhūtī six hundred and seventy-five breaths flow forth.

Thus they flow in sequence over the petals of the twelve rāśi, the signs of the zodiac, in the navel cakra. On the discordant petals, beginning with Aries, out of one thousand eight hundred breaths, fifty-six and a quarter flow through the avadhūtī. On the concordant petals, beginning with Taurus, out of one thousand eight hundred breaths, fifty-six and a quarter flow in the avadhūtīr.

Six of those breaths make one pala, a water-clock measure. Sixty palas make one nāḍikā. Five nāḍikās make one time-junction. One and a half time-junctions make each watch, so there are eight watches. Two time-junctions each make six periods. Three time-junctions each make four periods. Four time-junctions each make three periods. Six time-junctions each make the two courses. And all of these are the mount of the single consciousness. Having arisen from the six cakras and the three channels, they flow definitively from the places of the faculties to their objects.

This is taught clearly in the Guhyasamāja Tantra:

A jewel of five colors,
Just the size of a mustard seed,
At the tip of the nose, with diligence,
One should always meditate upon by the method of yoga.

The tip of the nose is the fifth cakra. In each cakra there are again five each, and the sixth is the secret of the cakra. Each cakra is counted from below. The six elements are the additional i a ṛ u ḷ aṃ.

Thus the nature of the winds, and all the aggregates, elements and sense sources, are nothing other than oneself. Showing the support and abode of the winds, and the mantra: for the arising, entering, abiding and bliss that are the nature of the body, speech, mind and wisdom of all the tathāgatas, it is oṃ āḥ hūṃ hoḥ. Likewise the four activities, pacifying and the rest, are accomplished.

Furthermore, one should train in the six-branch yoga. Life-effort is the vajra recitation. The mind's not being distracted, by the power of life-effort, is meditative concentration. The objects of concentration are five. The cessation of the five faculties is withdrawal. Holding them in their own place by that is retention. Then abiding in the state of bliss is recollection. Then non-distraction is samādhi. This is the stage of the reality of speech, the third.

Now, to explain the purification of mind: familiarity arises through habituation to space and so on and to the unroofed, and through habituation to training in the six branches of yoga. This was taught by the Blessed One. This meditation is from the Perfection of Wisdom, as follows: Then Śakra (Indra), lord of the gods, said to the venerable Subhūti, "Noble Subhūti, how should one apply oneself diligently to this perfection of wisdom? In what should one practice yoga?" Subhūti said, "kauśika, one who wishes to train in the perfection of wisdom should practice yoga in space. kauśika, one who wishes to train in the perfection of wisdom should practice yoga in the unroofed." So the Blessed One taught.

When the wind of earth dissolves into water, the crown cakra dissolves, from the syllable aṃ at the forehead, into the throat cakra, and the first joy arises. The coarseness of both the outer apprehended object and the inner apprehending mind ceases, the conceptual thoughts of the thirty-three natures cease, and the wisdom of joy arises. In its nature it is like smoke, and it is the first discerning of the mind. When the conceptual thoughts of the moon disk, of blazing and of the body cease, it is appearance, the nature of the body. And this great fire of discernment and wisdom appears as blazing.

It is said, "Vairocana, the great light, appears as the moon. Just as in the cloudless autumn sky the disk of the moon pervades all with its rays and makes things appear, so it is appearance, because it apprehends all entities without exception in an aspect that is luminous by nature." Its joy of body, joy of speech, joy of mind and joy of wisdom are the first, second, third and fourth lunar days, a i ri u. Embodying the four days of the waxing fortnight, it is the nature of the awakening mind that generates the concordant cause of the emanation body, in which the conceptuality of the waking state ceases, and of the concordant causes of discriminating wisdom: it is the nature of Kālacakra's appearance.

Then, as the nature of supreme joy, when water dissolves into fire, the awakening mind of a at the throat cakra is brought down to the heart cakra. When it is held for as long as one is able, the intermediate level of the outer apprehended and the inner apprehender ceases, and so the second discerning of the mind arises: increase of appearance, appearing like a mirage and like the sun. Through this the conceptual thoughts of the forty natures cease, and one becomes nonconceptual.

The lamp of beings appears as the sun. Just as the rays of the sun rise in the autumn season, so its essential nature, luminous and stainless, apprehending all entities without exception, is the awakening mind, Samantabhadra. As in the second stage, because it is empty and blissful, there are the supreme joy of body, the supreme joy of speech, the supreme joy of mind and the supreme joy of wisdom, on the fifth, sixth, seventh and eighth lunar days, with the nature of ḷ a e ar. The dream state ceases. It is the awakening mind that generates the concordant cause of the enjoyment body; compassion focused on phenomena, concordant with the wisdom of equality, with the nature of the peak: it is the essential nature of Kālacakra's increase of appearance.

Then, as the nature of the joy of cessation, when fire dissolves into wind, hūṃ, the nature of the awakening mind, descends from the heart cakra to the navel. When it is held for as long as one is able, even the subtle outer apprehended and inner apprehender cease. The essence of the joy of cessation is the discerning of the mind that attains appearance, appearing like fireflies and like the disk of Rāhu: the essence of mind only, free of the seven natures, nonconceptual; the essence of mind only, the lamp of wisdom, appearing as mere sound. The great splendor, luminosity, appears as lightning.

Likewise, the characteristic of space, the essential nature of non-entity, is without body and speech. As with the darkness of twilight, it is pervaded by an essence mingled with it, subtle and selfless. Life and effort are what moves and what does not move; it is mindless and unmoving; it is called the door that rests upon the seed of that.

Therefore there are the joy of cessation of body, the joy of cessation of speech, the joy of cessation of mind and the joy of cessation of wisdom, on the ninth, tenth, eleventh and twelfth lunar days, with the nature of o al ha ya. In it the nature of the supreme state ceases. It is the generation of the awakening mind, the concordant cause of the dharmakāya; through the concordant cause of the all-accomplishing wisdom, compassion without object: Kālacakra, the nature of attaining patience, the attainment of appearance.

To show its characteristic clearly: these too are clearly the body of mind only. Noble Nāgārjuna too said:

By the essence of the three wisdoms,
Apply them definitively to mind only.

The Daśabhūmika Sūtra too says, "O child of the Victor, these three realms are mind only," and by this too this very meaning is clearly taught. The three wisdoms too are of the nature of mind only. In the Perfection vehicle they are taught as three: heat, peak and patience. kambala too said, "Appearance is heat. Increase of appearance is the peak. Attainment of appearance is patience."

Then all entities without exception, as the essential nature of mere cognizance, arrive at the place of the sixty-four channel-petals of the navel cakra of emanation, and when they are held for as long as one is able, it is clearly complete.

Then the nature of the mental body is clearly taught. This was taught by the sūtras, the tantras and those who have attained accomplishment. The Perfection of Wisdom says, from "Form is like a dream" up to "Even if there were a phenomenon that is nirvāṇa, it too would be like a dream." The Abhisambodhi Tantra too says:

Bodies like rainbows
Will be seen by meditating on reality itself.

And the Guhyasamāja Tantra too says: "O Blessed Ones, all you tathāgatas, it is like this. For example, the awakening mind, because it gives rise to the wisdom of all the tathāgatas, is like the vajra seat. The awakening mind does not abide in body, does not abide in speech, does not abide in mind, does not abide in the three realms; it is unborn, and it is this vajra seat, the source of wisdom. O Blessed Ones, all you tathāgatas, one should think, 'This dream: I shall see the three realms as a dream,'" and "I shall see the dream too as a dream," and so on.

The meaning of this was also stated by Nāgārjuna:

Your body has no hollow within;
Like a rainbow in the sky,
You display your body.

And the Kālacakra teaches the passage beginning, "Just as a reflection appears in a mirror to a young girl."

Its meaning is this. By turning appearance in mind only back from its course, the king of knowledge, the supreme secret mantra, appears as a drop; so one imagines all entities as having become a single blue drop. It is like the shape of a butter lamp, and in its center is a black line just the size of a hair. Within that, as the sign that the breath has entered into the central channel, one sees in its center the enjoyment body of the Buddha, like the moon in water, inexpressible, of varied colors, all its aspects one's own mind, free of objects, not knowing the minds of others: the property-bearer comprised within one's own continuum.

Moreover, in the Dharmasaṃgīti the Blessed One spoke of five eyes: "Likewise, in order, the flesh eye, the divine eye, the buddha eye and the Dharma eye arise through the power of meditation." So one sees the three realms without exception.

The enjoyment body teaches the Dharma by means of sound. The tvatasaṃgraha too teaches the five manifest awakenings from the samādhi whose essence is the mental deity, with bodhicittetpadayāmi and the rest. The Lalitavistara too says:

Śākyamuni, the Tathāgata,
Wishing for this manifest awakening,
Dwelt on the bank of the nairñja
And entered the unmoving samādhi.
Then the Victors, vajras of space,
Filling it like sesame seeds packed in a pod,
Urged him on with a single voice:
"This thought is not pure;
By this, liberation is not attained."

With these words and others they urged him on. And so it is for the yogin too. Thus, from the mental body's attainment of appearance comes increased appearance; from that the six winds; and from that, in the center of the drop, the Victors arise as the six families and urge one on. Abiding in mind only, settled in equipoise, the six winds cease and one abides in the joy of cessation. This is the stage of conventional truth, the fourth.

Then there is this exhaustion of the fourth state, the essential nature of coemergent luminosity in which body, speech and mind are purified. The Sūtra of Non-Arising too says:

This Dharma is explained by sound and voice,
Yet here neither Dharma nor sound is found.
One who enters the real by way of the nature of reality
Will attain supreme, unsurpassed patience.

And the Kālacakra too says, "Conventional yoga is with color, arms and shape. Ultimate yoga is free of color, arms and shape." Moreover, the sūtra of the teaching on the unborn, the Sūtra of the licchavī Vimalakīrti, too says:

Mañjuśrī asked it rightly;
The son of the Victor remained without speaking;
By not speaking he explained it extensively.

Its meaning is this. When the wind of wind dissolves into space and wisdom, from the navel cakra to the secret cakra, in the center of the vajra jewel, one either holds the awakening mind as a whole, or by the instruction of subsequent dissolution brings it into the ultimate truth, and so all entities are purified. There are the coemergent joy of body, the coemergent joy of speech, the coemergent joy of mind and the coemergent joy of wisdom, ra ba la a, on the thirteenth, fourteenth, fifteenth and sixteenth days of the waxing fortnight. Beyond conception, having the nature of holding the sixteen halves, entering the vajra jewel, it is the non-emitting bliss, the coemergent body. Like the center of the sky, in which the sixteen parts of the neuter state are fully complete, it is realized in a single instant and appears like a cloudless sky.

The ultimate truth: whether one is settled in equipoise for one thousand eight hundred breaths or has risen from equipoise, in the secret place, from the sixteenth, in which the portions of sun and moon are exhausted, one attains completion and what is called abiding in the truth of the first ground: the perfection of generosity is complete.

Moreover, it is without color, without body, without shape, free of all activity and effort, to be known by each for oneself; it is to be learned from the mouth of the vajra master. It is the essential nature of the sixteenfold vajra, free of all natures. Because the ultimate truth is both existent and nonexistent, it is known in a single instant, since these are not contradictory as one.

Again, the Blessed One said of that very point: "In the resultant vehicle of mantra, joy is the coemergent body; the coemergent body has abandoned apprehended and apprehender, has transcended the nature of consciousness, does not abide in existence or in nirvāṇa: it is the coemergent of the buddhas, the vow of the goddesses."

The Nāmasaṃgīti too says:

Holder of the drop of sixteen halves,
Partless, beyond all parts,
Crowned with the peak of the fourth dhyāna,
Knowing all the limbs of dhyāna,
Knowing samādhi, knowing its lineage.

This is the stage that teaches the ultimate truth, the fifth.

Then one should train in union; this is the attainment of the reality of the fourth. Then, turning upward from the vajra jewel, when one period of three thousand eight hundred breaths ceases in the secret place, the second ground is attained: the perfection of moral discipline is complete. Then, through the cessation of three thousand six hundred breaths in each of the navel, the heart, the throat and the forehead, whether settled in equipoise or not, the four grounds, the fourth, sixth, eighth and tenth, are attained. Having completed the ten perfections, one attains the ten grounds, gains the five superknowledges in accord with Vajrapāṇi, lord of the ten grounds, and the obscurations of the afflictions and of the knowable are purified.

Then, by the instruction of the holy guru, one should train in the samādhi of heroic progress (śūraṃgama). These are the ultimate wisdom body, of one taste, the vajra-like samādhi. By training in it, the seminal fluid goes upward from the secret place to the navel, and so it has the nature of the four: the joy of body, the joy of speech, the joy of mind and the joy of wisdom of the waning fortnight. There is emptiness, and that is the syllables ya la va ra. Then, if one is able to draw the awakening mind upward from the navel to the heart, there are the supreme joy of body, the supreme joy of speech, the supreme joy of mind and the supreme joy of wisdom: having the nature of the six grounds, the great emptiness, endowed with emptiness, ta la oṃ ar.

As soon as one is able to draw it from the heart to the throat, there are the joy of cessation of body, the joy of cessation of speech, the joy of cessation of mind and the joy of cessation of wisdom: having the nature of the eighth ground, ultimate emptiness, e ā ḹ ai. Then, as soon as one is able to draw the awakening mind from the throat to the forehead and the place of the crown protuberance (uṣṇīṣa), through the innate joy of body, the innate joy of speech, the innate joy of mind and the innate joy of wisdom one masters the tenth ground: having the nature of the sixteenfold emptiness, having the nature of the letters ṛ ī a i, it is the culmination of the emptiness of space.

In the Sūtra of the Samādhi of Heroic Progress (Śūraṅgamasamādhi), the Blessed One said: "This holy person who has attained the samādhi of heroic progress abides in the domain of all the buddhas. He holds mastery over self-sovereign wisdom. Because he has not attained the samādhi of heroic progress, I do not call one a bodhisattva. Because he has not attained the samādhi of heroic progress, I do not say of that bodhisattva that he has attained the superknowledges. Because he has not attained the samādhi of heroic progress, I do not say of that bodhisattva that he has completed the training. I do not say of that bodhisattva that he is endowed with generosity, moral discipline, patience, diligence, meditative concentration, wisdom and skillful means. Because he has not attained the samādhi of heroic progress, I do not say of that bodhisattva that he has attained much. Because he has not attained the samādhi of heroic progress, I do not say of that bodhisattva that he is endowed with devotion. Therefore, sons of good family, this is the way of deliverance. Bodhisattvas who desire deliverance on every path should train in the samādhi of heroic progress, so that all conceit may be absent."

Therefore one should train in the samādhi of heroic progress, the samādhi of the treasury of space, the vajra-like samādhi and the samādhi of union.

Then, as soon as that bodhisattva, the twenty-one thousand six hundred breaths having ceased at the place of the crown protuberance, is able to draw the awakening mind to the place of the crown protuberance, in a single instant he is fully and completely awakened, illuminating all that moves and all that does not move, and wholly liberated from all afflictions. By the very power of that meditation, the one supreme being is to be beheld: peaceful, having the nature of skillful means; the ground from which the major and minor marks arise; endowed with the supreme of all aspects; arisen from the wisdom of emptiness; nondual and supremely peaceful; free of grasped and grasper by the very power of that meditation; the innate body, supremely immutable; wisdom, suchness, the great Vajradhara; abiding neither in the desire realm, nor in the form realm, nor in the formless realm; of the nature of luminosity, stainless; wisdom like an illusion; beyond sound, smell, taste, touch and mental objects; possessing the eye of samādhi. Because it bestows the wealth of qualities of the buddhas, such as all-knowledge, the knowledge of all aspects, the knowledge of paths, the knowledge of the aspects of the path, the ten powers and the fearlessnesses, it fully bestows the fruit of buddhahood.

Here, in the root tantra, the Blessed One said:
"Beyond existence and nonexistence,
nondual, with entity and non-entity exhausted,
emptiness and compassion not two:
vajra union, the great bliss.
The dharma of emptiness wholly abandoned,
vajra union is nondual."

And in the Nāmasaṃgīti it is said:
"The buddha without beginning or end,
the first buddha, without a cause,
the single eye of wisdom, stainless,
the tathāgata whose body is wisdom."

And in the Glorious Paramādya it is said:
"From space, too, space arises;
all is empty, a portion of space."

Therefore it is complete awakening in a single instant.

Awakening through five aspects is this: at the time of the person of suchness, six kinds of wisdom dawn. Through the purification of the aggregate of form and the letter lo, mirror-like wisdom appears, and all the phenomena of existence are known as mere illusions of wisdom. That very wisdom, as great compassion, through the purification of the aggregate of feeling and the letter ṛ, pervades everything like the sun, and so it is the wisdom of equality. That very wisdom of equality, through the purification of the aggregate of perception and the letter u, discerns each thing individually, and so it is discriminating wisdom. That very wisdom fulfills every need, desire and hope of all beings, all sentient beings, and through the purification of the aggregate of formations and the letter ī, it is the wisdom that accomplishes activities. That very wisdom, being indistinguishable from space, is the wisdom of emptiness: the purification of the aggregate of consciousness and a. Since all of these have the nature of self-awareness, great bliss, there is the purification of the aggregate of great bliss and e. The sixth is the pervader; the five are the pervaded.

So it is said in the Kālacakra:
"The wisdom body of the omniscient one,
like the light of the maker of day,
with eyes like lotus petals,
the buddha seated on the lion throne."

And again from that same text:
"The letters of emptiness and the drop,
and the holder of the supreme vajra."

And in the Glorious Hevajra it is said:
"That which is the lovely form of e,
adorned in its center with vaṃ,
the jewel casket of the buddhas,
the ground of all great bliss."

And in the Glorious Cakrasaṃvara it is said:
"Through delight in the supreme secret,
the self of all forever abides."

Therefore these are the five wisdoms. Great bliss inseparable from the sphere of reality (dharmadhātu) is what is so called.

Awakening through twenty aspects is this: each of the five wisdoms is in turn endowed with four aspects, body, speech, mind and wisdom. The enjoyment body, the flowers of its major marks bearing the fruit of the minor marks, is how great bliss itself appears to the lords of the tenth ground, the bodhisattvas. That is complete awakening through twenty aspects.

Complete awakening through the net of magical display is this: according to how the sentient beings to be trained can most easily be trained, in inconceivable buddha-fields of the emanation body, he displays bodies in whatever way will train them. So it is said in the Tantra Radiating from the One: "In world-systems as many as specks of dust, bodies for beings' sake, countless as atoms, emanations numberless as dust, inconceivable, are spontaneously accomplished in an instant, in a moment."

By way of speech he displays the immeasurable supreme. So it is said in the Nāmasaṃgīti:
"The great wish-fulfilling tree grown vast,
the supreme of great and excellent vases,
the doer who accomplishes the aims of all sentient beings,
seeking their benefit, kind and gentle to sentient beings."

Great bliss itself, however it may appear, is not any of the ways it appears. The nonduality of the two truths, the nonduality of skillful means and wisdom, is called the great Vajradhara. This is the realization of nondual union, the sixth.

Next, the pledges of the vajra holder are to be fully taught. There are three kinds of pledge: those concerning the master, the outer and the inner.

"The master is supreme above all:
the masters of the root tantra and the condensed tantra,
and the masters who attained accomplishment,
glorious Anaṅgavajra,
Padma of Oḍḍiyāna, Lavapa,
Indrabodhi, Lakṣmī,
Nāgārjuna with his disciples,
Śabari and Ḍombi,
Sara, Ārya, Lūyipa,
Dārika and the other accomplished ones.
As countless learned masters teach,
since the master is the chief of all,
if one's pledge toward him is not broken,
then even if the two hundred and fifty rules of the Vinaya
and the twenty bodhisattva vows and the rest are broken,
one still attains the state of Vajradhara.
The master who confers empowerment is like the eyes,
the one who teaches the tantra is like the body,
the one who teaches the instructions is like the heart.
Therefore, since the master is supreme,
one should not utter even a single idle word.
If one does not use respectful words,
one is certainly reborn in the Howling Hell (Raurava),
so the vajra holders and countless accomplished masters have said.
If ill will arises in one's mind,
the pledge of mind is broken,
and one will certainly go to the Vajra Hell.
If one does not prostrate and does not hold him higher,
and shows contempt with the body,
one will dwell packed inside a great vajra rock
a million leagues deep.
Even fierce, wild and piercing mantras,
though one practices them, will not be accomplished.
The glorious Vajradhara, too,
who had entered one's heart, tears it open and departs.
Even the ḍākinīs who have entered the ground of the knowledge holders
will feel no fear of such a one.
So have the glorious vajra holders,
countless bodhisattvas
and the accomplished masters taught."

From the great tantra, the Cakrasaṃvara:
"Just as in a delightful lotus pond
great flocks of many geese gather,
so, if the yogin's pledges are unbroken,
the mātṛkās and ḍākinīs who keep the pledges gather."

From the great glorious Guhyasamāja:
"One who reviles the master from the heart
will never attain accomplishment."

In the Glorious Cakrasaṃvara Tantra the pledges concerning the master, the outer and the inner are taught: "The practitioner in deep meditative equipoise should always keep the pledges; with broken pledges, in the maṇḍala one will not obtain empowerment or accomplishment." And: "The eight outer and inner pledges Vajrapāṇi has clearly taught. As the cause of the accomplishments, the pledges should always be kept. A messenger woman born of another family should not be approached out of lustful attachment. The nondual and the unobstructed, engaging in the pledges, accomplishing the conduct with women, and chaste conduct likewise, as he declared." From these words up to "This is the rite of keeping the pledges," it should be learned from the Upper Commentary on Cakrasaṃvara.

Thus, by breaking the outer pledges one becomes despised by worldly people. By reviling the Dharma one incurs great wrongdoing. By generating wrongdoing one's merit declines. When merit has declined there is no accomplishment. By breaking the inner pledges, as joy slips away, suffering arises from the wrongdoing of being parted from passion. Thus, by breaking the pledges one does not attain the accomplishment of the Great Seal (mahāmudrā). Having shown contempt for the vajra master and failed to honor him, one will be despised by other people. Since denigrating the body, speech and mind of the vajra master is like denigrating Vajradhara, one goes to the Vajra Hell.

The Kālacakra, great bliss, is like an ocean;
I have explained it with profound wisdom, like a ship.
O guru, whose mind is like the full moon,
if what was entrusted to me has gone astray, please bear with it.

The sādhana called the Ornament of the Essence of Kālacakra, composed by the great scholar, the brahmin Piṇḍo, who was born in the ocean land of the south, is complete. It was translated, edited and finalized by the Indian paṇḍita Bhadrabodhi and the Tibetan translator Gyijo Dawé Özer.

In the verse on the syllables of the name, "binding by the families" renders rigs kyis 'ching ba; the usual Kālacakra etymology glosses kra as the binding of sequence (krama), so rigs may stand for or be a slip for rim, but the text is translated as written. At the navel the Tibetan reads drug cu rtsa bzhin, which makes no sense as written; it is taken as drug cu rtsa bzhi na, "in the sixty-four," the standard number of navel petals. The text's assignment of the action faculties and activities to the cakras (feet with taking, hands with going, and so on) is given as it stands. In the Laṅkāvatāra quotation the Tibetan has klung, "river, current," where the sūtra's well-known image is the wind (rlung) of objects; it is kept as written. In the conclusion "one alone, without doubt," the Tibetan reads gcig kho na the tshom yin no, literally "one alone is doubt," which contradicts the author's argument; it is taken as an abbreviated or defective the tshom med pa, "without doubt." "Property" and "property-bearer" render chos and chos can. The Tantra of the Arising of Vajra Nectar (rdo rje bdud rtsi 'byung ba) could not be identified with confidence and is translated literally. Rdo rje can is rendered Vajrin. Here the text gives the crown cakra four channel-petals, where the previous piece gave it five; both are translated as written. The "Extensive Commentary on Glorious Kālacakra" is probably the Vimalaprabhā, but the title is rendered as the Tibetan gives it. The deity names Mohavajra, Dveṣavajra, Mātsaryavajra, Rāgavajra, Īrṣyāvajra and Īśvaravajra are Sanskrit equivalents of "Delusion Vajra," "Aversion Vajra," "Avarice Vajra," "Desire Vajra," "Jealousy Vajra" and "Lord Vajra"; the last two are less securely attested. "Appearing as mere sound" (sgra tsam du) is also given as written, though a reference to a lamp may be intended. The "Abhisambodhi Tantra" is probably the Vairocanābhisaṃbodhi. The Daśabhūmika title is written mdo sde s bcu pa, a slip for mdo sde sa bcu pa. The Dharmasaṃgīti quotation announces five eyes but lists only four; it is given as written. In the Blessed One's statement the Tibetan reads mig nas pa, which makes no sense; it is taken as mi gnas pa, "does not abide," which the context plainly requires. "This is the stage that teaches the ultimate truth" renders rig pa, evidently a slip for rim pa, "stage," which all the other section endings use. "Vow" renders sdom pa (saṃvara), which here may carry its tantric sense of union. The Sūtra of Non-Arising ('byung ba med pa'i mdo) could not be identified with confidence and is translated literally. The count of three thousand eight hundred breaths for the second ground, beside three thousand six hundred for the others, is given as written.
