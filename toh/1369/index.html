About this text

The Instruction of Glorious Kālacakra Called the Practice of Sun and Moon, in Sanskrit the Śrī Kālacakra Upadeśa Sūryacandra Sādhana and in Tibetan Pal Dükyi Khorlö Damngak Nyima Dawa Druppa, is a Kālacakra completion-stage instruction set in a frame story. It tells how an Indian paṇḍita who had received the Kālacakra teachings at Mount Nemindhara was visited in the evening, at midnight, and at dawn by the kings of Shambhala, who taught him the "inner Kālacakra" in three parts: the moving winds and their calculation, the abiding channels and the practice of inner heat, and the changing awakening mind, the vital essences called sun and moon, with the ways of guarding and increasing them and the qualities that follow. It closes with vajra songs of four ḍākinīs, and its colophon says that the Jagaddala paṇḍita Mahāvibhūticandra translated and taught it at Dingri Langkhor in Tibet. It is Toh 1369 in the Tengyur, where it belongs among the commentaries and instructions on the tantras.

Translation

In the language of India: śrī kālacakra upadeśa sūryacandra sādhana nāma
In the language of Tibet: The Instruction of Glorious Kālacakra Called the Practice of Sun and Moon.

Homage to glorious Vajrasattva.

The paṇḍita called Bhikuni was born at Odantapurī. He was learned in all five fields of knowledge, lived to the age of four hundred, and saw the truth of the first ground. Having received a prophecy from Mañjuśrī, he traveled by the power of swift-footedness to the foot of Mount Nemindhara. From the brahmin Sūryaratha, of the lineage of the Lords of the Three Families, he heard the Kālacakra, the Supreme First Buddha, and received its empowerment together with its means of accomplishment. At that time, in the evening, King Sucandra came and said, "You, who are going to the Central Land, know the outer Kālacakra, but you do not know the inner Kālacakra." When he replied, "Then I request it of you," the king gave him this instruction on the wind, the inner mover:

By the vajra of blissful wisdom
he turns the wheel of the six activities;
having destroyed the deluding wheel of ignorance,
he brings forth the five wisdoms:
to the lord Vajradhara I bow.

Because beings, set in motion by the wind of habitual tendencies,
are bewildered and suffer,
I shall explain the illuminating wheel:
generation, location, and divisions,
calculation, and taking it as the path,
the destruction of illness, and the result: these two.

The wind that stirs consciousness arises,
and from wind, fire is born.
From that, water and earth arise,
and space pervades them all.
Through the five poisons born from wind,
karma is accumulated, and its fruit, saṃsāra, ripens.

Male wind, female wind, neuter wind,
and the leaping wind: they abide as four.
Or else there are the winds of the five elements,
and consciousness abides in the manner of space.

Their names, locations, and colors,
their causes and the actions they perform,
their being seized, their reversal and blockage, and the means of treatment,
their speech, and the qualities they produce.

From the wisdom element comes the downward-clearing wind (apāna),
and from the empty, the life wind (prāṇa).
These two become threefold:
from the wind of wind comes the fire-accompanying wind (samāna),
in fire, the upward-moving wind (udāna),
and in water, the pervading wind (vyāna) itself.
From space, wind arises, and from that
nāga, kūrma, kṛkara,
devadatta, and dhanaṃjaya.
These five arise in order from earth, wind, fire,
water, and earth.
Being of the nature of the six, they bear ten names.

Earth at the navel, water at the heart, fire at the throat,
wind at the forehead, space at the crown, wisdom at the secret place.
The heart wheel is wind and the forehead wheel water;
in the other wheels, location and element agree.

Earth is yellow, water white, fire red,
wind black, space blue, and wisdom green.

Earth, water, fire, wind, and space
move outward twelve, thirteen, fourteen,
fifteen, and sixteen;
they extend outward and move short within.
Wisdom, earth, and space move evenly,
flowing through both nostrils.
From below moves earth, to the left water,
to the right fire, and above wind;
space moves in the center of the four,
and both nostrils flowing evenly is wisdom.

By the division into the twelve houses
there are twelve great shifts of the time-junctions;
by the division into the winds of the five elements
there are sixty small shifts of the time-junctions.
In each small shift of the five elements
three hundred and sixty move,
and in each of the twelve great shifts of the time-junctions
one thousand eight hundred move.
In one day twenty thousand
nine hundred and twenty-five conceptual winds move.
At each juncture between the small shifts of the five elements,
eleven and a quarter are set apart,
and at each juncture of the twelve time-junctions
fifty-six and a quarter move.
In one day six hundred
and seventy-five wisdom winds move.
For a person whose elements are balanced, in one day
twenty-one thousand six hundred move.
Birth, death, change of abode, the time of sexual union,
fainting, seizure by a planetary spirit, and the attainment of samādhi:
apart from these, the worldly is wholly wind.
That is the movement of the wind.

Delusion is in earth, pride in water,
attachment in fire, jealousy in wind,
aversion is space, and the mind of avarice
is explained as a division of the wisdom element.

Through wind, which has become the life of all sentient beings,
all actions are performed.
Of being seized by the concordant and the discordant,
one should know the twofold power and the threefold power.
When the life-holding wind is reversed and blocked, there is death or madness;
when the downward-clearing wind is reversed and blocked, disorders of the channels and retention of urine;
when the upward-moving wind is reversed and blocked, difficulty in breathing;
when the fire-accompanying wind is reversed and blocked, dropsy and tumors.
When the pervading wind is reversed and blocked,
it causes white leprosy and other illnesses.

With the staff wind and the application of the yogic exercises,
with visualization, blessing, and meditation on bliss,
with diet, conduct, medicines, and therapies,
and through auspicious connection, one should treat them.

When wind and mind are mingled and purified,
the five clairvoyances are attained.
Since all the mantras bless it,
the four activities are accomplished without effort.
The quality of the earth wind is deathlessness;
by fire come radiance and warmth;
by water, bliss, suppleness, and cohesion;
wind brings clarity and lightness;
space is accomplished as like space;
and the qualities of wisdom are inconceivable.

The wind-calculation of others is reckoned from outside:
direction, time, and the action performed,
form, shape, and color,
sound, tone, and the function of sound,
the manner of movement, deportment, and so forth,
the five elements and the minor elements,
whether they become enemy, slayer, or friend,
or remain neutral in equanimity: so is the wind reckoned.
This is the outer wind-calculation.

The inner wind-calculation is reckoned from within.
By thoroughly distinguishing the vowels and consonants (āli and kāli),
one enters by calculation into the winds of the six elements.
By examining the functions of the six winds
that move through the right, the left, and the center,
one should know the good and bad effects.
This is the inner wind-calculation.

Having come to know the calculation of yogic awareness,
one knows the manner of its arising, its form and color,
deportment, life span, and fortune,
and the omens of helpful and harmful actions as well.
One knows how to identify gods and demons by sound,
and knows illnesses and what helps through auspicious connection.
Outer and inner functions will dawn,
and in particular one engages in the four activities.
Male channel, female channel, neuter channel,
male wind, female wind, neuter wind:
through combining them with the leaping, the fourth,
by this too one is able to calculate the outer and the inner.

For one endowed with the preliminary practices,
the stages of taking it as the path are these:
arising, entering, and abiding: these three;
with the three syllables one purifies the pathways of the channels.
Sitting in the vajra posture and the like,
through the stages of vajra recitation
one will make the wind into the path.
Skilled in the application of the yogic exercises,
one holds the five winds in the avadhūtī
and meditates on their movement purified in its own place.
In five days the signs
of their entering the avadhūtī will appear.
By binding and calming them
with the vase breath and the forceful method,
bliss is certain to arise
as the life force enters the fire of the jewel,
and one sees the wheel of light.
If the head aches, one should rest it.
By holding the gentle wind of the moving downward-clearing wind,
the diseases of wind are eliminated.
With the life wind and exertion, phlegm is cleared away;
by releasing, bile is cleared away.
For combined diseases, block above and below;
they are cleared away by the application of the refined essence.
Blocking the life wind below the navel,
one clears away all of them without exception.
In particular, one clears them away with the wind of exertion,
and makes them clear in the avadhūtī.
With the wind of union, one guards against the appearance of adverse conditions.
Touch and the sword, elixir and the senses,
nectar-fruit pills and bezoar,
eye ointment, ointment for the feet, and the path beneath the ground: these eight;
by this the eight worldly accomplishments are attained,
and it bestows the supreme result.

So he spoke.

Then again, at midnight, King Yaśas came and said, "You are going to the Central Land of Jambudvīpa, and since its people are sick and full of craving for food and clothing, you need this instruction on the inner abider, the channels and inner heat (caṇḍālī)." And he gave the instruction:

How to meditate, the key points of the channels,
I shall explain, laying them fully open.
There are eighty thousand and four thousand,
and if divided precisely, a hundred thousand classes.
As many as the ten million body hairs,
so many too is the number of channels.
The channels of the functioning karma
are declared to be seventy-two thousand,
and thirty-two.
The lalanā, the sun channel,
coils around the four continents and the subcontinents,
and the rasanā, the moon channel,
likewise coils around as before and abides there.
As for the chief channels of the wheels:
at the crown protrusion four, at the heart eight,
at the crown of the head the number of the lord of men,
at the throat the number of the teeth,
at the navel eight multiplied by eight,
and at the secret place twice the king.
Their root branches out from the center of the navel,
and their tips pierce the five vital organs.
From the vital organs five channels branch out;
their tips pierce the five sense sources,
and they experience the five objects.
From the center of the navel four channels branch out,
and with eight they bring forth the body.
The chief channels are said to be three.
The one called avadhūtī
brings forth the coemergent;
this is said to be the king of channels.

Having completed the preliminary practices,
one should train and set the mind upon the key points.
Gather the four continents into Mount Meru;
straighten Meru, the tree of life;
seize the moving sun and moon.
Heated intensely by the light of sun and moon,
a stream of lightning comes forth from raṃ;
with its four characteristics
it burns as far as oṃ hūṃ āḥyi.
From haṃ falls a stream of nectar;
meditate until the syllables are nourished.
Set the sun in the middle of the ocean;
by the fire of inner heat that comes forth from raṃ,
the three realms blaze up in fire,
just as sesame oil is poured
into the fire of a fire offering.
Stir Meru and Vāyu, the wind god,
and rely on good firewood.
With the tip of the lightning fire
melt the moon at the crown of the head.
By whatever means the drop is melted,
from there one halts it at the throat, the heart,
the navel, and the secret place.
So declared the perfect Buddha.
All the poisons of the bhūta spirits are cleared away,
and one gains complete victory over illness.
Inner heat is endowed with eight qualities:
it reveals supreme bliss
and reveals the sphere of reality.
Practice it in four sessions, in order;
in seven or twenty-one days
it will arise and become stable.

So he spoke.

Then again, at dawn, King Puṇḍarīka came and said, "In Odantapurī, in the Central Land of Jambudvīpa, people crave intensely for what they are attached to. Since their lives are short, their illnesses many, and their fortune small, you need this instruction on the inner changer, the awakening mind." And he gave the instruction:

From the great lord Vajradhara
I heard this secret essence thus,
and I shall explain it fully to you:
its signs, the faults of its decline,
the manner of its decline and how to guard it from declining,
the means of increasing its clarity and its expansion,
and the qualities of clarity: these are taught.

From the body and the openings of the channels,
from the hair and the ten million body hairs,
from the complexion and the circle of the face,
from the state of mind and the tone of the voice,
from the shadow, the dwelling, and the body, one knows it.
The sages are skilled in examining marks and omens.

Without bliss and clarity, one grows dispirited;
one's strength is small, one's vigor low, one's heart joyless;
one is without luster, radiance, or bodily strength;
the hair on the chest turns backward;
the body becomes like a kinnara's or a preta's;
speech and hearing fail, and the heart wind sets in;
illnesses are many, obstructors enter, and life is short.
Because the water of sense pleasures has dried up,
one's fortune is small and one's wealth poor.
Because the wish-fulfilling tree of the awakening mind has dried up,
how could the fruits one desires come forth?
Men and women do not come under one's power,
and even those in harmony become separated.
Nonhuman beings do not gather,
and the peaceful, wrathful, and other rites are not accomplished.
The ḍākinīs do not gather under one's power,
and the accomplishments too decline.
The manure in the field has no strength,
and the richness of the soil disappears as well.
For one who lacks the human jewel,
how could well-being arise?

It leaks through continual dripping and through overflowing;
it leaks through seizing spirits, habitual tendencies, and the arousal of desire;
it is made to leak through food as well.
From the eyes, the ears, the nose,
the mouth, and the sense organ it leaks.
What is called the pervading drop
leaks from the pores and the face.
At times of water and of purging of the channels,
it is cast off through the bases of illness.
Through fire, the sun, and fatigue,
and through a continuing severe illness,
by these it is dried up.
The wind of the nose channel, by way of the mouth and the dhala,
and likewise the mouth and the lower channel, the dhala,
are what cast out the sun.

The conditions for a woman's decline
are somewhat like those of a man's white element;
in particular, the arousal of desire and sexual activity make it leak.
On the eighth and the fourteenth days,
and on the full moon and the new moon,
because Rāhu waxes outwardly,
the sun is cast out inwardly.
It is cast out from the mouth, the nose, and the breasts as well.
One should know the cause, the condition, the action performed,
and the harmful result.

Bringing the two leakings and the downward-clearing wind under control,
spread it well through the yogic exercises.
Guard it with mantras, substances, and auspicious connection.
Through the tantras, bring the secret wind under control.
One should know the call of the rutting she-elephant.
Avoid bad food and rely on the best food.
Through the application that blocks the upper wind,
perform the yogic exercises and hold the secret wind.
Lead the ḍākinī of the inner hollow into the dhūtī.
Through the applications of warmth and the like,
meditate on inner heat and spread it.
Avoid violent conduct and rely on carefulness.
Take the science of the sages as medicine.
Purify the six through the key points.
Hide the sun in the middle of Mount Meru.
Know the applications of spreading.
Rāhu in the middle of the ocean
having devoured both sun and moon,
draw sun and moon up to the summit of Meru
and spread them through the yogic exercises.
For such various applications,
ask the one who has omniscient knowledge.

Yogins of the highest faculties,
relying on the ultimate great seal (mahāmudrā),
through the application of unmoving meditative concentration
meditate on the samādhi of bliss and emptiness that accords with its cause.
In three years and three half-months,
it is said, they attain the supreme accomplishment.
Yogins of middling faculties,
having examined the mind, rely on the wisdom seal;
through the application of their own body endowed with method,
they meditate on the samādhi of bliss and emptiness that partly accords.
That they attain the accomplishments shared with the gods of the form realm
was spoken by the Victor.
Yogins of lesser faculties
rely on an action seal endowed with the defining marks;
through the application of sexual union
they meditate on the samādhi of bliss and emptiness with emission.
That they attain the accomplishments shared with the gods of the desire realm
is spoken in the tantras.

One with desire who wishes to cultivate bliss and emptiness,
having accomplished the binding and holding of the wind of union,
should make a young woman endowed with the defining marks
a fit vessel through refuge and empowerment.
Set the seeds of the six classes of beings upon the body;
through the stages of generation and gathering,
bless the vajra and the lotus.
Increase the passion for form and sound;
through that conduct, set the clear essence in motion.
Having trained through the three preliminaries,
by meditating endowed with the three perceptions,
through the application of the main practice, union,
let it descend, hold it, and taste the savor of bliss and emptiness.
Turn it back and gather it into its place;
spread it, mingle it nondually, and meditate.
Let it descend slowly, at a tortoise's pace,
and be skilled in the two purposes.
The downward-clearing wind becomes threefold:
the bliss-increasing, the continuously accompanying, and the fully entering;
hold it with the pervading wind of expansion.
Through the application that moves the continuously accompanying wind,
one becomes skilled in holding the moon.
When one does not gain control of the downward-clearing wind,
mingle nondually the bliss-increasing wind and the source of phenomena.
With the continuously accompanying wind, draw in the light of the sun;
with the fully entering wind, hold it in its place.
Drawing the moon with the continuously accompanying wind,
turn it back upward with the three syllables.
By gathering the experience of the coemergent into Brahmā,
if the head aches, spread it.
Spread mind and wind gradually,
in stages over twenty-one days.
Mingle wind and mind nondually,
make clinging and wisdom nondual,
and meditate on the samādhi of the union of bliss and emptiness.
Draw the essence of the clear essence toward the object;
when the bliss of the clear essence arises,
and one is skilled in the means of training the strength of bliss,
one becomes a great yogin.

When attachment to the object arises,
the essence of the clear essence leaks toward the object.
When grasping at things arises within,
the substance of the clear essence declines.
When one practices with clinging,
one obtains the substance of the clear essence.
If one abides in nonduality, the clear essence increases;
whatever one knows there, the clear essence expands.
The host of qualities is the power of the clear essence.
The clear essence of clarity is emptiness:
by the moon free of the faults of conceptual thought,
the torment of the afflictions is cleared away.
The clear essence of emptiness possesses luminous light:
by the sun of compassion and wisdom
ignorance and suffering are cleared away.
Unstained by the clouds of habitual tendencies,
let the sun and moon expand supremely.
The great lord Vajradhara
spoke thus, and I heard it.
It is the secret language of Kālacakra;
it is not explained elsewhere.

Through the sun and moon expanding in the body,
one is endowed with luster, radiance, and great splendor;
the skin is supple, firm, and clear;
one is free of illness and endowed with the vajra of bliss;
one possesses great blessing and clarity;
bliss arises, and one's voice becomes the voice of Brahmā.
Through clarity one knows outer and inner phenomena;
without conceptual thought, self-grasping is freed in its own ground;
the vajra of bliss and emptiness destroys suffering;
body and bones ripen into mind;
the qualities upon qualities are inconceivable.
Through the expansion of the drops of the body,
life, wealth, fortune,
and sense pleasures expand, and wisdom increases.
Relative bliss pacifies suffering;
the ultimate drop pacifies delusion;
the nondual drop pacifies the obscurations.
This is the activity of pacifying.

Women and ḍākinīs come under one's power,
and one delights the minds of all passionate women.
Gods, nāgas, yakṣas, and mātṛkās too
come under one's power: what need to mention humans?
Brahmā and the others come under one's power:
what need to mention men?
So it is with substances and sense pleasures as well.
The guardians of the directions come under one's power.
In wrathful activity it becomes a weapon:
killing, expelling, bewildering, and paralyzing;
it is the vajra that holds power over all.
Through the power of samādhi, expanded by the mingling of wind and mind,
one is able to do anything at all.

For the most part, through the drop of clarity
one sees the treasures beneath the earth,
sees the channels of the body, and dispels darkness.
Through the clear essence the mind dawns as bliss,
and one becomes like a buddha.
Through its becoming the essence of the clear essence,
bliss expands in the body,
and one makes wrathful, fierce tigers and the like one's companions.
Through utmost clarity one sees the supreme;
through supreme clarity the six clairvoyances,
and likewise the six recollections, dawn.
Through what is called the clear essence become strength,
one obtains the strength of Nārāyaṇa and the like.
Through what is called the clear essence as elixir,
appearances dawn as bliss,
and one is able to enjoy the savor of the three realms.
Through what is called the unmoving clear essence,
saṃsāra dawns as bliss,
and one attains the ground of non-return.
Through the mastering clear essence one sends forth emanations;
the bliss of nirvāṇa dawns,
and one is able to emanate supreme and ordinary emanations.
From what is called the vajra clear essence,
one knows things as they are and in all their multiplicity.
With a body that holds mastery over the grounds,
in an undefiled, pure field,
one appears as an undefiled vajra body,
teaches the Dharma of great bliss
to a retinue equal to oneself,
awakens manifestly together with the retinue,
and is empowered as the chief of the buddhas.

So he spoke.

Then, as the master Bhṛkupa was coming back, bringing the Kālacakra in both its words and its meaning, the four ḍākinīs escorted him all the way past Mount Nemindhara and said, "Paṇḍita, you have left our treasury empty." "If that is so," he said, "come to Jambudvīpa by miraculous power and act as guardians of the word for these teachings." At this they pledged to act as guardians of the word, and each of them sang a vajra song.

The Jewel Ḍākinī said:
"The avadhūtī is the supreme channel.
Having untied its first knot,
if you know how to insert wind and mind inseparably,
you will make the coemergent manifest.
Train the pathways of the channels, yogin!"

The Vajra Ḍākinī said:
"The king of winds is the life wind.
If you know how to hold it and purify it,
you gain mastery over the vajra aggregates
and can go to the realm of those who move through the sky.
Train in the control of the life wind, yogin!"

The Lotus Ḍākinī said:
"Adorned with e and vaṃ,
train step by step in the three joys.
Through being introduced to the coemergent,
even one who has committed the five acts of immediate retribution
comes to realize self-arisen wisdom.
Train in bliss, yogin!"

The Buddha Ḍākinī said:
"Through recognizing the stainless meaning,
unsought and spontaneously present,
saṃsāra is freed as nirvāṇa:
self-awareness is the unsurpassed buddha.
Realize the natural state, yogin!"

Having spoken thus, and having given the secret words as heartfelt advice, they departed to their own abodes. Then the master went to Odantapurī in the Central Land and made the teaching of the Buddha flourish.

Through the Kālacakra of the abiding channels
one comes to know all outer and inner phenomena;
through the Kālacakra of the moving wind
one steers both existence and peace;
through the Kālacakra of the changing awakening mind
one brings forth every excellence.
Having heard the meaning of the three wheels,
by setting down the meaning of the three wheels,
may the sentient beings dwelling in the three realms
manifestly realize the three bodies.

The Instruction of Glorious Kālacakra, the Stages of the Three Wheels, called the Practice of Sun and Moon, is complete. Mahāvibhūticandra, a paṇḍita of Jagaddala in India, translated it himself into Tibetan at glorious Dingri Langkhor and taught it.

Translator's notes

The birthplace dbus 'gyur tshal is taken as the usual Tibetan name for Odantapurī. In the list of the sources of the five secondary winds, the text names earth twice (earth, wind, fire, water, and earth); the first is perhaps meant for space, as in the preceding line, but it is translated as written. In "to the right fire," the text writes mi for me, fire, an evident slip. The disorder named rtsa chu sri is uncertain; it is taken as channel disorders and retention of urine, which suits the downward-clearing wind. The "staff wind" (dbyug rlung) is a breathing practice whose exact technique is not specified here. The numbers in the calculation section are the counts of breaths: 21,600 a day, of which 675 are wisdom winds and 20,925 conceptual (karmic) winds. The petal numbers of the wheels are given in the traditional number code: the "lord of men" and the "king" stand for sixteen and the "teeth" for thirty-two, so the series is four at the crown protrusion, sixteen at the crown, thirty-two at the throat, eight at the heart, sixty-four at the navel, and thirty-two at the secret place. The list of the eight worldly accomplishments is given as written; its first and fourth items (touch, the senses) do not match the more common lists and their exact sense is uncertain. This text assigns the lalanā to the sun and the rasanā to the moon. In this section "sun" and "moon" stand for the red and white vital essences (the female and male drops), and the "clear essence" (dangs ma) is the refined vital essence of the awakening mind. "Into Brahmā" refers to the crown of the head, the aperture of Brahmā. The term dhala in the lines on the nose channel is obscure and is left as written. The line on the kinnara and the preta is taken as describing the degraded appearance of the body; its exact construction is uncertain. "The bliss of the clear essence" reads dang sa ma in the text, evidently a slip for dangs ma. The master is here called Bhṛkupa, while the opening of the text names him Bhikuni; the two are evidently the same paṇḍita, and each spelling is kept as written. "Sends forth emanations" reads sbrul ba, evidently a slip for sprul pa, emanation. The four ḍākinīs are those of the Jewel, Vajra, Lotus, and Buddha families.
