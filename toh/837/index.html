About this text

This is a tantra in the Kangyur, Toh 837, belonging to the Nyingma-oriented collection of Mahāyoga tantras preserved in the Kangyur's tantra section. The surviving title lines point to a work known as "Dispelling Ignorance," described as a great magical display (mahāmāyā) of the Blessed One, and identified in the Tibetan colophon as the heart of secrets and the certainty of reality (tattva), a great teacher or lama of primordial wisdom. A Sanskrit title cannot be determined from the lines given, so none is offered here. The text is presented as the speech of the Blessed One, the glorious Samantabhadra, who is shown dwelling in a secret abode and revealing the nature of the lama as the embodiment of the body, speech, mind, qualities, and activities of all the tathāgatas. Its central claim is that all who accomplish this secret practice are Samantabhadra himself, so that it concerns the recognition of the teacher and of enlightened reality as the essence of the path.

Translation

All those who accomplish this secret practice are Samantabhadra himself. Thus ends the great magical display called "Dispelling Ignorance" of the Blessed One.

In Tibetan: this is the great lama, the very heart of secrets, the certainty of reality (tattva). Homage to the Blessed One, glorious Samantabhadra, the great lama of primordial wisdom.

At one time this was spoken. The great lama of magical display is the lama of all the buddhas, the supreme secret bliss of all yogis. Dwelling in the secret abode, the Heart-Essence, the lama, spoke thus.

The Tathāgata, the perfectly complete Buddha, the Blessed One, the great enjoyment of the secret lama, whose very nature is the vajra of body, speech, mind, qualities, and activities of all the tathāgatas of the ten directions and the four times. Everything without exception, whether remaining or not remaining, has the nature of being neither separate nor different, inseparable. In the dharmadhātu of the utterly secret great lama, in Akaniṣṭha, the abode without edge or center, there is a wheel of boundless primordial wisdom, luminous. In it stands a palace blazing with precious wisdom, boundless in all ten directions like the expanse of space. Because it is expanded by countless great qualities of the lama, it is square in form. It is adorned with four projecting balconies of supreme precious wisdom, the lama. At its peaks, all the maṇḍalas of the buddhas of the ten directions and four times, not separate and not different, are encircled entirely by one single essence, the wisdom lama. It is the inconceivable expanse of the wisdom lama, and it has distinctions and varieties such as different colors. It is adorned with immeasurable garlands of precious wisdom, the lama, with hanging tassels and with fringed ornaments. Forms, sounds, smells, tastes, and tactile sensations arise by themselves, clear and unobstructed. It is covered with special luminous ornaments, the lama. It has gateways entered through the four immeasurables, and is furnished with the staircases of the eight liberations. There is neither outside nor inside, for everything has become the interior of the great lama. In the pervasive and expansive palace of the great lama, the most secret, there are thrones of the five perfections: the throne of the lion, which is fearlessness; the throne of the elephant, which is power; the throne of the horse, which is miraculous power; the throne of the peacock, which is mastery; and the throne of the garuḍa soaring through space, which is unobstructedness. Upon these are stacked the sun and moon discs, which are naturally luminous, and the lotus seat of precious jewels, which is unstained.

All phenomena, without remainder, are none other than buddhahood itself. A dharma other than buddhahood itself cannot be found even by a buddha. Through this declaration, all the Tathāgatas were pleased.

Then all the assemblies of the Tathāgata consorts spoke this solemn utterance.

Emaho. The realms of the ten directions are secluded from the very beginning. The three existences are themselves a pure field. The five degenerations are themselves an abode endowed with bliss. The five aggregates are themselves manifest buddhahood. Since all is the supreme essence, the Victors do not seek the Dharma elsewhere. Even if one sought a dharma called "other than this very nature," the Victors would not find it.

Through this declaration, the Tathāgata himself knew that all phenomena are buddhas from the very beginning.

Then, from the state in which expanse and wisdom are not two, this solemn utterance was spoken.

Emaho. The Dharma is secret from the very beginning. The various appearances are secret in their nature. The supreme secret, the essence, is unsurpassed. All is definitively ascertained as the secret expanse. From the supreme essence of the guru, buddhas and sentient beings are not two.

Through this declaration, he knew that everything is definitively the expanse.

They cling to each as "self" and "mine." Yet no one has bound them; there is no binding, and nothing that could be bound. Through conceptual clinging to a self, one forcibly ties knots in the sky. The buddha-dharma, spontaneously present from the beginning, without bondage and without liberation, is manifold in its play, displayed for the sake of teaching. Yet though it displays, the expanse neither increases nor diminishes.

Even so, for those who have not realized it so, in order to arouse the essential nature of mind in the conventional aggregates, in the maṇḍala of the guru who is the awakening mind, this vajra secret word was solemnly uttered.

The very nature of mind, empty and secluded, has no family, color, or shape. Though nothing at all, it appears as anything. Unmoving, it brings forth various miracles.
