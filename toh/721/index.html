About this text

Toh 721 in the Kangyur is a Mahāyāna sūtra known in Sanskrit as the Ārya-sarvatathāgatādhiṣṭhānasattvavalokenbuddha­kṣetranirdeśanāvyūha and in Tibetan as Pakpa Deshин Shekpa Thamché Kyi Jingyilab Semchen La Sikching Sangyé Kyi Shing Gi Köpa Küntu, rendered in English as "The Noble Great Vehicle Sūtra called Showing All the Arrayed Buddha-fields by the Blessing of All the Tathāgatas, Beholding Sentient Beings." The text belongs to the sūtra section of the Kangyur and opens in the classic manner of a buddha-word discourse, with the Buddha residing on Mount Gṛdhrakūṭa at the abode of the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara, surrounded by a great assembly of arhats and bodhisattvas. Its central concern is the revelation of the arrayed pure buddha-fields made visible through the collective blessing and compassionate gaze of all the tathāgatas toward sentient beings. As a Mahāyāna sūtra, it thus celebrates the visionary splendor of the buddha-fields while emphasizing the saving power that flows from the unified grace of all awakened ones.

Translation

In the language of India: Ārya-sarvatathāgatādhiṣṭhānasattvavalokenabbuddhakṣetranirdeśanāvyūha-nāma-mahāyānasūtra. In the language of Tibet: The Noble Great Vehicle Sūtra called "Showing All the Arrayed Buddha-fields by the Blessing of All the Tathāgatas, Beholding Sentient Beings."

Homage to all buddhas and bodhisattvas.

Thus did I hear at one time. The Blessed One was dwelling on Mount Gṛdhrakūṭa, at the abode of the noble Avalokiteśvara, in the jewel-treasury of divine gems, amid a profusion of blossoms, arranged upon divine lion thrones. The great Saṅgha of monks, consisting of some five hundred monks, were all without exception arhats, freed of all defilements, who had completely crossed to the far shore of the supreme faculty of all sentient beings. And all the bodhisattvas, dwelling in the awareness of great compassion, were without exception those held back by only one more birth, or two births, or three, or ten, or twenty, or thirty, or up to one hundred births — and all of them were dwelling at the eighth or tenth bodhisattva ground. Among them were the noble Avalokiteśvara, Mañjuśrī, Vimalapuṣpa, Ratnaśrī, Vidyutprabha, Vimalāloka, Candana, Amṛtaprabha, and so forth — seven hundred great bodhisattvas. All the laymen and laywomen present had received prophecy and had attained meditative absorption. Five thousand had assembled from various world-systems. All the gods, nāgas, yakṣas, and gandharvas had served the buddhas of former times and had witnessed hundreds and thousands of the miraculous displays of the Tathāgata. All the great yakṣinīs had attained bodhisattva wisdom, had received prophecy, and were irreversible in a matchless way — Vimalāloka, Prabhāvatī, Bhayaṃkarī, Śrīmatī, Harā, Śaṅkhinī, and others: eighty great yakṣinīs. And together with all the world-protectors — Śakra (Indra), Brahmā, Vaiśravaṇa, Dhṛtarāṣṭra, Virūḍhaka, Virūpākṣa, Ratnasādhu, and Pūrṇabhadra — all these were gathered there together.

Then, all of them, perceiving that the Tathāgata was seated upon the lion throne, offered worship to the Tathāgata out of the roots of their own virtue, with divine ornaments, garments, flowers, flower garlands, incense, perfumed ointments, and the sounds of music, and they made representations in his honor. Some among them circumambulated many hundreds of thousands of times and sang his praises.

At that very moment the Blessed One entered into the meditative absorption called "Dwelling in the Awareness of Great Compassion Directed toward All Sentient Beings." Through the power of that absorption, this great trichiliocosm of world-systems blazed with light, and every material form was pervaded by that radiance. Sentient beings who were blind gained sight and beheld forms with their eyes. Those who were deaf heard sounds with their ears. Those afflicted by sickness were freed from illness. Those without clothing obtained garments. Those who were mad regained mindfulness. Those whose bodies were impaired and whose faculties were incomplete obtained bodies and faculties that were fully complete. Those who were poor obtained wealth.

Sentient beings who lacked wealth, provisions, and enjoyments came to be endowed with wealth, provisions, and enjoyments. All sentient beings came to be possessed of every happiness, and all their wishes were completely fulfilled. Then all the sentient beings of this great trichiliocosm of world-systems were also stirred and urged, and they all came to the presence of the Blessed One in order to hear the Dharma. Those beings who had been born among the gods entirely abandoned divine pleasures, kept the buddha in mind, and came to the presence of the Blessed One in order to hear the Dharma. Those beings who had been born among humans also entirely abandoned human pleasures and came to the presence of the Blessed One in order to hear the Dharma. Those beings who had been born among nāgas, yakṣas, rākṣasas, hungry ghosts, and flesh-eating spirits each individually recovered mindfulness of the buddha, came to dwell in loving-kindness toward all sentient beings, and, with body and mind at ease, came to the presence of the Blessed One in order to hear the Dharma. Those beings who had been born in the realms of Yama, within the pitch darkness of the deepest dark, also recovered their individual mindfulness for a brief moment through the power of the buddha, came to know one another fully, and were entirely liberated from that great darkness. All sentient beings came to dwell in loving-kindness toward one another and were freed from the proximate afflictions. Then this great earth trembled in six ways, rising and falling.

Thereupon, at that assembled gathering, the youthful Mañjuśrī was present and seated, and he said this to the great bodhisattva, the noble Avalokiteśvara: "Son of good family, the great assembly of bodhisattvas is fully manifest everywhere, and the omens of that great assembly are also fully manifest everywhere. Son of good family, the prophecies given to many tens of millions of billions of bodhisattvas are also fully manifest everywhere, and the omens of the great Dharma discourse are also fully manifest everywhere. Therefore, son of good family, the hopes of many tens of millions of billions of bodhisattvas will all be completely fulfilled, and they will attain great primordial wisdom. Son of good family, for this reason you must arouse compassion toward all sentient beings, and you must entreat the Tathāgata to establish them in benefit, happiness, and unsurpassed perfect awakening. Son of good family, in a future time, in a future age, there will arise sentient beings who commit misdeeds, who are impoverished, lowly, of poor complexion, thoroughly tormented by old age and sickness, with scanty enjoyments, with bodies not properly cared for, short-lived, of little intelligence, thoroughly tormented by attachment, aversion, and delusion. Son of good family, in order to teach the Dharma for their sake, obtain permission from the Tathāgata. Son of good family, for this reason you will long work for the welfare, benefit, and happiness of sentient beings, pacifying all illness, reversing all misdeeds, thoroughly pacifying all misdeeds, completely fulfilling all hopes, and bringing them all the way to unsurpassed nirvāṇa."

Then the great bodhisattva, the noble Avalokiteśvara, gazed in all ten directions and brought to mind as many Tathāgatas as there are grains of sand in the river Gaṅgā.

He bowed with his palms joined in the direction of the Blessed One, circumambulated three times, and prostrated with five limbs upon the ground. He then addressed the Blessed One in these words:

"Blessed One, in the last days and the last times there will arise sentient beings afflicted by old age, illness, death, and grief, oppressed by untimely death, of low condition, of poor complexion, of short life, of meager resources, and whose bodies are wholly without cultivation. These beings, through minds of miserliness and corrupt moral discipline, will destroy one another and will seize one another's wealth, resources, and prosperity, indulging in laughter, frivolity, dancing, and games. They will perceive what is impermanent as permanent and what is unpleasant as pleasant. Why is this so? Because, on that very basis and foundation, beings are harmed by non-virtues of many kinds, and are then reborn in the hells, in the realm of animals, and in the world of the Lord of Death. Blessed One, for this reason I wish to completely fulfill the welfare, remedy, happiness, and all the hopes of those beings, to bring them to attain the primordial wisdom of the Tathāgata, to cause them to be born in a buddha field, and to reverse all their misdeeds — and for these purposes I wish to beseech the Tathāgata. Blessed One, since there is nothing that the Tathāgata does not know, does not understand, does not see, has not heard, or does not fully cognize, I wish to hear teachings related to the Dharma. Having honored the buddhas of the past, and with all the many bodhisattvas endowed with wisdom in their nature, monks, nuns, laymen, laywomen, gods, nāgas, yakṣas, gandharvas (scent-eaters), asuras, garuḍas, kinnaras (half-humans), and great serpents who have assembled here and are without refuge and will find no return — I implore you, Blessed One, to please speak. Sugata, please speak."

Then all the members of the assembly who had arrived together also prostrated with five limbs upon the ground and with one voice spoke these words: "Blessed One, please speak well. Sugata, please speak well."

Then the Blessed One, having heard this supplication made two and three times and taken it deeply to heart, gazed in all ten directions, and with a voice that was melodious, pleasing, and delightful, addressed the great bodhisattva, the noble Avalokiteśvara in these words:

"Son of good family, there is a meditative absorption called 'The blessing of all the Tathāgatas, gazing upon sentient beings and revealing the array of buddha fields.' I myself, from the moment I first aroused awakening mind, heard this absorption in the presence of the Tathāgata called 'Displaying All Directions Like a Flower of Brilliance.' Son of good family, at the very moment of hearing the name of that meditative absorption, nine billion sentient beings attained the primordial wisdom of the Tathāgata, and all of them were then prophesied by those Tathāgatas to be born in various buddha fields. And I too received such a prophecy. Son of good family, through the divine knowledge of the Tathāgata, I declare that in former times a certain Tathāgata taught this Dharma exposition to all sentient beings in thirty-one instances.

In all of those instances as well, the ones who supplicated the Tathāgatas were none other than yourself, noble Avalokiteśvara, and Mañjuśrī the Ever-Youthful, together with the retinue of great bodhisattvas, and the assemblies of monks, nuns, laymen, and laywomen who were present at those times. Having heard it, they made images and performed offerings. Subsequently, many tens of billions of sentient beings received prophecies and attained bodhisattva meditative absorptions, and they came to go wherever they wished, to have all their hopes fulfilled, to be free from illness, to have their roots of virtue fully ripened, to abandon all obscurations, to be endowed with beautiful and lovely forms pleasing to behold, to be abundantly supplied with wealth, grain, storehouses, and treasuries, to be worthy of being openly praised by kings, princes, and ministers, to be worthy of being revered by all sentient beings, to be endowed with mindfulness and with wisdom, to possess unwavering faith in the Buddha, the Dharma, and the Saṅgha, to be endowed with miraculous powers, and to be possessed of long life, fine complexion, majesty, and strength, replete with excellence in every respect. They came never to be parted from what they loved, and to be free from the suffering of separation from those they held dear. With these things in mind, son of good family, any son or daughter of good family who arouses a mind of supreme reverence toward the Dharma-teacher, and who, after prostrating with five limbs to this Dharma exposition, adorns it beautifully with flowers, incense, perfume, flower garlands, unguents, parasols, victory banners, and pennants, and then recites "I pay homage to the buddhas" while prostrating, and who applauds it with the words "Excellent," and who receives it, retains it, reads it, copies it, or has it copied — all such people will come to be endowed with many excellent qualities. Even in this very life they will be endowed with many excellent qualities: they will be beautiful and lovely, pleasing to behold, free from illness, long-lived, steady of mind, endowed with mindfulness, endowed with steadiness, and worthy of being openly praised, revered, and honored by all kings, all royal consorts, all princes, all ministers, all enemies, and all sentient beings. They will also come to have abundant wealth and possessions. The fragrance of sandalwood will arise from their mouths. Their eyes will become like the petals of a blue lotus. By day and by night they will come to behold buddhas and bodhisattvas. All obscurations, including the five acts of immediate consequence and the rest, will be exhausted. The gods will guard them. At the time of death they will behold buddhas and bodhisattvas. They will pass away free from envy and with their minds undistracted. After passing away, they will be reborn in the world-system of Sukhāvatī.

Son of good family, those who receive this Dharma exposition, honor it, copy it, have it copied, read it, make offerings to it, retain it, or merely hear its name — these I prophesy with a direct prophecy: they have seen me, honored me, made images of me, and made offerings to me. Therefore let them have no doubt regarding the awakening of a buddha.

Those in whose ears this teaching has resounded should also be regarded as bodhisattvas and honored accordingly. All the karmic obscurations that have arisen through the karmic maturation of former actions — such as an unattractive form, poverty, lack of intelligence, excessive craving, separation from loved ones, disruption of royal rule, headaches, lack of food, poor clothing, harm to body and mind, a bed that is uncomfortable to the touch, and wasting illness — all such karmic obscurations will be completely exhausted through the power of hearing this meditative absorption. And one should arouse the mind in this way: "Whatever sins and non-virtuous acts of many kinds I have accumulated against sentient beings in the past while wandering through cyclic existence — I confess each and every one of them. I will clear them away. I will not conceal them." One should arouse the mind with unwavering faith in the Buddha, the Dharma, and the Saṅgha. All the karmic obscurations that would deprive that son or daughter of good family of the fruit of prosperity — whatever sinful acts they have committed and accumulated against buddhas, the Dharma, the Saṅgha, hearers, solitary buddhas, and their own parents — all of these will be completely exhausted, and great lordly powers will be fully achieved.

All the karmic obscurations that would cause that son or daughter of good family to experience the suffering of hell, to be separated from loved ones, to become blind, to be born as a woman, to be born with ambiguous sex characteristics, or — through the power of jealousy, pride, and anger — to be reborn in the realm of Yama, among hungry ghosts, or in the animal realm: all of these will be completely exhausted. Therefore, son of good family, this meditative absorption is the source of all good qualities.

Son of good family, this Dharma exposition causes the teaching to endure for a long time. It guards, protects, and shelters those sons and daughters of good family; it fully perfects all their good qualities and all their hopes; it grants prosperity, lordly power, and great happiness; it brings all wishes and desires to complete fulfillment; it exhausts all karmic residues; it thoroughly pacifies untimely death, all evil dreams, and all illness; it grants victory in every battle; it increases longevity, beauty, strength, diligence, and might; it brings all yakṣas, spirits, humans, and non-humans under one's sway; and it thoroughly pacifies plague, poison, fire, and flood.

There exist foundation dhāraṇī secret mantras through which one attains the stages up to receiving a definitive prophecy. Sons and daughters of good family who listen to them, retain them, honor them, read them, copy them out, and have them copied — all of these qualities will be obtained by them.

Then at that very moment this great earth shook in six ways, and those who had gathered in the assembly offered flowers, incense, perfume, garlands, powders, unguents, and pairs of fine cloth to the body of the Tathāgata. They paid homage, saying "Excellent!" and addressed the Blessed One: "Excellent, excellent! What are those foundation dhāraṇī secret mantras?"

The Blessed One declared:

Namaḥ sarva-tathāgatānām. Tadyathā: buddhe, su-buddhe, buddha-mate, loke vi-loke loka, atikrānte, sattva-avalokini, sarva-tathāgata-adhiṣṭhāna-adhiṣṭhite, sarva-āśā-paripūrṇe, dyuti-na-dhare, nara-deva-pūjite, tathāgata-jñāna-dade, tathāgata-adhiṣṭhānena, sarva-loka ma-sukhī bhavatu, pūrva-karma-kṣāpayatu, rakṣa bhavatu, sarva-ye-bhyas tathāgata, adhiṣṭhānena svāhā.

O sons of good family, these foundation dhāraṇī secret mantras were proclaimed and blessed by thirty Tathāgatas. I too have now set them forth for the sake of all sentient beings — for their benefit, their happiness, their protection, their refuge, their shelter, and the complete pacification of all illness — and so that they may be born in buddha fields.

Friends, if anyone thinks to themselves, "How may I obtain all those qualities proclaimed by the Tathāgatas?" — that person should rise early in the morning and, with a mind of love for all sentient beings, with a mind of compassion, with a mind of loving-kindness, and with a mind that has entirely abandoned jealousy, pride, concealment, and anger, and with a one-pointed mind, should make vast offerings to the buddhas and bodhisattvas, then pay homage to all the Tathāgatas of the ten directions. Holding in mind whatever aspiration one wishes, one should recite the mantra one hundred and eight times over flowers and offer them one by one to the Tathāgata. By doing so, all of that person's hopes will be completely fulfilled. They will behold a Tathāgata in dreams. Whatever excellent thing they desire, they will obtain. At the moment of death they will behold a Tathāgata. After passing away they will be reborn in the world-realm of Sukhāvatī. They will be endowed with long life, strength, radiance, and diligence. All their enemies will come under their sway.

When this dhāraṇī was taught, sixty thousand living beings attained patient acceptance of the non-arising of phenomena, and all of them were completely freed from all karmic obscurations and all their aspirations were perfectly fulfilled.

Then the great bodhisattva Vajrapāṇi looked out toward the ten directions and addressed the Blessed One thus: "Blessed One, there is a dhāraṇī called the Majestic Splendor of the Fearless Pledge of the Bodhisattvas. I have received and held it in the presence of the Tathāgata Fearless Array, Splendid King. Having received it, I have also taught it to all sentient beings. Blessed One, henceforth I sincerely wish that for any person into whose ear this dhāraṇī resounds or penetrates — there shall be no place for physical weakness or manifold mental afflictions, for fever, epidemic disease, bodily exhaustion, anguish of mind, untimely death, fear of water, fear of weapons, fear of poison, fear of poisoning, fear of ḍākinīs and spirits, fear of yakṣas, or assault, harm, or injury by enemies, humans, or non-humans. Therefore, Blessed One, please grant me your permission. For the sake of those who teach the Dharma and those who listen to the Dharma, for the sake of those who listen, those who make images, and those who make offerings —"

— and to fulfill all the hopes of those who hold it, and to bestow upon bodhisattvas the mudrā of their vows.

Namas sarvatathagatānām. Sarvabodhisattvānām. Arhatām. Tadyathā: Oṃ vajradhara, vajrakāya, vajrabala, vajrateja, hūṃ hūṃ, vajrapāṇi, tathāgatājñāmapālaya, smara pratigyām, sarvabyādhīna sarvapāvāna nāśaya, dehi me, yathaivasitam avara ma, yanayame abhiprāya nāyācami tantan me va samṛddhatu, he he, turu turu, āgaccha āgaccha māvilamba, darśaya, vajrakāyam, darśaya, vajrakāyam, buddha adhiṣṭhānena svāhā.

When this dhāraṇī is recited, this great earth trembles and heaves. All yakṣas and rākṣasas are utterly terrified. All the gods, humans, and those between gods and humans are filled with wonder, and the assembly exclaims, "Excellent! Excellent!" at these being set forth — these dhāraṇī secret mantras that are the foundation for the great accomplishment of sentient beings.

Then the bodhisattva, the great being Vajrapāṇi addressed the Blessed One with these words: "Blessed One, whoever desires the bodhisattva grounds, or wealth, or grain, or mastery over enjoyments, or mastery over a royal kingdom, or the holding of vidyāmantra, or long life, vitality, good complexion, diligence, and longevity — such a person should first undertake the preliminary practice, and on the eighth day of the waxing moon, he should cause a holder of the vajra fashioned from white sandalwood to be placed as a stake marker, adorned with every ornament, with a relic placed at the heart. Having caused the vajra-holder bearing the relic to breathe out the vidyāmantra properly, and having fumigated it with pure fragrant incense, he should perform a one-pointed fast under a single parasol. Then from the eighth day to the fifteenth, at a pure place where a Tathāgata image is present, he should make a maṇḍala of white sandalwood, and also set in place four full vases. In each of the four directions, he should offer tasty food, fragrant offerings, liquid libations, and torma cakes of milk-porridge, and having dressed in white cloth and made his mind one-pointed, three times each day he should make great offerings of incense, flowers, fragrant substances, and lamps. He should also make offerings to the Tathāgata, and having paid homage to the Tathāgata, he should recite the mantra one hundred and eight times over one hundred and eight red lotus flowers, and one by one press them against Vajrapāṇi. Thereupon a great sound will arise, the earth will shake, and rays of light will also appear. Following that, whatever excellent thing is desired will be granted. By the mere performance of recitation, all activities and all purposes will be perfectly accomplished. He will be free from illness, will live long, will be freed from all evil, and will also attain a thousand excellent qualities. At the time of death he will behold a thousand buddha Blessed Ones. And you, Blessed One, will also grant me the vision of yourself."

Then the Blessed One said "Excellent!" to Vajrapāṇi: "Excellent, Vajrapāṇi! Excellent! This mudrā is not to be transgressed by the world together with its gods. Toward it there should be no suspicion, no second thought, no doubt."

Then Mañjuśrī the Ever-Youthful addressed the Blessed One with these words: "Blessed One, why is this meditative absorption called 'Blessed by All Tathāgatas, Gazing upon Sentient Beings, Revealing the Adornment of All Buddha Fields'?" The Blessed One declared —

— through the blessing of all the Tathāgatas and the blessing of all bodhisattvas, one will attain all meditative absorptions and all gateways of dhāraṇī. Moreover, one will come to know fully the minds and mental factors — both the subtly moving and the overtly moving — of all sentient beings: gods, nāgas, humans, yakṣas, gandharvas (fragrance-eaters), hungry ghosts, those born in animal destinies, and all sentient beings in the world of the Lord of Death. One will know which of these sentient beings are drawing near to merit — performing virtuous actions and deeds of good conduct — and will come to gain faith in the Buddha, the Dharma, and the Saṅgha. And knowing which of them are heading toward the hells — performing harmful actions, engaging in wicked conduct, and having fallen into wrong views — one will teach the Dharma to those sentient beings, turn them away from all wickedness, establish them in the awakening of a buddha, bring all their aspirations to complete fulfillment, dispel their jealousy, pride, anger, and miserliness, and cause those very sentient beings to fully attain faith in the Buddha, the Dharma, and the Saṅgha, so that they are born in buddha fields and never again experience suffering or discomfort.

Therefore, son of good family, this meditative absorption is called "Blessed by All Tathāgatas, Gazing upon Sentient Beings, Revealing the Adornment of All Buddha Fields." Sentient beings who merely hear it will attain the ground of irreversibility, certain of never turning back.

Then Mañjuśrī asked: "Blessed One, for what reason will those sons of good family and daughters of good family receive a prophecy? Blessed One, even arhatship is difficult to attain — how much more so is unsurpassed, perfect, complete awakening unattainable — so how great will be the accumulation of merit and the austerities they must undertake?"

The Blessed One declared: "Son of good family, reckoning from when I first aroused the awakening mind up to now, all that I perfected of the perfection of generosity, the perfection of moral discipline, the perfection of patience, the perfection of diligence, the perfection of meditative concentration, and the perfection of wisdom; all that I gave away — my head, hands, feet, eyes, body, beloved sons, daughters, wives, male servants, and female servants — in order to accumulate merit and to accumulate roots of virtue: all that former accumulation of merit and roots of virtue does not come within even a hundredth part of the accumulation of merit gained by one who honors, venerates, copies, has copied, reads, and retains this Dharma teaching, who teaches it rightly to others, who thus accumulates merit, and who also honors and venerates the preachers of the Dharma.
