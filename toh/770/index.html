About this text

The Noble Ritual Procedure of Jambhala, Lord of Wealth, as Arises According to One's Wishes (Sanskrit: Ārya-jambhala-jalendra-yathālabdha-kalpa-nāma) is a short Buddhist ritual text centered on Jambhala, the deity of wealth, here also called Lord of Waters. The Tibetan title lines also refer to it as a dhāraṇī, a protective spell, called "Bringing Happiness," and the text promises practitioners the attainment of gold through its prescribed procedures. It opens with a homage to Mañjuśrī and the traditional frame of a discourse set near the island of Laṅkā, on the shore of the great ocean, where the Buddha addresses an assembly of bodhisattvas. It is Toh 770 in the Kangyur, belonging to the Tantra division and falling within the class of dhāraṇī and ritual-procedure texts.

Translation

If one does this, afterward one will obtain gold. This completes the dhāraṇī called "Bringing Happiness" of the noble one Compassionate Lord of Waters, the Holder of Harm.

In the language of India: Ārya-jambhala-jalendra-yathālabdha-kalpa-nāma. In the language of Tibet: The Noble Ritual Procedure of Jambhala, Lord of Wealth, as Arises According to One's Wishes.

Homage to the noble Mañjuśrī, who has become a youth.

Thus did I hear at one time. The Blessed One was dwelling near the island of Laṅkā, on the shore of the great ocean. He was surrounded by an assembly of great bodhisattvas, mahāsattvas, headed by Vikrāntagāmin and Candrasena, who had attained irreversibility from the qualities of a buddha, and he manifested before them.

Then the Blessed One rose into the sky, descended again, and sat at the foot of the wish-fulfilling tree, the Vicuvardha. Then the lord of the yakṣas, Dwelling-of-Harm Supreme Joy; the lord of the asuras; the nāga king Fearless; the king of the kumbhāṇḍas, Possessed of the Five Higher Knowledges; the king of the gandharvas, Five Topknots; the king of the rākṣasas, Kilikila; the king of the garuḍas, Winged One; the king of the kinnaras, Tree; Five Topknots; the lord of serpents; the divine son Cloud-Power; the divine sons Moon and Moon-Revering; and the divine son Given-by-the-Victor all came when the night had passed and the dawn was bright. They approached the Blessed One, bowed at his feet, circled him three times, and sat to one side.

At that time the colors of light from those great bodhisattvas, the mahāsattvas, were exceedingly radiant and vast, and the whole base of the wish-fulfilling tree became radiant and flourishing.

Then the Blessed One addressed those great bodhisattvas: "In the city of the great ocean, have you endured the eight wondrous and marvelous qualities, and has your realization been without obstacle or futility?" When he said this, the lord of the yakṣas, Dwelling-of-Harm, rose from his seat, arranged his upper robe over one shoulder, placed his right knee on the ground, and, putting his palms together toward the Blessed One, bowed and said: "Venerable Blessed One, in the city of the great ocean we take delight in patient endurance and in realization as it truly is."

Then the Blessed One said: "In the city of the great ocean, you leaders of the yakṣas, just as you take delight in the eight wondrous and marvelous qualities, so too, son of good family, whoever with longing and faith delights in these qualities and abides in these eight wondrous and marvelous qualities abides in pure conduct."

Then the lord of the yakṣas, Dwelling-of-Harm, said to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, for one who abides in these eight wondrous and marvelous qualities, I shall offer protection, full guardianship, full upholding, and full nurturing, along with peace and auspiciousness. Out of compassion for the world, for the benefit and happiness of many beings, Blessed One, please fully accept this essence of mine. Blessed One, this exposition of the Dharma which I have realized I have offered in full, as this essence, to Dispeller of Wrong Faith, to Kanakamuni, and to Kāśyapa, and to those fully awakened buddhas. And those fully awakened Blessed Buddhas have also fully accepted this essence."

Then the Blessed One said: "Commander of the yakṣas, speak what you are moved to say at this very moment, as the time has come." Then the yakṣa commander Nākuṭa (Gnodnas) spoke in this way, offering an inspired utterance for this purpose.

Among offerings, the fire offering is foremost.
Among the sacred sciences, the Vedic chant is foremost.
Among humans, the king is foremost.
Among waters, the ocean is foremost.
Among the stars, the moon is foremost.
Among sources of warmth, the sun is foremost.
Above, below, and across,
as many beings as there are who dwell there,
for whoever desires merit,
among assemblies, the noble Saṅgha is foremost.
In the world with its gods,
the perfectly awakened Buddha, the best of two-footed beings, is foremost.

Then once more the lord of the yakṣas, Gnodnas, having received the permission of the Blessed One, spoke his own mantra:

Namo ratnatrayāya. Namo maṇibhadrāya. Mahāyakṣasenāpataye. Oṃ jambhalajalendrāya svāhā.

Then, as soon as this king of mantras was spoken, at that very moment this world-system, the great trichiliocosm, shook in six ways. Then all the irreversible bodhisattvas and the lords of the yakṣas, with one voice, spoke these words: "Blessed One, Sugata, this exposition of the Dharma is truly wondrous and marvelous, such as has never occurred before. This exposition of the Dharma is of great benefit and astonishing. For those who suffer, who have no refuge, who have no protector, who have no place of shelter, may the Blessed One declare the refuge, the shelter, the protector, and the full guardian. For the sake of sentient beings, for the benefit and happiness of gods and humans, this essence, together with its ritual procedure, its maṇḍala, and its great secret, causes beings to fully accomplish the perfection of generosity, and through happiness itself to attain unsurpassed, perfect, complete awakening."

Then once more the retinue of those yakṣas, the great śrāvakas, the bodhisattvas, the gods, nāgas, yakṣas, and others, draped their upper robes over one shoulder, rose from their seats, joined their palms, and placing the right knee on the ground, made this request to the Blessed One.

Then once more the Blessed One said to those yakṣas together with their retinues: "Sons of noble family, listen to this exposition of the Dharma. This essence of the great yakṣa, together with its maṇḍala, its mudrā, its mantra, and its great secret mantra, is most wondrous and most marvelous. It was spoken by the Tathāgatas, and is honored and praised by all the bodhisattvas, gods, nāgas, yakṣas, gandharvas, asuras, kinnaras, mahoragas, pretas, flesh-eating spirits, and by humans and nonhumans. It is like a wish-fulfilling jewel, an excellent vase, and a wish-granting tree, for it accomplishes every aim just as one desires, for the complete benefit of sentient beings. Therefore, what I have permitted and blessed will not be heard or resound in the ears of beings who have not accumulated roots of virtue. Moreover, beings who have not accumulated roots of virtue will not even be able to listen to it, ask about it, hold it, or read it."

Yet any beings who hold this essence will be taken up by me and will never turn back. They will never turn back from unsurpassed, perfect, complete awakening. They will not be born in the lower realms. Merely seeing them will not cause illness to arise. Poverty, unhappiness, and bodily pain will not arise for them. They will bring the wishes of all beings to complete fulfillment. They will become a field of offering for all the wise.

Even if all the gods, nāgas, and yakṣas were assembled, they could not fully comprehend the qualities and benefits of holding this mantra. Indeed, I alone, together with the other Tathāgatas, arhats, perfectly complete buddhas, cannot declare its qualities and benefits in full detail to their final end. Others will not even hear a mention of it. Bodhisattvas, great bodhisattvas, śrāvakas, and the vast multitude of other gods cannot teach the qualities of this, nor can they even know it.

In brief, by the power of the Tathāgata's omniscience, endowed with ultimate qualities, there is no mantra king like this one. It has become the heart of all the Tathāgatas, the supreme essence.

For the wicked it is a place of harm,
For the destitute it brings nourishment.
It is the abode of sentient beings
And truly protects all migrators.
Knowing all faculties, it possesses the supreme rank.
It is proclaimed the Victor of Harm-Dwellers.

In brief, apart from this there is no other Tathāgata, no different Tathāgata. By holding this, one makes an offering to the Tathāgata and creates a place of worship near him. One is constantly blessed by the Tathāgatas, arhats, perfectly complete buddhas. One will attain the power of the perfections and the grounds.

Then the Blessed One looked upon the assembly gathered there and spoke these words to Gnodnas (Vighna-dwelling), lord of the yakṣas: "Lord of the Harm-Dwellers, your rites, maṇḍalas, mudrās, mantras, and great secrets have also been taught by all the buddhas of the past: Vipaśyin, Śikhin, Saṃsāra-destroyer, the Dispeller of Wrong Faith, Kanakamuni, and Kāśyapa. Now I, Śākyamuni, have also explained them. Listen well, and keep them well in mind. I now explain them for the benefit and happiness of gods and humans, for the increase of roots of virtue; this I truly know.

"Once, when I was selling firewood that I carried in the city of Kauśāmbī, a monk who lived in the wilderness came seeking firewood, worn out with exhaustion. I carried the wood to the wilderness and went to the monk. I lit a fire, healed the monk's illness, and also provided alms for a day. Then the monk, completely pleased, gave me the going for refuge in the Three, and having aroused the awakening mind, he bestowed on me the rite of the maṇḍala, mudrās, mantras, and secrets. I practiced it properly. At that time, having been given the mantra king, I meditated upon it just so, and in that very lifetime the noble Lord of the Harm-Dwellers appeared before me in direct vision. Then, completely pleased with me, he gave me the name 'Fully Liberated Hand.' There too I was consecrated as king, and I established the people of the city in the Dharma."

Having been established there as king, I firmly set all sentient beings upon the path of the ten virtuous actions. For those who sought it, and for all those who were fortunate, I fulfilled in each of their own homes, at all times and without exception, whatever would benefit them. In this and other ways, as I brought the perfection of generosity to complete fulfillment, I entered upon the ground of the bodhisattva. Gradually I attained perfect omniscience. I clearly and fully knew the means that ends all poverty and cuts off the suffering of all sentient beings. Moreover, all the Tathāgatas, arhats, perfectly complete buddhas, who have fully realized the wisdom of omniscience, themselves also practiced this king of mantras in former times, and so attained unsurpassed, perfect awakening.

Therefore, great general of the Harm-Dwellers (yakṣas), you who have become the mother of all the Tathāgatas, I shall explain the generating of the deity and the method for accomplishing every wish.

Whoever merely sees him,
from one life to the next,
is freed from every sin,
even from the actions accumulated in former times.
So too, though deluded in this life
and having committed the most unbearable sins,
whoever merely sees the Lord of the Harm-Dwellers, so full of splendor,
will meet a bodhisattva
and will be face to face with the buddhas.
And the ground of irreversibility
will soon be attained.
The virtues of merely seeing him
I shall tell; you yourself, listen.
Poverty and unhappiness,
a mind tormented by sickness,
spirits, spells, and sorcery:
by these one will not be made afraid.
Gods, nāgas, gandharvas,
asuras, garuḍas, and the rest,
and every other cause of distress:
one is always protected from them.
By merely seeing that very Harm-Dweller,
the maṇḍala taught by the buddhas,
these qualities arise here.
If one accomplishes it, what need to speak of the rest?

One who understands thus with faith,
who is steadfast in reverence and enters no other way,
who seeks this with reverence,
with the trainings, vows, and the like:
therefore, strive to keep purity.
For the supreme maṇḍala born of the mind,
in a clean and secluded place,
together with various kinds of fruit from trees,
clear the ground to the depth of a knee,
and smear it with the five products of the cow.
Pound it level and make it firm,
level like the palm of the hand,
about twenty cubits in extent.
There the Lord of the Harm-Dwellers is manifestly praised.
Scatter various flowers as offerings,
and perfume it with agarwood incense.
Choose a good day and likewise a good planet,
likewise a good hour,
in the bright fortnight, and the auspicious time.
When the teacher has properly consecrated it,
cleanse it well with the five products of the cow.
Having oneself been purified in the morning
by the king of mantra recitation,
guard it in meditative equipoise.
Above, below, and in the ten directions,
bind the directions likewise.
The wise praise the king of the Harm-Dwellers
with fragrant water one hundred and eight times.
Scatter the offerings in the east and the other directions,
binding the directions in the order given before.
Take a thread of the five colors
spun by a young girl,
smear it with saffron water,
and make the stakes of udumbara wood.
Begin from the eastern direction.
The laying of the lines is in the morning.
Make it square, with four doors,
four corners, and four sections.
In its first center,
draw the Tathāgata,
possessing all the supreme aspects,
the omniscient one, with the light of the moon.
Below him is the Lord of the Harm-Dwellers,
marked with all the characteristics,
blue in color like a cloud,
adorned with yellow ornaments.

He is adorned with anklets, bracelets, and necklaces,
and made beautiful with many kinds of jewels.
He sits upon a lion throne, ornamented with jewels
and with varied, diverse designs.
Many-colored patterns, the radiance of jewels,
a lovely, blazing radiance streaming forth,
he sits gracefully in the half-lotus posture.

In the very center, draw the excellent wish-fulfilling jewel.
With great care, make his right hand
in the gesture of supreme giving, bestowing the seed of what is wished.
Likewise, in his left hand
is a mongoose spitting forth jewels.
He sits well, above the many kinds of treasures.
Likewise a conch and a lotus
rain down a shower of jewels.
The lord is placed to the right and to the left.

On the side of that excellent jewel
is the excellent Yakṣa, silver in color.
As before, let the wise one draw him in the evening.
Lord of Conduct and Lord of Faces

Alike in form and in color,

Beneath him the seven Yakṣiṇīs

and apart from them is the Lotus-Born.
Jewel-Gazer, Giver of Wealth,
Fair Harmer and Another Fair Harmer,
Fair Navel and Great Navel,

the Yakṣa Lord, Mighty and Great,
Harm-Dweller Hulu-Hulu,
Fair Host and Moon Host,
Rigid One and Tamer,
Red Tongue and Great Belly.
On the third cloth are to be placed
the Yakṣiṇīs: Fair Gaze,
Vast One and Stainless One,
Moon Lady, Essence of Intellect, Eye,

The Yakṣiṇīs Hand and Many-Handed,
Great Cloud and Fair Hair,

Mind-Swift and Desire-Form,
Hearer of Speech and Divine Beauty,

Place them in succession, as is proper;
as before, place those in front and in the other directions.
Place Indra and the others.
These form the outer lattice.
Vajra, spear, staff,
sword, noose, banner,
wheel, trident, and the other weapons
are to be drawn as before.
Above and below is a lattice of arrows,

The wise one draws these on the outside.

Then begin the invitation.
The five colored powders are white,
yellow, red, blue, and black.
Praise them with the mantra of melody,
and place eight banners on the outside.
Likewise in the directions place vessels of arghya,
filled with water and flowers.
Make the invitation with offerings.
The mantra practitioner does this without doubt.
The Yakṣiṇīs are to be invited by the wise one

Gods and asuras are to be invited,
and the Harm-Dweller Lord, Mighty and Great.

Offer in due order, as the wise one does:
jasmine garlands, brilliantly white,

With camphor and guggulu,

Recitation is done as is proper,

Make the offerings with diligence.

At the intermediate points place vases

Cover them with mango and palāśa leaves,

As many times as the mantra of the Blessed One,
and each deity's own mantra, is recited,

Each of these is a mantra in its own right. Let the skilled practitioner recite them over the vase. Let him also bring the disciple into the maṇḍala, and let him carefully examine the one who is to receive empowerment. Let him perform the empowerment of the king. Those beings who desire accomplishments should undertake empowerment for that purpose, and should strive earnestly for the sake of adornment.

Thus, through the empowerment given according to the ritual taught for the great maṇḍala, through the teaching of the sacred commitments, and through the granting of permission, one should know that a person becomes fit for the great mudrā. Having conferred empowerment with each deity's own mantra and mudrā, one should also make vast offerings, perform the fire offering, and then gather in the deities and request them to depart. In this way one becomes fit to be taught the sacred commitments of the lords of the harmful spirits and fit to accomplish their respective mantras.

Therefore, three times each day, one should protect oneself with mantras and mudrās. One should invite the deity together with the retinue, offer water for the feet, drinking water, and the like, and present the twenty offerings of enjoyment with mantras and mudrās. Having made whatever gathered offerings one is able to, one should first do the preliminary recitation. Then I shall explain in full the collection of mudrās and mantras.

Then the lord of the Yakṣas, filled with reverence, made a request to the Blessed One. The Blessed One then looked upon the circles of his retinue, roared the lion's roar, and declared as follows: "Listen, all of you. This teaching, hard to fathom, whose very nature is great compassion, brings benefit and happiness to beings. It is the defining mark of the mudrā of the lord of the harmful spirits, together with its mantra."

Then the lord of the harmful spirits joined his palms, placed his knees upon the ground, paid homage, and remained silent.

Then the Blessed One, fearless as a lion, spoke this mantra and mudrā. Join the two hands palm to palm evenly. Bend the two middle fingers back and join them to the tips of the two ring fingers. Bend the two index fingers at the third joint and place them there. Set the thumbs to the side. Make the two little fingers like a needle. This is the root mudrā of the Blessed One. By merely forming it, the lord of the harmful spirits comes to stand near, looks upon the practitioner, and grants the supreme accomplishment.

Its mantra is: Namo ratnatrayāya. Namo maṇibhadrāya. Mahāyakṣasenāpataye. Oṃ jambhala jalendrāya svāhā.

One should bless the five places by applying the mudrā that accompanies this mantra. One then becomes equal to the lord of the harmful spirits, each in his own right.

Hold the two hands as fists and make the two little fingers in the form of a needle. This is the mudrā of the Lord of Conduct. It is to be applied specifically for protecting oneself. Its mantra is: Oṃ cārendrāya svāhā.

In the same way, make fists, place the two little fingers inside, and make the two index fingers in the form of a needle. This is the mudrā of the Lord's Face. It too is used specifically for protecting oneself. Its mantra is: Oṃ mukhendrāya svāhā.

From the mudrā of the Lord's Face, bend the third joint of the ring fingers. This is the general mudrā of all the yakṣiṇīs. Its mantra is: Oṃ ani. With this too one should protect oneself. At the time of invitation, one should also invite the yakṣiṇīs.

Above, below, and in the ten directions there are the fence, the net, and the stake. Make the two hands into fists, and make the two index fingers, each separately, like the tips of needles. This is the mudrā of the net. Its mantra is: Oṃ cārendra bandha diśa phaṭ. It is to be applied specifically for the purpose of binding.

From that same mudrā, join the two index fingers together into one, in the form of a needle. This too is a mudrā of the net. Its mantra is: Oṃ mukhenadāya svāhā. It is to be applied specifically for the purpose of the net.

Make the two hands into fists and place them downward. This is the mudrā of the stake. Its mantra is: Oṃ hrī tata.

To invite, bend the thumbs slightly from the root mudrā. This is the mudrā of invitation. Its mantra is: Ehyehi culu culu, tvara tvara, jālasayanaṃ priyā svāhā. By this procedure one should invite the Blessed One together with his retinue.

Then one should offer the arghya, the water for the guests' hands. Join the two hands with palms firmly pressed together, and place the index fingers against the sides of the thumbs. Its mantra is: Oṃ yakṣanāthaya kendhana priya paśa paśa svāhā.

Then one should offer a seat. Join the palms in the gesture of homage, hold the three fingers level, and bend them at the third joint. Join them at the middle, and set the others apart, each separate. This is the mudrā of the lion throne of the Blessed One. By merely showing it, the lord of the harmful spirits together with his retinue comes to sit upon the lion throne. Its mantra is: Oṃ jambhala jalendra indraya samakaṃ svāhā.

Then, having formed the mudrā of bathing, one should request the lord of the harmful spirits and his retinue to bathe. Again make the two hands into palms joined with one another, turn the two little fingers the opposite way, place the two ring fingers in the middle, and make the two middle fingers in the form of a needle. Outside of them, join the three joints of the middle fingers and set the two thumbs to the side. This is the bathing mudrā of all the deities. Its mantra is: Oṃ nānā priya ghaṭa kumbha jvala svāhā.

Having formed the mudrā of clothing, one should offer clothes. Again, make the two hands with the thumbs and index fingers in the form of a ring, and extend the remaining fingers. This is the mudrā of clothing. Its mantra is: Oṃ dhiri dhiri mahāsamudra aṃdhara dhaka svāhā.

One should then make the mudrā of ornaments. Cover the thumbs with three fingers of each of the two hands, curl the index fingers like a hook, turn them over each other above, and unite them into one. This is the mudrā of ornaments. Its mantra is: Oṃ megha bharaṇa jāla bughana svāhā.

The mudrā for offering fully nourishing food is this: hold the right hand as a fist, and with the left hand grasp it at the wrist, where a jewel would be bound, like a bracelet. This is the mudrā of offering nourishment. Its mantra is: Oṃ hara hara jogrāṇāya svāhā.

Then one should surely show the mudrā of the maṇḍala. From that same mudrā, extend the fingers each separately and turn them face downward. This is the mudrā of anointing the maṇḍala. Its mantra is: Oṃ kuṭi juga bharata priyāya svāhā.

The mudrā of the garland of colored powders is now shown. From the mudrā of the maṇḍala, turn the five fingers face downward and hold them so. This is the mudrā of the garland of colored powders. Its mantra is: He he phena bhali bṛiya sobhanāya svāhā.

The mudrā of fragrance is now shown. Join the two hands palm to palm, and shape the little fingers like a needle; then bend the ring finger, the middle finger, and the index finger inward. This is the mudrā of fragrance. Its mantra is: Oṃ lalā gāla gāla sugandha gandhāya svāhā.

Then the mudrā of flowers is shown. From the mudrā of fragrance, raise the thumbs straight upward. This is the mudrā of flowers. Its mantra is: Oṃ puṣpa prabhahara puṣpa megha svāhā.

The mudrā of incense is this: from the mudrā of flowers, bend the third joint slightly. This is the mudrā of incense. Its mantra is: Oṃ ru ru mahādhūpa priyāya svāhā.

The mudrā of the food offering (torma) is now shown. From the mudrā of incense, arrange all the fingers in the manner of enjoyment, and fold the two little fingers inward. This is the mudrā of the food offering. Its mantra is: Oṃ sakasaka bhari lajana gaṃ pakapalita yakṣa dehi dehi svāhā.

The mudrā of the lamp is now shown. From the mudrā of the food offering, curl all the fingers into fists, and shape the two little fingers and the two thumbs like a needle. This is the mudrā of the lamp. Its mantra is: Oṃ phuru phuru prakāsāya svāhā.

The mudrā of perfume is now shown. Turn the right hand downward over the empty hollow. This is the mudrā of perfume. Its mantra is: Oṃ kini kini gandha karṇa karṇa ghoṣaṃ metri mu svāhā.

The mudrā of the parasol is now shown. Again, face the two hands toward one another and join them as one, draw them inward, and place the thumbs to the side. This is the mudrā of the parasol. Its mantra is: Oṃ gṛhṇa cha prati achatrāṃ karakṣaka svāhā.

The mudrā of the yak-tail fan is now shown. With the thumb of the right hand press down the nail of the little finger, extend the remaining fingers as though forming a vajra, and circle them in both directions. This is the mudrā of the yak-tail fan. Its mantra is: Oṃ camara camakarbriya keṭuṭu svāhā.

The mudrā of the wind fan is now shown. With the thumb of the left hand cover the nail of the little finger, extend the remaining fingers as though forming a vajra, and circle them all around. This is the mudrā of the wind fan. Its mantra is: Oṃ bheraṃ bha bhayu bhagāya svāhā.

The mudrā of the banner is now shown. With the thumb of the right hand press down the nails of the ring finger and the little finger, bring the index and middle fingers together as one, and move them downward. This is the mudrā of the banner. Its mantra is: Oṃ tuśa tuśa kalasa haja priyāya svāhā.

The mudrā of the seat is now shown. Again, with the two hands, press down the ring fingers and the little fingers in turn, shape the middle fingers and the index fingers like a needle, and move them upward. This is the mudrā of the seat. Its mantra is: Jāla jāmaturu khejayanti priyāya svāhā. This is the mantra of the seat.

These are the twenty offerings in homage to the lord of the yakṣas. To the Tathāgatas of the past and to the bodhisattvas, one should present all these offerings, by means of their natural mantras and mudrās, together with the mentally created offerings, which one has seen and heard of. Then one should offer oneself, confess one's misdeeds, rejoice in merit, make requests to the Tathāgatas, and please them with praises, and one should recite the introductory sūtras, the inspired utterances, and the like.

Then, in the eighth hundred-and-eighth recitation, one should rouse the deity with verses of offering and praise, constantly pour out water, and give sacrificial cakes to all the gods. Having done all this, one should take up the mantra cord and visualize oneself as resembling the lord of the yakṣas. The syllable Oṃ is to be placed on the crown of the head. The syllable Jaṃ is to be placed at the throat. The syllable Bha is to be placed on the right hand. The syllable Lā is to be placed on the left. The syllable Ja is to be placed at the heart. In the right and left thighs, Lendra is to be placed. The syllable Ye is to be placed at the secret place. Then the wise one should place Svāhā on the soles of the feet.

These are the secret heart essence of the lord of the yakṣas, the Victor, widely renowned. The wise one who knows these will accomplish the siddhis. One should recite as much as one is able. Having first protected oneself, one should offer, in meditative equipoise, whatever merit there is for the benefit of sentient beings. Then, having taken what one wishes, one should request the deity to depart. From the earlier gesture of invitation, one makes the movement by extending the thumb, and requests departure with the mantra. The mantra is: Oṃ ḍi ḍi kītā te bhāgata ajra gaccha gaccha svāhā.

By this procedure one should first perform the preliminary recitation a hundred thousand times, and afterwards accomplish whatever one wishes.

Then the assembly, together with all its members, spoke to the Blessed One thus: "As long as this procedure is followed, all sentient beings will be greatly relieved. Blessed One, this is wonderful indeed. Sugata, this is wonderful indeed. Blessed One, please explain the rite of the lord of the yakṣas, which brings special joy to all sentient beings, accomplishes every aim, and is of great compassionate nature, and which accomplishes without remainder the aims of those who wish to hear it."

Then the Blessed One looked upon the assembly together with all its members and taught this rite of the yakṣa. Having performed the preliminary recitation by the rite described before, one should then make offerings of perfume and flowers to the lord of the yakṣas. Entering the water of the ocean, one recites ten thousand times, and the preliminary practice is thereby complete.

Then, on the first day of the waxing moon, one goes to the bank of a river that flows into the ocean and enters the water until it reaches the waist. Remaining in a day-and-night fast, if one performs a burnt offering of water, a hundred thousand handfuls, one will obtain a thousand sang of gold.

By this same procedure, entering the water until it reaches the knees and performing a burnt offering ten thousand times, one will obtain ten villages, or will be granted a thousand sang of gold.

In that same river, entering the water up to the waist, one smears lotuses with curd, butter, and honey and performs ten thousand burnt offerings, and one will find a vase of treasure.

Again, by this same easy procedure, if one performs eight thousand burnt offerings of zarma flowers, one will bring a king under one's power.

Lighting a fire with sticks of the aśoka tree, one smears sprigs of apāmārga with butter and honey and performs ten thousand burnt offerings, and one will bring a wheel-turning king under one's power.

Kindling a fire well with sticks of apāmārga, one anoints apāmārga seeds with curd, butter, and honey, holds them with the ring finger and thumb, and one will see a treasure in one's own house.

Then, day by day, smearing sesame flowers with curd, butter, and honey, if one performs eight thousand burnt offerings at each of the three times of day, one will obtain whatever one seeks. In a single day one will find food and drink, even without begging, for oneself and two hundred attendants.

If one smears flowers of the aśoka tree with curd, butter, and honey, and at each of the three times of day performs eight thousand burnt offerings, one will obtain whatever one prays for.

If one burns aśoka flowers together with apāmārga seeds, smears them with curd, butter, and honey, and performs ten thousand burnt offerings, a king's daughter will come under one's power. If one speaks her name and performs the same burnt offering eight thousand times, one will obtain that very king's daughter.

Taking red flowers that are fully open, one goes to a river that flows into the ocean, enters it until the water reaches the waist, and performs ten thousand burnt offerings. Then whoever perceives the fragrance of that flower, or touches it, all of them will come under one's power. By this same rite one can summon yakṣiṇīs.

Or, at that same water, if one performs a thousand burnt offerings with wood of the apāmārga, the Sun will grant a boon. In the same way, if one makes burnt offerings with mandārava flowers, Indra will grant a boon. With sesame flowers, Brahmā will grant a boon. With betel flowers, the god of wealth will grant a boon.

While reciting that very mantra, one holds the image with the little finger and the thumb, and for as long as one recites the mantra ten thousand times, for so long one will find whatever one desires. Then one will be given all accomplishments. By reciting it a hundred thousand times, one will summon a king. By reciting it two hundred thousand times, a yakṣiṇī will come of her own accord. By reciting it three hundred thousand times, one will summon all beings.

Or else, enduring hardship, one recites in silence for six months, and one will obtain whatever one wishes in mind.

One makes an image from the wood of the royal arka tree, adorned with all its ornaments in their natural, unaltered colors. In front of it, one recites in silence for six months. One recites the mantra ten thousand times into the palm of the left hand. Then one goes to a place where a treasure lies, places the hand upon it, and recites the mantra one hundred and eight times, and all treasures will be summoned.

On the fifth day of the bright fortnight, one stays at the root of an aśoka tree, observing the fast throughout day and night, and eating only food permitted for the fast. If one performs twenty-one burnt offerings, one will be able to accomplish all that is needed.

If one mixes the juice of sugarcane with curd and offers a thousand oblations into the fire, one will be given a thousand golden sarangs.

One smears sticks of apāmārga well with curd, butter, and honey, and, abiding in a fast day and night, performs eight thousand burnt offerings. One's wisdom will greatly increase, and one will be able to retain a thousand verses.

At the root of an arka tree, by this rite, one makes an image of the six-fingered one, endowed with a crown and a topknot. In front of it, with sticks of apāmārga, one recites:

Namo ratnatrayāya. Namo maṇibhadrāya. Mahāyakṣasenāpataye. So ma ma so vidkriṇi ya miriti maniriti svāhā.

If one performs a thousand burnt offerings with this mantra, one will obtain a hundred dongtses. With the essence of the harmful spirits, one will obtain a boon. Abiding in this way, one enters water up to the knees, and by reciting six hundred thousand times, one will obtain wealth.

On the wood of the nimba tree, one makes a fine jewel four finger-widths square, drawn by a painter who keeps the fast. One makes offerings near it and presents a torma. Compassion without dependence, teaching rightly and just as things are, the chief of beings through miraculous power, faultless in every respect, the afflictions of those who suffer are exhausted. Such is the meditator of Jambhala.

There is no counting of lunar days and no consideration of the lunar mansions. There is no ritual of fasting that is required. Of the wood of the arka or the nimba tree, or of other trees, one makes the form of a fine jewel. Sitting before it at midday, one makes offerings of perfumes, flowers, and so on. Then one makes pills from white mustard and sugar mixed with hot black butter, places them in the belly of the fine jewel, and recites the knowledge-mantra eight thousand times. One will then obtain a hundred dongtse. It is to be exhausted right there.

If for six months one performs a water burnt offering within water, one will obtain a hundred thousand. As it is prescribed, one presents a torma of the three whites together with offerings. Then, with sticks of the aśvattha tree smeared with curd, butter, and honey, one performs eight thousand burnt offerings. One will hear a voice from the sky, or the fruit of the knowledge-holder one desires in mind will be accomplished, and what one does with the mind will be fulfilled.

Then, with many black sesame seeds mixed with curd, butter, and honey, one performs eight thousand burnt offerings, and one will obtain the twelve best of villages. On the bank of a river, one sets down a fine jewel, eats butter and milk porridge, and before a stūpa containing relics recites one hundred thousand times. Fasting through the night under the open sky, one dedicates eight thousand jasmine flowers, one by one, with praise, and offers them to the stūpa. One will then be given thousands of livestock.

On the eighth day of the dark fortnight, one fasts through the night, lights a fire with sticks of the jambu tree, and, with black mustard smeared with curd, butter, and honey, performs eight thousand burnt offerings. One presents a pure meal of the three sweets and a torma, and when the burnt offering is complete, a brahmin will come and say, "What shall I do for you?" To him one says, "Give me five dongtse." He says, "So it shall be," and vanishes, and from then on gives them every day. This is to be exhausted without remainder.

After that, Śrī (the goddess of fortune) will come. To her who has come, one offers a lotus and presents arghya water scented with perfume. She says, "Only child, what shall I do?" The practitioner should say, "Bestow upon me many treasures." She says, "So it shall be," and vanishes. From then on, wherever one goes, she brings and gives treasure, and what she gives never runs out.

Before a fine jewel, one recites for half a month, and on the full-moon night one abides in fasting. Having offered a torma free of meat, one then places the fine jewel at one's heart, setting down the nameless finger, and for as long as one recites, the jewel will stir. Then every day she will give five dongtse each time. This is to be exhausted without remainder.

Going to a great charnel ground, one makes a stūpa from the ashes of the charnel ground. Living on alms food and bathing three times a day, one recites three hundred thousand times. On the fourteenth day of the dark fortnight one abides in a night fast. Having made a vast offering to the Blessed One, one should exhaust it entirely, leaving no remainder.

In a great charnel ground one presents śatapuṣpā to the fine jewel, and offers incense mixed with agaru and turuṣka. Placing it face down, one recites for as long as it takes to reach midnight. Then the lord of flesh-eaters comes of his own accord. One presents him with the torma set out earlier, and offers beer to his retinue as well. Then the practitioner should make the request. He says, "I am pleased with you." The practitioner says, "Each day, give me one servant." He says, "So it shall be," and vanishes. Further, there is the assembly of rites spoken by the Tathāgata. Climbing to a mountain peak, one sets down a fine jewel a cubit in size, and living on milk and milk gruel, bathing three times a day, one recites three hundred thousand times. Wherever and whatever one wishes, houses are built there, divine mounts are conjured, and to a thousand people who greatly desire it, the foods they greatly desire are given. One should recite until the great ocean itself comes. Within a cow pen, or at a confluence of rivers, one lives on alms food or eats milk porridge. If one says, "I am pleased," then he will grant whatever one desires of the eight supreme attainments: kingship; treasure; walking through miraculous power; becoming a knowledge-holder; bringing everything under one's control; and power over nāgas, yakṣas, rākṣasas, spirits, flesh-eaters, kumbhāṇḍas, and the reptilian beings, all of whom come under one's control. If one recites four hundred thousand times, one brings benefit both to oneself and to others, and one will protect one's land.
