About this text

Toh 662 in the Kangyur is a dhāraṇī text known in Sanskrit as the Ārya-Vasudhārā-nāma-dhāraṇī and in Tibetan as the Phags pa nor gyi rgyun zhes bya ba'i gzungs, meaning "The Noble Dhāraṇī Called Stream of Wealth." It belongs to the dhāraṇī section of the Kangyur and takes the form of a sūtra-style discourse in which the Buddha, dwelling in the city of Kauśāmbī amid a great assembly of monks and bodhisattvas, is approached by a faithful householder named Excellent Moon. The text centers on the dhāraṇī of Vasudhārā, a deity associated with abundance and prosperity, and presents the sacred formula along with the merits and blessings conferred upon those who uphold it.

In Sanskrit: Ārya-vasudhārā-nāma-dhāraṇī. In Tibetan: The noble dhāraṇī called Stream of Wealth.

Homage to all buddhas and bodhisattvas.

Thus did I hear. At one time the Blessed One was dwelling in the city of Kauśāmbī, in the great and magnificent forest known as the Thornwood, together with a great community of monks numbering some five hundred, and with very many great bodhisattvas endowed with all the qualities of a buddha. At that time, in the great city of Kauśāmbī, there lived a householder named Excellent Moon, whose faculties were at peace and whose mind was at peace, and who possessed many sons, daughters, and servants, and was possessed of faith, and of great faith. He went to where the Blessed One was staying, and having arrived, he bowed his head to the feet of the Blessed One, circumambulated him many hundreds of thousands of times, and sat down to one side.

Having sat to one side, the householder Excellent Moon addressed the Blessed One in these words: "If, having asked, the Blessed One — the Tathāgata, the arhat, the perfectly and completely awakened Buddha — would grant me the occasion to ask, I would put certain questions to the Blessed One."

When he had spoken thus, the Blessed One said to the householder Excellent Moon: "Householder, ask whatever you wish. By prophetically answering whatever question you pose, I shall gladden your mind."

When the Blessed One had spoken thus, the householder Excellent Moon said, "Excellent!" to the Blessed One, and listening respectfully to the Blessed One, he addressed the Blessed One in these words: "Blessed One, how may a son or daughter of noble family who has become poor be rid of their poverty? And how, if struck by a disease of the bile, may they be rid of that disease?"

Thereupon the Blessed One, knowing full well, said to the householder Excellent Moon: "Householder, why do you ask for the sake of the poor?"

When the Blessed One had spoken thus, the householder Excellent Moon replied to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, I myself have many who are poor, many who are sick, and many sons and daughters and servants. For that reason, may the Blessed One please teach well the enumeration of Dharma by which poor sentient beings may be freed from poverty, those struck by disease of the bile may be freed from that disease, and beings may come to possess wealth, grain, storehouses, and many treasuries; and may come to be loved, pleasing, delightful to behold, and powerful; and may be free from the need to seek out a generous patron; and may come to be abundantly possessed — without need to beg — of riches, gold, wealth, granaries and treasuries, jewels, pearls, vajras, lapis lazuli, conch, crystal, coral, golden wheat, and all manner of abundance; and may the servants and retinue of the household be made stable."

When he had spoken thus, the Blessed One said to the householder Excellent Moon: "Householder, in the past, beyond the passage of incalculable eons, at that time, there was the Blessed One, the Tathāgata, the arhat, the perfectly and completely awakened Buddha, endowed with knowledge and conduct, the Sugata, knower of the world, tamer of persons to be tamed, unsurpassed guide, teacher of gods and humans —

the Blessed One, the Tathāgata called Ocean-Voice of the Vajra-Bearer, appeared in this world. Son of good family, I heard this dhāraṇī called Stream of Wealth from that Tathāgata, held it, retained it, recited it, mastered it fully, rejoiced in it, and then taught it in full and in detail to others as well. Son of good family, I now teach it again.

Son of good family, by the power of this dhāraṇī, human beings will not be harmed. Non-humans will not be harmed. Yakṣas will not be harmed. Rākṣasas will not be harmed. Hungry ghosts (pretas) will not be harmed. Flesh-eaters will not be harmed. Elemental spirits will not be harmed. Pot-spirits will not be harmed. Forgetfulness-spirits will not be harmed. Oppressor-spirits will not be harmed. Corpse-reanimating spirits will not be harmed. Gods will not be harmed. Asuras will not be harmed. Those who feed on impurity, those who drink urine, those who drink blood, those who eat flesh, those who drink pus, those who eat fat, those who eat feet, those who eat mucus, those who drink what flows from an open wound, those who breathe in breath, and those who eat the elements — none of these will be able to do harm.

Son of good family, whichever son of good family or daughter of good family has this dhāraṇī called Stream of Wealth in their home, carries it at their heart, holds it in their hand, has it placed in a book, has heard it, has fully mastered it, has recited it, has held it, has rejoiced in it, or has taught it widely and in detail to others — upon that son of good family or daughter of good family there shall come long-lasting benefit, helpfulness, virtue, accomplishment, happiness, and an excellent harvest. Whoever makes offerings to the Tathāgatas and recites this dhāraṇī called Stream of Wealth twice, three times, or four times at midnight — the gods will be delighted, and filled with joy in the teaching of the Tathāgata, joyful that the Dharma has been proclaimed, joyful that the Saṅgha has been proclaimed, joyful in those who speak the Dharma, they will come themselves with supreme intention and send down a rain of grain.

O son of good family, whoever makes offerings to the Tathāgatas, arhats, perfectly and fully awakened buddhas, and recites the words of the secret mantra of the Stream of Wealth dhāraṇī for a single night — for that person the accomplishment will come to pass. For the purpose of increase, whether in one's own home or another's home, in a clean and pure treasury or in any place whatsoever, one should make a square maṇḍala of sandalwood for the Tathāgata Avalokiteśvara, all the buddhas, all the bodhisattvas, and the deities of the secret mantras, and recite it for a single night. "Excellent!" said the householder Sucandra to the Blessed One. Having heard this Stream of Wealth dhāraṇī from the Blessed One, he was satisfied, joyful, glad, and delighted — filled with great happiness — and bliss and mental ease arose in him. He bowed his head at the feet of the Blessed One and addressed the Blessed One with these words: "Blessed One, I have taken up what is called the Stream of Wealth. I have received it in the dhāraṇī. I have grasped it, recited it, thoroughly mastered it, and rejoiced in it. And I will teach it widely and thoroughly to others as well."

At that very instant, the storehouses of the householder Sucandra were completely filled. Then the householder Sucandra circumambulated the Blessed One many hundreds of thousands of times, bowed his head at the feet of the Blessed One, and departed from the Blessed One's presence.

Then the Blessed One said to the Venerable Ānanda: "Ānanda, go and look upon the home of the householder Sucandra — abundant with wealth and grain, perfectly complete — and upon his great treasury and the storehouses filled to the full."

Then the Venerable Ānanda, heeding the Blessed One's words, set out toward the great city of Kauśāmbī and toward the home of the householder Sucandra. Upon arriving and entering within, he saw that it was filled with abundant wealth and grain, perfectly complete, with precious jewels in abundance, a great treasury of all manner of possessions, and storehouses entirely full. Seeing this, he was satisfied, joyful, glad, and delighted — great happiness arose in him, and bliss and mental ease were born — and he went to where the Blessed One was.

Then the Venerable Ānanda, astonished and filled with joy and mental ease, addressed the Blessed One with these words: "Blessed One, what is the cause and what is the condition by which the householder Sucandra, who is so rich in wealth, now has great treasuries and storehouses so abundantly filled with wealth and grain?"

This dhāraṇī-mantra was spoken, expounded, rejoiced in, analyzed, praised, elucidated, illuminated, generated, blessed, sealed with the Dharma mudrā, extolled, beautifully expressed, made comprehensible, and commended by all the Tathāgatas — for the sake of all sentient beings who are impoverished, tormented by manifold illness, threatened by fear, and harmed by the enmity of other beings.

King of the Dharma, free from old age and death,
gone to the far shore of primordial wisdom —
O Buddha, O hero, to you I bow.

Then the Venerable Ānanda, satisfied, joyful, delighted, and rejoicing, filled with joy and mental ease, addressed the Blessed One with these words: "Blessed One, what is the name of this presentation of the Dharma, and how should it be held?"

Translator's notes

The title Ārya-vasudhārā-nāma-dhāraṇī renders the Tibetan "noble dhāraṇī called Stream of Wealth," where vasu means wealth and dhārā means stream or flow. This is the canonical dhāraṇī of the goddess Vasudhārā, a deity of abundance, though she is not yet named at this point in the text.

The epithet string at the text's close — "endowed with knowledge and conduct" — renders rig pa dang zhabs su ldan pa, the standard Tibetan for the Sanskrit vidyācaraṇasampanna, one of the ten epithets of a buddha. The passage breaks off mid-sentence in the source, and the translation faithfully reflects that open ending.

The phrase rendered "disease of the bile" translates bro nad, a bilious or jaundice-related illness in Tibetan medical terminology, here used both literally and as an example of physical affliction that the dhāraṇī is said to alleviate. The long catalogue of spirit-classes that "will not be harmed" uses a passive construction in Tibetan; the more natural English rendering in protective dhāraṇī literature is "will not be able to harm," but the Tibetan literally says they will not harm or harass — both readings are defensible. The rendering chosen preserves ambiguity.

The mantra text is transliterated directly from the Tibetan phonetic rendering of Sanskrit; individual syllables reflect the Tibetan orthographic conventions for Sanskrit and may diverge slightly from any reconstructed Sanskrit original. No attempt has been made to normalise or emend the mantra, as dhāraṇī texts are transmitted as received. The epithet Avalokiteśvara appears here in a somewhat unusual syntactic position — listed alongside "the Tathāgata" — which may reflect a specific local tradition or a slight irregularity in the Tibetan source text. No emendation has been made; the text is rendered as it stands.

The city name Kauśāmbī is transliterated from the Tibetan phonetic rendering ཀཽ་ཤྭམྦཱི. The passage contains a list of verbs describing how the Tathāgatas have treated this dhāraṇī, rendered as a flowing English series. The Tibetan ཆོས་ཀྱི་ཕྱག་རྒྱས་བཏབ་པ, literally "sealed with the Dharma seal," is rendered "sealed with the Dharma mudrā" to preserve the technical character of the term.
