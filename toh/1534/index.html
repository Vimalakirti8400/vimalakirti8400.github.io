About this text

The Ritual Procedure of the Path, known in Tibetan as Lam gyi rab tu bya ba, is a short tantric ritual manual. Its Sanskrit title is not preserved in the source title lines. Opening with homage to glorious Vajrasattva, it sets out the rites by which a disciple is brought into the maṇḍala of Cakrasaṃvara: the rite of approach, the rite of preparation, and the rite of empowerment. It begins with the disciple's formal request, in which he offers a maṇḍala, prostrations, and flowers, and asks the master for empowerment into the king of maṇḍalas, the Manifest Arising of the Wheel of Cakrasaṃvara. The master then gathers ritual substances and assigns the disciple recitation of the mantra of the wrathful Amṛtakuṇḍalin. The work is Toh 1534 in the Tengyur, where it belongs among the tantric commentarial and ritual treatises associated with the Cakrasaṃvara cycle.

Translation

Here is contained the Ritual Procedure of the Path.

Homage to glorious Vajrasattva.

There are the rite of approach, the rite of preparation, and the rite of empowerment.

For the approach, the first step is making the request. The disciple, endowed with faith and wisdom, offers a maṇḍala, makes three prostrations, and offers three flowers. Planting the right knee firmly on the ground and joining the palms, he requests three times: "Master, please grant me empowerment into the king of maṇḍalas, the Manifest Arising of the Wheel of Cakrasaṃvara."

The master grants permission, saying, "Then I shall perform the approach. You must gather the substances." He gives the mantra of the wrathful Amṛtakuṇḍalin as the mantra for recitation, and has the disciple recite it a hundred and eight times at dusk and at dawn.

Then the master himself, meditating through the yoga of Lūyipa, or as the Solitary Hero, or by the womb-birth method, performs the vajra recitation and the ḍoli recitation. Through the yoga of the four sessions, he generally recites the root mantra a hundred thousand times, or else a hundred thousand of whichever he chooses, the heart mantra or the near-heart mantra of the father and mother. He recites the mantra of each deity of the retinue ten thousand times, and Sumbhani a hundred thousand times. This is the approach by number.

Alternatively there is the approach by signs: in a dream the deity confers empowerment and grants permission by saying "Excellent," or even by a gesture; that also suffices. If circumstances do not allow this, one may perform one portion of recitation, vividly visualize the deity with the mind, think that permission has been granted, and then proceed; this is allowed by the command of the Lord. At that time one should offer the five offerings and one torma each, without attachment.

Then, in order to urge the master, the disciple, preceded by a maṇḍala offering, should make the request again as before. The master asks, "Have the substances been gathered or not?" Some reply that they have been gathered, and some that they have not.

Then comes the clearing away of evil and the protection. At the disciple's navel, on a sun, visualize a blue hūṃ. At his heart, from a black kaṃ of evil, visualize the form of a piśāca, emaciated, with thin limbs and hair standing on end. The light of the hūṃ pushes it upward and it comes out of the right nostril. That hūṃ turns into the eight goddesses of the gates and corners, who cut it up with their curved knives and eat it. Then, becoming hūṃ again, they dissolve back in through that same nostril; so one should visualize.

For the protection, by writing ha on the left hand, visualize a crossed vajra, and placing it on the disciple's crown, think that his body has become vajra. Recite Sumbhani a hundred and eight times, and visualize the crossed vajra remaining on his crown. Then, in an instant, visualize the disciples as two-armed Herukas, and make offerings, torma, and so on.

Then, in front, the chief of the disciples and the others, preceded by offering a maṇḍala, are made to prostrate and to join their palms, and they recite three times:

I go for refuge to the Three Jewels.
I confess each and every evil.
I rejoice in the virtue of beings.
I hold in mind the awakening of the buddhas.
I generate the mind set on supreme awakening.
I take all sentient beings as my guests.
By practicing the supreme and delightful conduct of awakening,
may I attain buddhahood for the benefit of beings.

Then make three drops of scent at the heart, the throat, and the crown, and, beginning with a touch of the vajra, say hūṃ āḥ oṃ. One should visualize them in order as a lotus and wheels, marked in order with those same three syllables, the lower two on suns and the wheel on a moon.

Then, saying oṃ pu jā o a and so on, and touching the twenty-four places with the ring finger of the left hand, one should imagine them as those lands and their sacred sites.

Then, for a man, visualize oṃ ha and so on at the heart and the other points; for a woman, bless the navel and the other points with oṃ baṃ and so on.

Then make offerings to them: flowers to the head, incense to the nose, lamps to the eyes, scent to the heart, and food, visualized as offered to the mouth. This is done with oṃ āḥ bajra puṣpe hūṃ and the like.

Then the tooth-stick is cast. In front, on a patch of ground the size of an ox-hide smeared with fresh cow dung, draw an eight-petaled lotus enclosed within a square courtyard, and arrange nine heaps of flowers on it. There the disciple faces east or north. A tooth-stick that is neither crooked nor split, its tip adorned with leaves, smeared with sandalwood, and tied with a garland of flowers, is handed to him with oṃ bajra da na da kaṣṭha bi ma la bi śuddhe svāhā. Having prepared it according to the rite, have him cast it from between his joined palms into the center of the maṇḍala.

Here the sign of the tooth-stick should be examined. If it points east, it signifies the accomplishment of pacifying. If it points north, enriching. If it points west, magnetizing. If it points south, fierce rites. If it points upward, the divine accomplishment of a vidyādhara. If it points downward, the accomplishment of the underworld. If it falls outside the maṇḍala, there is no accomplishment. So one should examine it.

Then, for the purification of speech, recite the mantra of the Yoginī of All Wheels over scented water mixed with the five nectars. Then, saying oṃ hrīḥ bi śuddha dharmāḥ sarba pā paṃ ni ca ā syā bi śo dha ya sarba bi kalpa a pa na ya hūṃ, pour it three times into the palm of the disciple's left hand.

Then give kuśa grass, over which the twenty-two-syllable mantra has been recited three times, to serve as pillow and mat. Make a thread the length of each disciple's own height and arm span, twisted threefold, and tie three knots, one at each third. Recite the heart mantra seven times over it, and tie it on the upper arm.

Then one should arouse their enthusiasm by teaching the Dharma:

In the world the omniscient ones
Are like the udumbara flower:
Only rarely, once in a hundred times,
Do they appear; otherwise they do not appear.
Rarer even than that
Is the appearance of the way of secret mantra conduct,
By which one is able, not passing into nirvāṇa,
To accomplish the incomparable benefit of sentient beings.
If whatever evil was done in the past
Over many tens of millions of eons
Is all purified
Even by seeing a maṇḍala such as this,
What need is there to speak of practicing
The way of secret mantra of boundless renown?
If one recites the secret mantra of the Protector,
One will attain the unsurpassed state.
Those whose understanding
Of this supreme conduct is utterly stainless
Have cut off completely the stream of the lower destinies,
Suffering and its origin.
For in this teaching given by the Victors
Together with their heirs,
You great-souled ones
Have today found an incomparable gain.
This supreme path, from which
The Great Vehicle greatly arises, is glorious;
Through it you will, in the future,
Become tathāgatas.

Having proclaimed the sacred Dharma with these words, one performs protection with Sumbhani and says, "Whatever dream you see at dawn, tell it to me." Then one has them lie down in the lion's sleeping posture at the edge of the maṇḍala ground. The master enters the maṇḍala house, and for the sake of protection should recite Sumbhani. This is the rite of the disciples' preparation.

Next the preparation of the vases is to be explained. To begin with, there may be ten vases, or six, or two, or even just one. They should be free of faults such as "root" and "black"; made of materials such as gold, silver or clay; turned white in color; marked with the emblem of the family; their mouths adorned with branches of milk-sap trees; their necks wrapped with cloth matching the body color of the family; filled with milky water from a sacred site, together with the five scents, namely sandalwood, camphor, saffron, musk and huluka; and filled with the five nectars, the five jewels, the five grains and the five medicines. Set each vessel on top of a lotus seat beneath it, spread a parasol above it, and, placing them in the maṇḍala in due order while reciting the Ḍākinī of All Buddhas once for each, consecrate them.

First, in the system of ten vases, there is one for the Victorious vase (vijaya); one each for the four mothers, together with the four lotus vessels; and one each for the wheels of mind, speech, body and commitment, making nine. With the all-action vase, there are ten. In the system of six, there is one for each of the five wheels, which with the Victorious vase makes six; the all-action vase is included in the wheel of commitment. Two is by the division into method and wisdom; the all-action vase is included in the principal one. In the system of one, all the deities of the maṇḍala without exception are gathered into one, and the all-action vase is also gathered into it. That is for empowering householders, not for novices and above, because the tantra rules out doing it with only one.

"Root" is a fault of shape, and "black" is a fault of color; the vases are to be free of these. As for the measure, it is twenty-one finger-widths up to the lip, and sixteen in breadth, without a spout. The neck is six finger-widths, the lip eight finger-widths, and the opening of the neck two finger-widths. For other activities, such as fierce rites, various other measures are stated. "Turned white" applies to those that are not white in color by nature.

As for the emblem of the family: for the Victorious vase, it is a vajra and a vajra curved knife placed within a kapāla, on the hub of a variegated lotus in the center of a source of phenomena. For the four mothers of the families, it is a curved knife and a kapāla. For the wheel of mind, a vajra and a curved knife. For the wheel of speech, a lotus and a curved knife. For the wheel of body, a wheel and a curved knife. For the wheel of commitment, a kapāla and a curved knife. For the all-action vase, a crossed vajra and a curved knife.

The milk-sap trees are the cūta and so forth. The five jewels are gold, silver, sapphire, pearl and coral. Barley, wheat, sesame, rice and māṣa beans are the five grains. Pṛhasti, kaṇḍakari, aparājita, daṇḍotpala and hasadeva are the five medicines. The lotus and parasol are made of paper. The maṇḍala is made of yellow powder alone, and on it one draws only the form of the seats and the celestial palace, without deities or hand emblems. If one is unable to do this, one makes something like the form of the maṇḍala on which the tooth-sticks were cast earlier.

Then one performs the consecration of the vases. Having prepared the various articles of offering, such as flowers, and the tormas, the master himself meditates fully on the meditative absorption, withdraws the maṇḍala, and abides in the meditative absorption of the two-armed one. Then, in an instant, one imagines the complete maṇḍala wheel in the Victorious vase. For the four mothers of the families, there is one mother and one kapāla each; and likewise for the wheels of mind, speech, body and commitment. Then, with the blazing mantra and mudrā, one invites before oneself the maṇḍala wheel complete in every aspect, and dissolves it into the deities of the vases. One imagines that they all melt entirely and become filled with the water of the nectar of wisdom.

Then, touching the five places of the vase with the vajra mudrā, one should say three times: oṃ vajra padma a mṛ ta su bi śu dha dharma dhā tu sva bhā va sarba dharmā na su bi śuddha dharma dhā tu sarba na ku ru ku ru svā hā.

Then one places a vajra joined to a garland of flowers on top of the vase and holds it with the left fist. Holding a rosary at the heart with the right hand, one recites one hundred times for the principal of each wheel, and twenty-one times for the deities of the retinue. For the all-action vase, one recites Sumbhani one hundred and eight times. Then one makes offering with the five outer offerings, the sixteen mind-born goddesses and the seven precious things, and, with hands holding the censer and the vajra and bell, one praises with these words:

Glorious Vajraḍākinī,
Universal monarch of the ḍākinīs,
Endowed with the three bodies and five wisdoms,
Protector of beings, to you I bow.
However many vajra ḍākinīs there are,
Who cut the bonds of conceptual thought
And fully engage for the sake of the world,
To all of them I ever bow.

Then, reciting the hundred-syllable mantra, one should bless the tormas.

The passage ending "Then one should consecrate the heroes' feast and the feast gathering" is the rite of consecrating the vase.

Next, the maṇḍala of colored powders. The maṇḍala is to be constructed, and on a painted cloth one visualizes the maṇḍala vividly before one. Having performed the self-entry, one should then empower the disciple. For this there may be four masters in the four directions, or one may also do it alone. Having bathed with scented water together with the five nectars, one puts on the adornments, the garments, the five mudrās and so forth, and sits on a high seat on the south side of the maṇḍala, facing north. Then, as before, one meditates on the samādhi in full. Abiding in the samādhi of the two-armed one, one receives in due order from the principal deity of the maṇḍala the seven empowerments, the third, and the culmination of the fourth. That is the self-entry.

Next, the entry of the disciple. Outside the curtain one has the disciple offer a maṇḍala and prostrate, and he stands holding flowers in his cupped hands. From inside, the master should say, "The command is sealed." "I am great bliss, the fortunate one" is the disciple's reply. This is done three times. "For what purpose do you enter?" "For the accomplishment of the Great Seal (mahāmudrā)" is the disciple's reply. Then one places the conch on the crown of his head, pours water three times into his cupped palms, and purifies him with kuśa water over which Sumbhani has been recited.

Then the rite of descent is to be performed:

Great vermilion together with the five nectars
is smeared upon a charnel-ground cloth;
one part of the gathered powder,
and black cat's dung,
in a share equal to that, is added;
in a share equal to that, use black guggulu.
Taking charcoal from a charnel ground,
mix the incense in the vessel for the guest-water offering.
The body maṇḍala, complete,
is set out upon the disciple's body;
by the rays of light that issue from it,
imagine that the hosts of heroes and ḍākinīs
enter him without interruption.
Holding the disciple by the hand,
recite "Ḍākinī of All Buddhas"
with a one-pointed mind.

If the body descends, bodily miracles such as leaping occur. If the speech descends, he will speak of matters that lie beyond the reach of the senses. If the mind descends, qualities such as the five superknowledges arise. If wisdom descends, he will abide in inconceivable samādhi. Then one withdraws the descent, places a hand on his crown, and protects him by reciting that same heart mantra seven times. Then one puts armor on him. Then one ties on a blindfold of multicolored or yellow silk cloth that has been purified with water over which Sumbhani has been recited, and one also gives him an upper and a lower garment. The mantra for tying the blindfold is this: oṃ dvadaśa aṃganirodhakaṛṇi hūṃ phaṭ.

Then the vows are to be conferred:

The vows to be given by those who travel the path are these: abandon killing, falsehood, others' wealth, others' wives, and likewise the drinking of liquor; these five are the sins that, by the vajra noose of saṃsāra, destroy one's own virtue. Those who have arisen at whatever time, O thirty, O guru of men, are to be fully given these in his name; O lord of all, destroyer of the terrors of existence, you too must keep this command. Gambling, unclean food, evil speech, the rites of bhūtas, and the religion of the lord of the asuras; cattle, women and men: these ten, O guru of men, and the five, you must not harm. Rancor toward close friends, lords and masters, likewise, O thirty, O guru of men, toward those who trust and toward the Saṅgha; and what is called the clinging of the senses: by these twenty-five vows of the lord of men.

Having conferred the vows in this way, one places in the center of the lotus of his hands a single flower over which that same mantra has been recited three times seven, and with śrī herukaya bidmahe bidyārājāya dhimahe svahā one leads him inside.

Then the vajra master leads him by the hand, with the action vajra following behind, and they circumambulate the maṇḍala three times. First, in order to determine his family, at the eastern door of the maṇḍala, the master, having first made the declaration of truth, has him cast the flower. The words of the declaration of truth are these:

Lion of those who speak the ultimate, called the yogin,
supremely skilled in the aim of cutting the afflictions of existence,
please conquer the afflictions stirred by the hundred faults of existence,
and bring to accomplishment the reality of the king of ultimate yoga.

Abode of all the tathāgatas,
Heruka, glorious supreme city:
into this maṇḍala of the heart
I shall have the disciple enter.
Whatever his accomplishment is to be,
whichever family he is fortunate in,
from whatever kind of merit it arises,
may such appear in this maṇḍala!

Having said oṃ sarva tathāgata kula biśodhani svāhā and then hūṃ, one has him cast the flower from between his hands into the center of the maṇḍala. Then with oṃ debaidundriya udghaṭaya svāhā one unties the blindfold and shows him the reality of the maṇḍala and of the deities, the place where the flower fell, and the families.

Then, circumambulating in the same way, one brings him to the northern door, and has him cast a flower into the conch of the Victorious vase, as distinct from the conches of the other vases. One pours in a little, and with the thumb and ring finger of the left hand purifies the five places of birth with the water of the conch, using these mantras. Then, in order to purify the five elements, one confers the water empowerment three times with kuśa water. The mantra for this is oṃ ā ī ṝ ū ḹ pañcaskandha biśodhani svāhā. Then, as before, one purifies the five places with oṃ a i ṛ u ḷ pañca tathāgata bi svāhā, and one fastens on him a crown having the nature of the five Jina families, whether drawn as a picture, carved in relief, or made of cloth. This purifies the five aggregates. Thus, at the northern ground of the body, the water and the crown, these two, are the empowerments that purify the body.

Then, circumambulating in the same way, one brings him to the southern door, and in order to purify the goddesses of the perfection of wisdom, one purifies the five places of birth as before with the water of the conch, using oṃ a ā aṃ ha hā haḥ ho phreṃ daśa pāramitā paripūraṇi svāhā, and one ties on him a silk streamer of gold, silver, silk or the like.

Then, in order to purify the sun and moon, one purifies the five places of arising, as before, with oṃ sūrya candra viśodhaka svāhā. First the vajra and bell are crossed three times at the heart, and then they should be placed on the crown of the head. Thus, at the southern ground of speech, there are the two empowerments that purify speech.

Then, in order to purify the vajra of mind, one leads the disciple to the eastern gate. In order to purify the sense objects and the sense faculties, one purifies the five places of arising, as before, with oṃ a ā e ai ar ār o au al āl viṣyinriya viśodhani svāhā. Then one ties ten garlands of flowers on the crown, the throat, the ears, the upper arms, the hands, the waist and the feet, and places a vajra ring in the hand. This is the empowerment of vajra conduct.

Then, in order to purify the afflicted mind, one purifies the five places of arising, as before, with oṃ ha hā ya yā ra rā va vā la lā catur brahma vihara viśuddhe svāhā. One takes up the flower that was cast earlier and places it on the crown of the head, and one should confer the empowerment with a name that accords with the family on which the flower fell. Thus the conduct and the name are given at the eastern ground of mind.

Then one leads the disciple to the western gate, the ground of wisdom. In order to purify wisdom, one purifies the five places of arising, as before, with oṃ kṣa dharma cakra pravartaka svāhā. Then one places the vajra and bell in the hands, and in order to show the path of complete purity, one should give the empowerment of permission.

Then, preceded by the outer purification of speech, the vase empowerment, the secret empowerment and the wisdom-knowledge empowerment should be conveyed to those of the householder class and those of the renunciant class respectively: to the former in their actual sequence, to the latter by indicating them through words. After the seven empowerments, or else at this point, one should confer the pledges of the six families and the pledges concerning the root downfalls and so on.

In this way there are ten kinds of empowerment for one's own benefit, and these are the mundane empowerments. The supramundane empowerment is twofold, divided into the conventional and the genuine. The conventional is the empowerment of the three kinds of pervading lord. The genuine is the wisdom born of meditation that is attained at the foot of the inner tree of awakening. This should be learned in detail from the guru's oral instructions.

Since this is for the sake of empowerment, one addresses those holding flowers as great ones and should speak to them as follows:

Some, to accomplish secret mantra,
enter into this maṇḍala.
Others, apart from them, desire merit.
Still others seek the aims of the world beyond.
With abundant faith in seeking
the aims of the world beyond,
the wise should enter the maṇḍala;
one should not seek the aims of this life.
For one who seeks the aims of this life,
the aims of the world beyond do not come about.
For one who seeks the aims of the world beyond,
the fruit flourishes in this life as well.

With these words one should direct the disciples to a firm resolve, not for the sake of mundane accomplishments but for the sake of supramundane accomplishments.

You who are victorious over the foe of saṃsāra,
great hero, since you are my teacher,
master, I pray, consider me.
Great protector, who reveals the way of great awakening,
I make this request of you:
grant me that very pledge,
grant me also the mind of awakening,
and grant me as well the three refuges,
the Buddha, the Dharma and the Saṅgha.
Protector, I pray, let me enter
the supreme city of great liberation.

This is recited three times, and it is the entry of the disciple.

Then the master, in an instant, together with his self-appearing consort, blesses the five places. By the method of drawing the disciples in through the mouth, he melts them into the form of drops, and at the culmination of the coemergent he emits them into the mother's womb. He imagines them as syllables hūṃ, as many as there are disciples, and imagines that these transform completely into two-armed Herukas. Then he draws them out along the vajra path and brings them forth from the heart, and imagines them seated before him, facing him, each on a seat of a variegated lotus. Then he worships them with the five offerings and should say the following:

Come, my child! For the Great Vehicle
you are a vessel of the great way.
The rite that is the domain of secret mantra
I shall teach to you correctly.
The buddhas who arose in the three times,
endowed with the vajra of body, speech and mind,
they too knew
this excellent rite of secret mantra,
and so the hero, at the foot of the tree of awakening,
attained signless omniscience.
Through that unequaled practice of secret mantra,
the glorious protector Heruka
destroyed even the mighty armies
of the utterly fearsome hosts of māras.
Fully knowing how the world engages,
he turned the wheel and passed into nirvāṇa.
Therefore, in order to attain omniscience,
my child, develop this resolve!

This is the giving of permission.

Thus, though my own mind is dull,
I have relied on the sublime instructions
and clarified the reality of empowerment,
relying, as scriptural basis, on the Glorious Ādibuddha,
the Vajraḍāka and the Pañjara,
likewise the Māyājāla,
and the Cakrasaṃvara.

This completes the Consecration Rite of Empowerment into the Path of Complete Purity of King Siṃhāsana, taught by the glorious Bhairavadeva, son of the king of the city of Sumbhari. It was translated by the Tibetan translator, the Śākya monk Drakjor Sherab, in the presence of that same Indian preceptor.

People of wrong belief
have no authority in this path.
Therefore this sublime empowerment
the lord guru bestowed on me.
May fortunate disciples of later generations,
by always relying on the sublime guru,
traverse this path stage by stage
and attain the supreme unchanging wisdom.

Translator's notes

The title "lam gyi rab tu bya ba" is rendered "Ritual Procedure of the Path." The exact sense of "rab tu bya ba" here is uncertain.

In the passage on signs in dreams, "reg" ("touch") is read as "legs" ("Excellent"), because the written form makes little sense in context.

"The Yoginī of All Wheels" translates the Tibetan literally. The specific mantra intended is not identified with certainty.

"Ḍoli recitation" is the palanquin recitation of the Cakrasaṃvara completion practice.

"Without attachment" renders "ma chags par." It may instead stand for "ma chag par," "without interruption."

The phrase "thal mo bskyed par bya" is unusual. The text may be defective here, perhaps for "join the palms and generate the mind [of awakening]."

In the passage on the three drops, the Tibetan names only "a lotus and wheels" for the three places. A vajra for the heart may have dropped out. "Without a spout" renders གཅེའུ་མེད་པ, whose sense is not certain; it may refer to a spout or projecting lip. "Huluka" among the five scents is left as written, being unidentified. "The Ḍākinī of All Buddhas," recited over each vase, is taken as the name of a mantra, parallel to the "Yoginī of All Wheels" mantra earlier in the text. The closing sentence of the disciples' section, "This is the rite of the disciples' preparation," marks the end of a quoted ritual section in the Tibetan. The master's words "The command is sealed" render a phrase whose sense is unclear. The disciple's reply suggests a challenge such as "Who are you?" The verse conferring the twenty-five vows is grammatically very obscure in this Tibetan. The repeated words "thirty" and "guru of men" look like metrical fillers or vocatives. It is translated as it stands, following the known structure of the twenty-five vows: five sins, five subsidiary faults, five beings not to be harmed, five objects of rancor, and the five clingings of the senses. "lha min dbang po'i chos" is taken as a single item, "the religion of the lord of the asuras", so that the subsidiary faults number five. "Three times seven" may mean twenty-one recitations. "dri chu" in the master's bath is taken as scented water, since urine is already one of the five nectars mixed into it; the reading "urine" is possible. The sentence introducing the first verse, "chen po rnams sbyar te", is obscure. I have taken it as addressing the flower-holding disciples as "great ones," but it may instead be a fragment of some other instruction.
