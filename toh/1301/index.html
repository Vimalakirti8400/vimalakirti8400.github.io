About this text

The Means of Accomplishing Hevajra, known in Sanskrit as the Hevajra Sādhana and in Tibetan as Kyé Dorjé Druppé Tap, is a short ritual manual in verse for the meditative practice of Hevajra, the wrathful Blood-Drinker or Heruka of the Hevajra Tantra. Opening with homage to Heruka, it sets out who is qualified for the practice, namely one who holds the vows and has received the empowerments, and where to practice. It then guides the yogin through visualizing the deity and the symbolism of his faces and arms, the inner yoga of the four cakras and the fire of Caṇḍālī, mantra recitation, and how to carry the practice into sleeping, eating, torma offerings and dedication prayers. Its colophon attributes it to the great master Mañjuśrījñānapāda. It is Toh 1301 in the Tengyur, where it sits among the tantra commentaries as a sādhana, a practice text of the Hevajra cycle.

Translation

In the language of India: he bajra sā dha no pi ka. In the language of Tibet: The Means of Accomplishing Hevajra.

Homage to glorious Heruka.

Bowing to the glorious Blood-Drinker (Heruka),
Whose abode is nondual wisdom,
Free of causes and likewise free of composition,
I shall teach the rite of relying on him.

The practitioner who holds the secret meaning,
Who knows the pledges that hold the true meaning
And the outer, inner and secret vows,
And who has the master, secret and wisdom empowerments
And likewise the fourth,
Will accomplish the supreme meditation.
At the foot of a solitary tree or in a charnel ground,
Meditation is said to be wholesome;
Or else in a secluded hermitage,
Or likewise in a shrine of the mātṛkās and the like;
Or else in a pleasant and delightful place
One should accomplish one's own aim.

There, seated properly
On a comfortable and ample seat,
With flowers and other various things
And with nectar, make the purifying torma.
With the vajra, imagine a sun disk
Set at the center of the space of the heart.
Upon it, by the blazing syllable hūṃ,
The supreme weapons are to be sent forth.
Drawing in one's own supreme maṇḍala,
Which fills the expanse of space like a pod full of sesame,
Make offerings with the goddesses
Born from the supreme seed at one's own heart.
Gaurī holds the emblem of the moon,
Caurī holds the vessel of the sun,
In Vetālī's hand is water,
Ghasmarī holds medicine,
Pukkasī the vajra,
Śabarī likewise the elixir,
Caṇḍālī sounds the small drum,
And Ḍombī, with great passion, in passionate attachment,
Embraces him fully at the heart:
By these the principal deity is worshipped.

After that come the limbs of merit.
Go for refuge to the Three and so forth, as follows:
Prostration and going for refuge,
Confession of wrongdoing, rejoicing in merit,
Generating the mind set on supreme awakening,
The pledge, and the offering of one's own body.
Then, first, loving-kindness;
After that meditate on compassion;
Third, one should meditate on joy;
And last of all, equanimity.

Then, in order to see wisdom,
Having examined all these outer and inner things
As completely pure and empty,
And imagining them as a cloudless sky,
One should recite this secret mantra:
oṃ śū nya tā jñā na bajra sva bhā ātma ko'haṃ
By this secret mantra that makes the mind steady,
The mind's own entity, arising from emptiness,
Is a haṃ joined with o;
From oṃ at the center, linked with haṃ,
Imagine seven suns upon raṃ,
And upon them meditate
On a crossed vajra arisen from hūṃ.
From that vajra, too, meditate
On a fence and an enclosing net.
At its center, with the nature of the expanse of space,
Meditate on wisdom in the shape of e.
Above it, imagine the maṇḍalas
Of earth, water, fire and wind
As green, white, red
And the color of smoke,
And imagine them as square, round, triangular
And crescent-shaped.
Above them, imagine a haṃ with oṃ
Abiding as one's own essence.
From the shape of e up to the a haṃ,
All of it merges and becomes the wheel:
Square, with four doors,
Adorned with eight pillars,
Fully furnished with nets and half-nets,
And adorned all around with vajra lines.

Above that, the wise should meditate well
On a corpse that has the nature of the dharmadhātu.
Upon it imagine a sun and a moon
Arising from the vowels and consonants.
Upon them, from their seeds,
Arisen from a and hūṃ,
Meditate on a vajra and a curved knife.
Into them, one's own mind, in nature a haṃ,
Fully enters;
By the nature of supreme bliss,
This is what is called the "being".
From these merging into one,
Meditate on Heruka,
Who has the nature of the five wisdoms.
The moon is mirror-like wisdom,
Likewise the sun is the wisdom of equality;
The emblem arisen from the seed
Is declared to be discriminating wisdom;
Their indivisible merging is the wisdom of diligent action;
Becoming the body is the wisdom of the dharmadhātu.

His body is the color of the light of breaking dawn,
His eyes are like red drops,
His tawny hair curls upward,
And he is adorned with the five seals:
Wheel, earrings, bracelets,
Necklace and girdle;
Being the pure forms of the five buddhas,
These are proclaimed to be the seals.
He is like a youth of sixteen years,
With an intensely wrathful gaze.
In his left hand is a vajra skull cup,
And with his second, in the form of the consort,
He embraces the khaṭvāṅga as well.
In his right hand is a black vajra,
Sending forth the sound of hūṃ.
Meditate on him as the very nature of aversion.

If one wishes to practice with a consort:
In the first left hand is a skull cup
Filled with the blood of gods and asuras,
In the first right hand a vajra;
The remaining two hands are as before,
And he is embraced by Vārāhī, who is like himself.
The rest is also just as before.

Further, with three faces and six arms:
The right face is like the dawn,
The left one is red; he is naked.
In the first left hand is a trident,
In the first right hand a vajra;
In the second left hand a bell,
In the second right hand a curved knife;
With the remaining hands, holding a vajra chain,
A knife and a skull cup, he embraces.
The rest is as before, and the consort likewise.

Further, the one to be meditated upon
Has eight faces and four legs,
And is adorned with sixteen arms.
With his feet he tramples the four māras;
He terrifies even the terrifying.
Adorned with a garland of heads,
He stands upon a sun in a dancing pose,
Bearing a crossed vajra on the crown of his head.
Black in color, he is terrifying.
hūṃ streams forth from his mouth,
And his body is smeared with ashes.
In union with Nairātmyā,
He is absorbed in the union of joy,
Abiding in the bliss of the nonconceptual,
The nonconceptual nature, in bliss.
The first face is a great black one,
The right is like the kunda flower,
The hero's left is a deep red,
And the upper face bares its fangs.
He has twenty-four eyes,
And the remaining faces are like bees.
In the eight blazing right hands,
Within eight lotus skull cups,
Are elephant, horse and donkey,
Ox, camel and human,
Cat and lion:
These should be known in this order.
Earth, water, wind and fire,
The moon and the sun likewise,
Yama and the yakṣas
Are in the eight skull cups of the left.
Grace, heroism, revulsion,
Laughter, wrath, terror,
Compassion, wonder, peace and so on:
He has the moods of the nine blazing dances.
Stamping the ground with his feet,
He menaces the asuras and the gods.

At one's own heart, meditate on a sun disk
Arisen from the syllable ra.
Then the syllable hūṃ,
The very essence of method and wisdom,

And the vajra arisen from the syllable hūṃ,
Black in color and greatly fierce;
Abiding at the center of the vajra's hub,
That itself should be meditated on as hūṃ.
Further, meditate on the very nature of aversion
That the syllable hūṃ has wholly become:
The great black one arisen from the vajra,
Just like a blue lotus,
Or else like the breaking dawn;
With faith one should meditate on him with certainty.

Then one should meditate on the wheel of the mothers
In a delightful celestial palace.
From the delight of the lovely union
Of father and mother, who are not two,
Gaurī arises, and in Indra's quarter
She abides at the eastern gate.
From the union that is not two
Caurī too is sent forth,
And Caurī abides as guardian of the southern gate.
From the union of gentle entering
Vetālī too is to be brought forth;
Sent forth, the destroyer of māras is at the western gate.
From the great union of absorption
Ghasmarī too is to be brought forth;
Sent forth, at the northern gate
She abides in the form of a wrathful goddess.
Through the union of the two joined together,
Pukkasī too is to be brought forth;
Sent forth, in the northeastern corner
She abides in a turbulent form.
Further, from the union that is not two,
Śabarī is in the southeastern corner,
Caṇḍālī in the southwestern quarter,
And Ḍombī in the northwestern corner.

Gaurī holds a curved knife in her right hand,
And likewise a fish in her left.
Caurī holds a small drum in her right,
And in her left hand a boar.
Vetālī holds a tortoise in her right,
And in her left a lotus skull cup.
Ghasmarī holds a snake in her right,
And in her left a yogin's vessel.
Pukkasī holds a lion in her right,
And likewise an axe in her left.
Śabarī holds a monk in her right,
And likewise a fire-offering vessel in her left.
Caṇḍālī holds a wheel in her right,
And likewise a plough in her left.
Ḍombī holds a vajra in her right,
And likewise makes the threatening gesture with her left.
Gaurī and the others are said to have two arms,
To stand in the half-cross-legged dancing pose,
To have three eyes and hair bristling upward,
And to be adorned with the five seals.
Gaurī's body is black,
Caurī is like madder,
Vetālī is like refined gold,
Ghasmarī is like emerald,
Pukkasī is like sapphire,
Śabarī like moonlight,
Caṇḍālī the color of the sky,
And Ḍombī is said to be of various colors.
Brahmā, Indra, Upendra (Viṣṇu),
Rudra, Yama, the yakṣa,
Nairṛti and Vemacitrin
Should be known as the seats
Of the goddesses Gaurī and the rest, in order.
Their seed syllables are these:
gaṃ caṃ baṃ ghaṃ paṃ saṃ laṃ ḍaṃ.
From these seed syllables the lord
Should bring them forth.

Then oneself, the vajra holder, with one's own consort,
Through intense passion
Melts into light, and from that state
The goddesses of the corners, as loving companions,
Make offerings with various songs and rouse him:
"O Hevajra, look with compassion and protect me, Pukkasī!
I long to be joined in great bliss: set aside your nature of emptiness and arise!"
"Having set aside your nature of emptiness, for the accomplishment that Śabarī needs,
If you would have me live, O Hevajra, arise!"
"When I, Caṇḍālī, see no place without you and make this plea,
Why do you stay so, you who vowed to liberate the world of gods and others?"
"I, Ḍombī, know your mind, which is free of signs;
To be without compassion is not fitting, so arise as if by magic!"
Having heard these, just as before,
From āḥ and hūṃ the great vajra holder
Arises from melting into light.

Then, by the blazing black hook
Arisen from the seed at one's own heart,
Which is the very nature of aversion,
The buddhas dwelling in the three realms
Are invited, and the eight mothers
Make offerings as before and supplicate:
"O great being, Blessed One,
Empowered by the blessed ones,
May all the tathāgatas
Fully grant me empowerment!"
oṃ a bhi ṣiñca tu māṃ sa rba bajra ta thā ga ta a dhiṣṭhā na
By this supplication,
All those sugatas, every one,
Take the form of Heruka,
And confer empowerment with vases of nectar
That have the nature of the five sugatas.
At that time a rain of flowers and saffron
Falls, the sound of drums resounds,
And Rūpavajrā and the others,
Locanā and the others, with vajra songs,
Fully perform the empowerment.
The buddhas, the Lord of Mind and the others,
Are to become the crown ornament.
By the tathāgatas Akṣobhya, Śāśvata,
Ratneśa and Amitābha,
Pukkasī and the others, abiding in the corners,
Are sealed, in that order.
By the vajrīs of aversion, delusion, avarice
And passion,
Gaurī and the others who abide at the gates
Are to be sealed, in that order.
Since they are stamped and marked with the seal,
That is why here they are called "sealed".
With the light rays of the seed at one's own heart
The buddhas are made to enter,
And through the union of tasting nectar
They are made fully satisfied.

The maṇḍalas of wind and fire,
Crescent and triangle, of two colors,
Marked with the syllables yaṃ and raṃ,
Are beautified by a banner and a vajra.
Above them is a skull cup arisen from a,
And there it is filled with the nectar of wisdom.
Marked with oṃ arising from a vajra
Fully generated from the syllable hūṃ,
The nectar, blessed by the three suchnesses,
Is purified through the union of the deity.
Make offerings and praises.

One should know the pure nature.
The aggregates of form, feeling, perception,
Formations and consciousness
Are pure as Vajrā, Gaurī,
The water goddess (Vāriyoginī), the ḍākinī,
And Nairātmyayoginī.
Earth, water, fire and wind, the four elements,
Are in reality Pukkasī and the others.
Forms, sounds, smells, tastes, tangibles and phenomena
Are Gaurī, Caurī, Vetālī,
Ghasmarī, Bhūcarī and Khecarī.
The faculties of eye, ear, nose, tongue,
Body and mind
Should be known as the vajrīs of delusion, aversion, avarice,
Passion and envy,
And Nairātmyayoginī.
Wheel, earrings, necklace,
Bracelets and girdle, the five seals,
Are by nature Akṣobhya, Amitābha, Ratneśa,
Vairocana and Amoghasiddhi.
The two arms are method and wisdom;
The four arms are the four māras purified;
The six arms are the six perfections;
And what are taught as the sixteen arms
Are by nature the sixteen emptinesses.

The one face is the door of emptiness;
The three faces are the three doors of liberation;
The eight faces are the eight liberations;
The four feet are the four māras purified.
The eyes are the three pure vajras.
The six faculties and the five aggregates,
The six sense sources and the elements,
Being completely pure by their essential nature,
The manifold is pure and fully liberated.
Whatever there is, visible objects and the rest,
Is for the yogin like a mirage;
Because all of it is pure by nature,
It is the manifold magical display of the buddhas.
Having carried out this purification,
One should, as the embodiment of yoga and the vow,
Undertake the outer and inner recitation.
Here, this is the vow:
Through the nature of e vaṃ ma yā
The four cakras abide in the body.
Namely, the cakras of emanation, of Dharma,
Of enjoyment and of great bliss
Should be known to be at the navel, the heart, the throat
And the crown of the head, in that order.
The lotuses of the cakras
Are known to have sixty-four, likewise eight,
Sixteen and thirty-two petals.
The seeds are oṃ and hūṃ,
oṃ and haṃ, in that order.
The emanation cakra should be known
As encircled by the vowels and consonants.
Likewise the Dharma cakra is encircled
By the seed syllables of the sugatas with their consorts.
The enjoyment cakra is encircled
By the letters i and e together with their lengthened forms.
The emanation body and the Dharma body,
Likewise the enjoyment body,
And the full knowledge of the three bodies
Are held to be the four cakras.
The nature of these cakras
Is held to be four goddesses, four seals
And four syllables:
Buddhalocanā and Māmakī,
Pāṇḍarā and likewise Tārā;
And they are called the action seal, the Dharma seal,
The pledge seal and the great seal.
The syllables e and vaṃ,
ma and yā are also their actual being.
Likewise body, speech and mind,
And the vowels and consonants too, are just these.
The wisdom known as the four moments,
Variety, maturation,
Consummation and freedom from characteristics;
Embracing, experiencing, enacting,
And the mind that is nothing whatsoever,
Abide in the four cakras.
Suffering, origin, cessation and path,
Which draw out the aggregates and the afflictions, the four:
The four truths, too, are just these.
The realities of self, of mantra, of deity
And of wisdom,
Whatever each of them truly is,
Are the essence of the four cakras.
Joy, supreme joy,
The joy of cessation and innate joy
Are the four portions of the wisdom of bliss;
The attaining of warmth, its increase,
The bliss free of attachment and the nondual bliss
Also abide in the four cakras.
The Sthaviras, the Sarvāstivādins,
Likewise the Saṃmitīyas,
And those called the Mahāsāṅghikas:
The four schools, too, are just these.
I shall also teach the vow of yoga and secret mantra
And the vow of wisdom.
By what blazes at the navel
The five tathāgatas are burned,
Locanā and the rest are burned,
haṃ is burned, and the moon flows down.
This should be understood as follows:
Through the vow of yoga and secret mantra
One should know the characteristics of burning.
The burning is fire and wind:
Whatever light of the fire of wisdom there is,
It is urged on by the wind of karma.
The karma, too, is this,
The reality of the wind of the Blessed Lady Prajñā:
It radiates from the center of the navel and is subtle;
It is the four, Dharma, enjoyment, great bliss and the rest,
The self of all that is stable and moving.
That which has the nature of the syllable aṃ
Is the basis of the wind of karma;
From it everything arises.
The order in which these winds move is this:
In one day there are four watches,
Likewise thirty-two periods,
Likewise sixteen phases,
Possessing sixty-four parts.
The essence of the vowels and consonants,
Which are called moon and sun,
Moves through the right and left nostrils.
It moreover has the nature
Of earth, water, fire and wind,
And is held to be the essence of the four elements.
These move at their proper times;
That which has the nature of space moves out of time.
Heavy and hard is the earth element;
Moving downward, it is held to be the first.
Cold and rough is the water element;
Moving forward, it is the second.
Warm and smooth is the fire element;
It moves back, close by.
Light and strong is the wind element;
It should be known as moving upward.
Likewise what are known as periods,
Phases and watches
Should here be taken as just this.
As on the right, so on the left,
And then again on the right;
There is no interruption.
What is the movement out of time?
At the very time of death,
When the bodhicitta flows down in a stream,
And at the omen of the onset of a grave illness,
They arise at one time:
This is called the space element.
Wind, wisdom, consciousness
And the poisons move together,
And are never separated.
What is called the wind of touch
Never passes out of the body;
If it passed out, one would die.
Through knowing the reality of wind
One should stir up the reality of fire.
The elements of breath and of heat
Do not operate separately.
Here the reality of fire is this:
From the union of the organs churning together
The fire of wisdom arises,
Just like fire in the outer world.
It, too, by the path of the organ
Blazes fiercely in the channel of the navel.
With great light and with smoke
Caṇḍālī blazes up.
When that light of the fire of wisdom
Has blazed up exceedingly,
The five sugatas abiding in the Dharma cakra,
Likewise together with their goddesses,
Are burned.
Having emerged at the enjoyment cakra,
It circles three times, turning from the right,
Emerges at the hair-curl between the brows,
And then emerges at the right ear.
Having roused the buddhas of the ten directions,
It enters the crown of the head.
Descending to the Dharma cakra,
This burning exceedingly
Is held to be the mantra vow.
The particular feature of the wisdom vow is this:
Having conferred with the buddhas at the heart,
It gathers the pure nectar,
And emerging by the earlier path,
It gathers the nectar of the ten directions,
And, as before, enters haṃ.
Thereby the moon of the mind melts,
And emerging from the tube of the teeth,
It descends to the enjoyment cakra.
Having rested there a little,
It circles back again as before,
And descends to the Dharma cakra.
Having filled the sugatas,
It circles back again as before,
And descends to the emanation cakra.
In the same way it makes its way everywhere.
The particular forms of the light of wisdom,
A hundred-thousandth part of the tip of a hair,
Are not seen by the eye.

This is what is called the vow. The teaching of the inner recitation is this: Below is the maṇḍala of wind; The triangular place is likewise fire; The belly is the maṇḍala of water; Above it is Mahendra, the earth, with Mount Meru. Above the maṇḍala of wind One should meditate on the cakra of Śāśvata, And there take up the vow of the syllable a, Whose nature is nondual. This nature of bodhicitta Is neither body, speech nor mind; It is not attachment, aversion or delusion; It is not color, shape and the like; It is free of wrathful, peaceful and the rest. One should meditate on the great Middle Way, Free of all signs. The rest is as in the vow. Because this is nothing whatsoever, It is held to be the neuter recitation; It is the supreme recitation. If one grows weary, One should undertake the outer recitation. With the light rays of the seed in one's own heart, From the very place of the wisdom being And from the places of body, speech and mind, One should emanate as one wishes. oṃ deba picu bajra hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ svāhā This is the heart mantra of Hevajra. oṃ trailokyākṣepa hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ svāhā This is that of the two-armed one. oṃ jvala jvala bhyo hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ svāhā This is that of the four-armed one. oṃ kiṭi kiṭi bajra hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ svāhā This is that of the six-armed one. oṃ aṣṭānanāye phiṃgordhakeśabartamane caturbiṃśatinitrāya śoḍaśabhujāya kṛśṇajīmūtavapuṣe kapālāmāla anekaṃdhariṇe ādhamtakrūraciṭṭaa'ya ardhendudaṃśṭīṇe māraya māraya kāraya kāraya garjaya garjaya tarjaya tarjaya śoṣaya śoṣaya sapha sāgarāna bandha bandha nāga aṣṭakān gṛhaṇ gṛhaṇ śatrūṃ ha hā hi hī hu hū he hai ho hau haṃ haḥ phaṭ svāhā This is that of the sixteen-armed one. When one has grown weary of recitation, Ringing the vajra and bell, One should make offerings, praises and aspirations well, And release the mudrā. Dissolving into the expanse and requesting the deities to depart Should be done just as is fitting. Then the great yogin, In all forms of conduct, Should not fall away from the samādhi of being the deity. Thus, when lying down to sleep, Through the samādhi of the great Vajradhara, Having brought the great passion into loving union, And having melted the body with the fire of wisdom, One sleeps as one who has become the mind of the dharmakāya. When the time comes to rise, Roused by the four immeasurables, And recalling one's former aspirations, One should rise through the stages of samādhi By the practice that is like an illusion. Whenever one eats, Just as in the actual practice of the vow, The body is the maṇḍala of the deities; One should bless the food into nectar, And by eating it, satisfy them inseparably. Or else one should make the offering of the fire offering: Rasayana, the element of fire, Blazes and radiates on the right, And should be known as the essence of the vowels; Lalanā, the element of wind, Churns and moves on the left, And should be known as the essence of the consonants. In the middle of these, in the very self of the body, One should make the fire offering of food and so on, and of milk. If one goes walking about, Begging alms from the five families, The ornaments such as the mudrās, And the conduct heard in the Vajra teaching Should be carried out just as it was taught. Through this, awakening is not hard to find. If one wishes to give a torma, Through the yoga of Heruka, One focuses directly on the bhūtas, And having blessed the foods into nectar, One should satisfy them with this mantra: oṃ inda jam jal jkakh bhūta bahni ba u rakśa canad sujja māda pappa pātāye aṣṭha sappa svāhā edaṃ baliṃ bhuṃja jigha phulla dhūppa māṃsa biṃgha amba kajja sādha khānti khuṇi pheṭ gāda oṃ akāro mukhaṃ sarbadharmāṇāṃ ānyanutpannatvāta oṃ āḥ hūṃ phaṭ svāhā Whoever always performs this Will accomplish all rites, And the hosts of bhūtas being pleased, Siddhi will come to him swiftly. At the end of the rites, too, One should make offerings and praises and make aspirations. Here the aspiration is this: Born into a noble family and endowed with intelligence, Keeping the pledges and teaching Hevajra, Endowed with compassion and devoted to the gurus, May I be born like this in all my births. Ringing the vajra and bell with my hands, Reading aloud the profound sacred Dharma, And partaking of the fluid of the pledge consort, May I be born like this in all my births. Then one gathers the emanations back into oneself, And requests the wisdom maṇḍala to depart again. Conduct and the rest are as before.

The Hevajra Sādhana, the Rite for Pleasing Heruka, composed by the great master Mañjuśrījñānapāda, is complete. It was translated, checked and finalized by the Indian preceptor Mañjuśrījñāna and Rongzom Dharmabhadra.

Translator's notes

"With the vajra ... the space of the heart" renders a terse line (rdo rjes mkha' snying dbus gnas par); it may mean the yogin as vajra-holder visualizes the sun at the center of his heart. The list of nine moods gives eight items and then "and so on", as the text has it. In the right-hand skull cups the text has cat and lion where other Hevajra sources differ; the text is followed as it stands. Śabarī's left-hand emblem (bsreg shang) is uncertain; it is rendered as a fire-offering vessel. In the second song the text has only "sa" for the singer; following the pattern of the four songs (Pukkasī, Śabarī, Caṇḍālī, Ḍombī), it is taken as Śabarī. At the gates the text spells Gaurī as "Kairī"; it is rendered Gaurī. Śāśvata and Ratneśa are the Hevajra names of Vairocana and Ratnasambhava. The line on the enjoyment cakra reads "the letters i and e together with 'dren'"; "lengthened forms" is a tentative reading of 'dren. The line "the four that draw out the aggregates and afflictions" is obscure in the Tibetan and is rendered literally. "ནམ་མཁའི་རང་བཞིན་དུ་ས་མ་ཡིན" is read as "dus ma yin" (out of time), which the following question on untimely movement confirms. "བཀའ་བ་གྲོས" is read as bka' bgros, "conferred". "The conduct heard in the Vajra teaching" renders rdo rje thos pa'i spyod pa literally; it most likely refers to the conduct taught in the Hevajra Tantra.
