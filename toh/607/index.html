About this text

Toh 607 in the Kangyur, known in Sanskrit as the Ārya-ratnamālā-nāma-aparājita and in Tibetan as the Phags pa gzhan gyis mi thub pa rin po che'i phreng ba, or in English as "The Noble Invincible Precious Garland," is a dhāraṇī-related text belonging to the broader category of such works collected in the Kangyur. The text opens with an extensive series of homages addressed to the Three Jewels, vast numbers of buddhas and their assemblies, bodhisattvas, various grades of noble practitioners from stream-enterers to arhats, and a wide range of divine and supernatural beings including gods, nāgas, yakṣas, and gandharvas, suggesting its character as a liturgically rich protective or merit-generating scripture. Texts of this type typically present powerful formulas alongside invocations intended to confer invincibility or spiritual protection on those who recite or honor them. Its placement in the Kangyur situates it among the canonical scriptures regarded as the word of the Buddha transmitted into the Tibetan tradition.

Translation

In the language of India: Ārya-ratnamālā-nāma-aparājita. In the language of Tibet: The Noble Invincible Precious Garland.

Homage to the Three Jewels. Homage to the Buddha. Homage to the Dharma. Homage to the Saṅgha. Homage to the seventy million perfectly and completely awakened buddhas together with their assemblies of śrāvaka Saṅgha. Homage to all bodhisattvas, Maitreya foremost among them. Homage to those who have rightly gone forth. Homage to those who have rightly entered. Homage to the arhats. Homage to the non-returners. Homage to the once-returners. Homage to the stream-enterers. Homage to the divine seers. Homage to those who have accomplished the discipline of austerities. Homage to the gods. Homage to the nāgas. Homage to the yakṣas. Homage to the gandharvas. Homage to the great serpents. Homage to the kinnaras. Homage to the rākṣasas. Homage to the flesh-eating ones with hair standing upright. Homage to all masters of knowledge-mantras. Homage to the moon and the sun. Homage to the twenty-eight constellations.

Then, the essence of the Noble Invincible Precious Garland, spoken by the perfectly and completely awakened Buddha, rejoiced in by gods and nāgas, accomplishing all deeds — this pure one is to be proclaimed:

Pure and likewise auspicious,
making merit, clean and glorious,
vanquishing rākṣasas together with the gods,
unvanquishable by any sentient being,
utterly destroying all evil,
glorious and accomplishing every aim,
dispersing and destroying all malevolent spirits —
this knowledge-mantra is to be proclaimed.

This always brings victory.
Even Śakra (Indra) cannot overcome it.
It drives away fear of poison and murderers,
fear of fire, of enemies, of kings,
of killing spirits, of malevolent possession, of zombies,
of evil dreams and of bodily decay,
of gods, asuras, gandharvas,
yakṣas, rākṣasas, and nāgas.
From all of these, to the extent of a hundred leagues in every direction,
may it always protect me.

From the fever that lasts one day,
and from that which lasts two days, three days,
from the four-day fever,
and from relentless epidemic — this protects.
Even poison and the food of poisonous plants
will be digested as easily as ordinary food.
One in whose womb no life takes hold,
and one whose male child is dying —
through this Precious Garland
may they swiftly be restored to life.

Those who desire wealth shall obtain wealth.
Those who desire glory shall obtain glory.
In battle, in dispute, and on the field of war,
it brings swift victory.

Seeing the harm done to sentient beings,
the Protector of the World proclaimed it fully.
This knowledge-mantra, called the Precious Garland,
is to be proclaimed:

Tadyathā: oṃ dhana-kari, vipula-kari, jaya-kari, yaṣṭi-kari, bṛddhi-kari, āyur-dadi, puṇya-dāri, alakṣmī-praśamani, dhana-dhanya, vivartani, śrī-kari, sarva-graha-nakṣatra-praśavartani, medhā-janani, putra-janani, parivāra-vṛddhani, āyur-dani, prīti-kari, jambhani, stambhani, mohani, amogha-śakti, kañcana-māle, graha-saumya-karaṇi, sarva-kali-kalaha, vigraha-vyādha-praśamani, para-cakra-nivāraṇi, para-saiṇya, stambhani, citta-nirmalī-kariṇī, sarva-pāpaṃ praśamani, sarva-kāla-viṣṭa-nāśani svāhā. Curu curu svāhā. Turu turu svāhā. Muru muru svāhā. Sarva-deva-anujñite svāhā. Sarva-nāga-saṃrakṣite svāhā. Oṃ niri niri phaṭ. Hana hana phaṭ. Daha daha phaṭ. Paca paca phaṭ.

Those who harbor malice toward me,
those who intend to harm me —

those who perform fire offerings against me, those who perform sorcery against me, those who cause others to do so, and those who rejoice in its being done — upon all of them: Phaṭ phaṭ svāhā. Agnīṃ stambhayāmi. Viṣaṃ śatrūṃ. Udakāna. Corāṇa. Sarva-duṣṭāna. Sarva-pratyarthika. Pratya-mantrāṇa. Stambhayāmi. Jambhayāmi. Mohayāmi. Phaṭ phaṭ svāhā.

"That Which Cannot Be Overcome by Others,"
adorned with a garland of jewels,
which accomplishes all works
and which forever affords protection —
wherever one may go, or in whatever circumstance,
let some portion of it be written and worn.

It is the supreme merit that vanquishes demons,
that destroys the stain of misdeeds.
In battle and in conflict alike,
it brings complete victory — a blessing.

Whoever, having first purified themselves,
writes and bears this vidyā-mantra,
and whoever recites, memorizes, and knows it with wisdom —
no fear of any kind shall arise for them.

If one reads and bears it according to the ritual,
all their secrets shall forever be protected,
they shall be fully guarded and sustained,
and they shall always be victorious.

Just as one who adorns their head
with a garland of precious jewels shines with beauty,
so too does one who bears this shine
with all splendor and excellence.

Whoever carries this vidyā-mantra
shall utterly dispel all harmful spirits,
shall not be struck down by illness,
sons and grandsons shall flourish,
and their retinue shall become fine and good.

For the benefit of all sentient beings,
and because it was composed with especial purpose,
all the Tathāgatas have blessed this vidyā-mantra.
The sovereign lords of the gods,
and likewise the bodhisattvas, protect the bearer,
and all the Tathāgatas of the ten directions
look upon them with regard.

Virtue and wealth shall increase,
and even misdeeds shall be purified.
From evil and harmful dreams
one shall be freed — of this there is no doubt.

Tadyathā. Muni muni. Muni muni muniri. Mati mati mati. Mahāmati. May I be protected from all fears. Protect me, protect me. I pay homage to all the buddhas. May I be well and free from plague and all harm. Svāhā.

The noble text called "That Which Cannot Be Overcome by Others: The Precious Garland" is complete.

Translator's notes

The text breaks off mid-sentence at the end, so the translation reproduces it exactly as it stands, ending with the incomplete verse about those who harbor malice and intend harm.

The phrase rendered "divine seers" translates the Tibetan for celestial rishis, beings who combine divine and ascetic sage status in Indic cosmology.

"Flesh-eating ones with hair standing upright" renders a class of beings often identified with a type of flesh-devouring spirit; the description is the Tibetan text's own specification.

"Great serpents" for the large serpentine beings here is contextually distinct from the nāgas already listed, likely referring to the mahorāgas.

The mantra syllables are transliterated from the Tibetan phonetic rendering into reconstructed Sanskrit; the Tibetan preserves Sanskrit phonemes imperfectly in places, so minor variations from attested Sanskrit dhāraṇī versions of this text are expected. The mantra syllables in the dhāraṇī passages (Agnīṃ stambhayāmi, Viṣaṃ śatrūṃ, Udakāna, Corāṇa, etc.) are transliterated Sanskrit phonemes embedded in the Tibetan text. They are rendered here in their reconstructed Sanskrit forms following the standard convention for dhāraṇī literature rather than being translated, as their function is performative rather than semantic.

The title rendered as "That Which Cannot Be Overcome by Others" corresponds to the Sanskrit Aparājitā, meaning "the Unconquered" or "Invincible." This is the standard title of the dhāraṇī text.
