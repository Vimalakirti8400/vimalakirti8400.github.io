About this text

Toh 474 in the Kangyur is a tantric scripture known in Sanskrit as the Śrī Raktayamāritantrarāja and in Tibetan as the Dpal gshin rje'i gshed dmar po zhes bya ba'i rgyud kyi rgyal po, meaning in English "The Glorious Tantra King Called the Red Yamāri." It belongs to the class of highest yoga tantras and centers on Raktayamāri, the Red Yamāri, a wrathful deity understood as the slayer or destroyer of Yama, the lord of death, who serves as a central focus of esoteric practice and ritual. The text opens in the classic canonical style with the declaration "Thus did I hear," locating the teaching in a visionary setting where the Blessed One abides within the secret essence of all the Tathāgatas, and the great bodhisattva Vajrapāṇi poses questions about the nature and number of those who destroy Yama. As a tantra king, this scripture holds a position of particular authority within its cycle, presenting doctrine, meditation, and the qualities of Yamāri practice in a revealed, dialogic form characteristic of the Vajrayāna tantric literature preserved in the Kangyur.

In the language of India: Śrī Raktayamāritantrarāja nāma. In the language of Tibet: The Glorious Tantra King Called the Red Yamāri.

Homage to the Blessed One, the Slayer of Yama.

Thus did I hear. At one time, the Blessed One dwelt within the secret places of the vajra-lady, the essence of the body, speech, and mind of all the Tathāgatas. There, the essence of the body, speech, and mind of all the Tathāgatas of the Blessed One — most supremely secret, beyond even the venerable secret — arose, and the bodhisattva Vajrapāṇi, of great compassion and great being, said: "Excellent! Excellent!" The vajra of aversion, the essence of the great bodhisattva — hear now the nature of the Red Yamāri.

Vajrapāṇi then asked: "How many are the destroyers of Yama spoken of here?" The reply was: there is the Vajra-Deluded Yamāri, the Vajra-Slanderous Yamāri, the Vajra-Attached Yamāri, and the Vajra-Jealous Yamāri. There are also Vajra Carcikā, Vajravārāhī, Vajra Sarasvatī, and Vajra Gaurī. At the directions there are the vases. By such an assembly of destroyers of Yama and others, it is accomplished.

Then the Blessed One, from within the very body, speech, and mind of the meditative absorption called the Vajra that Destroys All Māras — the master of all Tathāgatas — caused to emerge:

Mantras with the great power to summon and to command,
and the many rites of might in like fashion —
accomplishment of the sword, and kingship,
and the complete defeat of enemy armies;
invisibility, eye-medicine, and the extraction of essence,
likewise the learning of shadow-pressing,
the raising of corpses, the accomplishment of yakṣas,
and protection from deadly serpents and the like;
the rites of fire offering and of the maṇḍala;
the discernment of the awakening mind,
the conduct comprising all methods,
and the entering of enemies' towns —
for all of these, meditate upon
the vajra-protection, with five rays of light assembled.
For the portions of the vajra ground,
the enclosure and the tent are likewise so.

Then the Blessed One, having entered into meditative absorption called the Vajra that Completely Destroys All Māras, which all the Tathāgatas have generated, drew out from the very body, speech, and mind of this seed — the seed of all, beginning with the Deluded Yamāri — with certainty.

Having made this special aspiration,

This is the first chapter, which sets forth the sequence of direct realization, drawn from the Tantra of the Red Yamāntaka, the Body, Speech, and Mind of All Tathāgatas.

Then all the Blessed One Tathāgatas made supplication to the Blessed One, the great bodhisattva Vajrasattva, with this King of Praises. And so: "O great vajra-holder, hear how the vast oceans of gnosis arise from the detailed differentiation of karma, according to how sentient beings engage. Explain this!"

Then the King Vajradhara, who perfectly accomplishes all actions and whose very nature heals all faults, declared these very words.

Then the Blessed One Tathāgatas, having made the vajra-mind of all Tathāgatas perfectly manifest, made offerings and prostrations to the Blessed One, circumambulated him three times to the right, and again and again prostrated — and hearkened to the words of the Blessed One.

Then the King Vajradhara spoke of the mechanical device for bringing under control. The supreme mechanical device, the Yamāntaka, will not arise if it has not yet arisen. For the subjugation of women, for the purpose of protection, and likewise for the purpose of pacification, one should take musk.

Likewise on birchbark or bamboo peel, one should clearly draw the two wheels. The name should be established with the syllable NĀMAḤ. Free from black roots and the like, two bowls should be properly joined. Into the center, clarified butter and honey are poured. It should be bound with white thread. White flowers at the three junctures — the offering is made facing east. Visualizing oneself as Yamāntaka, clear as crystal water, one should perform the accomplishment. Before one, upon a lunar maṇḍala, one gazes and bestows manifest empowerment. A white vase, like the moon, is completely filled with the five nectars. Conceal it exceedingly well, hidden in the ultimate. As the rite here described is performed, it will be accomplished in a single day — through the joining with the red form of Yamāntaka.

On charnel-ground cloth as before, one who possesses the ascetic practice should draw the two wheels. Thorn-apple, salt, poison, and neem — or neem alone — the three pungent substances and salt alone, likewise charnel-ground charcoal, water from datura leaves, seeds of caṇḍā — likewise blood taken from the ring finger, or the extract of chitraka, soil taken from a place of slaughter, the strong clay of an outcaste's pottery, and a pen made from crow feathers: with these the ascetic practitioner should draw on the fourteenth day, with a mind of midnight wrath, for the secret subjugation of harmful beings — the subduer of various sentient beings — supported at the edges by the syllable HŪṂ. The yogin's face is turned to the south. Oneself is Yamāntaka: of wrathful form, immensely fierce, adorned with fragments of skulls, seated upon a buffalo, with a moving tongue, a great belly, a terrifier. Fangs bared, hair blazing upward, eyebrows reddish yellow. In the right hand a great vajra club, likewise in the left a skull-cup. Ugly, with fangs completely bared, blazing like the fire at the end of the age — thus should one meditate on oneself. Swiftly fix the object of accomplishment before you: seized and crushed by foul odors and wounds and by a malignant disease, stripped of clothes, causing trembling —

exhausted, the body covered with wounds. Seized about the body by a garland of intestines, weapons cause the wounds to gape open. Then the sādhana of Kurukullā shall be thoroughly explained by me. By merely meditating upon her, one summons the daughters of the nāgas. Red in colour, with four faces and endowed with eight arms, arisen from the seed-syllable Hrīḥ, she is the one who summons the nāgas. The eight classes of nāgas, beginning with Anantā and the rest, are to be placed in the eight arms. Her body is adorned with ornaments of serpents. One possessed of vows should meditate upon the beautiful one. The mantra is: Kurukullā Hrīḥ Phūḥ Svāhā.

Then, in brief rather than at length, the secret shall be explained. By merely knowing it, one summons the daughters of the gods. She has one face and two arms, holding in her hands an arrow and a bow. Her body is to be visualised as bodiless, of a great golden colour. In the east, visualise the joyful one; in the south, visualise the beautiful intoxicating one; in the west, the goddess of desire; in the north, the one who inspires intoxicated delight. At the intermediate directions, always place Roaming Woman, Unchecked Woman, and Commanding Woman; those called Crocodile-Banner are fully explained at the aspect of the door. Craving Woman, Proud Woman, and likewise Mindful Woman — these are lords of the intermediate directions. On the crowns of all those deities, meditate upon Yamāntaka. The women, facing the sky at the edge, meditate upon the bodiless vajra. Born fully from the mantra of the syllable Śī, radiating everywhere, she is to be visualised as beguiling and distracted, desiring pleasure with an intoxicated mind, entirely wrapped in red garments, falling at the feet. To her, the mantra is to be recited very diligently. The syllable Oṃ is given at the beginning as a distinguishing vowel; the syllable Svāhā is given at the end. Recite the mantras of the syllable Śīta. Che ge mo — by my own authority, the red speech, just as it is — if one has meditated upon everything entirely, the yogin will attain his desire.

This is the seventeenth chapter, the Body, Speech, and Mind of all the Tathāgatas from the Red Yamāntaka Tantra, arranged in sequence beginning with the bodiless one and so forth.

The Protector of the World praises you — remain!
Arise, Lord, merit of the world!

To the Protector of the World concerning awakening itself:
you contemplate emptiness.
Just as shooting stars stream forth,

Through love, the mind bound by attachment melts away —

First, meditate upon yoga.
Second, thereafter, subsequent yoga.
Third is the extreme yoga.
Fourth is the great yoga.

Regarding the perfection of Vajrasattva,

Regarding the perfection of the divine body,

The blessing of the divine eyes and so forth,
of body, speech, and mind,
the introduction of the wheel of gnosis,

One must not disrespect the master.

Never abandon the mind of loving-kindness toward all sentient beings.
Never abandon the awakening mind.
Do not disparage the Dharma of self or other.

Do not denigrate one's own aggregates.
Do not disparage the Dharma of the Blessed One.

with the highest patience —

such a person should always be taken as one's guru.
The student, with devotion to the guru,

The ultimate yantra is the great Yamāntaka.
The ultimate ascetic practice is the great skull.
The ultimate tantra is the assembly —

How wondrous — the teaching of great peace!
How wondrous — the wrathful one who inspires dread!
How wondrous — the supreme nirvāṇa!
How wondrous — saṃsāra itself as peace!

This teaching is great bliss.

What is generated through the supreme union of Vajrasattva

it is neither annihilation nor permanence,
without limbs, without color —
all things are not truly existent.
Beginning, middle, and end are abandoned —
this is the defining characteristic of supreme bliss.

Unconnected with merit or with sin,
supreme bliss — stainless, utterly pure —
the sole great treasure of suchness

The nature of this supreme bliss

Without form, without merit, without sin —
arising and ceasing simply do not exist.

Now the narrative account shall be fully told.

At the time when the Blessed One was on the verge of perfect awakening, in order to obstruct his awakening, an exceedingly terrifying great fear appeared and an immeasurable great host of the army of Māra arrived. At that time the Blessed One entered into meditative absorption in the samādhi called Supreme Victor over the Great Māra. From the vajras of his own body, speech, and mind, the wrathful Yamāntaka was definitively emanated. Having been thus emanated, while Vajrapāṇi was acting in accordance with the command of the Blessed One, the great mighty one, the Blessed One declared: "Vajrapāṇi, assume the form of this wrathful Yamāntaka and strike terror and fear into māras, yakṣas, asuras, gods, and rākṣasas!"

At that time, having heard what the Blessed One thus declared, I seized it in an instant, grasped its meaning, compressed it, and with great joy gave rise to excellent intention. To the great lord of secrets he then declared:

The methods for fully entering the city of the other shore
should be made perfectly clear.
Those who wish to go and return
should recite the mantra with samādhi.
By reciting ten million times, accomplishment is attained —
what need is there even to speak of fifty million?

Begin from the first point onward.
Oneself being like space —
at the time when one first begins to accustom oneself,
let the mind be introduced to mind itself.

For example, this mind, as in a dream,
can go and can turn back again,
and in a single moment abides just so.
Through uninterrupted practice of continuous application —

just as when a weapon is held steady
and an arrow is shot from a bow,
so too here, through the bow of the mind,

it proceeds without interruption.
Through the joining of moon and sun,
the mind is to be driven by the wind.
Having fully released the body,
the mind goes forth and enters therein.

By gaining a little warmth,
the meditator, turned toward the side of attainment,
enters within the body.

For example, departing from another village
and overwhelming the pace of moon and sun,
through the application of entering Rāhu,
when the destination is reached —
through the practice of riding the wind —
having recognized the defining marks of the signs of death,
one proceeds by means of the ritual possessing those defining marks,
moving as one wishes, stirring or not stirring,
all one's desires arise fulfilled.

The lower path of thur-sel, at the secret extremity,
is released, and one begins at the navel.
Proceeding to the heart and the throat,
the life-breath passes out through the nostrils.

This is the nineteenth detailed section: the entry into the body of another village, from the tantra of Yamāntaka the Red — the Body, Speech, and Mind of all the Tathāgatas.

The glorious Tantra King of Yamāntaka the Red is complete.

This was requested in the presence of the Śākya monk Candraśrī, drawing on the spoken luminous rays of the venerable Lama Dharmarāja, the speech of the Bhoṭa paṇḍita, and the speech of the human sovereign Künga Zangpo, with gold offered by Pön Küngzhön.
