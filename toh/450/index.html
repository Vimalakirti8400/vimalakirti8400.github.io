About this text

Toh 450 in the Kangyur is a tantric scripture known in Sanskrit as the Śrī Jñāna Vajra Samuccaya and in Tibetan as the Dpal ye shes rdo rje kun las bsdus pa, rendered in English as "The Glorious Compendium of All Wisdom-Vajra." The text takes the form of a buddha's discourse addressed to an assembly of great bodhisattvas, including Maitreya, who pose questions about fundamental topics such as ignorance, mental factors, and the nature of mind. The Blessed One, described as the very embodiment of wisdom dwelling in the secret expanse of all Tathāgatas, responds from within a profound meditative absorption that unifies the body, speech, and mind of all buddhas. The text belongs to the tantric section of the Kangyur and opens with homage to the Glorious Mañjuvajra, indicating its association with the Mañjuśrī or Vajradhātu cycle of tantric teachings.

Translation

In the language of India: Śrī Jñāna Vajra Samuccaya. In the language of Tibet: The Glorious Compendium of All Wisdom-Vajra. Homage to the Glorious Mañjuvajra.

Thus did I hear. At one time the Blessed One, being himself the essential nature of wisdom, dwelt in the secret expanse of all Tathāgatas, and entered into the undivided meditative absorption that is the sameness-taste sacred commitment vajra of the body, speech, and mind of all buddhas.

Thereupon the bodhisattvas — the great bodhisattva Maitreya and the others — made request and supplication. They then addressed the Blessed One: "What, pray, is what is called ignorance, mental factors, and mind?"

When they had spoken those words, the one who is himself the vajra nature of the body, speech, and mind of the Blessed One, the Tathāgata of all Tathāgatas, declared:

"Sons of noble lineage, listen well.

From the vajra of the three wisdoms
the very suchness of the vajra bodhicitta
truly arises — and for that reason
the intrinsic nature of the wisdom-vajra
is to be understood thus:

The three wisdoms — pure mind and the three appearances — are the vajra, the source from which they arise; inseparably, wind and rigpa abide as non-dual. Bodhicitta is the inseparability of conventional and ultimate. Vajra suchness is that which truly arises from the nature of reality (dharmatā), primordially and spontaneously accomplished — the great bliss that is the essential nature of all phenomena. Since it arises from, enters into, and abides as the vajra of the body, speech, and mind of the buddhas, all phenomena of saṃsāra and nirvāṇa are pervaded and governed by the wisdom-vajra, the inseparability of wind and mind, which is unobstructed.

The intent of all Tathāgatas is the non-duality of basic space (dharmadhātu) and wisdom; it is that which supremely arises from manifest awakening itself. From the inseparability of wind and rigpa, at all times the three appearances arise and do not cease. This very thing is to be fully understood as co-emergent.

The genuine meaning is profound. As for what is truly taught in the nature of the vajra: the secret, true bodhicitta — from the nature of reality (dharmatā), the inseparability of wind and mind — is a self-aware knowing (rang rig), profound and unchanging, free from conceptual elaboration, unceasing and pacified. From within the state of non-arising, equality, even though there is no conceptual realization, from causes and conditions, that very knowing-ignorance arises, sourceless, with no beginning and no end. From that, co-emergent ignorance — that which is proximately attained, without substantiality — arises unobstructed from luminosity, from the core of all selflessness, from non-appearance; though not sequential in its arising, it arises within profound great emptiness.

From ignorance, the condition of appearance arises; appearance, once attained, is non-conceptual. From that, intrinsic nature arises from mind; from that, appearance expands — utterly luminous and vast in emptiness. The ornaments arising from mind: from cause, from the eye, the object, the sense faculty, and from the immediately preceding condition, what is called mind arises. That has been declared to be the meaning of appearance and emptiness.

Mind abides in its object; mental factors individually display their distinctions. That very ignorance is the nature of reality (dharmatā). Since the nature of reality does not abide as a self, those appearances and emptinesses — three and three — arise free from conceptual elaboration. They arise from within luminosity, and because luminosity, bliss, and emptiness are inseparable, the essential nature of vajra bodhicitta is thus revealed."

Having taught this, the bodhisattvas — Maitreya and the rest — were at that time struck with the most profound wonder, and remained in silence, saying nothing at all.

Thereupon the Blessed One, who is himself the vajra nature of the body, speech, and mind of all Tathāgatas, seeing that those bodhisattvas had fallen silent and said nothing, declared: "Why do you remain silent?"

Then the great bodhisattvas prostrated at the two feet of the Blessed One, touched their heads to the ground, and made this further request:

How wondrous! O Teacher, Holder of the Vajra,
The consciousness so difficult to understand,
Divided into three in its most profound distinction —
May the Blessed One be pleased to teach it,
May the Sugata be pleased to speak.

When they had so requested, the Blessed One declared:

That which arises from luminosity itself
Is called mind, mentation, and consciousness —
Become the root of all phenomena.
From it, the self that is wholly afflicted
And the self that is thoroughly purified
Are recognized — two knowings arise from that.

The mount of that consciousness is the wind element.
From wind arises fire, from fire arises water,
And from water, it is taught, earth in turn arises.
From their assembling come the five aggregates themselves,
The six sense bases, and whatever appears as sense objects —
All of these are dependent origination,
The mingling of wind and consciousness in close union.

Thereupon the three consciousnesses make manifest
The appearing of the intrinsic nature with perfect clarity,
Experiencing conduct in its wake.
Through the cause and effect of appearances, the intrinsic natures
Arise thoroughly and correctly,
And yet appear like a dream or an illusion.

And that luminosity is without abiding,
Without cessation, and without arising —
The true ultimate truth, the perfect limit.
What arises from it as the darkness-appearance
Is taught as the cessation called ignorance.
Its intrinsic-nature appearances are
Confusion, distraction,
Lack of mindfulness, laziness,
Non-speaking, and the unilluminated.

Ignorance also arises from mind.
From the near-attainment of the appearance that blazes like a lamp —
The essential nature being dharmatā, free of conceptual thought —
Through the subtle entering of the wind element,
It is knowing and yet dim, like a neuter being.

Taught as resembling the light of the sun,
And like the most brilliant mirage —
Its intrinsic nature comprises attachment, joy,
Supreme joy, ferocity, and the like.
On the conventional level it is revealed through direct perception,
Through masculine form, through the assembly of father-gods,
And the symbol "vajra" is its own posited designation.

From mind, that which is called "mind" is explained.
Like the light of the moon, supremely clear —
Taught as resembling the reflection of the moon in water.
Its intrinsic nature is freedom from attachment and so forth.
On the conventional level, from the luminous appearance,
It is the symbol of women, the bhaga, the lotus, and the assembly of goddesses.

Now, in order to teach the abiding nature of all phenomena:

That which arises from luminosity itself is dharmatā, luminosity naturally and perfectly pure by its intrinsic nature. From the luminous expanse, awareness-as-non-conceptual knowing arises together with the subtle wind. Thus mind is the all-base consciousness (ālayavijñāna). Mentation-consciousness is that which engages in expansion and contraction. Consciousness is that which possesses the eight collections.

The root of all phenomena is the awakening mind, unchanging, vajra-like, equal to space itself.

From impure seeds, wandering beings appear as thoroughly afflicted in their delusion. From habitual tendencies, that which is the reverse of delusion appears as the thoroughly purified.

From the various conventional designations imputed by those outside the Buddhist tradition, taken as a self, conceptuality becomes twofold and appears as subject and object. Self and other — by the dual imputation of grasping at self and what belongs to self — one grasps at things and at signs, and this is made known in mind itself.

From that, it appears as consciousness itself, and from that very point, through the wind of dharmatā, luminous appearance dawns as self-arisen luminosity.

Thereupon, from the stirring of the wind of karma, fire arises from attachment, and the water-appearance of aversion arises from friction.

From warmth and moisture, the earth element of delusion arises, and there the five elements gather and collect together. From that, the root awareness of the all-base (ālayavijñāna) rests at the heart center alone, and from that, through straying into subject and object, these elements assemble, and from their assembly there appears the five aggregates (skandhas), the six sense sources (āyatanas), and the five sense objects. Since wind and consciousness are inseparably mingled, this is nothing other than dependent origination arising everywhere without obstruction.

From within the non-conceptual state of the nature of reality (dharmatā), arising together with the wind, the manner in which consciousness moves through existence in the intermediate state is as follows. From that point, the three consciousnesses arise: luminous appearance arising from condition, the spreading of luminosity, and the attainment of luminosity — from the causal condition, the object condition, the dominant condition, and the immediately preceding condition — manifest arising, the luminosity of a nature endowed with conceptuality, the thirty-three natures, the forty characteristics, and the seven moments of ignorance, clearly experienced in activity by day and by night. This is the time of impure experience.

When the wind subsides and awareness rests even slightly, all the causes and effects of the three luminosities become like illusions, and every aspect of intrinsic nature is self-purified, self-pacified, and self-liberated in the expanse of primordial wisdom. Then, through the arising of the non-conceptual wisdom of suchness (tathatā) — blissful, luminous, empty, and selfless — the intermediate state of the purification of habitual tendencies, the intermediate state of dream, is where body and speech appear everywhere like illusions.

That very illusory-body appearance purifies the activities of conventional truth. By fully engaging with luminosity, holding it as a unity, and through the meditation of subsequent dissolution, when co-emergent defilements are completely purified, there is no abiding anywhere, no obstruction with respect to all phenomena, no abiding, and no arising. This is the meaning of the non-abiding of the ultimate truth free from conceptual elaboration, and is called the non-appearance of the extreme of the real.

From that very state, a subtle wind of darkness-like luminosity, turned back from its natural course, is taught as the cessation that is ignorance. Its nature gives rise to appearance, to confusion, distraction, doubt, forgetfulness, laziness, speechlessness, and weariness.

Ignorance arises from mind. From the blazing of the luminosity of attainment like a lamp, the essential nature — the non-conceptual nature of reality — enters subtly into the element of wind, and consciousness remains unmoved, like one who is neither male nor female. At the navel, in a lotus of eight petals along with four voids, the syllable of the neuter rests. The Hrīḥ with its bindu is visualized in the center. This is the very meaning of the attainment of luminosity.

The spreading of luminosity is taught as being like the sun, supremely clear, like a mirage. Its nature includes desire, joy, supreme joy, intense joy, and the like. At the conventional level it is indicated by direct perception, by the form of the male, by assemblies of gods, and by the sign called vajra — all designated by one's own attribution. In that very same lotus at the navel, in the disc of the sun, a vajra with a bindu stands in the center; the thirty-seven syllables of the Kālī clarifiers illuminate the very spreading of luminosity.

What arises from mind is called mind. Luminosity like the moon, supremely brilliant, is taught as being like the reflection of the moon in water. Its nature is free from attachment, and so forth. At the conventional level, from the luminosity, it is the sign of the female, of the bhaga (womb), the lotus goddess and her retinue. As before, with the moon in the center together with the lotus, the vowels extend in the directions as blazing light; at the junctions, twelve pairs are set out in sequence. This completes all the progressive stages of the secret mantra wheel. Visualization with recitation causes the three minds to appear as illusion — the union arising from within the expanse of the clear-light wisdom.

The spreading of luminosity is bliss.

Without a fixed object to grasp, attachment and clinging are released. Like the brilliance of the sun, bliss arises naturally from within. The very dwelling, coming, and going — each instant — is meditative absorption free of signs, the opening that dispels darkness.

The mind of wisdom that has attained luminosity is of the nature of non-conceptual awareness. That very ignorance, now thoroughly purified, is without darkness and without luminosity as a separate appearance — arising like a lamp, spontaneously present, unmoved and unmoving, perfectly pure. Purified within the expanse of the nature of reality, which has no fixed abode: emptiness, great emptiness, utter emptiness free of substantiality, the emptiness of all things that is undefiled, the near-attainment without appearance — pure like space, luminous clarity, the great mudrā beyond compare and beyond duality, the dharmadhātu of co-emergent arising, transcending all objects of special insight, all-pervading peace free of conceptual elaboration. From within the expanse of emptiness, transcending the extremes of both conceptual thought and non-conceptual thought — such is the vast ocean that is itself the nature of reality, profound and immeasurable. Upon it, grasses, trees, moving things in all their variety rise and fall, come and go, and though covered and stirred by multitudes, by reason of its non-conceptual nature that has no fixed abode, it is of one taste, unchanging, like space.

This is the first chapter of the Śrī Jñānavajrasamuccaya.

The yogi's very body is like an illusion, like a dream. From that which is the certainty of the body as it actually abides, the perfect union of vajra and lotus gives rise to the wisdom of discernment. From the union of one's own vajra arises fire. From fire, water; from water, liquid substances — first those that are smooth and fluid, then those that are bright and reddish, then those that grow firm and solid, and those that become rounded. Then, from the luminosity free of attachment, from globules of blood arise those that become hard and cartilaginous. These, bearing the form of Viṣṇu's tortoise, are generated from the vital fluids and blood of the parents. Then, from the attachment that arises through the equal union of the two sense faculties, one should understand it in terms of that which is free from attachment and the middle portion free from attachment. From that, the channels arise manifestly in the body — seventy-two thousand and more. There are the central channel, the right channel, the left channel, the navel, the heart center, the throat, the crown, the secret center, the navel-hair wheel, and again thirty-two; the three-mouthed channel, the chain channel, the eye-like channel at the root, the middle channel that is the precious palace, the channel that is the seat of awareness, the shape of the great bow, and the bliss-sustaining jewel wheel. From these many and various forms, abiding at the heart as vital essence in the form of syllables and the like that have become life-breath, come the immeasurable streams of beings of the three realms, bearing within them all faults and all qualities. Therefore, the mudrā that appears as the maṇḍala arising from the body and emerging from the body — and so forth — together with the mudrā of vocal recitation and the mudrā of meditation become three. The mantras that arise perfectly from the vowels and consonants, along with wisdom as the method of mind, are to be held as objects of focus in the manner of great bliss that is undivided.

From that very bliss of the union of the father and mother,
Attaining the portions of the family through the successive stages,
Arising as the five wisdoms of manifest awakening,
The perfected body arises and will perform its activities.

All phenomena are to be established as the self, and the self is to be established as the deity — the deity itself is wisdom. Form and the other sense objects and the two sense faculties have become method. The perfect union of vajra and lotus is the method of mind and mental factors. The great bliss of samādhi arising from the equal union of mental factors makes method and wisdom of equal taste. This is indicated by the example of what resembles an illusion. Then the tathāgatas in buddha fields as numerous as the grains of sand of the Ganges—

an illusion, a reflection of the moon in water, a visual disturbance, a mirage, a dream, an echo, a city of gandharvas (scent-eaters), an optical illusion, a rainbow, lightning, a water bubble, and a reflection in a mirror — by these twelve examples of illusion, the samādhi of great bliss is indicated.

From the channels and winds of the vajra body,
the syllables, the bindus dwelling there,
and the assemblies of vowels and consonants,
method and wisdom arise as a single taste.

Clarity and bliss as the very ground,
non-conceptual dharmatā and activity —
wind and awareness, in equal union,
arising, entering, and abiding: that is what it is.

Dharmatā, luminosity, is the all-base —
consciousness unmodified, self-luminous,
unobscured, self-arisen, constantly
moving yet unchanging, constantly:
that suchness abiding is what serves as the ground.

The arising of aspects and mental factors —
their many natures, both sequential
and non-sequential in character —
are steered by the conditions of conceptual gathering and dispersion.
From the three free of attachment they arise.

Mental engagement — coming and going,
awareness becoming agitated, and mental withdrawal.
The three sorrows: faithlessness,
residing peacefully through absence of aversion,
pervasive conceptuality, anger —
these are mere fright, burning.
Contact, coarse conduct, and holding grudges —
great terror.
Craving arising as great, middling, and small;
the four appropriating clinging, which conceal.
Non-virtue — the torment of hunger and thirst.
The four mental engagements of a vidyādhara.
The knower — from abiding
comes the feeling that makes one wonder:
it is of three kinds — intention.
Sleep and regret, investigation and analysis,
sense of shame and conscientiousness,
non-harmfulness, compassion,
and tenderness and loving-kindness — three and three —
and doubt, accumulation, miserliness,
and jealousy: conceptuality is their intrinsic nature.
Within the state of luminosity, expanding and gathering.
Attachment, fixation, and joy,
delight and extreme delight —
these are the arising of the realization of attachment.
Contentment through bliss, absence of delusion,
great satisfaction, wisdom,
wonder, laughter, and fulfillment.
Embracing, kissing, pressing together,
and resting — that very samādhi.
Diligence, triumph,
action and seizure arise from effort.
Boasting of strength, pride, arrogance.
Through aspiration one becomes elated.
Innate self-nature is equanimity.
Non-differentiation, which is supreme.
These six — elegance, aversion, resentment.
The virtuous with conscientiousness —
clear of speech, truthful, with faith,
without deceit, endowed with mindfulness.
Untruth and, likewise, what is definite.
Not vigilance, what is uncertain.
Non-grasping, the mind of non-attachment.
Giving, exhortation — being straightforward.
Likewise, delight, and shamelessness.
Bold harm-doing.
Heedlessness and lack of shame.
That which lacks vigilance.
Through deception and guile, performing shows, inconsistency,
engaging in inconstancy, restlessness with remorse —
these are the arising of conceptuality.
From the awareness of luminosity's expansion,
they roam and enter into activity.
These characteristics have been taught.

Attachment: the intermediate moment —
like ignorance itself,
thick, devoid of luminosity.
Forgetfulness and dullness.
Great confusion with wrong views.
The inexpressible is exhausted through entering into cessation.
Weariness, laziness, and sluggishness.
Uncertainty, mental scrutiny.
The arising of indeterminate states, subtle and
gross — arising, ceasing, and abiding.
All habitual tendencies, defiled conceptuality —
in the very suchness of attaining luminosity,
through emptiness free of conceptual elaboration,
they become pure and luminous.

That very inner consciousness —
the dharmatā co-emergent, free of conceptuality,
luminous in essence, empty and selfless —

Moved by wind, the aspects of appearance arise.
In that very instant, intrinsic nature enters into arising.
The three appearances (appearances, increase, and attainment) become manifest.

Therefore, having ascertained these points definitively — just as butter has no deliberate combining with milk and sesame, and just as sesame oil simply arises from sesame — so too, without deliberately combining the vajra and the lotus through the mantra and mudrā of deliberate union, one is unable to closely examine the three consciousnesses. Where there is no wind, there is no support of the all-base consciousness. Where there is no support of the all-base, consciousness does not arise. Where consciousness itself is absent, there is no capacity to properly unite the bhaga and the liṅga, and thus the meditative absorption of great bliss is not attained. Just as butter arises from churning milk, and just as sesame oil arises from sesame, so too it is for the purpose of closely examining the vajra — the gnosis of skillful means — and closely examining the lotus — the gnosis of wisdom. There is no engagement with an object, and since there is no mental grasping at it, the wind, which is the mount of consciousness, is absent. With that absent, the outer four great elements and the inner elements and sense bases cease to be, and the apprehension of the three appearances does not arise. Since there is no entering into the ultimate limit, union does not occur.

The essential nature of awakening mind —
through accomplished devotion, brought to its end,
toward the guru, the vajra-holder —
all the stages of the path are perfected.
Obscurations purified, qualities attained,
as one who has found the victory of empowerment:
all activities and accomplishments without remainder,
and the inexhaustible abode of gnosis is reached.

Whoever, through the meditative absorption of great bliss in which all stages of the yogic path are simultaneously perfected, by aspiration alone, will come to abide as a beginner and will also come to abide in irreversibility.

This is the second chapter of the glorious Yeshe Dorje Küntü Dedupa.

The teaching of selflessness set forth in the general meaning, like a sūtra:

E is the dharma; BAṂ is the person.
Empty, peaceful, without inherent existence —
all aggregates, elements, and sense bases entirely,
all phenomena are free, lacking intrinsic nature.
The gnosis of equanimity, beyond acceptance and rejection.

E is emptiness free of conceptual elaboration;
BAṂ is taught as the supreme skillful means and great compassion.
Non-dual, equal, the bodhicitta of one taste.

The three doors of liberation and the six perfections,
the four immeasurables and the four means of gathering,
the thirty-seven factors conducive to awakening,
the four individual perfect cognitions, the unmingled qualities,
the ten powers, the four fearlessnesses, and the unguarded —
the vast expanse of knowledge itself pervades all.

From E and BAṂ — stability and movement, appearance and emptiness —
all aggregates, elements, and sense bases
are pure maṇḍalas of the divine nature.

The many meanings of syllables, sounds, and conventional expressions
are without conceptual fabrication; arising from various conditions,
causes and effects, distinguished in appearance, like an illusion —
these are the conventional notions of the world, signifying through sound.

That which is called "the hidden teaching of desire":
from the yogic union of skillful means and wisdom,
the various forms of first joy, and subsequent attachment,
and the highest ripening — supreme bliss itself,
free of characteristics, arising moment by moment, co-emergent,
the great seal of unchanging blissful gnosis —
the different aspects, free of joy, follow thereafter.
That very essence is the unsurpassed awakening mind,
bliss and emptiness inseparable, the great awakening.

All and always, the awakening mind purified by the fire of gnosis:
through undefiled bliss, body and mind become utterly pure.

Throughout the world, the many forms of stability —
dependent thereon, Mount Meru assembled through dependent origination,
celestial palaces and so forth appear from the delusion of things.
Dependent upon them, all sentient beings of the three realms, throughout their lineages,
arise and cease like illusions.
Saṃsāra has arisen from objects, sense faculties, and conceptual thought.
From the blessing of the Tathāgatas —

By the perfectly averting teaching of the conventional path,
turning back from grasping at phenomena as defining marks,
through realizing the true meaning of intrinsic nature, awakening is attained.

That suchness of luminosity
is distinguished from ordinary consciousness in general.
From it, mind, unmodified,
not falling to any side of the abiding nature,
also free of conceptuality, in the nature of reality,
manifests plainly as the three appearances.

Just as from a state where object and subject have subsided,
from an ocean utterly calm and clear,
moved by wind arising therefrom,
waves emerge and roll —
so from intrinsic nature, mental factors and masses of conceptuality arise.
Through the power of abiding place, cause, time, and condition:
while abiding, from luminous emptiness,
the cause being the inseparability of wind and mind,
in the three times and in the timeless, instant by instant,
what is empty in essence is stirred by wind.
From conditions it appears as phenomena bearing defining marks.
Through the differentiation of abiding things establishing particular distinctions,
that very consciousness is presented as an offering,
and distinguished from what is non-erring.
From the variety of virtue and non-virtue,
pleasure and suffering are experienced.
From karma, afflictions, and habitual tendencies,
actions of body, speech, and mind appear to manifest.
Through the instructions of a teacher who purifies what is impure,
one is liberated from all of it.
The appearances of merit, non-merit, and their actions —
the varied arising of causes and effects —
emerge from a space-like expanse
as space-like magical display.
That very reality, from its nature of unborn arising,
abides without cessation.
It is dependent origination:
that very suchness, from the nature of reality as selflessness,
beyond conceptual elaboration, free of mixing of existence and non-existence,
abides perfectly complete, without fixed location.

In what abides in the sequence of a string of beads,
the wheel that is firm gathers the sequence in its proper order.
The very heart of the mudrā, bearing all sacred commitments,
the five elements in their entirety, the goddess Vajradhātu (the deity who is the space of the vajra).
Great bliss, the vajra, the secret excellence:
that unchanging suchness, into which the arising ground dissolves —
in an instant, all adornments complete, like a filled vase,
the nectar of the crown becomes gnosis victorious.

The three syllables that express it denote the object: Vajradhṛk.
It is the manifestation born from the completion of activity.
Having attained appearance, and simultaneously abiding therein, the subject-bearing: the expansion of appearance.
Through awareness entering into the particular that conceptualizes, the brilliant: the appearance of radiance.

Maitreya (Loving-Kindness, the bodhisattva) then asked: What is the hidden meaning?

The Blessed One declared: What is hidden by conventional truth is this: the entire vessel and its contents, without remainder, appearing as if illusory — that I have blessed, and it is naturally true and perfectly pure.

What is hidden by the three appearances of mind is this: what arises perfectly from the nature of reality — appearance, expansion, and radiance — arises together with intrinsic nature, like the mind and gnosis free of all conceptual thought.

What is hidden by phenomena is this: the meaning of bliss — the bliss of uniting vajra and lotus equally — which signifies the three appearances of naturally pure intrinsic nature: that will come to perfectly abide in the state of great bliss. This is the explanation of the hidden meaning.

Then the great bodhisattva Maitreya asked: Blessed One, what is the general meaning?

The Blessed One declared: The general meaning is explained for the sake of those — such as śrāvakas — who practice the Dharma free of attachment, who have not been liberated from the habitual tendencies of conceptual thought, and who, throughout all the tantras of the Great Yoga, upon hearing the astonishing vajra words that explicitly set forth the phenomena of desire — such as the expression "dwelling in the bhaga of the noble vajra lady" and the like — would disparage them. It is spoken as the general meaning in a manner not contradicting those persons.

Maitreya asked: What is the literal meaning?

The Blessed One declared: Whatever is expressed in reliance upon the meaning of the sounds of the vowel and consonant series, making the limits of expression, the limits and expressions arising from conditions in their variety, together with its explications — this is taught as a partial indication relying upon the diverse conventional designations.

Maitreya then asked:

Maitreya asked: How is this supreme, unshared ultimate explained as being definitive?

Then the Blessed One declared, giving the word "Excellent!" to the great bodhisattvas: Excellent, excellent! O bodhisattva endowed with supreme compassion, listen. The limit is the boundary. "The mode of genuine luminosity" is the ultimate. The limit, having a limit, bound, the genuine limit, the limit itself, free from reversal, transcending all philosophical tenets — this is the ultimate.

The great bodhisattva asked: Blessed One, by what means is the ultimate attained?

The Blessed One declared: The three emptinesses of the limits of the aggregates, elements, and sense sources and so forth, as appearing in the three appearances — empty of those. The limit of the three emptinesses — free from referential object, like an illusion — is the bliss-emptiness of luminosity.

The great bodhisattva asked: Blessed One, how does one arrive at the ultimate of the three emptinesses as luminosity?

The Blessed One declared: Just as fire burns wood and causes it to blaze, so likewise the light of wisdom in luminosity is caused to operate by gnosis over a long duration. Just as it causes even the leaves of a palm tree to blaze, so likewise skillful means causes the expansion of gnosis into luminosity to be thoroughly purified. Just as the petals of a cotton blossom are burned, so likewise the gnosis that obtains appearance, becoming supreme, causes thorough purification. Therefore, just as the leaves of the palm tree and the petals of cotton, when burned, blaze and are reduced to ash, so likewise emptiness, great emptiness, utter emptiness, and total emptiness, through the proximate attainment of appearance, become luminous.

Luminosity is ultimate truth itself.
Through realization of the gnosis of equality,
the mass of conceptuality rising from the basic space —
the activity of the impure antidote —
and all things: cause, condition, object, engagement,
are burned by the self-arisen fire of non-conceptual awareness.
Just as the many varieties of wood
and the many bundles of cotton
are burned and rendered substanceless,
so likewise all grasping at form without remainder
is burned and dissolved in the very ground of the dharmadhātu
and abides no more.

Through the vajra-like meditative absorption
all conceptuality is utterly destroyed without remainder;
the knowing of perfectly pure gnosis,
the stream of the awakening mind,
flows without cessation, its enlightened activity an unbroken stream;
unchanging emptiness, great bliss,
union, the perfectly non-abiding —
arising from the unattainable, universally arising,
spontaneously accomplished, abiding as suchness.

From suchness itself, conceptuality and non-conceptuality become thoroughly purified, and through the distinction of the intrinsic nature as trained and untrained, one arrives at the essential nature of perfect non-abiding. This is spoken as the definitive teaching.

This concludes the third chapter of the glorious Jñānavajrasamuccaya.

Maitreya asked: Blessed One, how is it that all the Tathāgatas in the yoga tantras explain things by means of the six limits?

The Blessed One declared: By means of: taught with intent, and taught without intent; provisional meaning, and definitive meaning; literal, and non-literal.

Maitreya asked: Out of compassion, please, Blessed One, show the distinction of each term.

The Blessed One declared: That very definitive teaching — which is taught with intent in a manner that is wonderfully contrary to worldly convention — is as follows: such vajra expressions as "neither attachment, nor free from attachment, nor the middle between them, not to be found as an intermediate" and the like are taught with intent.

Bliss is not defiled bliss;
it is bliss that is without defilement.

Bliss has been relinquished, and without result,
it arises like an illusion.
That very wisdom of bliss as suchness
is not perceived even as a middle.

For persons of lesser faculty
it appears as a terrifying contradiction.
For those of supreme faculty, upon hearing
a teaching on phenomena never heard before,
wondrous certainty arises within them,
and through that they attain a marvelous meditative absorption.
This is the defining mark of attaining liberation.

In order to refute those who speak of outer and secret doctrines, who hold the impure to be pure, who wish the unliberated to be liberated, and who enter by their own whim — to reverse even a slight amount of conceptual worldly thinking — the meaning of profound wisdom, the purification of confused appearances about the abiding nature, that very essence was taught with intent.

Now, the following vajra expressions are taught without intent: "no body, no speech, no mind," "without self," "all is empty," "purity," "luminosity," and the like. They are taught without intent because they are not intended to confirm grasping at things and characteristics, or at a self, in all phenomena. Rather, they teach that all phenomena are selfless, and from conceptual-elaboration-free suchness they rely upon emptiness; all phenomena are primordially pure in their intrinsic nature; "no body" means that the aggregates, elements, and sense sources are not perceived; beyond verbal expression, there is no self; and "no mind" is taught without intent because all is empty of inherent existence.

The following vajra expressions are provisional in meaning: camphor, great blood, red sandalwood, vajra, lotus, syllable, and mudrā. Here, camphor refers to semen; great blood refers to menstrual blood; and the mixture with the fluid of red sandalwood refers to that fluid itself.

The following vajra expressions are definitive in meaning: vajra-liṅga (the mark of the father), the characteristic mark, skillful means, lotus-bhaga (the lotus of the mother), wisdom, syllable, mudrā, and the consorts, and so forth. Camphor refers to the maṇḍala syllable; great blood refers to the syllable of the solar maṇḍala; red sandalwood refers to the red eight-petalled lotus with its three syllables; the vajra-tantra characteristic mark is the father; the lotus characteristic mark is the mother; the syllable is the mantra-seed; and the mudrā is the hand-emblem — the fully accomplished body endowed with the four mudrās.

The fully perfected divine body, together with the deities thoroughly visualized, has some capacity to avert confusion beyond the ordinary — that is the common teaching. Furthermore, from the perspective of the meaning of non-difference in intrinsic nature: camphor is the semen called kapūraka, flowing from the motion of all forms; great blood is self-arisen blood, the siṃhalarakta, flowing from root and hair; the fluid of red sandalwood, mingled within the dharmodaya, is fully merged inseparably within the lotus-womb of the mother, and from that non-separation it is the very completion of the full awakening of the bodies of beings. The lotus-bhaga is the wisdom aspect; the vajra-liṅga is the skillful means aspect; from the balanced reverse of sun and moon becoming equalized, the syllable — the seed — accomplishes the qualities. The father-and-mother, the vajra consorts endowed with the four mudrās, truly demonstrate from the dharmodaya the meaning of manifest bliss as gnosis — that is the uncommon teaching.

Maitreya asked: What, then, is the provisional meaning of these?

The Blessed One declared: Whatever is expressed through the medium of the various sound-meanings of the vowels (āli) and consonants (kāli) — their limits, the common characteristics of what is expressed, and the uncommon secret: for the great bodhisattvas, the dhāraṇī-mantras and vidyā-mantras of the supreme great secret are to be thoroughly realized in dependence upon the suchness of this sound. And for those persons who have become logicians, the common verbal expression is the indivisible vajra syllable which functions as life-force; the many collections of teachings also arise from that suchness and dissolve back into suchness — this is what is taught as the provisional meaning.

For those as well, there are the five aggregates, the five families, the five elements, and the five wisdoms — all perfectly pure — together with the heaps of moral discipline, meditative absorption, wisdom, liberation, and the wisdom of the vision of liberation. The support of space meets with the four directions, turning. From the body of sun and moon abiding in their place are born the maṇḍalas of empowerment, trembling with movement, turning toward the directions from the fourth abode. The rivers number eighty-four: a thousand and eighty, bearing the light of Dhṛti. These become gateways to the Dharma and arise as the supreme aggregate. In the both-together tantra (ubhaya-tantra) and the action tantra and the rest, what is explained with clear meaning in the literal sense of the words — for instance, the maṇḍala, the fire-offering, the torma, the medicinal substances, the assemblage of rites for pacification and the rest, and likewise the teaching of the eight great worldly accomplishments — all of this is explained in the literal sense of the words, set forth according to the meaning of the syllables.

The maṇḍala's conceptual ritual and the rest,
the conduct of approach and accomplishment bearing fruit,
the detailed explanation of the meaning of the limbs —
this is what is taught as the literal sense of the words.

Koṭakṣa, Koṭabhaṇa, Koṭave, Koṭabhaśvapa, Katirka, Kolakhya, Kolaba, Kolabhaṣṭha, Kolastathā, Kolabhābhi — by these, the sense faculties and their objects are expressed. Such vajra-syllables as these are a hidden explanation, conveyed in a manner that is not literal. The secret of the Tathāgata's symbolic language, what the ḍākinīs teach by signs such as Koṭakṣa and so forth, and the secret symbolic usage unknown in the world — this is taught as the non-literal expression.

From bodhicitta itself, free of conceptual elaboration,
free of extremes, devoid of conceptual thought,
the great seal (mahāmudrā) without object —
non-abiding union (yuganaddha), unsurpassed.
The vajra's self-arisen meditative absorption,
the essential nature of the Tathāgata —
this the Victor has taught as the definitive meaning,
establishing the analysis of the six extremes.

This is the fourth chapter of the glorious Jñānavajrasamuccaya.

Then the great bodhisattva asked: Blessed One, what are the classes of tantra of the great Vajra Vehicle? The Blessed One declared: The classes of tantra are as follows: the tantra of unsurpassed yoga, the tantra of both-together, the tantra of conduct, the tantra of action, and the tantra of conceptual ritual.

Maitreya asked: What is the measure of those tantras? The Blessed One declared: The tantra of unsurpassed yoga consists of twelve thousand verses; if expanded in full, it is beyond counting. The yoga tantra consists of six thousand verses. The conduct tantra consists of eight thousand verses. The action tantra consists of four thousand verses. The tantra of conceptual ritual and the subtle tantra consist of four thousand verses. Together these total thirty-four thousand verses.

The final letters of the names "tantra" and "conceptual ritual" include all of these: ka, kha, ga, gha, ṅa; ca, cha, ja, jha, ña; ṭa, ṭha, ḍa, ḍha, ṇa; ta, tha, da, dha, na; pa, pha, ba, bha, ma; ya, ra, la, va; śa, ṣa, sa, ha, kṣaḥ; a, ā, i, ī, u, ū; ṛ, ṝ, ḷ, ḹ, e, ai, o, au, aṃ, aḥ. These are the assemblages of the syllables of names.

The great bodhisattva asked: Blessed One, what are the ritual enactment and accomplishment of those? The Blessed One declared: Arising in due sequence through the blessing of the ultimate truth by one's own empowerment, in order to fully reveal the abode of awakening that arises from desire, one abides in the mahāsamādhi of great bliss in the form of a wheel-turning king in the midst of the retinue of noble consorts, entering fully into the bhaga of the vajra consort — and so one enacts and accomplishes the six extremes and the four modes of vajra-speech, which are wondrous conduct contrary to the ways of the world.

Thus it abides in the great yoga tantra.

The vidyā-queen, the goddess, and the retinue of the maṇḍala together are to be appointed to their places.

Then, fully practicing the ten aspects of suchness beginning with the accomplishment of enacted activity, one accomplishes — this abides in the yoga tantra.

Fully practicing the vast and various activities of action, the characteristic of the mudrā and visualization belonging to the activity tantra, one accomplishes — this abides in the conduct tantra.

Focusing upon saṃsāra as utterly terrifying, maintaining extreme purity, absent the bliss of a wisdom bodhisattva, absent the pride of oneself as a deity, and practicing through the wondrous sphere of activity proceeding from causes by way of one's own condition — one accomplishes — this abides in the action tantra.

Fully practicing mantra, medicinal substances, fire offering, eye medicine, swift-footedness, and so forth — the eight great siddhis — one accomplishes in the subtle and conceptual tantra.

Now in verse:

The nature of the great secret tantra,
its definitive words, their application, and their grounds,
together with the means of its distinctive classification —
the measure of the text and the measure of its elevation,
the mode of teaching what is stated and what does the stating:

By generating the awakening mind, the preliminary, and the concordant,
the distinctive path of ripening, the path of blessing,
the subsequent appearance, the connection — all uncommon —
the bliss-path, swift insight, and the transformation of phenomena:
this very thing is the profound and unsurpassed skillful means.

Of the glorious great Vajradhara,
inseparable from his body, speech, and mind —
the very essence of supreme great bliss,
whose nature is wisdom, stainless and pure.

"Tantra" — the word tantra means continuity:
tenta, the knowing of things, means continuity in what is real;
traya, the three times, being without beginning —
it is that which leads and destroys the host of thoughts.

A continuity that is connected, without missing link,
that expands from the element of sentient beings:
the bodhisattva yogi,
the buddha's deeds, boundless,
uninterrupted, the stream of wisdom,
the inexhaustible wheel of enlightened activity —
upon that very reality the name "tantra" is applied.

The basis of exposition and the occasion of exposition in the great secret:
extensive and condensed, the nature unchanging,
entry, abiding, consummation — unsurpassed:
this is the measure of elevation in the Vajra Vehicle.

Since previously the cause involved great anguish,
and one was unable to abandon it upon entry —
it is like that of the Great Lord (Mahādeva, Śiva).

Abiding in the path, the two-staged Dharma,
the nature unchanging, the supreme secret —
having traversed the grounds and paths to completion,
with the guru's path as the chief,
the unsurpassed is attained in this very life.

Cause, method-path, and result —
the root tantra of the secret that accomplishes:
from the supreme essence, one practices immeasurably.
The subsequent tantra that illuminates, easy to transform,
performs the ascetic practices and supplies what is lacking,
dispels doubt and bestows certainty,
applies the signs of the time to the mind,
and reveals the distinctions of vessel, knowledge, and fortune.

In the great secret, in all its general and particular aspects:
abiding in difference and in unity — samādhi —
and the ultimate sequence — the samaya of body, speech, and mind —
the suchness of the vow, pervading all — the awakening mind,
sealed with the seal of genuine great bliss,
wisdom immeasurable, cut off from no direction, falling to no extreme,
equality free of conceptual elaboration, great purity —
beyond sound and beyond what is not sound: the tantra is unsurpassed.

This is the fifth chapter of the Glorious Jñānavajrasamuccaya.

Maitreya asked: Blessed One, what is the first act of engagement?

The Blessed One declared: In the immeasurable palace of the perfectly pure, boundless great maṇḍala of the dharmadhātu — the unchanging great bliss of Akaniṣṭha — the Tathāgata, dwelling at the center surrounded by the retinue of all tathāgatas by means of the bodhicitta vajra, having conferred the unsurpassed secret empowerment of the assembled tathāgatas upon immeasurable great bodhisattvas — the attainment of complete purity of the great secret, the vajra-essence of body, speech, and mind, and the complete purity of wisdom:

Having become the inexhaustible source of adornment — the qualities and enlightened activities of the entire maṇḍala — abiding in the very state of pure appearance free from conceptual elaboration, emanating and gathering: this is called the first act of engagement.

Maitreya asked: Blessed One, how is the supreme king of maṇḍalas to be revealed?

The Blessed One declared:

In this very immeasurable maṇḍala,
the body, speech, and mind of the Tathāgata —
the supreme, unsurpassed vajra —
together with support and supported:
the heart-essence of the palace of the Sugata.

For the beings of the desire realm,
upon the great Mount Meru,
in the multistoried mansion:
Śakra (Indra) together with his retinue,
the thirty-two close lords and more,
with self-arisen jeweled offering clouds —
the sequence of empowerment is displayed.

Together with Īśvara and Umā,
Brahmā, Viṣṇu, the six-faced one, and others —
in order to tame them, through the non-dual union
of object and sense faculty,
by way of the conduct of enjoyment and bliss,
for the sake of accomplishing complete purification,
they are introduced into the maṇḍala.

Then, in that very pure realm,
transcending the state of suchness itself,
the supreme awakening mind, the unsurpassed vajra,
enters the overwhelming vajra samādhi.
The inseparability of dharmadhātu and gnosis, equality itself,
the yoga of great bliss — fully awakened:
sun, moon, lotus, the vowels and consonants (āli-kāli),
from the very suchness of the vajra of mind, speech, and body,
light blazes forth and pervades the world-systems.
All animate and inanimate existence becomes utterly pure.
The stream of luminous appearance is refined through the blessing,
and the rank of Vajrasattva is established for beings.

Having obtained bliss and mental joy,
thereafter, from the ocean of yoga and gnosis,
the inseparable sovereign — the body of awakening mind —
from the arising of gnosis and the vajra samādhi,
displays the miraculous display of blessing,
and the supreme very blessing of mantra —
the unsurpassed miraculous display of empowerment —
in the nature of the spontaneously accomplished heart-secret:

Oṃ Āḥ Hūṃ Svāhā — light radiates forth,
rays of light pervade the ten directions.

From Meṃ Bhāḥ Eṃ Oṃ Oṃ Oṃ Hūṃ Oṃ Saṃ:
the great bodhisattvas, whose nature is the eight consciousnesses.
Rū Śa Ga Ra Pa — the very objects of the senses.

subdue the directional demons of the assemblages of limbs and abodes.

Supremely excellent yoga displays miraculous wonders.

appears with three faces and six arms, adorned with ornaments.
With complete major and minor marks, bearing the mudrās.
That very one — the dharmadhātu vajra together with the four consorts —
comes forth from the dharmodaya and from the center of the heart.
From the emanation of many tathāgatas,
the explicit teaching — that very secret — is proclaimed.
The inexhaustible wheel of conventional miraculous display is shown.

Then, thereafter, for the sake of taming beings of the desire realm,
by way of the form of Vajrasattva that pleases —
from the body of Vairocana (the Illuminator), the vajra-holding form —
a portion of the body melts, manifesting as a goddess.
The conduct of great bliss and joy — the manifest play
of gnosis, emanating and gathering — is joyfully displayed.

The glorious supreme maṇḍala of Akṣobhyavajra (the Unshakeable Vajra),
immaculate, square, blessed.
Then immeasurable hosts of tathāgatas
pervade all directions and times, entirely surrounding it.
Awakening mind itself, the vajra, abides at the center.
The Blessed Vairocana, the jewel-born,
Amitābha, the unfailing depth.

Together with the immeasurable Sugatas,
non-dual with the primordial wisdom mother of self-luminous appearance —
this is the utterly perfect sequence of the great yoga.
Blessed by the vajra of body, speech, and mind,
the samaya being and the great wisdom being —
the power is stabilized and the empowerment and seal are conferred.
The samādhi being abides definitively at the heart.
The supreme protector of the great yoga,
the Tathāgata who is the being of great awareness —
skillful means and wisdom abide as non-dual.

When the immeasurable tathāgatas have assembled,
the immeasurable maṇḍalas of the retinue are beyond all telling.
With vast clouds of offerings of vajra, jewel, and lotus,
the sky is filled and worship is offered through a host of offerings.
To the Tathāgata, the great Vajradhara,
all tathāgatas and bodhisattvas together
made supplication in common.

"From the secret assembly of all tathāgatas,
arisen from the secret,
gathered into the vajra essence —
Blessed One, please expound the essence."

"Here, to you who have applied yourselves with delight to the definitive secret,
who have requested the supreme great secret —
since even all the most excellent tathāgatas

how do all you bodhisattvas understand it?"

through the very power and strength of the tathāgatas,
taking hold of the fortunate ones,
with the most excellent power, fully accomplished —
bless us with utterly profound realization,

All the wondrous wisdom-displays,
please reveal them clearly right now."

The glorious Vajradhara,
abiding in the vajra samādhi of manifest perfect awakening,
as the Primordial Protector himself,
the being of great awareness,
non-dual with the dharmadhātu vajra mother,

The glorious Akṣobhyavajra,

arises from Vajradhṛk.

like indranīla sapphire and jasmine flowers,

lotus and wheel and jewel,

pervading through all the Sugatas

they re-enter through the crown and dissolve into the heart.
Thereupon, immediately,
the Blessed One becomes Vajra Aversion.
Seated upon a lion throne of various precious gems,
above moon and sun,
he abides at the center of the great maṇḍala.

This is the samādhi of the manifest arising of the lord of the maṇḍala.

Translator's notes

The term "rang rig" appearing in this passage is a key Vajrayāna and Dzogchen technical term, often rendered "self-awareness" or "reflexive awareness." Here it is used in the compound "profound, unchanging self-aware knowing" (zab pa mi 'gyur rang rig ni). The translation renders it as "self-aware knowing" to preserve the technical sense without a lengthy gloss in the body.

The phrase "wind and rigpa" (rlung rig) is a tantric technical pair: wind (prāṇa) and rigpa (pure awareness, vidyā). "Rigpa" is left as "rigpa" in the compound phrases where "wind and rigpa" (rlung rig) are paired, since it functions as a proper technical term in this Vajrayāna context; elsewhere "rigpa" is contextually rendered.

The threefold repetition "three and three" (gsum gsum) for "appearance and emptiness" reflects the standard Dzogchen-Tantra schema of the three appearances (snang ba gsum) and their three empty counterparts; the translation preserves this formulation rather than speculating on a specific referent. The passage presents a cosmogonic and psycho-cosmological schema quite specific to a tantric context, treating mind, mentation, and consciousness as arising from luminosity and correlating them with elemental arising, three modes of appearance (sun-like, moon-like, lamp-like), and tantric symbolism of masculine and feminine principles.
