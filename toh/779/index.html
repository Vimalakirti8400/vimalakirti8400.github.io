About this text

This short text, found as Toh 779 in the Kangyur, is a mantra-based practice instruction whose title in English may be rendered along the lines of a rite for prostrating to the Three Jewels; its Sanskrit and Tibetan titles cannot be confidently determined from the opening alone. It begins with homage to the Three Jewels (the Buddha, the Dharma, and the Saṅgha) and prescribes reciting a mantra once before a representation of them while performing a full-length prostration. The text promises that three such prostrations equal the merit of hearing, reflecting upon, and meditating on the three collections of the teachings, the Tripiṭaka. It further states that three prostrations made daily will lead, in this very lifetime, to the meditative absorption of the wisdom of the path of seeing, and that until then one will be protected from harm by humans, nonhumans, and spirits. As Toh 779 in the Kangyur, it belongs to the tantra section, among the brief ritual and dhāraṇī-style texts that pair a mantra with a devotional practice and a promise of its benefits.

Translation

Homage to the Three Jewels.

Before a representation of the Three Jewels, recite this mantra once and make one prostration with the body fully stretched out. If three prostrations are made, one becomes equal in merit to having heard, reflected upon, and meditated upon the three collections of the teachings (the Tripiṭaka).
