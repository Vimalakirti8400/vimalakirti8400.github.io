About this text

The Noble Dhāraṇī Called "The Armlet on the Crest of the Banner," known in Sanskrit as the Āryadhvajāgrakeyūra-nāma-dhāraṇī and in Tibetan as 'Phags pa rgyal mtshan gyi rtse mo'i dpung rgyan ces bya ba'i gzungs, is a short scripture centered on a protective incantation. It opens in the Heaven of the Thirty-Three, where the Buddha is seated on a stone slab as white as wool. Śakra, lord of the gods, comes to him in haste and distress after he and the gods have been utterly defeated in battle by Vemacitrin, lord of the asuras. In response, the Buddha teaches a dhāraṇī meant to be worn or carried as an armlet or fixed atop a banner, conferring protection and victory over adversaries. The work is Toh 923 in the Kangyur, where it belongs among the dhāraṇī texts, the class of scriptures devoted to sacred formulas recited for protection and blessing.

In the language of India: ārya dhvaja agra keyūra nāma dhāraṇī

In the language of Tibet: The Noble Dhāraṇī Called "The Armlet on the Crest of the Banner."

Homage to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas.

Thus did I hear at one time. The Blessed One was dwelling among the gods of the Heaven of the Thirty-Three, seated on the stone slab that is like white wool. Then Śakra, lord of the gods, was defeated by the asuras, utterly defeated. In a hurried and agitated state he went to where the Blessed One was. When he arrived, he bowed his head at the Blessed One's feet and said to him:

"Blessed One, when I went into battle with the asuras, as has now happened, I was defeated by Vemacitrin, lord of the asuras, utterly defeated, and the gods of the Thirty-Three were also defeated, utterly defeated. Blessed One, how should we act in this situation?"

The Blessed One said, "Lord of the gods, take up the dhāraṇī called the Armlet on the Crest of the Banner, which others cannot overcome. Long ago, when I was a bodhisattva, I too received it from the tathāgata Aparājitadhvaja. Having received it, I explained it correctly and extensively to others. I remember that from that time on I have never experienced fear, terror, or hair standing on end, nor has my body suffered harm even for an instant, even for a moment."

"Blessed One, what is this dhāraṇī called the Armlet on the Crest of the Banner, which others cannot overcome?"

The Blessed One said:

"King of the gods, through this dhāraṇī called the Armlet on the Crest of the Banner, which others cannot overcome, whether you engage in battle, combat, dispute, or conflict, wherever you may go, you will be fearless and victorious in every way. If it is tied to the crest of a banner or worn at the neck, it protects kings of men and all heroic warriors. Transforming itself into the form of a woman, it stands before them, granting fearlessness and giving protection. It increases glory. It humbles the enemy's armies. It causes auspiciousness, purity, fame, glory, and excellence to abide always."

When the Blessed One had spoken thus, Śakra, lord of the gods, and the world with its gods, humans, asuras, and gandharvas rejoiced and praised what the Blessed One had said.

This concludes the Noble Dhāraṇī Called "The Armlet on the Crest of the Banner."

It was translated and edited by the Indian preceptors Jinamitra and Dānaśīla and the chief editor and translator, the monk Yeshé Dé. It was then revised according to the new terminology and finalized.

Translator's notes

The "stone slab like white wool" is the Pāṇḍukambala stone in the Heaven of the Thirty-Three, where the Buddha taught. In the passage on its benefits, the Tibetan gives no explicit subject for "transforming into the form of a woman." I take the dhāraṇī itself, personified as a goddess, as the subject and render it "it."
