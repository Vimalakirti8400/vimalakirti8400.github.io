About this text

Toh 614 in the Kangyur is a dhāraṇī text known in English as "The Noble Excellent Gate," corresponding to the Sanskrit Ārya-Sumukha-nāma-dhāraṇī and the Tibetan Phakpa Go Zangpo Zhejawa'i Zung. As a dhāraṇī scripture, it belongs to the broader class of mantra and incantation literature within the Kangyur, presenting a sacred formula along with its narrative and ritual context. The text opens in the classical sūtra style, with the Buddha dwelling at the great stūpa at the site of awakening, surrounded by a vast assembly of monks, great śrāvakas such as Śāradvatīputra and Mahāmaudgalyāyana, and an array of bodhisattvas including Vajrapāṇi, Vajradhvaja, Vajragarbha, and Maitreya. This setting among the bodhisattvas of the Fortunate Aeon suggests the text's Mahāyāna orientation, combining the authority of a buddha-word teaching with the protective and liberative power characteristic of the dhāraṇī genre.

Homage to all buddhas and bodhisattvas.

Thus did I hear. At one time the Blessed One was dwelling at the great stūpa of the essence of awakening, beneath the tree of awakening, together with a great Saṅgha of twelve hundred and fifty monks — namely the venerable Śāradvatīputra, the venerable Mahāmaudgalyāyana, and all the other great śrāvakas — and also together with a great Saṅgha of bodhisattvas: the bodhisattva, the great being Vajrapāṇi; the bodhisattva, the great being Vajradhvaja; the bodhisattva, the great being Vajragarbha; the bodhisattva, the great being Maitreya; and all the other bodhisattvas, great beings, of the Fortunate Aeon.

Then the bodhisattva, the great being Vajrapāṇi, addressed the Blessed One with these words: "Blessed One, may the Tathāgata, the arhat, the perfectly and completely awakened buddha, please teach the exposition of the Dharma called 'The Excellent Gate' — that which will bring light to sentient beings, that will guard them against opponents and protect them so that no harm is done to them by all enemies, that will protect them from being frightened by the lords of the gods, the lords of the nāgas, the lords of the yakṣas, the lords of the rākṣasas, and the lords of the spirits, that will prevent harm from evil, weapons, poison, and sorcery, whether one is going forth or returning, lying down or drifting off to sleep, whether one is confused or utterly confused — that will protect one in all circumstances. For the sake of these ends, may you please teach the bases of such a secret mantra. Just as I constantly seek the service of the Tathāgata, constantly seeking to be guarded and fully protected, may you likewise teach it for the benefit of all sentient beings, and so that sons and daughters of good family who belong to the vehicle of the bodhisattva may be guarded and fully sustained against conflict, slander, quarrel, illness, and untimely death. Thus I beseech the Tathāgata."

Then the Blessed One, having understood the petition made by the bodhisattva, the great being Vajrapāṇi, declared to him thus: "Excellent, excellent, Vajrapāṇi! It is well that you beseech the Tathāgata for the sake of all sentient beings. Translator's notes

The names Vajradhvaja and Vajragarbha are reconstructed from the Tibetan: རྡོ་རྗེའི་རྒྱལ་མཚན means "Vajra-Banner" (Vajradhvaja) and རྡོ་རྗེའི་སྙིང་པོ means "Vajra-Essence" (Vajragarbha). These are stock bodhisattva names in the Fortunate Aeon list.

The mantra syllables are rendered in approximate phonetic transliteration consistent with their Tibetan orthography. The Sanskrit underlying these dhāraṇī phrases is formulaic and not always fully recoverable; no attempt has been made to normalize or correct them beyond faithful phonological rendering.

The phrase rendered "crown empowerment" (spyi bo nas dbang bskur) literally means "empowerment from the crown of the head," indicating an abhiṣeka-style bestowal transmitted through the head. The phrase translated as "ūrṇā hair-mark between the brows" renders the Tibetan སྨིན་མཚམས་ཀྱི་མཛོད་སྤུ, literally "the treasury-hair at the brow-junction," which corresponds to the Sanskrit ūrṇā, one of the thirty-two major marks of a great being. This is standard.

The phrase "secret place" for གསང་བའི་གནས་ is here a doctrinal designation for this particular class or category of dhāraṇī, not a topographical location; some editions treat it as a proper title of the collection. The ambiguity is real but does not substantially affect the meaning for a listener. The term མ་མོ་འཕྲོག་མ is a complex deity name. མ་མོ refers to a class of wrathful female spirits, and འཕྲོག་མ means "the Seizing One" or "the Snatcher." Rendered here as "the great mother of elemental spirits, Māmakī the Seizer" to convey both the class of being and the epithet, though the precise identity of this figure is uncertain.

The word རྩེན before the five great yakṣa commanders is obscure; it may be a proper name, a title, or a textual anomaly. It is left in transliteration pending further clarification. The phrase rendered "young commander" translates the Tibetan "sde dpon gzhon nu," literally "youthful captain of the host." The identity of this figure is not specified in the passage; it may refer to Kārttikeya (Kumāra), the young divine general, which would fit the context of a list of divine protectors, but the text does not make this explicit.

The mantra phrase "sarva bhūta pratiṣedhanaṃ karomi" means roughly "I perform the warding off of all harmful spirits," and appears twice with different preceding syllables; the rendering is kept consistent with both occurrences.

The passage ends mid-mantra, as the source text breaks off at "jvale," indicating this is a continuing passage. The name rendered "Himavat" for the yakṣa commander is a reconstruction from the Tibetan "Kha ba can," meaning "one who has snow," the standard Tibetan equivalent of the Sanskrit Himavat. The name rendered "Sukhāvāsagiri" for the great yakṣa commander is a reconstruction from the Tibetan "bde ba'i ri gnas," meaning "dwelling on the mountain of bliss." The great goddess name "Vajraluguryud" is a transliteration of the Tibetan "rdo rje lu gu rgyud," which may represent a Sanskrit original now difficult to recover with certainty; it may mean something like "Vajra Chain" or "Vajra Garland." The bodhisattva "Vajrasena" renders "rdo rje'i sde," literally "vajra host" or "vajra army." The closing colophon is cut off mid-sentence; the text ends with "the great editor" and the name of the Tibetan editor-translator (lo tsā ba) has not been supplied in the input. This is a standard Tibetan canonical colophon formula and is simply incomplete here. The terms "lord of the garuḍas" and "lord of the kiṃnaras" render ནམ་མཁའ་ལྡིང་གི་དབང་པོ and མིའམ་ཅིའི་དབང་པོ respectively; kiṃnaras are semi-divine musical beings of ambiguous form.
