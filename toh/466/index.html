About this text

The Net of Magical Illusion, known in Sanskrit as the Māyājālamahātantrarāja and in Tibetan as Gyutrul Drawa (rGyud kyi rgyal po chen po sgyu 'phrul dra ba), is a major Mahāyoga tantra belonging to the Nyingma class of the Vajrayāna tradition. It takes the form of a discourse delivered by the Blessed One in a transcendent jeweled palace and centers on the elaborate maṇḍala of Vairocana and the emanational display of the Tathāgatas, unfolding teachings on the nature of magical illusion as a key to understanding reality and enlightened activity. As a "great king of tantras," it holds a position of particular doctrinal importance within the tantric literature associated with the Mahāyoga vehicle. This text is Toh 466 in the Kangyur, classified among the tantric scriptures of the collection.

Translation

In the language of India: Māyājālamahātantrarāja nāma. In the language of Tibet: The Great King of Tantras called the Net of Magical Illusion.

Homage to the glorious Vajrasattva.

Thus did I hear at one time. The Blessed One was dwelling in a great palace of precious jewels, multi-storied, arranged upon the pure ground — adorned with vajra pillars, exceedingly beautiful, resplendent with the arrangement of precious gems, with vajra bells ringing, adorned with magnificent offerings born from the mind, arrayed with vajra wheels, and filled with the purposeful activity performed by those bearing the various emanation forms of the Tathāgatas. Activity having been set in motion arising from the very heart of Vairocana, through the gateway of the four faces there appeared many and various presences: vajras, great precious jewels, Amitābha, the assembly of great bodhisattvas, and the goddess-queens of the knowledge-mantras. He was encircled by an retinue of many wrathful kings, male servants, female servants, male messengers, female messengers, male attendants, and female attendants, all facing forward toward him. Suffused with the bliss of the primordial wisdom of meditative absorption, undeparted from that state, gratified by great compassion, he strove earnestly to liberate all sentient beings. Amid the multitude of gods, nāgas, yakṣas, gandharvas, asuras, garuḍas, kinnaras, great serpents, humans, and non-humans — there was seated the Blessed One, the great Vairocana, the Vajra Tathāgata.

Then at that moment, the Blessed One gazed upon Vajrapāṇi, the great bodhisattva, who was seated in the midst of the courtyard of the assembly, and smiled. The instant he smiled, Vajrapāṇi, the great bodhisattva, draped his upper robe over one shoulder, looked up with an expression of respectful attentiveness, took his vajra in hand, pressed the right knee to the ground, joined his palms together, paid homage to the Blessed One, and spoke:

"Blessed Ones who are buddhas do not smile without intention. For what reason, O Blessed One, has your gaze fallen thus and a smile arisen? This has caused us — myself and the retinue — to be filled with wonder. For this reason, may the Blessed One speak with the great roar of a lion."

Then, the very moment that Vajrapāṇi, the great bodhisattva, had thus made his request, the Blessed One, entering into meditative absorption upon the samādhi called the Net of Magical Illusion — the Great King of Tantras, the spreading forth of primordial wisdom — the ultimate means beyond all tantras, the later and the most final, for attaining the bliss of vajra-wisdom, the method of pleasing through creative display, the source of all suchness, the method of complete victory through the posture and stance, liberation through the vajra of great bliss through wisdom and method, joyfully spreading through playful display and the spreading of the vajra — having thus entered that state, spoke:

"I shall now expound the sacred pledges of the tantra of the Net of Magical Illusion, the maṇḍala, the forms of the drawn images, the meditation, the recitation of mantras, the postures and stances, the arrangement of the ritual application, meditative absorption, the descent, the dispersal, the scattering, the drawing in, the binding, the striking, the menacing gesture, the act of humbling, the cutting, the burning, the filling, the creation, the empowerment, the giving, the taking of master and student, and the many modes of engaging with the secret mantras."

"Therefore you too should enter the supreme meditative absorption and listen." Then all those Tathāgatas, all those bodhisattvas, and all others gathered in the retinue began to attend to the discourse on the Dharma-connection.

Now, first and foremost, since attainment (siddhi) follows in the wake of the master, if one is without a master, attainment will not be obtained. Therefore the characteristics of the master shall be explained.

O Vajra of Wisdom-Essence, listen!

Steady and tamed, possessed of discernment,
Patient, upright, free of guile and deception,
Skilled in the practice of the secret-mantra tantra,
One should perform the work of drawing the maṇḍala.

Fully knowing that very suchness in its ten aspects,
Granting fearlessness to every sentient being,
Ever joyfully devoted to the Great Vehicle —
Such a one is declared to be a master.

Fully versed in the treatises of outsiders,
Walking the path of the śrāvakas,
Gathering that very secret,
Yet not knowing the Great Vehicle —
Having no compassion,
Lacking faith and diligence,
Ever conducting oneself badly,
And disparaging the treatises —
One who is not endowed with the characteristics taught above
is not to be regarded as a master.

One who is endowed with those characteristics is to be held as a master. By such a one the maṇḍala is to be drawn, by such a one alone the tantras are to be taught, and concerning the secret mantras taught by such a one it is certain they will be accomplished — of this there is no doubt.

Delighting in virtue and in vows,
Ever reverencing the lama,
Continually making offering to the gods —
One possessed of such qualities is a student.

As for what kind of person the master is to be regarded: the lama is equal to all the buddhas; he is perpetually the Vajradhara himself, the Tathāgata, Jewel-Crown; he is the great ocean of wisdom, like a wish-fulfilling gem and precious jewel. Therefore grasp the master's qualities — never his faults.

By grasping his qualities one gains attainment; by dwelling on his faults one's attainment diminishes. Even his shadow should not be trodden upon — it is taught to be like demolishing a stūpa. One will be caused to abandon whatever is sacred among movable and immovable secrets. Those who disparage the master should not be seen even in a dream. One who disparages the master is seized by obstructors and evil misleaders; one who commits such vile and terrible deeds should always be abandoned by the wise. It is explicitly taught that one who disparages the master will come to dwell in the hell called Avīci, of utter and extreme anguish.

Therefore let everyone exert themselves with utmost effort, and let sentient beings possessed of faith offer worship to the master who ripens immeasurable fruit and who is the cause of desiring the fruit of buddhahood.

This is the first chapter of the great king of tantras, the Net of Magical Illusion, the chapter on the graduated entry.

Then the Blessed One, the great Tathāgata of the vajra of Vairocana, gazed upon the maṇḍala of the fully assembled retinue and entered into the meditative absorption called "Vajra of Supreme Secret Blessing, Outpouring of the Wisdom of the Great Net of Magical Illusion." Having thus entered that absorption, he declared to Vajradhara:

"Vajrapāṇi, if one confers empowerment in this maṇḍala — the great king of the tantra Net of Magical Illusion, the great king of maṇḍalas, the innermost essence beyond all other maṇḍalas, the Blazing Essence of Great Wisdom, known as 'Vajra Veneration' — one will have conferred empowerment in all maṇḍalas. Therefore, Vajrapāṇi, those sentient beings in whom one sees the colored pigments of karma, they too, having paid homage to all Tathāgatas,

will have made offerings — know that those who have thus properly entered are counted among the buddhas in the buddha-fields of the Tathāgatas. From the very outset they are to be fully received.

The master sits in the divine posture. With the right hand drawn in taut, he brandishes the vajra, and with the left hand he rings the bell, circumambulating in the vajra-step. Adorned with all ornaments and filled with wrathful pride, he recites the syllable HŪṂ; and encircled by the resplendent majesty of the maṇḍala, he meditates in the form of a wrathful deity. He then loudly proclaims these words to the hostile gods, asuras, and the holders of secrets assembled there:

"I, the master, named such-and-such, in order to bring a certain great disciple to the fulfillment of perfect awakening, and in order that all sentient beings may attain the unsurpassed primordial wisdom, shall here in this place draw the maṇḍala-king named such-and-such. Let all of you who are assembled here — gods and asuras, yakṣas and rākṣasas, hungry ghosts and flesh-eaters, the forgetfulness-causing spirits, elementals, ḍākinīs, oppressive spirits, garuḍas, kinnara, vidyādhara adepts, old men and old women together with their servants and retinues — depart! Hearing the command of Vajradhara, depart at once! Those who do not depart: Vajrapāṇi — whose face blazes with extreme fury, whose name is HŪṂ — with the blazing, all-consuming wisdom-vajra shall split their heads into a hundred pieces." Having said this three times, while remaining in that same posture, he consecrates the ground so that it becomes of the intrinsic nature of the vajra.

Then, with the right leg extended, and the left leg extended, the posture of sitting on a bench, the rounded sitting posture, the harmonious foot-stance, the tortoise foot-stance, the vajra-pedestal, the manner of the vajra-clasp, the one-legged stance, the lion posture, the lion's couching posture, the bee-in-flight posture, the vajra-chain, the vajra-brandishing, the vajra-stance, the vajra-hooking, the vajra-dance, the vajra-terrifying posture, the vajra-zombie posture, the vajra-hammer, the vajra-consecration, the vajra-bracelet, the magical-illusion posture of a woman, the vajra-net, the lifting of the great powerful one, the vajra-fang, the vajra-laughter, the vajra-sound, the sword method, the vajra method, the wheel method, the pestle method, the banner method, the lasso method, the lotus method, the spear method, the fearlessness method, the iron-hook method, the fruit method, the short-spear method, the bow method, the arrow-holding method, the arrow-shooting method, the whirling javelin method, the method of supreme bestowal, the lance method, the single-pointed weapon method, the prostration method, the vajra sitting posture, the meditative absorption sitting posture, the vajra cross-legged posture, the lotus sitting posture, the hero's cross-legged posture, the hero's stance, the excellent posture, the squatting posture, the playful posture, the bowing forward, the leaning back, the boundless posture, the victorious posture, the thunder-cloud posture, the broad-bellied posture, the binding posture in which one dwells pierced by the victor's peg, the pegging posture, the net, the encircling fence, and the binding of the directions —

One should meditate on the ground as blazing with the intrinsic nature of the vajra, luminous and compacted, and settle it definitively. Then, stating one's own name, one should recite the syllable Hūṃ — for when the sound of that secret mantra is uttered, one becomes supreme above all maṇḍalas.

Next, one should invoke the goddess of the earth as a witness, reciting three times:

Goddess, you are witness
to the conduct, the distinctions,
the grounds, and the perfections
of all the protecting buddhas.
In order to obtain all attainments,
I shall inquire concerning the maṇḍala.

Then one should invoke all buddhas and bodhisattvas as witnesses, reciting:

Buddhas, who dwell in loving concern,
turn your intention toward me.
Come together with your heirs
and take full possession of this ground.

Next, at the center of the maṇḍala one should make a Dharma maṇḍala, and all around its borders, stating the mark, one should make dots. Having thus invited and delighted the guests with offerings, one departs and offers the directional torma cakes.

Then one should invoke the sentient beings as witnesses. Having cleansed the crown of the head with scented water, one spreads the hands upon the heart and makes a fist with five tips, binds the protection cord with the right hand, and from the palm offers the wooden rod and kuśa grass.

Then, being seated, one should go for refuge to the Three, confess all wrongdoing, rejoice in merit, offer oneself, and arouse the awakening mind. The going for refuge is as follows, recited three times:

I go for refuge to the Three Jewels.
I confess all wrongdoing.
I rejoice in the virtue of beings.
I hold in mind the awakening of a buddha.

Then one should teach whatever Dharma one can and whatever is desired. Thereafter, as explained before, since the guests will be pleased, one performs a beautiful offering ceremony, and once again requests them to depart.

Then one enters inside and, on the ground of the maṇḍala, lies on the right side and examines whether the dreams are auspicious or inauspicious. The following day one draws the maṇḍala.

From colors and lines, manifest
the essences of the five buddhas.
This is pure dharmadhātu,
the liberation of sentient beings.

The abode of all Tathāgatas,
Vairocana, sovereign of victors —
free of every fault,
begin the auspicious painting.

First the master himself shall do it, then the students throughout. The first night is the taking possession of the ground, the second the preparation and abiding, the third the entering. With a cord of five-colored thread one should draw the sacred maṇḍala, measuring either a thousand cubits or five hundred, or else five cubits, two-and-a-half cubits, or down to one cubit.

It is square with four gates, adorned with four stairways (toraṇas). In the center one draws a fine wheel. At the center one places Vairocana. In the eastern direction, Akṣobhya; in the southern direction, Ratnasambhava; in the western direction, Amitābha; in the northern direction, Amoghasiddhi. In the corners of the wheel, draw four goddesses who bestow attainments. In the direction of the lord of empowerment, Locanā; in the direction of fire, Māmakī; in the direction free of deception, the White-Robed One; in the direction of wind, the goddess Tārā.

Proceeding outward by the force of the vajra, in the second ring: in the east, Vajrasattva-mā; in the south, Vajraratna-mā; in the west, Vajradharma-mā; in the north, Vajrakarma-mā. In the corners: the Impeller, the Precious Liberatrix, the Frowning One, and Vajrachain.

Then, rising up gracefully,

Proceeding outward by the force of the vajra, in the third ring: in the eastern direction, draw the bodhisattvas Maitreya, Mañjuśrī, the Elephant of Incense, and Jñānaketu. In the southern direction, draw Surakṣita, Sāgaramati, Akṣayamati, and Pratibhānakūṭa. In the western direction, draw Mahāvikrāmin, the Abandoner of All Lower Realms, the Intelligence that Decisively Destroys All the Darkness of Sorrow, and Jalinīprabha. In the northern direction, draw Candraprabha, Amitābha, Ākāśagarbha, and Sarvanīvaraṇaviṣkambhin.

At the eastern gate: Yamāntaka. At the southern gate: Prajñāntaka. At the western gate: Padmāntaka. At the northern gate: Vighnāntaka. In the cardinal positions: Acala, Takkirāja, Nīladaṇḍa, and Mahābala. In the corners, draw vajras. Draw vajras also on the inner side of the gates. The first wheel is drawn with white pigment. The second is drawn with three pigments: white, yellow, and blue. The third is to be drawn with five colors. Such, then, is the great maṇḍala.

Next, for the purpose of conferring empowerment, make a second maṇḍala that is square and has four gates. In the center, draw a lotus with eight petals and a heart. On the second cloth, in the east place a vajra, in the south a jewel, in the west a lotus, and in the north a sword. Thus is the maṇḍala to be drawn.

Then the master shall thoroughly wash his body, anoint it with incense, bind upon his head a well-strung flower garland, and bind his neck with silk. Now the ritual vases are to be praised:

The vase whose neck is bound with flowers,
filled with the five precious medicinal substances
and with perfumed water —
let this vase be praised aloud.

The four victorious vases belonging to the mistresses who are sovereigns of the maṇḍala, the four vases of all the gods, and the vases in the directions of the gates — let these all be recited over by the one who performs all the rites. All sentient beings are to be purified with the fumigation offering. Alternatively, this may be done with bundles of kuśa grass.

Then, having praised the vases aloud, present the divine food-offering and the ritual cake, and offer flowers, incense, fragrance, garlands, the water offering, the foot-washing water, and so forth. Having presented these, place the vases in the corners. The victorious vase is to be invited into the center.

The Tathāgata and the other deities of the maṇḍala are to be invited, made vivid, introduced, sealed, and brought under control. Having bound all directions with kīla-pegs, fence, and net, and having established the boundaries, dedicate and offer again the water offering, the mouth-refreshing water, the seat, flowers, and incense; then display to each deity its own mudrā in turn.

Offer also praises, vajra songs, the sound of ringing bells, cymbals, flutes, and the variety of stringed instruments. The other varieties of music are also to be offered in praise. Having praised, beg their forbearance and make supplication for the attainment of all siddhis.

Then the master himself enters, and after him introduces the student. Then, for all sentient beings, protect and perform the rite with this heart-essence and the mudrā at the five places, performing the rite at the five places:

Hūṃ oṃ jina-jik,
āḥ ārolik,
hūṃ vajra-dhṛk,
svā ratna-dhṛk,
hā prajñā-dhṛk.

Then introduce with this verse:

Into this palace, this maṇḍala heart,
of all the Tathāgatas —
the one who brings attainment of vajra-wisdom —
I now introduce my student.

Then, with the mind of Vajrasattva, scatter flowers: Pratīccha vajra hoḥ!

With this verse the face covering is removed:

Today, by the force of faith,
behold the maṇḍala here before you.
You are born into the family of the buddha-lineage,
blessed by mantra and mudrā.
All siddhis are brought to perfection,
and the supreme samaya is accomplished.
The supreme sport of vajra and lotus —
know this to be the very nature of mantra.
This is its heart-essence:
Paśya vajra!

Whichever deity's flower falls upon — that is that one's deity.

Then the empowerment is to be conferred. Adorned with perfumes, flower garlands, robes, and ornaments, with a crown placed on top, and covered with parasols, victory banners, pennants, divine and brahmic threads, anklets, girdles, and auspicious implements — thus the master confers empowerment upon the student who has been purified of misdeeds.

First with the victorious vase, then with the four goddesses, and then with the fourth of all the deities, with auspicious verses and melodious song, and with the ringing of the vajra-bell — all the protectors, directly and visibly present, hold the vase filled as full as a sesame pod, and with it confer the empowerment upon that very one. One should understand this to be the maṇḍala itself.

Having thus contemplated it, the defining marks of the Dharma are to be demonstrated: with a nail, a mirror, a wheel, a conch, a vajra, a book, pure gold, silver, and the like — ignorance, clouded vision, and so forth are to be cleared away to whatever extent is possible. Even if a nail and the rest are not available, the forms are to be shown by mudrā.

Then the samaya is to be taught:

From today forward, you, my son,
even should it cost you your life or your body,
must never hold the sacred Dharma,
the awakening mind, or the master in contempt.
Honor your vajra-siblings,
show loving-kindness toward those who hate,
and be free of attachment —
such is the conduct never to be abandoned.
If you act contrary to this,
the buddhas will cast you into hell.

Then the master is to accept the offering. Land, a beautiful young wife, precious gold and blazing jewels, pearls, coral, and the like — and even one's own body — are to be offered. The devoted student should do precisely this for the master. The master is to be pleased. Pleasing the master is the siddhi itself — not otherwise — so the Sage has declared.

Then the student is raised up, and in the presence of all the buddhas an offering is to be made, and the fire-offering rite is to be performed. Then, having performed the rite according to the observed ritual: through fire-offering, recitation, and making offerings, and likewise through the stages of meditation, by means of the stages of pacification and enrichment, all rites are to be brought to full completion.

Then all the Victors of the three times are requested to depart. The coloring powders are carefully gathered up, cast into a great river, and the site smeared with cow-dung. All the implements of the maṇḍala are to be collected by the master himself. The student must not use even the slightest portion — to do so would be a breach of samaya. The master may use whatever is appropriate, as he himself wishes.

This is the second chapter, the chapter on the stages of the maṇḍala, from the great King of Tantras, the Māyājāla.

Then the Blessed One, Vairocana the Tathāgata, entered into the meditative absorption called "The Vajra That Enters the Wheel of the Secret Mantra." Having entered that absorption, he declared to Vajrapāṇi:

Vajrapāṇi, hear these syllables, the heart-essence of secret mantra.

Vajrapāṇi requested: Blessed One, for the benefit of all sentient beings, may the Blessed One explain. May the Sugata explain.

Oṃ — this is Vairocana.
Oṃ Hūṃ — this is Akṣobhya.
Oṃ Trāṃ — this is Ratnasambhava.
Oṃ Hrīḥ — this is Amitābha.
Oṃ Khaṃ — this is Amoghasiddhi.

Oṃ Kiri Kiri Māmakī Hūṃ — this is Māmakī.

Oṃ Tāre Tuttāre Ture Svāhā — this is Tārā.
Oṃ Sattva Vajrī Hūṃ — this is Vajrasattvā.
Oṃ Ratna Vajrī Hūṃ — this is Vajraratnā.
Oṃ Dharma Vajrī Hūṃ — this is Vajradharmā.
Oṃ Karma Vajrī Hūṃ — this is Vajrakarmā.

These are the mantra syllables to be set in the maṇḍala. In reliance upon these, through the distinction of meditative absorption, the Blessed One declared many tens of millions of mantras from which definite liberation arises. All the Tathāgatas and the rest are invited by means of the hook mudrā. The bodhisattvas are sealed by the vajra mudrā. The vidyā goddesses are sealed by the mudrā of the mothers of their respective families. The wrathful ones are sealed by the face mudrā. The attendants are sealed by the lasso mudrā. The messengers are sealed by the hook mudrā. All of these and so forth constitute the practice of invitation.

This is the third chapter, the chapter on the sequential explanation of secret mantra, from the great King of Tantras, the Māyājāla.

Then the Blessed One, the great Vairocana the Vajra Tathāgata, entered into the meditative absorption called "The Vajra of Magical Emanation Through the Power of Great Wisdom," and declared to Vajrapāṇi:

Vajrapāṇi, hear this — the supreme secret, unsurpassed meditative absorption of all the assemblies of secret mantra — that which, when mantra-holders attend to it for the purpose of accomplishing images on cloth or on plaster and so forth, brings their wisdom to completion, and through that completion of wisdom they may with ease attain buddhahood itself; as for other attainments, there is no need even to mention them.

Accomplished through the wisdom of Oṃ —

Vairocana, the great king,
wisdom and gnosis fully expanded,
worshipped as supreme by all the buddhas,
wearing the crown with topknot, peaceful in aspect,
adorned with three faces,
golden in color, with six hands —
the first bearing the mudrā of meditative concentration,
holding a rosary, adorned with an arrow,
the left hand grasping the breast of a goddess,
holding a wheel and a bow.
Gods tremble and realms overflow;
amidst a halo of blazing light,
clouds of all the buddhas stream forth,
issuing out and embracing perfectly.
Meditate upon one whose domain is that very suchness,
and perform the inner recitation of the secret mantra.

This is the meditative absorption called "The Wisdom That Brings to Completion the Power of All Meditative Absorptions."

Through the path of the syllable Oṃ,
contemplate the great light of the lord of the world,
the god wearing the crown with topknot,
his diadem adorned with omniscience,
ornamented with all adornments,
his form like the moon in its glory,
blazing with three faces and three eyes,
with six hands and fourfold posture,
two hands in the lotus-holding gesture,
the right holding a rosary and a vajra,
the left grasping the breast,
on the third hand pouring three pills from above,
causing all forms to radiate out,
encircled on all sides with streams of rays of light,
gazing upon the obstacles that collapse and are thrown into confusion
when weapons fly from his hands.

Meditating upon the path through such words as these,
buddhahood is attained without long delay.

This is the meditative absorption called "The Dharma King, Supreme and Vast in All Aspects, Source of All."

On the moon-disc, upon the syllable Hūṃ,
meditate upon the vajra of mind,
of color like fine indigo,
lavishly covered with all adornments,
Akṣobhya residing in the crown,
completely adorned with precious jewels,
with three faces and three eyes,
slightly smiling, graceful in bearing,
ornamented with eight hands —
the first shaking a vajra,
the second raising a sword,
the third holding a fine iron hook,
and the fourth also shaking an arrow.
Likewise the left hand holding a bell,
the second grasping the breast,
the third a lasso,
and the fourth holding a divine bow.
Seated at ease in a posture of play,
blazing likewise in a cloud of buddhas.

Whoever meditates continuously on one like this,
their vajra mind becomes firm,
and without long delay they attain awakening,
passing beyond aging and death.

This is the meditative absorption called "The Vajra Force, King of All Meditative Absorptions, That Perfects the Immeasurable Power of Sentient Beings."

Accomplished from the wisdom of Trāṃ,
the very essence of the arising of jewels,
with three faces and three eyes,
adorned with a crown and topknot,
of the red color of great bliss,
seated in the meditative absorption mudrā,
in the bodhisattva cross-legged posture —
the right hand holding a vajra,
the left entirely holding a jewel.
From rays of golden light radiating outward,
as a great cloud of the arising of jewels,
or as other bodhisattvas
cause to expand through a rain of jewels.

This is the meditative absorption called "The Wisdom Vajra of Directly Attaining Jewels."

Accomplished from the wisdom of Khaṃ,
contemplate the vajra of meaningful fulfillment,
well-seated in the meditative absorption mudrā,
blazing like the light of emerald,
the right hand holding a sword,
the left an iron hook.
The cloud of buddhas streams forth,
and is gathered in again to accomplish perfectly.
Blazing with great rays of light,
like the radiance of an ocean of wisdom.

Whoever continually cultivates the mind in this way
becomes the mighty sage Ākāśagarbha.
Abandoning all conditioning factors,
they become equal to space itself.

This is the meditative absorption called "The Wisdom Vajra That Illuminates Space."

Accomplished from the wisdom of La,
adorned by all the Victors,
the goddess has three eyes and eight hands,
her three faces the color of gold.
Beautifully attired in graceful dress,
she is the Lady of Desire, smiling slightly.
The first pair of hands bestows the supreme gift,
the second likewise bestows fearlessness.
In the third right hand is a vajra,
and in the fourth she levels an arrow.
The left hands bear the threatening gesture with a lasso,
in the second a jeweled sheaf of grain,
in the third a rosary,
and the fourth is beautifully adorned with a bow.
Wreathed in rays of red light,
the gods are overcome with awe and rapture,
and press their palms together in reverence.
From the cloud of buddhas streaming forth,
the Wisdom Vajra Eye Goddess (Skt. Prajñācakṣus) is inconceivable.
Adorned with the fruits and branches of awakening,
meditate on a goddess such as this.

This is the meditative absorption called "The Vajra Method of Illumination by the Eye of Wisdom."

Accomplished from the wisdom of Ma,
Māmakī, she who is of the vajra lineage,
the goddess has three faces and six hands.
Her expression is laughing, yet tinged with wrath.
Her complexion resembles the hue of a blue lotus,
blazing intensely with red light.
The first right hand bestows fearlessness,
the second holds a vajra firmly clasped,
the third levels an arrow.
The left hands bear the threatening gesture of the pointing finger,
the second holds a lasso beautifully,
and the third grips a bow.
The Vajra Lady bears the three bodies,
characterized by an essentially immaterial nature.
The cloud of buddhas streams forth.
Meditate in this way, cultivating such a form.

This is the meditative absorption called "The Profound Wisdom Vajra Arising through the Power of Magical Emanation."

Accomplished from the wisdom of Pa,
a Mother whose complexion resembles the moon,
her name is known as Śvetavāsinī — the White-Robed One.
Adorned fully with three eyes,
her third face is joyful.
Six hands: the first bestows fearlessness and the gaze of compassion,
in the second is a vajra,
the third is adorned with an arrow.
The left hand holds a lotus,
in the second a rosary of precious jewels,
and the third bears a bow.
She is beautifully seated in a posture of ease and play,
causing the forms of the buddhas to stream forth.
Meditate upon her expanding like a cloud of lotuses.
If one desires the supreme awakening,
meditate upon the Jewel Body.

This is the meditative absorption called "The Wisdom Vajra That Draws Forth the Victor of the Lotus Form."

Accomplished from the wisdom of Tā,
the goddess Tārā, endowed with miraculous power,
abides in graceful and charming attire.
With three faces and three eyes,
six hands, seated in the bodhisattva posture,
she smiles with the radiance of emerald light.
The first right hand bestows fearlessness,
in the second she holds a vajra,
and in the third she levels an arrow.
The left hands bear the threatening gesture of the pointing finger,
in the second is a blue lotus (utpala),
and the third grips a bow.
Blazing with red rays of light,
expanding through the streaming forth of buddhas,
she who brings joy — the mother of all sentient beings —
cultivate her continually without interruption.

This is the meditative absorption called "The Wisdom Vajra That Brings All Sentient Beings Under Sway and Fills Them with Joy."

The names of the four female mind-consorts proclaimed by the supreme being, the Victor, are these:
Mind Consort, Jewel Lady,
Dharma Lady, and Vajra Action Lady.
Through their play they bring delight and satisfaction.
Meditate upon each of them appropriately.
By meditating upon them,
without long delay one attains awakening.

This is the meditative absorption of the four noble females who bring supreme joy to sentient beings.

Accomplished from the syllable Tsuṃ,
the form of the goddess called the Arouser (Skt. Codanī).
Seventy million Victors of the three bodies
praise her with the song of secret mantra.
The ninth syllable holds nine methods,
and is also the abode of nine deities.
Her three faces are the color of autumn lotuses,
adorned with every ornament.

Young, in the bloom of youth, a liberator of all vidyā mantras.
Her first face smiles just slightly,
adorned with three eyes.
Her right face is turned upward in an open expression,
radiant with the petals of a blue lotus,
her fangs like the crescent of a new moon,
her tongue made thick as lightning.
Her left face is red in color,
crushing all arguments with her sharp teeth.
She sits beautifully in the posture of ease.
Her twenty-six hands are adorned with bracelets of various kinds.
Two of those hands, the root hands, bear the root mudrā and adorn the region of the heart.
The two right hands grant fearlessness.
The third holds a sword.
The fourth grants a jewel.
The fifth holds the fruit called bījapūra.
The sixth draws an arrow.
The seventh holds a noose for enemies.
The eighth holds a staff.
The ninth holds a hammer.
The tenth holds an iron hook.
The eleventh holds a vajra.
The twelfth holds a garland of skulls.
The thirteenth holds a rosary of beads.
The second left hand holds the victory banner of the wish-fulfilling jewel.
The third holds a lotus.
The fourth holds a round vase for pouring over the head.
The fifth holds a lasso.
The sixth holds a bow.
The seventh holds a short lance.
The eighth holds a wheel.
The ninth holds a conch shell.
The tenth bears the threatening finger.
The eleventh holds a fine vase.
The twelfth holds a javelin.
The thirteenth holds the Perfection of Wisdom.

Meditate upon her seated on a lotus at the center of a maṇḍala of red rays of light,
wearing over the upper body an upper garment of thin multicolored silk,
adorned on the head with a tiara of many colors.
Various magical displays appear,
and the buddha bodies radiate forth.
The thirty gods, together with transgressors and obstructors, being utterly terrified,
join their palms together in reverence.
Whoever continually meditates upon such a form,
then exerts themselves and recites the secret mantra
and thereby swiftly attains buddhahood —
what need is there even to speak of other siddhis?
One will attain the very essence of meditative absorption
and be fully liberated from all misdeeds.

This is the meditative absorption called Glorious Victor through the Power of Rousing the Wisdom of Boundless Victors.

Accomplished from the wisdom of the syllable Ra,
the form called Jewel Lamp.
Three faces, three eyes,
six hands of golden color.
The measure of one young and in the bloom of youth,
the face smiling just slightly.
The hands are filled with jeweled bracelets.
Two hands join in a jeweled gesture of homage,
churning forth a garland of maṇḍalas of light.
The right hand grants fearlessness,
and the third nocks an arrow.
The left bears the threatening finger toward those who are hostile,
and the third hand also holds a bow.
Red rays of light blaze forth brilliantly.
The foot of skillful means presses down in the posture of ease.
She sends forth and gathers in clouds of buddhas,
a delightful upholder of the Dharma.
Meditate upon such a goddess.

This is the meditative absorption called Illuminating with the Wisdom of the Jewel Cloud of Buddhas.

Accomplished from the wisdom of the syllable Bhrī,
the wrathful-browed liberator from all fears.
Three faces, three eyes,
like the light of an autumn moon.
Six hands, seated upon a lotus,
intent upon the posture of play.
The face smiles with a wrathful expression,
gazing toward a brilliantly blazing summit.
The right hand holds a vajra,
the second is said to hold a sword.
The third nocks an arrow.
The left bears the threatening finger,
the second carries a round vase for pouring,
and the third holds the weapon of a bow.
She sends forth clouds of buddhas
and likewise gathers up others excellently.
Whether lying down, standing, or sitting,
always meditate upon such a goddess.

This is the meditative absorption called Wisdom Vajra that Renders All Fears Without Support.

Accomplished from the wisdom of the syllable Hūṃ,

Its name is Vajra Chain,
the one who produces the binding of all things,
destroying unruly sentient beings.
Three-faced, three-eyed,
six-armed, blue-green in color, of beautiful form,
she smiles a little.
The right hand holds a vajra;
the second holds a vajra chain;
the third nocks an arrow as well.
The left bears the threatening finger;
the second is said to hold a noose;
the third strives to hold a bow.
She sends forth clouds of buddhas.
When one meditates upon the Vajra Chain,
awakening is attained in an instant —
what need is there to speak of other accomplishments?

This is the meditative absorption called Wisdom Vajra of Vajra Fetters, the View.

Accomplished from the wisdom of the syllable Māṃ,

the supreme being born of the Sage's power, Maitreya (Loving-Kindness),
three-faced, three-eyed,
with a lotus beneath him,
seated in the bodhisattva's cross-legged posture,
adorned with four hands.
Two hands bear the gesture of teaching the Dharma.
The second right hand
performs the supreme act of protecting sentient beings.
The left holds the divine substance: the flower of the nāga tree
with a cluster of blossoms at its head.
He sends forth great clouds of buddhas and bodhisattvas just so.

This is the meditative absorption called Wisdom Vajra Adorned with the Power of Loving-Kindness.

Accomplished from the wisdom of the syllable Muṃ,

the youthful one, gentle of speech, path of words,
who has attained the supreme of wisdom and gnosis,
teacher of the profound way —
Mañjuśrī — three-faced, three-eyed, with three top-knots,
adorned with the ornaments of youth,
wearing a fine crown, unperturbed,
covered with various flowers.
Below, upon a great lotus,
he sits in the bodhisattva's cross-legged posture,
playing with grace and skillful means,
his face smiling a little.
The great hero, six-armed:
the first hand bestows fearlessness;
the second holds a sword;
the third nocks an arrow.
Likewise, the left holds wisdom;
the second holds a blue lotus;
the third is a bow.
He is covered with the highest wisdom and gnosis,
carrying the nature of buddhas as a cloud.
Meditate upon him, yearning to send forth buddhas.

This is the meditative absorption called Wisdom Vajra of the Supreme Wisdom and Gnosis of Mañjuśrī, the Illuminator.

Fragrant Elephant, Wisdom Pinnacle,
likewise Surakṣita (the Well-Guarded),
Ocean of Intelligence, Intelligence itself,
Inexhaustible, and Eloquence Amassed —
he who has attained the great power of abandoning all lower rebirths,
the intelligence that dispels all darkness of sorrow,
Suvarṇa (the Good Treasure), Net of Light,
likewise Moonlight and Amitābha,
Ākāśagarbha (Treasury of Space) and Remover of All Obscurations —
all those protector bodhisattvas, thus and so on, in incalculable number,
arise from the seed syllables,
endowed with the union of syllable and mudrā,
three-faced, three-eyed,
playing through various means.
The source of these bodhisattvas:
meditate by reciting the secret mantra.
Those who are colorful, who gather and send forth —
the yogin should always contemplate them.

Accomplished from the wisdom of the syllable Hūṃ,

the glorious one, Mañjuśrī,
famed as the Foe of the Lord of Death (Yamāntaka),
blazing like the fire at the end of an age,
yet resembling the light of a rain cloud —
six-faced and six-armed,
likewise six-footed, like a short man in stature,
of exceedingly great girth,
he is supremely fierce.
His tongue is curled, his teeth are vajra-like,
adorned with ornaments of snakes,
wearing a skirt of tiger-skin,
bearing the stance of sport, he rides a great buffalo.
Below him a lotus is made.
He wears a garland of human skulls, his hair coiled,
frowning, yellowish, emitting a great roar.
Above his head, the Unperturbed One (Akṣobhya) —

he abides in perfect glory within the three bodies,
adorned completely by the splendor of Mañjuśrī.
The first face laughs openly;
the right face has the tongue curled;
the left face presses the lip with the teeth in a wrathful expression.
The right hand holds a sword;
the second holds a vajra;
the third brandishes an arrow.
The left hand bears the threatening gesture with a lasso;
the second grasps a breast;
the third holds a bow drawn taut —
adorned most excellently.
A goddess embraces him from the right;
her second hand holds a blue lotus (utpala);
the left, bright as the moon,
is to hold a white lotus.
Beneath him, gods and hosts of obstructing spirits (bgegs) scatter in terror and prostrate.
With blazing red rays of light,
clouds of buddhas radiate forth and are gathered in.

When yogins have meditated on this and, with focused concentration, recite the secret mantra,
whether sitting, lying, or walking,
having contemplated it well and consumed everything,
even though they enjoy the five objects of desire,
they will not be stained, like the sky.

This is the meditative absorption called "The Vajra-Wisdom that Bewilders all Slayers — the Entering of the Discipline of the Yamāntaka."

Accomplished from the wisdom of Pra,
the wrathful one whom no other can withstand,
three-faced and three-eyed,
adorned with six arms,
golden in color, of middling stature,
with a maṇḍala of red rays of light,
seated on a lotus in the stance of sport,
adorned with ornaments of snakes —
the first face laughs openly,
likewise the right face is turned aside,

the right hand holds a vajra;
the second holds a staff;
the third holds an arrow.

the second grasps a breast;
the third, it is said, holds a bow.
Above his head, the Unperturbed One (Akṣobhya),
in sequence radiating and gathering —
one should meditate, growing familiar with exactly this.
By repeating it, buddhahood itself is attained.

This is the meditative absorption called "The Vajra-Wisdom that no other can withstand."

This is the meditative absorption called "The Vajra-Wisdom of the Supreme Steed."

Accomplished from the wisdom of Hūṃ,
named the Wrathful Destroyer of Obstructing Spirits,
he abides in the midst of fire and bestows accomplishments,
a follower of Vajrapāṇi.
Three-faced and three-eyed,
six-handed, a destroyer of all obstructing spirits —
the first face laughs;

The clouds of buddha-nature radiating forth —

This is the meditative absorption called "The Vajra-Gnosis that Vanquishes All Obstructing Spirits and is Victorious throughout."

Accomplished from the wisdom of Hūṃ,
possessing the form of the Unmoving Child —

three faces, three eyes,
adorned with the ornaments of youth.
The first face is laughing;
the right face is raised upward,
its tongue moist with blood, rolling;
the left face wrathfully presses down with its teeth.
The Hūṃ syllable fills the region of the heart.
Dark indigo in color,
it dwells upon a lotus atop a jeweled mountain,
seated in the posture of royal ease.
The mighty one who vanquishes the water-holders of obstructing spirits,
ablaze with six arms radiating forth —

the right hand holds a sword;

the third brandishes an arrow.

it bears a crown and head-ornament
bound with various flowers,
the left side-lock dark as a bee in color,
adorned throughout with the Unmoving One.
Clouds of buddha-nature radiate
many great miraculous displays.
Whatever yogin always meditates
in this way through meditation —
that one will ascend the stairway
to unsurpassed awakening.

This is the meditative absorption called "The Vajra of Gnosis of the Unmoving One who Acts in Accordance with the Command of all Tathāgatas."

This is the meditative absorption called "The Victorious Vajra of Gnosis that Draws All Hearts."

This is the meditative absorption called "Victorious through the Power of the Gnosis Vajra Free from Confusion."

Accomplished from the gnosis of the syllable Bhruṃ —
the wheel-turning sovereign of the uṣṇīṣa,
possessing the three bodies, three-faced,
of golden color, with beautiful radiance,
adorned throughout with a fathom's reach of light,
abiding in the mudrā of meditative absorption,
seated in vajra posture upon a lotus
atop a jeweled mountain.
The four mothers of the family,
beautiful in body and in every limb,
their faces adorned in charming, graceful manner,
clothed and covered with garments and ornaments,
exerting joyful effort born of longing,
bearing mudrās, precious sheaves of grain,

throughout, a garland of wheels encircles him,

adorned with a garland of rays of light.

Those who seek the essence of awakening
should meditate upon such a wheel-bearing one;
applying themselves and cultivating this yoga,
they will without long delay obtain what they desire.

This is the meditative absorption called "Gnosis Vajra, the Adornment of All Awakening Essences."

Accomplished from the gnosis of the syllable Jaṃ —
a yakṣa possessing the nature of harm and dwelling, with three bodies,
three-faced, with two hands,
bearing the vījapūraka citrus fruit.
Seated upon a lotus throne,
or abiding in the graceful seated posture.
On every surface adorned with all ornaments,
four yakṣa women
cause a rain of jewels to fall.
Above their heads dwell the five buddhas,
and from that excellent crown
further ornaments cluster at the left peak.
Radiating the clouds of the buddhas,
they cause a rain of jewels to descend.
Whoever desires wealth and riches
and meditates upon such a dwelling of harm —
that one's great earth will swiftly,
without long delay, be filled with wealth and riches.
Vajrapāṇi (the Holder of the Vajra) shall always
present the water of ambrosia as an offering.

This is the meditative absorption called "Gnosis Vajra of the Precious Cloud that Bestows the Rain of Giving."

Accomplished from the gnosis of the syllable Māṃ —
radiating like the rays of the sun,
dwelling in a chariot drawn by seven horses,
illumining the blazing maṇḍala,
three-faced, with three eyes,
the six hands appearing in yellow color,
striving to guard the ten directions,
the face smiling in a charming, graceful manner,
wearing ornaments of youth that sway and shimmer,
the crown adorned with the five perfect buddhas,
decorated with topknots and flowers.
The right hands: the first holds a vajra,
the second bears a needle and thread,
the third draws an arrow taut.
The left hands: the first bears a noose with the threatening finger,
the second an aśoka branch,
the third a bow itself.
Radiating many reflected forms,
he binds hostile sentient beings
and makes their eyes and mouths contract.
Those who exert themselves reciting secret mantras,
if they desire the supreme awakening,
should meditate upon the goddess who makes the syllable Hūṃ.
One who meditates upon this
thoroughly brings sentient beings under control

sovereign of the supreme vajra of the three bodies,
pressed their two breasts with his feet.
He will be remembered as the one who vanquishes with wisdom.
Torn apart by the iron hook,
bound by the vajra-lasso,
all the daughters are to be summoned —
he is supreme in the summoning of daughters.

Then the Blessed One, by fully extending great wisdom, entered into meditative absorption in a samādhi called Displaying Joy, and recalled the awakening of the buddhas, enacting the arising of Vajradhara, the supreme of the three bodies.

Dwelling magnificently in the great wheel,
the clouds of the buddhas,
and likewise the clouds of Vajradhara,
pour forth the vajra iron hook and lasso.
The chief born from omniscient wisdom —
if one meditates on oneself as the sacred mantra being,
even the buddhas are summoned;
what need then is there to speak of Vajradhara and the rest?
The female deities, daughters of the Great God (Śiva),
placing their feet upon the wind,
drawn to the heart by the iron hook,
all sentient beings too shall be summoned —
in this there is not the slightest doubt.
Vajra Vairocana
has a great body the color of the moon,
dwelling within the moon maṇḍala.
Contemplating the descent of vajra-nectar (amṛta),
by reciting it ten times,
one will inevitably draw them in.
Arising from the vajra maṇḍala,
through the blazing light-rays
of the great vajra iron hook,
young maidens are certainly summoned.
The wrathful one who stirs the three vajras
dwells beneath the vajra earth.
He burns all the asuras,
and through the passionate attachment of the asura maidens,
knocking them down with a torch,
he draws them with the vajra hook and lasso,
summoning the asura daughters.
When one has obtained the lance, skull-cup, and wheel,
and is fully endowed with hair-rope,
and when the eclipsing planet (Rāhu) has seized the moment — placing it in the mouth,
the king of substances accomplishes it through the vajra iron hook.
The fierce one, together with the wind deity and Viṣṇu,
the goddess, the yakṣa and the yakṣinī,
those who are sent with their full retinue — stricken with fear and terror —
by inscribing their names, one summons them continuously at all times.
The buddhas dwell beautifully in the wheel.
Meditate clearly upon Mañjuśrī as vajra.
The great wrathful one, Yamāntaka,
holds in his hand the vajra iron hook.
Blazing in fury, with radiating flames,
he summons and controls the yakṣa daughters.
Through the practice of mudrā and mantra,
and through the practice of dhyāna and maṇḍala,
the words of summoning are spoken —
if performed otherwise, accomplishment will not arise.
By dwelling always before the feet of the king,
Vajrasattva, the great bodhisattva,
perfects the minds of all,
and urges them again and again.

Then the Blessed One Vairocana the Great entered into meditative absorption called the Wisdom-Vajra of Victorious Gnosis of the Vajra Tathāgata, and drew forth this vajra of transformation, the Great Vajra Queen, from the three vajra bodies.

Oṃ śūlinī svāhā.

Then, as soon as these words were spoken:

The renowned and mighty nāga queens,
unclothed and just as commanded,
recall the lord Vajra Vairocana.
By this mantra, this knowledge-mantra,
all nāgas are summoned.
By the power of vajra concentration
and by the recitation of the secret mantra,
by adorning them with ring ornaments,
the wide-eyed nāga queens are drawn in,
and those nāga queens bound by desire
through the five qualities of sensual pleasure
are brought into service.

Then the Blessed One entered into meditative absorption called the Samaya of the Surge of the Great Ocean of Force of Vast Space, and proclaimed this great knowledge-mantra dhāraṇī of the Great Propelling Mother:

Namaḥ samantabuddhānāṃ samyaksaṃbuddhākoṭīnāṃ. Tadyathā: Oṃ cale cule cuṇḍa svāhā.

Then, as soon as this great knowledge-mantra was proclaimed:

The buddha-fields all trembled greatly.
The bodies of the bodhisattvas
were roused from their meditative absorption.
The māras fell stunned and swooning,
all of them crying out in lamentation.
The daughters of the earth below,
the asura daughters, and the nāga queens,
and the daughters of the hidden lords — all were stirred,
and they recalled the lord, Vajra Vairocana.
By the recitation and
the application of meditation upon this knowledge-mantra,
through recitation alone accomplishment arises,
and having summoned them, one fully enjoys their company.

Then the Blessed One entered into meditative absorption called the Vajra of Victorious Space-Treasury, and drew forth this wrathful frown-vajra of the samaya of Dharma-nectar from the three vajra bodies.

Oṃ bhayanāśani trāsani trāse, trāsaya trāsaya, vairaṭi bhṛkuṭi tiṭe, vaiṭaṭi vaiṭaṭi, śvete śvete, jaṭini svāhā.

Then, as soon as these words were spoken:

The mantra-holders and the gods
were all shaken and seized with fear.
The māras, terrified and thrown into turmoil,
recalled with one-pointed mind
the lord, Vajra Vairocana.
By the shaking of the jewel earrings,
the female mantra-holders and nāga queens,
the secret daughters of the asuras —
all are summoned and brought into service.
By this, all activities are performed,
and the ḍākinīs are struck with fear.

Alternatively, for all these secret mantras,
in a forest empty of people,
the mantrin having recited ten million times,
is said to accomplish all activities.

Whoever denigrates the master,
whoever disparages the Great Vehicle,
whoever mocks the person who is joined to the pure streams of secret mantra practice —
that one will not attain accomplishment.
Such a person is perpetually seized by spirits.
After compelling them, one should keep this secret,
or else one's very body will be destroyed.
But the mantrin who acts rightly in this way
will excellently attain the accomplishment of awakening.

All sentient beings of the ten directions
should meditate with special attention
upon what arises from the gnosis of the three bodies.
As for the enemy, here there is no doubt.

With garments moistened by blood-drinking water,
or with garments dampened by cremation ashes and excrement,
one dresses in such robes,
presses the mark with the foot,
and applying this to the wrathful king
and reciting the secret mantra one hundred and eight times —
even a buddha will certainly be destroyed.

Draping oneself in water-soaked garments,
binding them before the wrathful one,
and pressing down with the left foot —
even a buddha will certainly be destroyed.

Garments smeared with excrement and urine,
foul-smelling and contemptible —

and having donned it, if one recites the secret mantra,
the target will die or wither away at once.

Garments gathered with cremation-ground ashes,
having donned them and roused the wrathful one,
if one recites one thousand times,
even Vajrasattva will be utterly destroyed.

With hair loosened and disheveled,
the face turned away and averted,
and pressing the mark with the foot —
even the element of space will be destroyed.

One should commence the fierce practice
at a place of the Mothers, or a cremation ground,
or an empty house, or a crossroads,
at a single mark or at a single tree.

In the footprint there upon the ground,
pegs made from human bone,
measuring about eight finger-widths in length —
having recited one hundred and eight times,
one drives in such a peg
and buries it at the gate of the enemy's house.

One who is skilled in bestowing the supreme Three Bodies of the Buddha,
whether knowing or unknowing,
within half a month in the interval of the fish,
will be bound or destroyed.

Having obtained a complete and especially fine skull,
one writes the words of the secret mantra upon it,
recites with vajra syllables,
and conceals it at the enemy's gate or in the village —
they will certainly be expelled.

On a palm leaf or another suitable material,
one writes well the secret mantra of the wrathful one,
conceals it at the enemy's house or at the threshold —
they will die or wither away.

Thus declared the Blessed One, the great Vajra Vairocana.

Then the Blessed One entered into the meditative absorption called the Vajra of Wisdom of the Kīla of the Vajra of the Three Bodies of All the Tathāgatas, and from the very vajra body, speech, and mind of this same entity called the Wisdom Body, Speech, and Mind Vajra Kīla of all three realms, these emerged:

Oṃ ghaghra, ghātaya ghātaya, sarvaduṣṭaṃ phaṭ phaṭ, kīlaya kīlaya, sarvapāpaṃ phaṭ phaṭ, hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ vajrakīla vajradhara ājñāphayati, kāyavākcittavajra, kīlaya hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ phaṭ.

Then, as soon as this was uttered:
all the great and renowned lords of vajra
fell into a swoon and were seized with terror,
and remembered Vajra Vairocana.

With a peg of human bone,
or one fashioned from acacia wood,
or a peg made of iron —
even Vajrasattva will be utterly destroyed.

Resting in equipoise as Vajrasattva,
blazing with beautiful light and leaping flames,
touching the extremities with the vajra of the Three Bodies,
one visualizes the image and applies the practice.

The great seal (mudrā) of Vairocana,
or the vajra of desire,
the great seal of Yamāntaka —
one should visualize the vajra kīla
as the very wrathful one of desire itself.

O great and mighty self-existent being,
with the vajra of nectar-method union
and by means of vajra yoga,
one severs hostility and ferocity.

Even the great self-existent buddha,
from the crown down to the very soles of the feet,
one meditates upon completely by means of the vajra kīla,
and through the application of vajra meditative absorption,
even buddhas will certainly be suppressed.

This is the great wave of the kīla's power,
the samaya pledge of the gods above and below.
The great king Vajrasattva,
and the application of the meditative absorption that suppresses all buddhas —
through this, one will swiftly die or wither away.

Then the Blessed One, the great Tathāgata Vajra Vairocana, entered into the meditative absorption called the Vajra of Gathering the Wisdom of the Tathāgata Body, and from the very vajra body, speech, and mind of this same secret mantra of the kīla of the non-dual samaya pledge of the vajra body, these emerged:

Oṃ chinda chinda daha daha, dīptavajracakra hūṃ phaṭ.

By means of reciting alternately,
gather it to the near side of the thumb.
Having pressed with the feet of Vairocana,
one drives it in with the vajra kīla.

By the mere act of driving it in, the great bodhisattva,
born from the wisdom of the Three Bodies —

raise it up with the supreme pledge vow. If done otherwise, it will be destroyed.

Then the Blessed One entered into the meditative absorption called the Vajra of Gathering the Great Wisdom of the Vajra Mind, and from the very vajra body, speech, and mind of this same secret mantra called the kīla, by means of the posture of the great pledge vow, these emerged:

Oṃ vajra rāja hūṃ.

Having bound the five-pointed tip,
meditate on blazing tongues of fire.
Pressed by the feet of the Vajra Mind,
one drives it in with the vajra kīla.

By the mere act of driving it in, the great king,
born from the wisdom of the Three Bodies —
raise it up with the supreme pledge vow.
If done otherwise, it will be destroyed.

Then the Blessed One entered into the meditative absorption called the Vajra of Gathering the Supreme Pledge of Īśvara of the World (Avalokiteśvara), and from the very vajra body, speech, and mind of this same secret mantra of the kīla of the speech pledge posture, these emerged:

Oṃ tri oṃ bhūrbhuvaḥ.

With the vajra fingers clasped together,
with the lotus blazing open,
pressed by the feet of the Vajra of Desire,
drive it in definitively with the vajra kīla.

By the mere act of driving it in, the great king,
born from the wisdom of the Three Bodies —
raise it up with the supreme pledge vow.
If done otherwise, it will be destroyed.

It is bound by body, speech, and mind.
By the mere performance of the excellent rite,
the vajra, touching the expanse of space at its edge,
will press down — of this there is no doubt.

Thus did the Blessed One Vairocana declare these words of the vajra kīla.

Then the supreme buddhas of the Three Bodies, those who aspire to benefit the realm of sentient beings, having found delight and joy, from the vajra expanse (dharmadhātu) proclaimed these words:

How wondrous — the supreme word of the secret!
How wondrous — the secret in which all is gathered!
How wondrous — the peaceful word of Dharma!
Marvellous is the wisdom vajra kīla!
This, with the kīla of all secret mantras,
follows in the wake of the Sugata's word.
These are of all the buddhas,
arising from those very kīlas.

Then the Blessed One entered into the meditative absorption called the Vajra that Blinds All Thieves, and proclaimed this great vidyā mantra (Skt. vidyā), queen of knowledge:

Oṃ māricye devatāye svāhā.

At the very moment this knowledge mantra was proclaimed,
all the renowned buddhas of great power
became delighted and joyful at heart,
and the eyes of their heirs were opened as well.
This is the mother of all actions,
she who binds those who despise and rob.
One who abides in her practice
will not be seen when passing through existence.

Then the great Blessed One, the Vajra Tathāgata Vairocana, entered into the meditative absorption called Victorious through the Power of Great Wisdom that Destroys All Aversion and Liberates All Sentient Beings, and proclaimed this great queen of knowledge mantras that utterly destroys the great thousands:

Oṃ mahāsahasrapramardanā vara siddhi dāmīge maṇi vajraṇi hūṃ phaṭ svāhā.

Then, when this great knowledge mantra was proclaimed,
the buddha realms all trembled greatly,
all the bodhisattvas
and the ranks of the māras were shaken.
The chief, the Vajra Vairocana,
all having bowed according to the word,
by the power and might of the Blessed One's knowledge mantra,
all who do harm are nailed down.
Wherever the great and powerful knowledge mantra —
this subduer of adversaries — is recited,
there shall we not venture,
and there also shall we offer protection.

Then the great Blessed One, the Vajra Tathāgata Vairocana,

entered into the meditative absorption called Vajra Victorious Through the Crushing Power That Overcomes the Demigods by the Supremacy of Śakra (Indra), and proclaimed this great dhāraṇī, the shoulder ornament of the victorious banner's crest:

Oṃ ghagabatī dhājagrakeyūre parasainyavidhvaṃsanakāsvāsainyaparilani uyukāmukhi khakha khāhi khāhi parasainya ma anantabhujeṃ anantamukhe praharā praharā hūṃ phaṭ phaṭ svāhā.

Then, when this great knowledge mantra was proclaimed,
the buddha realms all trembled greatly.
The bodhisattvas were greatly shaken,
and the ranks of the māras were scorched.
The yakṣas and rākṣasas, stripped of their garments,
became terrified and filled with dread,
and remembered the Chief, the Vajra Vairocana.
Scorched by the splendor of the Blessed One's queen of knowledge mantras,
they said: "We pray to abide by our pledges — be our protector, and fully accomplish all our bodies."

Then the great Blessed One, the Vajra Tathāgata Vairocana, entered into the meditative absorption called Vajra Pervading All Through Complete Birth from Every Womb, and proclaimed this great individual-following queen of knowledge mantras:

Oṃ maṇidhari vajraṇi mahāpratisari svāhā.

When this great knowledge mantra was proclaimed,
by the power of the queen of knowledge mantras,
those who harm human beings,
those of great power who steal the womb,
all evil guides and adversaries —
scorched by unbearable tongues of flame,
filled with terror and dread,
they approached Vajra Vairocana,
and touching their heads to his feet, spoke these words:
"We pray to abide by our pledges.
She who constantly keeps the pledge of the knowledge mantra,
who steals no womb whatsoever,
but ever causes the womb to flourish —"

Then the great Blessed One, the Vajra Tathāgata Vairocana, entered into the meditative absorption called Vajra Holding the Stream of Wealth in Full, and proclaimed this great stream of wealth, queen of knowledge mantras:

Oṃ vasudhari mahāvasudhāra vṛṣṭanipātani pasusvāhā.

The very instant this knowledge mantra called Stream of Wealth was proclaimed,
all obstructors and evil guides
became terrified and filled with dread of mind,
and drawing near to Vajra Vairocana,
spoke these sworn words:
"When the name called Stream of Wealth, Blessed One,
is proclaimed by a noble human being,
upon that one a rain of wealth shall fall,
and we shall cause them to be endowed with great riches."

Then the Blessed One entered into the meditative absorption called Vajra Taming All Who Do Harm, and proclaimed this essence mantra of the rākṣasī of blazing lightning:

Are dutre ṇibrakuptaṃ caṇḍi mahābegadhari bidsādhu jitre mahoda nirautrā kapālamāladharaṇe jāṭamukuṭa maṇḍite nānāpraharaṇa dhari hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ phaṭ sarvatathagatāsamaya manusmara kuru kuru hūṃ hūṃ pramadha sarvaśatrūṇāṃ śoṇitavipapracaṇḍe begadhari pacata pacata bekirata sataya hūṃ daha daha sarvaśatrūṃ sarvāduṣṭāna nigṛṇta prasādhaya hūṃ mahābhagavate jvāla jvāla hūṃ phaṭ — and one great blazing one — mevaśamānāya svāhā.

Then, when the great knowledge mantra of the rākṣasī of blazing lightning was proclaimed, the abodes of the māras were scorched, and the hearts of enemies withered. The rākṣasī is drawn with charcoal on a strip of charnel-ground cloth, carefully concealed, burned with an offering of guggul incense, and the mantra recited one hundred and eight times, together with red flowers, red perfume, a torma offering,

a red food offering made for the deity, then buried at a charnel ground. Over that spot, calling the enemy by name, mustard seeds mantricized one hundred and eight times are scattered. At the very moment of burying, all adversaries and enemies shall become bound, oppressed, and stupefied.

Then the Blessed One entered into the meditative absorption called "The Vajra of Accomplishment that Gladdens the Assembly of Yakṣiṇī Revelers at the Feast of the Snatcher," and as the great Vajradhara, he proclaimed this secret mantra of the Snatcher:

oṃ hāritye mahāyakṣiṇīye hara hara sarva yakṣiṇī praveśanī svāhā.

Then, when the great mantra of the joyful snatcher, Hāritī the Snatcher,
was proclaimed by the great yakṣa lord,
all the yakṣiṇīs stirred and moved forward
in order to receive the accomplishment.

The wheel of the wrathful goddess — a yakṣiṇī —
eight-petaled, adorned with the Snatcher at its center,
bearing an iron hook and bearing fruit of the nelu tree,
surrounded by eight yakṣas —
through these may all the yakṣiṇīs be accomplished.

Then the Blessed One entered into the meditative absorption called "The Vajra of Complete Victory over All Great Illnesses," and proclaimed this great knowledge mantra of the leaf-clad forest-dwelling woman who pacifies great pestilence:

oṃ piśāci parṇaśavarī sarva māri praśamanī hūṃ phaṭ.

Then, when the great knowledge mantra was proclaimed,
the buddha realms trembled.
The hearts too of harmful illness-spreaders, rākṣasas,
and those who bear hatred, shrank.
Yamadanda wood, milk-tree, and sesame —
with milk as the smoke-offering — all become pacified.
Filling the sky in all directions
with the queen of knowledge mantras in fullness,
when one contemplates a rainfall of nectar,
harm is immediately stilled.

This is the fifth chapter from the great king of tantras, the Magical Web, the chapter presented in the sequence of the unfolding of mantra wisdom.

liberates completely from all misdeeds —
if one consecrates the body here present

Then all the Tathāgatas and all the bodhisattvas made supplication with a single, harmonious voice to the Blessed One Vairocana the Great, the Tathāgata of the vajra of body, speech, and mind:

O Tathāgata — the precious awakening mind, the stainless and cherished wisdom of all Tathāgatas, the unsurpassed — please expound it.

Then the Blessed One, understanding in his heart the supplication of all the Tathāgatas, declared this to all the bodhisattvas:

Mind is the form of the body,
and the body, too, is the form of mind;
and mind again is expressed through speech.

Then those bodhisattvas, having heard the union of body, speech, and mind of all the Tathāgatas, uttered this solemn declaration:

How wondrous! Samantabhadra,
holder of the vajra of body, speech, and mind —
phenomena are without arising,
without movement and without abiding.
This, free from the stain of conceptuality,
is proclaimed to be what must be generated.

Then the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Akṣobhya, entered meditative absorption in the samādhi called the Vajra of Speechless Non-elaboration, and spoke this awakening mind:

These things have never arisen;
they have no substrates and no nature of reality.
Like space, objectless —
this awakening mind is firm and sure.

Thus spoke the Blessed One, the vajra-Tathāgata Akṣobhya.

Then the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Ratnasambhava, entered meditative absorption in the samādhi called the Objectless Vajra of All Tathāgatas, and spoke this awakening mind:

These things are without substance;
they have relinquished the defining marks of phenomena.
Born excellent from the Dharma of selflessness —
this awakening mind is firm and sure.

Thus spoke the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Ratnaketu.

Then the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Amitābha, entered meditative absorption in the samādhi called the Blazing Vajra-Flame, and spoke this awakening mind:

Things are without arising,
for things have no intrinsic nature;
and so it is said that they attain the rank of space —
this is the thorough proclamation regarding things.

Thus spoke the Blessed One Amitābha.

Then the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Amoghasiddhi, entered meditative absorption in the samādhi called the Gnosis-Vajra, Supreme above all Supremes of all Tathāgatas, and spoke this awakening mind:

Phenomena are luminous by their intrinsic nature,
clear and undefiled from the very beginning.
There is no sentient being and no living soul,
no buddha as such and no awakening as such.
Appearing like a dream or magical illusion —
this awakening mind is firm and sure.

Thus spoke the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Amoghasiddhi.

"That in which all phenomena are without inherent existence, in which there are no marks of either of the two extremes, in which there is no wandering even in the middle, and which is like the sky that has transcended the three times — with skillful means and wisdom both relinquished, how can awakening be realized in that? "The awakening mind, which is the companion of those for whom the aggregates and phenomena have no inherent existence, is to be cultivated through joy and through drinking. Through this, one venerates the supreme three kāyas. Through mind, the body is attained; through mind, the Dharma teachings are given; through mind, awakening is reached. Therefore cultivate it in the manner of suchness.

"Mind is of the nature of illusion, and awakening too is like an illusion. Take as an example a skilled illusionist who recites mantras over a woman, and through habitual application, inserts them into forms beautifully fashioned — then in the mind of one who is attached, the form of a quadruped and the like appears. "Those who do not know phenomena in their ultimate reality, who cling to outer things as substantially real, and who defile this precious mind with the mental fabrications of corrupt conceptual thought — it is said that it is endowed with all things together with the four great elements. Without remainder is free of habitual tendencies, pure like the sky, without stain. Unsurpassed awakening is just like this, as all buddhas have fully explained.

Sentient beings, confused through ignorance, have abandoned all wisdom entirety —

Those who cling to outer objects with attachment, made dull by the web of conceptual thought — these beings have divided phenomena into the two heaps of virtue and non-virtue. Yet all these phenomena are pure by their intrinsic nature, without arising and without abiding. The buddhas, through their skillful means, have taught this so that the understanding of sentient beings might be cultivated.

If virtue itself is abandoned for the sake of the ultimate aim, what need is there to speak of the absence of merit? Consider this example: sentient beings who wish to cross to the far bank of a river full of water gather grass, wood, and similar materials and fashion a raft to cross the river.
