About this text

The Noble Mahāyāna Sūtra Immeasurable Life and Wisdom (Sanskrit: Āryāparimitāyurjñāna nāma mahāyānasūtra; Tibetan: 'Phags pa tshe dang ye shes dpag tu med pa zhes bya ba theg pa chen po'i mdo) is a short Mahāyāna discourse set at Śrāvastī, in Anāthapiṇḍada's park in the Jeta Grove, where the Buddha addresses Mañjuśrī. It concerns the buddha Immeasurable Life and Wisdom, who dwells in a distant world-realm, and it teaches the benefits of hearing, remembering, and propagating his name and the sūtra itself, especially as a means of gaining longevity, merit, and wisdom. Texts of this kind belong to the widely practiced tradition of longevity sūtras and dhāraṇī-related literature. This work is Toh 849 in the Kangyur, the Tibetan canon of translated Buddhist scriptures, and it is classed among the sūtras (mdo).

In the language of India: Ārya Aparimitāyurjñāna nāma mahāyāna sūtra. In the Tibetan language: The Noble Mahāyāna Sūtra called Immeasurable Life and Wisdom.

Homage to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas.

Thus did I hear at one time. The Blessed One was dwelling at Śrāvastī, in the Jeta Grove, in the park of Anāthapiṇḍada, together with a great community of monks numbering one thousand two hundred and fifty, and with a great many bodhisattvas, great beings.

Then the Blessed One spoke to Mañjuśrī, who had become a youth. Mañjuśrī, in the upper direction there is a world-realm called Endowed with Immeasurable Qualities and the like. There dwells the Tathāgata, the arhat, the perfectly complete buddha, Immeasurable Life and Wisdom, the King of Utterly Certain Splendor. He sustains his life, remains to the very end, and teaches the Dharma to sentient beings.

Listen, Mañjuśrī, who have become a youth. The people of this Jambudvīpa have short lives; scarcely any live to the age of one hundred, and even of those, most are shown to die untimely.

Mañjuśrī, any sentient beings who write down this Dharma teaching called The Full Recitation of the Qualities and Praises of the Tathāgata Immeasurable Life, or who have it written, or who even hear its name, or who read it, or who write it in a volume and keep it in their home, or who honor it with flowers, incense, perfumes, and garlands, will, even if their life is exhausted, live to the age of one hundred.

Mañjuśrī, for any sentient beings who hear the one hundred and eight names of the King of Utterly Certain Splendor, Immeasurable Life and Wisdom, their lives will also increase. For any sentient beings whose lives are running out, if they hold the names, their lives will also be extended.

Therefore, Mañjuśrī, sons or daughters of noble family who wish for long life should listen to the one hundred and eight names of the Tathāgata Immeasurable Life, or write them down, or have them written. The benefits and good fortune that come to them are these.

Translator's notes

The dhāraṇī is given in a standard Sanskrit reconstruction from the Tibetan transliteration, which has minor variant spellings between its repetitions. The text as given here breaks off at the third repetition without the usual concluding colophon, so the closing is left as it stands.
