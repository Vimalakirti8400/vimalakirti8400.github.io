About this text

Toh 599 in the Kangyur is a dhāraṇī text known in Sanskrit as the Samantamukhapraveśaraśmivimalośṇīṣaprabhāsasarvatathāgatahṛdayasamayavilokitanāmadhāraṇī, rendered in Tibetan as "The Dhāraṇī Entitled Gazing upon the Heart-Essence and Sacred Pledge of All the Tathāgatas by means of the Radiance of the Uṣṇīṣa's Stainless Light that Enters through Every Gateway." It belongs to the dhāraṇī class of canonical literature, a genre concerned with sacred formulas and their associated teachings and ritual power. The text opens with the Blessed One residing in the divine abode of Tuṣita, teaching the Dharma of the six perfections to an vast assembly that includes bodhisattvas, gods of the Long-Life Heaven, and great divine beings such as Brahmā, Viṣṇu, and Śiva. It was translated into Tibetan by the master Gyaltsen Zangpo at the great temple of Glorious Tharpa Ling.

Translation

This was translated by the master Gyaltsen Zangpo at the great temple of Glorious Tharpa Ling.

In the Sanskrit language: Samantamukhapraveśaraśmivimalośṇīṣaprabhāsasarvatathāgatahṛdayasamayavilokitanāmadhāraṇī.

In the Tibetan language: The dhāraṇī entitled "Gazing upon the Heart-Essence and Sacred Pledge of All the Tathāgatas by means of the Radiance of the Uṣṇīṣa's Stainless Light that Enters through Every Gateway."

Homage to all buddhas and bodhisattvas.

Then the Blessed One, dwelling in the divine abode of Tuṣita, together with a great assembly of bodhisattvas, a vast gathering of the gods of the Long-Life Heaven, Brahmā, Viṣṇu, the Great God (Śiva), and many other divine beings, taught the Dharma of the six perfections to the great multitude gathered around him. This is what he taught:

The perfection of generosity, through which the accumulation of the great stores of merit from giving ripens as the fruit of lordship over a realm as a wheel-turning sovereign, as a rain of the seven precious things, and as the arising of a vast spontaneously accomplished treasure. The perfection of moral discipline, through which the possession of the Tathāgata's teaching, the five higher knowledges, and pure conduct ripens as the fruit of birth as a Brahmā or as a god. The perfection of patience, through which the fruit of being excellently adorned with major marks and minor features as pleasing to behold as the lovely form of a god ripens into maturation. The perfection of diligence, through which the fruit of beginning to exert effort, of taming māra, of liberation from aging and death and from birth, and of going by miraculous birth to a buddha-field ripens into maturation. The perfection of meditative concentration, through which the fruit of attaining billions and hundreds of millions of meditative absorptions such as the courageous-advance absorption and many others ripens into maturation. The perfection of wisdom, through which the fruit of accumulating a great store of wisdom and gnosis and an ocean of learning, along with its ripening as great learning, matures.

Then those divine sons, contemplating and practicing this very gateway of Dharma of the six perfections day and night, also cultivated it on account of its great difficulty.

Now, within that very divine retinue, there was a divine son of the Heaven of the Thirty-Three named Stainless Jewel-Essence. He was attended by ten million divine sons who served as his retinue, was encircled by eighteen thousand divine daughters, and dwelt in a magnificently fashioned celestial palace of divine transformation, built with walls of the seven precious materials, beautifully adorned with parks of many kinds, and graced with ponds made lovely with divine flowers. That divine son, Stainless Jewel-Essence, was together with his foremost divine daughter named Fully Blooming Flower, experiencing great abundance of delight, attached to divine pleasures, and in that palace with four gateways built of the seven precious materials, richly supplied with and intoxicated by the five qualities of sensory desire, he lay down puffed up with pride, arrogant and self-satisfied. Then, around midnight, that divine son fell into sleep, and all the hosts of divine daughters fell silent and likewise lay down to sleep.

In that excellent place there dwelt a chief among the divine yakṣas named Arrow-Face. He roused the divine son Stainless Jewel-Essence and spoke these words: "Hey, Stainless Jewel-Essence, wake up! Wake up! What use is it to be so fond of sleep and attached to it? What use is it to be habituating yourself to pleasure and remaining in attachment?

What use is it to go on enjoying immeasurable divine pleasures? In just seven days you will meet the hour of death. You will be separated from all divine pleasures and will experience great and terrible suffering. What then will be the good of your splendid palace, that excellent mansion built of seven kinds of precious jewels? Your life will not come back — seek swiftly now a means of rescue.

Having spoken these words, the chief of the divine yakṣas, Arrow-Face, vanished from sight.

Then the divine son Stainless Jewel-Essence, upon hearing those dreadful words, fainted and fell headlong from his bed, crashing to the ground. At that, the whole company of divine daughters shook loose their hair, their ornaments fell away, their garments became disordered, their mouths ran with blood, their lips, palates, throats, and tongues dried up, and they wept and wailed with many thousands of cries, lamenting and calling out in anguish again and again. From vessels of divine jewels they sprinkled cool perfumed water and cool sandalwood water over him. Some clasped him by the neck, some by the hands, some stroked his hair, some held his feet, some grasped his head, and some fainted and fell themselves. Some writhed on the ground like fish out of water, and some twisted as though their bodies were being seared by fire, while others cried out in many lamentations, wailing again and again.

Then the divine son Stainless Jewel-Essence recovered from his faint. Coming back to his senses, he vomited blood, experienced fierce and terrible suffering, breathed out a long and shuddering breath, and stood trembling, swaying like a tree buffeted by the wind. With a heart stricken and shattered by grief, he went straight to where Śakra (Indra), the lord of the gods, was to be found. He came before Śakra, touched his feet, and clasped both of them in his hands, saying: "Blessed One, I beg you, protect me! Blessed One, you who are master of the great king of the gods, protect me! Having heard the announcement of the chief of the divine yakṣas, Arrow-Face, I know that in seven days I shall meet the hour of death." And weeping he added: "Contemplate the suffering of the hell-beings, I implore you. Lord of the gods, please grant me, I beg you, a remedy free of fear — a remedy by which my death will not come about through my own doing and by which I will not descend into the lower realms. Lord of the gods, by whatever means possible, I beg you, protect me through the rite of liberation, that my death may pass away on its own terms."

Then Śakra, the lord of the gods, hearing those words, was filled with deep sorrow at heart, and spoke thus to the divine son Stainless Jewel-Essence: "Divine son, do not be afraid! There is one who can defeat the lord of death, one by whom beings are liberated from the sufferings of hell — the awakened Blessed One, the Teacher of gods and humans in this world, the unsurpassed physician who has gone beyond birth, aging, sickness, death, and sorrow. He is your refuge, your good kinsman and ally, and like a father and mother to you. He is the one who destroys all lower destinies. Let us go swiftly to wherever that guide abides."

Then, at dawn of that very day, Śakra the lord of the gods, the divine son Stainless Jewel-Essence, and many thousands of gods and divine daughters all set out together from the Joyful Heaven (Tuṣita) and went to where the Blessed One was to be found. They arrived in the presence of the Blessed One, bowed their heads to his feet in homage, circumambulated him three times, and stood before him.

Then Śakra, lord of the gods, addressed the Blessed One in words of humble supplication, saying: "Blessed One, the divine yakṣa-chief Comet-Face has urged this divine son Stainless Jewel-Essence, and has announced that in seven days, under the parasol, this Stainless Jewel-Essence will fall into the power of the lord of death. Blessed One, what is to be done for him?"

Then the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Śākyamuni, having heard the words of Śakra lord of the gods, smiled. As he displayed that smile, rays of light of many hues streamed forth from his face. Those rays of light illumined the great trichiliocosm, the world-realm of the three-thousandfold great thousand worlds. That brilliance of light stirred and roused the dwellings of all gods, nāgas, yakṣas, gandharvas (scent-eaters), asuras, garuḍas, kinnaras (half-human beings), great serpents, rākṣasas, and all manner of spirits. Having roused them, the light turned back, circled the Blessed One three times, and then entered through the door of the Blessed One's mouth. No sooner had it entered than the Blessed One declared to Śakra lord of the gods:

"Lord of the gods, hear this: this divine son Stainless Jewel-Essence will in seven days face the terror of the lord of death's door. If he comes to die at that time, he will experience the feelings of the great hells — immense, unbearable suffering, fierce and terrible. First, in the great city of Vārāṇasī, he will be born as a flesh-eating sow with a pig's head, in the mire and filth of a reed-cutter's cesspit, and there he will eat excrement and urine. Within that very cesspit dwell many billions of creatures, and at all three times of day they will eat the flesh of that sow, leaving nothing but bones. Then, through the force of karma, a body will arise again, and he will survive for seven years. Then he will come to the time of death. After that he will be born in a wilderness as a turtle, in a place where the very name of water is unheard and there is no shade of a tree. Scorched and blistered by the heat of the sun, he will eat grains of sand, and crows will tear at his body, rending it limb from limb. Then again through the force of karma a body will arise and perish, and so it will continue for five full years. Then he will come to the time of death, and will be born as a great fish in a great river at that same Vārāṇasī. Then through the force of karma he will be cast out onto dry land, and that great body will be devoured by venomous serpents, crows, water-birds, dogs, leopards, bears, jackals, and the like, as well as by various other creatures. Then again through the force of karma a body will arise and be consumed, and so it will continue for three years. Then again, having died, he will be born into seven houses and experience the sufferings of Jambudvīpa: he will be born into a house of lepers, into a house of the poor, into a house of slaves, into a house of butchers, into a house of low birth, into a house of outcasts, and into a house of the blind, experiencing suffering for sixty years. Having experienced those sufferings, he will then die and transmigrate, and in the sixteen great hells he will experience suffering for eighty-four eons. Lord of the gods, such are the great sufferings he will experience. Moreover, if even after a hundred such rebirths he obtains a human body, it will be a body marked by poverty, incomplete limbs, feeble wisdom, illiteracy, and separation from the Buddha, the Dharma, and the Saṅgha.

He will be scorned by all the world, perpetually tormented by thirst and hunger, short-lived, and beset by many illnesses.

Then Śakra, the Lord of Gods (Indra, king of the gods), having heard this dire account concerning the divine son Ratnagarbha Vimala, was deeply terrified and distressed in the presence of the Blessed One, and spoke these words: "Blessed One, I have lost all hope. There is no refuge for me."

The Blessed One declared: "Lord of Gods, there is a great antidote to the punishment of the Lord of Death — therefore do not give up hope. In future times, at the moment of death, it will bring those sentient beings a peaceful passing. They will be fully liberated from all rebirths in the hells and in the animal realm. By mere recollection, all obscurations will be purified. Their lives will be long. They will obtain utterly pure bodies."

Then Śakra, the Lord of Gods, together with the Four Great Kings, Brahmā, Viṣṇu, and the Great Lord (Śiva), pressed their palms together, bowed toward the Blessed One, and spoke to him thus: "Blessed One, please increase our hope. Blessed One, please protect all the gods. Blessed One, please liberate all the worlds of living beings. Blessed One, please pacify the fear of death. Blessed One, please remove the unbearable suffering of the hell-beings. Blessed One, please summon Yama, the King of Dharma (the lord of the dead). Blessed One, please grant relief to the sentient beings of the four-continent world-system. Blessed One, please look upon the realm of the Thirty-Three Gods. Blessed One, please seal all the Tathāgatas of the ten directions throughout all the world-systems of the ten directions with the great mudrā of the Tathāgatas' primordial wisdom. Please bestow blessing through the blessing of the Tathāgatas. Blessed One, out of your compassion for us, please expound the great antidote. Sugata (the Well-Gone One, an epithet of the Buddha), please speak."

Then the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Śākyamuni, having taken to heart what was thus requested, entered into meditative absorption called Seeing All Aspects in All Ways. The instant he entered that absorption, a ray of light called All Aspects emerged from the center of his crown protrusion, illuminating all directions, and it remained in the sky like a jeweled parasol. The Blessed One smiled and, gazing upon Śakra, the Lord of Gods, declared to him these words:

"Lord of Gods, receive this dhāraṇī mantra called Omnipresently Illuminating Crown Radiance, Stainless — the essence and samaya (sacred pledge) of all the Tathāgatas, Gazing on All Aspects. This mudrā has been spoken and rejoiced over by as many Tathāgatas as there are grains of sand in sixty-two hundred-thousand-million Ganges rivers. By merely seeing or hearing this dhāraṇī mantra, one will be fully liberated from all sins, obscurations, the hells, the animal realm, and every fear of the lower destinies. All karmic obscurations from the past will be utterly destroyed, and all hells will be obliterated. Just as fire burns grass and wind swiftly scatters the ashes, so too will all the sins accumulated over former lives be burned. Just as when rain descends and the water that falls upon the mountains sweeps away all the dust and filth upon the earth, so likewise by merely seeing and hearing this dhāraṇī, sins will be cleansed."

Just as gold refined by fire
becomes fit to be worked and beaten with ease,
so likewise by mere recitation
the body is purified — of this there is no doubt.

Just as when the sun has risen
its rays blaze forth in all directions,
so too whoever holds this dhāraṇī
will find their very body made luminous and beautiful.

Just as a fish cast out on dry land
turns and thrashes, parted from the water, at the gate of death,
but then is thrown back into the water once more
and lives there, thriving and flourishing again —
so too by mere recollection of this dhāraṇī
people's lives are reversed and restored.

O Śakra, lord of the gods (Indra, king of the gods), listen now: here is the dhāraṇī mantra to be taught.

Namas sarva-tathāgatānāṃ. Oṃ mahācintāmaṇi-jvalana, sāgaragambhire, ākarṣaya ākarṣaya, āyundhara āyundhara, sandhara sandhara, kṣaṇa-kṣaṇa, kṣeṇi-kṣeṇi, kṣunu-kṣunu, sarva-tathāgata-mahāsamaya, tiṣṭha tiṣṭha, mahābhuvana-sāgara, saṃśodhaya, māṃ sarvasatvāṃś ca, bhagavati, sarva-pāpaṃ vimala, jaya jaya, jayalabhe, sphuṭa sphuṭa, sphoṭaya sphoṭaya, vigata-āvaraṇi, bhaya-haraṇi, hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ, mṛtyudaṇḍadhare, abhayaprade, uṣṇīṣa-vyavalokite, samanta-muṣe, samanta-vyavalokite, mahāmāyi, mahāpāśadhare, amogha-vimale, ākarṣaya ākarṣaya, ākāḍhaya ākāḍhaya, bhara bhara, sambhara sambhara, indriya-viśodhani, bhūṣita-bhuje, mahāmudrā-vilokite, jaya jaya, siddhe siddhe, buddha buddha, bodhani bodhani, saṃbodhani saṃbodhani, śodhani śodhani, saṃśodhani saṃśodhani, viśodhani viśodhani, hara hara, mama sarva-pāpaṃ, sarva-tathāgata-kula-bhuje, samaya-tiṣṭhe, prasaratu mama puṇye, vinaśyantu pāpaṃ, sarva-kilbiṣa-hare, maṇi-viśuddhe, śodhaya vimale, vikāsita-padme, gabhacaṃta-bhuje, ṣaṭpāramitā-paripūraṇi, sarva-tathāgatoṣṇīṣa-vilokite svāhā, sarva-tathāgata-guhya-adhiṣṭhāna-adhiṣṭhite svāhā, āyur-dade svāhā, puṇya-dade svāhā, puṇya-vilokite svāhā, puṇya-avalokite svāhā, mṛtyudaṇḍe svāhā, yamadaṇḍe svāhā, yamadūte svāhā, saṃharaṇi svāhā, sambharaṇi svāhā, sandhāraṇi svāhā, pratisaraṇi svāhā, ojovatī svāhā, tejovatī svāhā, jayavatī svāhā, sarva-tathāgata-mudrā-adhiṣṭhāna-adhiṣṭhite svāhā.

O Śakra, lord of the gods, teach this dhāraṇī mantra to the god's son Ratnagarbha Vimala. By doing so, you will bring that god's son long-lasting benefit and happiness.

Write out this dhāraṇī mantra — which is the Tathāgata Sarvanivāraṇaviṣkambhin (the "All-Illuminating Crown Protrusion Free of Defilement, Perceiving All Entrances"), the essence and sacred commitment of all Tathāgatas — and place it inside a stūpa. Whether the stūpa is new or old and in need of repair, place it within. Offer incense and unguents as well, and recite the mantra essence of the dhāraṇī one hundred and eight times with mindfulness, meditating upon it one hundred and eight times. Each day, recollect it three times during the day and three times at night.

Then, when the sun has risen, face the eastern direction, create a maṇḍala of incense, and scatter flowers toward the sun. Burn incense of aloeswood and turushka resin, make prostrations to all the Tathāgatas, and recite it one hundred and eight times.

If this mantra essence is written, inserted into one hundred and eight constructed stūpas, and placed upon a maṇḍala, it will be as though the body-relics and sacred remains of Tathāgatas as numerous as ninety-nine hundred thousand billion grains of sand of the Ganges River have been placed within each and every one of those stūpas. It will also become the very essence of sacred relics. Write out this essence and insert it.

Oṃ namas traya-dhvikānāṃ.

Sarva tathāgata hṛdaya garbhe jvala jvala. Dharmadhātu garbhe saṃbharam ā āyuḥ. Saṃśodhaya saṃśodhaya. Mama sarva pāpaṃ. Sarva tathāgata samantoṣṇīṣa vimala viśuddhe. Hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ. Aṃ vaṃ saṃ jaḥ svāhā.

This text, called Radiant Light of the Uṣṇīṣa, Pure and All-Entering, is the very heart-essence of all Tathāgatas of the three times. If one pays reverent homage to it and performs the stūpa rites, a life that is drawing to its close will be turned back, and life will be purified and made long. Even if death and transmigration do occur, one will cast off the body just as a serpent sheds its skin, and through the equality of fortune will journey to the world-realm of Bliss. One will not experience the suffering of death. Henceforth one will never again experience the sensation of dying. Henceforth one will never again take birth in hell, among animals, in the realm of the Lord of Death, or in any lower destiny. If even the sound of the word "hell-being" will never again reach one's ears, it goes without saying that the ripening of such karmic results is simply impossible — there is no basis for it whatsoever.

Then Śakra (Indra), lord of the gods, having received this mantra from the Blessed One, departed together with the divine son Ratnaviśuddha at the break of dawn and returned to his own abode. Following the scriptural instructions as declared by the Tathāgata, and performing the stūpa rites in their proper sequence, he then also performed the recitation rite. The very moment he had done so, the divine son Ratnaviśuddha was pacified of all suffering and freed from all misdeeds. His body became purified, his complexion became like the color of gold, and his hair and eyes became like deep blue beryl. He beheld all the Tathāgatas directly before him, and from the expanse of space the Tathāgatas gave forth the words "Excellent! Excellent!" Then, seeing that the divine son had attained a body free of obscurations, Śakra was struck with great wonder and filled with great joy, and he exclaimed with fervent utterance: "How marvelous — the buddhas! How marvelous — the mantra! How marvelous — the Dharma! I pay homage, I pay homage, to the Blessed One the Tathāgata Śākyamuni, who reveals the fruit immediately, whose compassion is supremely great, who is like a wish-fulfilling jewel." He uttered these words of fervent praise again and again.

Then that divine son returned to his own abode, took up divine flowers, incense, perfume, garlands, and various unguents, took also divine garments, robes, and ornaments, and together with all his attendants and retinue, divine maidens, the host of gods, Śakra, lord of the gods, and the rest, he set out for the Tuṣita realm. Going to where the Blessed One was and arriving there, he made great offerings to the Blessed One, circumambulated him a hundred thousand times, and then sat down in the very presence of the Blessed One in order to hear the Dharma.

Then the Four Great Kings, Brahmā, Viṣṇu, the Great God (Śiva), and Vajrapāṇi, great lord of the yakṣas, joined their palms and bowed toward where the Blessed One was, and addressed the Blessed One thus: "Blessed One, through what cause will there arise for the divine son Ratnaviśuddha karmic maturations that are unbearable and exceedingly fierce?"

The Blessed One declared: "Excellent! Excellent, noble ones! Noble ones, you have asked well. Therefore, listen and I shall explain. Vajrapāṇi, in a time gone by, in a past age, in the southern region there was a city called Vistīrṇa. In that place there lived a brahmin named Vimala — learned, refined, clear-minded, of settled intellect, handsome and beautiful in appearance, pleasing to behold, of splendid and excellent complexion —"

— faithful, of virtuous intention, and a speaker of sacred learning. He dwelt there continually recollecting and meditating upon this queen of vidyā-mantras (dhāraṇī), and he desired the welfare of all sentient beings. Now in that very same city there lived a householder named Prabhāvara — wealthy, of great riches, of great possessions, with many servants and attendants. This householder had no faith whatsoever, and instead placed his faith in the tīrthikas. Though the brahmin repeatedly proclaimed this queen of vidyā-mantras together with its essence-syllables to that householder, the householder generated no mind of faith toward it. On the contrary, he thought to himself: "I shall hack this brahmin to pieces, toss him away as one discards burnt fish and turtle, and stuff his mouth with filth." The very moment he conceived this thought, the householder was struck by skin disease and leprosy. He then died at the appointed hour of death, experiencing great suffering in the feeling at the moment of passing. After dying, he was born in the great hell Avīci, and there he experienced the feeling of suffering for the measure of an aeon. After dying and passing away from there, he experienced suffering for an aeon among fish, pigs, and turtles. Dying and passing away from there, he was born in the hell called the Pitch-Black, and there too he experienced great suffering for the measure of an aeon. After dying and passing away from there, he was born blind in a household in that very same city. Being blind, he went about begging from one place to another, and one day he came to a temple. There was a monk dwelling there, a monk who abided in compassion. Seeing him, that monk treated him with loving-kindness, gave him food, and also proclaimed this queen of vidyā-mantras. Upon hearing this dhāraṇī-mantra, the blind man recalled his past lives. He remembered that brahmin and, reflecting upon him, thought: "Alas, I have committed an unbearable act," and he became filled with remorse and shame.
