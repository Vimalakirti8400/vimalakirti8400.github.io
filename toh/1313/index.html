About this text

The Means of Accomplishment of the Circle of the Maṇḍala of Nairātmyā, whose Sanskrit title line reads Nairātthamaṇḍalacakrasādhana and whose Tibetan title is Dakmemé Kyilkhor gyi Khorlö Drubtap, is a tantric meditation manual, or sādhana, for the maṇḍala of Nairātmyā, the goddess of selflessness and consort of Hevajra, surrounded by her retinue of fourteen yoginīs. Written largely in verse and drawing on the Hevajra and Pañjara tantras, it leads the practitioner from the preliminary offerings, refuge and awakening mind, through meditation on emptiness and the generation of the protective circle, the palace and the fifteen goddesses from their seed syllables, to the purification of the aggregates, elements, senses and afflictions as goddesses, the meaning of their bone ornaments, recitation and torma offering, and it closes with shorter forms of the practice handed down as the oral instruction of the great Nāropa. It is Toh 1313 in the Tengyur, where it belongs to the tantra commentaries section among the practice texts of the Hevajra cycle.

Translation

In the language of India: nairātthamaṇḍalacakrasādhanaṃ. In the language of Tibet: The Means of Accomplishment of the Circle of the Maṇḍala of Nairātmyā.

Homage to Vajranairātmyā.

To the summit of the maṇḍala, whose nature is the guru,
I bow down deeply in reverence with a clear and faithful mind,
and I set down the circle of goddesses drawn from the Five Hundred Thousand,
so that imprints of the profound meaning may arise.

Here the person who is the basis for this practice, possessing the vow of individual liberation, the bodhisattva vow and the vow of a vidyādhara, should meditate by day in a charnel ground and by night in his own home.

At an auspicious time, rising in the eighth part of the night, he bathes and makes the water offering, offers the maṇḍala, and then sets out the offerings, the torma and so forth. Sitting on a soft cushion in the vajra cross-legged posture, he says vajranairvadma and so meditates on himself instantly as Nairātmyā. She is blue in color, with one face and two arms. Her right hand holds a curved knife with a handle of iron or copper; her left holds a skull cup, white outside and red inside, filled with blood, together with a khaṭvāṅga; and she is adorned with the five mudrās. In the heart of oneself so imagined, within an open eight-petaled lotus, one imagines from raṃ a sun maṇḍala and upon it a blue e. With its light rays one either visualizes clearly the guru, the venerable one in a form like Akṣobhya, as Vajradhara and the goddesses abiding by their very nature as non-dual and inseparable, or one invites them from the palace of the dharmadhātu in Akaniṣṭha and visualizes them. To them one makes prostration; the outer, secret and suchness offerings; confession of wrongdoing; rejoicing in merit; going for refuge to the Three; reliance on the path; the generation of the supreme mind of awakening, both relative and ultimate; and the dedication of the roots of virtue to awakening. Then one mentally releases the assembly that served as the support for gathering merit and requests them to depart. As it is said in the Pañjara:

Worshipping the guru, the vajra holder,
one should purify the seven.

After that:

First, one should cultivate loving-kindness;
second, one cultivates compassion;
third, one cultivates bliss;
and at the end of all, equanimity.

Then, preceded by emanating and gathering, one meditates on the whole world and its inhabitants as emptiness, and by saying oṃ śunyatājñānavajrasvabhāva ādmako'haṃ one should make the emptiness stable. Then:

From the letter repha, first visualize a sun;
on that sun arises hūṃ, a crossed vajra;
with that very vajra, the fence
and the binding of the tent are visualized.
Within that, one should imagine:
from hūṃ, a wheel with ten spokes,
vast, hollow and unmoving;
one should meditate on it as the nature of saṃsāra.
In the sphere of space imagine a bhaga.
What one visualizes in the center
is how the wheel comes first, as fitting,
and how the deities arise.
The wheels of earth and water come first,
then, as is fitting, fire,
and the great wind of the deities;
from hūṃ, the drop of the wheel.
From the arising as meditated,
the wheel itself, born from the source of phenomena:
two rings, flawless,
and from the pericarp they become one;
the outer is described by the triangle.
It is square, with four doors,
adorned with eight pillars,
furnished with nets and lattices,
endowed with the eight charnel grounds,
and decorated with vajra lines.
In its center, imagine the corpses of fifteen dead
as the very substance of the seats.
Upon ā one should hold the pride of the vajra.
On the fifteen corpses, upon moons,
from the melting of fifteen sets of vowels
are produced fifteen moons marked by them.
After that, from the melting
of fifteen sets of consonants, with the two that were mentioned added,
are produced fifteen suns marked by them.
Between the sun and moon in the center,
a nāda falls, which one imagines as ā.
From that, the fourteen vowels
are emanated between the suns and moons in the directions and intermediate directions.

One emanates ā i yī u in the east and the other directions of the ring. khru ṛ jā lo are those of the intermediate directions of the outer ring, for Pukkasī and the others. thḹ e e o are those of the directions, for Gaurī and the others. au aṃ are the seed syllables of below and above.

From these, by emanating and gathering,
imagine fifteen curved knives with the seed syllables.
From these, emanated and gathered, they merge into one;
from that, the bodies of the fifteen goddesses
are all generated black in color.
The moon possesses the mirror-like wisdom;
the seven-horsed sun is equality;
the seed syllable and hand emblem of one's own deity
are to be known as discernment;
all becoming one is the accomplishing of what is to be done;
the completion is the purity of the dharmadhātu.
Through the rite the wise have taught,
one meditates on these five aspects.
Among them, the yoginī Nairātmyā
is black in color and greatly fierce,
adorned with the five mudrās.
In her right hand she holds a curved knife;
in her left, a lotus vessel;
in the crook of her left arm, a khaṭvāṅga;
her lower body is wrapped in a tiger skin.
First, in the inner ring
five yoginīs abide without fail;
as the very nature of the five aggregates
one who knows yoga always meditates on them.
First, Vajrā; in Yama's quarter, Gaurī;
in that of the Lord of the Waters, Vāriyoginī;
in Kubera's, Vajraḍākinī;
and in the center, the yoginī Nairātmyā.
Then the outer ring is explained:
in Indra's quarter, Gaurī; in Yama's, Caurī;
in the direction of the Lord of the Waters, Vetālī;
in Kubera's, Ghasmarī;
in Īśāna's, Pukkasī;
in Fire's, likewise, Śavarī;
in Nairṛti's one places Caṇḍālī;
and in Wind's one places Ḍombī.
Below is said to be Bhūcarī,
and above is named Khecarī.
In the phase of the generation stage,
these two goddesses abide
as the nature of existence and of peace.
All the goddesses are black in color,
greatly fierce, adorned with the five mudrās,
with one face and red eyes,
their hands filled with curved knife and skull cup.
The wheel, the earrings, the necklace,
the bracelets on the arms, and the girdle:
by the purity of the five buddhas,
these five are the pure mudrās.
Just as the yoginī Nairātmyā is,
so are all of them described:
one hand is filled with a skull cup;
the right holds a curved knife;
the left, the khaṭvāṅga itself.

Her lower body is wrapped in a tiger skin;
she rides upon a corpse, blazing with splendor;
she has two arms, and her yellow hair stands upright.
To cut off the six faults, pride and the rest,
she holds the curved knife.
In the lotus vessel that is the skull
of concepts of entity and non-entity,
the blood of the four kinds of māras
is to be drunk for the sake of accomplishment.
The khaṭvāṅga, in the aspect of emptiness,
is to be understood as the taste of skillful means.
If one meditates on the circle in this way,
accomplishments will swiftly be attained.
After that, meditate on the yoga of the armor.
First, meditate on it as black;
second, visualize it as red;
third, meditate on it as yellow;
the fourth likewise is green;
the fifth is blue in color;
and the sixth has a white body.
The yogin who meditates on the six limbs
meditates on the coemergent nature.

Then comes the blessing as the three mantras, the blessing by the circle of wisdom beings, and then the blessing by empowerment, at the end of which one takes pride with arñiṣekaiḥ kulapati.

With the seal of aversion, Nairātmyā;
with the seal of delusion, Vajrā;
with the seal of avarice, Gaurī herself;
with passion, one seals the Water Lady;
with the seal of jealousy, the Ḍākinī.
With the seal of aversion, Pukkasī;
with the seal of delusion, Śavarī;
with the seal of avarice, Caṇḍālī;
with the seal of desire, Ḍombī herself.
Again, Gaurī with the seal of aversion;
with the seal of delusion, Caurī;
with the seal of avarice, Vetālī;
with the seal of desire, Ghasmarī;
with the seal of delusion, Bhūcarī;
with the seal of desire, Khecarī.

Then one enters into union with the khaṭvāṅga, or, by mere recollection, makes the offering of passion, and takes pride with anuraga. Then, just as with the torma, one blesses the nectar and tastes it, and takes pride with amṛtavajras. Then one makes the outer, secret and suchness offerings, and takes pride with pūjavajras. Then one offers praise with the verses beginning "In order to tame those hard to tame, of various inclinations," and takes pride with stutivajra.

Next is the self-blessing: whichever of the three one prefers, the instantaneous path, the gradual path, or the path of close accomplishment, sealed with the yoga of the coemergent, one meditates on the three together as one. This is to be received from the guru.

Then the fourteen yoginīs of the retinue are gathered into the seed syllable of oneself as the chief lady, and then one meditates on the inner sealing.

By this, the recollection of the purity of faults is as follows:
The aggregate of form is Vajrā;
for feeling too, Gaurī is named;
perception is Vāriyoginī;
formations, Vajraḍākinī;
and as the very nature of the aggregate of consciousness
abides the yoginī Nairātmyā.
For form, Gaurī is always taught;
for sound, Caurī is renowned;
for the domain of smell, Vetālī;
for taste, Ghasmarī is renowned;
for touch, Bhūcarī is named;
for the dharmadhātu, Khecarī.
Since these are always completely pure,
the yogin accomplishes reality itself.
In the eye, Delusion Vajrā;
in the ear, Aversion Vajrā;
the nose is called the Lady of Avarice;
in the tongue, Desire Vajrā;
in the body, Jealousy Vajrā;
in the mind, the yoginī Nairātmyā.
Earth is said to be Pukkasī;
the water element is called the Huntress, Śavarī;
fire is to be known as Caṇḍālī;
wind is renowned as Ḍombī.
Aversion is taught to be Vajrā;
desire, Vāriyoginī;
jealousy, Vajraḍākinī;
avarice is the secret Gaurī herself;
delusion likewise is taught as Vajrā.
This purifies aversion and the rest:
from the side of the generation stage,
by this the aggregates are to be purified.

To bow to the guru, the master and the chosen deity,
she wears the wheel.
So as not to listen to reviling words
against the guru, the vajra holder,
she wears earrings on her ears.
For reciting mantra, the necklace;
the bracelets, the renouncing of killing living beings;
and to display the mudrā, the girdle.
With the seals of the five buddhas
one should always seal the body.
I shall explain the purity of the seals:
in the form of the wheel is Akṣobhya;
the earrings have the nature of Amitābha;
the necklace, the nature of Ratnasambhava;
in the bracelets is Vairocana;
in the girdle abides Amoghasiddhi.
These are the five completely pure seals.

Then, as for verbal recitation: oṃ aḥ a ā i yī u be ṛ jā lo drva e i o sna e hūṃ svāhā. This should be recited at the junctures of the general and the particular practice. With verbal utterance as well, one gives the torma endowed with the four suchnesses. Then one makes aspiration prayers, with "Born in the family, and the pledges" and so on. Then one practices the yoga of daily conduct. One should understand that the activities of the later sessions are just like those of the first session.

If one wishes fewer than these fifteen deities, one leaves out those above and below and meditates on thirteen. If that is still too many, one sets aside the inner four and meditates on nine deities. If that is still too many, one leaves out the outer eight and meditates on five deities. If that is still too many, one meditates on the chief lady alone at the center of the maṇḍala. This is the oral instruction of the guru, the great Nāropa.

By the virtue of setting down this circle of goddesses,
may the beings included within the three realms of existence
realize the meaning of Nairātmyā
and, free of wrongdoing, go to the realm of the sky-goers.

The Means of Accomplishment of the Circle of the Maṇḍala of Nairātmyā is complete.

Translator's notes

"The Five Hundred Thousand" refers to the extensive Hevajra Tantra, said to have contained five hundred thousand lines; "the Pañjara" is the Ḍākinīvajrapañjara Tantra. In the lists of the inner and outer rings, the directions are named by their guardians (Indra for the east, Yama for the south, Varuṇa, Lord of the Waters, for the west, Kubera for the north, and Īśāna, Agni, Nairṛti and Vāyu for the intermediate directions); "the White One" (dkar) in Yama's quarter is read as Gaurī, as in the Hevajra Tantra. The line "upon a one should hold the pride of the vajra" is obscure, as is the line on "the two that were mentioned added" to the consonants; both are rendered literally. The final word "byel" in "one hand is filled with a skull cup" is uncertain and taken in that sense. "Dran pa tsam rmis" is read as dran pa tsam gyis, "by mere recollection". The phrase rendered "the four suchnesses" reads literally "those very four faults" (skyon de nyid bzhi); "faults" appears to be a slip, and the sense is uncertain. "Inner sealing" renders nang gis sa gdab, taken as rgyas gdab, "sealing"; this too is uncertain. The text assigns Vajrā both to delusion and to aversion in the last list of afflictions, which is translated as it stands. Chos rkun ma is read as chom rkun ma, Caurī, and pukka sbo as Pukkasī.
