About this text

This text, catalogued as Toh 4419a in the Tengyur, is the dedication and printing colophon of the Derge edition of the Tengyur, the Tibetan collection of Indian treatises that explain the Buddha's words. Its verses of dedication and aspiration are titled A Distant Song Summoning the Dear Friend of the Virtue and Excellence of Ground, Path and Fruit, in Tibetan Gzhi lam 'bras bu'i dge legs yid gcugs mdza' bo 'gugs pa'i rgyang glu. Opening with Sanskrit verses of blessing and continuing in ornate Tibetan poetry, it praises the Buddha and his teaching, dedicates the merit of the great printing project toward the awakening and happiness of all beings, and then records in prose how the blocks were carved: at the command of the great abbot Tashi Lhündrub of Ngor, under the patronage of the Derge rulers Tenpa Tsering and his son Kunga Trinlé Gyatso, beginning in the fire snake year of 1737 and completed at Derge Lhündrubteng in the water dog year of 1742. It was composed by the edition's chief proofreader, the monk Tsultrim Rinchen, and it stands among the catalogue and colophon materials that close the Tengyur, rather than among its translated Indian treatises.

Translation

saṃghaḥśu bhāmitahetu nodhādāśayasarbapūrṇaṃkalpadrumo jñānaścakāruṇapallahitasokhyabepākyammañjarī
Grown from the cause of immeasurable masses of virtue, the wish-fulfilling tree that fulfills every wish,
Its branches and leaves of knowledge and love outspread, its clusters of benefit and happiness swaying and bending low:
sarṅasyalokarathādhamuktānāṃparipāraṇonnaṃsaṃbudbhatā mandārabarṣapraveśayādyātraṃkusumāḥśubhaṃśreyāmmadgalaṃ
The perfect Buddha, who fills to overflowing the chariot of the aspirations of all the world,
Here and now, may he shower down like rain the mandārava flowers of virtue, goodness and good fortune.

The scripture that expresses, made of collections of names, phrases and letters, sends forth the sweet cry of the crane; Its subject, excellent meaning, bears along sands of gold; it cleanses the stains of the afflictions Of beings in the three realms; it is cool, and in it the image of the sublime moon Appears clearly: the three streams of the Dharma, the essence that nourishes the youth of awakening. By the nectar that has flowed from the throat of the Sage, the bodily strength of realization is supremely increased; Their faces beautified by golden nets of the three trainings, their tusks sharp in discerning all phenomena, Bearers of the burden of the vow to deliver endless beings from existence, Endowed with knowledge and conduct, the noble Saṅgha, a line of elephants guarding the earth, companions toward awakening.

The maṇḍala of his knowledge, unpolished by the hands of various deeds, is perfectly clear;
Appointed as no one's servant, his compassionate gaze turns toward beings;
Though he has reached the bloom of sixteen-year-old youth, he is the father who gives birth to all the Victors:
To Mañjughoṣa himself I join my palms, praying that he be my spiritual friend in every birth.
Her speech blended with the sound of a beryl vīṇā sweetly resounding,
From a melody complete in its sixty aspects she draws all that is knowable into sweet song;
Thus she, the Lake-Born (Sarasvatī), steals the steadiness of the hearts of ordinary and noble beings:
That she may enter the lotus pond of my throat, I send forth offering clouds of melodious words.

A guest summoned long ago by the messenger of aspirations to benefit the teaching of the Sage,
The great lord of men, of supreme lineage, together with his line of tall sāla trees,
With courage rising ever higher and higher, has made the great commentaries on the intent of the Sugata's word,
All those that have been rendered into the language of the Land of Snows, into inexhaustible master blocks.
How wonderful! Such a work of supreme courage by one endowed with courage
Has never before been enjoyed by the faculty of the eye, nor likewise
Has it fallen into the wide hollow of the ear. "What is this?" the proud maidens of the directions
Ask one another and reply, as if loudly beating the drum of conversation.
In accomplishing this, from the complete purity of field, of focus and of application,
Whatever pure white virtue has arisen, if it could take on a form,
For fear it would not fit within the three worlds, the maker of all the world,
So I think, has fashioned this vessel called Sahā especially vast.
Even so, as long as it is not transformed into the seed of supreme awakening,
It yields a temporary fruit of the higher realms, or else is brought to its end by misdeeds.
Therefore, in the utterly peaceful expanse free of the poison of conceiving the three spheres,
Delighting in the play of dream and illusion, I make a dedication that rests in evenness.

To the ends of space, in the directions and the intermediate directions,
Above and below, in buddha fields as numerous as the particles of the ocean,
Those who have come, those who will come,
And those who now live and remain, the great drums of the Dharma,
Each of them encircled by their successors,
The heirs of the Victors and tens of millions of disciples,
A retinue beyond all counting:
I look to them as witnesses who make this aspiration bear fruit.
The treasury of the wheel of the inexhaustible ornament of the three secrets
They have attained; a message of some part of their qualities
I draw clearly upon the surface of my mind,
And bowing countless bodies, I prostrate.
Taken from the opened storehouse of the mind,
Articles of enjoyment and the seven precious things,
Clouds of empty forms lacking none of the pleasures of the senses:
May they appear before the eyes of all who are worthy of offering.
Driven off by the enemies of attachment, aversion and delusion,
The precepts of the three trainings, those sovereign kings,
Have been cast into the mire of faults and downfalls; their stains
I wash away with the water of confession through the four opposing powers.
In the ocean of virtue gathered by the four kinds of noble beings and by ordinary beings,
Where chains of waves of benefit and happiness stir,
Without scattering whirlwinds of the dust of envy,
I bathe it in the white moonlight of a smile of rejoicing.
You who steer the training of beings,
Though you have no fault of weariness or fatigue,
In keeping with the ways of the world, do not remain in equanimity:
I urge you to turn the wheel of the profound and vast Dharma.
The rainbow patterns of your manifold display,
Do not let them sink into the wilderness beyond sorrow;
Mindful of your former aspirations,
I pray that you remain for countless eons.
By the good deeds done in this way, may I,
Skilled in traversing the sky of the inclinations of sentient beings,
Make manifest the omniscient sun of the Dharma
And fill the world with the light of virtue.
The cuckoo who longs for such an awakening,
To draw near the heart's dear friends, the goodness of ground, path and fruit,
Takes up sweet names
And calls with a far-carrying song of words of dedication and aspiration: please heed it.
Whatever accumulations of virtue have been well performed
Through body, speech and mind, linked through the three times,
By tathāgatas and bodhisattvas,
Pratyekabuddhas, śrāvakas and ordinary beings;
And the hand that gives for the sake of this Dharma tradition,
Stretched out like a wish-fulfilling tree to all who have wishes,
Offerings and gifts lacking nothing that is needed,
Together with the virtue of striving with body, speech and mind in every moment:
Here and now gathered into one, a mountain of snow,
By the great sun of not conceiving the three spheres
Melted into a river turned into a stream, peaceful, cool and sublime,

May it become of one taste with the ocean of supreme awakening.

The unceasing radiance of the appearances of its interdependence,
Wondrous offering clouds of illusion:
May they appear before the eyes of all who are present,
Worthy of offering, true witnesses to the fulfillment of our aspirations.

The Dharma which, when heard, protects from suffering together with its causes
And bestows the supreme state:
May its splendor of benefit and happiness, high above the three worlds,
Blaze forth brightly and long remain firm.

The unerring account of the intended meaning of the complete teaching,
The treasury of nourishing understanding and speech
Bestowed by the youthful Mañjuśrī: the teaching of the Sakyapas,
Praised by the Sage, may it be victorious.

In the speech of the Omniscient One,
Kunga Zangpo was prophesied to attain buddhahood
At the end of doing the deeds of a scholar for the teaching:
May his supreme tradition spread everywhere.

Emanated from the ocean of the activity of the glorious Victor,
The supreme of speakers, protector of the day,
In order to protect the kingdom of the complete Dharma,
May his lotus feet stand firm upon the vajra throne.

Laying down the prātimokṣa in keeping with the texts of the Vinaya,
Explaining the trainings of the bodhisattva in the manner of the sūtras,
Instructing in secret mantra according to the profound meaning of the tantras:
May many skilled in teaching uphold the teaching.
With the machinery of bow and arrow of grammar and logic,
From all the quarters of evil speakers devoid of truth
Rescuing Sītā, the renown of victory:
May many skilled in debate overcome wrong understanding.
The meaning expressed by the three baskets,
Through Menakā, expression in verse, prose and their blend,
Playing hundreds of thousands of flutes of ornaments of sound and sense:
May many skilled in composition illuminate the teaching.
The root and branches of the teaching, the assembled communities of the Saṅgha,
Through teaching and practice make the face of the earth sway and tremble:
Terrified of that, may the Flower-Bowed One (Kāma)
Think of slipping into a disguise of emptiness.

Where the Great Sage's own sublime Dharma of scripture and realization
Is highly praised as having its place to spread and flourish,
Great Tibet of the four divisions, rich in the ten virtues:
In the vast kingdom exemplified by this region,
May the gods who delight in the teaching
Gather in the sky in due season, holding fans of cloud,
Chanting svaste in melody,
And let fall sapphire drops of pure water.
Then may the lovely Gold-Bearing Earth display her own body,
Fresh and green, with beautiful ornaments of flowers,
With hosts of grasses, plants, leaves, shoots and fruits,
And every festival of ripening.
Of embodied beings throughout the vast world,
The illusory bodies emanated by karma, the origin of suffering,
May all kinds of disease that impair them and destroy them untimely
Vanish like darkness when a lamp is raised.
Messages that show that the contaminated is suffering itself,
The inauspicious crises of year, month, day and hour,
The sri demons, and others' curses, and hostile sorcery:
May they be stripped of the power to bring forth their results.
Driven helplessly by the river of perverse karma and aspirations,
Obstructing the good,
The grahas, obstructors and bhūtas who do harm:
May they remain as if embraced by great loving-kindness.
Eyes rolling and brows twisted, inflamed with rage,
Raining down weapons smeared with blood,
The hosts of savage ones who cut and pierce one another:
May their mindstreams become gentler even than compassion.
Their bodily strength wasted by the torment of hunger,
Longing, crying out the very names of food and drink,
Their hair and body hair coated with dust and bristling:
May not even the name of an eon of famine remain.
Mutual theft, robbery and the like, wrong livelihoods
That are like the Bodiless One (Kāma) slipping into the disguise of hands:
Having given them up entirely, each with their own wealth,
May they enjoy the inexhaustible treasure of contentment.
Beings having become friends to one another,
When they meet, with smiles adorned by great love,
Led by the rays of light from their white rows of teeth,
May they live as though going out to welcome one another.
Deathless life, the strength of merit,
And wealth in keeping with the Dharma:
May they rival the stream of the Ganges, the moon of the waxing fortnight,
The light of the sun, and the lakes of summer.

In the Dharma kingdom of the Second Victor,
From the auspicious, spiraling Dharma conch of his throat
Thunders the sound of teaching, debate and composition:
May all the aims he intends be spontaneously accomplished.
The divine abode of the sublime teaching, by strength of heart
That wondrous upholder raises upon his shoulders:
May he reach the ground of omniscience,
The state aspired to through many lifetimes.

Led by the golden wheel that comes through the power of karma,
The vast dominion with its mountains, continents and oceans,
The message of the first envoy of sovereignty:
"Great Lama," proclaim the maidens of the directions.
He who raises ever higher the teaching of scripture and realization that bestows the glory of all joy,
Whose activity, a garment of ocean, pervades everything,
He who possesses the four divisions:
May his throne, the merit of beings, stand firm.
His two feet, lotuses born of water,
Shed garlands of deep-red pollen of power
Upon the crowns of tens of millions of proud rulers of the directions:
May he stand firm, victorious over all.
May they increase and flourish: deathless life,
Undeclining dominion, power and glory,
An invincible retinue and strength,
And the letters of a fame that never sets.
Even those swollen with pride
In lineage, strength, armies and wealth, of their own accord,
Their cheeks colored with the red glow of single-minded devotion:
May every beauty of that spreading glow appear.
As he means to remain long to complete
The activity of Dharma and state to the ends of space,
May the obstructing harms of humans and nonhumans
Never find an opportunity to enter.
Through the wheels of abiding in ethics, of study, reflection and meditation,
Having become a Dharma king who reigns
Over the great kingdom of scripture and realization,
May he beat the victory drum of the teaching across the three levels.
Those vicious ones hostile to accomplishing such awakening,
As if seized at the heart by evil:
If they may live even for a moment, hosts of protectors,
Why do you raise the banner of those who delight in the white side?
The bakula tree of his intelligence, examining a hundred systems,
Has blossomed at the mouthful of wine from the lovely lady of Mañjuśrī's blessing:
May he enjoy long life, the fortune of karma,
And the glory of an ocean of good qualities.
Into the house of the merit of all embodied beings,
The deathless protector, summoned as a guest of happiness and joy,
In the vast divine palace of teaching and state,

May the thousand eyes of his knowledge never close.

The great ministers of Dharma who came as if summoned
By the beckoning hand of the merit of the supreme ruler of the earth:
Through laws in keeping with the ten virtues,
May they establish the beings of the three realms in happiness.
He upon whose crown has fallen the excellent letter
Appointing him worthy to direct the work of this gift of Dharma:
May he too, from birth to birth,
Gain mastery over all the scriptures of the Victor.
Those who correct the language, with an intelligence that distinguishes
The three genders, prefixes and suffixes, agent, act and object,
The three tenses and the cases:
Until awakening, may they serve the supreme teaching.
The images made by those who have reached perfection in the crafts,
The writers, readers and carvers in the art of letters, and the rest,
All who are connected through the work of their body, speech and mind:
May they make manifest the fruit of completing the two accumulations.
May I too, through the chain of births until awakening,
Pure in the three kinds of moral discipline,
Holding the inexhaustible precious treasury of scripture, reasoning and instruction,
Accomplish great benefit for the teaching and for beings.
The rows of volumes of the complete teaching:
Wherever they reside, whoever invites them,
And whoever listens to them, reads them, and makes offerings to them there,
May the truth of virtue protect them in the beginning, the middle and the end.

In short, may beings in general and in particular,
All who are mentioned by name,
In this life and the next,
Never be parted from the Three Jewels, the certain and undeceiving refuge.
May they never be parted from the support for achieving higher rebirth and definite goodness,
The eight freedoms and the ten endowments,
And knowing it to be perishable by nature,
May they make it full of the essence of virtue.
Saying, "You are the very embrace that binds us to existence,"
May they revile attachment to their own aims in a hundred ways,
And may their intention of aspiring to supreme awakening for the sake of others
And of entering upon it become completely pure.
To give relief to the feelings of the destitute and poor,
From the wish-fulfilling tree of a generous hand stretched out,
Unbound by the tether of hoping for return or for karmic reward,
May generosity be brought to perfection.
Free from longing for existence, for the sake of others,
Greeting with a smile, cheeks flushed with longing for awakening,
May the lovely maiden of Kāśī, the restraint of conscientiousness,
Be adorned with the ornaments of the three kinds of moral discipline.
The torments of hardship in study, reflection and meditation,
And the harms inflicted by others, abuse, beatings and the rest,
Like a drawing made on water,
May they leave no wound, and may the vow of patience be firm.
Just as a blazing fire is never sated by firewood,
So the heavier the burden of the conduct of the Victors' heirs,
The more may enthusiasm grow,
And may I take up the diligence to engage in it.
With exceedingly tender compassion for beings,
Trained a hundred times over, through the mind that equalizes
And exchanges my own happiness and virtue with the suffering of others,
May I crush the head of self-cherishing.
Through calm abiding free of the thorns of conceiving signs,
And insight that realizes the inexpressible abiding nature,
In a one-pointed yoga where they are never apart,
May I tear out self-grasping by the root.
With armies skilled in various means,
The weapon that discerns the selflessness of all phenomena,
Merely by being brandished, destroys the great enemy, the afflictions:
May wisdom be brought to perfection.
From the palms of hands that give relief to the needy,
Red as though smeared with the dye of love,
By gifts of material things bestowed in abundance,
May I gather a great many people as my circle.
Gazing on them with a pleasing white smile of joy at seeing them,
And speaking sweetly in conversation of the benefits of Dharma and liberation,
May I arouse in all of them
Enthusiasm for the side of virtue.
Praising and heartening the discouraged, arousing weariness in the excited,
Enticing the dull with wondrous tales,
Through purposeful conduct, in accord with the inclinations of each,
May I join them well to the Dharma that suits them.
To make firm the virtuous minds
Of persons who have entered the path, I myself
By consistent conduct, striving in generosity and the rest,
May I bring sentient beings to full maturity.
The essence churned from the ocean of the two accumulations over three countless eons,
The grounds and paths, and the lord of the stars, the moon,
Full in every part with the qualities of the result:
May it triumph over all the darkness of the Five-Arrowed One (Kāma).
In the midst of countless assemblies whose two obscurations are exhausted,
The glory of the enjoyment body, union of the two bodies,
The great drum of Dharma that teaches the union of the two truths:
May the culmination of the two aims become manifest.
Just as from a single moon, in as many vessels of water as there are,
So many reflections appear without hindrance,
So from that very one may the supreme emanation body
Effortlessly appear in all the worlds.
The inexhaustible qualities of the three secrets,
Like the wish-fulfilling jewel, the excellent vase and the wish-fulfilling tree,
For disciples however many, superior, inferior and middling,
May they fill to overflowing the chariot of their hopes.
The drum of the saying, "In the three worlds there is no refuge,
Certain and undeceiving, like the Three Jewels,"
Beaten resoundingly by the clarity of the declaration of truth:
May it make my aspiration weighty with its essence.

In this place, the vast mansion of our devotion, sprinkled everywhere with the scented water of faith, In the midst of the gods of the white side who, holding precious parasols and victory banners, make offerings with scattered flowers, May the Victors and their heirs, voices joined, play a hundred thousand sweet flutes of svaste jaya, So that the stainless teaching long remains, and all the auspiciousness of happiness protects the world. Passing from the divine palace of Tuṣita to make this Jambudvīpa rich with the sublime essence, Entering the womb, born like a lotus, enjoying youthful sports, surrounded by a retinue of queens, Renouncing, practicing great austerities, attaining supreme awakening, victorious over Māra, teaching the excellent Dharma: By the sublime auspiciousness that pervades the three levels with the light of Dharma, make here a landing place of good fortune. In the boundless fields of the ten directions, without limit or end, the Sugatas of the ten powers, From the smile of their lips, red lotus petals, pour forth the voice of Brahmā together with the song of bees; By the feast of the nectar of the well-spoken, profound and vast, suited to the inclinations of every realm of beings, And by the sublime auspiciousness of erecting the life-pillar of liberation, may all creatures be happy. That the fruit of what I have thus spoken will surely not deceive is not because I have attained vajra speech,

Nor is it that I have gained the weapon of speech of the immortal sages whose words of truth come to pass;
Rather, following the ocean of aspirations of the conduct of the Victor's heirs of old, Mañjuśrī and the rest,
I ripen these roots of virtue into great awakening: may whatever I have aspired to never be in vain.

Thus there is this garland of volumes, two hundred in number and exceeding that by the number of the auspicious signs, called The Wondrous Display, the Master Copy of the Unceasing Dharma Gift of the Translated Treatises that Explain, Without Exception, the Intent of the General and Particular Teachings of the Great Omniscient One, Kinsman of the Sun: a Vast Gathering of the Cloud-Mass of Merit of the Perfect Age.

It came about in this way. There is the great being, the regent who received the nectar of prophecy from the excellent vase of the throat of the Blessed One, the Lord of Sages; the great one who in wisdom is indistinguishable from the Victor himself, renowned in every direction as the glorious Vajradhara Kunga Zangpo. Upon the high throne of that very one, its footstool upheld by fearless five-faced lords stretching forth, he placed his pair of feet marked with wheels; and with the wheel of his pure white activity he seems to measure the limits of the path of the immortals. If one speaks his name, he is the Great Abbot Mañjughoṣa Tashi Lhündrub Drakpé Gyaltsen Palzangpo. From him came a command, prefaced by its immeasurable benefits, such as that if these great commentaries on the intent of the complete teaching were carved into printing blocks, it would become a cause for the precious teaching of the Victor to remain long in the world.

In reliance on that command, there is one whose secret drum of fame pervades the whole circle of the horizons: the great lord of the land of Derge, known as Tenpa Tsering. Among the lands most highly praised as the great region where the stainless tradition of the Omniscient Ikṣvāku has fully spread, he holds sway over most of the earthly sphere known as Great Tibet, with a wheel of activity and power in accord with the Dharma system of the ten virtues; and in particular, by the decree of the great Emperor of the East, appointed by Heaven, he was enthroned as the general lord of the beings of the land of Great Tibet. Among all the ways of conduct of the noble and excellent that are worthy of praise, he held in his heart, as the excellent jewel of scripture and reasoning, that to accomplish the guru's command just as it is given is the very highest. He promised to accomplish it exactly as the excellent Great Abbot had commanded, and he began the work on an auspicious day of a month of the fire snake year called Piṅgala.

From then on, the dominant condition that brought this work to completion, in its beginning, middle and end, through a burden borne with marvelous courage, is his stainless son. When he was enthroned, with inconceivable varieties of offerings from gods and humans, as victorious over all directions, upon the high throne that guards as one the jewel heap of the merit and the fortune of all the beings of the land of Great Tibet, he became widely renowned as the Derge Lama Kunga Trinlé Gyatsö Dé, whose thousand-spoked wheel of activity in Dharma and in government travels to the ends of the directions. In the broad womb of the Gold-Bearing Earth, the beloved one of his karmic power and immeasurable merit, he climbed to the peak of supreme delight in taking up the whole burden of this great ocean of good deeds that no one before had been able to create, and he steered the activity of accomplishing his wishes just as they were intended.

At the head of the workers were the treasurer Guru Tashi, the steward Adrö, the monk Tsultrim Rabten, and the master of the printing blocks Zungkyab; and the proofreader, myself, together with my assistants Muksang Jinpa Gyatso, the Polu secretary Lodrö Gyaltsen, the Chakra geshé Tenzin Lhündrub, and Dzomtokpa Orgyen Tashi. Having compared the text carefully against some three authoritative master copies, extensive and abridged, and having proofread it with great diligence, we gathered a little over eighty scribes from the upper and lower regions and about five hundred carvers. On an auspicious day of a month of the newly dawned year of the male water dog called Dundubhi, the work was completed well at the great kingdom of glorious Derge Lhündrubteng, the great seat of more learned and accomplished great beings than can be counted, who have come, who are coming, and who will come. May it spread and flourish in all directions!

This verse of dedication and aspiration on the full completion of this master copy of the unceasing Dharma gift of the translated treatises that explain, without exception, the intent of the general and particular teachings of the Great Omniscient One, Kinsman of the Sun, a vast gathering of the cloud-mass of merit of the perfect age, is called A Distant Song Summoning the Dear Friend of the Virtue and Excellence of Ground, Path and Fruit. It is set down together with a merely brief printing colophon. It was spoken by the proofreader, the monk Tsultrim Rinchen, that idler who is also called, by a name aspiring to qualities that may yet arise, Ngakgi Wangpo Jikdral Choklé Nampar Gyalwé Lha Yangchen Nyempé Langtsö Dé, as a young bee might vie with the soaring flight of a garuḍa. May virtue and excellence increase!

It is said once more:
The river that falls with a sweet sound, murmuring lhung lhung,
If it is not bound by the curse of a wrathful sage,
Fulfills the hopes of the fortunate Charioteer;
That cleanser of stains, the River of Three Paths, is a marvel.

Translator's notes

The two romanized lines are a Sanskrit version, irregularly spelled, of the two Tibetan verses that follow each of them. In the second Tibetan verse, pos ba is read as mos pa, "aspiration," which matches adhimukti in the Sanskrit line. Ma mo in the stanza on the king is read as master copies, that is, carved printing blocks, not as mātṛkā goddesses; the passage praises a ruler who had the translated commentaries carved for printing. The "maker of all the world" is the creator god of Indian myth, Brahmā, said to have fashioned the Sahā world. The passage ends in mid-sentence; it continues in the next piece. The syllables ཏསྲུ་རས in the stanza on Kunga Zangpo make no sense as written; the line is rendered by its evident sense, "in the speech of the Omniscient One." Several stanzas praise a ruler and a hierarch of the Sakya tradition in ornate kāvya style, and some phrases, such as "ocean of the activity of the Victor" and "ocean of good qualities," may conceal personal names; they are translated by their meaning. Kunga Zangpo is probably Ngorchen Kunga Zangpo, founder of the Ngor tradition. "Sapphire drops" reads ཨནྡྷ་རྙིལ as a form of indranīla, sapphire. In the first line, འཛུམ ("smile") is read as ཟུམ ("close"), since "may his eyes never smile" makes no sense here. In the stanza on the Buddha's descent from Tuṣita, ཕྱི་བའི་བེེད is unclear as written; it is rendered by its likely sense, "to make rich." In the following stanza a syllable is missing before རྩི; the evident reading is bdud rtsi, "nectar." The verses on generosity through wisdom follow the six perfections, and the four that follow the four means of attraction. This piece is the printing colophon of the Derge Tengyur. The two hundred volumes "exceeding that by the auspicious signs" is taken as two hundred plus eight (the eight auspicious signs). Piṅgala (fire snake) corresponds to 1737 and Dundubhi (male water dog) to 1742. "Kinsman of the Sun" (Ādityabandhu) and "the Omniscient Ikṣvāku" (literally "Sugarcane One") are epithets of the Buddha. "Five-faced lords" are lions supporting the throne; "the path of the immortals" is the sky. The final verse alludes to the Ganges (Tripathagā, "River of Three Paths"), brought down by Bhagīratha (here "the fortunate Charioteer") and once swallowed by the angry sage Jahnu. In "Lodrö Gyaltsen" the text adds a stray syllable "ske", left untranslated. In the first line, སྟོར་བ is read loosely as "wield/gain".
