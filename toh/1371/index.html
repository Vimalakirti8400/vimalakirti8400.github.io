About this text

Illuminating the Essence of the Three Yogas, whose Sanskrit title appears in the Tibetan as Trijogi Hṛda Byākāśa, roughly Triyogahṛdayaprakāśa, and whose Tibetan title is Rnal 'byor gsum gyi snying po gsal ba, is a brief verse treatise on tantric meditation. It sets out the meditation of the Great Seal through three yogas, of blessing, of conceptual meditation, and of the natural state, and then gives a compact practical guide to the six-branched yoga, from withdrawal and the visionary signs such as smoke, mirage, fireflies, and lamp, through breath control, retention, and the kindling of the inner fire at the navel, to the great bliss and the attainment of the grounds and full awakening. Its colophon attributes it to the bodhisattva Mañjuśrī in his birth as King Yaśas, linking it to the Kālacakra tradition, and records its translation by the paṇḍita Somanātha and the translator Dro Sherab Drak. It is Toh 1371 in the Tengyur, where it belongs among the commentaries and treatises on the tantras.

Translation

In the language of India: trijogi hṛda byākāśa nāma. In the language of Tibet: Illuminating the Essence of the Three Yogas.

Homage to Mañjughoṣa.

With oṃ, appearance is ascertained as the path.
Having paid homage to Vajrasattva,
I shall explain the path of the thoroughly trained,
The meditation of the Great Seal.

To one ripened by empowerment, who keeps the commitments,
To such a one the three yogas are taught,
And never to anyone else.
Blessing, meditation with conception,
And the natural: these are fixed as the three yogas.

Having come to know the very signs of death,
One should settle firmly in the drop of life.

Withdrawal and meditative concentration,
Breath control, and likewise retention,
Recollection and absorption:
These are called the six branches of yoga.

At night, in a house without any openings,
The practitioner, in deep equipoise,
Should learn from the guru's own mouth
The five operations and the two bindings.

Beyond existence and nonexistence,
Smoke, mirage, fireflies,
And a lamp are seen by the mind in the sky.
As a young maiden, in a divination,
Sees the thief who has stayed hidden,
So with the eye that is limited
The practitioner goes on and sees.

When the sky has become clear,
Free of clouds,
In the morning, with the sun behind one,
Or likewise in the afternoon;
Avoid midday, for it harms the eyes.

Arisen from space, self-arisen,
Great fire of wisdom and gnosis,
Great light that makes all things illuminated,
Blazing light of gnosis,
Lamp of beings, torch of gnosis,
Great splendor, clear light,
Supreme mantra, lord of mantras, king of vidyā-mantras,
King of secret mantras, accomplisher of the great aim.

When within the drop
A black line the breadth of a hair
Is seen, the state of clear light is reached.
Through the equipoise of withdrawal
That the yogin has attained,
All mantras are blessed.

Wisdom, examination and analysis,
Joy and bliss, unmoving:
Through the five limbs of meditative concentration made firm,
You attain the five superknowledges, O lord of men!

The middle flow of breath is supreme,
Fully joined with the four channels,
Of feces and urine, and of moon and sun.
The breath whose nature is the five gnoses,
The essential nature of the five elements,
Blessed by the five buddhas,
Is explained as what is called breath control.

Through the power of constant retention,
Vajrasattva truly enters.
By kindling the fierce one (caṇḍālī) at the navel,
The five tathāgatas are burned,
And Locanā and the rest are burned as well;
From the burning of haṃ, the stream of the moon descends.

The commitment being, within one's own domain:
Having seen that body, day after day,
At the proper times, the yogin who desires liberation
Should fill the life winds
Through the practice of the vase.

Until one comes to see
A wheel of light rays, ever turning,
Encircling one's own body all around,
In the body spoken of before, it is one's own.
Through this, in six months, the bliss that is free of touch,
Shining brightly like the sun in water,
Stainless, of various colors,
Is in every aspect one's own mind;
One's own mind itself is bliss itself.

For the yogin of the samādhi of great bliss,
The five eyes and the six superknowledges arise.
In the houses, Makara and the rest,
By reversing the order of ignorance and the rest,
The twelve grounds are attained;
And for one whose nature is free of conceptuality,
In a single instant there is full awakening.

Vajra Sun, great illumination,
Vajra Moon, stainless light:
The full completion of the grounds
Is taught as these two realities.
Because gnosis is present,
He is called the protector who possesses gnosis.

Through nonreferential compassion,
Through the net of illusion, awakening.
Like a wish-fulfilling jewel,
Fulfilling the hopes of infinite beings,
The great bliss that liberates beings
Dispels the desire that leaks away.
May the supreme path that dispels
The suffering of the six classes of beings be sought and found!

Illuminating the Essence of the Three Yogas, composed by the bodhisattva Mañjuśrī, who, with his disciples in mind, took birth as King Yaśas, is complete.

It was translated by the paṇḍita Somanātha and the translator Dro Sherab Drak.

Translator's notes

In the simile of the maiden, sngа las is read as referring to a mirror or prognostic divination (Skt. prasenā), in which a young girl sees what is hidden; the following couplet, "with the eye that is limited the practitioner goes on and sees," is terse and its exact sense is uncertain. In "fulfilling the hopes," re skor is read as re skong. The line "Through the net of illusion, awakening" is one syllable short in the Tibetan and is rendered as it stands. The eight-line passage beginning "Arisen from space" corresponds to verses of the Mañjuśrīnāmasaṃgīti. King Yaśas refers to the Kalkī king of Shambhala in the Kālacakra tradition, regarded as an emanation of Mañjuśrī.
