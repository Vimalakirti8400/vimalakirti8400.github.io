About this text

Toh 227 in the Kangyur is a Mahāyāna sūtra known in English as The Noble Sūtra of the Great Vehicle Called the Compendium of All Knowledge, in Sanskrit as the Ārya Sarva Vidyā Saṃgraha Nāma Mahāyāna Sūtra, and in Tibetan as Phags pa rnam par 'thag pa. It belongs to the sūtra division of the Kangyur and presents itself as a discourse delivered by the Buddha on Vulture Peak Mountain at Rājagṛha, set dramatically at the time of his approaching final nirvāṇa, in the presence of a vast assembly of monks, bodhisattvas, gods, and laypeople. As its title suggests, the text is concerned with the gathering or synthesis of knowledge, situating this theme within the solemn context of the Buddha's last rains retreat and his imminent passing.

In the Indian language: Ārya Sarva Vidyā Saṃgraha Nāma Mahāyāna Sūtra.

In the Tibetan language: The Noble Sūtra of the Great Vehicle Called the Compendium of All Knowledge.

Homage to all buddhas and bodhisattvas.

Thus did I hear. At one time the Blessed One was dwelling on Vulture Peak Mountain at Rājagṛha, together with a great Saṅgha of sixty-two thousand monks, with eight hundred million bodhisattvas, with sixty thousand gods, and with many thousands of laymen. At that time the hour of the Blessed One's final passing into nirvāṇa had arrived. This was the last occasion on which the Blessed One spent the rains retreat and dwelt at Rājagṛha.

Then the Blessed One entered into a meditative absorption of such a kind that this great trichiliocosm — the world system of the triple thousandfold universe — became adorned with silken streamers and banners, adorned with bowls of perfume and filled with fragrant incense, overflowing with scattered blossoms, and covered over with lotuses bearing a hundred thousand petals each. Into such a meditative absorption he settled.

Within this great trichiliocosm, however many Brahmās (Skt. Brahmā) there were in number — each one alone attended by retinues of tens of millions of followers — all of them proceeded to where the Blessed One was, approached him, bowed their heads to his feet, joined their palms together, paid homage to the Blessed One, and took their places to one side.

Likewise, all the lords of the sons of the gods of the Pure Abodes, all the lords who are sovereign sons of the gods, all the great lords, all the lords of the gods, all the lords of the nāgas, all the lords of the yakṣas, all the lords of the asuras, all the lords of the garuḍas (Skt. garuḍa), all the lords of the kinnaras (Skt. kinnara), and all the lords of the great mahoragas (Skt. mahoraga) — however many there were in this great trichiliocosm — each attended by retinues of many hundreds of thousands of millions, all proceeded to where the Blessed One was, approached him, bowed their heads to his feet, joined their palms together, paid homage to the Blessed One, and took their places to one side.

In the world systems of the ten directions, as numerous as the grains of sand in the Ganges River, however many there were of the great bodhisattvas (Skt. mahāsattva) who are of great renown and great power — all of them likewise proceeded to where the Blessed One was, approached him, bowed their heads to his feet, joined their palms together, paid homage to the Blessed One, and took their places to one side.

At that time, in this great trichiliocosm — from the summit of existence down — not even a space the breadth of the tip of a staff remained that was not filled with powerful and renowned beings: gods, nāgas, yakṣas, gandharvas (Skt. gandharva), asuras, garuḍas, kinnaras, and great mahoragas.

Then the Blessed One, while abiding in knowing awareness, arose from that meditative absorption. Having arisen, he gazed upon the assembly three times. Having gazed upon the assembly, he performed a great lion's roar three times. Having performed the lion's roar, he extended his wondrous tongue three times. Having extended his wondrous tongue three times, he caused the great trichiliocosm to be pervaded by that wondrous tongue three times.

Having thus pervaded the great trichiliocosm with his wondrous tongue three times, and having pervaded it with that miraculous display, he gazed out once more upon the assembly.

Then the entire retinue rose from their seats, joined their palms together, paid homage to the Blessed One himself, and sat back down.

The Blessed One then spoke to the bodhisattva, the great being, Maitreya: "Unconquerable One, the Tathāgata will pass into complete nirvāṇa before long. This is now the time for you to ask the Tathāgata about the Dharma. This is the occasion. Those among you who have any doubt about the Dharma — if you do not ask the Tathāgata now, you may later come to regret it, thinking: 'How could we have failed to ask while the Tathāgata was present in person?'"

When he had spoken these words, the bodhisattva, the great being, Maitreya addressed the Blessed One thus: "Blessed One, you yourself are the wise one. Blessed One, you yourself know the time, know the occasion, know what is fitting. Blessed One, you who know all and see all, who have penetrated and understood all phenomena — please teach the Dharma in such a way that the manner of the Dharma of all perfectly and completely awakened buddhas may long endure. Please teach the Dharma well in just such a way."

Then the divine son Maheśvara (the great lord among the gods of the Pure Abodes), accompanied by a retinue of eight hundred million divine sons of that same divine realm, bowed his head at the feet of the Blessed One, joined his palms together, and addressed the Blessed One thus: "Blessed One, this Dharma teaching called 'The Compendium of All Weavings' — when the previous Tathāgatas, arhats, perfectly and completely awakened buddhas proclaimed it of old, it brought healing to many beings and happiness to many beings. For the sake of that same teaching of the Tathāgata enduring long, may the Blessed One likewise teach it."

Having made this request, the Blessed One remained silent and by that silence gave his consent to the divine son Maheśvara.

The divine son Maheśvara, understanding from the Blessed One's silence that consent had been granted, withdrew to one side.

Then the Blessed One addressed the bodhisattva, the great being, Maitreya, and declared: "Unconquerable One, any Tathāgata, arhat, perfectly and completely awakened buddha of the past who did not teach this Dharma teaching — the sūtra called 'The Compendium of All Weavings' — is no longer present. And in the future, any Tathāgata who shall arise and not teach this Dharma teaching will simply not arise. Why is this? Because one who teaches the Dharma without having trained in this teaching slanders the Tathāgata, abandons the Dharma, and disparages the Saṅgha. Whoever has gone astray with respect to the Three Jewels — that person I declare to be one bound for the lower realms, bound for hell.

"Therefore, Maitreya, those sons and daughters of good family who have genuinely entered upon awakening and who wish to have no confusion regarding me — they should first thoroughly understand and internalize this Dharma teaching, and only then teach the Dharma. Maitreya, in the time to come, the gathering together of this sacred Dharma is your task — be diligent in this."

The bodhisattva, the great being, Maitreya replied to the Blessed One: "So be it, Blessed One. I shall listen in accordance with the Blessed One's word."

Then once again the Blessed One called out to the bodhisattva, the great being, Maitreya and declared: "Unconquerable One, on the very night when the Tathāgata achieved manifest and complete unsurpassable perfect awakening,

Maitreya, from that very night when the Tathāgata attained manifest and complete unsurpassable perfect awakening, until the night when the Tathāgata passes into nirvāṇa without remainder — was there anything whatsoever in which the Tathāgata erred, or babbled, or spoke carelessly, or spoke with ill intent, or acted inappropriately in his enlightened activity?"

Maitreya replied: "Blessed One, there was not."

The Blessed One declared: "Unconquerable One, everything whatsoever that the Tathāgata spoke — from the night when the Tathāgata manifestly and completely awakened to unsurpassable perfect awakening, up to the night when the Tathāgata passes into nirvāṇa without remainder — all of that is true, real, just as it is, unerring, and nothing other than what it is. In this regard, certain foolish people who neither know nor have understood the Tathāgata's skillful means take to carping at my plain words, saying: 'This is admissible,' and 'This is inadmissible.' Such as these are those who reject the sacred Dharma. Such as these are those who slander the Tathāgata. For that reason I declare them to be hell-beings.

Unconquerable One, in the future, in the last era of the final five hundred years to come, there will arise monks, nuns, laymen, and laywomen who claim to be bodhisattvas, who were formerly tīrthikas (non-Buddhists), who by the power of their aspirations have gone forth into the well-proclaimed Dharma and Vinaya of the Tathāgata's teaching, who dwell in one-sided conduct, who are harmful to their companions, who are attached to gain and honor, who are miserly toward their households, who disparage their households, who are ugly in appearance, who delight in inferior things, whose counsel brings no ease, who are obscure, who are of low birth, who are utterly attached to material goods, who are entirely bereft of all the food of dwelling in ease through the Dharma, who are very far removed and completely distant from the food of meditative absorption — those who dwell in deceit and duplicity, who say one thing and do another. Though knowing nothing, they say: "We are possessed of knowledge." Though utterly without learning, they say: "We are learned." They say: "Dwelling in equanimity toward all sentient beings, through great compassion and skillful means, resting in the unforgetful power of the Tathāgata, we teach awakening to all sentient beings in accordance with their dispositions." Some of those same people then say: "One should not train in those sūtras taught for śrāvakas. One should not listen to them. They are not our Dharma — they belong to the śrāvakas, and so bodhisattvas should not train in them. One should not train in those sūtras taught for pratyekabuddhas. One should not listen to them. They are not our Dharma — they belong to the pratyekabuddhas, and so bodhisattvas should not train in them." Such words will arise.

Unconquerable One, as for me — I have taught the Dharma to the gods and tamed them in accordance with their dispositions. In the same way I have taught the Dharma to nāgas, yakṣas, asuras, garuḍas, kiṃnaras, great serpents, gandharvas, and human beings, and tamed each of them in accordance with their dispositions. Unconquerable One, were I to travel to world-realms in the ten directions as numerous as the grains of sand of the Ganges for the sake of benefiting sentient beings, and there too teach the Dharma to sentient beings in accordance with their dispositions — even then those people who claim to be bodhisattvas, those wild and arrogant so-called bodhisattvas, those who slander bodhisattvas,

those who speak falsely, those of little learning — these individuals, without being properly distinguished by my two awakenings, speak of this as two things: "In this a bodhisattva should train; in this a bodhisattva should not train." With that mind they denigrate the Tathāgata, they abandon the Dharma, and they disparage the Saṅgha. When their bodies perish, they will be reborn in the hells. There, after many aeons and many hundreds of aeons have passed, they will attain liberation — and then they will be reborn in a lineage of poverty. When in a hundred times and a hundred more times they would have received a prophecy of awakening, it will instead be that in the age of the five degenerations they will receive that prophecy. For example, it is just as I myself, having long postponed it through delay, finally achieved perfect and complete awakening in this degenerate age. Those who listen to the words of those people, or act as they do, or think to trust them — they too will go along in just that way: drawn by attachment to evil companions, they will proceed in exactly that direction.

Unconquerable One, in the distant past, in a time beyond innumerable and ever more innumerable aeons, there appeared in this world a Tathāgata, a Worthy One, a fully and perfectly awakened buddha whose name was Renowned Arising from the Rays of Stainless Light. He taught the Dharma to sentient beings for eighty trillion years. Among his scriptural collections there arose a monk named Completely Pure in Livelihood — a Dharma teacher, a speaker of varied discourses, one who correctly taught the multitude, who properly led the multitude to take things up, who properly praised the multitude, and who properly gladdened the multitude. Then the Tathāgata Renowned Arising from the Rays of Stainless Light entrusted to him the preservation of the sacred Dharma in the final time, in the final age. After the parinirvāṇa of that Tathāgata, for ten million years Completely Pure in Livelihood maintained the form of the sacred Dharma. Entering into this Dharma category that gathers together all the weaving, he taught the Dharma, teaching sentient beings in accordance with each one's disposition. He gladdened sentient beings throughout eight hundred thousand cities in accordance with his aspirational prayers, and then he went to the great city called Excellent, where he taught the Dharma in accordance with the dispositions of eighty trillion households, and from that city he established eighty trillion living beings in firm faith. Of those, one trillion were set on the path to unsurpassed awakening. Seventy-nine trillion sentient beings were set on the path of the śrāvakas and tamed.

After that, the monk Completely Pure in Livelihood, together with ten thousand monks, traveled about going to the place of the heart of awakening. Unconquerable One, not long after Completely Pure in Livelihood had departed, there came to that great city Excellent a monk named Dharma, who grasped one thousand sūtras of the weaving. He also attained the four meditative concentrations. As for Completely Pure in Livelihood, he grasped ten trillion sūtra collections and sixty thousand sūtras of the weaving.

Now the monk Dharma, teaching the Dharma in that great city Excellent by means of only one approach, did gladden the people, yet he was not skilled in teaching the Dharma in accordance with each person's individual disposition. He set forth emptiness to those people with the pronouncement "all phenomena are empty," following after emptiness and continuing to expound by means of emptiness, saying: "This teaching of mine that all phenomena are empty is the authentic word of the Tathāgata alone. What Completely Pure in Livelihood has said is flawed."

"If that monk does not have completely pure livelihood, why is that monk called by the name Completely Pure in Livelihood? His livelihood is not completely pure. Why is that? Because he accepts and receives flowers and makes full use of them. He accepts and receives perfumes and unguents and makes full use of them. He is not one who knows; he is a childish person. I have practiced the holy life for a long time, whereas he is newly ordained — he has not been long since his full ordination, so do not place such trust in him." That monk was indeed heedless, and since the people had deep faith in the monk Completely Pure in Livelihood, when that monk tried to create division among those people, he was unable to divide them.

Then the monk Dharma came to the time of his death, and upon dying, because he had slandered the Tathāgata in such a manner and had abandoned the Dharma in such a manner, he was born in the hells for seventy aeons. When those seventy aeons had passed, for sixty aeons he lost the awakening mind. When those sixty aeons had passed, the Tathāgata Pervading with the Light of Precious Fragrance caused him to generate the mind set upon awakening in that Tathāgata's scriptural collection, and he generated the mind set upon awakening. For nine hundred lifetimes he was born in the animal realm. For six hundred lifetimes he was born among destitute human beings, and throughout all of those he was born without a tongue.

The monk Completely Pure in Livelihood taught the Dharma in the presence of five hundred trillion buddhas, and throughout all of those he was endowed with the five superknowledges. He was worthy of being invited by those blessed buddha-bhagavāns. He also came to teach the Dharma with complete purity in all phenomena. O Ajita, if you think that at that time and in that age the monk called Completely Pure in Livelihood was someone else, you should not view it that way. The one called the Tathāgata Amitābha — at that time and in that age he was the Dharma-teaching monk called Completely Pure in Livelihood.

O Ajita, if you think that at that time and in that age the monk called Dharma was someone else, you should not view it that way. At that time and in that age I myself was the monk called Dharma. Such improper conduct as that arose from none other than myself.

O Ajita, the karmic obscuration that causes one to be impoverished of Dharma is difficult to know in just this way. Through the karmic maturation of that action, I have attained full and perfect awakening in the age of the five degenerations. O Ajita, those bodhisattvas who create division and teach the Dharma — through the karmic maturation of that action they will attain full and perfect awakening in the age of degeneration, yet in their buddha-fields, while awakening and teaching the Dharma, many māras and obstacles will arise for them.

Then the entire retinue shed tears and wept, and cried out these words: "From this day forward, may no one teach the Dharma in the manner that the monk Dharma spoke!" At that moment the great bodhisattvas gathered in that retinue also rose from their seats and prostrated themselves on the ground. Having prostrated themselves on the ground, they wept.

Then the Blessed One, fully knowing, addressed those bodhisattvas with these words: "Noble sons, why do you prostrate yourselves on the ground and weep?" They replied: "Blessed One, we ourselves have seen that we are endowed with that very karmic obscuration." The Blessed One declared: "Noble sons, that is so. After the Tathāgata Dīpaṃkara (Lamp-Maker) had passed into nirvāṇa, there appeared in his scriptural collection a Dharma-teacher named Accumulated Wisdom, and you reviled him,

"From that time forward you never saw a buddha. You never found the awakening mind. You found neither dhāraṇī nor meditative absorption. Noble sons, although you have entered rightly upon my awakening, in this good aeon, at the time of the last of the Tathāgatas to appear, you will attain forbearance with respect to the non-arising of phenomena. After that, beyond three incalculable aeons, you will fully and perfectly awaken to unsurpassed, complete, and perfect awakening.

Noble sons, therefore, when those bodhisattvas see a bodhisattva, they should not think otherwise in their minds. They should think: 'We are seeing a stūpa, we are seeing a Tathāgata. A buddha is present before us.' Noble sons, if a bodhisattva sees a bodhisattva and gives rise to any perception other than the perception of a stūpa or the perception of a Tathāgata, that bodhisattva inflicts a wound upon himself.

With this in mind, the Tathāgata Akṣobhya (the Unshakeable) took this solemn vow: 'If I see a bodhisattva who has first aroused the awakening mind and do not give rise to the perception of a buddha, may I thereby have deceived all the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, dwelling in the innumerable world-systems.'

Noble sons, in the future, in the last five-hundred-year period, all those who come to obtain dhāraṇī or to obtain meditative absorption will obtain them through the power of the Tathāgata. Noble sons, therefore, whoever speaks ill of a Dharma-teacher in this context will have abandoned the Tathāgata. Noble sons, whoever teaches the Dharma of the Tathāgata to sentient beings in accordance with each being's own disposition — all of that is the power of the Tathāgata.

And in any assembly where those who follow the bodhisattva vehicle cause their hair to stand on end even a few times or shed tears even a few times while reading — all of that too should be understood as the power of the Tathāgata. Yet there are those foolish persons who, though not bodhisattvas themselves, claim to be bodhisattvas; who malign bodhisattvas; who steal the Dharma; who put on a false pretense; who act by bad ritual means. Some of them say things like: 'What does he know? What can such a one possibly understand?'

Maitreya, when I was practicing for the sake of awakening, the verses I purchased I purchased by giving up my own body entirely, by giving up my wife entirely, by giving up my sons and daughters entirely, by giving up my kingdom entirely. Maitreya, if I recollect that for the sake of a single well-spoken verse I gave away Jambudvīpa and purchased it — those people, while protecting gain and honor, do not think of approaching anyone equal to a buddha.

Ajita (the Unconquered One, Maitreya), the grasping of this mode of the holy Dharma belongs to those endowed with means, and not to those who lack them. Ajita, after I have fully and perfectly awakened, when I teach Dharma through the wisdom of a buddha, if those beings say of it, 'That should not be accepted as scripture,' — consider how great a serious fault those beings thereby incur, having gone to a wrong birth!

Ajita, a monk who, by means of that Dharma, teaches a thousand sūtras of analysis, and who cultivates the four meditative concentrations, yet comes to such a pass — what need is there to speak of one who merely makes contact with a partial or proportional element of it, or who, having grasped analysis, is displeased with monks who hold to analysis and finds fault with analysis itself? Such beings denigrate Dharma-teaching monks, abandon the Dharma of the buddhas, and denigrate with great arrogance. That they are reborn in the hells — I do not call that well done. And why is that?"

Those who denigrate Dharma-teaching monks denigrate the buddhas; they denigrate the Dharma. Those who show no respect to Dharma-teaching monks show no respect to the buddhas. Those who speak ill of Dharma-teaching monks do not wish to see the buddhas; they speak ill of the buddhas. Those who generate a mind of anger toward a bodhisattva who has only just first aroused the awakening mind have thereby abandoned the buddhas, and they must be prepared to dwell in hell for as many eons as the moments of anger they generate. For as long as they look upon another with eyes of anger, they will be reborn in a blind sense base. For as long as they speak ill of another, they will be reborn without the sense base of a tongue.

Ajita, I do not see any other Dharma like this — where arousing a mind that harms bodhisattvas causes the bodhisattva themselves to fall, plunging downward to a lower destiny. Ajita, for those who abide in the perception of sentient beings and the perception of phenomena, there is the accumulation of karma; but for those who abide in non-apprehension, there is none.

Bodhisattvas who differentiate Dharma into separate divisions and teach it in that way abide in conceptual elaboration.

Ajita, since the perfect accomplishment of the six perfections by bodhisattvas is for the sake of awakening, those very foolish ones who say, "A bodhisattva need train only in the Perfection of Wisdom," and then disparage the remaining perfections — what can one say of them?

Ajita, what do you think? Is the king of Kāśika who gave away his head an instance of mistaken wisdom?

He replied: "Blessed One, it is not."

The Blessed One declared: Ajita, the sūtras I have taught that are endowed with the six perfections — will they not lead to attaining awakening?

He replied: "Blessed One, they will lead to attaining awakening."

The Blessed One declared: Ajita, if you practice the perfection of generosity for sixty eons, practice the perfection of moral discipline for sixty eons, practice the perfection of patience for sixty eons, practice the perfection of diligence for sixty eons, and practice the perfection of meditative concentration for sixty eons — and then those foolish ones say, "One attains awakening through the single method of non-apprehension alone" — they are of impure conduct. They are content with mere words and do not make accomplishment their core. They are diligent in the activity of speech but far from earnest effort. They have become jealous, they dwell in attachment, and they are troublesome to their kin.

Ajita, I myself did not attain the sovereignty of a wheel-turning king merely through being pleased at giving away my head. Yet those foolish ones, being miserly with material goods, go into the homes of others and say: "This is how one practises toward awakening. This is a bodhisattva's conduct; this is the proper way. Anything else is not the way. Most Dharma-teaching monks also know this rule of Dharma." They say such things, and out of that they generate much ill-will toward Dharma-teaching monks, all for the sake of gain and honor.

Ajita, for those people who make their living in this way, I would not say that even in a hundred eons would they attain even a compatible patience — let alone awakening. Ajita, for one endowed with deceitfulness there is no awakening. For the jealous there is none. For those who show no respect there is none. For those without right understanding there is none. For those who trouble households there is none. For those who engage in duplicity there is none.

Those foolish, unconquered ones boast that they are even wiser than I, and say that whatever teachings belong to the śrāvakas or to the pratyekabuddhas are teachings not spoken by the Tathāgata.

Then the Blessed One spoke to the venerable Subhūti. "Subhūti, the Perfection of Wisdom does indeed exist for one who practices non-duality." "Blessed One, it is so." "Subhūti, awakening does indeed exist for one who does not abide." "Blessed One, it is so." "Subhūti, awakening does indeed exist for one free of conceptual elaboration." "Blessed One, it is so."

"Subhūti, consider how much karmic maturation in the form of becoming impoverished those shameless ones — who are shameless because they harm their own kin — intend to fully take on. Furthermore, Ajita, a bodhisattva should not be alarmed thinking that all phenomena are phenomena of the śrāvakas. A bodhisattva should not be alarmed thinking that all phenomena are phenomena of the pratyekabuddhas. A bodhisattva should not be alarmed thinking that all phenomena are phenomena of the buddhas, nor should one cling to that. A bodhisattva should not be alarmed thinking that all phenomena are not phenomena of the śrāvakas. A bodhisattva should not be alarmed thinking that all phenomena are not phenomena of the pratyekabuddhas. A bodhisattva should not be alarmed thinking that all phenomena are not phenomena of the buddhas, nor should one cling to that. One should not be alarmed thinking that all phenomena are phenomena of affliction. One should not be alarmed thinking that all phenomena are phenomena free of affliction. One should not be alarmed thinking that all phenomena are phenomena of purification. One should not be alarmed thinking that all phenomena are phenomena without purification. One should not be alarmed thinking that all phenomena are phenomena with clinging. One should not be alarmed thinking that all phenomena are phenomena without clinging. One should not be alarmed thinking that all phenomena are conditioned phenomena. One should not be alarmed thinking that all phenomena are unconditioned phenomena. One should not be alarmed thinking that all phenomena are new phenomena. One should not be alarmed thinking that all phenomena are not new phenomena. One should not be alarmed thinking that all phenomena are phenomena of existence. One should not be alarmed thinking that all phenomena are phenomena of non-existence. One should not be alarmed thinking that all phenomena are phenomena of mind. One should not be alarmed thinking that all phenomena are phenomena without mind. One should not be alarmed thinking that all phenomena are phenomena of intention. One should not be alarmed thinking that all phenomena are phenomena without intention. One should not be alarmed thinking that all phenomena are phenomena of karma. One should not be alarmed thinking that all phenomena are phenomena without karma. One should not be alarmed thinking that all phenomena are phenomena of karmic maturation. One should not be alarmed thinking that all phenomena are phenomena without karmic maturation.
