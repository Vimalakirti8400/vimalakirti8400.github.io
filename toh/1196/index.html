About this text

This work is A Commentary on the Difficult Points of the King of Tantras, the Ḍākinī Vajra Net, called The Expansion of Reality, known in Sanskrit as the Ḍākinīvajrapañjaratantrarājasya Tattvapauṣṭikapañjikā and in Tibetan as Rgyud kyi rgyal po mkha' 'gro ma rdo rje dra ba'i dka' 'grel de kho na nyid rgyas pa. It is a pañjikā, an explanatory commentary that clarifies difficult passages of a root scripture, here the Ḍākinī Vajra Net tantra, an esoteric Buddhist scripture closely associated with the Hevajra cycle of tantric practice. After paying homage to the compassionate Avalokiteśvara, the author offers verses praising a fierce, sixteen-armed, blue deity adorned with skull cups and dancing with fanged faces, a form that recalls Hevajra, and then bows to his own spiritual teacher before turning to the work of explanation. The text is Toh 1196 in the Tengyur, where it belongs among the Indian commentaries on the tantras.

Translation

In the language of India: ḍākinīvajrajālipañjatantrarājasya tattvapauṣṭīkpañjikā nāma. In the language of Tibet: A Commentary on the Difficult Points of the King of Tantras, the Ḍākinī Vajra Net, called The Expansion of Reality.

Homage to the great compassionate one, the noble Avalokiteśvara.

As the water of a lake stirred by masses of waves is adorned with a garland of geese resting on red lotuses, so are his hands made exceedingly beautiful by a garland of skull cups; blue like a fresh rain cloud, he stands with sixteen hands like a forest of creepers; to him who dances, possessed of fanged faces and bodies, I bow down deeply. Bowing respectfully at the lotus feet of the sublime spiritual friend, who possesses the pure intellect able to realize the system of the sublime Dharma, profound, its depth most hard to fathom, a repository of the jewel of the ultimate, I shall, following the correct sequence, write this commentary on difficult points, making that very text clear, in order to recall reality.

On the passage beginning "Space and": "space" is the nature of the lotus. It is the mudrā of the source of phenomena of the four awarenesses, white in colour, triangular, narrow below and wide above, marked inside with a crossed vajra and a lotus. "Solid" is the earth maṇḍala: yellow in colour, square, marked with a three-pronged vajra, arising from laṃ. "Clear" is the water maṇḍala: white in colour and round, marked with a vase, arising from paṃ. "Without respite" is the wind maṇḍala, so called because it moves in its activity and has no respite: smoke-coloured, shaped like a bow, marked with a banner, arising from yaṃ. "Bright" is the fire maṇḍala, because its essential nature is the blazing light of unsurpassed wisdom: red in colour, triangular, marked with flames, arising from raṃ. "Variegated" is the crossed lotus that lies beneath the four maṇḍalas. "Vajra base" is the crossed vajra, which is the support of all supreme vajras. "Dwelling house" is the multistoried mansion. It comes from the four great elements, melted by the stream of nectar that issued from the pores of Vairocana, who arose from bhrūṃ and a wheel.

"Realm of beings" is that very mansion, because it is completely filled with the emanating hosts of buddhas and bodhisattvas. That same mansion is also called "delightful," because it is fashioned from jewels most excellent and clear. Or else, it is delightful because it is completely filled with sense objects such as forms. By these words the text teaches that one should meditate on the multistoried mansion resting on the crossed vajra and lotus set within the source of phenomena; or else, it teaches the interior of the five palaces. Some, however, say that, following the order of the tantra that comes below, these words set forth the wheel of the maṇḍala of Nairātmyā.

On that reading, "space" is Nairātmyā, who perfectly gathers all the qualities of the buddhas. "Solid" is Pukkasī, the nature of the earth element. "Clear" is Śabarī, the nature of the water element. "Without respite" is Ḍombinī, the nature of the wind element. "Bright" is Caṇḍālī, the nature of the fire element. "Variegated" is the mirror-like wisdom, the transformation of the all-base consciousness that appears as body, enjoyments and abode, and it is Vajrā, the nature of form. "Vajra base" means the support of vajras: the wisdom of the equality of all buddhas, and the inner Gaurī, the nature of feeling. "Dwelling house" is the discriminating wisdom, the remedy in which all conceptual thoughts about the apprehended, whose nature is form and the rest, have dissolved, and it is Pārīḍākinī, the nature of perception. "Realm of beings" is the all-accomplishing wisdom, which tames the various realms of beings to be tamed, and it is Vajraḍākinī, the nature of formations. "Delightful" is that which delights and draws in the wisdom maṇḍala of all the tathāgatas: it is Gaurī, the nature of faith and of the sense base of form. "Beings" means their entering: Caurī, the nature of diligence and of the sense base of sound. "Vessel" means producing as an abode and binding: Vetālī, the nature of mindfulness and of the sense base of smell. "World" means looking upon various objects and bringing delight: Ghasmarī, the nature of meditative absorption and of the sense base of taste.

On "below the earth, upon the earth, above the earth": "below the earth" is she who moves below the earth, the nature of the sense base of touch. "Upon the earth" is the nature of the aggregate of consciousness, which again teaches Nairātmyā herself. "Above the earth" is the sky-goer, the nature of the sense base of phenomena. "Wall" extends up to the words "not moving to the sides but going above and below." By this the wheel of Nairātmyā is taught.

Now I shall explain what was left over above. "The world of beings and the vessel" refers to the transformation of the all-base consciousness. Here, "vessel" is the very multistoried mansion explained earlier, the transformation of what appears as an abode. "World of beings" is the permanent: the nature of the mirror-like wisdom, the transformation of what appears as the bodies and enjoyments of bodhisattvas and others. "Below the earth, upon the earth, above the earth" is the Tongs goddess, because she pulls out the necks of the wicked who dwell below the earth, upon the earth, and above the earth. "Between" is the Noose goddess, because she seeks out the wicked and binds them within. "Mind" is the Cooking goddess, because she seeks out the wicked and they are praised by the true net of the mind of the purified world. "Body" is the Hook goddess, because she pierces the bodies of the wicked and draws them in. "Speech" is the Flower goddess: she is called the Flower goddess because she is the essential nature of the scriptures, which offer a casket of sublime flowers to the limbs of awakening. "One" is the Incense goddess, because she makes all phenomena one through the incense of the undefiled.

"Two" is the Lamp goddess, because the lamp of nondual wisdom, which arises from the oil of compassion, dispels the dimness of duality and makes the qualities of the buddhas appear. "Sky-bliss" is the Unguent goddess, because her nature is to delight in, and to be renowned for, the fragrance of the unsurpassed form that pervades all of space without remainder. These are the deities of the Eternal maṇḍala.

"Six and five" is Vajrasūrya, because he is of the nature of equality, the antidote to the six afflictions such as pride and to the five obscurations. "Six-two" is the Sun-in-Hand goddess, because with the light of twelve suns gathered together she drives back the black darkness of ignorance. "Four" is the Lamp, because with the light of the wisdom of the four yogas it clears away the cataract of conceptuality. "Beings" is the Jewel Lamp goddess, because she destroys the net of darkness that covers beings. "Twenty lacking one" is the Cloud-in-Hand goddess, because with the light of nondual wisdom she clears away the river of nineteen conceptions, namely the eighteen elements together with grasping at a person. "The three realms without exception" is the Graceful goddess, because she shows herself as beyond each and every one of the three realms. "Father" is the Garland goddess, because she frees from bondage by the afflictions and the like, and because she delights in and holds the jewel of the ultimate. "Son" is the Song goddess, because she leads into the city of liberation and protects with a melody that teaches the unsurpassed Dharma. "Mother" is the Dance goddess, because by sustaining the mudrā of dance she frees from the ocean of māras. These are the deities of the Vajrasūrya maṇḍala.

"Outer object" is the Blessed One Padmanarteśvara, the Lord of the Lotus Dance, because, having bound and suppressed conceptions such as that of the grasped object, he is of the nature of discriminating wisdom, which looks upon objects. "And inner" is Padmapāṇi, the Lotus-in-Hand, because through the great passion of great compassion he shows the nature of the lotus, which is called "inner." "And the dharma of all the queens" is the goddess Holding the Source of Phenomena: she holds the source of phenomena in order to spread great bliss. "All actions" is the Chain-in-Hand goddess, because she performs the action of binding all buddhas and bodhisattvas with loving-kindness. "Nairātmyā" is the All-Embracing goddess, because great bliss teaches that all phenomena are selfless, and because she shows herself fully embracing the self in order to bind the buddhas in passion. "Fruit" is the Flute goddess, because with the mudrā of blowing the flute with its holes she splits phenomena open into emptiness and so shows it. "Cause" is the Vīṇā goddess, because with the sounds of arising, ceasing and the like she shows that attachment and the rest are to be removed and cast off. "Renowned as action" is the Clay Drum goddess, because by beating one side of the clay drum she shows what is to be done for the awakening of selflessness. "What is to be accomplished, the sādhana, the vajra-holder" is the Great Drum goddess, because by hanging it at her throat and beating it with both hands she shows the three bodies together with their cause. These are the deities of the Padmanarteśvara maṇḍala.

"Thus the all-base consciousness becomes" is the Blessed One Paramāśva, whose nature is the aggregate of formations, because, like the all-base consciousness, through the wisdom that accomplishes activities he emanates various bodies and destroys conceptuality. "Dharmadhātu" is the Vajra Bolt goddess, because with the undefiled sphere she guards the wisdom of omniscience. "And unarisen" is the Key-Holder goddess, because, having taken hold of wisdom and shut its door, she does not let afflictions and other conceptions enter. "Realm of beings" is the Vajra Door goddess, because, like the realm of sentient beings, she does not abandon the wisdom that has arisen and gives faults no opening, and so is without beginning or end. "Realm of space" is the Vajra Curtain goddess, because she holds a curtain that pervades the sphere of space in order to guard well the queens of her own light. "Selflessness" is Buddhalocanā, because all buddhas, by being the very self of sentient beings, are the dharma of mirror-like wisdom. "Self-designation" is Māmakī: when the two are engaged, nonduality is a conventional designation, and since that is present in her, she possesses the designation of self, because that is the dharma of the two. "Free of entities" is Pāṇḍaravāsinī, the White-Robed One, because, having analyzed the emptiness of dual entities, she is the nature of reality free of conceptuality. "Without entity" is Tārā, because through the various emanations of the buddhas she goes teaching the Dharma in all world systems without exception, and is the nature of reality free of the entities of obscuration. These are the deities of the Paramāśva maṇḍala.

"Blissful" is Gaurī, because the wisdom maṇḍala whose nature is great bliss is guarded and summoned by her with faith. "In the triangle" is Caurī. Concerning this, some say that she displays a form that overpowers the three realms. The corners of the wisdom maṇḍala are definitely two, and if it is definitely a single corner, that is because one holds to it through habituation. Or else it is because body and the rest are placed within the body of diligence and so forth. "Dustless" is Vetālī, whose nature is mindfulness: dust is the conception of the samaya being and the wisdom being, and she makes the two one with the chain, by means of the net of emptiness. "Faultless" is Ghasmarī, because she brings about the descent with the sound of the vajra and bell, and through her nature of meditative absorption removes all faults of distraction. "Free of dust" is Pukkasī, because through the perfection of generosity and through compassion she removes the faults of stinginess and hatred. She is the first ground, the Joyous, and the nature of the earth element.

"Destroyer of evil" is Śabarī. Through moral discipline and loving-kindness she destroys the evils of broken discipline and unkindness. She is the nature of the Stainless ground and of the water element.

"Free of angry frowns" is Caṇḍālī. Through joy and patience she clears away dullness, dejection and the frown of anger. She is the nature of the Luminous ground and of the fire element.

"Abiding in equipoise" is Ḍombinī. Through equanimity and the perfection of diligence she destroys sloth and distraction. She is the nature of the Radiant ground and of the absence of conditioning activity.

"Knower of the supreme inner bliss" is glorious Heruka. "Inner" refers to worldly and transcendent things, and their bliss is the mastery that is utterly peaceful and unchanging. "Supreme" means beautiful and pleasing. Because he has the way of knowing this, he is "knower of the supreme inner bliss." These are the deities of the maṇḍala of glorious Heruka.

To teach those same five universal monarchs once more, the text says "secret" and so on. "Secret" means solitary. It is the wisdom of the utterly pure sphere of phenomena (dharmadhātu). It is secret because all this variety arises from its very emptiness. Or else it is secret because the afflicted conceptions, such as desire, have been abandoned and cast off, and it abides in that state. That itself is Heruka, and his abode is also given this name.

"Supreme": what has been weighed, discerned and ascertained by pure worldly wisdom is the discriminating wisdom. That itself is Padmanarteśvara, and his abode is also given this name.

"Delightful" is the wisdom of the equality of all buddhas. Because through it one delights in and realizes equality, that itself is Vajrasūrya, and his abode is also given this name.

"Self of all" is the wisdom that accomplishes activities, because it is the very self of the emanation bodies of all buddhas. That itself is Vajra Paramāśva, and his abode is also given this name.

All of these words carry the ending of the seventh case, the locative; they should be converted and understood as having the ending of the first case.

"Ever abiding" means abiding unmoving through the three bodies. The Dharma body is permanent by its nature. The enjoyment body is permanent because it abides without decline. The emanation body is permanent because it is uninterrupted.

"The actual buddha" is the mirror-like wisdom, because it is the nature of the bodies, enjoyments and abodes of all buddhas. That itself is Śāśvata, the Eternal One, and his abode is also given this name.

"Great being" is Vajrasattva, by way of the nature of what is to be known.

"This" means "in this," since it is formed in the same way as "always."

"Self-arisen" is said because ultimately there is no cause or the like.

As for "Blessed One," bhaga means the six qualities, beginning with lordship. Because he possesses them, he is the Blessed One. As it is said:

Lordship and beauty of form,
glory and fame and wisdom,
and diligence in its perfection:
these six are what is called bhaga.

"The One alone" means nondual, by way of the Dharma body. "Supreme god" means a god who has become supreme, by way of the enjoyment body and the emanation body.

"The Hevajra of five hundred thousand" is the extensive tantra spoken of earlier. "The fivefold maṇḍala" refers to the tantra that condenses those five hundred thousand lines. Because its five maṇḍalas are principal, it is taught as the Ḍākinī Vajrapañjara. For that very reason "Thus did I hear" and the rest are not stated in this tantra, since it depends on the extensive tantra.

"With her left hand at her heart in the threatening gesture,
she blazes greatly in various colors."
This means she has colors such as white. These colors will be taught in the condensed maṇḍala.

"Very great delusion": delusion is the all-base consciousness together with its habitual tendencies. Because it strongly suppresses this, it is "very." It is the mirror-like wisdom, whose character is mind-only and whose nature is the undefiled expanse. Because it suppresses that very delusion, it is "great."

"Very great splendor": splendor is the six afflictions, such as pride. Because the wisdom of the equality of all buddhas suppresses them, it is "very splendid," and for that reason it is "great splendor."

"Very desirous, great desire": desire is clinging to objects. Because the discriminating wisdom suppresses it, it is "very," and for that reason it is also "great desire."

"Great jealousy, great obscuration": because it realizes its own essential nature, and because through emanations of body, speech and mind it remains for as long as saṃsāra lasts and has become nonabiding nirvāṇa, it is called "great jealousy, great obscuration." It is the wisdom that accomplishes activities.

"Removes the cataract of conceptual thought": this is the cataract of the view of self.

"Utterly rejects the other vehicles": this is because it turns away from the nirvāṇa of the śrāvakas and others.

"Arisen from unshakable wisdom": since it is not shaken by afflicted conceptions, the wisdom of the utterly pure sphere of phenomena is the unshakable wisdom. "Arisen" means born from that.

"To tend the inexhaustible aim of beings": here "beings" are the buddhas, and "inexhaustible" means without diminution. If one asks what of them: they serve the one who tends the guest and make an image of it. The "guest" is perfected wisdom, and because it is protected, the "tending" is wisdom and great compassion. That itself is the "aim," meaning purpose; this is the extent of the line.

"Destroys the five obscurations" means destroying these five: objects and beings, self, conceptual thought, formations, and the engaging consciousnesses. Of these, what destroys the conceptions of objects and of beings as objects is the mirror-like wisdom. What destroys the view of self is the wisdom of equality. What destroys the conception of grasped and grasper is the discriminating wisdom. Formations are the very distraction of beings, and what destroys that is the wisdom that accomplishes activities.

Destroying the five kinds of engaging consciousness is the wisdom of the utterly pure dharmadhātu. "Destroying" is derived from "having vanquished."

"Through the path of whichever faculty" means whatever objects of the faculties are found, that is, made directly perceived.

On "by the yoga of supreme benefit": "supreme" is so called because it has the nature of the superknowledges of all buddhas. "Benefit" is so called because it engages spontaneously. "Yoga" is so called because it has the nature of wisdom and means.

Someone may suspect that, because things appear as grasped and grasper, they are nothing but delusion. To meet this, the text says, "The nature of all buddhas engages." The meaning is this: whatever is experienced by the wisdom of all buddhas, which is empty of duality, is enjoyed as false, and is therefore entirely free of delusion.

"Through whichever entity" means through whichever family. The families of sentient beings are divided into five: the families of attachment, aversion, delusion, pride and envy. "Therefore one goes to its nature" means that by this they become the five families of awakening.

"The jewel with various forms" is crystal, because it can take on the color of a flower placed near it, such as the hibiscus. "Arisen from the mind vajra" is said because it is born from vajra-like wisdom. "One's own conceptualization" means views such as grasping at a self, because they impute duality. "Pure" means free of adventitious stains. "The mind that is pure is bliss" is said because by abandoning the three stains it becomes great uncontaminated bliss.

Thinking, "How can elaborations such as colors and marks become a means of liberation?", the text says, "If emptiness were the means," and so on. If emptiness alone, devoid of means, is held to be the path, then one would not become a buddha even in ten million eons. Instead, through peaceful results such as the sphere of infinite space, one would fall into a state without leisure for the Dharma. This is not accepted even in the system of the perfections, which says, "One should cultivate the perfection of wisdom endowed with the means of the five perfections, not wisdom alone devoid of means," and so forth.

Suppose one thinks: "Emptiness and bliss is the means at the stage of the path and the result at the stage of ripening." To answer this, the text says, "In the cause," and so on. The meaning is that in that case, since there would be no difference between the emptiness of the path and that of ripening, emptiness would not be the means, just like the result as you hold it. The cause has the nature of elaboration, because it has an objective support. The result has the nature of non-elaboration, because it has no objective support. Therefore cause and result are established as different, and therefore it is established that the means is not emptiness alone.

If one wonders, "Why then was emptiness taught?", it was taught in order to show it as a limb of the means, by reversing inverted views. "Those mistaken in view" are those whose views about selflessness and the rest are wrong and discordant. "By those seeking a view of self" means those who seek a view of self, who are attached to grasping at a self. The meaning is that the Victor taught emptiness in order to reverse their view that grasps at a self.

Someone may ask: "In the way of the perfections it is taught that awakening is accomplished by practicing, for three countless eons, the five perfections held by the means of the perfection of wisdom, and by various white deeds such as giving away one's head. What then is the use of means such as the maṇḍala wheel?" Therefore the text says, "Therefore, the maṇḍala wheel; the means is the saṃvara of bliss," and so on. "Therefore" means that those very five perfections, together with their retinue, gathered into the wheel of the deity's maṇḍala, are the means. "The means of bliss" means that it is not austere conduct. "Saṃvara" is taught as bliss itself: enjoying all the objects of desire and being endowed with the experience of the bliss of the two organs. "Buddha pride" means being free of ordinary pride. "Swiftly" means in this very life.

If one asks whether the buddha bodies and the Dharma bodies should be meditated on as nonexistent, the text says, "Thirty-two," and so on, showing that the means abides as the nature of the three samādhis that take the form of the three bodies. In "supreme of all aspects" and what follows, the supreme of all aspects is the sublime, the nature of emptiness. "Vast" is the nature of the great means. Because by it beings enter the five cakravartins, it is "beings enter." Clarifying this is the first chapter.

"Then" refers to what follows immediately after the first chapter. "Attained the three worlds" means the three levels. Here its intended meaning is that the palace has three stories. "Samaya offering" means that one should not transgress the substances, with offerings distinguished according to the division of each one's own cakravartin. "Lord of the seed of Samantabhadra" means generated from the seed hūṃ. "Counting" means engaging the index finger in killing, that is, the left index finger. "With both, join the mudrā" means this: form the vajra bond, twist and interlace the two ring fingers, press the nails of the two middle fingers with the two thumbs, and bend the two index fingers slightly in a hook. Having formed it in the manner of a great jewel, place it at the heart.

On "Yakṣiṇīs and nāginīs": when the yakṣiṇī is accomplished, the golden pedestals and so forth are summoned by the light rays of the syllable kṣaṃ and drawn in. One should visualize the yakṣiṇī as yellow in color, holding a white parasol, like the fire at the end of the eon, trampling the head of Yama the Ender, and marked at the heart with kṣaṃ. For the nāginī, the syllable is ni.

On "Abiding in the samādhi of great power" and what follows: "eight lotus petals" means this. In order to accomplish the fierce rite that comes below, a nāga maṇḍala is drawn in the sequence of wind, fire, water and so on: a lotus of eight petals, with the petals colored like the eight nāgas of the directions.

On "the eight nāgas" and what follows: in the east is Ananta, black; in the south Vāsuki, white; in the west Takṣaka, red; in the north Karkoṭa, blue. In the southeast and in Nairṛti's quarter, the southwest, are Padma and Mahāpadma, pale yellow. In the northwest is Śaṅkhapāla, yellow. In the northeast, Īśāna's quarter, one should visualize Kulika, of various colors.

"He makes the vajra garuḍa his seat" means this: their tails are pinned down by the outstretched left leg of the garuḍa born from gaṃ, blazing like the fire at the end of the eon; and through the yoga of the Mighty One, one is seated upon its seat. "The lords of the nāgas" are Ananta and the others. Imagining those very nāgas in one's hands,

"Interspersed with hūṃs,
One should recite the mantra of the syllable phu."

This means that at the end of the garland mantra one says hūṃ phu hūṃ phaṭ.

"Meditate intently" means this: one meditates that in the expanse of the sky the nāgas, stupefied by the light of the wrathful one, send down masses of water. Rain will certainly fall.

"The reality of the buddhas is to be discerned" refers to the reality of the path that is the cause of buddhahood. The rest is easy to understand. The further particulars of the meaning should be learned from the guru's instructions.

"The pure places" means the places such as the first holy site.

"The vajra chain" and what follows teach the rites of summoning, binding and so on, for accomplishment by fierce means.

"The four ḍākinīs" are those same goddesses, Gaurī and the others. Through them the following is taught. If even someone devoted to meditation and recitation is not sustained by the deity's gaze, then for about six months one should worship with every kind of offering and nourish the deity so that it remains. Then for another six months one performs the summoning with the hook at the heart and so forth, and after that, in a further month, it is taught that one strikes with the fierce kīla.

"The buddha dancer" means the buddhas performing a dance. Someone may ask: "Since he teaches song and music, which are like uttering idle chatter, will one not fall into the lower realms through that very wrongdoing?" In answer it is said,

"Through the vajra of the buddha dancer,
The awakening of the passionate is accomplished,"

and so on. "The vajra of the buddha dance" means mudrās, gestures and the like. "The passionate" are those attached to sense objects, those possessed of attachment to desire. "Awakening is accomplished" means buddhahood itself. Through hands, feet, gazes and mudrās they accumulate merit and wisdom. It is just as a lump of iron or the like, though small, sinks in water, yet that same iron made into a vessel floats on the surface even when large.

Likewise, since the vajra dance and the rest are forms in which the hands, feet and faces are gathered together, the deity should be made with one face, two arms and two legs. "Vajra-holder" means: whoever has a vajra is the vajra-bearer, that is, Hevajra. Of him, the gathering together of the right-hand skulls is the vajra, and the gathering together of the left-hand skulls is the skull cup and the khaṭvāṅga. It is taught in the same way for the others: in the right hand of Śāśvata is a wheel, in the right hand of Sūrya a jewel, in the right hand of Padmanarteśvara a lotus, in the right hand of Paramāśva a sword, and in the left hands a khaṭvāṅga and a skull cup. The feet are placed in a dancing posture with the legs half crossed.

Just as the hands and so on are gathered together, the members of the maṇḍala, the mātṛkās and the rest, are also to be gathered together. The gathering of Gaurī and the others is Māmakī. The gathering of the Tongs goddess and the others is Buddhalocanā. The gathering of the Lotus-in-Hand goddess and the others is Pāṇḍaravāsinī. The gathering of the Sun-in-Hand goddess, the Door-bolt goddess and the others is Tārā. The gathering of the wrathful ones and the gatekeepers is the wrathful Acala. Some say that it is the gathering of the wheel of wrathful ones.

On "By fully joining the cupped flower of the palms": on the two hands, from ā a moon disc, and on it from hūṃ a white vajra is generated, and the fingers too are imagined as vajras. Joining the flower-like palms at the crown of the head and prostrating is "the cupped flower." Then, having prostrated to the tathāgatas, to offer oneself completely is "fully joining." Then one makes the aspiration prayer as it appears below.

"Without perception" refers to the sphere of infinite space and the others.

"hūṃ" is body, speech and mind: body is ha, speech is u, and mind is ma. "Akṣobhya" means unmoved by dualistic conceptuality. "One whose power over self is destroyed" is said because, the two being absent, there is mere appearance.

"oṃ" is emptiness of objects together with mind; it is the nature of mind-only. "Brahmā" means non-abiding nirvāṇa. "Vairocana" is so called because he illuminates with the blazing fire of wisdom.

"ā" means equal to space. On "Narteśvara" and what follows: "dance" means play, and "lord" is its chief; he discerns all phenomena and delights in disciples of every kind.

"trāṃ" means attachment and equality through experiencing the reality of all buddhas: the reality of all buddhas is the letter ta, the equality of attachment through experience is ra, and mind is ma. "Single Parasol" means sheltering equally. "Dispeller of Fever" means dispelling the torment of the afflictions. "Wheel-formed" means the sunlike wisdom.

"khaṃ" is the emanation mind equal to space, khaṃ. "Vajra Swift-Goer" means going swiftly by means of the Dharma, displaying various emanations here and there.

"Wine of compassion" means the nature of equality, of a single taste, once the conceptions of duality have melted away. "Māmakī" means the activity in which the two together destroy illusion, and Māmakī is Akṣobhya. "Peaceful" means mind only, because conceptual thoughts of the apprehended object and so on are pacified. "Passionate" means abiding in the decisive certainty of the experience, by the two together, of the wisdom of emptiness. "Great Mistress" means the nature that cares for the emanations in the various world systems. "Crooked-eyed" means looking above and below, that is, looking at saṃsāra and at peace.

Here is a partial account of the practice. Build a three-storied house. In the lower story, place the dancers who will perform the dance. In the middle story, place the provisions, such as food and drink. In the upper story, set the maṇḍala upon the wheel, and perform the three samādhis in the order given in the sādhana of the condensed maṇḍala. Together with four disciples and four yoginīs, each should be costumed as their own lord of the wheel. If there are no yoginīs, men should take on the form of women. Then, together with the wrathful ones and all the gatekeepers condensed into Acala, all māras are to be vanquished.

The condensing is done as follows. The tiered pavilions are condensed into a tiered pavilion. The faces are condensed into the face, the arms into the arms, and the feet into the feet. The skull cups holding the elephant and so on are condensed into an imitation vajra. Likewise, the skull cups holding the peacock and so on are condensed into a wheel. The skull cups holding the vessel and so on are condensed into a jewel. The skull cups holding the bear and so on are condensed into a lotus. The vessels holding earth and so on are condensed into a sword. In the same way, the emblems of the left hands are condensed into the khaṭvāṅga and the skull cup.

First, screened off by a curtain, the master alone sets a mirror before him. He paints himself with pigments in the colors of jewels and so on, taking on the faces and arms of his own deity, together with the imitation vajra, khaṭvāṅga and skull cup. He adorns himself with ornaments, such as imitation ones carried for the worship. On the soles of the feet he visualizes, in sequence, a one-pointed, a three-pointed and a five-pointed vajra arising from hūṃ, and a crossed vajra generated from a. These are to be joined with the vajra steps described below.

Likewise, with a gaze whose brows are knit in the wrathful frown of a great wrathful one, he places in the left eye a moon arising from ṭa, and in the right a sun arising from ma, each marked with hūṃ. With that blazing gaze and with the pride of Heruka, he utters hūṃ and circles with the vajra steps described below.

Moreover, the curtain is first removed gradually. To show the removal of the obscurations of the grounds, beginning with the ground called Perfect Joy, he is revealed in order: the crown ornament, the forehead, the eyes, the chin, the throat, the shoulders, the arms, the heart, the navel, the waist, the thighs, and finally the feet. In this way the vajra-holder comes forth. Adopting the half-crossed posture, pressing down with the right foot, he holds himself in the even stance. As explained before, he holds palms full of flowers at the crown of his head. He prostrates, saying, "I prostrate to all the tathāgatas," and offers himself, saying, "I offer myself to all the tathāgatas." Then he makes this aspiration:

"However many sentient beings are counted in the realm of beings, whether born from eggs, born from warmth and moisture, born miraculously, or born from a womb; whether with form or without form; whether with perception, without perception, or neither with nor without perception, all of them I shall establish in the state of the Great Seal (Mahāmudrā)."

He looks upon the realms of beings as steeped in darkness. With oṃ krodhe dṛṣṭi hī, and with the light rays from the eyeballs of hūṃ blazing like the fire at the destruction of the world, he burns the three worlds to ashes. Then, holding the dance in the half-crossed posture, he again takes up the great banner and the sound of the bell and circles with the play of the great dance.

The four kinds of vajra steps that come from the oral instructions should be applied. With the one-pointed vajra arising from the hūṃ on the soles, he strikes the ground, holds the vajra and bell at his hips, and recites oṃ eka sūcika vajra pāda vajra mahā krodha bhañja bhañja sarba duṣṭāṃ asūrāṇa hūṃ phaṭ. He then circles with the step of the one-pointed vajra. Next, in the same way, he circles with the step of the three-pointed vajra. The mantra is oṃ tri sūcika vajra pāda vajram hā krodha bhañja bhamja sarba duṣṭāṃ asūrāṇa huṃ phaṭ. After that he circles with the step of the five-pointed vajra. The mantra is oṃ pañja sūcika vajra pāda vajra mahā kroṅha bhañja bhañja sarba duṣvṭāṃ asūrāṇa huṃ phaṭ. After that he visualizes the crossed vajra born from the final vowel and circles with the step of the crossed vajra. The mantra is oṃ viśva vajra pāda vajra mahā krodha bhañja bhañja sarba duṣṭāṃ asūrāṇa hūṃ phaṭ.

Then it is further explained:

Visualizing oneself as one's chosen deity,
adorned with the ornaments of the deity,
and visualizing all the requisites,
one should make the fire offering in the fire of the belly.
In the east and west, the north and south,
the yogins dwelling in the directions,
and the goddesses dwelling in the intermediate directions,
are to be worshipped with the supreme of great offerings.

Likewise, the other yogins too should each screen off their own form with a curtain, and each, costumed in the condensed form, should imagine themselves dwelling on the summit of Mount Meru, and dance.

In detail: at the eastern gate, one white in color, holding an imitation wheel, does everything as before. With eyeballs of oṃ, he looks with a loving gaze, and with a garland of light of cool nectar he imagines establishing those who have fallen into the view that holds the discipline of nonexistence in the Buddha's nonabiding nirvāṇa, which is delusion transformed. Likewise, at the western gate, Padmanarteśvara, red in color, holding an imitation lotus, with eyeballs of hīuḥ, recites oṃ vajradṛṣṭa ruṭa maṭa, and with the light of the hook of his gaze turned to the left, he imagines establishing those who abide in the awakening of the śrāvakas and others, through loving attachment, in the awakening of attachment.

Likewise, at the northern gate is Paramāśva, green in color, holding an imitation sword and wearing a green horse's face. With his gaze blazing as is fitting, and by its hook, one imagines this: relying on the path of omniscience, those who have entered the śrāvaka path are led onto the path of great awakening by various emanations, such as bodhisattvas, that are obtained from them.

Likewise, at the southern gate is Vajrasūrya, yellow in color, holding an imitation jewel. With the words oṃ dīpta dṛśya aṃkuśa jaḥ, he flicks his sun and moon eyes to the right and moves them swiftly. With the lotus hook of his gaze he removes faults such as avarice, and one imagines that he joins beings to the wisdom of equality. As it is said:

In the east, the west, and the north,
and in the south, apply them in order.

All of them wear imitation tiger-skin garments and imitation tawny hair, have crowns of the five buddhas' skulls, and are three-eyed. They sit in a dancing posture with legs half-crossed on an imitation variegated lotus and sun. In particular, they have a seat of the moon of permanence and are radiant.

Likewise, in the northeast, the quarter of Īśāna, is Locanā, white in color, holding an imitation wheel. With her gaze of loving-kindness she pacifies the harms that afflict sentient beings.

Likewise, in the southeast, the quarter of fire, is Māmakī, blue in color, holding an imitation vajra. With her vajra gaze she protects those threatened by enemy armies.

Likewise, in the southwest, the quarter of Nairṛti, is Pāṇḍaravāsinī, red in color, holding an imitation lotus. With her passionate gaze she makes people passionately devoted.

Likewise, in the northwest, the quarter of wind, is Tārā, green, holding an utpala. With her blazing gaze she summons those to be trained.

As before, these too have imitation tawny hair, imitation faces, and imitation garments. They sit with the left leg extended on an imitation lotus and sun, their colors done in paint. The left hand, at the heart, makes a fist with the threatening forefinger.

Likewise, the wrathful Acala holds a sword, makes the threatening forefinger, and holds a nāga noose. He stands with the left leg extended on an imitation variegated lotus and sun. He wears various ornaments of jewels and the like and is dressed in fine garments. Universal monarchs (cakravartins) and goddesses surround him on the sides, and he subjugates. By gazing above and below, one imagines saṃsāra and peace as equal.

All of them, with the vajra gait and the rest, and with the five lotus turns of the hand, should dance in order with ninety-eight modes of steps, and they should sing songs:

Emptiness, the unstained supreme lord,
in the nature of dream and illusion:
for as long as it is not accomplished,
one should meditate on the nature of mind.

This is the song of Māmakī.

That which is neither existence nor nirvāṇa
is the vajra of great bliss.
Whoever has no meditation in mind,
that one accomplishes the task.

This is Locanā's.

Abandoning mantras and letters,
the mind free of bindu and of sound:
that is supreme great bliss.
Here there is nothing to remove and nothing to establish.

This is Pāṇḍaravāsinī's.

Just as a reflection is of its very nature,
so is the one who meditates on things:
emptiness, the unstained supreme lord.
From that there is neither merit nor evil.

This is Tārā's.

Like the moon in water,
neither true nor false:
through the supreme wheel of the maṇḍala,
the body too should be viewed in this way.

Thus the whole retinue sang.

"In the city of dance, the source of phenomena" refers to the source of phenomena, the place of the colored powders.

This concludes the explanation of the second chapter, from Illuminating Reality, a commentary on the difficult points of the Ḍākinī Vajra Net.

"Abiding in the samādhi of the vajra net" means abiding in the samādhi of Hevajra.

"One should make the torma for the great bhūtas" means the torma offering, together with its accompanying offerings, that satisfies the great bhūtas. As it is said:

Adorned on the limbs with every ornament,
made beautiful with vajra ornaments,
holding the vajra and bell,
one should cast the torma at a crossroads.
Flowers, incense, and lamps,
the conch of scent and the rest, place on the right;
on the left, the arghya vessel of the mantra;
having set another vessel in front,
first, by the rite of mouth-rinsing water,
present the flower offerings and the rest in order.
Having made the flower offerings and the rest,
one should offer them into the excellent vessel.
Moving in the manner of flowers,
moving in the manner of incense,
in the manner of raising the lamp,
and in the manner of scent, one should anoint.
With playful hands, the graceful one;
in the manner of a garland, the garland ḍākinī;
singing songs with hūṃ,
and for the dance, summon with the knees.
These are the unsurpassed offerings
that delight the buddhas.

"Abiding in the center of the space element" means having entered within the source of phenomena. "Lotus" means an eight-petaled red lotus. "One should meditate" means after meditating on emptiness. "From hūṃ" means from the hūṃs: on the petals of the directions and intermediate directions, Śakra (Indra) and the others should be made blue in color. Likewise, Brahmā and the others above arise from the oṃs, and should be made white in color. The nāgas and the others below the earth arise from the āḥs, and should be made red in color. All of them hold a vajra and bell and are embraced by their own consorts.

"On the seat, with the right leg extended" means on a variegated lotus and sun. "Brahmā and the others are to be imagined" refers to Vairocana. "Indra and the others, vajra" refers to Akṣobhya. "Karkoṭa and the others, Narteśvara" refers to Amitābha. "All are born from the three families" means that they arise from the three syllables that are the nature of delusion, aversion, and attachment. "Gaurī should summon" means that, after the commitment wheel has been generated, the wisdom wheel is summoned by Gaurī's hook. "Having made them enter with Caurī's noose" and the rest is easy to understand.

"Then, with arghya water and water for the feet, one should satisfy them with this mantra." The arghya water and the water for the feet named here are only examples. Having made offerings and praises, one purifies with the three syllables beginning with oṃ, turns the offering into nectar by cooking and so forth, and then satisfies them. For the peaceful and other rites, one offers into the slightly opened mouths of the eight, marked with a white vajra and so forth. For killing and the other fierce rites, one offers into gaping mouths adorned with a wrathful vajra. The rest is easy to understand.

In the various words of the root text beginning with "Then," the vajra names appear jumbled. Here they should be arranged in their proper order and stated. Vajra Laughter, Vajra Play, Vajra Swagger and Vajra Alala belong to the family of aversion. Vajra Gnosis, Vajra Descent and Vajra Perfection belong to the family of delusion. Vajra Sprout, Vajra Lotus, Vajra Lotus Sprout, Vajra All-Seeing and Vajra Passion belong to the family of attachment. Vajra Wish-Fulfilling Jewel, Vajra Anaṅga, Vajra Supreme Joy, Vajra Bliss of Joy and Vajra Joy belong to the jewel family. Vajra Wisdom, Vajra Wisdom Sprout, Vajra Compassion Sprout and Vajra Space belong to the samaya family.

The names of the ḍākinīs should be stated as follows. Vajragarbha, Vajra Weapon, Vajra Wondrous Bliss, Vajra Nairātmyā and Vajra Hand belong to the family of aversion. Vajralocanā, Vajra Ḍākinī, Vajra Tawny Lady and Vajra Illusion belong to the family of delusion. Vajra Blazing Lady, Vajra Creeper, Vajra Bringer-Down, Vajra Vetālī, Vajra Hook Goddess and Vajra Garland Lady belong to the jewel family. Vajra Garland Lady, Vajra Coquette, Vajra Laughing Lady, Vajra Embracer, Vajra Abundant Lady, Vajra Lotus and Vajra Passionate Lady belong to the family of attachment. Vajra Source Lady, Vajra Laughter-Maker, Vajratārā, Vajra Playful Lady, Vajra Intelligence, Vajra Kiṅkā, Vajra Saliva Lady and Vajra Reviver belong to the samaya family.

On the passage beginning "attachment": attachment is great compassion. Aversion is the inexhaustible nondual. Delusion is not knowing in terms of duality. "Pervasive samaya" means pervasive emanation. "Sattva" means the three things become one, the utterly pure expanse. "Jewel" means the distinctive excellence of the sameness of all the buddhas. By this there are six families: the five families and one more; the others are the five families of the bodhisattvas. "Desire" means the mind possessed of attachment, aversion and delusion.

This concludes the explanation of the third chapter from The Expansion of Reality, a commentary on the difficult points of the Ḍākinī Vajra Net.

"Vajragarbha, manifest awakening,
the very first king of sections,"
and what follows is easy to understand.

On "the mudrā of the hand of the body": "of the body" means bodily activity. "Mudrā" means what is not to be transgressed: holding a vajra, a wheel and so forth with the fingers of the hand purified by the vajra of hūṃ. In the same way, the stepping of the feet, the mudrā of the gaze and so forth are also to be included. "The mudrā of speech" means verbal activity: reciting the mantras of the wrathful ones and others with the tongue marked by a white vajra arisen from hūṃ.

On "The mudrā of mind is the emblem," and what follows: the mudrā of the practice of mind is the emblems, the tongs and so forth of the Tongs goddess and the others, in fashioned images, painted scrolls and the like. Some scriptures say, "The emblem of the drawing is the mudrā." The drawing is the colored-powder maṇḍala; as for the mudrā, the emblems made of gold and other materials are to be drawn in it.

"That very one is in the maṇḍala of meditation" refers to the emblem of the deity of meditation, that is, the emblem of the mind. These are the tongs and others mentioned explicitly. Therefore one should understand that the lion and the others are to be drawn following the drawn maṇḍala of Pukkasī and the rest.

"In the right hand a noose" is easy to understand. On "moving throughout space": all space is the gnosis of wisdom. "The tantra of the ḍākinīs" means the causal tantra, the resultant tantra and the tantra of method. Among these, it is the cause because it benefits and passes into the nature of the result: it is the nature of the expanse, without beginning or end, in which all phenomena are apprehended. Therefore it is the result, because the result is not gathered in by the cause; for there is no essential nature of a concept to be gathered. "The tantra of method" means mantras, mudrās, maṇḍalas, wheels and so forth.

"Supreme of all tantras" means the unexcelled yoga tantra, because it is superior to the kriyā and caryā tantras and the others. On "vast, its depth hard to fathom": the vast is the tantra of method, and the depth hard to fathom is the resultant tantra. That is because the gnosis of the five families of buddhas is not the domain of others. Hence it is said, "It cannot be known by the family or by others." The rest is easy to understand, so I have not explained it.

"Delighting, abiding in supreme samādhi" means this. In accordance with the instructions, one performs the accomplishment of the maṇḍala with various offering articles and so forth. Then, at the beginning of the rite of the disciple's entry into the maṇḍala, one first enters oneself and receives from the lord of the wheel the empowerments from the water empowerment up to the culmination of the permission. At the end of the proclamation of the blessing of truth and so forth, one makes a face-covering and an upper-body covering of cloth over which the mantra of the Enemy of Obstructors has been recited. Then one abides in the samādhi in which, at the heart, which has the nature of supreme joy, that is, at the heart of Heruka, the light rays of a fill the entire body made of subtle particles.

"The vidyā empowerment" means the reversal of ignorance. Because it washes, it is called empowerment. There are six empowerments, which in order wash away the obscurations to suchness: objects, sentient beings, self, conceptual thought, formations, and the engaging consciousnesses. This very tantra says:

The water empowerment is the first;
the crown empowerment is the second;
the vajra empowerment is the third;
that of one's own lord, the fourth;
the name empowerment is the fifth;
buddhahood itself is the sixth;
the vase empowerment is the seventh;
the secret empowerment is the eighth;
wisdom-gnosis is the ninth.

The last three will be explained below.

Someone may ask: "If the empowerment of the perfect Buddha is conferred first, how can it be placed sixth?" Although it comes first in sequence, it is placed sixth in relation to the purity of the five wisdoms, beginning with mirror-like wisdom, because it is the purity of the knowable.

After the student has entered the maṇḍala, he casts the flower garland into the maṇḍala with "pratīccha vajrahoḥ". He is given the mantra of whichever emblem it falls upon. Then the flower garland is bound on the student's head with "pratigṛhṇa svamimaṃ satva mahābala". This is done because he accepts buddhahood, and so it is the empowerment of the perfect Buddha, that is, the flower garland empowerment. Its nature is the purity of the nature of reality, which has neither beginning nor end.

Next comes the line "With the water of the self-aware person, and all around with the eight vases." "The water of the self-aware person" means the vase filled with the water of bodhicitta that arises from union with the lord of the wheel together with his consort (vidyā). This is the Vijaya vase. Alternatively, one should properly prepare the eight vases that arise from the offering of union. Since they are "together with" it, they are to be understood as serving one purpose. "All around" means placed in the eight directions. They are marked with the emblems of the eight goddesses who stand at the four gates and the corners, and they are also filled inside with them. The tenth is marked with the emblem of the enemy of obstructors, because it is said, "One should not make fewer than ten vases."

"Should gratify" means one should wash. If one asks whom, the text says, "imagining the vajra-holder": it means the student who has been visualized as Heruka. "Fully visualized as Vajrasattva" means visualized as the mirror-like wisdom, which is the storehouse consciousness transformed. Alternatively, "Vajrasattva" means the tathāgatas, and "fully visualized" means that the diadem is made by them and marked with them. "One should fully visualize the vajra sun" refers to the student. Through the empowerment he is transformed in this way, and so this is the crown empowerment. "Apply the vajra words" means that at each of these empowerments one should also say "vajra abhiṣeñca".

"Nine-pointed" means the five-pointed vajra itself. If one wonders how this is to be reconciled, the four points of the lower side are the nature of the gatekeepers. The four points of the upper side are the nature of the four goddesses of the corners. The two central points, above and below, are the nature of the lord of the maṇḍala and his consort. Accordingly, Ācārya Ānandagarbha said in his commentary on the Sarvabuddhasamāyoga, "The four points of one side are the purity of the goddesses such as the Illuminating One, and the other four are those of the Swift One and the others." The center is the nature of Vajrasattva. Since the center is not divided, the five also come to number nine. Accordingly, the Catuṣpīṭha says, "Vairocana is the center of all." The center itself has the essence of Vairocana, and the essence of both the upper and lower points should be understood in the same way.

"Great vajra" is so called because it has the nature of Padmanarteśvara, who is discriminating wisdom. "At the heart" carries the unstated remainder "it should be held." "The student is to be made Lokeśvara" means the student is to be made in the form of Padmanarteśvara. "Today you are empowered" means empowered there by the perfectly complete buddhas. "This is all the buddhas themselves" means generating complete awakening. "The student, Paramāśva, the goddess" refers to Vajra Paramāśva. "One should imagine him embraced by Tārā" means in union with Tārā. "Vajra lord" means the lord of vajras, because he tames those to be tamed by the wisdom of accomplishing activities through their purification. "Ring the vajra and bell with the hands" means that, holding vajra and bell, he embraces Tārā and rings the bell behind her. "Having made the student Vairocana, generated in permanent form, the name empowerment": this means empowering by placing the hand on the crown of the student's head and giving a name. Its nature is the pure expanse of reality.

To summarize the six empowerments, the text says "Whatever" and so on. "Whatever appears" refers to the appearances such as water. "Own chief of all" means that this is the very self of all. "Chief" means lord. "Heruka" is given as an example: each is to be seen as the lord, the universal monarch of his own family. "Since" carries the sense of "because." "Pure by nature" refers to the washings, that is, the empowerments. "Stainless" is said because they are naturally pure. "Dharma" means the Dharma of the buddhas. If one asks what that is, the text says "the wisdom of the buddhas": the five wisdoms together with the pure expanse of the nature of reality make six.

Does it end with these? It does not. One should also confer the vows and the prophecy, and this is what the passage beginning "This" states. "sarva tathāgata sibhā vajra" is perfect, complete awakening. As for "samaya tiṣṭha", samaya is perfect, complete awakening, because all-pervading wisdom has been attained. "tiṣṭha" is its abiding. This is taught through six syllables. Among them, "hi" means free from the dust of the faults of objects and sentient beings, and so it is mirror-like wisdom.

"hi hi hi hi" means free from the stains of grasping at a self and the like. These are the wisdom of equality, the discriminating wisdom, the wisdom that accomplishes activities, and the wisdom of the pure dharmadhātu. "hūṃ" is the pure nature of reality, free from the stains of the apprehended object and the like.

"By the rite of all the sādhanas" refers to the requisites of the maṇḍala. The rite is to offer them just as they are, with the conviction that they are like an illusion and so forth. "All the unsurpassed offerings" are the outer, secret and suchness offerings. "The vow" is the unbreakable commitment.

"Then the vajra from hand to hand" means that after the verse has been recited, it is given from the master's hand into the disciple's hand. What is it like? To answer this the text says "Without beginning or end" and so on. "Without beginning or end" is the dharmakāya of the pure nature of reality. "Sattva" means that all phenomena have a single taste. "Vajrasattva" is the mirror-like wisdom, the nature of Akṣobhya. "Great joy" is Ratnasambhava and the wisdom of equality. "Samantabhadra" means wholly virtuous; it is the nature of Amitābha and of the discriminating wisdom. "Self of all" is the nature of the wisdom that accomplishes activities and of Amoghasiddhi. "Vajragarbha" is the wisdom of the pure dharmadhātu, in the manner of Vairocana. "Lord" is said because he is the lord of the tathāgatas.

"Following the voice of wisdom" is said because it brings about the realization of the nature of reality in accordance with the individual dispositions of the mindstreams of all who are to be trained.

"Having given all the reassurances" is done with words such as "By seeing and entering."

"Oneself becoming Śākyamuni" means: holding the robe at the heart with the left fist, making the boon-granting mudrā with the right hand, and standing in an even stance, one should give the prophecy together with the tathāgatas.

"In order thoroughly to accomplish existence" means in order to attain non-abiding nirvāṇa.

"bhār bhu va svaḥ" means the three worlds.

To show that one who has obtained the knowledge empowerment meditates on the three samādhis, the text then says "Wisdom" and so on. "Vajra samādhi" is the samādhi of the initial yoga, of the supreme victory of the maṇḍala, and of the supreme victory of action.

"Having drawn out the vajra samaya": imagining one's own seed syllable on a moon or sun at one's heart, emanate its light rays, and visualizing the master who is the universal ruler of whichever maṇḍala it is, together with the buddhas and bodhisattvas, one should recite "I go for refuge to the Three Jewels" and so on. "The master who holds the vajra" is an illustrative example. "Offering" means offering by means of the offering goddesses who arise from the light at one's own heart.

"The seven should be purified" refers to confessing wrongdoing, rejoicing in merit, dedicating merit, going for refuge to the three, requesting, supplicating, and relying on the path.

"Having generated the mind of awakening" refers to the ultimate and the subsequently arisen; the latter, arising from worldly wisdom, is the protection wheel and so forth.

"Meditate on the source of phenomena" means: having first meditated on the protection wheel, one meditates there on the mudrā of the source of phenomena, which has the nature of the thirteen grounds including the ground of practice through aspiration, narrow at the base and wide at the top, white, and in essence Vajradhara.

"There meditate on the dwelling of the buddhas" means: within it, upon a vajra and a lotus, one meditates on the four great elements, and on the tiered mansion that arises from Vairocana, into whom the syllable bhrūṃ and a wheel have transformed.

"One should meditate on the five aspects": from the fifteen vowels beginning with a comes a moon, and from the thirty-four consonants beginning with ka a sun. With their light one draws in all the qualities of the buddhas and absorbs them like stars, and one sees these mirrored in one another like reflections; this is the mirror-like wisdom. Upon this, from hūṃ and aṃ transforming, there arise in order a skull cup marked with a crossed vajra and a flaying knife, and at the center, on the sun, they are marked by the seed syllable; this is the discriminating wisdom. From its light rays the wheel of the maṇḍala of the fifteen goddesses radiates out and works for the benefit of beings; this is the wisdom that accomplishes activities. Then, when these are gathered back, there arises from the transformation of all of them, the seed syllables, the hand emblems, the moon and the sun, the one called the Primordial Person, Samantabhadra, sixteen-armed, eight-faced and four-legged, endowed with the colors and characteristics described below; this is the utterly pure wisdom. These are the five awakenings. "One should meditate" is said accordingly.

"Emanate the wheel of the ḍākinīs" should be joined at the end of "Then Vajra, great passion."

Regarding "Having entered like a gandharva, falling, recollect the melting": in the manner of a deity being born, one places hūṃ and aṃ between two red hoḥ syllables. These four syllables enter through one's own mouth, and one imagines them in the center of the lotus. One sees the great passion melting, and by the power of its light one meditates that the Primordial Person together with his consort also blazes with light like a burning flame and turns into melted form.

"He should be urged by the goddesses of the directions" means that the four ḍākinīs arising from the light of the melted form should sing songs such as "Lord, you are the vajra of aversion."

"Having urged the leader of the wheel" means that after urging him with song, the four goddesses are gathered into the melted form, and from the transformation of the seed syllable and the emblems he arises in a body endowed with the faces and so forth described below.

Regarding "Delusion and so on in the eyes and so on": in the eyes is Mohavajrā, in the ears Dveṣavajrā, in the nose Mātsaryavajrā, in the tongue Rāgavajrā, in the body Īrṣyāvajrā, and in the mind the ḍākinī Nairātmyā. Their colors and so forth will be taught below.

"Place the three vajras in the three places" means this: at the heart, the throat and the crown of the head one should place the three, namely the one who moves in the sky, the one who moves on the earth, and the one who moves beneath the earth. On the moon at the heart of each one should imagine the syllables hūṃ, āṃ and oṃ, blue, red and white respectively.

Next, to teach the empowerment that is conferred when one has asked for empowerment, the text says, "Empowerment is to be conferred by the eight consorts (vidyās)." They are called forth by light arising from the seeds of the skull cup and the copper knife resting on the sun at one's own heart. One meditates that the eight goddesses, the lotuses in their hands adorned with vases filled with nectar, pour water over one's crown.

Then, on "impassioned by the vajra": the vajra is the tathāgatas. Passion for them means this: having joined vajra and lotus, by the illusory sound of union one draws all the tathāgatas into oneself, and with oṃ sarva tathāgata anurāgana vajra svabhāva ātmako'haṃ one enjoys great bliss. These make up the samādhi of the First Union, whose nature is the nature body.

Then, within the lotus, from the transformation of each one's own seed, as one recites each one's syllable, one should emanate the goddesses of the gates and the corners and place them in their respective positions.

"The wisdom wheel is to be made to enter" means: having summoned the wisdom wheel by light arising from those seeds, one again dispels the obstructors and makes offerings, and then accomplishes the summoning, the entering, the binding and the bringing under control.

"Then the nectar is to be tasted" should be placed after "One should praise the lord of the wheel." "Having made offerings with the eight goddesses" means making offerings with the eight consorts emanated from the seed at one's own heart. "The lord of the wheel is to be praised" is connected with the song that comes later.

"Then meditate on the six limbs" teaches six colors as the places that are the nature of the six universal monarchs. There one should meditate on the lords of the wheel, in the order black, red, yellow, green, blue and white, as having the nature of innate joy alone. These make up the samādhi of the Supreme King of Maṇḍalas, whose nature is the enjoyment body; this is the second.

On "It is blessed by self-blessing": self-blessing is one's own wisdom, and blessing others by it means joining them to one's own wisdom. This is the third samādhi, called the Supreme King of Actions, whose nature is the emanation body.

This rite is also to be learned from the sādhanas. For now, what comes from the approach of the tantra should be applied here. Self-blessing, teaching five kinds of signs, is set out in the fourteenth chapter, which says:

"The blessing of self-blessing
I shall now explain in brief.
At the center of the heart, the moon;
upon the moon's form, the indestructible,
radiating light rays of many colors everywhere.
By that, meditate upon a vajra.
By the vajra, the yoginī Nairātmyā;
having meditated, and urged on by the rite,
dwelling within the tip of the ḍākinī's nose,
meditate on what is called a mustard seed.
The three realms, the moving and unmoving,
all beings without exception, meditate there.
By constantly meditating in this way,
self-blessing will be accomplished."

Here, "in brief" means in sequence. "At the center of the heart" means within the lotus of wisdom. The moon is the moon disc. "The indestructible" is the syllable hūṃ, adorned with a drop, a crescent and the sixth vowel. "Radiating light rays of many colors everywhere" means having rays of five colors. "By that, meditate upon a vajra" means imagining a blue vajra the size of a barley grain, arising from the transformation of hūṃ. "By the vajra, the ḍākinī Nairātmyā; having meditated" means marked with the vajra. "Urged on by the rite" means causing the descent through the union of the two organs. "Dwelling at the tip of the ḍākinī's nose" means at the meeting of the tips of both noses. "The three realms, the moving and unmoving" are the supporting and supported maṇḍala. "There" means within the hub of the vajra.

Alternatively, "at the center of the heart" means at one's own heart. The moon and the indestructible have already been explained. "Subtle" is the drop into which the vajra has transformed. "The ḍākinī Nairātmyā" shows Nairātmyā herself. "Urged on by the rite" means urged from the heart, as explained before. Where? "Dwelling at the tip of the ḍākinī's nose," that is, at the tip of the nose of the lotus. The rest is to be applied as explained before.

On "By the order and the stages of the rite, the signs are then to be marked": the order of the rite is the perfection of familiarization. "The stages" means that the signs arise one after another. Others say that the stages differ in their characteristics, yet arise at a single time.

"The first has the aspect of a cloud" means that the first ray of the subtle drop is dense like a cloud; it belongs to Heruka. "The second is like smoke" means that the second ray appears like a cloud of smoke hanging in the sky; it belongs to Śāśvata. "The third resembles a firefly." "Sky" means the dharma realm. udaya means rising and arising; it is the body of the buddha, and the light appearing in that way is the third; it belongs to the Sun. "The fourth blazes like a lamp" means it blazes like the flame of a lamp; it belongs to the Lotus. "The fifth, constantly appearing, is like a cloudless sky" means the appearance that shines continuously and clearly, like the sky; it is Paramāśva. "It is the cause of omniscience" means attaining samādhi. "Afterwards, in the manner of an illusion" means that, having attained samādhi, one gathers it into the maṇḍala of the signs, and, since it is false like a dream, one should gather the sun and moon discs, in an extremely subtle form, into selflessness itself. Through this one abides having realized everything dual as emptiness; this is the blessing at the stage of conduct through aspiration.

"Becomes in an instant" means that the mind is exceedingly joyful, because one realizes the nature of reality that one had not realized before. Moreover, because one realizes the dharmadhātu in its sense of pervading everything, the three spheres of self and other are equal, and a body appears like a reflection. This is the blessing of the ground called Perfect Joy.

In "The three realms, moving and unmoving," the moving are sentient beings, and the unmoving is the vessel world. "Without remainder, with nothing left out" means the whole of the desire realm and the formless realm. "Accomplished" means attaining the Dharma body, in which the three bases are transformed. "Having a vajra body" means entering the Immovable ground and the others.

If one asks what that is: the body that has gone beyond was spoken of above with the words "mass of wisdom" and so on. Here, "mass of wisdom" is the enjoyment body, adorned with the thirty-two major marks and the eighty excellent minor marks, because its nature is mirror-like wisdom and the other wisdoms. "Intelligent" is the emanation body, because it accords with the minds of all beings. It knows all aspects just as they are through pure worldly wisdom; that is the meaning of "learned." "Path of the three realms" is the nature of a completely pure buddha field, that is, a multistoried palace. "Clear" is said because it is built of seven blazing precious substances.

"All those born and arisen from karma" refers to the beings included in the four modes of birth: born from a womb, born from an egg, born from heat and moisture, and born miraculously. "Following the Great Seal" means that distinguished beings such as gods become the retinue of one who attains the Great Seal (mahāmudrā). "Enjoys all of them" means engaging in the enjoyment of the unsurpassed Dharma.

"Eight faces" is as the words say; it is so because of the purity of the eight doors of liberation. "With radiance like sapphire" is the color of the body; it is so because he is endowed with a mind of loving-kindness. "Four-legged" is because of the purity of the four māras. "The first face is black" is because of the purity of the door of signlessness. In "Adorned with white and red," it is white because the door of emptiness is purified, and red because the door of wishlessness is free of attachment. "Trampling the four māras evenly" refers to the māra of the aggregates, the māra of the afflictions, the māra who is the Lord of Death, and the māra who is a son of the gods. "Holding a vajra on the crown" means Akṣobhya.

In "The consorts of various forms," Mohavajrā is white, Dveṣavajrā is blue, Mātsaryavajrā is blue, Rāgavajrā is red, Īrṣyāvajrā is green, and the yoginī Nairātmyā is black. In "Holding a curved knife and a skull cup in the hands," the curved knife is in the right hand and the skull cup in the left. "Standing upon the lifeless" means standing with the left leg extended on a sun at the heart of a corpse. "Sky-goers" are at the heart and are blue in color. "Earth-goers" are at the throat and are red in color. "Underworld-goers" are at the crown and are white in color. In this tantra she possesses the light of the sun and a seat, so one meditates on her upon a sun maṇḍala; that is what is stated as "white form." The rest is easy to understand.

"The vase of bodhicitta, in the fire hearth, the great banner": these are the emblems of the goddesses who stand at the corners of the condensed maṇḍala. Their door guardians are in the corresponding order. Alternatively, one meditates on the emblems alone. The sādhana of the five universal monarchs should be learned from the Condensed Abhyudaya.

Bowing to Nairātmyā, who holds
a copper knife, a lotus, and a khaṭvāṅga,
I shall write her sādhana
step by step, in due order.

Here, the mantra practitioner is endowed with qualities such as the realization of emptiness, has a mind of awakening preceded by loving-kindness and the other immeasurables, has been empowered and authorized in the maṇḍala of the glorious Ḍākinīvajrapañjara, has special devotion to the profound, knows the ten suchnesses, abides in the pledges and vows, and strives to please his own mantra. In some delightful place such as a mountain, in a meditation house smeared with fragrant substances such as sandalwood, he should sit on a comfortable seat in the vajra posture, form his hands into the mudrā of meditative absorption, place the syllable aṃ on a sun at his own heart, and perform the washing of the face and so forth. With the mantra oṃrakṣarakṣa hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ phaṭ phaṭ he protects the place and himself. Then, by the light of the seed syllable at his own heart, he visualizes the circle of the maṇḍala before him, prostrates, and makes offerings. There, Gaurī holds a vessel of the moon, Caurī holds a vessel of the sun, Vetālī holds a vessel of water, Ghasmarī holds a vessel of medicine, Pukkasī holds a vessel of vajras, and Śabarī holds a vessel of corpse substance. Having made offerings with these, Caṇḍālī beats a small drum and Ḍombinī embraces him by the neck, and in this way he is to be pleased.

After that, one recites from "I go for refuge to the Three Jewels" up to "May I attain buddhahood for the benefit of beings." Then one says: "Before the assembly of sugatas I confess my heaps of wrongdoing"; "With faith I rejoice in the glory of the accumulations of merit of the sugata sages and of the holy beings of the three realms of existence"; "Until I reach the heart of awakening, I go for refuge to the victorious buddhas, who have utterly destroyed conceptual thought"; "Likewise I go for refuge to the supreme Dharma of the Great Vehicle"; "Likewise I go for refuge to the Saṅgha of bodhisattvas, the assembly of those who do not turn back"; and "I shall follow the path to unsurpassed awakening, the king of the reality of the Ḍākinīvajrajāla."

Then, in the manner taught in the words,

"Through the yoga of non-arising
this birth is well proclaimed,"

when all things are analyzed they are not found. Like the center of the autumn sky, they are without appearance. One meditates on them as free of conceptual elaboration, and blesses this with oṃ śūnyatājñvana vajra svabhālba ātmako'haṃ.

Then in front, from raṃ, a sun disk; on that sun hūṃ arises, and one imagines a vajra. One makes manifest the protection as in the words, "With that very vajra one should meditate; the fence and the net are to be firmly bound," and so on. In its center, from bhū, a yellow wheel with ten spokes, whirling like a darting glance. With the words,

"Through the application of ten hūṃs,
meditate on the ten wrathful ones,"

one imagines the ten wrathful deities. On the spokes, beginning in the east, is Yamāntaka, blue. In the south is Prajñāntaka, white. In the west is Padmāntaka, red. In the north is Vighnāntaka, blue. They hold, respectively, a hammer, a staff, a lotus, and a raised vajra. They are short and big-bellied, clad in the eight serpent ornaments, their tawny hair standing on end and blazing upward, their eyes red and round. They wear crown ornaments of Vairocana, Akṣobhya, Amitābha, and Amoghasiddhi, and skirts of tiger skin.

The wrathful ones of the intermediate directions are black. From Sumbha up to Uṣṇīṣacakravartin, they hold an uṣṇīṣa, a hook, a staff, a trident, a sword, and a pestle. In the southeast is Ṭakki, adorned with jewels and wearing divine garments. In the southwest is Nīladaṇḍa. In the northwest is Mahābala. These resemble Yamāntaka and the others. In the northeast is Acala, who is like Ṭakki. Above is Uṣṇīṣa, yellow. Below is Sumbha, black; his ornaments and so on are like Ṭakki's. All of them stand in the left-extended posture on seats of variegated lotus and sun. With their left hands they hold nāga nooses and make the threatening gesture. They bear crown ornaments of Akṣobhya and of jewels. In the center, the five maṇḍalas are furnished with yet other wrathful ones.

Then, having made the blazing mass, the lotus, the binding of the directions and so on, in its center is the mudrā called the source of phenomena, triangular, its interior of the nature of space. Upon a variegated vajra and lotus, from the seed syllables yaṃ raṃ baṃ laṃ, arise the maṇḍalas of wind, fire, water, and Mahendra (earth), smoky, red, white, and yellow in color. There Vairocana arises from bhrūṃ and a wheel. From the pores of his body streams of nectar drip, the four elements melt, and from that arises a multistoried mansion with four doors and four porches, adorned with four archways and adorned with garlands of flowers. In it, at the places of the fifteen deities, one meditates on variegated lotuses bearing corpse seats, adorned with sun disks at the heart. One should recite oṃ sarba tathāgatā nīlaya vajra svabhāva ādmako'haṃ.

Then, upon the central seat, one imagines a moon arisen from the fifteen vowels and a sun arisen from the thirty-four consonants, kāli and the rest. By their light, one should draw in the great qualities of the buddhas, which are like them, and increase them. Upon that, from the first vowel together with the bindu, arises a flaying knife marked with that very seed syllable. By means of its light rays, through the stages of emanating and gathering back the circle of the fifteen yoginīs, one should meditate on oneself as Nairātmyā. She sports in a dance with one leg half crossed upon the sun on the corpse's heart. Toward her head her tawny-red hair blazes upward and stands on end, flaming like the fire at the time of destruction. Her red body is formed from the subtlest particles of the supreme victors. She has three round eyes, and with light like ten million suns she burns up the māras. She is adorned about her body with a garland of fifty fresh heads, and ornamented with the five mudrās. When one imagines her as the nature of the dharmadhātu, abiding neither in existence nor in peace, one takes on the divine pride with oṃ dharmadhātu svabhvava ādmako'haṃ.

Her right hand brandishes the flaying knife; with her left she holds a skull cup filled with the blood of gods, asuras and others, and a khaṭvāṅga, held as an offering in the manner of method.

In the two eyes is Mohavajrā. In the ears is Dveṣavajrā. In the nose is Mātsaryavajrā. In the tongue is Rāgavajrā. In the body is Īrṣyāvajrā. In the mind is Nairātmyayoginī.

Again, for the aggregate of form and for delusion there is Vajrā. For feeling and for pride there is Vajragaurī. For perception and for desire there is Vāriyoginī. For formations and for jealousy there is Vajraḍākinī. For consciousness and for anger there is Nairātmyā. Likewise, the earth element and love are Pukkasī. Likewise, water and compassion are Śabarī. Fire and joy are Caṇḍālī. Wind and equanimity are Ḍombinī. By these, respectively, form, sound, smell, and taste are purified inwardly, while the tactile and the dharma element are imagined as the Dweller Below the Earth and Khecarī. Having imagined these with special care, from hūṃ on the moon at the heart arises Khecarī, blue.

At the moon in the throat, from a, arises the red Bhūcarī, the Earth-Goer. At the moon in the head, from oṃ, generate the white one who dwells beneath the earth. Then take on the divine pride with these: oṃ sarva tathāgata būja vajra svabhāva ātmako'haṃ, oṃ sarva tathāgata anurāgata vajra svabhāva ātmako'haṃ, oṃ sarva tathāgata vatalasini vajra svabhāva ātmako'haṃ.

With the light that streams from the aṃ at the hub of the copper knife on the sun at one's own heart, summon the tathāgatas, and say:

May all the tathāgatas
grant me empowerment!

At this they take the form of Heruka. Amid a rain of flowers and the sound of drums, Locanā and the others sing songs, Rūpavajrā and the others praise and make offerings, and then they confer the empowerment. Once empowered, meditate on Akṣobhya at the crown of the head and take on the divine pride with oṃ sarva tathāgata abhiṣekata vajra svabhāva ātmako'haṃ. This is the samādhi of the first union, which has the nature of the body of essential nature.

Then, from the seed syllable at one's own heart, send out the respective seed syllables onto moons and suns resting on the hearts of corpses in the fourteen places. From the complete transformation of the seed syllables and copper knives, one should meditate on the goddesses as before. āṃ īṃ īṃ uṃ are Vajrā, Gaurī, Vārī and Vajraḍākinī. ūṃ ṛṃ ṝṃ ḷṃ are Pukkasī, Śabarī, Caṇḍālī and Ḍombinī. ḹṃ aṃ eṃ aiṃ are Gaurī, Caurī, Vetālī and Ghasmarī. oṃ belongs to Bhūcarī. auṃ is Khecarī. Generate them in this order. Meditate on all of them standing in a dancing posture on the hearts of corpses, alike to Nairātmyā in color, attributes and the rest.

If one asks in which places, it is taught in another tantra:

In the east is Vajrā, in the south Gaurī;
in the western quarter, the Water Yoginī;
in the north, Vajraḍākinī;
in the center, the yoginī Nairātmyā.
In the northeast is Pukkasī;
in the southeast corner, Śabarī;
in the southwest corner, Caṇḍālī;
Ḍombī dwells always in the northwest.
At the eastern gate is Gaurī;
in the south, the one in the form of Caurī;
in the west is Vetālī;
likewise in the north, Ghasmarī.
Below is Bhūcarī,
and above, Khecarī is held to be.

Having thus seen the maṇḍala fully complete, with the hook of light that issues from the hearts of all of them draw in the lords of sages in the form of the fifteen yoginīs. The gatekeeper goddesses drive away the obstructors, and Gaurī and the others make offerings. With these, oṃ vajra gaurī ākarṣaya jaḥ n, oṃ vajra caurī phraveśaya hūṃ, oṃ vajra petālī bandhaya vaṃ, oṃ vajra ghasmarī veśyaṃ kuru hoḥ, perform the summoning and the rest. Then, as before, bless the eyes and the other senses. Preceded by the blessing of body, speech and mind, confer the empowerment, and one should meditate on the lord of the family upon the head. For Vajrā and the others these are Vairocana, Ratneśa, Amitābha and Amoghasiddhi. For those in the corners and the gatekeepers they are the Lord of Mind, Śāśvata, Ratneśa and Amitābha. For Bhūcarī it is Vairocana; for Khecarī, Amitābha.

Then Gaurī and the others, with skull cups brimming with their own offering substances, fully worship the Blessed One, the Buddha, with nectar, and the other members of the maṇḍala partake of it too: oṃ sarva tathāgata amṛta sābhāva ātmako'haṃ. Caṇḍālī should beat a small drum, and Ḍombī should embrace his body.

Then, with passion for the vajra produced in the secret lotus, make offering with oṃ sarva tathāgata būja vajra svabhāva ātmako'haṃ. Praise him with the verses beginning "You are surrounded by the yoginīs," and praise with oṃ sarva tathāgata stuti pūja vajra svabhāva ātmako'haṃ.

Again, with oṃ padma sukha dhara mahārāga sukhaṃ catur ānanda bhaga viśva hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ karyaṃ kuruṣva me and oṃ vajra mahādveṣa caturānanda dāyaka khag mukhai karāso nātha hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ karyaṃ kuruṣva me, engage in passion with the vajra. Then oṃ sarva tathāgata anurāgata vajra svabhāva ātmako'haṃ: this is the samādhi of the supreme king of maṇḍalas, whose nature is the body of complete enjoyment.

Then set out aṃ in the center of a sixty-four-petaled lotus at the navel, hūṃ in the center of an eight-petaled one at the heart, oṃ in the center of a sixteen-petaled one at the throat, and haṃ in the center of a thirty-two-petaled one at the head. Then, as in the passage that begins:

By kindling caṇḍālī at the navel
the five tathāgatas are burned,
and Locanā and the others are burned as well;
once they are burned, the moon drips from haṃ,

He meditates on himself as the fierce wrathful one who slays, and at midnight or at noon he sits with a staff of leg bone and of nimba leaves and presses down with his left foot. If he recites, with wrathful words, the garland mantra interspersed with "So-and-so māraya phaṭ," the target will die within seven days.

With a sharp blade he cuts the effigy, beginning from the little toe of the left foot, recites the garland mantra together with words of urging, and offers the pieces in the fire offering in great wrath. In namaḥ sarva nāganaṃ, nāganaṃ means the eight nāgas, Ananta and the rest. hṛdaya philata means piercing the heart, in order to make the rain fall. In the last month of summer this is done through Karkoṭa, in the first month of autumn through Śaṅkhapāla, in the middle month of autumn through Takṣaka, and in the last month of autumn through Vāsuki. In the same way, one should understand that rain is brought down in order in the middle month of spring and the other months.

Although it was taught before, it will be explained here. As taught before, one abides in the samādhi of the vajra of Mahābala, four-faced and eight-armed, or one-faced and two-armed. Abiding in that, on an unfired earthen bowl, one draws with indigo juice a wheel of eight sword blades. In its center, at the hub of an eight-petaled lotus, one writes the heart mantras of the nāgas interspersed with the mantra of Śūlinī. One joins the bowls mouth to mouth and binds them with blue thread. One summons Śūlinī and the nāgas, makes offerings, and gives them commands. Then the joined bowls, which urge on the nāgas, are placed in a pond where nāgas dwell, or a similar place.

Alternatively, on a palm leaf one writes in ink a five-pronged crossed vajra together with the words of the mantra, and visualizes it as blazing flames. From it arises the goddess Ekajaṭī, with one face and two arms, holding a copper knife and a skull cup, blue in color, her tawny hair standing upright and bound with three skulls. One meditates on her, makes offerings, gives her commands, and throws the palm leaf into a nāga lake.

Then, in another solitary place, one draws a fire maṇḍala, on it a wind maṇḍala, in its center a water maṇḍala, and on that an eight-petaled lotus. At its hub one draws the water god (Varuṇa), with the upper body of a man and seven serpent hoods, white in color, holding a snake noose in his two hands. In the east and the other directions are Ananta, pale blue; Vāsuki, white; Takṣaka, red; and Karkoṭa, blue. In the southeast and southwest corners are Padma and Mahāpadma, gray. In the northwest corner is Śaṅkhapāla, yellow. In the northeast is Kulika, multicolored.

Then one generates all the nāgas from their syllables and performs the rest of the procedure. Reciting the syllable phuḥ, one offers molasses, milk and the other things that delight the nāgas most. With praises, one offers blue flowers, scents, blue banners, and incense: aromatic powder, Chinese incense, kunduru resin, nakha, white mustard seed and powdered nāgakesara, or else powdered nāgakesara mixed with sugar. One also offers lamps fed with butter.

In the same way one offers a blue vase filled with blue water, summoning the nāgas and making them enter with the mantra interspersed with the syllable phuḥ. Having worshipped them in this way, beginning with the offering of arghya water, one says: "By the command of the Blessed One, nāgas, take delight in me and let the rain fall!" One visualizes the nāgas sending rain down from the sky and recites the mantra together with the words of urging, oṃ śula pāni varṣa paya śāghraṃ oṃ jyoti nirana da parṣa payaśīgbraṃ. Then the rain will fall.

If even then it does not fall, make a round fire pit and draw a nāga maṇḍala right there. Invite the nāgas, and visualize the syllable naṃ on their tongues. Preceded by offerings such as argha water, offer three ladlefuls of butter mixed with molasses, and pour in fire offerings of milk and the like mixed with molasses. When one offers what has been consecrated with oṃ anan, they are satisfied and send down rain.

If even then it does not fall, they should be punished. Draw the maṇḍalas in the sequence of wind, fire and so forth. In their center, enter the meditative absorption of the wrathful Mahābala, and visualize a garuḍa trampling the tails of the nāgas, and above it Mahābala, like a blazing mass of fire, tormenting the nāgas with his eight arms. Recite this very mantra, interspersed with oṃ and together with naṅ, joined to the urging words varṣapaya śīghraṃ, intoning it forcefully and uttering threats. If one then imagines the nāgas, terrified of the wrathful one, sending rain from the sky as though intoxicated, it will fall.

If even that does not make it fall, then fierce rites should be performed against the nāgas in their small, middling and great forms. Their sequence is as follows. On the cloth of one killed by a weapon, or on charnel-ground cloth, using poison, mustard seed, salt, black butter and pungent spices, draw an eight-petaled lotus at the hub of a wheel of swords. On the petals write the heart mantras of the nāgas, and at the center write paṃ interspersed with hūṃ. From earth taken from both banks of a river, from a charnel ground, ashes, earth from an anthill and earth from the foot of a solitary tree, together with excrement and urine, make an effigy of a nāga and insert the cloth into its heart. Preceded by summoning, smear it with the three pungent spices and mustard seed. Kindle a fire of charnel-ground wood in a triangular fire pit, burn the effigy in the fire, and recite the mantra one hundred and eight times; then it will fall. This is the small rite.

The middling rite: if even by that it does not come, draw a nāga maṇḍala on that same ground. Wearing blue clothing and a blue crown tied on, or else naked, with the left foot, marked by a blazing vajra and resting on a sun, press down the effigy laid on its back. Split its crown with a forcefully intoned phaṭ, and imagine a rain of blood falling from the sky. Then, interspersing the mantra with eight naṃ as before, intone the mantra joined to the urging words varāpaya śīghraṃ and recite wrathful words; then it will fall.

The great rite: if even by that it does not fall, make an effigy of a nāga from poison, salt, mustard seed and datura, and drive a nine-finger peg of khadira wood into its heart. In a triangular fire pit kindle a butcher's fire, or the charnel-ground fire called "phü." Invite Mahābala into the fire and meditate on him. Smear one's body with excrement and urine and face south. Mahābala opens his wrathful mouth, and his tongue is consecrated with hūṃ. Beginning from the effigy's tail, chop it with a sharp blade and offer it in one hundred and eight fire offerings, reciting the mantra in reverse order; then it will fall.

If it has not fallen after three times, then make an effigy of Mahābala himself from six ingredients together with mustard seed and datura, and summon him. Visualize oneself as Sumbha, and with a foot marked by a vajra resting on a sun, press down the effigy laid on its back. If one recites the four-hūṃ mantra joined to the urging words varṣapaya śeghraṃ forty thousand times, Mahābala will set about sending down rain. If even by that it does not come, then invite King Sumbha into the hearth for fierce rites. Visualizing oneself as Sumbha with open mouth marked by hūṃ, chop that same earlier effigy with the four-hūṃ mantra including the words cchinda clinda, and perform the fire offering ten thousand times; then without doubt Mahābala himself will send down rain. So it is renowned in all the practices.

Once the rain has fallen, in order to bring relief to the nāgas and to Mahābala, make a white, round fire pit, and into the mouths of Vairocana and the others perform ten thousand fire offerings of butter, milk and so forth, each with their own mantras; then without doubt peace will be restored.

When the rain is excessive, in the hearth for fierce rites taught earlier, offer one hundred fire offerings of old flowers that were offered to the hero Vajrasattva, together with cotton; then it will stop.

Here, "existence" is the outer object of sentient beings, and also arising. "Nonexistence" is the inner apprehending, and also cessation. "Middle" is the in-between, and also abiding. It is free of all of these. If one asks what it is: "It arises from the elements and from what has come from the elements." This is the undefiled aggregate of form. "All sentient beings" are the buddhas. "Body" is the secret body. "Generating" is arising. "Victor" means what is definitively shown. Explained by way of etymology, it is jin jikra.

vajra sūrya is a name. tomo bidyana means what dispels the darkness of the six kinds of pride. samya stāṃ haṃ means one whose nature is the equality of all the buddhas.

ratna dhṛk: this too is explained from the explanation of the Guhyasamāja, where it is said:

"Because all aims arise from it,
Mind is explained to be the jewel.
Because the knower knows with certainty,
Feeling is held to be the jewel-holder."

Here, "mind" is mind itself. If one asks what it is like: "because all aims arise from it" means that the qualities of the buddhas and bodhisattvas arise from it as a single taste. That very thing is the knower, the one who knows suchness. "Certainty" means without doubt. "Knowing" is experiencing: the object of feeling that is the nature of mind. "Feeling" is the nature of the knowing of equality. Explained by way of etymology, it is ratna dhṛkra.

phu phu phu hi hi hi is the natural sound of a horse. Or else, "phu" means emanating and soaring, and "hi" means going like a horse, that is, going into all the emanation bodies.

prajñaṃdhṛkra: this too is explained from the explanation of the Guhyasamāja, where it is said:

"Because it is utterly clear, consciousness
Is declared to be prajñā.
Because mind is held through formations,
It is declared to be the wisdom-holder."

Here, "utterly clear" means naturally luminous. If one asks what it is: it is consciousness. By etymology, jñā is consciousness and phra is luminosity. "Formations" is the aggregate of formations. If one asks what it is like: it is the buddhas contemplating suchness respectfully and with diligence.

This concludes the explanation of the ninth chapter from The Expansion of Reality, a commentary on the difficult points of the Ḍākinī Vajra Net.

"With the vajra eye" means with unobstructed eyes. "With a net of various miracles" means miracles endowed with intense conviction, with manifest orientation, and with freedom from deliberate effort. Here, intense conviction is being convinced that sentient beings are inseparable from the form of the buddhas, and that the buddhas too are inseparable from the bodies of sentient beings. "Manifest orientation" means displaying treasuries and the like out of the sky. "Freedom from deliberate effort" means displaying birth, what is called awakening, the turning of the wheel of Dharma, and so on, as spontaneously accomplished.

This concludes the explanation of the tenth chapter from The Expansion of Reality, a commentary on the difficult points of the Ḍākinī Vajra Net.

"Peaceful mind" means a mind that is clear and serene. As for "empowerment": abiding in the yoga of a white-colored deity such as Vairocana, one deals with those stricken by illness, such as one who looks like a monkey. One places the person who is the object of the rite in one's own heart, or in the lotus of the wisdom consort, upon a water maṇḍala, empowers him with a stream of nectar from a white vase that one holds, and views him as having the body of the deity formed from the finest particles of all the buddhas.

"Expanding mind" is mentation. Here too, through the yoga of Vajrasūrya, the person who is the object of the rite, placed upon the earth maṇḍala of Mahendra, is empowered with a stream of water from a yellow vase, as before.

"Passionate mind" means a mind of attachment. Abiding in the yoga of Nartiśvara and the others, one empowers with a stream of water from a red vase, as before.

"Fierce mind": fierce means not tolerating what is not fierce, that is, a mind that is terrible. As for the man, he is killed by the rain from massed clouds.

This concludes the explanation of the eleventh chapter from The Expansion of Reality, a commentary on the difficult points of the Ḍākinī Vajra Net.

"Mountain hollows and thickets" means resting places on the slopes. Or else, because one does not stir from tasting the awakening mind, the mountain hollow is the wisdom consort, and the cave in the thicket is the lotus that lies within her source of phenomena.

In "adorned with flowers, fruits, and so on," the flowers are past karma, and the fruit is the body of this present life, because it is karmic maturation. Or else, the flower is the pollen of the flower. "And so on" should be taken to include youth and the like.

Regarding "the awakening mind" and what follows: one may wonder, "If things such as the apprehended and the apprehender are merely imagined, how do they become the ultimate awakening mind?" To answer this, the text says, "When things are examined as true or false," and so on. The meaning is that when they are examined by reasoning, they cannot be things that are apprehended or apprehenders, so they are not true, and mind alone remains.

Suppose one says, "Even though the apprehended and the rest cannot be external things, they exist substantially as consciousness itself." To answer this, the text says, "By this reasoning of nonarising," and so on. Just as this reasoning of one and many and the like refutes external objects, it refutes consciousness as well. Here nothing at all exists ultimately, whether as something left in equanimity or as an entity.

"This arising" is empty, that is, it arises as the nature of emptiness.

Regarding oṃ and what follows: oṃ means this, śānyatājñāna, because it is mere utter clarity. "Vajra" is so called because its own nature is indivisible and because it destroys all obscurations. Or else, the mirror-like wisdom that has emptiness as its meaning is called oṃ, the wisdom of equality is śūnyatā, and the discriminating wisdom is jñana.

The wisdom of accomplishing activity is vajra, because the emanation body remains for as long as space endures. The utterly pure wisdom is svabhāva ātmako'haṃ.

"Deathless union" means abiding in the yoga of the enemy of obstructors within the protection wheel. "Vajra maṇḍala" means the wind maṇḍala. The light maṇḍala is the mirror maṇḍala. The ḍāka maṇḍala is the maṇḍala of Hevajra. "Portion of earth" means the maṇḍala of Nairātmyā.

This concludes the explanation of the twelfth chapter from The Expansion of Reality, the commentary on the difficult points of the Ḍākinī Vajra Net.

"Action tantra" means focusing on deities such as Jambhala as external, and practicing chiefly cleanliness, silence and the like. "The application of action" means focusing on something outside oneself. "Yoga" means focusing on the wisdom of one's own wheel-turning lord and oneself as being of one taste. "Higher yoga" means practicing the great secret of the supreme joy that arises from embracing one's own consort. "Unsurpassed yoga" means focusing on the supreme bliss born from the union of vajra and lotus of one's own deity.

Regarding the line "Emptiness and compassion inseparable": Emptiness has the characteristic of the complete absence of the two; it is the limit of reality. It has synonyms such as the ultimate and suchness. Compassion is the maṇḍala of deities in which the five perfections are gathered together, and being inseparable from it, it has become of one taste. Into it enter the two syllables ho of the causal Vajradhara and the two interspersed syllables, or else the two bodies may enter. When they are melted by the fire of passion blazing in the lotus at the navel, the nature of the two bodies having melted is emptiness. Benefiting beings through the rays of light that arise from it is compassion, and inseparable from it is the nature of the deity's body.

This concludes the explanation of the thirteenth chapter from The Expansion of Reality, the commentary on the difficult points of the Ḍākinī Vajra Net.

Regarding "the domain of the excellent," the excellent are the accomplishments such as pills. The domain is conduct, extending up to the words "the means of accomplishment." "Vajra" means Hevajra. "Three methods": here one should apply the determination stated elsewhere:

Fair Lustre, earth, the hare-marked one, and the light-maker,
Three, four, and below the number of the Vedas,
And according to the numbers of the sages, the qualities and the arrows:
Together with ratis, one should roll them into pills.

Or else, as it is stated elsewhere:

Gold and silver and likewise copper,
Below fire, arrows and pair,
Sixteen, three and six, in that order:
Together with olsé, roll them into thick pills.

It is to be done just as stated there. "King of nāgas" means a two-headed snake. "Seed" means semen. "Vajra finger" means fingers shaped like an elephant's trunk and so on. "Vajra tongue" means the tongue marked with hūṃ and a vajra. "Vajrasattva" means one's own organ. "Vajra embrace" means binding tightly at the neck. "Vajra essence" means indestructible bliss. "Lotus attached" means joined with the vajra.

"One should draw in the one of vajra nature" means performing union, and this is to be learned from the guru's instructions. "One will go to the place of the vajra-holder" means: upon a chariot drawn by a ram, one places a sun disc arising from raṃ, and there, from hūṃ, one generates him in the form of the vajra-holder; one blesses the eyes and so on, protects him, leads him to the place of union and so forth, and causes him to dwell there. That is the meaning.

"The yoga of self-blessing" means the yoga of the completion stage. "Becomes the center of the heart" means at the juncture where the two jewels are joined. "Vajra Nairātmyā ḍākinī" means imagining, from the transformed vajra, a precious jewel, a tiny white mustard seed whose nature is Nairātmyā. "Stable and moving" means the circle of the maṇḍala; some say it is the dripping of the awakening mind. "Pure end" means the limit of purity, that which has become the central channel. "Net of conceptions" means the grasped and the grasper. "Not inert matter" is said because it is mere luminous clarity. "False" means the grasped and so forth. "Self" means through the form of thorough affliction. "The five obscurations" are longing for sense pleasures, ill will, lethargy, sleep, and doubt. "Omniscience" means mastery of all aspects.

In order to refute accomplishment from a cause that does not accord with the result, the text says "Thus the thirty-two major marks" and so on. Its meaning is this: only the circle of the maṇḍala in which the five perfections are gathered, embraced by the Perfection of Wisdom, is the method; other approaches, and emptiness alone, lead through mere peace to the attainment of an unfavorable state, like the sense source of infinite space and the rest.

"Not true" is said because, when examined in terms of one and many, it is mere appearance. "Not false either" is said because it displays the various enjoyment bodies and emanation bodies. "Not to be rejected" is said because there is no essential nature of obscuration to be abandoned. "Not to be adopted" is said because that would be producing what did not exist before.

"Fist" means all the fingers bent. "Lion's ear" means the thumb and middle finger slightly bent. "Kāma" means dri yang ku. "Bola" means gandharisa. "Sālija" means khanadasa. "Kacora" means śati.

Regarding "This is the maṇḍala of accumulation," the word "this" gives the reason: because the accumulation of merit is gathered. "Vajra mind" means generated by hūṃ. "There" means the tip of the lotus, the jewel of the lotus.

"Melody" means speech: in that jewel one places the speech of the buddhas, whose nature is a. "Wish-fulfilling jewel" means the vajra jewel. "At the door of the jewel, the syllable phaṭ": this is a yellow phaṭ.

"The eye hand" means this: from behind the index finger, the middle finger lifts the tip of the index finger, making it like an eye, and it is placed above the ear. "The listening hand" means making oneself as if two-tongued and turning all around. "One should turn with the left" means this: above the left hand turned palm down, one holds the right hand in a fist and raises the thumb.

"Vajra steps" means the gaits of the one-pronged vajra, the three-pronged vajra, the five-pronged vajra, and the crossed vajra. "The ten-mouthed manner" means moving all ten fingers and making them pointed. It is of four kinds: the manner of the lion's gait, the manner of the lion's stretch, the manner of the terrifying vajra, and the manner of the snake's head.

"Bright staff" means joining "staff" with "bright," and producing ordinary sounds. These are the seven modes beginning with "arising," the seven modes of making the sounds of the lute and other instruments. "The manner of the staff" means raising the thumb as a staff. These are twelve kinds, such as the powers of a buddha. "The second banner" means holding the index finger and thumb like tongs. "The third banner" means doing the same with the thumb and middle finger.

This is the explanation of the fourteenth chapter from the commentary on the difficult points of the Ḍākinī Vajra Net, The Expansion of Reality.

kurukulle means the goddess Kurukullā. The oral instructions explain that she is seated in a dancing posture with legs half crossed, adorned with ornaments of human bone and the like, and dwells in the center of the eight charnel grounds.

maṇi means the discerning wisdom, which is like a priceless jewel. Someone may ask, "If wisdom discerns, does it not become conceptual?" For this reason padma is mentioned. The meaning is that just as a lotus is not stained by mud, that wisdom is not stained by the fault of conceptual thought. For that very reason hūṃ phaṭ is mentioned. hūṃ means the mind empty of grasped object and grasping subject. phaṭ means tearing apart conceptual thought.

mañjuvajraya is a name. mu means the supreme mind of all the buddhas. hrīḥ means throwing all the māras onto their backs. ṣṭṛḥ means subduing, by baring the fangs, the hostile ones such as hungry ghosts and hell-beings. vikṛtānana means terrifying with a distorted face. The two pairs of hūṃ phaṭ dispel aging and death.

In mārīci, mā means māra and ci means suppressing; because she suppresses the māras she is Mārīci. vasudhar means inviting. In vasupṛṣṭinipadani, vasu means wealth such as jewels, and vṛṣṭi means its rain. jambhala is a name. jalendrāya means that by giving water he grants lordship. maṇidhara is so called because men and women hold the jewel in the womb. vajri means that, by destroying the lower realms, he leads beings out of their terrors with the vajra. mahāpratisāra means destroying the great sufferings of those who have entered hell and the like, and making them no longer appear. hūṃ phaṭ means destroying and causing to fall.

mahākālāya is a name. śasana upahari apa means bringing harm upon those who harm the teaching. yati pratijñā smarasi means "you have made a vow." kha kha means offered. khāhi khāhi is explained as giving. gṛhṇa gṛhṇa is to be joined with "fever and the like." bandha bandha is to be joined with "a noose at the throat." daha daha is to be joined with "make the blaze fierce." paca paca is the same; it means that the fierce-rite wheel described earlier is to be performed in three stages.

"The cherished moment" is complete joy. Of these, "moment" is supreme joy. "The moment of the cause" is special joy. "Nature" is the nature of coemergent joy. That itself is great bliss and is also uncontaminated bliss. Special joy is what lies beyond, and supreme joy is the limit.

In the passage beginning "entity," the sixth is Vajrasattva. "Perfect buddha" is to be joined with the first. "Dharma" means the view of selflessness, the root, which is the reason for the purity of generosity and the other perfections. Sacred commitment means guarding and eating. Of these, guarding concerns harming the Three Jewels and the like. Eating means the five nectars and the five foods.

"The maṇḍala should be drawn" means that one should always perform the maṇḍala of colored powders, the feast gathering, and the yoga of the three meditative absorptions.

"Binding mudrās is to be given up" applies to yogins of the completion stage who experience the bliss of the great seal, not to beginners. For beginners, giving up the binding of mudrās would itself become a misdeed. In "maṇḍala and so on," the word "and so on" includes other bodily movements.

"One should not perform bodily acts" means giving up the acts of the three kinds of yoga. Those who have gained a little mastery over wisdom cause the divining image to descend upon a boy and a girl and have them perform the maṇḍala and so forth. Those who have gained mastery over all wisdom emanate and withdraw the maṇḍala of the mind. Those who have brought perfect wisdom under their control should show that very emanated maṇḍala to be like a reflection and so on.

"Do not bow to one who is not the master" means not bowing to kings and others for the sake of gain and the like. "Guru" means one who teaches mantra and so forth. "Elder" means a senior master. The rest concerns beginners and is easy to understand.

In the passage beginning "empowered by the glorious Vajradhara," the master meant is one who knows the ten principles, is skilled in the work of drawing the maṇḍala, abides by the pledges and vows, is always endowed with the yoga of the three meditative absorptions, and moreover has the qualities already described. Such a master is called "the glorious Vajradhara" because he is very close to Vajradhara. He should apply the rite of empowerment exactly as it is laid down, and no one else should do so.

In the passage beginning "death," "death" is not death in the true sense. It is like going from one grove to another to attend a sacrificial feast.

"The abode of the sky-goers" is reached by those who have gained mastery over true wisdom, and mastery over wisdom means attaining that state.

"Sukhāvatī" means the world realm of Supreme Joy, Abhirati. It is attaining lordship over the ten grounds.

"If one wishes" refers to those who have gained a little mastery over wisdom. Because they have gained mastery over birth, they will be born wherever they wish.

As for beginners, even if they go to the lower realms by the force of their karma, the buddhas and bodhisattvas take care of them by various rites. This is because of their corresponding aspirations, and because they have received empowerment.

The line "Places the vajra and bell in the hands" is given as an illustration, and the other items should be applied in the same way. Their meaning is this. When one generates oneself in the form of one's chosen deity, preceded by inviting the wisdom being, one blesses the eyes and so on, and then recites the garland of mantras many times.

The line "By applying the vajra fire pit" means the following. One draws the maṇḍala properly and, preceded by the sādhana of the maṇḍala and so on, fully confers the empowerments of water, crown, vajra, lord, name and vase. Having made offerings and praises and tasted the nectar, one recites the mantras many times. Then, at its heart, one generates Vajrasattva, or one's own special deity together with the deities of the maṇḍala, merges the wisdom maṇḍala into one with oneself, and visualizes requesting them to depart into sameness. Then one should burn it in the place of the peaceful fire offering and thoroughly purify it. If one does not do so, it becomes equivalent to destroying a stūpa.

This concludes the explanation of the fifteenth chapter from The Expansion of Reality, a commentary on the difficult points of the Ḍākinī Vajra Net.

Through the instructions I received at the feet of my guru,
I have unraveled, just as it is, this vast king of tantras, so hard to understand.
By whatever stainless, perfect virtue has been gained from this,
may all the multitudes of beings travel the path that is free of suffering.

In the passage on the Tongs goddess, the text reads "those who sit" (འདུག་པ), which makes little sense; it is evidently a slip for "the wicked" (གདུག་པ), as in the following lines, and is translated so. The yogini names Pārīḍākinī and the goddesses "she who moves below the earth," Tongs, Noose, Cooking, Hook, Flower and Incense are rendered as the text gives them rather than assigned uncertain Sanskrit forms. The point seems to be grammatical: a nominative-looking form is used with a locative or adverbial sense. "Rtag pa" in the gloss on "the actual buddha" is taken as the deity name Śāśvata, matching the parallel naming of the other wisdoms. The commentary on "to tend the inexhaustible aim of beings" (མགྲོན་དུ་གཉེར་བ…) is obscure in the Tibetan, and the rendering is tentative. Ḍombinī's ground is paired with "the absence of conditioning activity," not with an element, as the text has it. "Chos kyi dbyibs" ("form of the Dharma") is read as "chos kyi dbyings," the dharmadhātu, since the five wisdoms are being enumerated; "steng pa" in the quoted line is read as "stong pa" (emptiness); "pha rol tu phyin pa lA" is read as "five perfections"; "rin pa che" is read as "rin po che" (jewel). The flower "mdza' ba'i me tog" is taken as the japā flower (hibiscus), the standard example in the crystal analogy. The chapter colophon gives the commentary's title as "Clarifying Reality," whereas the opening title has "Expanded Reality"; translated as it stands. The phrase rendered "if even someone devoted to meditation and recitation is not sustained by the deity's gaze" is obscure in the Tibetan (literally "is not nourished by the eyes"); the sense is tentative. "The feet are placed" reads ཞབས for the written ཞགས ("noose"), an evident slip. In the cited verse, "fully joining" reads རབ for the written བ, as the commentary's own gloss (རབ་ཏུ་སྦྱོར་བ) shows. "'jig rten dpang" is read as "'jig rten dbang" (Lokeśvara). The phrase "de dang bcas pas don gcig por go bar bya" is obscure, and the rendering "serving one purpose" is tentative.
