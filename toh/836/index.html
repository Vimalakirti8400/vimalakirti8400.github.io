About this text

The Tantra Called the Goddess, the Great Net of Illusion (Sanskrit: Devī Jāla Mahāmāyā Tantra; Tibetan: Lhamo Gyuthrul Draba Chenmo zhes jawé Gyü) is a Buddhist tantra, a scripture presenting esoteric teachings and practices centered on a feminine deity associated with illusion and the interwoven "net" of manifestation. It opens with homage to the Blessed One, the Great Secret, and frames the revelation as a discourse in which the Blessed One, the Tathāgata, is present with an assembly of great yogīs and yoginīs, each bearing a symbolic name such as Wheel of Yoga and Gathered into Vajra Itself. In the Tibetan canon it is Toh 836 in the Kangyur, the collection of texts attributed to the Buddha, where it belongs to the Tantra section.

In the Indian language: Devī Jāla Mahāmāyā Tantra. In the Tibetan language: The Tantra called the Goddess, the Great Net of Illusion.

Homage to the Blessed One, the Great Secret.

Thus it was spoken. At that time the Blessed One, the Tathāgata, was present together with the great yogī who is equal to space, with the great yoginī, with the great one of supreme devotion, with the one who guards those who have devotion, with the great consort who protects and thoroughly guards, who stays close, with the great pacified one, and the great one who brings about peace, beautiful and radiant in color, endowed with knowledge and refuge. They are these: the chief called Wheel of Yoga; the great yogī called Gathered into Vajra Itself; the great yogī called Equanimity; the great yogī called the One Who Brings Under Power; and the yogī who thoroughly holds the manner of the full expanse. In whatever form the Teacher showed, they were endowed with four colors, and in the manner of sitting they wore the five-fold crown. Unobscured, not obscuring, not blocking what obscures, from the unobscured expanse, in the great, truly present celestial palace, by the great vajra manner of equality, with the body unimpeded, the Blessed One dwelt, his activity inconceivable.

Moreover, each one thus clearly distinct, the great equal one with the retinue, great in resources, abiding in the nature of holding the burden of all, unmistaken through reliance, and without any multiplicity of two, is the dhāraṇī of the great yoginī. They are: the Great Awakened Lady called Space; the Great Awakened Lady called Space-Holder; the Great Awakened Lady called Earth-Guardian; the Great Awakened Lady, the Great Moistener; the Great Awakened Lady called Dispeller of Arrogance; the Great Awakened Lady called Accomplisher of Action; the Great Awakened Lady called Holder of the Great Intense Attachment of Vajra, who also slays non-attachment; the Great Awakened Lady called Thorough Dispeller of Bewilderment; and the Great Awakened Lady called Wheel of Speech.

They too are thus: of a color like a most vivid blue lotus, and with sapphire, beryl, ruby, and emerald, and other substances praised, they dwell noble and radiant, with none unripened. These are the yoginī Samantabhadrā together with her retinue.

Moreover there is great reverence, the secret; great reverence, the manifold fragrances; and great reverence, the one who commands, who is called Lord of Secrets. There is the one who holds clarity, the one who becomes the abode of fragrance, and the one who holds the form that makes sound. There is the faculty that causes seeing, the faculty that causes hearing, the faculty of the great one who causes smelling, and the faculty of the great one who causes tasting. There is the one who thoroughly releases, the one who thoroughly conceives, the great sovereign who thoroughly holds, and the one who thoroughly accomplishes, together with the consort who never abandons her form.

There is the one who conceives what exists, the one who dispels what does not exist, the one who brings about the perception of victory, and the great one who thoroughly destroys cessation. Bound as non-dual through the action of manifest conduct, they dwell in the great celestial palace of the expanse of phenomena (dharmadhātu), the great equality.

Then the Blessed One, delighted in the celestial palace of the great secret yoginī, with the great gathering of wisdom of the unborn expanse, complete with every ornament, a maṇḍala like the full moon pervading without center or edge, complete with all jewels, with corners, sides, colors, and shapes of immeasurable extent, with inconceivable wisdom, adorned in splendid manner, richly decorated with the ornaments of the great wish-fulfilling jewel.

Bearing the lineage of the Victors through their various distinctions, possessed of the four gates of great magical display, the outer yogas of the vehicles become the Great Vehicle, together with the supreme of the great virtue and vice, the very great secret, at the center of the great lotus, beautified by the distinctions of the four colors of the tongue, it became the maṇḍala of the great secret, in the manner of the four directions and the three tiers.

Then the Blessed One, the Great Samantabhadra, together with the yoginīs, abided in a single thought. He knew the great lord of yoga, endowed with supreme practice, to be the entirety of conduct and the meaning of the profound. Knowing this, he spoke this method of the great magical display.
