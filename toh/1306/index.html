About this text

The Sādhana of Nairātmyā, known in Sanskrit as the Nairātmyāsādhana and in Tibetan as Dakmema zhejawé drubpé tap, is a short verse manual for the meditative practice of the goddess Nairātmyā, "She Who Is Selflessness," the consort of Hevajra and the embodiment of the wisdom that realizes no-self. Composed by the Indian scholar Durjayacandra and translated into Tibetan by the Indian preceptor Gayadhara with the Tibetan translator Shākya Yeshé, it guides the practitioner from the choice of a solitary place and the preliminary offerings, confession and refuge through the four immeasurables, the protective circle and the visualization of the palace, to the generation of Nairātmyā and her circle of fifteen yoginīs from the vowels of the Sanskrit alphabet. It goes on to the empowerment, the six-limbed yoga and the inner fire, sets out the purity of each goddess as the aggregates, elements, sense objects and afflictions, and closes with her mantra and a torma offering. The work is Toh 1306 in the Tengyur, where it belongs among the tantra commentaries as a sādhana of the Hevajra cycle.

Translation

In the language of India: nai ra ātma sādhanaṃ nāma. In the language of Tibet: The Sādhana of Nairātmyā.

Homage to the Blessed Lady Nairātmyā.

Seeing the women of saṃsāra who were to be tamed,
You transformed into the yoginī Nairātmyā:
Homage to you, Sage who has gone to the far shore!
Homage, I bow to the mudrā of illusion.

When the yogin first begins to train,
Having examined a good place that is sure,
The place where the mantrin will practice,
With a single-pointed mind in meditative equipoise,
A mountain, a thicket or a rock overhang,
Adorned with flowers, fruits and the like,
Sitting there in the vajra posture,
At the heart, from paṃ, a lotus;
Upon it, from raṃ, a sun maṇḍala.
There one should clearly imagine an a;
From it light rays stream forth,
And one visualizes in the sky the Venerable Lady,
Arisen from the vajra, of great compassion,
And worships her with eight goddesses
Who hold all the ornaments.
Gaurī holds the hare-marked moon;
Caurī holds the vessel of the sun;
Vetālī has water in her hand;
Ghasmarī holds medicine;
Pukkasī has a vajra in her hand;
Likewise Śavarī holds the elixir;
Caṇḍālī beats the ḍamaru drum.
With these the principal deity is worshipped.
Clinging to her with great passion,
Ḍombinī embraces her about the shoulders.
Through the guru one worships the buddhas well;
Having worshipped the vajra guru,
One should perform the sevenfold purification:
Confession of the wrongs one has done,
And the vow to refrain from doing them again,
Rejoicing in all merit,
Then the full dedication.
Go for refuge to the Three Jewels,
Generate the mind set on supreme awakening,
Abide in the path, and offer oneself
For the sake of others,
And also receive permission.
With the four abodes of Brahmā, refresh the mind:
First one should cultivate loving-kindness;
Second, cultivate compassion in its particulars;
Third, one should cultivate joy;
Last of all comes equanimity.
After that, make the mind of awakening firm.
Then comes the pledge of protection.
If at first one examines true and false
And engages in conceptual thought, one cannot meditate.
Because one is wholly joined to that which is without generation,
This generation is fully explained.
oṃ śūñatā jñaana vajra svabhāva ātam ko'haṃ.
Fully generated as emptiness, from the hūṃ that came first,
One should meditate on the crossed vajra.
To dispel the māras of beings,
One should clearly send forth emanations from hūṃ.
Say: oṃ rakṣa rakṣa hūṃ hūṃ.
From this one should also meditate
On a tent encircled by vajras, and a canopy.
In the realm of space imagine a bhaga.
In its center one should meditate:
From yaṃ arises the maṇḍala of wind,
Blue, bow-shaped, marked with banners;
From raṃ arises the maṇḍala of fire,
Triangular and red, encircled by a garland of vajras;
From baṃ arises a round maṇḍala,
White, marked by a ring of vases;
From laṃ arises a square maṇḍala,
Yellow, encircled by a garland of vajras.
From bhrūṃ, upon these circles, oneself,
Having imagined the being of the intermediate state,
Blends completely into evaṃ.
What arises from that blending
Is the palace common to all the buddhas,
Four-sided with four doors,
Adorned with four porticoes,
Fully furnished with nets and half-nets,
And beautified by eight pillars.
The wheel born from the source of phenomena,
With two in union and without fault,
Is single at the anthers,
And is spoken of as other than the triangle.
In its center imagine a corpse;
The seat of the fifteen is the self.
Upon it is a moon,
And upon the moon the seed syllables,
Which afterwards are pressed down by the sun.
The great bliss of the two united:
The vowels abide in the form of the moon,
The consonants abide in the form of the sun,
And from the union of moon and sun
Come the seed syllables of Gaurī and the rest:
a ā i ī u ū ṛ ṝ ḷ ḹ e ai o au aṃ.
The first vowel is Nairātmyā;
The second vowel is Vajrā;
The third vowel is Gaurī;
The fourth is the yoginī Vārī;
The fifth is Vajraḍākinī;
The sixth is called Pukkasī;
Śavarī is the seventh;
Caṇḍālī is to be known as the eighth;
The ninth is Ḍombinī.
Then Gaurī is twice five, the tenth;
Caurī is the eleventh;
The twelfth is called Vetālī;
The thirteenth is Ghasmarī;
The fourteenth is called Bhūcarī;
The fifteenth is Khecarī.
These are the seed syllables of the yoginīs.
The moon is endowed with mirrorlike wisdom;
The seven-horsed sun, with the wisdom of equality;
One's own deity's seed syllable and hand emblem
Are said to be discriminating wisdom;
All becoming one is accomplishing wisdom;
Their completion is the pure dharmadhātu.
The completion of all of these
Is the nature of wisdom and skillful means.
By distinguishing the moon and the sun,
The vowels are wisdom, the consonants means,
And from these arise Gaurī and the rest.
First, in the inner union,
Five yoginīs are surely stationed:
In Indra's quarter Vajrā, in Yama's Gaurī,
In the quarter of Varuṇa, lord of waters, the yoginī Vārī,
In Kubera's, Vajraḍākinī,
And in the center the yoginī Nairātmyā.
In the outer union,
In Īśāna's quarter is Pukkasī,
In Agni's corner Śavarī,
In Nairṛti's Caṇḍālī,
And in the corner of the wind, Ḍombinī.
These are the yoginīs of the intermediate directions.
In the eastern direction is Gaurī,
And in the south, the west and the north
Are Caurī, Vetālī and Ghasmarī.
These are the yoginīs of the directions.
The one facing upward and the one facing downward
Are the goddesses Bhūcarī and Khecarī.
Black in color, the great fierce one,
Lady of the wheel, Nairātmyā,
Has one face with three red eyes
And two arms, her tawny hair standing upright.
In her left hand are a skull cup and a khaṭvāṅga;
Her right hand holds a curved knife.
She wears a wheel ornament, earrings, a necklace,
Bracelets and a girdle.
In the half-crossed posture she presses down a corpse,
Her lower body wrapped in a tiger skin.
Just like the yoginī Nairātmyā,
All of these are described as alike.
Imagine the seed syllable at the heart of the lotus in one's own heart;
Meditate on wisdom at the heart of the wisdom being.
Having summoned them, worship them properly,
And meditate on the wheel of wisdom.
On the eyes and the rest set delusion and the rest;
Set the three vajras at the three places.
Mohavajrā on the two eyes,
Dveṣavajrā in the ears;
On the nose, the one called Mātsaryavajrā;
On the mouth, Rāgavajrā;
On touch, Īrṣyāvajrā;
In the mind, the yoginī Nairātmyā.
From these mantras of body, speech and likewise mind,
oṃ and āḥ and hūṃ,
One should meditate on the sky-goer, the earth-goer,
And the one who dwells beneath the earth,
Rightly declared to be of various forms.
Thus Vairocana and the others:
The light rays from the seed syllable at the heart,
In the form of hooks, summon the sugatas.
When the eight goddesses have made offerings,
Empowerment is to be received.

"All you blessed tathāgatas, Grant me empowerment and the rest!" Then, in the form of Heruka, One imagines that one is being empowered. Mind, the Eternal One, the Jewel Lord, Amitābha and Amoghasiddhi Are sealed by the five yoginīs, Nairātmyā, Vajrā of the east and the rest. Mind, the Eternal One, the Jewel Lord And Amitābha are sealed by the yoginīs of the intermediate directions; Aversion, delusion, great pride And desire by the vajra ladies dwelling in the directions; And they are sealed with body and speech By Khecarī and Bhūcarī. The first one should meditate as black; The second one meditates as red; The third one meditates as yellow; Likewise the fourth is green; The fifth is blue in color; The sixth one visualizes as white. One should meditate on the six-limbed yoga. By the caṇḍālī fire blazing at the navel The five tathāgatas are burned, And Locanā and the others are burned as well; Through that burning, the moon drips from haṃ. The yogin who is wearied by meditation Should then recollect the purity. The aggregate of form is Vajrā; Feeling is Secret Gaurī; Likewise perception is the water goddess, Vārī; Formations are Vajraḍākinī; In the form of the aggregate of consciousness Abides Vajranairātmyā. The earth element is said to be Pukkasī; The water element is said to be Śavarī; Heat is declared to be Caṇḍālī; Wind is famed as Ḍombinī. For visible forms Gaurī is famed; For sounds Caurī is famed; For the field of smell, Vetālī; Likewise for taste, Ghasmarī; For tangible objects, Bhūcarī; For the element of phenomena, Khecarī. Aversion is explained as Vajrā; Desire is the yoginī Vārī; Jealousy is Vajraḍākinī; Avarice is Secret Gaurī; Likewise delusion is Vajrā; For the mind, Vajraḍākinī. These are the purities of aversion and the rest. The māra of birth is the head she holds; The curved knife cuts the divine māra; The skull cup is the head of the afflictions; The māra of the aggregates is said to be the corpse. Akṣobhya is the form of the wheel ornament; Amitābha is lord of the earrings; On the necklace is the Jewel Lord; On the bracelets, it is said, the Eternal One; On the girdle abides Amoghasiddhi. Thus the five mudrās are wholly pure. When wearied by meditation, recite the secret mantra: The vowels, with the a at their end left out, Joined with the syllable oṃ and with hūṃ phaṭ, And svāhā placed at the end: this is the secret mantra. oṃ a ā i ī u ū ṛ ṝ ḷ ḹ e ai o au aṃ hūṃ phaṭ svāhā. To accomplish every desired aim, And so that obstructors are exhausted and pacified, By the rite explained below One gives the torma as Acala. In a skull cup set upon wind and fire, Warmed by the blaze and so forth, One prepares the great flesh and the five nectars, Blessed by the three syllables. oṃ inda jama jala jakkha bhūta bahni ba u rakkha canad sūjja māda pappa tala patālā aṣṭva sappa svāhā idaṃ baliṃ bhuñja jiṃgha phulla dhūppa puṣba māṃsa piṅgha amba sarbba kajda sādha khanti khuṇi pheḍa gada oṃ akāro mukhaṃ sarba dharmāṇāṃ ādyanutpannatvāta oṃ āḥ hūṃ phaṭ svāhā.

The Sādhana of Nairātmyā, composed by the great scholar, the glorious Durjayacandra, is complete. It was translated by the Indian preceptor Gayadhara and the translator, the monk Shākya Yeshé.

Translator's notes

The opening line of homage reads literally "seeing saṃsāra, women, to be tamed"; a syllable seems to be missing in the source, and it is taken as "the women of saṃsāra who were to be tamed". "Holds the elixir" renders ro, which can mean taste, essence or corpse; the Hevajra parallel has Śavarī holding rasa, elixir. The four lines on the wheel born from the source of phenomena (two in union, single at the anthers, other than the triangle) are terse and their exact sense is uncertain. "The seat of the fifteen is the self" may also mean that the corpse itself forms the seat of the fifteen goddesses. The deity's directions in the inner ring are given by their guardians: Indra (east), Yama (south), Varuṇa (west), Kubera (north). Gaurī appears both as the third and as the tenth goddess, as the text has it. "Mind", "the Eternal One" and "the Jewel Lord" are the Hevajra system's names for the tathāgatas Akṣobhya, Vairocana and Ratnasambhava. The text has du ba, "smoke", in "the yogin who is wearied by meditation"; read as dub pa, "wearied", as in the later line "when wearied by meditation, recite the secret mantra." The four lines on the head, knife, skull cup and corpse match the four māras (of death or birth, the divine māra, of the afflictions, and of the aggregates). The list of purities places delusion with Vajrā and the mind with Vajraḍākinī, and aversion also with Vajrā, as the text stands.
