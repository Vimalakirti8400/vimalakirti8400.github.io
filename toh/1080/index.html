About this text

The Dhāraṇī That Saves the Preta Jvālāmukha, known in Tibetan as Yi dgas kha nas me 'bar ba la skyabs mdzad pa'i gzungs, is a short dhāraṇī scripture whose Sanskrit title is associated with the name of the flame-mouthed hungry ghost, Jvālāmukha. It opens with the Buddha teaching in Kapilavastu, while the venerable Ānanda, meditating alone at night, is confronted by a terrifying preta whose mouth blazes with fire and whose throat is as thin as a needle. The preta warns Ānanda that he will die in seven days and be reborn among hungry ghosts. The text goes on to present the protective dhāraṇī and the practice of offering food to pretas, by which beings in that realm are relieved and the practitioner's life and merit are safeguarded. This work is Toh 1080 in the Kangyur, where it belongs among the dhāraṇī texts, the class of scriptures centered on spoken formulas of protection and blessing.

Translation

The Dhāraṇī That Saves the Preta Jvālāmukha

At that time the Blessed One was staying at the Saṅgha's residence in Kapilavastu. Surrounded by the Saṅgha of monks and by countless bodhisattvas, he was teaching the Dharma.

At that time the venerable Ānanda was sitting in a solitary place, reflecting on the Dharma and keeping mindfulness present. In the last part of that night a preta called Jvālāmukha came before Ānanda. Its form was hideous, exceedingly gaunt and withered. Flames blazed from its mouth, its throat was as thin as a needle, its hair was matted, and its nails and fangs were long and sharp. It was utterly terrifying. It said to Ānanda, "Seven days from now you will die and be born in the realm of the pretas."

When Ānanda heard what it said, he was overcome with terror, and he said to the preta, "Since I am to die and be born in the realm of the pretas, what means can I use to be completely freed from that suffering?"

Then the preta said to Ānanda, "If tomorrow you give torma to as many pretas as there are grains of sand in a hundred thousand billion Ganges rivers, offer a meal of one full bushel, by the measure of the Middle Country, to each of a hundred thousand brahmins and sages, and make offerings to the Three Jewels on my behalf, then you will live long, be parted from the suffering of the pretas, and be born as a god in the higher realms."

At that time Ānanda had seen the preta Jvālāmukha, with its hideous body, exceedingly gaunt and withered, flames blazing from its mouth, its throat as thin as a needle, its hair matted, and its nails and fangs long and sharp, and he had heard its unpleasant and unwelcome words. Terrified, with his body bristling with gooseflesh, he rose from where he sat and went swiftly to where the Blessed One was. Coming into the Blessed One's presence, he touched the five points of his body to the ground and bowed with the crown of his head at the Blessed One's feet. His body trembling, he said to the Blessed One, "Please be a protector to one tormented by suffering."

He said, "While I was sitting in a solitary place reflecting on the Dharma, one called the preta Jvālāmukha said this to me: 'Seven days from now you will surely die and be born in the realm of the pretas.' When I asked it what means I could use to be completely freed from that suffering of the pretas, it told me, 'If you give torma to as many pretas as there are grains of sand in a hundred thousand billion Ganges rivers, and offer the finest, most excellent meals to a hundred thousand brahmins, sages and the like, you will live long.' Blessed One, how could I ever come by so much food for pretas, sages and the rest?"

Then the Blessed One said to Ānanda, "Do not be afraid. I have a means by which you can provide food and plentiful torma for as many pretas as there are grains of sand in a hundred thousand billion Ganges rivers, and for the brahmins, sages and the rest, so do not be dejected."

Then the Blessed One said to Ānanda, "I have a dhāraṇī-mantra called Victorious Force of Light Rays Endowed with the Power of Immeasurable Splendor. If anyone recites this dhāraṇī, it becomes abundant food and the finest, most excellent torma for as many pretas as there are grains of sand in a hundred thousand billion Ganges rivers, and for the brahmins, sages and the rest. Each one among them will receive as much as seven bushels of food, measured by the bushel of the Middle Country.

"Ānanda, when I too was a brahmin, I heard this dhāraṇī from the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara and from a tathāgata called Lord of Qualities. Through its power I gave food and torma to countless pretas and to many sages, and those who had been born in the realm of the pretas were freed from suffering and were born as gods in the higher realms. Ānanda, you must hold this dhāraṇī. Your merit and your lifespan too will increase and grow."

Then the Blessed One spoke this dhāraṇī-mantra to Ānanda:

namaḥ sarba tathāgata abalokite oṃ samdhara sambhara hūṃ

The Blessed One said to Ānanda, "If any son of good family or daughter of good family wishes for long life, or wishes for great and increasing merit and for the perfection of generosity to be swiftly and completely fulfilled, let them do this always in the morning, or else at any time at all; there will be no obstacle. Pour clean water into a clean vessel and put in a little cooked rice, parched grain, cakes or the like. Place the right hand over the vessel, recite this dhāraṇī seven times, and then one should recite the names of the four tathāgatas:

"Homage to the Tathāgata Prabhūtaratna.

"Through the blessing of reciting the name of the Tathāgata Prabhūtaratna, the nonvirtuous karma of avarice that all pretas have committed over many lives is destroyed, and their merit becomes complete.

"Homage to the Tathāgata Surūpa.

"Through the blessing of reciting the name of the Tathāgata Surūpa, the hideous forms of many pretas are destroyed, and they come to possess perfect form and marks.

"Homage to the Tathāgata Vipulakāya.

"Through the blessing of reciting the name of the Tathāgata Vipulakāya, the thirst of many pretas is removed and they are satisfied; at the place of offering they eat as they wish and become content and full.

"Homage to the Tathāgata Free from Fear.

"Through the blessing of reciting the name of the Tathāgata Free from Fear, many pretas are freed from every fear, and the whole realm of the pretas is pacified."

The Blessed One said to Ānanda, "After the son of good family or other practitioner has recited the names of the four tathāgatas and so blessed the offering, he should snap his fingers three times, take up the torma vessel, stretch out his arm, and pour it onto clean ground."

"When the torma is given in this way, the pretas who have come from the four directions, as many as hundreds of thousands of trillions of grains of sand in the river Ganges, will each receive seven measures of food, measured by the standard measure of the Middle Country. The pretas eat that food as they wish, become satisfied and full, leave the realm of the pretas, and are born as gods in the higher realms.

"Ānanda, if any monk, nun, layman, or laywoman constantly blesses food with this dhāraṇī and gives it to the pretas, they will bring immeasurable merit to completion. That merit is no different from the merit of having made offerings to hundreds of thousands of trillions of buddhas. Long life, merit, radiance, and strength will arise and increase in them, and their roots of virtue will be brought to completion. No human or nonhuman, no yakṣa, rākṣasa, or malevolent preta will be able to trouble or harm them, and in the eyes of others they will be endowed with immeasurable splendor.

"If anyone wishes to give to brahmins and sages, one should fill a single vessel with pure food, recite this dhāraṇī-mantra seven times, and pour it into clean running water. When this is done, it becomes the most excellent divine food. Then those brahmins and sages, having received food empowered by the mantra, find that through the blessing of the mantra the merit of the roots of virtue that each of them had formerly delighted in and aspired to is fully transformed. So each of them, in harmony and at the same moment, makes the aspiration, 'May that person have long life, be endowed with radiance and strength, and be happy!'

"Moreover, that person, just as he intends, becomes completely pure through what he sees and hears, and with unerring knowledge he comes to possess the supreme pure and divine conduct and brings it to completion. Moreover, his merit is equal to the merit of having made offerings to buddhas, blessed ones, as many as hundreds of thousands of trillions of grains of sand in the river Ganges, and no adversary whatsoever will be able to harm him.

"If any monk, nun, layman, or laywoman wishes to make offerings to the Buddha, the Dharma, and the Saṅgha, they should recite this dhāraṇī-mantra twenty-one times over incense, flowers, and fine food, and offer them to the Three Jewels. Then the food offered by that son or daughter of good family becomes divine food, and offerings will have been made to the Buddha, the Dharma, and the Saṅgha in the world systems of the ten directions. Thereby they will come to possess the merit of praise, of supplication, and of rejoicing, and they will always be praised and held in mind by the buddhas, the blessed ones. Many gods will constantly guard and protect them, and the perfection of generosity, too, will be brought to completion.

"Ānanda, from now on take this to heart in accordance with the Dharma, and teach it fully and extensively so that all sentient beings may see and hear it, and you will obtain immeasurable merit."

This completes the dhāraṇī that gives refuge to the preta Jvālāmukha.

Translator's notes

After its first mention as ཀུན་དགའ་བོ, the Buddha's attendant is called simply དགའ་བོ throughout. Context makes clear this is Ānanda, not Nanda, so "Ānanda" is used consistently.

"Bushel by the measure of the Middle Country" renders ཡུལ་དབུས་འགྱུར་ཚལ་གྱི་བྲེ་ཚད་མ. The element འགྱུར་ཚལ is obscure; the Chinese parallel speaks of a Magadha measure.

The tathāgata དབང་སྒྱུར་ཡོན་ཏན is translated as "Lord of Qualities," since its Sanskrit form could not be identified with confidence. The tathāgata འཇིགས་པ་དང་བྲལ་བ is translated as "Free from Fear." The Chinese parallel's 離怖畏如來 is often reconstructed as Abhayaṅkara, but this is not certain.

The dhāraṇī's title is translated from its Tibetan meaning. The phrase rendered "the standard measure of the Middle Country" contains an unidentified word that may name a place or a kind of measure. A Chinese parallel of this text speaks of the measure used in Magadha. The large number rendered "trillions" is a conventional rendering of a Tibetan term for a very high power of ten. The addressee is called by the short form of Ānanda's name, as the text's opening names him in full. It is therefore rendered Ānanda, not Nanda.
