About this text

The Noble Mahāyāna Sūtra Called "The Twelve Buddhas" (Sanskrit: Āryadvādaśabuddhaka-nāma-mahāyāna-sūtra; Tibetan: 'Phags pa sangs rgyas bcu gnyis pa zhes bya ba theg pa chen po'i mdo) is a short Mahāyāna sūtra concerned with twelve buddhas and the benefits of hearing and remembering their names. It is set at Rājagṛha on Vulture Peak, where the Buddha addresses the bodhisattva Maitreya and begins describing a buddha dwelling in a distant world-realm called Muktikā, Pearl-Possessing, to the east of our own. Texts of this kind belong to a familiar current of Mahāyāna literature in which the names and buddha-fields of particular buddhas are taught as objects of devotion, recollection, and protection. In the canon it is Toh 853 in the Kangyur, the collection of translated words of the Buddha, where it is classed among the sūtras.

Translation

In the language of India: Ārya-dvādaśabuddhaka-nāma-mahāyāna-sūtra. In the language of Tibet: The Noble Mahāyāna Sūtra Called "The Twelve Buddhas." Homage to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas.

Thus did I hear at one time. The Blessed One was dwelling at Rājagṛha, on Vulture Peak Mountain, together with a great assembly of monks, twelve hundred and fifty monks, and likewise together with a great assembly of bodhisattvas, twenty thousand bodhisattvas, headed by the bodhisattva Aparājita and others.

Then the Blessed One addressed the bodhisattva, the great being Maitreya: "Maitreya, to the east of this buddha-field, beyond buddha-fields as numerous as the atoms in ten ineffable hundred-thousand-millions of buddha-fields, there is a world-realm called Muktikā, Pearl-Possessing. There dwells the Tathāgata, arhat, perfectly complete buddha, Gaganaśrī-vimala-rajohara-surucira-guṇaketu-prabha-puṣpa-padma-vaiḍūrya-raśmi-ratnarūpa, whose body is like a jewel and who is honored with the finest incense. His body is exquisitely adorned and beautified, and from the crown of his head, the uṣṇīṣa, countless rays like the sun's pour forth. He is adorned with the moonlight of his aspirations and with vast, magnificent emanations. Manifestly arisen from the dharmadhātu, he is the noble one called Unattached King of Jewels. Even now he abides there, living and sustained, and he teaches the Dharma.

Whoever holds the name of that Victor will turn back from saṃsāra for as many eons as there are atoms in the great continent of Jambudvīpa, over great eons.

That Victor prophesied about the bodhisattva, the great being called Peerless: Immediately after that Tathāgata, arhat, perfectly complete buddha, the one described above, passes into nirvāṇa and his sacred Dharma has disappeared, Peerless will manifestly and fully awaken to unsurpassed, perfect, complete awakening. He will appear in the world as a Tathāgata, arhat, perfectly complete buddha, endowed with knowledge and conduct, Sugata, knower of the world, unsurpassed guide of beings to be tamed, teacher of gods and humans, a buddha, a Blessed One, called Jaladeva-kumāra-sūrya-pradīpa-candra-puṣpa-padma-suvarṇa-darśana-gaganasadṛśakāya, Vairocana, Raśmivyūha, Asaṅgacakra-maṇḍala, he who radiates light in the ten directions, Sarvalokadhātu-avabhāsakara-ketu-dhvaja-rāja.

One should also hold the name of the Tathāgata, arhat, perfectly complete buddha of that same eastern direction, Sarvavyūha-vimala-prabha, Light of All Adornments, Free from Stain. One should recite the words. One should pay homage.

One should also hold the name of the Tathāgata, arhat, perfectly complete buddha of the southern direction, Pratibhānavyūha-adhyāśaya, He Who Contemplates the Adornment of Eloquence. One should reflect on it.

One should also hold the name of the Tathāgata of the western direction, Vimala-candraketu-rāja, Famous King, Banner of the Stainless Moon. One should pay homage."

One should also hold the name of the Tathāgata of the northern direction, Pushpavyūha-prabhāsa, He Who Illumines the Adornment of Flowers. One should pay homage.

One should also hold the name of the Tathāgata of the southeastern intermediate direction, Prabhākara, the Maker of Light. One should pay homage.

One should also hold the name of the Tathāgata of the southwestern intermediate direction, Ratnottama-ketu-yaśasvin, Renowned Crest of the Supreme Jewel. One should pay homage.

One should also hold the name of the Tathāgata of the northwestern intermediate direction, Abhaya-vikrānta, Fearless Beholder. One should pay homage.

One should also hold the name of the Tathāgata of the northeastern intermediate direction, He Who Is Free from Fear, Without Anxiety, Whose Hair Does Not Stand on End. One should pay homage.

One should also hold the name of the Tathāgata of the nadir, Siṃha-vijṛmbhita, He Who Stretches Forth Like a Lion. One should pay homage.

One should also hold the name of the Tathāgata of the zenith, Suvarṇaprabhāsa-śrī-abhayakara-rāja, Golden Light, Splendor, King Free from Fear. One should pay homage.

Maitreya, if a son of good family who has faith, or a daughter of good family, recites the names of these twelve Blessed Ones, Tathāgatas, arhats, perfectly complete buddhas, then for ten days one should confess the faults of all one's misdeeds. One should rejoice in all roots of virtue. One should entreat all the buddhas to teach, and request all the buddhas to remain. And one should dedicate all roots of virtue by dedicating them to the dharmadhātu. By doing so, all misdeeds will be exhausted, and all karmic obscurations will be completely purified.

Thereafter one will also attain the perfection of the qualities and adornments of a buddha-field, the perfection of fearlessness, the perfection of the marks, the perfection of the community of bodhisattvas, and the perfection of dhāraṇīs. One will also attain the perfection of meditative absorptions. According to one's wish, one will swiftly gain the perfection of a completely pure buddha-field, and the perfection of virtuous spiritual friends. One will not fall away from unsurpassed, perfectly complete awakening, nor ever be wholly diminished in it. While wandering in saṃsāra, one's lifetimes will be altogether beautiful, and one will gain perfection of wealth, perfection of lineage, perfection of birth, perfection of family, perfection of bodily form, and perfection of a retinue of like-minded people.

On this it is said:

In whatever place one is born,
in each and every lifetime,
one will be a joy to behold, and a giver of joy,
one will be radiant and of great power,
and will come to great prosperity.

One should also recite these foundations of the secret mantra of the dhāraṇī.

Tadyathā: Akhe, Makhe, Samanta-mukhe, Sautiyukte, Nirukte, Prabhe, Samayoge, Cittacitte, Akhe, Makhe, Madane, Vivarte, Samanta-guṇi, Satyārame, Yukte, Prayukte, Hili Mili, Masale, Ānale, Came, Acite, Coce, Arahe, Bahuccho, Māyugrahe, Hemavati, Jyotivati, Dharmacitte, Cyutapadke, Avikṣebe, Radhṛkṣame, Skandha-vighakte.

I pay homage to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas. By the blessing of the buddhas, may these foundations of the secret mantra be accomplished.

sixty million in number,
will turn their minds toward that person.
In every birth one will also
find virtuous spiritual friends.
Whatever small virtue one has,

Those who dwell upon the heart of a lotus,

One will always remember one's former lives.

Having deep faith in the buddhas,
one will make supreme offerings.
Pleasing the buddhas who appear,
one sees the Teacher and has deep faith.
Having seen the Lamp of the World,

Whoever upholds these names

the vast eye by which the Guides are seen
will be purified.
Any person who upholds

in whatever birth and wherever born,
will become worthy of homage.
Whoever upholds these names,
for inconceivable millions of eons,

For one who upholds these names,
the gods will show reverence

and one will swiftly attain great awakening.

Whoever upholds these names

Deceit and trickery are wholly abandoned,

Whoever upholds these names

If a woman takes up the names

Whoever upholds the names of these Protectors

Having made offerings to the teachings of the buddhas,

Knowing the thoughts of those who ask,

Having received prophecy

Those who uphold these names

When the Blessed One had spoken thus, the bodhisattva, the great being Maitreya, together with those bodhisattvas, those monks, that whole assembly, and the world with its gods, humans, asuras, and gandharvas, rejoiced and praised what the Blessed One had said.

This concludes the Mahāyāna sūtra called "The Noble Twelve Buddhas."

Translator's notes

The long epithets of the eastern buddha and of Peerless's future buddha-name are compounds of many elements whose Sanskrit I have reconstructed only approximately; the sequence of elements in the Tibetan is clear, but the parsing of some epithets (for instance, whether "Vairocana" stands as its own name or as part of a compound) is uncertain. The Tibetan "muktikā" (mu tig can) is rendered "Pearl-Possessing." The text breaks off after the western buddha, apparently incomplete or continuing in the next passage. The Sanskrit names of the twelve buddhas are reconstructed from the Tibetan and are provisional. The mantra syllables are given in the Sanskritized transliteration of the Tibetan, which appears corrupt in places (for example "Sautiyukte," "Coce," "Radhṛkṣame"), so it should be recited as it stands in the transmitted text. The passage breaks off mid-verse at its end, with a sentence continuing into the next section. The phrase "ten days" for the practice follows the Tibetan ཉི་མ་བཅུར. The Tibetan has some corrupt or unclear readings, notably "མཚན་རྣམས་སུ་ཞིག་རྗོད་བྱེད་པ" (rendered as reciting the names), the "lotus petals" stanza with its garbled syntax, and the "དཔུང་དང་བཞོན་པར་བཅས་པ" line; I have followed the evident sense. "Ordinarily rendered 'Garuḍa' for མཁའ་ལྡིང and 'yakṣa' for གསང་བ་པ" is my reading of "guhyaka," a class of yakṣa-like beings. The passage on a woman becoming a man reflects the text's own doctrinal framing and is rendered as written. The names in the verse beginning "These buddhas are Śrīgarbha, Meghārāja..." are rendered as epithets from the Tibetan (dpal snying po, sprin gyi rgyal po, mi yi mchog). The line is read as a list of buddha names and epithets, not a single name, and the identification is not certain. The Tibetan text ends in mid-colophon at "skad" (language), so the colophon is left unfinished.
