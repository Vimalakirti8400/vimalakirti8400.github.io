About this text

The Noble Great King of Dedications, together with Mantras (Sanskrit, Ārya Mahāpariṇāmarāja Samantraka) is a short devotional and liturgical work centered on the dedication of merit, the Buddhist practice of directing the benefit of one's virtuous actions toward the welfare and awakening of all beings. It opens with homage to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas and with verses of going for refuge, confession of non-virtue, rejoicing in the virtue of others, and requesting the buddhas to turn the wheel of Dharma, before turning to its dedications and accompanying mantras. The Tibetan text records that it was translated, revised, and finalized by the Indian preceptor Vidyākaraprabha and the great reviser-translator Palbrtsegs, who were active during the early Tibetan translation period. It appears as Toh 810 in the Kangyur, where it belongs to the tantra section, reflecting its combination of prayer with mantra recitation.

Homage to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas.

I go for refuge, without exception,
to the buddhas, ocean of boundless qualities,
to the noble and sacred Dharma,
and to the Saṅgha who abide throughout the three times.

I confess all non-virtue.
I rejoice in the virtue of beings.
I request the fully awakened ones
to turn the wheel of Dharma.

Whatever small virtue I may have,
seen by the all-knowing eye,
by it may all those who are forever separated
from happiness be completely pacified.

May the samādhi that knows emptiness,
endowed with the supreme of all aspects,
be, throughout my lives until awakening,
always accompanied by happiness.

May I abide constantly in equipoise,
free of concepts and spontaneously accomplished,
and, like the sun, the moon, and the wish-fulfilling jewel,
may I engage in the benefit of sentient beings.

Just as the wise Victors
dedicate through the perfection of wisdom and skillful means,
so too do I dedicate all my virtues
to awakening.

Whatever virtue of my body, speech, and mind
is directed toward the Three Jewels,
and whatever arises from generosity, moral discipline,
patience, diligence, meditative concentration, and wisdom,

by all of these, may I myself
be endowed with pure conduct,
and in the teaching of the Protector of the World
may I be master of delight in going forth.

Having become skilled in the meaning and words
of the scriptures spoken by all the buddhas,
may I uphold the sacred Dharma
and have no attachment to body or life.

When the time of my death comes,
with an undistorted and unconfused mind,
having seen the Buddha Amitāyus,
may I go from here to Sukhāvatī, the Blissful Land.

When the realm of Māra becomes manifest,
may the great sage Amitāyus
constantly be my teacher,
and having attained the ten grounds,

by their stages of complete purification,
may I attain supreme awakening.
Having attained the state of the buddha Samantabhadra,
having uprooted all accumulated faults together with their habitual tendencies,

endowed with unobscured wisdom and compassion,
may I bring healing and happiness to beings.

I pay homage to the Blessed Buddhas of the ten directions, whose minds pervade without exception all that is to be known throughout the boundless ten directions, and to the bodhisattvas, numerous as the ocean.

That Dharma is free of conceptual elaboration and without letters; it is to be known individually, each by his own awareness, by the noble ones. In his presence may I receive the going forth, the going forth in youth, the going forth openly and not in secret, the going forth in suchness, the going forth in the true limit, even the going forth that culminates in the seeing of reality itself. And may that going forth be exalted above the worldly and the transworldly, faultless, unmixed, free of turbidity, a joy to gods and humans, praised by buddhas and bodhisattvas. May I go forth for the sake of healing sentient beings.

Then, from childhood onward, may I please all the fully awakened buddhas of this Fortunate Eon, and all the bodhisattvas and the Saṅgha of hearers, with robes, food, bedding, and the requisites of medicines for the sick. In their presence may I receive the going forth, the going forth in youth, the going forth directly and in person, the going forth openly and not in secret, the going forth in suchness, the going forth in the true limit, even the going forth that culminates in the seeing of suchness. And may that going forth be exalted above the worldly and the transworldly, faultless, unmixed, free of turbidity, a joy to gods and humans, praised by buddhas and bodhisattvas. May I go forth for the sake of healing sentient beings.

Then may I please all the fully awakened buddhas dwelling in the world-systems of the ten directions, together with their bodhisattvas and Saṅghas of hearers. In the presence of each of them, in a single instant, may I receive the prophecy of my name and my buddha-field, and may I receive the going forth, the going forth in youth, the going forth directly and in person, the going forth openly and not in secret, the going forth in suchness, the going forth in the true limit, even the going forth that culminates in the seeing of reality itself. And may that going forth be exalted above the worldly and the transworldly, faultless, unmixed, free of turbidity, a joy to gods and humans, praised by buddhas and bodhisattvas. May I go forth for the sake of healing sentient beings.

I pay homage to the bodhisattva, the great being, endowed with great compassion, Noble Maitreya.

Tadyathā: maitri maitri, maitri, samacitte, svāhā.

Then may I apply myself intently to the six perfections. May I abide in the Perfection of Wisdom; abide in emptiness, in signlessness, and in wishlessness. May I attain the Treasury of Space, the Jewel in the Hand, the five higher knowledges, mastery, and the Kṣitigarbha-like attainment of the individual exact knowledges.

May I never become one whose faculties are weak or deprived of faculties. May I never fall into wrong views. May I never be born in a bad region, nor among bad people. May I never be born in an unfree state. May I never be separated from seeing, hearing, honoring, and attending upon the Buddha, the Dharma, and the Saṅgha. May I never become one of broken moral discipline; and even in dreams, may I never, from a wish for the awakening of a hearer or a solitary buddha, become separated from the awakening mind. May I never be separated from bringing sentient beings to full maturity. May I never be separated from spiritual friends. May I never, even for an instant, be separated from skillful means and wisdom. May I never be of low birth, of weak diligence, or lazy. Life after life, may I be born with trained diligence in the qualities of a buddha. For the sake of healing sentient beings, may I take miraculous birth. Just as the Buddhas, the Blessed Ones, and those bodhisattvas were born, so may I be born into the family of the buddhas. Then may all treatises, all crafts, all meditative absorptions, all doors of dhāraṇī, and all factors conducive to awakening become manifest to me.

I pay homage to the bodhisattva, the great being, Noble Vimalakīrti (Famed Free of Stain).

Tadyathā: kīrtite, sarvajinebhiḥ, atikīrta, sarvajinebhiḥ, vajra ākare, vajra saṃbhave, vajra bhedakare, svāhā.

Just as earth, water, fire, wind, and space sustain beings, so I make common to all sentient beings whatever of the five aggregates has been rightly accomplished through these paths of the ten virtuous actions, and I offer it to those who make use of the Buddha, the Dharma, and the Saṅgha. When I die, may vultures, jackals, dogs, foxes, beasts of prey, and birds devour my skin, flesh, sinews, bones, limbs, and blood. May all of them become certain of unsurpassed, perfect, complete awakening. Then may I bring to liberation, in a single instant of mind, as many sentient beings as there are fine particles of dust in the three realms. Having liberated them, may I establish them on the grounds from which there is no turning back. Then may beings be brought to full maturity merely by seeing me; may beings be brought to full maturity by hearing of me and by remembering me.

I pay homage to the bodhisattva, the great being, Ākāśagarbha (Treasury of Space).

Tadyathā: garbhe garbhe, garbha viśodhani, svāhā.

To those buddhas, the Blessed Ones, dwelling in the world-systems of the ten directions, who intend to pass fully into nirvāṇa, with my intention and my superior intention, with joy to the marrow of my bones and joy to the depths of my limbs, bowing with all my mind and all my body, I make this request: for the sake of healing sentient beings, for as long as the realm of sentient beings, the realm of karma, the realm of afflictions, and the realm of space endure, may you not pass into nirvāṇa, but remain in ease.

I pay homage to the Tathāgata Guṇākara (Source of Qualities).

Tadyathā: gagana ākare, gagana saṃbhave, gagana kīrtikare, svāhā.

I rejoice in those bodhisattvas dwelling in the world-systems of the ten directions who have thoroughly gathered the accumulations, and in those whose accumulations are small, and in those who have generated the mind of awakening.

To those who have not yet generated the mind of awakening, I make this appeal, and to them I offer whatever good conduct I have performed with my body, speech, and mind. Through that root of virtue, may the accumulations be thoroughly gathered, and with the roots of virtue well accumulated, may I swiftly become fully awakened into unsurpassable, perfect, complete awakening, and turn the wheel of the Dharma. May I vanquish the four māras. May I bring sentient beings to full maturity. For the sake of healing sentient beings, for as long as the realm of sentient beings, the realm of karma, the realm of afflictions, and the realm of space endure, may you remain, I request.

I pay homage to the bodhisattva, the great being, the noble Akṣayamati (Inexhaustible Intelligence).

Tadyathā: akṣaye akṣaye, akṣaya viśodhani svāhā.

In the world-systems of the ten directions, there are as many perfectly complete buddhas and bodhisattvas of the past, future, and present as there are atoms of dust, and they have made countless hundreds of thousands of millions of great aspirations, tens of them. Just as they enter spontaneously into the accomplishing of those aspirations, so may I, life after life, enter into them.

Then, just as the buddha-fields become completely pure: just as the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Amitābha, has the world-system Sukhāvatī, the Blissful; just as the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Akṣobhya, has the world-system Abhirati, the Joyful; just as the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Padmanetra (Lotus Eye), has the world-system Padmāvatī, the Lotus-Possessing; just as the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Samantabhadra, has the world-system called Unblinking Eyes; just as the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Ratnaśikhin (Jewel Crest), has the world-system Ratnāvatī, the Jewel-Endowed; just as the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Gandhakūṭa (Heap of Incense), has the world-system of Sweet Fragrance; just as the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Ratnavyūha (Jewel Array), has the world-system of Jewel Array; and just as the future Blessed One, the Tathāgata Satyamati Śikhirāja (Truth-Intelligence, King of Crests), has his buddha-field; just as the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Suvarṇapuṣpa (Golden Flower), has his buddha-field; just as the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Ratnadāna (Jewel Giver), has his buddha-field; just as the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Jālinīprabha (Net of Light), has his buddha-field.

Just as the Blessed One, the Tathāgata, the Arhat, the perfectly complete Buddha Śākyamuni, has well taught the buddha-fields of the bodhisattvas in the Perfection of Wisdom; and just as the Blessed One, in the introductory discourse called Entering the Domain of All Buddhas, the Array of Radiant Wisdom, manifestly brought forth the buddha-field of the great precious Lotus Heart: in such a buddha-field, by the power of those bodhisattvas, may they emerge, and there may they become fully awakened into unsurpassable, perfect, complete awakening.

Just as the buddhas, the Blessed Ones of the past, future, and present, and those bodhisattvas, have accomplished their buddha-fields through great aspirations that are utterly and completely pure, born necessarily from their own merit, and taught in the Perfection of Wisdom, and just as they have embraced, hold, and will hold them, so may there come to be for me a single buddha-field of that kind, of immeasurable breadth, with the immeasurable names and adornments of its qualities.

May I become fully awakened together with the seeing, the touching, the hearing, and the remembering of the supremely noble, exalted, and wondrously adorned array of all the buddha-fields.

In that buddha-field, those beings who have been abandoned by all the buddhas and all the bodhisattvas; who hold to no mother and no father, who hold to no ascetic and no brahmin, who do not follow the elders of the lineage; who are of wretched character, who practice evil conduct in full; whose continuum is burnt, whose intention is burnt, whose seed is burnt; whose roots of virtue are severed; who are bent toward the lower realms; who possess the five deeds of immediate retribution; who are fierce and who reject the sublime Dharma, the bodhisattva's teaching: these beings will appear in the first assembly in that buddha-field, and may I establish all of them in the unsurpassable buddhahood, the path of peace.

Then, as many beings as are born in the three realms, those beings will appear in that field in the second assembly, and may I establish all of them in the omniscient wisdom in which all afflictions, together with their habitual tendencies and their junctures, have been abandoned.

I pay homage to the Tathāgata, the King of the Light of the Conch. Tadyathā: śaṃkhe śaṃkhe, śaṅkha-pāṇḍare svāhā.

Of those fully awakened buddhas and bodhisattvas gathered in the world-systems of the ten directions, whatever the activities of the buddhas and the activities of the bodhisattvas may be, all of these I, alone and without a second, delight to increase step by step in the qualities of a buddha. May those buddhas, the Blessed Ones, and those bodhisattvas, having made manifest the nature of reality that is emptiness, signlessness, wishlessness, unborn, and unceasing, and having also made manifest the other factors that are on the side of awakening, dwell in ease and with little exertion, I pray.

I pay homage to the Tathāgata, the King of the Light of the Summit of the Mountain. Tadyathā: śikhe śikhe, śikhā-bandhe svāhā.

Thereafter, having greatly gathered the accumulations, having completely fulfilled the dark practices, having completely fulfilled my intention, and having completely fulfilled the work of a buddha, may I become fully and perfectly awakened into unsurpassable, perfect awakening. Having become fully awakened, may I set in motion the wheel of Dharma: the wheel of suchness, the wheel of the true limit, the wheel of things as they are, the wheel of no birth and no cessation, the wheel of emptiness, signlessness, and wishlessness, the wheel of definite emergence, the wheel of definitive distinction, the wheel in which aggregates, elements, and sense sources are not apprehended, the wheel that is like space, the wheel of the inseparable dharmadhātu, the wheel of abandoning all afflictions together with their habitual tendencies and junctures, and the wheel in which nothing is apprehended, from the clinging to "I," to self, to permanence, to annihilation, to the grasping at self and what belongs to self, up to the clinging to śrāvaka, to pratyekabuddha, to bodhisattva, and to perfectly awakened buddha. In brief, may I set in motion such a wheel of Dharma, in which there is no wheel and no turning.

Having set it in motion, may I bring beings to full maturity. Having brought them to maturity, for the sake of healing beings, may I remain for as long as the realm of beings, the realm of karma, the realm of afflictions, and the realm of space endure.

I pay homage to the noble Mañjuśrī, who has become a youth. Tadyathā: manye manye.

Sarva manya prahāṇe svāhā.

Through those roots of virtue, and through the roots of virtue that purify the qualities of the buddha-fields; likewise through the roots of virtue by which I have brought beings to full maturity; through the roots of virtue of going forth; through the roots of virtue of having made offerings, rendered service, and shown reverence to the Tathāgatas; through the roots of virtue accomplished toward parents, teachers, worthy recipients of offerings, preceptors, and masters, and toward the lowly, the poor, the destitute, and the afflicted; through the roots of virtue of discerning the sacred Dharma; and through those endowed with rejoicing, confession, and purification of wrongdoing, endowed with homage and offerings: whatever has been gathered, from childhood until now, or in other births within saṃsāra, which has no beginning and no end of births, and up to the final limit of nirvāṇa without remainder of the aggregates, all of this I make common to all sentient beings.

I shall dedicate it with a dedication that is supreme, chief, foremost, excellent, exalted, unsurpassed, without anything higher; higher even than the highest; without equal, equal to the unequalled; without objective support, without conceptual thought, without sign, without elaboration, without letters, without affliction, without attachment; equal to space; liberating body and mind; and endowed with the most excellent of all aspects, that of emptiness.

Just as the buddhas, the Blessed Ones of the past, the future, and the present, and those bodhisattvas, having directly realized the nature of reality that is without duality, have dedicated, are dedicating, and will dedicate, so too shall I dedicate. Just as that is the nature of reality, just as that is suchness, just as that is the true limit, just as that is the dharmadhātu, just as that is emptiness, just as that is omniscience, up to the seeing of reality as it is, in which there is neither duality nor non-duality, so shall I dedicate.

I pay homage to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas. Tadyathā: buddhe buddhe, sarva buddha viṣaya viśodhani svāhā.

Here ends the noble Dedication, King of Dedications, together with its mantras, in its full extent.
