About this text

Toh 458 in the Kangyur is a tantric text known in Sanskrit as the Śrī Vajra Caṇḍa Citta Guhya Tantra and in Tibetan as the Dpal rdo rje gtum po thugs gsang ba'i rgyud, rendered in English as the Glorious Tantra of the Secret Mind of the Glorious Wrathful Vajra. It belongs to the class of Buddhist tantras and centers on Vajrapāṇi in his wrathful form, presenting esoteric teachings on the enlightened body, speech, and mind of the Sugatas as experienced from the pure realm of Akaniṣṭha. The text opens in the classical sūtra style with the phrase "Thus did I hear," situating its revelations within a celestial assembly where the Buddhas work for the benefit of beings through the sambhogakāya, nirmāṇakāya, and other emanation bodies arising from the dharmakāya. As a tantra devoted to a wrathful emanation of Vajrapāṇi, it is placed among the tantric scriptures of the Kangyur, reflecting the tradition's deep engagement with the transformative power of wrathful enlightened activity.

Translation

In the language of India: Śrī Vajra Caṇḍa Citta Guhya Tantra. In the language of Tibet: The Glorious Tantra of the Secret Mind of the Glorious Wrathful Vajra. Homage to the Glorious Wrathful One, Vajrapāṇi.

Thus did I hear at one time. In the palace of Akaṇiṣṭha adorned with spreading branches, all the Sugatas who behold body, speech, mind, qualities, and enlightened activity accomplish the benefit of beings by means of the saṃbhogakāya, the nirmāṇakāya, the vajra body, and the body of manifest awakening, each arising from the dharmakāya.

They accomplish the benefit of beings through speech that is beyond expression yet meaningful, speech that is symbolic intention, speech that is the melody of Brahmā, speech that is inseparable, and speech that is consecrated awareness.

They accomplish the benefit of beings through mind that is the dharmadhātu, mind that is mirror-like, mind that is perfect equality, mind that is individually discerning, and mind that is all-accomplishing.

They accomplish the benefit of beings through inconceivable abodes, thrones, retinues, fields of beings to be tamed, and rays of bodily light.

They accomplish the benefit of beings through enlightened activities that are pacifying, enriching, empowering, wrathful, and spontaneously accomplished.

At that time, the Blessed One, perceiving that the time had come to tame sentient beings through the buddhas of the five families, the three families, and so forth, accomplished the benefit of beings through manifold emanations with unattached compassion.

At that time, perceiving that the time had come to tame gods, demigods, and rākṣasas of extreme arrogance — those mired in the swamp of attachment to the web of pride, those dwelling on the Mount Meru of conceit, those whose minds are stirred by the wind of jealousy, those dwelling in the eternal darkness of bewilderment — all the Tathāgatas became of one mind. From the great non-abiding wisdom, they arose as the vajra family, the buddha family, the jewel family, the lotus family, and the karma family, each seated together with their respective consorts. Through the blessing of their unified minds, the buddha of all families appeared. His body was blue in its nature, like vaiḍūrya; he appeared blue, was luminous blue, shone with the color of the precious stone indranīla, like green. He appeared adorned with the splendid attributes of the palace of spreading branches, holding a vajra in his hand, and arose firm at heart.

Through that power and might, the proud ones dwelling in the world were subdued, bound under oath, and the supreme awakening mind was aroused.

This concludes the first chapter, the chapter of introduction, from the Glorious Tantra of the Secret Mind of the Wrathful Vajra, the All-Secret.

Then again at that time, the Blessed Ones of the five families, seeing that there were sentient beings dwelling in afflictions who could not be tamed by peaceful means, commanded the Lord of Secrets: "Ho! Lord of all secret mantras (Skt. mantra), awareness mantras, and dhāraṇī mantras — tame those sentient beings who cannot be tamed by peaceful means, taming them all in the manner of the wrathful one!" Thus they declared. Oṃ vajrapāṇi krodha krodha hūṃ hūṃ — this was deliberately proclaimed three times.

From that proclamation arose Vajrapāṇi, the great chief of the yakṣas, clad in blue garments, his fangs bared and gnashing, his eyes blazing like a mass of firebrands, his breath flaming like the tongues of fire in a charnel ground, his terrifying roar resounding like the end of a cosmic age. His hands and feet were adorned with the nāga kings Norbu and Norgyé as ornaments. He was crowned at the topknot with the black nāga king and the one called Closely Black. His ears were adorned with the blazing nāga kings Delightful and the Closely Delightful as earrings. He devoured poisonous blazing serpents. His face was broad and full. His brow was furrowed with dark yellow eyebrows drawn together at the forehead. He bore the aspect of the Lord of Death. His tongue flickered like lightning. His belly was great, devouring serpents. The clapping of his palms filled all with terror. He was bound at the waist with the nāga king Excellent-Wings as a belt.

Crying "Alala!" he terrorized and filled all with dread. He had plunged into the ocean depths to the depth of eighty thousand leagues, so that only his knees were above the water. He carried Indra and the sun upon his shoulders. He destroyed all nāgas. His great roaring laughter shook everything into tumult. He made the three realms tremble and quake. He was a destroyer of nāgas. And he uttered his dhāraṇī mantra for this very purpose:

Tadyathā: bho bho pānānāga adhivataye, inadnīlāmbaradhara śarīram, vajrapāṇi hṛdayaṃ, yās māti kramath, mākulodssādanaṃ bhaviṣyanti. Tadyathā: buddha buddha vibuddha vibuddha, māti kramath, hara hara nāgānāṃ, prahara prahara nāgānāṃ, cchinda cchinda nāga hṛdayāni, bhinda bhinda nāga marmāṇi, viśīryantū nāga śarīraṃ, visphōṭaya nāga nayanāni. Tadyathā hūṃ phaṭ. Nāga vidāraṇāya phaṭ. Nāgotsādanāya phaṭ. Nīlāmbarasanāya phaṭ. Apratihahabalaparākramāya phaṭ. Kṛtāntāya phaṭ. Kṛtāntarūpāya phaṭ. Roṣaṇāya phaṭ. Trāsanāya phaṭ. Kusmāṇḍotsādanāya phaṭ. Preta piśāca vināśakarāya phaṭ. Yakṣa apasmāra vināśakarāya phaṭ. Hasa hasa, dāra dāra, māraya māraya, nāśaya nāśaya, sarva vighnāṃ vināśakarāya phaṭ. Sarva śatruṃ saṃgha vināśakarāya phaṭ. Nīlāmbaradhara vajrapāṇi rājñāpayati svāhā.

No sooner had he uttered these words than the majority of the proud and arrogant ones became still as painted images, and those who had fallen into deep unconsciousness recovered their senses and spoke these words: "O Lord of Secrets, you are the sovereign of compassion, the refuge and protection of all. Please liberate us from this suffering." Then those proud and arrogant ones were bound by oath, received empowerment and the samaya vow, and generated the awakening mind.

This is the second chapter, on the subjugation of the common arrogant ones, from the Tantra of All Secrets of the Glorious Fierce Vajra.

Then there arose, among the worldly arrogant ones, those who were supremely arrogant: the king of the nāga royal lineage called Ocean; the king of the lord-spirits' lineage called All-Canopied; the king of the brahmin lineage called Water-God; the king of the outcastes called Śulapukara; the king of māras called Kośrje Drangkar; the great wild planet-spirit; the one with a white conch-shell crest; the king of the btsans called Red Life-Taker; the king of the death-lords called Migpa; the queen of the māmos called Mother of Space; the sister of the Lord of Death, Revati the Devastating, and others — all of these supremely arrogant ones who wreak ruin upon the world. As even the five saṃbhogakāya bodies had not subdued the unsubdued, the Lord of Secrets declared: "The male and female arrogant ones have not been tamed. By all means, the afflicted and arrogant must be subdued." He so commanded.

All the buddhas of the three times also spoke thus to the Lord of Secrets: "These worldly arrogant ones, abiding in attachment, harm sentient beings. Since they bring harm to the body and life of all, you must subdue them entirely in the mode of wrath." So they commanded.

The buddhas and bodhisattvas dwelling in the pure buddha fields of all ten directions, gathered together from each of their respective lineages, likewise spoke in one voice: "Lord of Secrets, display the supreme mode of wrath and subdue the arrogant ones with unimpeded power." So they commanded.

Furthermore, the awareness-holders and mantras —

all the lords of the secret mantras and dhāraṇīs assembled together — the lords of the lineages, the mothers of the lineages, the wrathful ones of the lineages, the emanations of the lineages, the female messengers of the lineages, and all the rest — and with one voice they declared: "Lord of Secrets, because these fields of beings to be tamed cannot be disciplined by peaceful means, these ones who are accompanied by aversion and malice, who indulge the afflictions as they please, and who are tormented by the thirst of desire — display toward them the aspect of supreme wrath through the power of your might, and without delay subdue them swiftly and utterly." So they commanded.

Thereupon, his form becoming invisible, he remained in the state of great primordial wisdom.

This is the third chapter of the tantra of the secret mind of the Glorious Vajra Ferocious One: the chapter on commanding the wrathful one.

Then, from the sky of the non-dual union of the fathers and mothers of the five families, there arose through the compassionate wrath the seed-syllable HŪṂ — dark blue like the hue of black crow-feathers — blazing with rays of light of many colors, blazing intensely, blazing through and through. From the blessing of that, from the compassionate blessing, there streamed forth: E, dark blue; YAṂ, blue-grey; RĀṂ KHĀṂ, dark red; SŪṂ, dark yellow; RĀṂ, dark red; KĒṂ, pale white; and a multicolored BHRŪṂ.

From the convergence and dispersal of these there arose: a dark-blue triangular sky; a blue-grey crescent of wind; a dark-red ocean of blood; a dark-yellow vast earth and ground; a pale-white skeletal Mount Meru; and a spontaneously accomplished maṇḍala — a palace of various precious jewels, arising of itself from a dark-blue vajra-cross.

At the center, unchanging as the nature of reality and spontaneously accomplished, stood a dark-blue circular hub. The tips of the spontaneously accomplished vajra-cross, perfected in the four activities, radiated in all ten directions. The intervals were spontaneously accomplished from four kinds of precious jewels. The corners gleamed with green precious jewels. The four gates, fashioned from four kinds of precious jewels, appeared as the activities of the four immeasurables. Ornaments of many kinds naturally appeared and arose of themselves.

Then from that dark-blue HŪṂ, the wrathful seed, there streamed forth the fine sharp rays of the manifold vajra. From those fine vajra rays there shot forth inconceivable fine vajra iron hooks, hooking all the arrogant ones of the world and the various male and female rākṣasas who menace life. Outside the palace they were cast and stood — like trees uprooted at the root — and, like painted figures, became utterly motionless. Through the blessing of that power they were brought, as though stones hurled into the hub of a wheel.

Then all the Tathāgatas together uttered the words "sthira baṃ," and the male and female rākṣasas regained awareness and were brought to stand in the presence.

This is the fourth chapter of the tantra of the secret mind of the Glorious Vajra Ferocious One: the chapter on the spontaneously accomplished palace.

Then from that blazing, dark-blue HŪṂ of compassionate wrath, all the Tathāgatas with one voice proclaimed "abhiṣoṣaṇa hūṃ," whereupon there appeared one with brows knitted in wrath, mouth spread wide, teeth gnashed in three points — the fangs that cut the three poisons at their root — blazing in such a blazing aspect. The three great world-systems trembled, shook greatly, shook throughout, and shook entirely and utterly.

This is the fifth chapter of the tantra of the secret mind of the Glorious Vajra Ferocious One: the chapter on generating the blazing wrathful one in the mode of wrath.

At that time, at that moment, all the lords of the vehicles of the three times gathered together. With wisdom vases from the sky of secret wisdom they bathed the entire body of that blazing wrathful one, and performed his empowerment with a stream of water from wisdom vases. The kuśa-grass officiant who performs the purifying ablution —

— purifying obscurations and protecting all beings utterly: with the ash of a ritual implement, with the sap of the nature of reality, with the essential butter of primordial wisdom, and with other such substances he performed the empowerment in accordance with the conduct of beings. The wrathful one of primordial wisdom was absorbed into the body, spread throughout it, and bliss arose.

Then the lord of all buddha-families, together with the five buddhas and their consorts, entered into meditative equipoise in bliss. From the sky of non-duality, syllables of primordial wisdom appeared: Kṣīṃ in dark blue, Kṣīṃ in white, Kṣīṃ in yellow, Kṣīṃ in red, Kṣīṃ in green, and Kṣīṃ appearing in many colors. With a single voice he uttered the mantra of generation and invocation:

Hūṃ e yaṃ raṃ khaṃ su raṃ keṃ bruṃ. Abhiṣaṇa hūṃ. Oṃ niyama sa slā lā nān garuṇa cale cale hūṃ phaṭ.

From the utterance of this, garuḍas emanated from primordial wisdom: one dark blue, one white, one yellow, one red, one green, each appearing separately, and then gathered together into a garuḍa whose body is white, whose wings are blue, whose horn is a blazing wisdom sword, and who leads away and devours the malevolent — thus abiding in the compassion of the blazing wrathful one. The body bears five colors; the beak is formed of meteoric iron and vajra; the wings are vast enough to cover the entire reach of the sky; the eyes shine like molten gold. Four kinds of malevolent serpents are devoured each in their own place — at the crown of the head, at the forehead, at the right and left sides behind the ears, and at the back of the crown, and so on.

Each buddha-family performed the empowerment with its own hand-implement. The lord of the Tathāgata family (the vajra family) placed the wheel at the crown of the head, saying:

Oṃ sarva-tathāgata-kāya-vajra-svabhāva-ātmako'ham.

And thus the wheel was bestowed. The lord of the speech family likewise placed the wheel of Dharma at the throat, saying:

Āḥ sarva-tathāgata-vāk-vajra-svabhāva-ātmako'ham.

The lord of the mind family placed the vajra at the heart, saying:

Hūṃ sarva-tathāgata-mahācitta-vajra-svabhāva-ātmako'ham.

The lord of the qualities family placed a blazing jewel at the shoulders and navel, saying:

Svā sarva-tathāgata-ratna-guhya-abhiṣiñcantu māṃ.

The lord of the karma (action) family likewise placed the wheel at the secret place, saying:

Hā sarva-tathāgata-karma-guhya-abhiṣiñcantu māṃ.

Then there emerged small wrathful ones of primordial wisdom — small of body yet large of eye, holding vajras and pointing the threatening gesture — in white, red, yellow, green, dark blue, and so on, in manifold variety. Through the blessing of these, this great earth trembled in six ways, shook greatly, shook throughout, and shook entirely and utterly. Even the sound of the fire of the cosmic conflagration rang out. They gathered here and descended from the sky, abiding at the center of the blazing wheel. Then with a single voice all the Tathāgatas declared:

Roṣaṇa yakṣa gṛhṇa gṛhṇa phaṭ!

From the utterance of this, the wrathful one of primordial wisdom appeared — the body flashing with a hue like venom-dark black poison, the three eyes blazing with the ferocity of primordial wisdom, darting like lightning at the end of an aeon, their fierce heat capable of destroying whatever they behold, like the rays of a scorching sun. The furrowed brows blaze dark yellow like fierce lightning, dispelling the darkness of delusion and mental afflictions. The mustache and beard are dark yellow, blazing with wisdom fire, burning the habitual tendencies of the afflictions. He wears a fresh elephant hide, a symbol of subjugating the affliction of delusion. As a sign of taming the affliction of aversion, he is clothed in a tiger-skin skirt, the garment of the lord of wild beasts. He is adorned with the head of a leopard as a symbol of taming the affliction of jealousy.

As a sign of not having abandoned the cycle of afflictions, he wears as a garment the hide of a great bull. He is adorned at the crown with white-speckled serpents, wears yellow-speckled serpents as a throat ornament, wears red-speckled serpents as armlets, wears green-speckled serpents as a belt, and wears black-speckled serpents as anklets. As a sign that the three poisons are not spontaneously abandoned, he consumes the flesh and blood of the afflictions. He is adorned upon his body with dry skulls, fresh skulls, and a garland of skulls, which are endowed with the significance of body, speech, and mind. He is adorned upon his body with a pattern rendered in blood and serum, because he gazes with compassion upon cyclic existence — which, while endowed with the significance of the unborn nature of reality, resembles blood and serum. He is adorned upon his body with a cluster of great ash, in order to tame all beings through the power of the Great Vehicle. Though endowed with the significance of the unborn nature of reality, blazing jewels appear as his ornaments; his dark-blue hair is bound with a crown of matted locks. His right foot is drawn in, resting in a majestic posture; his left foot is extended, resting in a wrathful posture, trampling upon male and female rākṣasas who threaten life. In his right hand he holds a five-pointed vajra in a striking posture; in his left hand the threatening finger-gesture is raised, appearing in all its clarity, together with a khaṭvāṅga and a blazing sword — with these four limbs he is empowered as the agent of enlightened activity. Having been empowered with the five types of sacred substance and nectar, he is empowered as the agent of all enlightened activities, and this is accomplished through the sport of non-duality.

This is the sixth chapter of the tantra of the secret mind of glorious Vajra Caṇḍa — the chapter on the empowerment of the Blazing Wrathful One by all the Tathāgatas.

Then glorious Vajra Caṇḍa, with Vajrapāṇi raising the threatening finger-gesture and lifting the vajra aloft, gazed in all directions with the glance of a blazing wrathful one; his eyes rolled back; his mouth was thrown wide open; and from his mouth burst forth the resounding roar of fearful laughter: Ha ha! Hūṃ hūṃ! A la la! Aṃ aṃ! Dhi dhi! Ho ho! Through the blessing of these sounds, both shoulders blazed intensely, and white vajra wrathful ones, utterly white and boundless, emerged. From the blazing forehead, dark-blue wrathful ones of various hues emerged. From the intensely blazing throat, red wrathful ones, fully crimson, emerged. From the intensely blazing heart center, black vajra wrathful ones, blazing and of various hues, emerged. From the intensely blazing navel of the body, green vajra wrathful ones, fully blazing, emerged.

All those blazing wrathful ones — blazing, intensely blazing, blazing in every direction, like tongues of flame — were supremely terrifying, supremely fearsome, appearing with an expression of wild laughter, appearing with an expression of snarling at the perverted and craving, pervading all with great compassion. Their minds engaged in the four activities of pacification, enrichment, magnetizing, and wrath; they transformed the substance of their bodies entirely and accomplished the welfare of beings. All of those who roared fierce sounds from their mouths likewise emanated as wrathful ones in the postures, leaps, and striding of wrathful beings — playing, wide-eyed and glaring, grinding their teeth, knitting their brows in unbearable fury — emanated as supremely unbearable wrathful ones who, with the scent of the supreme essence, the king of perfumes known as mākita, utterly rot the hearts of all malevolent beings. Such blazing wrathful ones emanated and thoroughly emanated.

Then, in a fierce roar, the wrathful mantra was proclaimed:

Oṃ vajra caṇḍa. Oṃ vipule. Oṃ vajra caṇḍa pravara nāga. Oṃ vajra caṇḍa mahānāga hṛdaya. Āvarte. Jvala jvala. Sarva nāga hṛdayāni. Āvarte jvala. Sarva nāga hṛdayāni. Kimi kimi. Nāga kulāni vidhvaṃsani. Hana hana. Sarva nāga duṣṭāṃ hṛdayāni. Daha daha. Sarva duṣṭān nāga bandhani. Paca paca. Pācaya pācaya. Sarva duṣṭāṃ nāgānāṃ. Akrama akrama. Sukrama sakara vimali. Vikrama.

Mahānāga tejā bare svāhā.

At these words, all of them became utterly paralyzed and limp. The world-realm of the great trichiliocosm shook in six ways, shook greatly, and shook thoroughly throughout.

This is the seventh chapter, "The Chapter of Subjugation," from the tantra of the secret mind of Glorious Vajra the Fierce.

Then, in order to overwhelm those arrogant ones, the vajra was hurled into the sky, and those arrogant ones were shaken like leaves tossed by the wind. Then, from that wrathful and majestic form, with a thunderous and terrible voice, these words were spoken:

I, the king of wrathful ones,
overwhelm without exception
all who dwell within the three realms of existence.
The nāga kings of the four clans with their retinues
shall be subdued by fierce and blazing splendor.
I am the sovereign of power and might,
with wrathful voice and majestic form.
By the fierce mudrā I overwhelm all.

Having thus proclaimed these words, he formed the mudrā known as the mudrā of majestic subjugation: he interlaced the fingers of both hands, brought the two thumbs and two little fingers to touch tip to tip separately, and extended them. Then, with the wheel-marked palms of his hands pointed downward, he pronounced the supremely irresistible mantra:

Oṃ vajra caṇḍa vajrapāṇi hūṃ hūṃ. Garuḍa pakṣe. Hūṃ hūṃ. Jeṃ māla bari ci. Ci śri ca hūṃ phaṭ.

At these words, all the arrogant ones and all the nāgas became intoxicated, utterly drunk, and cried out in supplication: "We shall do as you command." All of them, as if they had drunk a powerful intoxicating brew or like people overcome by sleep, spoke together with one voice:

Ah, Lord of Secrets, Blessed One,
lord of vidyā-mantras, king of wrathful ones,
by the power of your vidyā-mantra's proclamation
we are utterly and inexhaustibly intoxicated.
We come as your subjects and seek to follow your command.
O compassionate one, look upon us with favor.
We come as your subjects.
Henceforth we vow not to harm others.

Thus they swore their oath.

This is the eighth chapter, "The Chapter of Overwhelming the Arrogant Ones," from the tantra of the secret mind of Glorious Vajra the Fierce.

Then the great commander of the gods of the Heaven of the Thirty-Three, known as Śakra (Indra, lord of the gods), came to that place, made offerings with various and abundant offering articles, and addressed the wrathful vajra deity in these words: "O great lord of wrathful ones, since these malicious and arrogant beings have become thoroughly unruly, please, having tamed them, be so kind as to engage in the practice of sustaining the teachings."

At this, the wrathful vajra spoke: "O lord of the gods, hear this. Gather the various substances of the nāgas in their many varieties. Purify and cleanse your own body, dress in clean white cloth that has not been used before, and anoint the body with fragrant drops possessing sweet and warm scent. Prepare a mixture of raw cane sugar, flesh-sugar, and honey, and from seven or nine days beforehand abandon all unwholesome foods. On the body, affix five peacock feathers; let this be done by one possessing wisdom. Blend various nāga medicines together with soft grain into a powder, mix it with sweet water, and make one hundred and eight pills of the size of a rī-lu seed of thick meat. Add also different varieties of nāga medicines so that they include white and other colors.

"Then, regarding these with great compassion, pronounce:

O nāga kings with your retinues,
with joy and reverence I invite you.
With playful affection and longing, come hither.
With joy and reverence, harbor no resentment.
All of you, come hither with loving care.

When these words were proclaimed, the senses of all of them became exceedingly clear and pure, like bees gathering on a ripe fruit, and all, arrayed in a joyful countenance and demeanor, assembled in that very place. Then the mantra was proclaimed:

Nāgarāja samaya svāhā.

At these words, all were satisfied. Then all of them together with one voice presented their pledge sequence:

O great hero, lord of wrathful ones,
should we transgress your command,
may our heads, bodies, and hearts —

be shattered into pieces and rot away.
Tadyathā: oṃ caṭā caṭā, biciṭu, śatha śatha, svacate svāhā.

Thus was the pledge presented.

This concludes the ninth chapter of the tantra of the secret mind of Glorious Vajra the Fierce — the chapter on binding the malevolent ones under command.

Then again the Great Lord (Śakra, the lord of the gods, Indra) made supplication: O vajra wrathful ones, if these arrogant ones have become utterly unruly — some with broken and fractured bones, some whose life-formations are nearing their end, some tormented by the suffering of seizure dwelling in the crest-jewel atop the head of Garuḍa, some with blood dried up, some with broken nails and limbs, some fallen into the sleep of delusion and unknowing of the heart of awakening, some with channels and sinews severed and afflicted with manifold diseases, some with the entire body exhausted by fever, some perished with not even their name resounding, some with afflictions proliferated and no longer remembering the awakening mind previously generated, some who, though remembering the generation of the awakening mind, do not behold the face of the Tathāgata, and some who, like beings of the hungry-ghost realm, have no share of food — please grant your command on the means of healing all of these.

At these words, the Lord of the Gods, by the hand of the lord of the gods, healed severed hands. The claws of the water-monster (makara) healed broken nails. The filament of the nāga flower caused the mind of awakening to be remembered. The nāga blossom transformed the nāga's dwelling place into a garden of flowers.
