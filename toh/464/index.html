About this text

Toh 464 in the Kangyur is a tantric scripture known in Sanskrit as the Bhagavān Vajrapāṇi Guhyābhideśa Tantra Rāja and in Tibetan as the Bcom ldan 'das phyag na rdo rje gsang ba mngon par bstan pa'i rgyud kyi rgyal po, rendered in English as The Tantra King Called the Blessed One Vajrapāṇi's Manifest Revelation of Secrets. It belongs to the class of tantric texts in the Kangyur and takes as its central subject the bodhisattva Vajrapāṇi, the Lord of Secrets, whose esoteric teachings and wrathful power are here revealed in the form of a royal tantra. The text opens in the familiar sūtric style, with the Buddha residing in the Willow Palace surrounded by an immense assembly of buddhas, bodhisattvas, vidyā wrathful ones, asuras, and yakṣas, before entering a meditative absorption called Subduing All Māras and setting the stage for Vajrapāṇi's revelation of secret doctrine. As a tantra king, it occupies a prominent place among the esoteric scriptures of the Kangyur, reflecting the tradition's emphasis on Vajrapāṇi as a supreme guardian and revealer of tantric teachings.

In the language of India: Bhagavān Vajrapāṇi Guhyābhideśa Tantra Rāja Nāma. In the language of Tibet: The Tantra King Called the Blessed One Vajrapāṇi's Manifest Revelation of Secrets.

Homage to the glorious Lord of Secrets, Vajrapāṇi.

Thus did I hear at one time. The Blessed One was dwelling in the Willow Palace together with a retinue of buddhas equal in number to the grains of sand along the Ganges River, together with the bodhisattva assembly including Maitreya and others, together with an immeasurable assembly of vidyā wrathful ones, together with an assembly of asuras, and together with five hundred yakṣas, all gathered in one place.

At that time the Blessed One entered into the meditative absorption called "Subduing All Māras." Then the bodhisattva Vajrapāṇi (Skt. Vajrapāṇi) draped his upper robe over one shoulder, made prostration, and spoke these words:

O holder of the vajra of secrets,
arise swiftly from the samādhi king,
the immovable, and reveal the supreme secret:
the domain of the five wisdoms,
the supreme secret of body, speech, and mind.
How is the sequence of accomplishment
and the activity of action, in brief?
By what manner of person is it accomplished,
and the supreme, who has become the king of awareness?
May the Blessed One, speak this well.

When he had made this request, the Blessed One arose from his meditative equipoise. He struck the vajra of the five inseparable wisdoms at his heart three times, causing all world-realms to tremble and shake and quake entirely; then, having sent forth rays of light called "Taming the Malevolent," which dissolved back into his heart, from the navel of the vajra arose the consort Vajrasukhā, the courageous vajra woman. She circumambulated her own teacher three times to the right, then with palms pressed together in prostration, spoke these words:

What you have well declared,
O Vajradhāra, is the supreme
sacred secret of your body, speech, and mind.
This great tantra, difficult to find,
is like a wish-fulfilling jewel,
born from immeasurable accumulations.
From the emptiness of compassion's nature,
may you, Blessed One, explain it.

When she had made this request, the Blessed One smiled, and sent forth rays of light called "Maitreya's Manifold Display," which pervaded all world-realms and then gathered back, dissolving into his own face. Then he spoke:

O Vajrasukhā, the courageous one,
just as you have asked,
the nature of selflessness and emptiness
shall be explained. Listen with devotion and retain it well.

The body, speech, and mind of Vajradhāra,
from the nature of indivisible emptiness,
are compassion's skillful means in all their variety.
Because this is most difficult to realize,
it is the domain of the buddhas
of the past, the present, and the future:
inconceivable, utterly pure,
beyond expression, immovable, unconditioned,
the dharmakāya like a stainless sky,
the supreme understanding of the three times and beyond time.
How then can this be taught?

And yet, because its nature is compassion,
know the nature of skillful means and wisdom
as the method of the non-dual pair.
This is the supreme secret:
by manifestly revealing this very reality to others,
those to be trained will be satisfied, like a king,
and the fruit of one's own aspiration will arise.
Therefore the great tantra king of the manifest revelation of secrets shall be taught.

E ma ho! The supreme secret, the indivisible vajra.
E ma ho! The great tantra of manifold jewels,
non-dual in skillful means and wisdom,
this is your own supreme secret.

This concludes the first chapter, the chapter of joyful occasion, from The Tantra King Called the Blessed One Vajrapāṇi's Manifest Revelation of Secrets.

The courageous Vajrasukhā then asked: O Blessed One, Great Vajradhāra, what manner of person is the one who accomplishes you?

The Blessed One declared:

O beautiful one, well asked is your question,
O mother who gives rise to the cause of undefiled bliss.

Supreme one, O mother of no-self wisdom,
I shall explain to you — listen well.
The person who accomplishes me
is endowed with compassion and possessed of great wisdom,
patient and replete with diligent effort,
adorned with faith and the three trainings,
courageous and versed in secret conduct,
devoted and unwavering — the three refuges his sure support.
He makes constant offering without distraction,
trusts in his chosen deity (iṣṭadevatā)
and has received the outer, inner, and secret empowerments,
holding the samaya, its signs and meanings, with certainty.
Should one practice in this way,
before long one will become learned
in my three secrets.
The fruit obtained through such effort
dwells in the heart of that fortunate one.

Thus did the Blessed One declare.

This concludes the second chapter, the chapter teaching the person who is the practitioner, from The Tantra King Called the Blessed One Vajrapāṇi's Manifest Revelation of Secrets.

The courageous Vajrasukhā then asked: O Blessed One, Great Vajradhāra, what are the places suited to accomplishing you?

The Blessed One declared:

O beautiful one, well asked is your question,
O mother who gives rise to the cause of undefiled bliss.
Supreme one, O mother of no-self wisdom,
I shall explain to you — listen well.
The supreme places for accomplishing me
are the eight charnel grounds or their vicinities,
within a charnel-ground forest,
a mountain peak or a blue-hued rocky crag
bearing the appearance of a wrathful form, facing north,
adorned with lovely blue flowers,
groves of nebu trees and utpala lotuses,
solitary, delightful, and conducive to joy,
auspicious, wealth-increasing, and commanding power,
bearing a name that accords with Indrabhūti (Śakra's realm),
and apt for practices of every kind.
Salt flats without water, a barren midlands place,
a royal palace, a marketplace,
or a dwelling place frequented by śrāvakas and the like —
in such places I am not to be accomplished.
As for those who are ripened persons,
what need is there to scrutinize location and so forth?
For them, all is the buddha-field of great bliss,
and they abide at play within it.
Therefore one should examine by means of one's family (kula).

This concludes the third chapter, the chapter called Teaching the Place of Practice, from The Tantra King Called the Blessed One Vajrapāṇi's Manifest Revelation of Secrets.

O beautiful one, well asked is your question,
O mother who gives rise to the cause of undefiled bliss.

The stages of accomplishing me

one trains in the benefit of others.
One visualizes the vajra-like sky

Purifying one's stream of being, and before the assembled buddhas,
laying regret aside, one rejoices.
One makes supplication to those who have gone to nirvāṇa,
urges upon others the meaning of what is supreme,
develops through one's family line the great offering,
and accomplishing the benefit of others, one extends it to all.
One does not regard one's own suffering
but instead places all others in happiness.
Like beings abiding as if engulfed in fire,
one cultivates compassion for all sentient beings.
One trains perfectly in the mode
of being free from conceptual elaboration and of relinquishing the two extremes.
Buddhas and sentient beings are not two;
from the outset empty and devoid of self,
all phenomena of appearance and existence
are utterly pure in their essential nature.
The meaning of the Middle Way — like space, free from conflict —
in such a dharmatā as this,
the nature of mind is non-dual.
For sentient beings who have not realized this and whose minds go astray,
in order to free them from all suffering,
one cultivates great compassion.

One's own mind itself as the syllable HŪṂ —
the mother who gives birth to all buddhas,
lotus-born, endowed with skillful means and wisdom,
free from both extremes and furnished with a seat,
non-dual, of vajra nature —
refined by compassion, the third is complete.
Single-faced, dark of body,
the right hand holds the vajra,
blazing with the vajra's pressing power,
the left hand rests upon the hip holding a bell,
poised in the sky with an alluring bearing,
adorned with jewel-bearing water ornaments.

The body adorned with silk of tiger-skin,
the right leg drawn in, the left extended, trampling the māras,
three eyes glaring, fangs bared in ferocity,
dwelling within the light of the fire of the cosmic aeon.

The three places of the three syllables —
bless the sense bases, made divine.
In the place, generate the wrathful seed syllable;
scatter immeasurable emanations in the ten directions.

By the four syllables, invite the wisdom down.
The Lord of the Family and the other buddhas —
contemplate the bestowal of empowerment and blessing as a crown.

That samādhi is the cause from which it arises.
The bodhisattva of the third rite
meditates without interruption for the benefit of others.

Alternatively, marked by the syllable of speech,
the yogin recites, performing activities without obstruction.
This is pure awareness —
ever undiminished, endowed with fruit.

The retinue of body consists of ten wrathful ones,

at the crown, a precious vajra horn.

Norgyé Tobgyu, Dungkyong,

In a great clawed hide he blazes with fire,

by fire and a rain of vajras.

do not perform this practice hastily.

has been taught here to you, Beautiful One.

This Black Yakṣa of mine
is unobstructed in all actions to be performed;

In a charnel ground, on a rocky cliff, or in a forest cave,
make the maṇḍala upon black ground,
with a lotus of eight petals and a gateway,
dark blue, triangular, blazing with firelight.

From the syllable Suṃ, visualize a Hūṃ above Mount Meru,
with rays of light radiating and gathering, transforming into a vajra.

From that arises the Black Yakṣa,
with three faces, six arms, and three legs.
The right face is terrifying, with the visage of a tiger;
the left is mighty, with the visage of a lion.

The first right hand holds a vajra;
the second holds a three-pointed trident;
the third holds a phurba dagger.
The left hands hold a great bell, an iron hook,
and a skull cup brimming with blood.

With his right he seizes a female māra in violent wrath;
with his left he presses down a male māra with a sun-like face.
His phurba-feet, red and blazing with fire,
are planted on the heads of the harmful obstructors.

He wears a garland of skulls and a robe of human skin;
he is adorned with charnel-ground ornaments of bone.
His red hair hangs downward;
his three eyes are wide, his fangs bared fiercely.
He laughs with a great ha-ha-ha and tames the malicious.

Together with a thousand emanated yakṣas,
he accomplishes all things according to the command of Vajradhara.

The vidyā mantra he offers is:
Oṃ vajra yakṣa kāla caṇḍa nīlāṃ para dhara śarīra hūṃ phaṭ.

Recite this root secret mantra one hundred thousand times. Once accomplishment is attained, target the object: strew it with grains of pepper and with a wheel of salt, insert it into the target visualization, then place it in the accomplishment vessel. Sprinkle upon it white mustard seed, cumin, and poisonous substances. Then recite this secret fierce mantra:

Oṃ vajra yakṣa kāla caṇḍa nīlāṃ vara dhara sarva śatrūṃ vidhārāya phaṭ. Hara hara phaṭ. Prahara prahara phaṭ. Chinda chinda phaṭ. Bhinda bhinda phaṭ. Visphoṭaya visphoṭaya phaṭ. Sarva śatrūṃ kāya vāk citta vināśakaraya phaṭ.

Sprinkle it until death has not yet come. Once accomplished, insert it into a skull of unknown lineage, conceal it beneath a great shrine, and press down upon it from above with a Mount Meru vajra — by this it will certainly be destroyed.

Alternatively, draw the great yakṣa upon charnel-ground cloth, and write this mantra upon his heart, enclosed within the letter E: Oṃ vajra yakṣa kāla caṇḍa samaya manusmara siddhi hūṃ.

Upon that, on cloth or on a leaf, draw the yakṣa's life-wheel with a hub, ten spokes, and a rim, then a further ten spokes and rim, blazing with fire. Arrange the mantra-machine around it, and write the name of the target together with a multiplier. This is the life-force of the yakṣa; it is a samaya command that must not be transgressed. Whatever tasks are assigned, he accomplishes them in body.

Alternatively, draw the yakṣa upon a skull of the royal class, anoint it with spiced butter mixed with beer, and each day apply it to whatever karmic activity is desired. Sprinkle it with flowers of matching color, and the assigned karmic activity will be accomplished.

On a ground of blue water-silk, write a wheel in gold with a hub, eight spokes, and a rim. Tied around the neck, this will protect against all adverse forces — like a bee at a flame.

— that enemy will be driven away from whatever place they inhabit, once the mantra has been recited a hundred or a thousand times over the field. Make a cord from the hair of a dog's coat, tie one hundred and eight knots in it, and with each knot recite the mantra once and place a yakṣa inside a grain of rice; thrown in this way, it will not fail to strike its mark like an arrow.

Likewise, when one is confined in a house, if humans, cattle, and the like are copulating, they will become stuck together like dogs. Place the image of a yakṣa, together with the yakṣa's heart and mantra, inside the heart of a black dog, and by merely keeping it, the yakṣa itself will become inseparable from the practitioner, like a body and its shadow, and will not depart from the practitioner even for an instant.

Such is the sequence of karmic activities, O beautiful one, that I have explained to you.

Then the heroic woman Vajra Dema made her request: Blessed One, what is the inconceivable completion stage yoga of the Blessed One Vajradhara?

The Blessed One declared: Well have you asked, O beautiful one. O source of the cause of undefiled bliss, supreme mother of wisdom of no-self, listen well, for I shall explain it to you.

The great inconceivable completion stage: first, the activities of the deity are performed previously. Then, having attained the visualization of the generation stage, contemplate the wisdom being at the heart. Within that, at the center of the heart, adorned by a drop on a four-petaled lotus, between sun and moon, the size of a lentil seed, contemplate the vital wind dwelling within it like the tip of a lamp's flame standing upright. When the visualization of this has become stable, do not think of anything at all; yet if one contemplates the inconceivable, wisdom will arise from it. Luminous and non-conceptual, the body of awareness — that is what is called wisdom. It is free of support, utterly pure, and clear.

For one who wishes to cultivate conceptual meditation with a support, at the place of the body there is a white wheel with thirty-two spokes and petals, adorned with the marks by means of method. At the place of speech, sixteen red petals — this is wisdom — adorned with certainty by means of method. At the place of mind, the eight junctures of the seasons, with the eight principal syllables dwelling there. At the places of the karmic activities, channel-spokes of the number of a staff. Wisdom, transformed into two, is red in color. In the non-dual center thereof, the division into right and left is the separation of method and wisdom.

The wheel that awakens conceptual thought has six spokes on the side of method and six on the side of wisdom. The last is the ground that holds bliss, where the non-conceptual hero dwells, adorned by four syllables. The wheel that holds bliss has no syllable; all verbalization has been abandoned — it is the supreme unconditioned nature. Seminal fluid, blood, and lymph reside here. This is what destroys the three paths.

In an instant, the horse of the wind rouses both method and wisdom, and non-dual wisdom arises. By wisdom, conceptual thought is burned. Even the ash is not found as an object — thus the five signs arise in sequence. Then the glorious qualities must be cultivated again and again. The enjoyments of the four assemblies will truly arise in their place. Having attained the ground of non-dual bliss, the continuum of birth, death, and the rest is severed. This is the perfectly pure and excellent ground — need it be said that it will be attained?

In this way, purified thus, when all of it — the reckonings of day and night, the portions of time, staffs, water-hours, seasonal junctures, and so forth — is collected into one, non-conceptual, equal like the pure sky, wisdom arises effortlessly and spontaneously. The great wisdom of the primordial buddha — to the mother of wisdom who liberates all beings of the three realms, I bow down. Non-abiding, free of conceptual elaboration, unconditioned — to the excellent guru who has realized this perfectly, I bow down.

This yoga, difficult to express, I have explained to you, O beautiful one. If the hero is not yet stable, then through the practice of the syllable Hūṃ —

All world-realms will be destroyed.
Fire burns up firewood,
and when the wood is gone, how can the fire remain?
When what is stable and what moves have fallen to ruin,
the indestructible syllable, utterly pure —
this is the supreme secret.
This supreme, unsurpassed secret,
the realization of selflessness, I have explained to you.
Otherwise, even all the buddhas
as numerous as the grains of sand in the Ganges
would not be able to express it.

The Blessed One declared:

Beautiful one, your question is excellent.
You who are the source of undefiled bliss,
supreme mother of wisdom and selflessness —

beautiful in form, tamed, possessed of a loving mind,

one drapes the upper robe over one's shoulder,

those who have entered into perfect awakening,

the west and south are the master's positions,

Apart from circumambulating to the right,

In this way, divide everything by measure.
If the measuring cord snaps, the master will die;
if it is too short or too long, illness will follow;
if the directions are confused, the disciples will be bewildered.
Therefore, let the skilled one perform this without fault.

With five precious substances or powder of grain,
or else with that which arises from the primary elements,
bless it with the ten syllables,
and from the purity of the five families,
draw with white, yellow, red, green, and black,
beginning from the direction of the lord.

If the pigments are uneven,
or if they are lopsided or low,
there will be no attainment and affliction by conflict;
if thick, illness will arise; if thin,
wealth will diminish; if cut off altogether,
death will follow; if the clockwise circuit is not drawn,

draw yellow, red, and green respectively.
The center is like beryl,
in the aspect of the lotus direction.
The borders are adorned with beautiful-colored vases,
together with the ornaments of the gates and so forth.
Draw it within a charnel-ground enclosure.
Place the fierce and wrathful ones and the rest at the corners and borders.

with reverence and with flowers,

Glorious great Vajradhara,
who shows the way of great awakening,
great protector, I supplicate you.
Grant me that very samaya.
Grant me the supreme awakening mind.
Grant me also the Three Refuges —
the Buddha, the Dharma, and the Saṅgha.
Great protector, lead me
into the supreme city of great liberation.

Come here, my child: you are
the great vessel of the Great Vehicle's noble way.
The supreme ritual conduct of secret mantra
shall now be properly explained to you.
By the indestructible power of the vajra,
you will attain incomparable wisdom.
Therefore, wishing to attain omniscience,
let the child turn their mind to this:

Honor all the guru-buddhas;
abandon root and branch transgressions.
Toward sentient beings not yet free in cyclic existence,
arouse the great compassion,
liberate them and place them on the path of liberation,
and breathe with ease.

examine carefully the stages for the disciple.
Give water for bathing and the tooth-stick.
Examine in order the ritual activities, the directions, and the attainments.
Bind on the protection cord and give kuśa grass.
Examine the dreams, and if they are inauspicious,
dispel them with the fire of the pacifying rite.

all previously accumulated non-virtues without exception
are purified by the mere sight of the maṇḍala.

"Child, who are you, and what do you desire?"
"I am of fortunate and noble family,
and I delight in you, Vajradhara."

encourage them with delight in that family.

Then confer the empowerment by name
with the water, the crown, the vajra, and the bell.

the vow, the prophecy, and the permission.

Give relief, and accomplish the vajra master.
Confer the secret empowerment, the wisdom empowerment,
and the fourth, the empowerment beyond words,
in the maṇḍala of the supreme secret.

Because they have fully entered the supreme

"Today you are liberated without remainder from all your misdeeds,
for you have been born into the family.
You have been declared equal to all the buddhas,
and by this permission, you are relieved."

"Child, always guard the samaya vows
that are the cause of attainments.
The student bows to the teacher, and pledges:
'As the master has commanded,
so shall I accomplish it.
As the Blessed One has taught,
with ornaments, clothing, and jewels,
with the kingdom, sons and wife,
and with my own body as well,
I shall honor the teacher without limit or satisfaction.'"

Today my life has borne fruit.
I, the living, have obtained the great result.
Today I am born into the family of the buddhas.

The vajra-heroine Vajrāsukhā (Dorje Dema) then asked: O Bhagavān Vajradhara, what are the signs that indicate the arising of diseases from the malevolent nāgas of your retinue?

Among the nāgas there are many classes, including the six families and others, varied in kind. Yet they have the nature of the five elements. Norgye and Śaṅkhapāla are of the royal lineage — the poison of [being] seen: yellow, ruling over earth, their bodies heavy and bloated, with four folds of flesh hanging down in a distinctive manner. Their time of movement is the fifth day at midday.

The seat of the illness descends certainly to the crown of the head. When it falls upon the wind-channels, it afflicts the flesh and bones in between. Having rubbed the hair and performed the bull-remedy, it afflicts the ears, and through connection with the lungs, putrefaction of the flesh arises in the lungs.

Gakyö of the brahmin caste — the poison of thought: red, ruling over fire, hot and tormenting. The body is red, slender, and lean, the eyes, channels, and body dry. Falling in the middle, its time of affliction is the western direction, the evening hour. Moving through the fifteen of the sky, when it falls upon the heart, the heart is joyless and mindfulness weakens. Ultimately it falls upon the head and body, and fever is great. When putrefaction reaches the heart, the tongue cannot taste. Padma Jokpo of the noble lineage — the poison of the breath: white, ruling over water, afflicting with cold and chill. The body's color is white, the skin does not acquire a sheen. Falling as the youngest, it afflicts on the eighth and the eighteenth, in the early morning at dawn — discomfort in the neck and right shoulder. Falling finally to the liver, that is its seat. The stomach does not digest food, the eyes do not perceive forms, the flesh and skin have no sensation, the skin is slightly reddish.

Padchen Tobgyu of the commoner caste — the poison of touch: black, ruled by wind, and therefore light and moving. The body trembles, the mind is agitated, the flesh leaps and shudders as if being struck. The four joints of the limbs ache, and wind disorders and phlegm arise. The upper body aches and putrefies, the lower body seizes, and nasal bleeding also occurs. It afflicts and causes bodily exhaustion in the evenings and the nights. The youngest falls after a day, on the third and the thirtieth. This one, of the gongpo class, through its roughness causes madness, derangement, and violent killing. Finally it descends to the kidneys, and the ears do not hear sound. Through the weakening of the seminal fluid, the fingers of water-sprites grow thin. The pledge of the water deity is the sky, and so it pervades everywhere. Whatever arises as described above, and knowledge of the time being absent, the spirit flags. The body's vitality diminishes and is lost, presenting varied symptoms. This is difficult to cure; it must be thoroughly ascertained. In particular, the spleen aches and moles appear on the face. The body is infested with many lice, smelling of goat, horn, and grass. The downward-clearing wind exudes the stench of decay. This one descends to the spleen, and blood also appears in the urine. A skilled physician should recognize the individual signs.

In general, whichever of the five has entered,
sequentially these manifestations occur.
Fire, caverns thick with darkness,
mountain grottos with poisonous entrances, pits,
temple precincts, swamps with ugly forms,
herds of animals, lakesides, and steep slopes —
unease of heart and burning sensation
should be known to arise first.

Singing in the evening, the body aching, the mind heavy,
the limbs uncomfortable, itching and scabbing,
pustules and small red rashes coming,
gnawing and itching, the color of flesh diminished.
Like mottled fire, cold as though cold water has been poured,
cold and damp in patches, hot in spots, the flesh mottled in large and small markings.
The flesh throbs, is numb, and seethes with pins and needles.
The eyes are round, hot, and the brows twitch.
The fingernails weaken and grow thin.
The body emits a foul smell, water blisters form,
and the smell resembles a dark lump of rotting fish or putrid water.
The upper body pierces with pain, the lower undergoes diarrhea.
The outer flesh wastes, the complexion fades, and the smell dissipates.
The orifices fluctuate, the vital essences are untimely.

The fruit of the clarity of the sense faculties
is the gateway of the liver — examine the eyes.
The queen's wheel-fortress is a good sign.
The fruit of abiding in the bones
is the beauty of the fortress of the teeth —
examine the color, the lower legs, and strength.
The fruit of strength in the marrow
is the array of copper-colored nails —
examine the mode of arising, color, and roots.
The fruit of blood and flesh
is pervading pride —
examine how it clings, the color, and the changes.
The fruit of blood dependent on warmth
is the fluid of a woman's secret place —
examine the color, the kuya vessel, and the form.
The fruit of the vital essence in the lower legs
is the root — examine the mode of abiding and the place of arising.

If all of these have their signs,
how could even I have the power to cure?
When the sequential stages of three, four, five, and so on are present,
the cure is to be effected through mantra and medicine.
By means of the seat of affliction, inner and outer, and their connection,
examine carefully and then proceed with the cure.

Even after you have departed to your own abode,
when you are once more invited, at that time come again.
Om vajra-samaya muḥ — so say.

whatever the eight accomplishments — pacification and the rest —
one will attain the supreme result.
