About this text

Toh 632 in the Kangyur is a tantric scripture known in Sanskrit as the Ārya-vajra-krodha-rāja-kalpa-laghu-tantra and in Tibetan as the Phakpa Dorje Khrowö Gyalpö Tokpa Düpé Gyud, rendered in English as "The Noble Abridged Ritual Tantra of the Wrathful Vajra King." It belongs to the class of Buddhist tantric texts and concerns the wrathful vajra deity known as the Wrathful Vajra King, presenting an abridged or condensed ritual tantra associated with this powerful figure. The text opens in the traditional sūtra style, with the Buddha residing in a magnificent celestial palace in the Heaven of Mastery over Others' Emanations, situated upon a vajra essence maṇḍala, suggesting a revelatory setting appropriate to tantric scripture. As part of the Kangyur, it is counted among the translated word of the Buddha and is placed within the broader collection of tantric texts preserved in the Tibetan Buddhist canon.

Translation

In the language of India: Ārya-vajra-krodha-rāja-kalpa-laghu-tantra-nāma. In the language of Tibet: The Noble Abridged Ritual Tantra of the Wrathful Vajra King.

Homage to the glorious Mañjuśrī.

Thus did I hear. At one time, the Blessed One dwelled upon the vajra essence maṇḍala of the wind circle of the sky in the Heaven of Mastery over Others' Emanations (Paranirmitavaśavartin), consecrated by blessing, at the center of a palace of stacked stories, exceedingly tall, extending for a hundred thousand leagues, adorned with divine, immeasurable mansions numbering eight hundred million, themselves constructed from the seven precious substances — with thrones of many jewels, lotus-seats and lion-supports; with reliquary shrines of multi-faceted precious stone at the crowning pinnacles, adorned with lotuses, wheels, and parasols raised on high, from which silken pennants streamed and billowed; with shadows cast upon the ground below, whereupon the assemblies of bodhisattvas descended from their divine vehicles and entered within. Beautiful with manifold ornaments: varied bells, victory banners, garlands of jeweled flowers, nets, half-nets, fly whisks, half-moons, half-vajras, hanging festoons and tassels, and much else besides; pervaded by porticos, vestibules, door panels, jeweled mirrors, parasols, and yet more; beautified by many layered tiers of precious stones — amid all of this, the Blessed One dwelled together with the great maṇḍala of his retinue.

That assembly was as follows. The gathering of bodhisattvas included noble Avalokiteśvara, Mañjuśrī, Samantabhadra, Maitreya, Sarvanīvaraṇaviṣkambhin, Śāntamati, Vicitravikīrṇa, Gaganalocanadhara, Ākāśagarbha, Rūpaśuddha, Kṣitigarbha, Vajrapāṇi, and others — seventy million great bodhisattvas (mahāsattvas) in all. Śāriputra, Maudgalyāyana, Aśvajit, Kāśyapa, and others — forty million śrāvakas. The great and famed Śakra (Indra), Brahmā, Viṣṇu, Maheśvara, Kubera, Yama, Nandīśvara, and others — three hundred classes of deities. Together with this multifaceted retinue of bodhisattvas, śrāvakas, and world-protectors, the Blessed One generated a great, majestic display in harmony with the diverse intentions and aspirations of those present, performing many miraculous displays and emanating many rays of light, and thus remained in their midst.

Then the Blessed One, gazing upon the retinue with a gentle smile, extended the faculty of his tongue, and spoke to the great bodhisattva Vajrapāṇi:

Through the gateway of letters, names, words, and speech,
blessed by the Dharma of mantra-form —
whether a mere syllable, a half or a whole,
direct perception and inference, long and short,
indicated by the marks of vowels and consonants —
I, who embody the ten grounds, the ten perfections,
the ten masteries, the ten aims, the ten aspects,
the four fearlessnesses and the unshared qualities,
loving-kindness and great compassion:
through the mantra of a single vowel,
may all manner of suffering be perfectly stilled,
may captivating wealth and lordship be granted,
may many forms of might and power be produced,
may stable abiding be attained, may definitive meaning be attained,
and may the supreme vajra awakening mind be aroused.

In brief, so that the minds of whoever desires whatever
may be fulfilled according to their wish —
Sharpen the unsurpassed vajra,
Proclaim the unsurpassed sound of Dharma,
Turn the unsurpassed wheel,
Take up the unsurpassed sword,
Grasp the unsurpassed lotus,
Make the unsurpassed jewel shine bright.
Having elaborated in the manner of a maṇḍala,
then condense it in the manner of a reliquary shrine.
Gods, nāgas, yakṣas, gandharvas (scent-eaters),
asuras, garuḍas, kiṃnaras,
rākṣasas, flesh-eaters, the hosts of forgetfulness —

Transform these many emanations
into something that strikes terror even in the terrifying.
The moment that supreme voice is heard,
Vajrapāṇi, the greatly powerful,
joyfully joins his palms together
and makes this supplication in pleasing words:

From gold and the five precious substances
arises the supremely excellent jewel-mountain,
which is the support and dwelling place of all beings
and is held as their true sign and symbol.
By fully purifying consciousness
and the four aggregates and the four elements,
those who know mantras, having taken up
the deities, mudrās, and enlightened activities,
will come to possess the sequential path of gnosis
of discipline and awakening, distinguished and supreme.

Then the Blessed One, the great Sage,
supreme among the most excellent, the lord of omniscient gnosis,
sent forth from his body many rays of light,
brilliant and radiant in various hues.
When those rays pervaded all the fields
adorned with their foundations, their cores, and their flowers,
the earth trembled in six ways,
the world-protectors and the hosts of māras
all fell into a swoon without exception,
the śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas trembled with fear,
and the assembled hosts of wise bodhisattvas
were utterly filled with awe.

At that moment, from the ten directions' fields,
from the hair pore-reliquaries (Skt. ūrṇā-koṣas) of all the buddhas,
the hosts of the great wrathful ones arose,
drawn by the tip of the light-ray,
and gathered here in this maṇḍala at the center of the field.
The assembled mass of light rays then converged
and dissolved into the auspicious vase.
At that moment, from the blessing of that light,
this sound rang out through the sky:

"All the sons of the Victors who have arisen
from the dharmadhātu — come every one
to where the Sage, the perfected buddha, protector of the Śākyas,
dwells in his great splendor.
Having heard the supreme, stainless, sacred Dharma
and the virtuous way of practice in secret mantra,
may you reach the unconditioned awakening
and attain the Protector of the World."

Then the entire assembly of Vajrapāṇi and his retinue,
the whole of the assembled maṇḍala,
cupped their hands, filled them with flowers,
scattered them in offering, made prostrations, and circumambulated.
With clear and reverent manner,
kneeling with palms joined, they made this supplication:

"O supreme great gnosis that does not abide at any extreme,
O chief source from which an ocean of qualities arises,
O great Sage fully adorned with all defining marks,
who has relinquished all that bears no mark —
what is that stainless virtue?
What is the mode of practice in secret mantra?
What is that unconditioned awakening?
How does one become the Protector of the World?
As the supreme medicine for us
and in your great compassion for all beings,
O Protector, O Blessed One, O great Sage,
please explain this again and again."

Then the perfectly and fully awakened one,
supreme among the two-legged, declared:
"O Vajradhara, lord of secrets,
you who are endowed with the eye of great wisdom,
in order to strive for the benefit of self and other —
listen well and hold it in your mind.
The way of being perfectly and truly at the ultimate —
I shall now explain this.

Taking life, taking what is not given, lying, sexual misconduct,
harsh speech, divisiveness, idle chatter,
covetous intent, harmful intent, wrong view, and intoxicants,
foul-smelling and unclean food and drink,
the flesh and blood of living creatures, and wrong livelihood —
the guru, the Saṅgha, stūpas, and so forth,
and in particular the possessions and implements of worthy recipients,
stealing from those who are lowly and impoverished,
selling texts, exploiting the Dharma for profit,
weapons such as swords, poisons, and the like —
seeing these as you would a pit of fire, abandon them,
and practice every form of whiteness of conduct.

Writing, making offerings and gifts,
listening, retaining, reading,
making offerings, reciting,
and with the mind reflecting and meditating upon these —
strive in the ten white paths for a long time
with ability, without interruption.

The awakening mind that lacks all fixation
is pure and stainless as crystal.
By practicing this supreme path, one severs
the mass of suffering swiftly — it is virtue.

The selflessness of phenomena, equality,
and the original primordial purity of one's own mind,
the absence of cause, result, and conditioning factors,
and freedom from defiled happiness —
from these arise, without exception,
all the qualities that are wings of awakening."

The conduct of the selflessness of phenomena is this: by making the wisdom of the dharmadhātu manifest, the unmixed phenomena are perfectly and clearly present — this is the unconditioned awakening, the very ground and body of all phenomena.

In the desire realm and the other realms, one engages in enjoyment, delighting thoroughly in their qualities. This is the secret, hidden from buddhas and non-Buddhists alike. For immeasurable, inconceivable eons, the vast, profound, and extensive trainings difficult to practice — mantras, mudrās, and meditative absorptions dwelling in body, speech, and mind — because they protect in an inseparable manner, all the buddhas declare this to be mantra.

The form endowed with the major and minor marks, perfect in all qualities, dwelling in the two truths — this is the protector of the bodhisattvas. For some it appears as the form of a wheel-turning king, for some as the form of a great ascetic, for some as the form of a rākṣasa or rākṣasī, for some as the form of a virtuous being. Turning the wheel of Dharma, gazing like a lion, and performing many diverse acts — for the two-footed ones, asuras, and the rest of the two divisions and so on, one has become the protector of all beings.

Those conceived by a mind of a Lord who is permanent, similar, and without certainty — such are the protectors of ordinary worldly people. Distinguished by form, appearance, and color, varied in land, time, and manifestation, they are worthy of worship beyond number.

The moment the Sage had spoken, Vajrapāṇi rose up, made prostrations and circumambulation, and then, seated before him, spoke these words: "O Blessed One, foremost guide, what exactly is what is called mantra? What is the nature of what is called mudrā? How is meditative absorption explained? How do they dwell in body, speech, and mind? How does one protect in an inseparable manner? For the sake of benefit to myself and out of love for others, please explain."

Then the foremost Victor, supreme among victors, extended the power of his tongue, and with the melodious voice of the great Brahmā, in order to exhaust conceptual thought, declared this:

"Essence-mantra, proximate essence, and root mantra — these are the activity mantras. Essence is what is to be recited, and seed. Proximate essence is known as supreme wisdom, and also as the secret essence. Activity mantras are two: the activity mantra that stabilizes one's own nature, and that which accomplishes the assemblage of preparatory practices.

Mudrā is the movement of the formations of the hands, and the change of stance of the feet. Because it symbolizes, seals, and renders unchangeable, it is explained as mudrā.

When one gathers the mind to single-pointedness, and through this brings superior phenomena down upon the mind — that is called meditative absorption.

Thus, relying upon these, as one's former condition is wholly transformed, this body — like a fine-winged bird freed from its egg — soars greatly into the sky. In all the buddha-fields of the Victors, hearing the true Dharma from the Victors, one then expounds it to all the sons of the Victors. And through a knowledge that cognizes in a single instant, knowing objects filling every direction without remainder — this abides in body, speech, and mind.

When mind and mentation merge, wisdom arises in a blissful manner. This is what all the buddhas of the three times have declared: protect in an inseparable manner."

This concludes the first chapter, the chapter setting forth the meaning of superior abiding, extracted from the tantra known as the Compendium of Awareness of the noble Vajrakrodharāja, King of the Wrathful Vajra.

In order to tame great obstructing forces difficult to subdue through peaceful means, and to tame those who lead beings astray, having understood the distinctions of their minds from within the heart, and in order to overpower those by assuming the same form — remaining on the conceptual-thought-free path of space, the form that tames those difficult to tame — the fully awakened one, supremely noble, wrathful, of great power and glory, son of the sons of all the buddhas: to that one whose name is famed as Immovable, victors and sons of victors here constantly make prostrations. Brahmā and all the gods and every embodied being constantly make offerings to that one.

Performed by the victors, sustained by the sons of the victors, the fulfillment of aspirations, inconceivable —

Having perfected gnosis, one attains the ground of the Immovable;
by that attainment one is called Immovable.
A hero who vanquishes adversarial opponents —
the wrathful one is the one who cuts them down completely.
Through the unfailing true words of mantra,
the hosts of māras and śrāvakas cannot move him.
Victorious through wrath, free of conceptual thought, toward māra —
through miraculous power he is Immovable, beyond all motion.

The supreme essence of suchness,
the body of the Victor arisen from emptiness:
abiding in the very nature of dharmadhātu,
this emanation purifies the realms of conditioned existence.

Like space, stainless and free of conceptual elaboration,
all the buddhas of the three times arise from it.
In the nature of space, free of elaboration,
what is called secret mantra is only a conventional designation —
yet it transcends the domain of speech.
The Protector taught it for the sake of understanding through convention.

Mind-free, nature-free, emptiness —
one meditates upon it with the mind as though upon an illusion.
The goals one desires — beautifully equal, without substantiality —
are granted by the proclamation of this lord of secret mantra.

The non-Buddhists hold to self, life-force, person,
a primal cause, and a supreme being pervading all existence —
deluded and arrogant, clinging to cyclic existence as permanent,
bound by the snare of error, they generate suffering.

There is no self, no life-force, no sentient being,
no agent, no primal cause,
no all-pervading ground, no substantial thing,
no perfect inherent nature in matter.

In order to cut the snare of erroneous views that bind to existence,
one meditates on forms of various kinds, without substantiality.
Through release from the substantial, one attains the level of emptiness,
cultivating familiarity with that distinction between outer and inner.

Meditate on the head as a water-bubble, as though an illusion.
Arising from emptiness through meditative absorption,
freed from the binding of conceptual thoughts and misdeeds,
one attains the level of deathless gnosis.

No aggregates, no elements, no sense bases,
no all-base consciousness, and no person,
no apprehended and apprehender, no bondage —
so the Victors constantly teach.

Without anything, parted from everything, abandoning everything:
if with diligence one constantly meditates on emptiness, bodhicitta, and the likeness of an illusion,
the sprout of the awakening mind will arise.

Like a wish-fulfilling jewel, of great miraculous power,
a body like space, unstained and unobstructed —
a bodhisattva who engages in mantra practice
thereby attains meditation upon the awakening mind.

What is proclaimed as wondrous, and upon hearing it straightaway,
one offers praise to the chief of the wrathful ones:

Constantly pure and stainless,
the stream of conceptual thought cut off, free of all mental proliferation —
to the great hero who has entered beyond examination and thought,
I bow down and offer praise.

You, for whom all phenomena are unmoved,
who has perfectly refined every domain of activity —
the omniscient one, ever unmoved,
whose nature is free of elaboration: to you I bow down.

For the sake of benefiting the many various sentient beings,
various forms of conduct and activity have been taught.
The paths of purity arise perfectly and completely
from the pure mind: to that I bow down and offer praise.

Like clear sky, pure and parted from clouds,
having abandoned the finest particle of darkness —
you, the Protector, whose body is like a crystal sphere,
free of all conceptual proliferations: to you I bow down and offer praise.

Like space free of elaboration,
utterly parted from all substantial things —
the aggregates, elements, and sense bases utterly purified:
to the ocean of gnosis I bow down and offer praise.

Like a rainbow appearing in manifold colors in the sky,
you, the chief, behold beings with a pure mind.
The great hero arisen to liberate all beings from the lower realms,
arrayed in great armor: to you I bow down and offer praise.

The essence arisen from the nature of reality,
the single syllable of great splendor —
dispelling the dense darkness upon the ground of beings' suffering:
to that I bow down and offer praise.

By this secret teaching of the supreme mantra's lord,
it is said that the sons of the Victor increase Dharma and give rise to wisdom,
and swiftly approach the unsurpassable level.
To you, the lord, I bow down and offer praise.

Liberated from karma and birth — from all that is conditioned and subject to change —
the Victor, arisen as a self-cause without any cause:
to you, supreme lord, I bow down and offer praise.

With the flame of gnosis, without substantiality,
having burned all the mountains of conditioned phenomena,
victorious in the single nature of dharmadhātu —
to the unsurpassable body I bow down and offer praise.

Having understood the meaning of the praise, he taught the ritual for the benefit of beings.

From the wrong practice of secret mantra and from habituating oneself wrongly to miserliness, from mental ideation overpowered by desire, mantra practitioners are reborn in the realm of hungry ghosts.

At the first stage, such a being interrupts offerings of butter lamps, flowers, food, torma cakes, garlands, and the like — the substances and implements of worship, human bodies, and food. It causes harm and gives rise to unpleasant illness, and brings harm to the crops.

At the second stage, having become a fierce yakṣa, it roams about craving flesh, blood, and alcohol; it interrupts hearing, reflection, and practice; it gives rise to illness, steals life-force, and produces wrong views.

In order to vanquish the spirit-possession that bears the knowledge mantra, the practitioner should exert himself and recite the mantra. By the suppression-rite, with the mantra's power generated through torma, fire offering, mudrā, and mantra — like a forest fire — they are vanquished without obstacle.

The gods are what stabilize those, and the maṇḍala diagrams are what illuminate them. What purifies all of them utterly — the supreme, peaceful bodhicitta — to the churning mass of wrong-conceptualized saṃsāra is like the blade of a sharp sword.

This is the second chapter, the chapter on suchness, from the tantra known as The Condensed Points of the Noble Vajra Wrathful King.

Then the mantras and mudrās shall be thoroughly explained as they arise.

Homage to all the fully accomplished ones, to all the Tathāgatas, in every way: Hūṃ. This is the heart essence.

Homage to all the fully accomplished ones, to all the Tathāgatas, in every way: sarva-thā caṇḍa-mahāroṣaṇa-sphoṭaya hūṃ, ṭa-ṭa-haṃ-maṃ. This is the essence.

Homage to all the fully accomplished ones, to all the Tathāgatas, in every way: hṛata, amogha, caṇḍa-mahāroṣaṇa-sphoṭaya hūṃ, tramaya hūṃ, ṭa-ṭa-haṃ-maṃ. This is the near essence.

Homage to all the fully accomplished ones, to all the Tathāgatas, in every way: acalārkana, naṭṭa-naṭṭa, moṭṭa-moṭṭa, saṭṭa-saṭṭa, taṭṭa-taṭṭa, tiṣṭha-tiṣṭha, āveśa aḥ, maṭṭa, malaka, dhaṇa-dhaṇa, tiṇi-tiṇi, khāda-khāda, vighnān, māra-māra-duṣṭan, rakṣa-rakṣa-satvaṃ, kuru-kuru, kiri-kiri, mahā-vijaya-vajra phaṭ hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ, tri-vilata-rāga, nartaka, haṃ-haṃ-haṃ, acala, tece, phaṭ-phaṭ, a-hūṃ, asamanta-ka, ṭa-ṭa, mahā-bala-sātāmanaya, traṃ-maṃ-haṃ, śurya-duloka, tuṣyantu-loka-vajrī, namas-svā-pratihatavale-bhyo, jala-matra-ta, asata namas-svāhā. This is the secret essence.

Then, the mantras to be recited together with mudrā and posture are taught here:

Homage to all the fully accomplished ones, to all the Tathāgatas, in every way: oṃ hana-hana māra-māra-yajñāna, khaḍga hūṃ phaṭ. This is the mantra of the sword mudrā.

Homage to all the fully accomplished ones, to all the Tathāgatas, in every way: oṃ gṛhṇa-gṛhṇa, khāda-khāda, pāśana, bandha-bandha, mahate, dharmate svāhā. This is the lasso mantra.

Homage to all the fully accomplished ones, to all the Tathāgatas, in every way: oṃ rakṣa-rakṣa, hana-hana, kuṭṭa-kuṭṭa, mārayā-duṣṭa-vināyakaṃ hūṃ jaḥ. This is the armor mantra.

Homage to all the fully accomplished ones, to all the Tathāgatas, in every way: oṃ dhara-dhara vajra-nali, khaṭvāṃga-pāśa-dhara svāhā. This is the uṣṇīṣa mantra.

Homage to all the fully accomplished ones, to all the Tathāgatas, in every way: oṃ bhañja-bandha-daha-paca-bhasmi-kuru-kuru-hana-hana vajra, gṛhṇa-gṛhṇa phaṭ. This is the club mantra.

Homage to all the fully accomplished ones, to all the Tathāgatas, in every way: oṃ ṣaṭ-na-ta-mūrti, daha-daha, sādhaya-sādhaya, vijaya-vijaya, vijaya-kīrtisāhā. This is the head mudrā mantra.

Homage to all the fully accomplished ones, to all the Tathāgatas, in every way: oṃ bhu-dhara-dhara, acintyabala-vikramaṇaṃ, bura-busa-laya, rakṣa-rakṣa-sarva svāhā.

This is the stone mudrā mantra.

Homage to all the fully accomplished ones, to all the Tathāgatas, in every way: oṃ vajra-hṛdaya, vajra-mataṃgi svāhā. This is the heart mudrā mantra.

Homage to all the fully accomplished ones, to all the Tathāgatas, in every way: oṃ hūṃ daha-daha, kiṃcirāyasi, ānaya śīghraṃ, rakṣa-rakṣa, bandha-bandha, duṣṭaṃ, dama-dama svāhā. This is the direction-binding mantra.

Homage to all the fully accomplished ones, to all the Tathāgatas, in every way: oṃ duna-duna, mahā-pāśa-khaṭvāṃgha-dhāra-sapari-pūrake svāhā. This is the seal-of-command mantra.

Homage to all the fully accomplished ones, to all the Tathāgatas, in every way: oṃ gagana-gandhodbhava, mahā-samaya, paripūrake svāhā. This is the incense mudrā mantra.

Homage to all the fully accomplished ones, to all the Tathāgatas, in every way: oṃ viśuddha-jñāna-rāji-saṃbhave gaganānāṃ hāḥ svāhā. This is the offering-spirit mudrā mantra.

Homage to all the fully accomplished ones, to all the Tathāgatas, in every way: oṃ śuddha-dharmate, śuddha-śuddha, gṛhṇa-gṛhṇa, sarva-sapari-pūrakebhyaḥ svāhā. This is the torma mudrā mantra.

Homage to all the fully accomplished ones, to all the Tathāgatas, in every way: oṃ śuddha-mahā-jñāna-svabhāve, tiṣṭha-tiṣṭha, mahā-naye svāhā. This is the incense mantra.

Now the ritual procedure for a supreme painted cloth image shall be explained.

For one who desires a painted image, first obtain good, clean cloth appropriate for the purpose. Wash it, protect it with mantras, and dye it saffron yellow.

The painter must be of good lineage, be calm and disciplined, have heard the Dharma, be skilled in drawing, meticulous in work, have few desires, a steady mind, and be free of anger. Apply to the cloth a sizing of lac-resin, bark extract, and alum solution.

For the colors: for green, use bronze-flower and malachite; for upper-yellow, use orpiment; for red pigment, use lapis-red, vermilion shell, and red ochre; for blue, use indigo, blue-bark, and gypsum blue; for black, use soot and añjana; for white, use śvetā chalk, which renders white. Prepare the ground of the cloth with sizing and priming. Write with the brush — finest, medium, or coarse — and choose an auspicious day: a victory day, a Saturday, a square-constellation day, a full-moon day, a sixth-lunar-day, or a day on the divine boundary. The passage ends mid-sentence in the source text, listing beings before the verse is complete; the translation follows the Tibetan precisely and does not supply the missing conclusion.

The term "སྟོབས་མིའམ་ཅི" (kiṃnara) is rendered as "kiṃnaras," the mythological half-human beings, which is standard. The phrase rendered "hair pore-reliquaries of all the buddhas" translates a compound that literally means "reliquary" or "treasury" at the hair-pore (ūrṇā or more generally from the body-pores). In tantric iconographic context this refers to light-rays and hosts of deities emanating from the pores or hair-follicles of buddha-bodies; the rendering preserves this technical sense while remaining accessible.

The line "does not abide at any extreme" renders "mi gnas ye shes," the gnosis of non-abiding, a reference to non-abiding nirvāṇa (apratiṣṭhitanirvāṇa), the awakening state that abides neither in saṃsāra nor in a one-sided nirvāṇa. This is a key Mahāyāna doctrinal term.

The final list of ten virtuous practices (writing, offering, giving, listening, retaining, reading, making offerings, reciting, reflecting, meditating) corresponds to a standard tenfold enumeration of Dharma practice found across Mahāyāna sūtras and here adapted to the tantric context. The phrase rendered "the form of a great ascetic" translates dge sbyong chen po, literally "great śramaṇa," referring to a great renunciant; in context this likely alludes to the Buddha's own appearance as a wandering ascetic, or to a monastic form.

The verse "for some as the form of a rākṣasa or rākṣasī" renders srin po srin mo, the male and female flesh-eating demons of Indian mythology, here listed among the wrathful emanation forms of the bodhisattva-protector.

The final two lines of the passage are syntactically incomplete in the source — the text breaks off mid-sentence at the colophon transition into the next chapter, hence the translation ends at the last grammatically complete unit. The phrase rendered "the Victor arisen as a self-cause without any cause" translates "rgyal ba rgyu med bdag nyid 'khrungs," which is a paradoxical doctrinal claim: the awakened one is beyond both cause and the causal process of conditioned existence, yet arises as his own ground. This reflects the tathāgatagarbha-inflected tantric view of the text, not a theistic creator-god claim.
