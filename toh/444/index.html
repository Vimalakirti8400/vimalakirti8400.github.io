About this text

Toh 444 in the Kangyur is a tantra known in Sanskrit as the Sandhivyākaraṇa-nāma-tantra and in Tibetan as the Dgongs pa lung bstan pa zhes bya ba'i rgyud, which may be rendered into English as "The Tantra Called 'The Explanation of the Intent.'" It belongs to the tantric section of the Kangyur and opens with homage to Vajrasattva, the presiding deity whose gnosis and inconceivable qualities are celebrated throughout the text. The tantra is concerned with the nature of mind and reality as understood through Vajrayāna teaching, weaving together themes of dependent origination — the twelve links from ignorance through aging and death — with the luminous, space-like awareness identified as the pure heart of all beings. As its title suggests, it takes the form of an authoritative revelation that discloses or "explains" a profound inner intent, situating it within the broader tradition of tantric scriptures that communicate awakened wisdom through the voice of Vajrasattva as the Lord of Secrets.

Translation

In the language of India: Sandhivyākaraṇa-nāma-tantra. In the language of Tibet: The Tantra Called "The Explanation of the Intent."

Homage to the Blessed One, glorious Vajrasattva.

Thus did I hear. At one time of supreme clarity, the Blessed One Vajra, Lord of Secrets, whose supreme samaya is the three vajras, whose gnosis is that of all the Tathāgatas, whose qualities are of inconceivable excellence, who transcends both existence and non-existence and is therefore said to dwell where there is no dwelling — here, in this single expanse of space-like nature, the great Sage abides, cultivating the omniscient gnosis in full array, known as the pure heart of beings.

Ignorance, and formations, and consciousness, and name-and-form, the six sense bases, contact, feeling, craving, grasping, becoming, birth, and what is called aging-and-death — these are not to be regarded as knowledge. Pride, the pride of greatness, the pride of "I am," the manifest pride of "I am victorious," pride that surpasses pride, a trace of pride, and inverted pride — all of these, the supreme one of vajra-extreme, the bodhisattva and Tathāgata, becoming a number that cannot be fully expressed in words, became the very inexhaustibility of expression.

Innumerable as the dust particles of Mount Meru in uncountable buddha-fields, the great bodhisattvas and mahāsattvas, equal to a sesame-filled vessel, and likewise the Tathāgatas themselves, appeared to fill the entirety of the expanse of space.

Then the glorious Vairocana, possessor of the vajra of body, speech, and mind, gazed upon the maṇḍala of the retinue and manifested bodily form arising from those three realities.

Brahmā, Viṣṇu, the Great God (Śiva), Desire and the Veda — and paired with them, the god Brahmā and likewise the moon — equal in number to the aggregates and the rest; the drum-sounder, and likewise the Delighter, the Tawny Ascetic who is Desiccator, the Sage of the Nose-ring, and the Three Jewels — all the Tathāgatas as a single unity. At that time, what was extracted and beseeched was declared to be the very first teaching of secret mantra. What the great Sage acted out in the manner of the awakening mind of a bodhisattva is explained to be the second.

The subtle yoga of fully awakening through the fivefold manifestation is the third. Empowerment of four kinds is the fourth, which is itself the maṇḍala of form. The fourfold basis of precepts to be protected is there, and conduct is here the fifth. The sixth is the yoga of subtle meditative absorption proper to the pratyekabuddha. The seventh is described as the offering of suchness itself, performed by the Lord in relation to the secret empowerment. Empowerment by mudrā-seal is the eighth, as taught. The four samayas constitute the ninth. The correct contemplation divided into ten aspects — the mantra of recitation was also taught. The eleventh is what arose as names, wherein the maṇḍala is explained as having a single mudrā-seal. The twelfth, whose essential nature is the body, demonstrates awakening to śrāvakas and bodhisattvas. Likewise, the accomplishment of the conduct of awareness, and the five superknowledges, is the thirteenth. In the fourteenth, the fourfold garland mantra is shown by the Teacher. The one who has become directional and ascetic — the unsurpassed maṇḍala of accomplishment. The drum-sounder, possessed of the right moment, is the maṇḍala adorned with colored sand. The sun and moon are the empowerment of the student, who has become free from attachment and of manifold nature. The symbolic meaning of manifold words, the single name that carries manifold designations — thus the separate gathering of all these manifold aspects is fully demonstrated.

Liberated from both the one and the many, one sees truly and cultivates fully. The self that has been fully cultivated is also to be shown as dependent origination. Then, regarding the joining of the unborn, the great Sage, the great Vairocana, demonstrated the meditative absorption beneath it: the twelve links of dependent origination. The multitudes of beings belonging to the links of desire — all the second ones are Tathāgatas. He entered into meditative absorption known as "the great manner of attachment," and thereby all the Tathāgatas caused the Lord to enter into his own body.

The great bodhisattvas, the great beings, came to be joined to that continuum. Then all the Tathāgatas and likewise all the bodhisattvas, in order to please the Lord Tathāgata, entered into him and emerged as: the vital breath, the upward-moving wind, the downward-clearing wind, the equally-abiding wind, the pervading wind — these are called by name — and the nāga, the tortoise, the lizard, the god-given, and the bow-king. Into these very forms he manifested, and then emanated again in the form of a woman, just as his own essential nature truly is, arising from the body of that very body. From the union of those three very ones, cultivate their own secret mantras. Becoming definitively liberated from all the links, one becomes supremely subtle.

What arises from needing a head and from names — that is calm abiding and insight. Since its essential nature is without substantial existence, it does not know even itself. Yet even so, the Tathāgatas make this supplication to the Sage: "May the Blessed One, from his intention, grant the prophecy; may he teach the supreme Dharma."

"O sovereign pervader, you who have taken to heart these very Dharmas as being without substantial existence, fulfilling the aim of beings, you are beautiful as a wish-fulfilling jewel. Whoever comes to know truly, they have no dispute with the worldly; and likewise with those who have gone beyond the world, and likewise those who have gone utterly beyond the world. These Dharmas of peace itself, with calm abiding as their supreme preliminary — even if a bodhisattva merely hears them, one finds bliss in suchness itself."

Then Kṣitigarbha and the other great bodhisattvas, the great beings, made this request to the protector, great Vairocana: "The very secret mantra from which arises the meaning of that unsurpassed awakening, which future beings will come to hear — may the yogic Blessed One please teach it." Even though the request was made twice, the protector remained utterly silent, saying nothing. Vajrapāṇi, knowing this, then made the request once more: "This profound, unsurpassed yoga of calm abiding and the rest — thus it is to be praised by mantra practitioners; this is what the primal lord has declared. The yoga of activity to be performed, and yoga that is solitary alone, and what the scripture truly is — it is fitting that these be taught." Knowing the meaning of what was requested, the Blessed Vajrapāṇi then declared: "This supreme mode of secret mantra — the secret — shall be taught to you. The great secret mantra called 'thus it is said' is the mantra that bestows the meaning of all mantras. There one becomes master of the five, and master of the fourteen, the four joined ones. Apply the name of each of the five to the clear and the unclear. The joining explained in the direction of the evident expansion is adorned by the five maṇḍalas. By not knowing the meaning of what is spoken here, called 'seventeen,' how at that time would mantra practitioners perform the mantra joinings? Having understood this meaning, and teaching the various meanings, and accomplishing the aim of beings — these are what the supreme vidyā-mantra practitioners do. Join self with the thousand in the proper way. The syllable A resides above; having made what is called 'the latter' its abode, the mantra practitioner should accomplish the aims of sentient beings. Whatever the joining of two A syllables may be, it becomes that which is born from the A-gate. That form of above, below, and the directions is the essential nature of the absence of substantial existence. What is spoken here — that is the nature of the method which is inconceivable. 'By myself' — that understanding, that change: this is where unobstructed thought abides. Teacher, teaching, and abode have not even the slightest essential nature. Joined as one in the three times of conventional reality, this becomes the hearing of mantra practitioners. In an immeasurably vast number of aeons, what the buddhas have known — mantra practitioners obtain, all at once in a single moment, perfect and complete awakening. Known by the omniscient, cognized by the all-knowing, fully understood in mind —"

Mantra is the very subject of what the Teacher proclaims.
That suchness itself is the supreme Blessed One,
born from the vajra of body, speech, and mind —
non-conceptual, like open sky.

All mantras are like the sun.
The mantra practitioner, like wind upon the earth,
moves freely through all that is desired.
Whatever transformations occur, each and every one,
those become emptiness itself.
Seeing that yoga of suchness thus,
the all-knowing one is the Tathāgata.

Suchness become a reflected image —
that is arisen as the vajra of body.
Perfectly satisfying all beings,
it arises as the indivisible vajra of speech.
Whatever yoga abides in the expanse of space,
its own nature radiating in inconceivable ways —
from that, from the very ocean of primordial wisdom,
the vajra of mind has perfectly arisen.

The mind of every sentient being
is thoroughly examined like space.
Because all abides there in that,
the form of woman, the single phenomenon —
that which all buddhas rely upon —
having become a stage, is the thirteenth.
That is called "woman,"
and therefore that phenomenon is bhaga.
All that is spoken of as action and so forth
becomes unified in the great sacred commitment.
In the single nature of reality, devoid of things,
all these become equal.

The sacred commitment of body, speech, and mind,
and likewise meditative absorption, the Victor,
earth and water, and likewise fire,
wind and space themselves,
form and sound and scent and taste,
touch and likewise the dharmadhātu —
all these have arisen with vajra as their ultimate reach.

The bodhisattvas of great miraculous power —
as it is said, "beyond all expression" —
those who have truly become inexpressible:
as many as the dust-motes of Mount Meru
in all the buddha-fields,
together with the great-minded bodhisattvas,
and the Tathāgatas equal to space:
Akṣobhya, Vairocana, Ratnasambhava,
Amitāyus, and the Inexorable —
these, equal to the expanse of space,
are the Tathāgatas of vajra-essence.

Just as sesame seeds fill space,
so all the Tathāgatas
completely cover the expanse of space,
and these appear perfectly and truly therein.

Then the protector Vairocana became unified with suchness itself.
For the sake of those to be tamed,
all with the great sacred commitment as their foundation —
those bearing names such as vajra of body and so forth —
the great-minded bodhisattvas, and the Tathāgata of all-accomplishment:
these are renowned as the limbs of the body.
Arising from inconceivable calm abiding,
the vision of the single unborn
appeared in the manner of great desire.

In the supreme mode of great desire,
entering meditative absorption,
the entire assembly of all the Tathāgatas
was drawn into that one's very body.
That is all the Tathāgatas;
that is the great Vairocana.
For the purpose of giving delight,
he rendered his own body into the very form of woman.

Then from the inconceivable protector
there arose the appearance of a woman's form.
That truly abides as the form resembling the eye of the buddha,
and joined with the form resembling Māmakī,
the form resembling the White-Robed One,
and truly present as the form of Tārā.
There is the form whose nature is sound,
the form whose nature is scent,
the form whose nature is taste,
the form whose nature is touch,
and for some, by way of the nature of form itself
those Tathāgatas are present.

Then, in order to demonstrate the single meaning of the three modes,
and likewise Akṣobhya,
and for the sake of taming those far off in the world,
the mode of transcending the world arose.

The yoga of non-dual equality,
the vajra-woman, supreme bhaga —
that is to be drawn as Akṣobhya himself
within the maṇḍala of the great sacred commitment.
Having four corners and four gates,
with all foundations joined into one,
consecrated to stainlessness,
its form utterly resplendent in every direction,
pervaded everywhere by clouds of buddhas,
stirring with many flames,
possessed of maṇḍalas of luminosity and the rest —
it is the abode of all the Tathāgatas.

Then the Blessed One, the Tathāgata, lord of all —

was established and remained at the center of the great maṇḍala. Akṣobhya, Ratnasaṁbhava, Amoghasiddhi, and Vairocana — those Tathāgatas — were fully present within the mind of the vajra of awakening mind. In order to pervade them all, the Tathāgata beheld the awakening mind arising from the vajra, singular in its nature of non-arising, like space. Wisdom and meditative absorption being of a single ground, he comprehended it in his mind like a shadow. Having assumed the nature of the vajra, he acted for the benefit of beings equally and unequally. Just as the sun at the horizon awakens those who sleep, so all the Tathāgatas awaken beings by means of awakening mind. Body, speech, and mind are one single vajra. Through the nature of awakening mind, which is pleased to give rise to those, it was made into the vajra-mantra. The forms of awareness-beings were blessed with mantra without compare. All the Tathāgatas beheld that as if with a triple face. Then, arising from the compassionate equipoise of the Protector, the Blessed One, he caused the awakening mind to be completely encircled and deliberately proclaimed it three times. Whatever is inconceivable and immeasurable, the secret of all the Tathāgatas, has come to be that very domain of the single vajra awakening mind.

Then, upon hearing the manner of the secret, the Lord of Secrets, the master of mysteries, prostrated and made request in proper form to the all-pervading Vairocana: "The phenomena such as the aggregates, elements, and so forth — those that are gathered together and abide therein — I am humbled by the teaching that sets forth the distinguishing doctrine that they arise from dependent origination. How could these inferior phenomena, conditioned by aversion and the rest, be fitting for a Sugata and for great miraculous power? How could that great-minded bodhisattva come to be possessed of a body and so forth? Whatever future persons seek out these things — may they not remain without them."

Then the Blessed One, the Vajra-holder, the one possessed of great radiance, declared: "Seeing the beings of the future, and that which you have asked me — Excellent! Excellent! Great hero, Lord of Secrets, well asked! Just as your words speak their meaning, so shall I prophesy for you accordingly. In the future, human beings will be drawn to what is most degenerate in the world. Abiding in wrong views, they will engage in whatever pleases them. Some will delight in song and dance, given over supremely to laughter and display. They will delight in fragrance and garlands, and likewise take pleasure in sexual intercourse. Some will be averse to the deluded and similarly given over entirely to bewilderment. Some will cling to desire, while others will pursue sense pleasures. Fish and meat and likewise alcohol, urine, excrement, semen, and blood — the deluded will eat these, and proclaiming the absence of these things will cry it out as Dharma. Going to what is not to be gone to — such as one's mother, sister, and the rest — killing one's father and mother as well, and likewise killing other living beings, speaking nothing but false words, and in particular stealing, approaching another man's wife, and committing further acts of degradation. Some will abandon the sacred Dharma and likewise commit the acts without interval, and having performed all manner of misdeeds, will still desire accomplishments as mantra-practitioners. Tormented and afflicted by diseases with piercing pain, by poison combined with toxins, and by unbearable and varied illnesses, they will come to desire liberation. Those deluded ones who resort to abandonment, who have committed unbearable misdeeds and are given supremely to wrong views — they will go to the three lower realms. In order to take those ones under care, this secret has been taught in this way. Whoever truly attains it will in this very place become a Tathāgata. This is to be understood by the Lord of Secrets and by the wise —"

Having obtained this supreme method, and having obtained the clear and genuine meaning, they still fall into plain arrogance. For those ones, meditative absorption, peace, and wisdom itself are very far away. Filled with excessive delight, the yogi, calling himself "the self," takes constant pleasure in overt pride. Possessed of deceptive and fabricated qualities, he disparages both nirvāṇa and other paths in turn. Relying on talk of secret mantra, they fall into quarreling with one another. Those ones, through the blessing of māra, if they obtain even minor accomplishments, become arrogant on that account and come to think of themselves as learned. In assemblies they engage in dispute and conduct themselves toward food like dogs. They harm the benefit that arises from their own actions and results. Of those same ones, they claim that both virtue and non-virtue are "our power." They revile their teacher, who is like a manifest buddha, and show him no reverence. In one moment they feel hatred toward him, and in the very next moment they feel attachment to him. They display the conduct of dogs, pigs, and crows. They come to take manifest delight in harming one another. Without understanding the Guhyasamāja, they perform pledges and the like. Without keeping the secrets guarded, they reveal them everywhere. Having disparaged those of this world, they then lay everything openly bare before others. Being themselves without any foundation, they transgress and violate their pledges. Urged on by those who are noisy and contentious, they find no opening within themselves. They will not come to understand that samādhi from which primordial wisdom arises. For the sake of even a trivial fault, they engage in fierce and extreme conduct. Mantra-practitioners of inferior yogic engagement and application will not attain accomplishment. They delight perpetually in the treatises of non-Buddhists and become expert solely in those methods. Having wholly abandoned their own pledges, they conduct themselves like a hook without a handle. Through the practice of mantras and mudrās they will seek their livelihood. Wherever they can obtain material gain, there they teach the sacred Dharma. These things have been taught through the intent of gathering them into the secret, into the genuine. Those who have degraded and broken their pledges — all of them are to be drawn back by this. If one does not know how to recognize the traces of one's own condition, how could those ones attain peace? Then, for the purpose of taking them into care afterward, these methods came to be.

Thereupon the Blessed One, the Tathāgata, having gathered together as one, offered to the Lord of all Tathāgatas his own self-offering — that very pledge-cloud — and having made that excellent offering arising from the genuine, he prostrated and spoke these words: "O supreme secret Tathāgata, O gathering of all Tathāgatas, this vajra-essence of the assembled non-dual yogi's delight — the supreme of all the condensed essences of suchness — please, Blessed One, explain that suchness."

Thereupon the Blessed One again spoke to all the Tathāgatas: "Whoever makes such a supplication — to those Tathāgatas: Excellent! Excellent! If even the Tathāgatas themselves can give rise to doubt regarding this, what need is there to speak of other bodhisattvas?" — thus he spoke to those Tathāgatas.

Thereupon, filled with wonder and amazement, he who cuts through all doubt — the Lord of all Blessed Tathāgatas — was addressed in these words: "Blessed One, Lord of all Tathāgatas, though distinguished by all qualities, why have you not been pleased to teach this as the supreme matter here? Please make it a place of blessing. For the Tathāgata's delight and for the attainment of the cause and fruit of primordial wisdom, please explain this genuinely."

The Lord of all Tathāgatas, knowing their supplication, then — Vajradhvaja, the lamp of primordial wisdom —

From the meditative absorption that arises from the aversion family, the chief one — the essence of Akṣobhya, resplendent with body, speech, and mind — was then perfectly drawn forth: the mantra called Vajradhṛk. Then, merely by its utterance, one who arises from the aversion family, born of that karma, becomes a being endowed with gnosis. Joined to Akṣobhya the Vast by skillful means, the three faces — dark, white, and red — are the faces of that great self. The Tathāgata dwelling within these three vajras is called Wisdom.

Then the Lord of the Tathāgatas, the origin of sacred commitment, called Samaya-arising-Vajra, entered into meditative equipoise as the sovereign of meditative absorption. Thereafter the essence called Jinadhrik was likewise perfectly drawn forth. Then, merely by its utterance, one who arises born of the delusion family becomes a being endowed with gnosis: the Tathāgata Vairocana, appearing in front with faces of white, dark, and red. That one, possessed of the arising of jewels, abiding to the south, facing north, is called the Wisdom of Equality.

Then the Blessed One, the lord, the master of the three vajras and the sacred commitments — the one arisen from great attachment — entered meditative equipoise in the meditative absorption called Vajra. From the family of great attachment arose the essence called Ārolik, then drawn forth from the three vajras. Merely by its being drawn forth, the Blessed One below — the sovereign lord — became a being endowed with gnosis: the Sovereign of the World, equal to light, with faces of red, white, and dark, lord of the great glorious family, sovereign of the Tathāgatas, abiding in the western direction.

Then the Blessed One called Vividha, entering meditative equipoise in the vajra of the meditative absorption called the Sacred Commitment of Meaningful Activity, drew forth what is called the supreme among supreme essences. Of the class that bows down to the sacred commitments, the essence called Prajñādhrik arose from what is called the three vajras. Merely by its being drawn forth, that very protector, the Blessed One — possessor of the vajra of body, speech, and mind, joined to the vajra of Meaningful Activity — became a being endowed with gnosis, with faces of green, dark, and white. The sovereign of the Tathāgatas abiding in the northern direction: thus arising from the aversion family, the wish-fulfilling jewel family, the attachment sacred-commitment family — desire and liberation are supremely accomplished.

Then Vajradhara, the all-pervading lord, entered meditative equipoise in the meditative absorption that is the arising of the sacred commitment of attachment. From the vajra of body, speech, and mind of Vajradhara, the noble supreme queen — the supreme holder of the vajra — was drawn forth. She is rightly proclaimed as the Mother, the sole mother of all buddhas. She is that sovereign of the Tathāgatas. Since she is the supreme noble goddess-queen, arisen from body, speech, and mind, she is known as Dveṣaratī. That being who is called the gnosis-being — she who bears the female form — abides in the direction of fire with a form resembling three faces.

Then the one renowned as Anuragavajra, arisen from the vajra of body, speech, and mind, entered meditative equipoise. From the three vajras arose she who is Moharatī — for from that Tathāgata also arose the gnosis-being.

She is the yoginī resembling three faces. Through the practice of the yogi of non-dual equality — the sole delight of all buddhas, united by the practice of the Compendium of Secrets — she abides in the direction free from truth. Then the one known as Anuragavajra, arisen from the three vajras, entered meditative equipoise, and from that great bliss was drawn forth. Thus was Rāgaratī brought forth — she who bears a form with three faces, the supreme principal, bearing the female form. She arose as the gnosis-being, born from the stainless wisdom, the supreme pledge (samaya) of the three vajras, the queen of the Tathāgatas. She abides in the direction of wind.

Then the Blessed One, the lord, who holds the pledge of the three vajras, entered the meditative absorption known as Bsluvajra, and from that arose the supreme queen of all the Tathāgatas — she who bears a form resembling three faces — known as Vajraratī, drawn forth from the three vajras. The moment she was drawn forth, she too arose as the supreme gnosis-being, bearing the female form, and she abides in the direction of Indra.

Then that Blessed One, the protector — entering meditative equipoise in the absorption arising from Vairocana-vajra, in the vajra of body, speech, and mind — drew forth within this maṇḍala the great wrathful one who is the slayer of Yamantaka, the great wrathful deity. Once drawn forth, he abides in the eastern direction to terrorize the Tathāgatas.

Then the lord, the Blessed One, entered meditative equipoise in the absorption known as Abhisaṃbodhivajra, that of all the Tathāgatas, and drew forth the great wrathful one — he who is Prajñāntakṛt, the vajra-holder. The glorious one so drawn forth arose perfectly as the gnosis-being. Through the practice of the great maṇḍala, he who resembles the form of a great blazing stake, who enforces the vajra-pledge, abides at the southern gate.

Then the one renowned as Ratnasaṃbhava entered meditative equipoise, and from the three vajras arose the great wrathful one — Padmāntakṛt, the supremely luminous lord, foremost among all the maṇḍalas of speech — drawn forth from him. The moment he was drawn forth, he appeared here together with his supreme gnosis-being. Manifesting as the speech of all the Tathāgatas, he of stainless wisdom, supreme and unsurpassed, abides at the western gate.

That very Blessed One, the variegated one, entered the meditative absorption of the mind-vajra throughout, and thereupon the great wrathful one was drawn forth by the principal — the great wrathful deity who is drawn forth everywhere and who brings all obstructors to their end. Through the practice of the mind-maṇḍala, he abides at the northern gate.

Then the glorious Vajradhara, upon hearing of this supreme maṇḍala, addressed once more the great protector Vairocana with this question: "Indivisible from space, united as one, the Tathāgatas who are without materiality having performed the highest joy and so forth — how did they then make that request? If these have no beginning or end, how can they have a principal? With the directional parts thus established, by what is this purified? This secret is said to be impossible. Those who conceptualize through the three vehicles, and even through the words of ordinary beings, will also come to abandon it. He who, rejected by the śrāvakas and likewise excluded from the sūtras — engaging with aversion and the like, and with the five objects of desire and the like — makes the Tathāgatas his support: those activities common to subsequent practice are inverted. Those practices at the extreme of pleasure, those very deeds performed by the Tathāgata — in a single word lie immeasurable meanings. These most sacred secrets, protector — how shall future beings come to seek them?"

Then the Blessed One declared: "Vajrapāṇi, this method —

the unsurpassed secret intention — all that was asked shall now be explained.

Aversion, delusion, and likewise attachment, and the five aggregates beginning with the body: upon realizing that these are without self, a fully awakened buddha abides in the threefold manner. What is called the father is calm abiding; the mother, it is taught, is special insight.

One who knows suchness becomes embodied in it. Tranquility is the food of the yogin. Just as a craftsman fashions an image of the Sugata, so too that which is like the body in form, and likewise the speech in the form of speech, and the mind in the form of mind — all arise from calm abiding and the like.

Phenomena such as the aggregates are seen to be empty and arisen through dependent origination. Yogins, from calm abiding, bring forth the form of the sun of primordial wisdom. Therefore the vajra of body, speech, and mind arises like a shadow.

Likewise the aggregates, the elements, and so forth all abide within a single sacred pledge. From this, the three modes, being of one meaning, arise from calm abiding and the like. Therefore, O Lord of Secrets, listen to the pleasing manner of the vajra. What is called the vajra of the yogin arises from emptiness and dependent origination.

The yoga of infinite space, the arising of phenomena such as the aggregates, and all conceptuality of body, speech, and mind — these have arisen and arise from the aggregates and the like. Apply these properly to non-substantiality, and from this, meditative absorption is born. Joining these in the non-dual meditative absorption, they become the indivisible vajra itself.

Awakening, understood as the realization of depth and vastness — through realizing this, one becomes a bodhisattva. These, equal to the sky and become great, are known as what is called the Great. Boundless realization is taught in the Vajra Vehicle. Meditative absorption itself arises from that suchness — it is the Vajra Vehicle that has arisen from it.

From this one transcends the world, and likewise supremely transcends the world. The actual reality — suchness alone — is spoken of as the secret of the world. What arises from the unborn is both conventional and ultimate truth.

What is taught as having become māra and the children of the gods — accompanied by the aggregates, the afflictions, and the lord of death — over these the Victor, through non-substantiality, is proclaimed as the Victor's vajra. The body and the like are spoken of as the outer aggregate; whatever element arises and has arisen from element: great bodhisattvas, the greatly courageous ones, these come to possess the lineage of the vajra.

Whatever entities by which the five aggregates are conceptualized — these, in exactly those terms, being truly apprehended, become Tathāgatas themselves. Just as a mirror, perfectly clear and like unto space, holding within it the self-nature of all things — therein the self seeing the self: the yogin becomes the supreme maṇḍala.

The aggregates are renowned as Tathāgatas. What is to be elaborated from suchness — this, for the Victor possessed of the dharmakāya, becomes the great offering.

These — the elaborate and the condensed — are fully proclaimed to be sources of delight. Whatever sentient being may enjoy this: that is what is called supplication.

True wisdom and skillful means being non-dual, perfectly joined inseparably as the expanse of the sky — that becomes the manner of great attachment, and the yogin thereby abides in direct presence.

The great yoga of the Vajra Vehicle — whoever applies themselves definitively to their own nature — from this arises mental ease, which is the source of bliss in all things.

Whatever three liberations are seen in the three faces, and from those the aggregates and the like — the nature of the deity comes to be recognized. This is what is taught as the three suchnesses.

Non-substantiality is the supreme path; without duality, it enters into nirvāṇa. Yet that which has become differentiated — this arises as aversion from the dharmakāya. That very self-nature of the Blessed Lord, drawing forth the lineage of the Tathāgata, possessing the vajra-manner of nirvāṇa — that very thing is what accomplishes all faculties.

The phenomena arising from the sacred pledge and the like —

Even though it has not been realized by oneself, abiding in that is correctly taught. That is delusion, and likewise mind — by whatever phenomena one examines and imputes, one performs what is called "complete enjoyment." That is the wish-fulfilling jewel, the lord of the lineage of the great precious ones.

That very thing is the Blessed Lord's attachment, arisen from omniscient wisdom, become a union abiding in non-abiding — the supreme manner of great desire. Just as a lotus in the mud is not stained by its impurity, so likewise the supreme awareness of the yogin engages with all objects of desire; desire pervades all beings alike. That very thing is the supreme vajra lineage — dharmakāya, saṃbhogakāya, and nirmāṇakāya: those three are the summary.

Aversion, delusion, and likewise attachment — the chief, whose limit is vajra-joy — by drawing out the three lower destinies, the woman, though singular, becomes manifold. When those three are properly established, they become Yama's wisdom likewise; the lotus becomes the wrathful one who obstructs — named "the one who opens the extreme," with the appellation of "limit." Regarding entering and so forth, those very three are known as the aggregate — thus-gone. Even through the terrifying executioner of Yama, it is the maṇḍala from which all arises.

The vajra sacred pledge that terrifies, the foremost that utterly ends through the Tathāgatas, has likewise come to be called the speech of the lotus — the accomplishment of the yoga of deathlessness. Though it ends obstructers, it is the chief of mind, the master possessed of the nature of the lineage.

Thus the yogin who ever practices moves through the secret body. Whoever's mind becomes patient, resting in the prophetic intent, let such a one act accordingly, and thereafter guard the secret rightly. Just as a child is nourished by mother's milk, the Blessed One is chief in the three planes of existence, satisfying sentient beings as their minds desire with the nectar of Dharma.

From the union as one of the three bodies — dharmakāya, saṃbhogakāya, and nirmāṇakāya — the Blessed One intends the secret. It is neither true nor false. Aversion, delusion, the precious, and attachment — the various lineages that arose — having become the sign of the lineage of intent: this is the foundation, the first.

Then the Tathāgatas once more offered supreme worship to the Tathāgata, the self-lord, and addressed the Great Sage with these words: "We pray that the Blessed One expound the secret — the vajra intent, the supreme, the wisdom of all Tathāgatas, the supreme awakening mind."

The Blessed One fully comprehended their request in his heart, and being omniscient, the Sage himself thoroughly contemplated all bodhisattvas. Thereupon the Blessed One spoke to all the awakening minds in this manner:

"Body and mind are like this, and likewise speech in the manner of uttered words — the supreme awakening mind: let all of you generate it."

In this way the Teacher taught the bodhisattvas exactly as things are. Thereupon the Tathāgatas, having conjoined body, mind, and speech in that very manner, proclaimed and proclaimed again to the great bodhisattvas — the great heroes possessing the vajra of body, speech, and mind:

"How wondrous! Born from the dependent origination of Samantabhadra (the All-Good), this birth is here supremely proclaimed by way of non-arising."

Thereupon the lord of Tathāgatas, the three vajras of the Tathāgatas, entered into meditative absorption — the vajra samādhi of perfect manifest awakening of all Tathāgatas — and proclaimed this glorious awakening mind:

"In the abiding of non-existence of things, it is neither thing nor non-thing. Thus, since things and non-things are as they are, cultivation does not exist as a thing."

Thereupon the glorious Vairocana entered into meditative absorption — the samādhi known as the non-dual sacred vajra (samaya-vajra) of all Tathāgatas — and proclaimed this awakening mind, the supreme secret intention-vajra of all Tathāgatas, in every respect and truly:

"Pure, free of the aggregates and elements and so forth of all Tathāgatas, liberated from apprehended object and apprehending subject, endowed with the emptiness of selflessness of phenomena — this awakening mind is proclaimed to be that which bears the name 'dependent origination,' the sign of non-arising from beginningless time, and likewise the very equality of one's own mind."

Thereupon Akṣobhya (the Unshakable), the Tathāgata, entered into meditative absorption — the samādhi of non-dual, inexhaustible wisdom of all — and proclaimed this awakening mind:

"Since these things are unborn, the nature of what is designated as the aggregates does not exist. That which is difficult to behold, like space, and free of self — this is to be called awakening."

Thereupon the Blessed One, the glorious Ratnasambhava (Jewel-Splendor), entered into meditative absorption called the vajra of selflessness and proclaimed this awakening mind:

"All phenomena are without substantial existence and free of the defining characteristics of phenomena. Arising truly from the selfless nature of phenomena — this is shown to be the manner of awakening."

Thereupon Amitābha, bearing that name, entered into meditative absorption — the vajra lamp of blazing wisdom — and proclaimed this awakening mind:

"In phenomena that are unborn there is no substantial existence and no cultivation. By joining to the abode of space, what is called 'substantial existence' is supremely explained."

Thereupon the truly indomitable, the vajra of overwhelming splendor, entered into meditative absorption and proclaimed this awakening mind:

"This Dharma is luminous by nature, primordially pure, like space. There is no awakening and no realization — this is explained to be the awakening mind."

Thereupon the glorious Vajrapāṇi, upon hearing the awakening mind and in order to explain clearly the meaning of the intended sense, spoke to the great Vairocana, the Mighty One:

"Lacking substantial existence and lacking any object of focus, the bodhisattva is like space. Upon hearing this secret manner of things, the thought of substantial existence arises. Through the application of body, speech, and the rest, the mind brings forth those conceptions. Ordinary persons speak of this as the culmination of the many aspects of true awakening. They grasp the speech of the Mighty One as though it were just their own words. Please proclaim it in accordance with the power of intention, for the benefit of beings."

Thereupon the Blessed One, this lord, the great Mighty One Vairocana, having thrice said "Excellent!" spoke thus to Vajrapāṇi:

"The very reality of non-conceptualization must be distinguished and explained. What is expressible in words and what is described as an object of focus are the conventional sign and the ultimate truth. Through conditions, through śrāvakas, through sūtras, and through practitioners of secret mantra, the awakening mind taught by the Tathāgata is unsurpassed. Likewise, this true joining is the unsurpassed secret of things. From the non-dual samaya-vajra and the lotus it is generated. What is expressible in words is common; the body of the buddha is that which has an object of focus. 'Secret mantra' is the conventional sign of suchness itself; suchness is the ultimate truth.

"The first, the one, dwelling in the center of the nine — that which is not bound by the ten — is to be known as the unbound; knowing that is the supreme abode. Vowels and consonants, the letters: the nine in number are followed by them. From the joining of 'A' binding them mutually with one another — whoever knows this is the crown of beings. Whoever knows it is completely liberated. One who desires to accomplish the excellent fruit desires to accomplish that very suchness. By being without form, form is abandoned."

The extremity of the elements is perfectly endowed.
The sixteen, beginning with the parts, are established.
The fifth, perfectly endowed with the five —
the four are applied to the three with certainty.
The syllable together with OṂ, made long,
combined with qualities and so forth, matches what follows.
Not a single short one fails to become speech.
It is neither one nor many.

By the meaning of YA, whatever it may be —
one who desires the accomplishment of activity:
through RA and the other three themselves,
one enters fully into the aim of beings.

Whichever syllable was explained before,
and all those that are directly approached —
feminine, masculine, and neuter —
are to be examined thoroughly with respect to the elements and so forth.

From below upward, thus arranged:
apply them precisely with the wisdom-mind.
Six explained, four, three, and one —
arising from the single essential nature of what has no substance.

When the meaning of YA and the rest is applied to that,
the knowledge of the three times too will arise.
As the skilled one conjures an illusion for the eyes,
let him bring beings to the end of illusion.

These mantras of expression,
arising from the essential nature of what has no substance —
that is form fully transformed,
that which points toward the divine.
Through a sign, suchness abides in three;
this is the characteristic of recitation according to intrinsic nature.
Without name, without expression —
this is the supreme awakening mind.

Those three, having become one,
have no going and no coming,
no cessation, perfectly at peace,
free from both eternalism and nihilism.
The uncontrived thought of the knower of the three times
is characterized as inseparable from space.
That very thing alone is the ultimate truth,
and it is the characteristic of self-knowing awareness.

This should be called the totality of sentient beings —
the very essence of buddha and awakening.
Even though a single bodhisattva arises,
it has the nature of dependent origination.
Whoever possesses this awakening mind as their very self,
that yogin becomes like the sun.
Though this is free from all conceptual thought,
it will enter into every family of beings,
like fire that burns, dries, brings ease,
and becomes the transformer of everything.

This is something to be venerated even by the gods,
and it abides distinctly within certain sentient beings.
The one who desires the awakening mind as their very self,
regarding all beings as an only child —
whoever abides equal to oneself in that way —
let great loving-kindness be aroused.
Seeing this without error and distortion,
the awakening mind arises authentically.
Through that, compassion grows immense,
and through that, saṃsāra is certainly overcome.

If all sentient beings — and likewise oneself —
do not perceive the awakening mind,
yet one who still acts for the benefit of beings —
from that, joy will arise.
Since saṃsāra has no real existence,
sentient beings too are like that.
Upon seeing the awakening mind,
one becomes possessed of great equanimity.

Then Vajrapāṇi, recalling the awakening mind he had heard,
asked great Vairocana again
about the characteristics and meaning of that very thing:
"If the retinue and the other — all of this — is the awakening mind,
how is it that, having each become distinct,
they abide in mutual contradiction?
There is nothing that, by itself, negates itself in a twofold way.
How does something that arises from a single jewel
appear in an opposing direction?
The inferior and the middling — likewise the supreme —
tormented by unbearable afflictions in saṃsāra,
certain sentient beings are oppressed,
and this too comes to be applied to the awakening mind.
Those who conceive of such conceptuality and fabrication —
future people will be making that error —
but what arises from dependent origination,
they will not seek out."

"Since childish ones will dispute regarding phenomena that have no real existence,
so that those disputes may cease,
please be so good as to explain the intended meaning."

Then the lord, the Blessed One,
spoke to Vajrapāṇi the concealed intent:
that awakening mind most difficult to find —
this he declared authentically.

"That which is empty, the single extreme of the future,
is just the same as the ultimate extreme of the past.
Having made that mind manifest directly —

do whatever one pleases —
yet the mind is not seen by that,
and so views have become separate and distinct.
Therefore those beings have come to bear
the names of the six classes of beings.

For that reason they rely wholly
on Brahmā, Viṣṇu,
and the Great God (Śiva),
performing all the conduct associated
with the moon, the sun, and fire.
Through their views they pass in mere moments
to what is taught as the Heaven of the Thirty-Three,
to the abodes of Tuṣita,
and likewise to the various hells.
They go as hungry ghosts,
and they go to the manifold destinies.

At the time when ignorance is wholly relied upon,
one does not know even oneself.
Those who are reckoned as sentient beings —
whether possessed of perception or without perception —
abiding here, arise from the wind
and by that wind are again dissolved.
Day and night as well,
they fall into their own conceptual thoughts,
and then, trembling before saṃsāra,
those beings wander through the many destinies.

That non-conceptual mind,
though it has come to abide in the ocean of saṃsāra,
through becoming inverted
passes into being a dog, a jackal, an elephant —
the mind travels thus as well.
Likewise it comes to arise
as a father's lineage, a mother's lineage,
one who plaits grass, one with a mole,
a low lineage, every kind of thing to be eaten or not eaten:
the awakening mind arises in those forms.

Brahmins and so forth — what the world knows —
arises from the awakening mind.
From the non-existence of things such as the aggregates,
one comes to know what is experienced by oneself:
that is what is called knowing the world.

Omniscience is the awakening mind.
Through the application of proximate meditation upon it,
this arises as the body of a buddha —
yet it is of a nature like an illusion
and becomes nothing more than an expression in words.

Then Vajrapāṇi, the Blessed One of many forms,
who had declared the meaning of the vajra-essence,
having paid homage to the sole teacher of beings,
entreated him once more:

"When you teach the awakening mind
according to the worldly scripture,
how will those of the future understand
what has gone beyond the world?
Since you teach the Dharma
in order to thoroughly abandon all views,
how can that very awakening mind,
that view, become firmly established?

Those who are confused by the words of the intended meaning,
not having understood the meaning of what was declared,
grasp it literally as the mere sound: 'It is nothing other than what was declared.'"

Then the Blessed One of many forms
spoke to Vajrapāṇi:
"Excellent! Excellent! You ask
without having understood the concealed intent of what you said.

The awakening mind has become wind
and dwells pervasively in space.
It is the life-force of all sentient beings,
bearing the names of the ten in the identity of the five,
renowned as the twelve links of dependent origination,
having arisen in three aspects of essential nature.

The awakening mind called 'wind' —
this is the chief of all the faculties.
Since it is ever subtle and non-manifest,
it is always declared to be luminous.
The activities of beings — domains and so forth — are performed;
everything here depends upon it.
Pacification, enrichment,
magnetism, and likewise the wrathful activities —
all of these are accomplished by the awakening mind,
resting in dependence upon the three abiding-states.

For as long as the world is designated,
and likewise the many things that are labeled,
they always arise from the wind
of the conceptualizing awakening mind-consciousness.
They are said to be like the seed
of all phenomena such as pleasure and pain.

This essential nature of the awakening mind
is equal to space — non-dual with the aggregates and so forth.
Through the joining of wisdom and skillful means,
the awakening mind comes to travel.

Abandoning reading and meditative concentration,
one who recites it to oneself as master —
through the distinction of day and night,
through the joining of sun and moon,
pervading everywhere without the existence of things:
that single wind of the awakening mind,
having abandoned the fruits of virtue and non-virtue,
comes to be like space.

By knowing the benefit of beings as well,
its name is just suchness."

Short vowels are said to be day;
long ones are said to be night.
From the distinction of vowels and consonants,
suchness is to be joined to names.

"How wondrous! The activity of beings has arisen.
Suchness illumines suchness from what is other than itself.
From that, the conventional signs of the worldly
will swiftly come to be directly known."

Then, upon hearing this awakening mind,
the Lord of Secrets, from the great sovereign Vairocana (the Blessed One who is the foremost radiant lord),
addressed the Pervader once more:

"This mind, which generates all sentient beings
and appears in manifold forms —
though by that token it is devoid of things,
it is renowned throughout all the states of existence.

The awakening mind is equal to space;
it is that which well savors the experience,
the one who performs the benefit of beings.
May it be taught to the supreme person.

One who knows this as merely a means of accomplishment
and turns one's back on it in one's own stream —
in the future such a one will speak,
calling oneself 'a teacher.'

One who says 'These very words of mine
are the authentic ones,' and then destroys what has been done —
that person does not know the awakening mind of intention
for what it truly means.

Likewise, worldly people who are without good fortune,
having abandoned all Dharma,
and who do not see awareness either —
for them too there will be no abiding place.

Therefore it is fitting to teach
the awakening mind as nectar.
One who obtains the sun of connection
will come to arise in the future.

Then the Blessed One of manifold forms
spoke again to the renowned Vajrapāṇi,
the holder of the vajra,
on the definitive meaning of the awakening mind:

It is not within, it is not without,
nor does it come to be named as both together.
It is neither permanent nor momentary —
it is self-aware, each one for oneself.

Non-conceptual, just like space,
the awakening mind, O Vajrapāṇi,
arises for the benefit of beings and the Tathāgata
like a wish-fulfilling jewel.

The past, the future itself,
and what is called the present,
and the body of a buddha too, which comes to arise —
the ultimate is one; the conventional is all of these.

The Vedas, time, the signs of fire,
the forms of sun, moon, and wind:
from the three three-quarters arising,
the awakening mind itself is just that distinction.

That is the union of wisdom and skillful means,
and likewise holding bliss,
and the syllable Ha abiding at the teeth —
however many occasions there are in that way,
the name does not exist.
That becomes this awakening mind.

Having entered within the vajra,
the very essence of what is to be contemplated is just that.
What is called "the state of relaxed abiding"
is the wind with heavy and light syllabic beats.

The supreme one that causes oneself to reverse,
that is also the vital force and the mind.
Pride, and likewise intellect —
the awakening mind endowed with these
arose perfectly from the four-part division,
imbued with the thorough pacification of existence and non-existence.

This mind, which is devoid of things,
is the domain of self-awareness alone.
The one who realizes the meaning of suchness itself
has explained it rightly with intention.

Then, having heard this supreme letter of the Dharma,
this secret intention,
the great bodhisattva Maitreya and the others
gave rise to wonder and amazement,
and exclaimed their heartfelt proclamation:

"How wondrous is the Buddha! How wondrous is the Dharma!
How wondrous is the one who teaches the Dharma!
The single meaning of the purity of all things —
that purity bestows the pure meaning itself.
Described as non-conceptual and equal to space,
we bow to the awakening mind!

Perfectly arisen from the Dharma of selflessness,
the one that perfects the accumulations of awakening,
without essential nature, without focal object —
we bow to the awakening mind!

Perfectly arisen from Samantabhadra alone,
the bestower of awakening through the intention's meaning,
the supreme conduct of awakening, the supreme vajra —
we bow to the awakening mind!

The pure mind of the Tathāgata,
the supreme accomplishment of the awakening of buddhas,
the three vajras of a single nature —
we bow to the awakening mind!

Those whose minds come to be patient
in faith in the prophetic intention,
from the vajra through the three samayas —

— this is indeed the action of suchness. The explanation of the perfectly arisen awakening mind from the secret of the three vajras of all the Tathāgatas — this is declared to be the second chapter.

Then that very one, the Secret Vajra, who encompasses the three — the three vajras, the three modes, the three supreme paths, the three liberations (vimuktis), and the Three Jewels — the sovereign of all Tathāgatas, the arranger of all Tathāgatas, the Vajra One of the radiant cloud, entered into meditative absorption and then spoke to Vajrapāṇi: "Hear this chapter of noble conduct regarding this supreme arrangement of the vajra.

This is where all Tathāgatas dwell in the subtle yoga. The first is Om, perfect and unified. The second is Śūnyatā. The third is Jñāna. The fourth is Vajra. The fifth is Svabhāvātmakaḥ. The sixth is said to be Ahaṃ. In this way, the six Sugatas are taught to the supreme beings.

Om and Śūnyatā form the first pair. Jñāna and Vajra are the second pair. Svabhāvātmako 'haṃ — these three realities are examined here. The three realities having become one bear the name of the unclear made clear. The maṇḍala of the Buddha of the sky's center, the great assembly of rays of light and clouds, pervaded by the light of the syllable Mother, entirely rounded on all sides, fully pervaded by the five sense pleasures, adorned by offerings of five kinds — in short, having meditated in this way, one should visualize the form in the center.

One should visualize the supreme mudrā of Vairocana, marked by the vajra of the three realities, or alternatively one should meditate on the mudrā of the lord of the three vajras. The great mudrā of Akṣobhya, combined with the non-dual accumulation, the vast expanse of the glory of the Jewels, the illuminating light of Amitāyus, and likewise the seal of Amoghasiddhi possessing form — meditate on these in the pure maṇḍalas of the buddhas.

The single vajra of the three vajras, Vajrapāṇi, whose color resembles indigo sapphire (indrānīla), blazing and bearing the vajra in hand, gazing with a terrifying wrathful expression, like the golden light of the Jambū River, utterly crowded with buddha-clouds — meditate on that great Vajrapāṇi with the nine-pronged vajra held in hand.

One like the light of crystal and the moon, adorned with a crown of hair-tufts, blazing and bearing a wheel in hand, adorned with various ornaments, like the light of an emerald (marakata), adorned with a blazing vajra, pervaded by a blazing and flaming assembly — meditate on that Jewel One.

Of great passion, bearing the vajra, of a color resembling a ruby (padmarāga), adorned with a crown of hair-tufts — meditate visualizing the lotus held in hand. Indisputably of the various vajras, like the great light of the five colors — meditate on the peaceful one in the buddha-maṇḍala bearing a sword in hand.

When the Lord of Secrets heard this explanation of the yoga thus given, he then asked the Sovereign Tathāgata also about the parts of the subtle yoga: "If the Tathāgata gives instruction on the connections of the aggregates, the elements, and so forth, how will those — the maṇḍala of the Buddha of the sky's center and the rest — come to be? How are those who meditate to contemplate the thing-that-is-no-thing called the maṇḍala of the buddhas, when those who meditate upon a maṇḍala externally — whatever is to come in the future — should indeed also hold the highest doubt about this? O Protector, which maṇḍala does this subtle yoga declare?"

Vajrapāṇi, bearing his renowned name, has himself given rise to that very subtle yoga. Through the non-dual union of beings, it is spoken in the subtle intention. This fivefold manifest awakening, become the cause for beings of future times, whatever Dharma has come to be truly gathered — it is taught just as it is, precisely as it is. The aggregates themselves have become buddhas, arisen from the conditions of awakening's aim — this too is what the Tathāgata has declared.

It is through the non-dual union of suchness. This one who teaches and this one who establishes — this is the teacher who reveals the accomplishment of mind. The yoga that is without beginning or end is to be examined as precisely what is called "subtle." The joining of what is unclear with clarity is perfectly accomplished. It arises from the essential nature of what is without inherent existence. It is perfectly established from the very beginning, free from both unity and multiplicity; it is known as the Dharma of dependent origination, whereby all things have arisen as one. Having made oneself thus, draw the maṇḍala of Dharma accurately. Red, black, and likewise yellow, and pure white — these in brief — the four combined into one: draw these correctly, including white cloth and so forth. In the center of these, together with the goddess, are those possessing both the supreme and the non-supreme form of peaceful union. Above, directly across, at the same level, and below — according to their respective arrangement with white cloth and so forth, fire and wind, the Great Lord (Śiva), and water: for each separate maṇḍala, it is correctly taught in eighteen portions. This too is taught to those who are pure. Just as there is a special quality in milk — it abides in the state of butter — so too in white cloth and so forth, pacification itself abides there. The perception of inner phenomena such as form and so forth is insight (vipaśyanā). The conceptualities of Akṣobhya and the rest, according to their number, are calm abiding (śamatha). These two have the selfhood of that which is without inherent existence; suchness itself is called "peace." Into the maṇḍala of suchness, one should draw in all buddhas. The yogin's inhalation and exhalation — that very union is singular. Abiding in wisdom and skillful means, recite the branches of mantra without interruption. In all activities whatsoever, the yogin who knows should recite always — even while sleeping and so forth — that which is beyond the expression of many names and has passed beyond the three times. The recitation prescribed for white cloth and so forth is two hundred and twenty-five. Through the fourth one, well-kindled, combined by the fourfold union, nine hundred: what has been taught as nine hundred arises from the sequence of twenty-four, step by step — and that is twenty-one thousand six hundred. Here, through the secret intention, the subtle yoga has been taught. Although there may be neither reading nor meditation, it is taught to be recitation nonetheless.

Having heard in this way the speech of many kinds that benefits beings, Vajrapāṇi again addressed a question to the Lord of all Tathāgatas: "Whatever, when cultivated, causes all beings of future times to become without conceptual thought — O Blessed One, please explain the supreme essence of the three vajras."

The non-dual union of those very three as suchness — meditate upon that as the subtle vajra. We too shall become yogins who have abandoned the three of swiftness and so forth. If one understands this through self-knowledge and trust, yet suchness — pacification itself — will remain unknown, and the subtle yoga being abandoned as primary, all manner of wrong yogas will be continually relied upon. Speaking of the union of the teacher and uttering that very thing called "subtle" — in future times, that will come to be abandoned. That very suchness settles this with certainty.

Then, having heard the prophecy spoken by Vajrapāṇi, the Lord of all Tathāgatas, from within that meditative absorption (samādhi) called "the essential nature of the dharmadhātu," entered into a state of equanimity. Having gloriously entered into equanimity, he declared this secret mantra: body, speech, and mind are taught as vajra — conferring blessing in a special way. Through the non-dual union of the three vajras, it is to be correctly explained.

The woman of the dharmadhātu itself is to be pleased through wisdom such as this. She is not within, not without, not in both together, not from elsewhere. She is alike to space; she becomes the abode of the peaceful buddha. Therefore what is conceptually distinguished as the domain — the gatheringness — is excretion. The emptiness that is the sense-faculty of wisdom is the pure essential juice of the nature of reality (dharmatā). The blood that is the wisdom of omniscience — that which is comprehensively examined as the nature of phenomena — those very ones are called the divine ones. Non-thing is the offering of poverty. By doing this, the sons and daughters of the buddhas will be pleased in a special way. Whatever is examined by worldly people — those are spoken of exactly as stated. Cultivating the three vajras as inseparable, spread out well on the ground with great precision, with extremely measured beauty — the skilled one draws the lines with a cord. The wise one who examines the maṇḍala: twelve cubits is the measure. It has four sides and four corners. It is to be beautified with stairways. In its center is the wheel — explained first through intention. By way of the reversal of that, those who are worldly conceive here within it. Whoever dwells in the transcendence of the world — that one is empowered through the dhāraṇī. Those who hold to non-thing — the mind is always alike to space. The noble one has utterly transcended the world. Abandoning saṃsāra and the other extreme, neither grasping nor releasing, neither absent nor abiding — the wise one who knows this throughout, supreme among all yogins in awareness: this chapter, the chapter of the mind maṇḍala, is declared to be the yoga of certainty. The intention, the prophecy, is pleasing, and for those whose mind has accepted it, wisdom and method remain just as they are, and they practice accordingly. From the secret intention and prophecy, this is the very fourth chapter of the mind maṇḍala.

The speech of the intention of those ones: he was beseeched to declare the conduct, O Blessed One. Then the sovereign, the Tathāgata, having heard the words of those vajra-holders, declared words with meaning, the sole benefit of beings: "Arising rightly from the non-conceptual aim, the defining characteristic is entry into aversion."

Delusion abides in what is without substance.
Attachment arises from attachment itself —
one who possesses attachment bears that name.
The supreme vehicle is the unsurpassed.
Attachment, aversion, and delusion —
these are rightly taught according to their class.
That accomplishment (siddhi) is achieved through this.
The ignoble class is to be swiftly recognized.

Those who engage in deception are the leisured ones.
Both kinds commit the acts without interval (the five acts of immediate consequence).
One who harbors the intention to kill
will accomplish this, the supreme vehicle,
the unsurpassed Great Vehicle.
A sentient being who has committed great evil
and another who commits acts without interval —
whoever, moreover, reviles the teacher,
even if they strive, will not attain accomplishment.

By trusting in this very thing with faith, swiftly,
by abiding in aversion, one abides.
Whoever, through a mind arisen from calm abiding,
renders the aggregates and other things as substanceless —
that one commits the act of taking life
and abides in the supreme vehicle.

One who teaches dependent origination
as both inner and outer, as twofold —
that one who delights in speaking falsehood
is a yogin who abides where there is no abiding.

Whatever is the gnosis of a fully awakened buddha,
that is to be known as a material substance.
The manner of seizing it
is considered attachment to another's wealth.

Whoever holds that reality is a single substance,
and continuously applies themselves to suchness —
that sentient being who delights in such desiring
becomes supreme in the practice of accomplishment.

Its emptiness is delusion;
its object is known as excrement;
that which is called urine of the sense faculties —
these are the food that consumes peace.

In this supreme doctrine of the Great Vehicle,
these become the vessel for accomplishment.
Those who, in their lives, desire falsehood
and who use excrement and urine as food —

the wisdom of the Perfection of Wisdom,
the dharmakāya, not being a single entity,
the practice of non-dual suchness —
this is called substanceless; practicing the conduct of desire,

whatever is equal to the perfect enjoyment body (saṃbhogakāya),
that itself is considered the sister.
One possessed of the yoga of one's own deity
is to be pleased with the body through the secret mantras.

That girl who uses excrement and urine as food,
possessed of the form of the emanation body (nirmāṇakāya) —
she is to be meditated upon by the practitioner.
Mother, sister, and daughter —
having been pleased by the yogin in this manner,
through the nature of reality of the supreme vehicle,
that vast accomplishment is attained.

Just as the non-conceptual sky
abides pervading all —
this is explained as meditation,
called the samādhi that pervades thought.

That very thing arises in the world
bearing the name of conceptuality's delight.
Through the gnosis of the mother of the buddhas,
one is not soiled by the conduct of desire.
Through a single application of non-conceptual practice,
buddhahood will swiftly be attained.

From one's own vajra united with the lotus, through the perfect union of the two faculties, one meditates on the buddha Vajrasattva born from the drop of one's own seminal essence.

As for the engagement of the recollection of the speech — the five: the vajra, the Dharma, the speech of excellence — whatever their speech may be like, hold to the Dharma knowing oneself likewise to resemble that.

As for the engagement of the recollection of the mind: whatever is the mind of Vajradhara and whatever is the mind of Samantabhadra — oneself likewise becomes to resemble that.

As for the engagement of the recollection of sentient beings: Vajra itself recollects sentient beings; just as conduct is equal to space — oneself likewise becomes to resemble that.

Then Vajrapāṇi entreated the Protector — he who is the singular intent, the chief, the omniscient, the knower of all, the one who has spoken that very samaya:

"You who have become supreme among the non-substantial, who teach the phenomena — why, O Protector, do you teach with words that contradict worldly people?

That which is a single reality of suchness — the buddhas who arise throughout the three times, born from the dharmakāya alone — how is it that the vajra grinds them to powder? And once they have been ground to powder, how can the mind be truly accomplished? Having arisen from the dharmakāya alone, and then having become something called 'superior' — how is it possible to grind to powder the buddhas who arise throughout the three times?

As for the one who grinds to powder: since there is no substantial reality, even the powder itself becomes distinguished. The yogin of buddha-mantra, endowed with inconceivable power — to grind thus to powder is not something other mantra-practitioners can do.

Just as a sentient being grinds sentient beings to powder as sentient beings, and a buddha grinds buddhas to powder — future people will say: 'There is no difference between these two.' People will affirm with certainty: 'A single buddha is not the final end,' and whatever has been taught by the buddha, they will fully abandon.

If one accomplishes the excellent Vehicle and thereby awakens — the supreme excellent vase of intention — through swift union with the Blessed One, will one be born in the realm of Akṣobhya? When sentient beings revolve in saṃsāra, will great aversion then arise? Or, saying 'One must slay with compassion' — mantra-practitioners do not abandon that.

The teachers of times past, those who teach by following now, and the future buddhas who are to be pleased — it is the buddhas who bestow awakening. How are those buddhas with form — Akṣobhya with the vajra — to be destroyed? Those realities of various kinds with meaningful words — how are they to be understood through them?

Through knowing this, the sons of the Victors will become free of conceptual thought regarding the intended meaning. For the sake of future beings, please, Blessed One, explain that very reality."

Then all the Tathāgatas — the Blessed Ones from whom the three vajras arise — gathered their intent once more, and the great Guru, the Blessed One, bowed in homage to the suchness of the vajra samaya, to the one who has awakened completely and who is the foremost guide, and to the sovereign Tathāgata who is the pure lord of body, speech, and mind, and made this request: "O Blessed One, the essence mantra that gathers all together, the truly secret three vajras, the supreme and excellent mode of great attainment — may the Blessed One explain that suchness. May the primordial wisdom of all the Tathāgatas, the attainment of body, speech, and mind, be granted. The single speech in its many aspects of conceptual appearance — the Tathāgata is the true and gathered meaning of this."

Then the sovereign Blessed One, lord of all the afflictions' ocean, with blazing fierce wide eyes, spoke these words: "As for the three vajras of body and the rest — to meditate on body, speech, and mind is to be equanimous and non-conceptual, without focus, and without abode."

Then the Protector, the Blessed One — the Tathāgata of intrinsically pure nature — entered into the great heroic meditative absorption called the vajra of the perfection and mantra mode, and spoke to all the Tathāgatas: "Since the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, and all the Tathāgatas here — the Protector Akṣobhya and the many other Tathāgatas — together with their awareness numbering ten million hundreds of thousands, sport at bringing benefit to sentient beings and teach the supreme among those in the supreme world-realms of the ten directions, enjoying and delighting in the five desirable qualities, living among them and partaking of them fully — yet for those who are diligent in the conduct of mantra practice, alas! O heroes, Tathāgatas — thinking that for these ones the supreme mode of secret mantra conduct, that which has the nature of the selflessness of phenomena, is already complete — one does not sustain them with a caring eye. Therefore this is taught in order to truly bless those engaged in the conduct and discipline of secret mantra, through the careful analysis of this meaning."

Then Vajrapāṇi made this request to the supreme Tathāgata, the Sage: "O Protector, as for those Tathāgatas — when they have become ordinary beings, without intrinsic existence and without a focus, having become equal to the expanse of space, and endowed with the five desirable qualities — how do they engage in enjoyment? All their activities for the benefit of sentient beings in the worlds of the ten directions, and the complete assemblies there — all these activities for beings' benefit are taught through desire. O Protector, these desirable qualities are said to arise as the six such as generosity and so on. All of these become that very suchness: they are meditative equipoise itself in the perfections. Thus from the arising of the perfections, endowed with the five desirable qualities, all activities for the benefit of beings are likewise performed through these five desires. Through these, this Protector — Akṣobhya and the other Victors — engage in enjoyment and delight, and likewise carry out benefit for beings; even what contradicts the worldly is performed in just the same way. Through these, the supreme pure vajra words will be understood. The awareness that is worthy of veneration — one asserts it as definitively endowed with certainty. Whatever makes distinctions of existence and non-existence and so forth will become dual and will come to bear characteristics. The Dharma taught in the ten directions as the path — that which in the world designates the five aggregates, the elements, and the rest — is called by such names as form and so on. All of these aspects together constitute the activities of the śrāvaka vehicle."

That very same is the vehicle of conditions, and this — the teaching of those words — abides in the vajra vehicle, grasping sound just as it is, in its true nature. In the Perfection of Wisdom, the three modes of definitive arising — such as the essential nature of the dharmakāya and so forth — the yogin of boundless space: by means of practice that abides in groundless abiding, he plays within the heart of beings, the protector abiding in singular peace, having abandoned the other side of saṃsāra. That which arises truly from mantra, variously enumerated as Akṣobhya and the rest — because it abides in the domain of that which is without substance, the practitioners will not perceive it. Through the practice of the conditions of the secret mantras, they shall behold it as the saṃbhogakāya. Since those who have not sought the meaning of this secret teaching spoken by the Blessed One — arising from the manifold fabrications of conceptuality — will not come to know the nature of reality through it, some people in a future time, not knowing what was spoken, will speak. In order to guide worldly beings, please teach what has become secret.

Then the glorious Blessed One, who is manifold, spoke to the greatly renowned Vajrapāṇi, who had understood the intended meaning: Son, your great wisdom is excellent. Whoever knows this will ensure that people in the future will be free from conceptual elaboration. You have gathered the manifold meanings into a single phrase and explained them rightly.

Then the holy Blessed One — the guru, omniscient, the knower of all — in order to care for those noble beings with his gaze, declared these samaya words: this secret of the three vajras, the supreme of all Tathāgatas, the supreme abiding place of the secret of the vajra of body, speech, and mind, called "the single invocation, the essence of all mantras," the single secret of all Tathāgatas, proclaimed as the supreme of the supreme — the true understanding of body, speech, and mind, along with the samaya as its focus; the body, speech, and mind of the vajra-holder, together with the samaya as their focus; the body, speech, and mind of the Dharma-holder, together with the samaya as their focus — all this is so likewise.

The definitive expression in words arising from the very vajra of body, speech, and mind is gathered truly into mantra: the single form of the manifold speech. The syllable HŪṂ was spoken as one; then the syllable OṂ was spoken; the protector likewise uttered the syllable ĀḤ; SVĀ is likewise the fourth; and HĀ has become the god of desire. So it is with the five buddhas thus: HŪṂ, OṂ, ĀḤ, SVĀ, and HĀ. By the mere utterance of these, all the buddhas together with their sons trembled greatly and fell into a swoon. They remained in the practice of non-duality, recalling Vajrasattva. In the peaceful practice, as the Dharma of singular unity, one non-conceptual dharma arose.

Then the Blessed One — the sovereign of all Tathāgatas — for the purpose of the blessing-practice thereof, declared these samaya words: "Abiding in the center of the expanse of space, what is called 'the arranged maṇḍala' — by means of the practice of three in each case — one should meditate with the practice of meditation. Even examining the three distinctions as one, the arrangement itself is not made clear.

Meditate upon the syllable HŪṂ abiding in the center of the moon, as the single form of one's own body. One should contemplate the blazing radiance of the vajra, radiating outward in all directions. Having cultivated the body, speech, and mind of the buddhas upon one's heart through that syllable, in that very glorious instant one becomes the vajra-holder of the three vajras — Vajrasattva, the great sovereign lord — and arises as the supremely excellent great one. By one's own mantra it is to be generated; this is the sacred ritual of mantra. The essential mantra of one's own vajra, the heart of one's own nature, is called 'the gathered vajra.' It arises from the manner of one's own mantra."

One should meditate upon it in the form of a being. By the single application of the fourfold joining, one places it in the four pure abodes. With three colors and a vision of three faces, it is called "meditation upon the three-in-one suchness." Thus spoke the supreme Blessed One — the foremost sovereign of the sky — through the principal vajra-pledge.

Then the supreme Tathāgata, the Great Sage of peaceful nature, was again petitioned on this secret by Vajrapāṇi, the chief one: "Hūṃ, Oṃ, Āḥ, Svāhā." And when that was declared, all the buddhas together with their sons trembled greatly and fell into swoon. Recollecting Vajrasattva, the Great Sage, just as he is here, and the protector in the future — what was declared non-conceptually — the solitary being, the buddhas equal in number to the sky, and the bodhisattvas of great power: that they trembled and swooned is something childish ones cannot comprehend.

Teaching it in counts of hundreds of thousands and tens of millions, and teaching it through manifold teachers, still there was no trembling and swooning on that occasion. In the future too it shall arise. Those who are arrogant and unskilled, having received the oral transmission from a master, will claim the signs as their own. They do not know this secret characteristic of what was said as the meaning of mantra, and so they broadcast unpleasant sounds about others who are skilled in the very meaning of mantra. If they see even the slightest confusion, they will break into laughter. Such a person, comparable to a venomous thing one touches, will never come to peace.

Those mantra practitioners whose minds are like precious jewels, working for the benefit of beings — even in the future time, they will attain the supreme accomplishment.

In order to gather all of these together, Vajrapāṇi, reflecting upon the intent to apply the subsequent utterance in an instant for the sake of accomplishment, humbly requested of the Blessed One: "Please expound this."

Then the Teacher, the Blessed One, for the benefit of beings, spoke to Vajrapāṇi, joining perfectly the joining, and examining the object of meditation: the very sovereign of the senses, the Sun-Sage. Those abiding in the ten directions, called "buddhas together with their sons" — all those Tathāgatas — arose from the body possessed of aggregates and the like in equal number. From the joining applied to the condition of emptiness, one desires to become a buddha together with one's sons. That mantra, that mantra-practitioner, that buddha — that very self, together with one's sons, joins truly. From the joining equal to peace and the rest, the bliss of peace then commences. When the self-nature is pure by its own nature and rendered stainless in nature, in the various modes of gradual, swift, and stationary, that very one dwells in the pure single one. Even through the lowly-minded and the gradual and so forth, that very joining abides in purity. Then, when the faculties have ceased, when there is only the single bliss of peace — the joining that is pure by its own nature — the Dharma of that is for the one who sees.

Then the mantra of perfect enjoyment, arising from the gentleness of all buddhas — because it arose and was returned — the five secret mantras of the syllable Hūṃ: at that time there is great trembling from the heart, and all the faculties fall into swoon. Whatever the limbs of the aggregates and so forth — all those buddhas together with their sons — through the equal number of just that, the body of the yogin trembles greatly. The words such as "nearby" and "signified" and the rest — whatever was explained regarding the aggregates and the like — having become buddhas together with their sons, thereby one trembles and swoons.

Entering meditative absorption upon those two, all the buddhas together with their sons, recollecting Vajrasattva himself — that mantra arose from the very self in actuality. This is here set forth as the supreme teaching. The wondrous secret vajra, dwelling at the center of the subtle heart: one should contemplate the maṇḍala in full. At its center, one should contemplate the supreme state of peace. That great mass of light called Hūṃ — the vajra into which it is fully transformed — this is the supreme five-pointed one. The yogin should always meditate upon it. Through the joining of the vajra-hook, one should contemplate the three vajras.

Sealed within its heart center, one should ardently rouse the deities. This is the supreme accomplishment of all the vajras of all the buddhas. The wheel, the lotus family, and so forth — this music is the sacred one. Having meditated on the vajra-hook, one is roused within the heart center. By practicing this supreme mode of the vajra for seven days, one fully accomplishes the secret of the wisdom-vajra holder and the three vajras.

Whatever buddha is found among the aggregates and so forth — that is the view of non-existence. Through the supreme secret mantra of that very suchness, one sees the ripening of terror. These people who are terrified: through the non-dual joining of a single mantra, seeing brings terror, yet grants the supreme. These are the ones who have examined this very suchness. From the single practice of peace alone, the body of the mantra becoming fully complete, there arises joy and delight in the mind, and vast siddhis (spiritual accomplishments) are bestowed.

Buddhas examined through the aggregates, and likewise bodhisattvas — through accomplishing the meaning of the mantra-practice, having caused the deluded to pass beyond, their life-force is exhausted there. From the single joining of peace itself, this is the supreme transcending of the world. What is reversed from that is the worldly.

Then the sovereign, the Blessed One, Teacher of the three worlds, supreme in the three worlds, with the vajra as chief, proclaimed these words: Whoever is described as a person of the mantra, the form of wisdom of the three vajras — that one always meditates on the oneness of the joining of the two faculties. The meditation of the lord of mantras, in which there is no apprehension of a self or of sentient beings, is the excellent joining of the two faculties, the examination of all phenomena. This is the supreme meditation on the samaya (sacred commitment) of the mantra of all the buddhas.

Joining one's own form as vajra correctly, and meditating on the sovereignty of the awareness-consort as oneself — that non-dual attachment to non-duality is the view, the samaya, the maṇḍala. Applied to what is called the Secret, that very suchness is the identity of equality. Then the Teacher, the Blessed One, proclaimed the secret intention of the supreme empowerment of all, the accomplishment of the indivisible three vajras. Through the joining of limitless space, not apprehending the self-nature of the aggregates, the progression through the dharmadhātu and so on — that woman becomes vast. Through this supreme practice, what is called the woman of the destinies is the very suchness, the secret meaning of the secret mantra. Through that, one practices by means of the secret.

There, the delightful buddhas — innumerable tens of millions who hold the vajra — declared there that the supreme awareness-holders of mantra are jubilant. Thus it was proclaimed by the Blessed One, the chief. The samaya of the bodhisattva is here the supreme guru of the Teacher. This is taught here for the worldly, reversing the transcendent. Through this, sky-like awakening is thoroughly attained. The ocean of qualities of the awakening mind — that supreme Vajrasattva attains. Whoever has a mind that is faithful toward the secret intention, prophecy, and secret — that one earnestly and correctly guards the secret and always acts well.

From the secret of the three vajras, and likewise of all the Tathāgatas — this chapter, called Rousing That Very Suchness, is proclaimed to be the tenth.

Then the Teacher, the Blessed One, having entered meditative absorption with the supreme offering of the three supreme vajras — the single supreme of all the Tathāgatas, the supreme called the Twenty-Five — declared this chapter entitled The Mantra-Awareness Person of the Vajra of All the Tathāgatas.

That supreme mantra is the great mudrā (seal). One meditates on the wisdom of the three vajras. Through the vajra wisdoms, in order to attain realization and to write it, Brahmā and the others are correctly explained. The sacred essence of wisdom — the three vajras and so forth as taught — is what brings attainment of the body-vajra. The letter A paired together is the speech-vajra.

It is that which brings attainment of selflessness and awakening. HŪṂ is the very limit of the three vajras — it is that which brings attainment of vajra-body, vajra-speech, and vajra-mind. That is thoroughly explained as the abiding-place of entering and so forth, together with arising. This becomes the form of the person who bears the name of the Twenty-Five and so on. Therefore, each and every one is itself the triad — it becomes the very vajra and the secret mantra.

From the eighth seed-syllable that is beyond speech, that becomes a hundredfold from space. From that single breath of the half-hundred, from it certainly the twenty-five arise. When they arise as the person, there is one bearing the name "Ten-Throated," and there arises delight in the slaying of sound-proclaiming ones. Then Brahmā becomes one alone — Brahmā, Viṣṇu, and the Fierce One — explained in sequence as "entering" and so forth. These, fearless, are that single one. That is peace — the very nature of non-substantiality. From that too there arises correctly the supreme noble saṃbhogakāya. Then from the emanation of the single mantra there comes the activity for the benefit of beings. The boundaries, the sense bases, the wealth-deity, the pair of moons, the Vedas — these and others transformed in enumeration abide as the mantra-awareness person.

Taking up one from among these, one should display the buddha's music. Vajrasattva, that very one, is to be conceived at the center as the space-vajra. What is called "adorned with the maṇḍala" — the syllable HŪṂ is to be meditated upon there. The Vajra-Cloud, rousing emanation — the bodies of the six Tathāgatas — the yogi abiding at the center of that, one who possesses the nature of meditation, should meditate upon them.

The crown of all mantras, the Secret Mantra-Awareness Person — becoming the sole sovereign — is the accomplisher who fulfills the aim of mantra-practice. The single wheel of Dharma, the supreme wheel, victorious and wondrously glorious, the great crown — body and complete victory likewise — is thoroughly explained as supremely excellent activity.

Like the non-duality of application and activity, in application alone — this one: BHŪṂ is taught to be the crown. This essence of the wisdom-vajra bears the name of the single conjunction of time. Endowed with equality with the dharmadhātu of space, the mantra-awareness person bestows attainment. At the center of the vajra-maṇḍala, meditate on OṂ there. At the center of the subtle maṇḍala, meditate fully on the syllable ĀḤ. In the application of the Dharma-maṇḍala as well, meditate on the syllable HŪṂ there. The syllable HRŪṂ, bearing the name of the abode — the arising of the three vajras is to be meditated upon. This very samaya of the secret was declared as the arising of the three vajras.

The essence — the three vajras of the buddhas of the three times — is the supreme that brings delight. Body, speech, and mind from beginning to end are equal in number to OṂ ĀḤ HŪṂ. The supreme body of the buddha, the path of speech, and the primordial wisdom of mind — these are to be made resounding. Each and every one is correctly explained as the act of utterance itself.

The application of the mantra-awareness person, moreover, bears the name of being the very nature of the cause. This is the supreme mode of awakening — the thorough accomplishment of the awakening of buddhahood. The vajra-wisdom is emanation — it is that which brings attainment of the fruit of buddhahood. What is proclaimed as the mantra-awareness is this: the person of the buddha. The three vajras inseparably equal — through meditation on completion and so forth — the vajra of all Tathāgatas: those three bestow the wisdom-vajra.

What is called the Great Glorious Blessing is the samaya that has become samādhi. In fitting seclusion, on appropriate seats — the four applications are correctly applied to the mantra-awareness person. One possessed of discipline undertakes them throughout. Skillful means and wisdom, sun and moon, the half of each — examine them well in a single session. By that half, by the lunar half, the three vajras are certainly accomplished.

The very nature that transcends the world, and yet, from the worldly side, utterly inverted — by joining the three vajras, siddhis are bestowed. That they will be accomplished, there is no doubt.

In the center of the sky-vajra abiding, one should contemplate the subtle maṇḍala. At the time of one's own mantra-samaya, meditate on the arising of the syllable OṂ.

What is the reasoning of the four applications? It is a thorough examination of the essence. The tip of the nose and the very root, the crown and its edge — the abode of Tuṣita. They become the substance of one and three. Those of the pure abode, the city-dwellers, named by the half of the three — the three vajras are the distinction of lineage.

Through the application at the tip of the nose, one possessed of the discipline of joining the three vajras: Māmakī, White-Robed Pāṇḍaravāsinī, Tārā, and the eye of the buddha — a great cloud of five rays of light — Vairocana, the abode of non-duality. From that vajra-Vairocana, one will receive the vast siddhi.

Like the light of a buddha's body — in that very instant the yogin is accomplished. By practicing the five wisdoms, one abides for three vajra-aeons. The three vajras are the wisdom of the past, the future, and the present arising. What is examined here as an aeon — that itself is what is called innumerable.

This very secret of the body is taught in the sequence that transcends the world. From the bliss of peace, that which is to be known — from that one application of self-experience, this supreme secret of the body is taught by all the Tathāgatas. The samādhi of the arrangement of vajra-rays of light is perfectly proclaimed.

A and U are in the center of the palate, named at the top of the shank. I and CA are on the left hand; U and MU, examine them on the right. RI ZU — place these in the secret. LI TI on the other leg. One maṇḍala is that very thing — victorious among the five maṇḍalas.

HA is born of the body, the uṣṇīṣa; CUT — the three that cut. The four of the four qualities: the fifth among the five becomes that. The sky-vajra joined with one half — that becomes the five rays of light. This very great awareness (mahāvidyā) is proclaimed entirely as CHA.

The self-endowed lord of faculties, possessing time — one single embodied form of its own joining. A becomes the means; the application is the two faculties as one.

Contemplating the supreme dharma-maṇḍala with the sky-vajra abiding at the center, having contemplated the mantra-born person, ĀḤ is placed on the path of speech.

The great vajras adorned entirely by the five colors — by meditating supremely on Īśvara, the lord of the world, one should meditate on the samaya-wisdom. The samaya of speech is taught as elaboration: the all-pervading lord, inexhaustible and permanent, ultimate and without beginning or end — this is called the peace of nirvāṇa. Through the Dharma speech, the master of yoga becomes like vajra-Dharma. By practicing with the five wisdoms, one abides for the three vajra-aeons. This very secret of speech is taught by all the Tathāgatas. What is called the arising of samaya is fully proclaimed as samādhi.

Supremely abiding at the center of the sky-vajra, the noble vajra-maṇḍala — for those mantra-born persons, HŪṂ is placed to completion in the mind. Endowed with that very great samaya, meditate on the five colors. The abode of all vajras and Victors — let it be accomplished by wisdom-vajras.

The Teacher, like the vajra-mind — the ocean of perfect wisdom-qualities. By practicing with the five wisdoms, one abides for three vajra-aeons.

Palate, shank, and then lips, crown — named as the ground: the secret of the mind of the three vajras is one. That has entered into HŪṂ.

The wisdom of all the Tathāgatas is called the samaya-vajra. The vajra-wisdom arises from the one — it is taught as the secret of samādhi.

Contemplating the great vajra at the center of the wisdom-maṇḍala, and visualizing KHĀ throughout the entire body — vajra-wisdom will arise.

KHĀ itself illuminates the meaning of non-existence of things, and by KHĀ alone everything is explained. Through the condition of wind and total union, one comes to pass into KHĀ as well.

Buddhas and bodhisattvas, again and again making offerings, and abiding in the samaya of the three vajras — this is the non-dual, spontaneously present essential nature. The engagement of a buddha with suchness abides perfectly and distinctly. The great samaya of the space-vajra: phenomena are not seen by it. The samaya of all buddhas is non-duality and non-appearance. When one reaches the state of non-existence of things, it is not seen even by a buddha.

These three vajras of non-appearance arise truly from body, speech, and mind. The meditative absorption that is correctly taught as what is called "the arranged garland" thereof — this is the essential nature of Dharma unmixed with anything else, the great light endowed with form. One should contemplate the self-mantra person dwelling at the center of the vajra maṇḍala.

HŪṂ is taught as the four vajras, beautiful in the heart. Through constant practice of union, one should meditate upon it as radiating rays of light.

The samaya-vajra of the enjoyment of body, speech, and mind is gentle. If one fully abides on the ten grounds, then at that time the intention is thoroughly born. This wisdom itself is the moon. The meditative absorption correctly taught is called "that which possesses appearance," belonging to the bodhisattva who bears the vajra.

Contemplating HRĪḤ blazing with fine light, dwelling at the center of the expanse of space, and with the supreme weapon of the vajra, one becomes like a vajra-form. This is the union that is like the vajra-space, having become the place of the arranged samaya. The arising of HRĪḤ is correctly taught as the non-appearing samaya-absorption.

Correctly taught as the samaya in which a buddha dwells in superknowledge — this, comparable to the five superknowledges, causes one to become a supreme person. Accomplishing the supreme superknowledge of a buddha is taught as what is holy. The supreme among practices for accomplishing the great accomplishment of all accomplishments thereof: one should contemplate the noble buddha maṇḍala dwelling at the center of the expanse of space.

Having meditated upon the supreme intent of the buddhas, one should meditate upon the syllable OṂ. This secret of the three vajras — the syllable OṂ is the inexhaustible foremost. By practicing this without separation, through meditation one becomes like Akṣobhya. The supreme samaya of Akṣobhya: that is correctly taught as the meditative absorption of the vajra of awakening embodied — and that is a holy person.

From holding body, speech, and mind as one-pointed, equal to Akṣobhya, in every world-realm one who bears the vajra will be worshipped.

Contemplating the buddha maṇḍala adorned at the center of the expanse of space, meditating thoroughly on the space-vajra and then meditating on the vajra — by meditating on the three vajra-samayas, one becomes brilliant and radiant like the splendor of the Three Jewels. Equal to the splendor of the Three Jewels, like the splendor of the Blessed One, the Three Jewels — through the vajra of the union of body, speech, and mind, one reaches the secret place of primordial wisdom.

Born from immeasurable absorption, one becomes the holder of vajra-wisdom. What is called "the enjoyment-samaya" — the precious vajra arises as one. Remaining in the secret intent of those very three, the precious vajra establishes itself there.

Contemplating the buddha maṇḍala dwelling at the center of the expanse of space, meditating fully on the Lord of the World, one should meditate there on the Dharma-OṂ. At that time too, the Dharma-OṂ, through the equal union of the three vajras, is like the immeasurable vajra — the Blessed One who is the immeasurable vajra-holder. Through the vajra of body, speech, and mind, one becomes like Amitāyus.

Contemplating the noble buddha maṇḍala dwelling at the center of the expanse of space, meditating on all the enjoyments of the entire realm of sentient beings, one should meditate on the buddha maṇḍala dwelling at the center of the expanse of space. That is explained as Vairocana-vajra. One should meditate fully on those rays of light, and on the syllable OṂ of the Victorious One. One should meditate fully on that great light. Then, from the three vajras, one becomes like Vairocana. So declared the Blessed One, the Protector: that is what it means to possess the Vairocana-vajra. From the non-dual union of the three vajras arises the light resembling Vairocana. That very thing is wisdom, the perfection of awakening. The accomplisher of the three vajra bodies — through body, speech, and mind — perfects awakening.
