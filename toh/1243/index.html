About this text

The Means of Accomplishment Called Hevajra, known in Sanskrit as the Hevajra Nāma Sādhana and in Tibetan as Kyé Dorjé zhé jawé drubpé tap, is a tantric ritual and meditation manual, or sādhana, devoted to the deity Hevajra. Opening with homage to the eight-faced, sixteen-armed Hevajra, embraced by Nairātmyā and encircled by Gaurī and the other goddesses, it guides the initiated yogin through preliminary protections, the visualization of the maṇḍala and its deities, empowerment, offerings and praises, mantra recitation, the dissolution into the Great Seal, a torma offering to the worldly gods, and the inner fire offering performed while eating. Its colophon attributes it to the Indian master Avadhūtīpa, also called Advayavajra. It is Toh 1243 in the Tengyur, where it belongs to the commentaries on the tantras, among the many practice texts connected with the Hevajra Tantra.

Translation

In the language of India: hevajra nāma sādhanaṃ. In the language of Tibet: The Means of Accomplishment Called Hevajra.

Homage to Hevajra.

Eight-faced and sixteen-armed,
born from hūṃ, encircled by Gaurī and the rest,
pressing down the four māras with his feet,
embraced from behind by Nairātmyā,
possessed of twenty-four eyes,
wearing a garland of fifty fresh skulls,
blue, and adorned with the five mudrās:
bowing to him, I shall explain the sādhana.

Now first of all, the yogin performs such acts as washing the face, generates the resolve for the sake of all sentient beings, and with oṃ rakṣa rakṣa hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ svāhā performs the protection of the place and so on. Having entered the maṇḍala taught in the glorious Hevajra, he has received empowerment in the proper sequence, the authorization of the true command, and the utterly pure one; he has gained the unerring wisdom of reality and has found the signs. Seated on a comfortable seat marked with a crossed vajra (viśvavajra) in a pleasing place such as a charnel ground, having been shown the pledges of the five nectars and the rest, relying on no one whatever, and possessed of wisdom free of doubt, he should be endowed with the pride of glorious Hevajra.

Then, in one's own heart, upon a lotus, from a melted raṃ there is a sun disc, and upon it one visualizes a blue hūṃ. Then five rays of light stream out from the syllable hūṃ, illuminating the three realms, and are drawn back again. In the sky in front, one imagines the Blessed One, sixteen-armed, eight-faced and three-eyed, embracing his own consort, his yellow hair bristling upward, adorned with the five mudrās, surrounded by eight goddesses, with colors and hand emblems as will be described below: a form that emanates and gathers back.

In front of him is black Gaurī, holding in her hand the moon of bodhicitta. On the right is red Caurī, holding the sun, which is called the self-arisen flower. Behind is yellow Vetālī, holding a bowl of vajra water. In the north is green Ghasmarī, holding the secret medicine. In the northeast is blue Pukkasī, holding various kinds of meat. In the southeast is white Śavarī, bearing liquor. In the southwest is slightly reddish Caṇḍālī, beating a small drum. In the northwest is smoke-colored Ḍombinī, embracing his very body.

Having thus clearly visualized the Blessed One surrounded by the yoginīs, one afterwards dissolves the rays into one's own seed syllable. Again rays stream out from one's own seed syllable, and one makes offering to the Blessed One with the special offerings: outer, inner, secret, and of reality. Presenting to him flowers, incense, food offerings, the seven precious things and so on is the outer offering. The eight goddesses presenting the five nectars, the five meats and so on is the secret offering. Experiencing, through the instruction of the true guru, the bliss that is nondual with Nairātmyā is the offering of reality.

Having made these utterly pure offerings in this way, one prostrates with faith of body, speech and mind, and in their presence one confesses misdeeds and so forth:

All the sinful deeds that I, born of mistaken conceptual thought, have committed from beginningless saṃsāra, my rejoicing in what others have done, and whatever I have caused to be done even in small measure: all of these I confess, and henceforth I shall not do them again. I rejoice in all the roots of virtue of all ordinary beings, gods, śrāvakas, pratyekabuddhas, bodhisattvas, and perfectly complete buddhas. I dedicate all merit entirely to unsurpassed, perfectly complete awakening.

Then: Until I reach the essence of awakening, I go for refuge to the Buddha, glorious Hevajra, who through the nature of great bliss is the very self of the three realms; to the Dharma, whose nature is the mantras, mudrās, maṇḍalas and so on that he taught; and to the assembly of yoginīs who abide in the wheel of his maṇḍala.

One should generate great bliss, the true awakening mind, emptiness endowed with the supreme of all aspects, whose nature is to be the cause of acting for the benefit of all sentient beings. Thus I shall rely on the vajra path of the unsurpassed awakening mind. One should urge them to turn the wheel of Dharma and request them not to pass into nirvāṇa.

Then one should cultivate great love toward all sentient beings as toward an only child, its very essence being supreme bliss; great compassion, which, seeing all sentient beings without refuge, sunk in the ocean of suffering and of all the conceptions that are the cause of suffering, wishes to free them; joy, which sees them as inseparable from great bliss; and equanimity, characterized as the path turned away from the eight worldly concerns.

Thus, with unsurpassed wisdom, whose characteristic is fully to discern all phenomena, one should know clearly that all phenomena, being merely arisen from dependent origination, are by nature unborn from the very beginning. Having understood the phenomenon whose nature is the vajra of the wisdom of emptiness, in which the bliss of all things without exception is gathered into the essence of reality, one recites mentally the mantra oṃ śūnyatā jñāna vajra svabhāva ātmako'haṃ, and through the instruction of the true guru one abides in the nature that is unborn and unceasing, not remaining even for an instant. That itself is luminosity.

It abides in utter bliss;
all phenomena are taught to be that alone.
That perfection of wisdom
is supreme in vanquishing the māras.

Then, through the force of one's former aspiration, one rises from the samādhi of emptiness and luminosity. In the sky, upon a sun disc arisen from raṃ, a blue hūṃ melts into light, and from it one visualizes the five rays of a crossed vajra. One sends forth, as many as one wishes, masses of fiercely blazing rays like the fire of the Avīci hell, and one should imagine a vajra fence in the directions, a vajra tent above, and below a vajra ground reaching down to the hells. Within it, upon a sun disc, is a crossed vajra; rays radiate from it, and one makes the enclosure firm with vajra walls in the directions and intermediate directions and so on. In its center one imagines a yellow bhrūṃ.

From the melted bhrūṃ comes a wheel of ten spokes, yellow in color, spinning swiftly and moving nowhere else. In its center is a crossed vajra; upon that a variegated lotus; and upon that one imagines a sun disc. Upon that, in the middle of a moon and sun joined face to face, one beholds the syllable hūṃ. Through the sequence of the five wisdoms, beginning with the mirror-like wisdom, one imagines the Blessed One Hūṃkāra.

Born from hūṃ, his body is the color of indranīla sapphire, adorned with various green ornaments; he wears a jeweled crown and presses down Bhairava and black Kālarātrī; with his two hands holding vajra and bell he embraces his own consort; and he stands with one leg outstretched and the other bent. So one should imagine him.

Then, following the instruction of the true master, one should meditate on the ten wrathful ones through the application of vajra and skull cup. In the four directions, beginning with the east, are Yamāntaka, Prajñāntaka, Padmāntaka and Vighnāntaka; these four are black, white, red and blue in that order. In their hands they hold a vajra hammer, a white club, a red lotus, and a black vajra. They too are stout and short, with hanging bellies and wrathful mien, adorned with eight clusters of snakes, their yellow hair bristling upward, their eyes red and round, their heads adorned with the four, Vairocana, Akṣobhya, Amitābha and Amoghasiddhi, and they wear skirts of tiger skin.

Likewise in the four intermediate directions, the southeast, the southwest, the northwest and the northeast, are Ṭakkirāja, Nīladaṇḍa, Mahābala and Acalanātha. They too are black in color, and in their hands they hold a hook, a staff, a trident and a sword. Ṭakkirāja and Acalanātha both wear fine garments, are adorned with jewels and the like, and have jeweled crowns. The staff-bearer Nīladaṇḍa and Mahābala both resemble Yamāntaka and are extremely terrifying. Below is Sumbharāja, holding a black hammer in his hand, his head adorned with jewels. On the upper spoke, moreover, is Uṣṇīṣacakravartin, yellow, his head adorned with jewels, holding a yellow wheel, his head adorned with Vairocana. The five beginning with Ṭakkirāja have their heads adorned with Akṣobhya.

These are the ten wrathful ones. They stand with legs spread upon sun discs on variegated lotuses, their left hands raising the threatening forefinger at the heart, and they hold snake nooses.

Having made the protection wheel in this way, in its center, from the melted Vajrahūṃkāra, one should imagine the great element, the great space, the triangular source of phenomena, whose nature is the great Vajradhara: white like the autumn moon, narrow at the base and wide at the mouth, three-cornered, with protruding corners. So one should imagine it.

In its center is a variegated lotus, and upon it a great crossed vajra with twelve prongs: the three eastern prongs are white, the three southern yellow, the three western red, the three northern green, and the center blue. So one should imagine it. Upon it, arisen from yaṃ, is the wind maṇḍala, shaped like a bow, blue, marked with two fluttering banners. Upon that, from raṃ, is the fire maṇḍala, triangular and red, marked with raṃ. Upon that, arisen from vaṃ, is the water maṇḍala, white and round, marked with vaṃ. Upon that, arisen from laṃ, is the earth maṇḍala, square and yellow, marked at its corners with three-pronged vajras.

In its center, upon the crossed vajra, arisen from the melting of the four great elements, which are sentient beings born of dependent origination, is the celestial mansion, the great gathering of utterly pure buddha-fields, whose nature is Vairocana. The mansion, whose nature is the four various jewels, is square, with four doors, adorned with eight pillars, four-cornered, and beautified by four porticoes.

There is a yellow goddess playing a lute, a red goddess blowing a flute, a smoke-colored goddess beating a ḍamaru, a white goddess beating a great drum, a yellow goddess holding a garland of flowers, a red goddess full of grace, the goddess of song, reddish-yellow in color, the goddess of dance, multicolored, the white goddess of flowers, the black goddess of incense, the red goddess of lamps, the yellow goddess of scented water, the white goddess of form, the red goddess of taste, the green goddess of touch, and the goddess of phenomena. These sixteen bear the half-moon and drop, are accomplished from their own seed syllables, and abide with eye and form distinct. The mansion is adorned with the offering goddesses at the sides of the doors, beginning at the base; thus they stand two by two, in order, circling to the right.

It is beautifully adorned with nets and half-nets, parapets, garlands of flowers, canopies, mirrors, yak-tail whisks and the like; it shines with the light of vajra garlands, with many buddhas radiating forth and so on; bells jingle at the tips of various fluttering streamers; and it is adorned with the five qualities of sense pleasure. One should imagine this delightful celestial mansion encircled by the eight charnel grounds.

Of these, the great charnel ground of the east is called Caṇḍogra. In its śirīṣa tree is a god with an elephant's head, yellow. There are Śakra, yellow, and the nāga Vāsuki, yellow, and the cloud called Thundering, which is blue.

The great charnel ground of the southeast is called Aṭṭahāsa. In its karañja tree is a god with a goat's face. There are Agni, the fire god, red, and there the nāga Mahāpadma, green, and the cloud called Massing, red in color.

The charnel ground of the south is called Terrible Host. In the tree called āmra is a god with a buffalo's head. There are Yama, black, and there the nāga Padma, white, and the cloud called Swirling, yellow in color.

The charnel ground of Nairṛti, in the southwest, is called Terrifying. In its pakaha tree is a god with a goat's face. There are Nairṛti, black, the nāga Ananta, gray, and the cloud Full, yellow.

The charnel ground of the west is called Blazing Mass. Its tree is the aśoka, and in it is a god with the head of a makara, yellow. There are Varuṇa, white, and there the nāga Karkoṭa, red, and the cloud Fierce, white.

The charnel ground of the northwest is called Dark and Terrifying. In its arjuna tree is a god with a deer's face, white. There are the wind god, pale blue, and there the nāga Kulika, multicolored, and the cloud Raining, green.

The charnel ground of the north is the Dreadful Thicket. In its tree of awakening is a god with a human face. There are the yakṣa, yellow, and there the nāga Takṣaka, black, and the cloud Murky, white.

The charnel ground of Īśāna, in the northeast, is called Lakṣmīvana. Its tree is the bodhivṛkṣa, and in it is a god with a bull's face. There are the Great God (Śiva), white, the nāga Śaṅkhapāla, yellow, and the cloud Caṇḍogra, multicolored.

In the great charnel grounds one should behold stūpas. Thus one should imagine them. The eight beginning with Śakra appear from the waist up; with their left hands they hold skull cups and with their right they hold tormas. Thus there are eight great trees; the others are various trees beyond measure.

There are crows, owls, white birds, dritas, dogs, wolves and she-wolves, hawks and female hawks, lions, boars, tigers and other terrors; all kinds of snakes and pythons; fragments, and the bha ma gar hi bha, causing utter terror; skeletons, impaling stakes, hanging corpses, beheaded trunks, skulls, knees, headless bodies, hands, heads, and terrors of every kind. Many gods, such as those possessed of accomplishment, and gathering hosts of yoginīs whose very nature is the pledge, along with yakṣas, rākṣasas, pretas, and takers of life, roam and dwell there. Such is the great charnel ground, delightful to oneself, displaying various miracles and resounding with a great clamor.

In its center, upon a red lotus of eight petals, one beholds the four fearsome māras lying on their backs: the māra of the aggregates, Brahmā; the māra of the afflictions, Viṣṇu; the māra of the son of the gods, Śakra; and the māra of the lord of death, Maheśvara (Śiva). One should imagine their hearts pressed down by suns. In the four directions beginning with the east, Brahmā and the others are pressed down. In the four intermediate directions beginning with the northeast are Yama, a yakṣa, an asura, and a rākṣasa; the hearts of these are pressed down by moons. All of these one imagines after the mansion has been accomplished.

There, from the sixteen vowels of the āli series, doubled, comes the moon disc, whose nature is the form of wisdom: this is the mirror-like wisdom. Upon it are the thirty-four consonants of the kāli series, with ḍha ḍa dha da ya la, so that with these six they are forty. From these, doubled, comes the sun disc, whose nature is method: this is the wisdom of equality. In the middle of the two joined together, hūṃ and aṃ melt into light, and from this comes a curved knife joined with a skull cup, marked with their own seed syllables: this is the discriminating wisdom. Rays stream out from the two seed syllables and illuminate all the tathāgatas; drawn in by their rays, they dissolve back into oneself, and all becomes one: this is the wisdom of accomplishing activities.

Thereupon, from the complete transformation of moon, sun, seed syllables and hand emblems, oneself, the Blessed One Vajrasattva, arises in the form of glorious Hevajra, with body color and hand emblems as will be described below, having the nature of the causal Vajradhara, embraced from behind by Nairātmyā. One should imagine oneself as the very self of the dharmadhātu. This is the mode of awakening through the five wisdoms.

Then, with oṃ padma sukha dhāra mahā rāga sukhaṃ dadata caturānanda bhaga viśva hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ karyaṃ kuruṣva me and oṃ vajra mahā dveṣa caturānanda dayaka khagamukhe karasonātha hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ karyaṃ kuruṣva me, one should bless the lotus and the vajra. With oṃ sarvva tathāgata anurāgaṇa vajra svabhāva ātmako'haṃ, immediately afterward one should engage in passionate union. This explains the first union. Such is the procedure of the action seal (karmamudrā).

Then, having beheld the seed syllable hūṃ in the heart of the Blessed One and the seed syllable oṃ in the heart of the Blessed Lady, rays stream out from the seed syllables in their hearts, draw in all the tathāgatas, and dissolve into the seed syllables of the heart. These melt into light. In the east and the other directions, upon the seats, Brahmā and the rest, which lie there, are moon and sun joined face to face; the drop of bodhicitta melts and streams outward, and in order one imagines the eight seed syllables gaṃ caṃ vaṃ ghaṃ paṃ śaṃ laṃ ḍaṃ. From their transformation one imagines a curved knife, a ḍamaru, a tortoise, a snake, a lion, a monk, a wheel and a vajra. Upon these, again, are the seed syllables. The moons, suns, hand emblems and so forth melt, and through the full awakening of the five wisdom beings, in the same way as for the blue one, one should meditate on Gaurī and the others.

At the eastern door is Gaurī, born from the syllable gaṃ, the fair one, black in hue. In her right hand is a curved knife, in her left a rohita fish; she presses down Brahmā. One sets her out as having the nature of the aggregate of form.

At the southern door is Caurī, born from the syllable caṃ, red. In her right hand is a ḍamaru, in her left a boar; she presses down Indra, and she has the nature of the aggregate of feeling.

At the western door is Vetālī, born from the form of vaṃ, like refined gold. In her right hand is a tortoise, in her left a lotus vessel; she presses down Upendra, and she has the nature of the aggregate of perception.

At the northern door is Ghasmarī, born from the form of ghaṃ, green. In her right hand is a snake, in her left a skull cup; she presses down Rudra, and she has the nature of the aggregate of formations.

In the northeast is Pukkasī, blue, born from the form of paṃ. In her right hand is a lion, in her left an axe; she presses down Yama. One imagines her as having the nature of the element.

In the southeast is Śavarī, born from the form of śaṃ, white in color. In her right hand is a monk, in her left a fan; she presses down Yama. One imagines her as having the nature of the water element.

In the southwest is Caṇḍālī, born from the form of the syllable laṃ, blue. In her right hand is a wheel, in her left a skull cup; she presses down a rākṣasa. One should meditate on her as having the nature of the fire element.

In the northwest is Ḍombinī, born from the form of ḍaṃ, multicolored. In her right hand is a vajra, and her left makes the threatening gesture; she presses down Vemacitrin. One should meditate on her as having the nature of the wind element.

All of them have three round red eyes and are seated in the dance of the half-crossed posture. Their yellow hair bristles upward; they have two legs, are endowed with the five mudrās, are seated on moon seats, possess the nine sentiments of the dance, bare their fangs, and are naked. One should rightly meditate on them as having arisen from the streaming forth of bodhicitta.

Then they, whose nature is the three syllables oṃ āḥ hūṃ, marked with two red hoḥ, resting on no extreme, are seen to be like a city of gandharvas; through passion they enter the mouth of the Blessed One, and by way of the vajra path they enter the maṇḍala of the goddess. One meditates on their nature as such.

After that, one imagines that the Blessed One, of one taste in utterly great bliss, melts together with his own consort and abides in the form of his own seed syllable. Then the four goddesses beginning with Pukkasī, knowing that they are without their protector, become greatly sorrowful in mind. With minds of intense longing and wishing to awaken him, they urge him with vajra songs, requesting the Blessed One to arise:

"Lord, arise with a mind of compassion!
Protect me, Pukkasī!
Abandon that nature of emptiness
and join me in great bliss!"

"Without you I shall die;
O Hevajra, arise!
Abandoning the nature of emptiness,
bring Śavarī's fruit to accomplishment!"

"Chief of joy, host of the world,
do you abide in emptiness?
Since without you I know no direction,
I, Caṇḍālī, make this plea."

"I seek your mind:
you who possess magical illusion, arise!
I, Ḍombī, am losing my senses;
do not cut off the stream of compassion!"

Urged by these songs of reality, the Blessed One arises, as though fully awakened from a dream; and from his melting into nectar comes the form of a moon. Upon it is the hand emblem into which the two seed syllables have transformed, marked with hūṃ and aṃ. Then, having become one together with the sun disc, the Blessed One born from hūṃ, through entering into union with Nairātmyā, who arises from the form of aṃ, is oneself as the resultant Hevajra.

He stands with one leg outstretched and the other bent, wrathful, threatening gods and asuras, his head adorned with five skulls, like a youth sixteen years of age. With his four feet he presses down the four māras. He wears a garland of fifty fresh heads, and the crown of his head is sealed with a black crossed vajra. He blazes like the light of the sun, the sound of hūṃ resounds from his mouths, and his body is smeared with ashes.

He has eight faces. The root face is a great black one; the right face is white, and the two further right faces are black; the left face is red, and the two further left faces are black; the upper face is the color of a bee. He bares his fangs, each face has three red eyes, and the fangs are slightly bared. He abides in the manner of the nine sentiments of the dance: grace, heroism, ferocity, wrath, laughter, terror, compassion, majesty and peace. His yellow hair swirls upward, he is adorned with the six mudrās, and he is seated in the dance of the half-crossed posture upon a sun disc.

He has sixteen arms. In the eight right hands, in eight skull vessels, are an elephant, a horse, an ass, an ox, a camel, a human, a lion and a cat, the elephant and the rest in that order; and there are earth, water, fire, wind, moon, sun, Yama and the yakṣa. One imagines such a form.

Nairātmyā and the others have two legs and two arms and one face; they are black, with three red and round eyes, bear the five mudrās, and are naked; their yellow hair swirls upward, their heads are adorned with five skulls, and they wear necklaces of fifty dry heads. In the right hand is a curved knife, in the left a skull cup. One should imagine her embracing the Blessed One with her two arms and tasting the flavor of the Blessed One's lips.

Then, in the heart of the Blessed One is a lotus; upon it a sun; and upon it a blue hūṃ. In the heart of Nairātmyā, upon a lotus, is a moon; from its center comes a curved knife arisen from a, with a moon on its handle, and upon it one should imagine aṃ. In the hearts of the eight yoginīs, too, is a lotus; upon it a moon; upon that each one's own seed syllable; from its transformation comes the hand emblem; and upon this, again, upon a moon, one should imagine the seed syllable. These are the samādhi of the supreme king of maṇḍalas.

Then, having completely accomplished the maṇḍala, light streams outward from those seed syllables and spreads to pervade space, illuminating them. Then from the seed syllable in one's own heart, with rays like hooks, one draws in the wheel of wisdom beings, which takes its place in the sky to the east. With the hūṃ of the eight faces one expels the obstructors, presents welcoming water and the like, and again makes offerings and praises. Urging them with jaḥ hūṃ vaṃ hoḥ, one summons Gaurī and the others in their order, causes them to enter, binds them, and pleases them, and one imagines the pledge wheel and the wisdom wheel becoming one, like water poured into water.

In the eyes, Mohavajrā;
in the ears, Dveṣavajrā;
in the nose, Mātsaryavajrā;
in the mouth, Rāgavajrā;
in the body, Īrṣyāvajrā;
in the mind, the yoginī Nairātmyā.

White Vajrā, black Nairātmyā, yellow Inner Gaurī, red Vajra Water Lady, and green Vajraḍākinī; and again, the black one is Nairātmyā. These are the four names, Vajrā and the others. Mohavajrā and the rest, arising from their own seed syllables, are the armor, the purification of the faculties.

Then, from the syllables oṃ āḥ hūṃ, which are body, speech and mind, arise Bhūcarī, Khecarī, and Nairātmyā, the three families. These too have two arms holding curved knife and skull cup, three eyes and one face; one should imagine them standing upon a moon upon a corpse.

Light rays radiate from the seed syllables at the heart of each, inviting the buddhas, who abide in the sky. The eight goddesses make perfect offerings to them and supplicate them: "May all the tathāgatas please grant me empowerment!" Then those buddhas, in the form of Heruka, embodying the five tathāgatas, with vases filled with the five nectars, speak thus:

As, the moment all appearances were born,
a bathing was performed with water,
so the tathāgatas grant empowerment,
empowering with pure and excellent water.

Saying this, they confer the empowerment. After the empowerment, a rain of flowers and saffron falls, and the sound of drums resounds. Through the empowerment, the crown of each becomes marked by the lord of its own family. Here, the head of the Blessed One is adorned with Akṣobhya. Likewise, Akṣobhya, Vairocana, Ratnasambhava and Amitābha adorn the heads of the four goddesses, Gaurī and the others; these are aversion, delusion, miserliness and attachment. Pukkasī and the others bear those same seals. One recites the mantra oṃ sarbba tathāgata abhiṣeka vajra svabhāva ātmako'haṃ.

Then, from a black yaṃ, a wind maṇḍala; above it, from a red raṃ, a red fire maṇḍala; above that, three skulls; above them, a skull cup arisen from a red a, white outside and red within. Inside it, from a, a moon maṇḍala; upon that, bhūṃ āṃ jṝṃ khaṃ hūṃ, the nature of the buddhas of the five families, the five nectars, and the five meats, all of them, go gu da ha na. Above them, from the syllable oṃ, the substance of a lid, and from a, a moon maṇḍala. Upon that, from hūṃ, one should imagine a five-pronged vajra, marked at its center with hūṃ. The light rays streaming from the hūṃ set the wind's banner in motion, so that the rest, the moon and vajra, melts; and the vajra resting in the skull cup too, as the nectar blazing five times over is churned throughout, blazes, melts right there, and falls into it. The three syllables oṃ āḥ hūṃ draw in the nectar of the three realms, which dissolves into it, and so, blessed by the three syllables, it becomes one.

On the tongues of the gods and goddesses of the circle, one should meditate on a hollow vajra the size of a barleycorn, arisen from hūṃ. Through that vajra they draw up the essence, and one then imagines that the Blessed One and the others drink it. Then one gives it into the mouths of Nairātmyā and the others, as is fitting. By reciting the mantra oṃ sarbba tathāgata amṛta vajra svabhāva ātmako'haṃ, all the deities of the circle are delighted.

Light radiates from the seed at one's own heart, and there emerge Rūpavajrā, Rasavajrā, Sparśavajrā and Dharmadhātuvajrā, holding a mirror, a lute, a conch, and a vessel of music; Sparśavajrā holds various garments, and Dharmadhātuvajrā holds in her hand the source of phenomena (dharmodaya). These and the rest are the maṇḍala of various articles, and the offering to oneself. oṃ sarbba tathāgata pūja svabhāva ātmako'haṃ. Having recited that, the four, Buddhalocanā, Māmakī, Pāṇḍaravāsinī and Tārā, praise the circle:

You are surrounded by the host of yoginīs;
blessed by your own mind, your intent is fulfilled.
Beings moving and unmoving are under your sway;
with the mind of minds you set sentient beings free.
Toward the inconceivable you strive and never turn back.
Be pleased, glorious Hevajra!

Thus the yoginīs offer praise with vajra song. One recites oṃ sarbba tathāgata stutira vajra svabhāva ātmako'haṃ.

The purification of the palace is Vairocana; this is the first limb. The purification of passion is Vajrasattva; this is the second. The purification of empowerment is Akṣobhya; this is the third. The purification that is the tasting of nectar is Amitābha; this is the fourth. The purification of offering is Amoghasiddhi; this is the fifth. The purification of praise is Ratnasambhava; this is the sixth. By meditating on the circle with these six limbs, one will swiftly attain accomplishment.

The signs of accomplishment from meditating in this way are as follows. At first it appears like a full moon veiled by clouds. With greater familiarity, it appears like an illusion. With still greater familiarity, it appears like a dream. When, through thorough familiarity, it has ripened, it appears just as in the waking state.

Then the eight yoginīs, Gaurī and the others, and the principal deity of the maṇḍala emanate unceasingly into the ten directions. Emanating in the forms of sentient beings and the like suited to the various beings to be trained, they work for the benefit of beings, accomplishing the aim of training each as is fitting; afterward they gather back, and one views the maṇḍala as great bliss. This is the stage of the Dharma seal. In this way one imagines all the three realms in the form of the maṇḍala.

Knowing that all this has thus streamed forth from the awakening mind born of great bliss, in which method and wisdom are of one taste, one imagines that the eight goddesses, Gaurī and the others, dissolve into one's own faces; after that, the seven faces dissolve into the root face; likewise the fourteen arms together with their hand emblems dissolve into the two root arms; and likewise the two legs dissolve into the root legs. Thus one sees all the three realms, a form whose nature is method and wisdom, become one; and accordingly the four places are sealed as the four cakras.

Below the navel is the cakra of emanation, a lotus of various colors with sixty-four petals; at its center is the seed oṃ, black, with yellow light, facing upward. At the heart is the white cakra of Dharma, with eight petals; at its center is the form of hūṃ, blue, facing downward, and on the eight petals of that lotus one sets bhrūṃ āṃ jṝṃ khaṃ in the four directions. At the throat is the red cakra of enjoyment, a lotus of sixteen petals; at its center is the form of oṃ, red, facing upward, surrounded by the four, oṃ laṃ a laṃ.

At the crown is the cakra of great bliss, a white lotus of thirty-two petals facing downward. At its center one views, very vividly, the form of haṃ, white, dripping nectar. Having meditated perfectly on the cakras, one should emanate a mass of light rays pervading the sky, and in drawing them back, dissolve them into that very syllable.

At such a time the yogin should without fail recite the syllables of the mantra in this manner of recitation. Radiant light streams from the goddess's mouth and enters the mouth of the principal deity; emerging by the vajra path, it enters the lotus; and again from the goddess's mouth it enters the mouth of the principal. In this sequence one recites without interruption. Among the divisions of activity, such as pacifying, one performs the white activity. Its mantra is:

oṃ aṣṭānanāya gordhapiṃ keśavartmani caturabiṃśatinetrāya śoḍaśabhujāya kṛṣṇajīmātavapuṣe kapālamālānekīdhārine adhmātakrūracittāya ardhendudaṃṣṭīṇe oṃ māraya māraya kāraya kāraya garjjaya garjjaya tarjjaya tarjjaya śoṣaya śoṣaya saptasāgarāna bandha bandha nāgāṣṭakāna gṛhṇa gṛhṇa śatrūṃ ha hā hi hī hu hū he hai ho hau haṃ haḥ phaṭ svāhā

This is the root mantra of the Blessed One. oṃ devabicu vajra hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ svāhā: this is the heart mantra. oṃ vajrakartāri hevajrāya hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ svāhā: this is the near-heart mantra.

Then Nairātmyā melts, together with the seed aṃ with its moon, and one should imagine that the Blessed Lady dissolves into the seed at the heart of the Blessed One. Then, from the seed of the emanation cakra, Caṇḍālī is kindled like a blazing lamp; because she is fierce, she has the form of Caṇḍālī, the Fierce One. She burns the five tathāgatas abiding in the cakra of Dharma, and burns Buddhalocanā and the others as well; she burns the cakra of enjoyment with its vowels and consonants, and the haṃ, the form of wisdom, abiding at the center of the cakra of great bliss. Having burned it, she takes up the awakening mind, the form of indestructible great bliss, and becomes of one taste with the Blessed One; then she turns back again, and taking up the sun and the skull, she dissolves into the seed at the heart. So one should imagine.

Then the seed alone remains, the very nature of the five families, as sheer luminosity. As scripture says:

The drop is Vajrasattva,
and Akṣobhya is that very one;
the crescent moon is Vairocana;
its own being is Ratnasambhava;
ha is Amitābha;
u is Amoghasiddhi.
Knowing these as the five buddhas...

So it says. From that nāda a reddish light radiates. The u, the form whose nature is jealousy, dissolves into the ha. Then the ha, whose nature is attachment, the form of miserliness, dissolves into the crescent moon. Then the form of delusion dissolves into the drop. The half of the drop, the form of aversion, dissolves into the drop. That drop dissolves into the sheer form of the dharmadhātu. Thus all phenomena are nondual; without permanence, annihilation, or a middle, abiding nowhere at all, it is great bliss, and only the nāda remains. Thus the characteristic of the Great Seal (mahāmudrā) has been explained. As it is said:

a is supreme among all syllables;
arising from within, it is unborn;
it lies beyond verbal expression;
it is the sacred letter of great meaning.

Moreover, it is said, kāro mukhaṃ sarba dharmāṇā ādyanutpanna tvāt; and in the Tantra of the Net of Illusion in five hundred thousand lines it is also said:

Phenomena are unborn; letters have no abode;
from what is inexpressible in words and beyond the reach of mind,
phenomena arisen in this way are fully taught;
thus phenomena are unborn.

So it says. Then, through the power of earlier aspirations, in accordance with how the minds of various sentient beings are to be trained, the enjoyment body and the emanation body emanate from the Dharma body and work for the benefit of all beings without exception. Elsewhere too, scripture says:

From that empowerment of the Great Seal,
the great bliss, as it is known,
by its power is this;
in that maṇḍala there is nothing other.
Like an illusion, like a dream,
like a city of the gandharvas,
just so the maṇḍala, vividly,
is seen that way once it is familiar.

So it says. Thus, knowing that all the three realms are of the nature of the maṇḍala and, like the moon in water, arise in dependence, the yogin who does not close the doors of the senses, relies on nothing whatever, and is free of doubt, conducts himself in bliss with the pride of the deity, just as it is. As the master Nāgārjuna said:

This is not the three realms; it is a celestial palace.
These are not living creatures; they are the lords of the Victor's circle.
They are not objects, not faculties, not the elements and the rest;
they are not form and the rest; they are the very self of dharmatā.
And the one within the maṇḍala is this as well:
knowing the varied maṇḍala and its circle, how could one be deluded?

This is the explanation of the seal of commitment (samayamudrā).

Therefore, all pure mindfulness, the purity that abandons all obstacles, is the vajra fence and the rest. Through the purity of all phenomena being like space, there is the source of phenomena. Through the purity of being unstained by phenomena, there is the variegated lotus. The purity of the five nondual wisdoms, in which all phenomena lack intrinsic nature, is the crossed vajra. Through the purity of being endowed with the venerable Vairocana, there is the celestial palace. Through the purity of the eight collections of consciousness, there is the indivisibility of the various phenomena. Through abiding without intrinsic nature in the tathāgatas and in suchness, the four corners are equal. The purity of the four applications of mindfulness is the eastern gate. The purity of the four right abandonments is the southern gate. The purity of the four bases of miraculous power is the western gate. The purity of the five faculties is the northern gate. The purity of the four meditative concentrations is the four archways. The purity of the four meditative absorptions is the four friezes. The purity of patience toward the profound Dharma is the sixteen goddesses. The purity of the nine branches of scripture is the bells, banners and the rest. The purity of the seven limbs of awakening is the nets and half-nets, the sun and the moon. The purity of the ten white yak-tail whisks is virtue upheld by compassion, and the accumulations of merit and wisdom.

The purity of supreme excellence is the eight vases. The purity of the wheel of Dharma, the vajra path of all buddhas and bodhisattvas, unbound in every respect, is the black vajra garland. The purity of form and the rest is the five qualities of sense pleasure, boundless, incomparable, and desired in every way. The purity of the dharmadhātu is the five kinds of offering. The purity of a sense of shame and of conscience is the silk hangings at the gates. The purity of the eightfold noble path is the eight pillars. The purity of all phenomena being without mind is the adornment of the eight charnel grounds. The purity of great attachment is the red lotus of eight petals. The purity of drawing out, at the center, the reality whose nature is nondual is the seats of the four, Brahmā and the others. The purity of generating the sprout of the awakening mind, in order to dispel the condition of all sentient beings, whose mind streams revolve and sink into the swamp of nonvirtue through the darkness of ignorance, is the sun disc upon Brahmā and the others. The purity of what are taught as the eight worldly concerns is the seats of the deities dwelling in the eight directions. The purity of pacifying the fire of beings' afflictions is the moon seat.

The purity of the aggregate of form, great delusion, mirror-like wisdom, form, the perfection of generosity, the view of the perishing collection, and the cognitive obscuration is the rohita fish. The purity of the six afflictions is the curved knife.

The purity of the aggregate of feeling, of pride and slander, of the wisdom of equality, of moral discipline, of the view that holds to extremes, and of the afflictive obscuration is Caurī. The purity of the nine limbs is the small drum. The purity of delusion is the boar.

The purity of the aggregate of perception, great attachment, discriminating wisdom, wrong view, the obscuration of birth, patience, and smell is Vetālī. The purity of jealousy is the tortoise. The purity of emptiness is the skull cup.

The purity of the aggregate of formations, jealousy, all-accomplishing wisdom, the view that holds views supreme, the obscuration of karma, the perfection of meditative concentration, and taste is Ghasmarī. The purity of aversion is the snake. The purity of union in passion is camphor.

The purity of the earth element, the gateway to liberation that is emptiness, great loving-kindness, and the perfection of skillful means is Pukkasī. The purity of pride is the lion. The purity of body, speech and mind is the axe.

The purity of the water element, the gateway to liberation that is signlessness, great compassion, and the perfection of aspiration is Śabarī. The purity of the awakening mind is the monk. The purity of unforgetting mindfulness is the ringing staff.

The purity of the fire element, the gateway to liberation that is wishlessness, great joy, and the perfection of power is Caṇḍālī. Because it turns over the ground of the afflictions, it is the plough. Because it cuts through unknowing, it is the wheel.

The purity of the wind element, the gateway to liberation that is the unconditioned, great equanimity, and the perfection of wisdom is Ḍombinī. Because it is pure wisdom, it is the vajra. The purity of the hosts of māras as a single essence is the threatening gesture.

The purity of the aggregate of consciousness, the purity of great aversion, the wisdom of the dharmadhātu, moral discipline and the conduct of the ascetic vow, the tangible, the perfection of diligence, and form is glorious Heruka. The purity of the loving mind is the black body. The purity of compassion is the red eyes. The purity of Akṣobhya and of body, speech and mind is the red. The purity of the eight liberations is the eight faces. Among them, the purity of subjugation is the left face, red as a lotus, and the purity of pacifying is the right face, white. The purity of Akṣobhya, Amitābha, Ratnasambhava, Vairocana and Amoghasiddhi is the wheel, the earrings, the necklace, the armlets and the girdle. The purity of Vajrasattva is the smearing with ash.

The wrathful aspect is for taming those who are hard to tame. Smiling inwardly is the tawny hair bristling upward. Realizing the lack of intrinsic nature is the crossed vajra on the crown. Belonging to the five families is the five skulls on the forehead. The sixteen emptinesses are the arms. Method and wisdom are the skull cups holding the gods and the rest, in the left and the right hands. Cough, difficulty in breathing, torpor, blood disease, leprosy, pox, disease of the liver and disease of the spleen are what the elephant and the others purify, for they are there to remove the diseases of all sentient beings, such as cough. The purity that serves to accomplish every kind of mastery is the eight, earth and the rest. The purity of the vowels and consonants is the garland of fifty skulls. The purity of the four means of attracting disciples is the feet. The purity of the seven limbs of awakening is the skin. The purity of the four truths is the bones. Flesh is the Swollen One; blood is Śabarī; semen is Caṇḍālī; Ḍombī is marrow and fat. Form, feeling, perception and formations are the four, Gaurī and the others. The purity of emanation, enjoyment and Dharma is the three bodies. The purity of the body of great bliss is inner recollection.

To purify the entire three realms without reference is the half-crossed posture. The purity of every face of the gates is the big toe of one foot touching the sun. To purify the faults of existence and nonexistence in selflessness, the nature of the perfection of wisdom, the purification in which all phenomena are absent, is the curved knife. The purity of conceptions of things and non-things is the lotus vessel of the head. Purifying the blood of the four māras is the kapāla filled with blood. The purity of the five families is the garland of skulls. The essence of existence and nirvāṇa is the hands. The purity of the two truths is the two feet. The purity of experiencing emptiness and compassion as of an inseparable nature, and of the final attainment, is the outward display of method and wisdom in union. The purity of day and night is the purity of method and the purity of wisdom.

The purity of the sixty-four daṇḍas, the eight watches, the sixteen shifts, and the thirty-two channels should be understood accordingly.

Abiding beyond the four cakras, that of emanation and the rest, the yogin of the commitment of Hevajra, holding vajra and curved knife, should in this way bless the torma for the benefit of all sentient beings.

For this, one views a triangular source of phenomena, adorned with red lotuses of eight petals at the center, above, and below. On the eight petals of the central lotus: on the eastern petal is Indra, thousand-eyed, vajra in hand, seated on a white elephant. In the south is Yama, staff in hand, black, seated on a buffalo. On the western petal is Varuṇa, white, seated on a makara, a noose in his hand. On the northern petal is the yakṣa, yellow, seated on a cat, holding a bilva fruit. All of them wear jeweled crowns. Yama has a sun seat and the radiance of the sun; the rest have moon seats and the radiance of the moon. On the petal of Īśāna is the Lord of the Earth, three-eyed, with matted locks and a crown, seated on a bull, holding a white trident in his hand, wearing a tiger-skin garment, smeared with ash, adorned with bone ornaments, with a moon seat and its radiance. On the fire petal is the god of fire, with a hanging belly, deep orange-red, riding a goat, holding a rosary and a water pot, crowned with matted locks, with a sun seat and its radiance, and wearing a Dharma robe. On the petal of Nairṛti is a rākṣasa, his orange-red hair coiled upward, holding a curved knife and a skull cup, with crooked brows, black, with the radiance of the sun and its seat. On the wind petal is Vāyu, a green banner in his hand, with a jeweled crown, riding a deer, with the radiance of the moon and its seat, wearing every ornament. At the center is the goddess of the earth, yellow, on an excellent seat, adorned with every ornament, a vase in her hand, on a moon seat with the radiance of the moon, abiding surrounded by hungry ghosts.

On the eight petals in the upper source of phenomena: on the eastern petal is the Moon, white, seated on a white horse, with a jeweled crown, holding a white water lily in his hand, adorned with every ornament, with a moon seat and the radiance of the moon. On the southern petal is the Sun, red, four-armed, holding a lotus, riding green horses, with a jeweled crown, adorned with every ornament, with his own radiance. As represented by the sun and moon, those that mark the directions and the intermediate directions are Mars, and the planets Mercury, Jupiter, Venus, Saturn and Rāhu, with radiance red, blue-green, yellow, white, black and smoke-colored, each with its own seat, emblem and ornaments. At the central hub is Brahmā, four-faced and four-armed, holding a triple staff, with the hand of fearlessness, holding a rosary and a water pot, yellow, riding a goose, with a moon seat and the radiance of the moon. He carries what is borne for worship; his body is stout, his matted locks short, and he is surrounded by the gods of Brahmā's assembly. Before him one should visualize Purohita, Tuṣita, Nirmāṇarati, Akaniṣṭha and the rest, each in its own form.

On the eight petals of the lotus in the lower source of phenomena, in the directions and intermediate directions in order, are Karkoṭaka, green; Śaṅkhapāla, yellow; Ananta, yellow; Vāsuki, red; Takṣaka and Kulika, both yellow; Padma, yellow; and Mahāpadma, white. These eight wear jeweled crowns, have human faces with every ornament, and serpent hoods, and they are accompanied by asuras and hell beings. At the hub is a white god bearing a human skull, with a human hood.

All of these are on the three lotuses. Gaurī, arisen from the seed at one's own heart, summons them; Caurī places them upon the lotuses; Vetālī binds them with iron chains; and Ghasmarī brings them under control with the bell. Those abiding on the central lotus, their own colors, insignia, marks and shapes transformed, arise from the seed letter as black, crowned with Akṣobhya. Those abiding above are transformed into the essence of their own forms, arisen from the letter oṃ, white, crowned with Vairocana. Those abiding below abandon their own forms; arisen from the letter a, they are red, crowned with Amitābha. Those abiding on the three lotuses hold vajra and bell, have one face, are embraced by the limbs of consorts who are their own appearance, and abide on their seats with the right leg extended; so one should view them.

These are to be shown honor. To them, with the mantra oṃ pra par satkāra arghaṃ pratīccha svāhā, one gives offering water before them, with the mudrā, in a conch bowl. Then, as before, by the rite of tasting nectar, one accomplishes the enjoyment of the torma as nectar itself, and with this, oṃ inda jama jala jakkha bhūta bahni bā u rakkha canda sujja māda babbatāla bātāle aṭṭa sappa svāhā, one praises them and rings the bell.

He to whom these perfect purities, made pure by the purities of the Victors, ever appear,
free from the very beginning of arising and ceasing, rid of the ten million conceptions that tally up others,

Translator's notes

The text names four kinds of offering (outer, inner, secret, and of reality) but then describes only three; the inner offering is not explained in this passage, and the translation follows the text as it stands. The "utterly pure one" in the list of empowerments renders ཤིན་ཏུ་རྣམ་པར་དག་པ, which seems to name a further stage of empowerment. Three apparent copyists' slips are read by sense: འཁོར་ལོ་རྩི་བས for རྩིབས ("spokes"); མིའི་མཚམས for མེའི་མཚམས ("the fire quarter," southeast); and ལྷམ་ཧེ for ལྷ་མ་ཧེ ("a god with a buffalo's head"). The color phrase "the color of indranīla sapphire, adorned with various green ornaments" is uncertain in its construction. The phrase "abide with eye and form distinct" is rendered literally; its precise sense is unclear. The names of the south, southwest and west charnel grounds, and of the clouds, are translated rather than given in Sanskrit, since they differ from the standard lists and cannot be identified with confidence; the tree name pakaha is given as spelled. གླུ་རིགས་ལྡན is read as ཀླུ་རིགས་ལྡན, the nāga Kulika, since "song" makes no sense in a list of nāgas; likewise དན in སྐུ་མདོག་དན་ཕྱག་མཚན is read as དང. The bird འབྲི་ཏ is not identified and is given phonetically as "drita"; the sense of the marked run in the list of terrors is unclear. Gaurī is called both "white" (her name means "the fair one") and black in hue, as the text says. Pukkasī is said to have the nature simply of "the element"; by the pattern of the others the earth element is presumably meant.
