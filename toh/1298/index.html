About this text

The Stages of the Torma of Glorious Hevajra, known in Sanskrit as the Śrī Hevajrabalikrama and in Tibetan as Pal Gyepé Dorjé Tormé Rimpa, is a short ritual manual for offering torma, consecrated ritual cakes, within the practice of the Hevajra Tantra. After paying homage to Heruka, it explains the purposes of the offering, from long life, health and prosperity to the averting of harm and the subduing of enemies, and then sets out how the torma is prepared and blessed, how the eight great gods, the guardians of the directions, the great nāgas and the hosts of field protectors and spirits are invited and fed, how fierce rites are entrusted to them, and how the rite closes with the teaching, aspiration, auspicious verses and dedication. The colophon attributes it to the great master Alalavajra and records its translation into Tibetan by the Indian paṇḍita Gayādhara and the Tibetan translator Gyijo Dawé Özer. It is Toh 1298 in the Tengyur, where it belongs among the commentaries and ritual texts of the Tantra section associated with the Hevajra cycle.

Translation

In the language of India: śrī hevajrabalokrama. In the language of Tibet: The Stages of the Torma of Glorious Hevajra.

Homage to glorious Vāgīśvara.

Embodiment of method and wisdom,
Arisen perfectly from two syllables,
Principal lord of body, speech and mind:
Bowing to Heruka,
I shall explain the stages of the ritual method
For offering the nectar torma
To the maṇḍala of Hevajra,
Endowed with the divisions of body, speech and mind.

Now, as for the purpose of the torma: at the time of the maṇḍala of glorious Hevajra, of the fire offering, of worship, and of practice, the yogin, in order to benefit and to subdue; and sons of good family who wish for long life, who wish to be free of epidemics and disease, who wish famine to be pacified, who wish harm to be pacified, who wish glory and fame to increase, who wish to avert untimely death, who wish for wealth and prosperity, who wish to turn back adversaries, and who wish to obtain the mundane and supramundane accomplishments: the wise should apply themselves to the torma rite.

If one asks what that is, the scripture says:

To pacify the obstructors,
To please the deities,
And to restore broken commitments,
Undertake this torma rite as far as you are able.

Again, in some texts it is said:

When the yogin has made fine offering
With these tormas to all the bhūtas,
He will be happy at all times through gain,
The deities will rejoice, and beings will prosper.
If one gives a single torma to the hosts of bhūtas,
It crushes hatred, fierce rites, and the ranks of enemies,
It brings about separation, killing and summoning,
And it brings pacification, happiness and increase.

And again, a certain master says:

For all the rites of mundane
And supramundane accomplishment,
The glorious, perfect Buddha
Has highly praised this torma rite.

Since this is said, this torma rite has very great benefits.

Here the mantra practitioner must give torma to the twenty-four bhūtas, as it is said:

To the eight great gods with their retinues,
The eight great nāgas and the eight guardians of the directions,
The eights that belong to the retinue,
Each together with its own retinue,
Constantly, or else monthly,
Or likewise in a yearly cycle,
Or on the tenth day of the waxing moon
And the tenth day of the waning moon,
At maṇḍala rites, fire offerings and recitation,
Likewise at times when harm has arisen,
Or as is fitting and appropriate,
And as accords with oneself,
With whatever provisions one can afford,
Give the torma with diligence.

And how is the torma prepared? It is said:

Flowers, incense, lamps and unguents,
Medicines, perfumes and food offerings for the gods,
Vegetable dishes, sweetmeats and khurwa pastries,
Warm food together with sixty-day rice,
Mixing in things made of flour and the like,
Cooked rice, the outer and inner parts of rice, the seven grains,
Mix in curd, oil, milk porridge and so forth.
Blood, meat, beans, fish and the like,
Brewed beer, grain beer, various cooked foods and so on,
Cooked, raw and aged things alike,
Mixed properly with the five nectars:
Fill the vessels with all of these,
And set them upon a vase filled with water.
Place khurwa pastries, dough and lamps on top,
And offer them to those beings at nighttime.

For the fierce rites, it is placed upon an effigy of the enemy. The color of the torma is changed according to the rite.

Next, the stages of blessing the torma. First, having clearly meditated on oneself as the body of the deity, the great seal, one visualizes: at first, in what is like space, from yaṃ a wind maṇḍala marked with yaṃ; from raṃ a fire maṇḍala marked with raṃ; above that, from a a skull cup. Inside it, upon a sun, from hūṃ bhrūṃ āṃ jri'aṃ khaṃ and go ku da ha na, one generates the five hooks and the five nectars. Above them is a vajra arisen from hūṃ, half sunk into a moon arisen from a. Above that, with oṃ one invites the nectar of wisdom, with āḥ one generates splendor, and with hūṃ one makes the nectar of wisdom and the nectar of commitment nondual.

Having blessed it in this way, one imagines in front a triangular source of phenomena, and within it three stacked lotuses. First, in the center of the white lotus is a white Hevajra, surrounded by the eight gods generated from oṃ. In the middle, in the center of the blue lotus, is a blue Hevajra, surrounded by the ten blue guardians of the directions generated from hūṃ. Below, in the center of the red lotus, is a red Hevajra, surrounded by the eight great nāgas generated from a.

To invite the guests, one binds the vajra-lotus mudrā and says oṃ e a ra li hoḥ hriṃ hriṃ hriṃ jaḥ hūṃ vaṃ hoḥ vaj ḍāka ḍākinī vajra dṛśya hoḥ vajra samaya hoḥ vajra samaya stvaṃ, together with saying pheṃ three times; with that, one summons them with light rays from the seed syllable in the heart:

oṃ āḥ hūṃ
Eight gods together with your retinues,
Eight guardians of the directions and eight great nāgas,
And all who belong to the retinue as commanded:
Mindful of your commitment, come to this place!

And further, to the hosts of field protectors belonging within the triangle, thus:

By this command of Vajreśvarī,
All of you, without exception, assemble here!

Having summoned them with this, one imagines them seated on lotus seats. Then one worships them with offerings: oṃ kuru kuru adhipati tiṣṭha samaya hūṃ jaḥ. One should make them nondual. Then, after they have dissolved into the commitment deities, one blesses their tongues as vajras arising from hūṃ upon lotuses. Then, opening the lotus hands and directing them toward the torma, one recites the mantra for offering the torma:

Then one should perform the four rites of killing. Then one sings the vajra song. Then one teaches the Dharma:

oṃ Phenomena are like reflections,
Pure and clear, without turbidity;
They arise entirely from causes and from karma,
And are definitely born from suchness.
By this truth, may all
See the maṇḍala without stain.
May all sentient beings be happy!
By whatever path they go,
May they meet with buddhahood itself!

Then one makes the aspiration:

oṃ śīla caṇḍan lepetaṃ agaṅdyanaṃ prabharaṇa potaṃ kakumumikīraṇaṃ viharataṃ buddhaviśaya punarāgamanaya muḥ

oṃ vajrasatva samaya manupālaya vajrasatva tvenopatiṣṭha dṛḍho me bhava sutoṣyo me bhava anurakto me bhava supoṣyo me bhava sarvasiddhimme prayaccha sarvakarmasucchame cittaṃ śreyaḥ kuru hūṃ ha ha ha ha hoḥ bhagavān sarvatathāgatavajramāmemuñca vajrībhava mahāsamayasatva āḥ

Then one speaks words of auspiciousness:

oṃ You have accomplished every aim of sentient beings;
Grant the accomplishments that accord with them.
And though you depart to the buddha-fields,
I pray you to come again.

With this one requests the guests of the nature to depart. The guests of commitment are made into the three syllables and gathered into the seed syllable in one's own heart.

Done to purify the yogin's stains
Of avarice and of poverty,
To swiftly obtain the accomplishments,
And to increase the setting of beings on the path:
By this virtue may all beings swiftly go
To the jewel isle of supreme awakening!

The Stages of the Torma Rite of Glorious Hevajra, composed by the great master Alalavajra, is complete. It was translated by the paṇḍita, the Kāyastha Gayādhara, and the Tibetan translator Gyijo Dawé Özer.

Translator's notes

The Tibetan has "Teruka" in the opening verse, evidently a copyist's slip for Heruka, which is translated. In "to please the deities" the text reads 'go ba, which makes little sense here; it is taken as a slip for mgu ba, "to please." The syllables go ku da ha na are the initial syllables standing for the five meats (cow, dog, horse, elephant, human); this text calls what they produce "the five hooks." "Sixty-day rice" renders drug cu pa (Skt. ṣaṣṭika), a quick-ripening rice. Many names in the list of goddesses are Tibetan renderings whose Sanskrit originals cannot be identified with confidence; these are translated by meaning (the Black One, the Fierce One, the Ager, the Lady of Thirty, and so on). "Fierce ones without compassion" reads drag for the written dag ("pure"), which does not fit the context. "Madness-bringers" and "forgetfulness-bringers" render smyo byed and brjed byed, the classes of unmāda and apasmāra spirits. The closing lines of the dedication are part of a fierce rite directed against an enemy. "Kneeling upon the ground" renders pus mo lha nga sa la btsugs, where lha nga is unclear; the sense of kneeling is plain. "Subjugation or wrath" renders tsham ngam drag po, where tsham is uncertain. The crown ornaments follow Hevajra usage: Śāśvata ("the Eternal") is a name for Vairocana, Akṣobhya for the vajra-mind family, and the Lord of Dance (Narteśvara) for the speech family. In the second group the text reads "adorned with sixteen hands" (phyag cu drug), evidently a slip for the six mudrās (phyag rgya drug) of the parallel verses, and translated accordingly. In the nāga list "Vāsuki and his son" renders nor rgyas bu; the list otherwise matches the eight great nāga kings. "Ridden corpses" (ro zhon) are the mounts of these deities. "Pūtanas" renders srul po, a class of foul-smelling spirits. "Draped" renders bshal in the description of the elephant hides, where it has its sense of hanging loosely rather than washing. "The four rites of killing" translates gsad pa'i las bzhi as written. "Guests of the nature" and "guests of commitment" are the wisdom beings, who are asked to depart, and the commitment beings, who are dissolved into oneself. In the colophon, ka stha pa is taken as Kāyastha, the scribal caste title regularly attached to the paṇḍita Gayādhara, and ga thā ga ya dha ra as his name.
