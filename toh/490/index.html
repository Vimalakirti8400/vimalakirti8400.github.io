About this text

The Glorious Ornament of the Vajra Essence, Great King of Tantras, known in Sanskrit as the Śrī Vajramaṇḍālaṃkāra Mahātantrarāja and in Tibetan as Pal Dorjé Nyingpo Gyen, is Toh 490 in the Kangyur, where it belongs to the Tantra section among the tantras of the yoga class. It opens in the manner of a sūtra, with the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Body, Speech and Mind Vajra, presiding over an assembly of tathāgatas and bodhisattvas, and it goes on to unfold teachings in which mantra practice and maṇḍala ritual are joined with the Perfection of Wisdom. The text presents a series of meditations on the equality and emptiness of all phenomena, each sealed with its own mantra. Later sections describe how to choose sites for practice, how to lay out and draw the maṇḍalas of the buddha families, and how to give the consecrations that admit a disciple. According to its colophon, the Tibetan translation was begun by a translator called the glorious Chöjé, left slightly incomplete, and finished by Lodrö Tenpa.

Translation

In the language of India: śrī bajra maṇḍa a laṃ ka ra nā ma ma hā tan tra rā jā. In the language of Tibet: The Glorious Ornament of the Vajra Essence, Great King of Tantras.

With my head I bow in reverence at the lotus feet of the guru and of Mañjughoṣa.

Thus did I hear at one time. The Blessed One, the Tathāgata, Body, Speech and Mind Vajra, was dwelling there. He was endowed with the distinction of the manifold wisdom of the commitment consecrated by the vajra, the jewel, the precious gem, the lotus and the action of all the tathāgatas. With the jeweled crown of all the tathāgatas he had obtained empowerment as Dharma king of the three realms. He was the great lord of the yoga of the wisdom of the vajra commitment of all the tathāgatas, and had obtained the wisdom mudrā of all the tathāgatas. He had realized the equality of all the mudrās of all the tathāgatas, performed manifold activities, and carried out the vajra activity that fulfills every hope of all the realms of sentient beings, entirely and without exception. He was great compassion, the Great Vairocana, abiding permanently through the three times.

He dwelt in the following place. It was endowed with great precious jewels relied upon and praised by all the tathāgatas, hung with bells of many colors, and beautifully adorned with silken banners stirred by the wind, with clusters of flowers, and with nets and half nets. It was the whole expanse of reality, perfectly and thoroughly purified: the mansion of the expanse of reality, of boundless light. It was the temple of the Perfection of Wisdom, arrayed with the ornament of the supreme essence of flowering vines, its clouds spreading out oceans of world systems in succession and pervading the entire realm of space. It was consecrated by the Tathāgata. It was the center where buddhas beyond all extremes are born, the luminous celestial palace that is the essence of Vairocana, the abode of the tathāgatas, the Ornament of the Vajra Essence, the Body, Speech and Mind Vajra of all the tathāgatas. It was the sphere of activity of the limbs of the Tathāgata, which neither arise nor cease nor come to cessation, the clear limit of reality. It was the domain of the omniscient wisdom of all the tathāgatas, the mansion of the essence of the expanse of reality, the wisdom body of all the tathāgatas, the seat of awakening of all the tathāgatas, the supreme great ground. There he dwelt.

There, surrounded and attended in front by bodhisattva great beings as numerous as the atoms of all the buddha fields, and by tathāgatas, he taught the Dharma. He taught the gateway to the pure way of all phenomena, good in the beginning, good in the middle and good in the end, excellent in meaning, excellent in its words, unadulterated, complete, wholly pure and wholly immaculate.

Then again the Blessed One was the wisdom being of the nondual equality of the Body, Speech and Mind Vajra of all the tathāgatas, the sun of the wisdom of all the tathāgatas, the Vajra Essence that is the source of the commitment of complete victory. He had attained the wisdom of equality in which all phenomena are pure by their essential nature. He was deep like the ocean. He had obtained the wisdom of the mudrā of manifold activities. His body, speech and mind were vajra, like space. He was the great body, the vajra body, the body beyond extremes, the precious body, the Dharma body, the emanation body, the body of the equality of activity. He was the awakening mind, the unfailing one, the one who gathers all in, the increaser of virtue, the great king, the radiant one, the banner, the one who laughs, the one endowed with Dharma, the wheel of wisdom, the one of all languages, the manifold one, the protector, the one with the yakṣas, the one who joins, the one who has become the crown protuberance, the supreme one, the vajra bearer, the wheel bearer, the wisdom bearer, the great all pervading lord. His body, speech and mind were vajra, abiding perfectly throughout the whole expanse of space. He brought together all the tathāgatas. He was the wisdom being who comprehends the whole vajra expanse, the wisdom essence of the vajra blessing, as vast as the atoms of all the realms of space. By spreading through all the realms of space the great commitment of the unsurpassed vajra jewel empowerment of all the tathāgatas, he had become manifestly awakened in the direct knowledge of the tathāgatas. Through the pure essence of the very self of all the tathāgatas, he pervaded all of space, in which all phenomena are pure by their intrinsic nature. Through the wisdom that fully sees all forms, he was endowed with the supreme conduct of taming sentient beings entirely and without exception. Because all the tathāgatas gave him their unfailing command, he was possessed of the manifold, unsurpassed activity of the equality of all sentient beings.

Vairocana, the unchanging Akṣobhya,
Ratnaketu, the one who makes light,
Amitābha, protector of the victors,
And Amogha, who performs all activities.

He was the maṇḍala of the mirrorlike wisdom of all the tathāgatas. He was firmly established in the wisdom of equality of all the tathāgatas. He had attained the nondual discerning wisdom of all the tathāgatas, and had fully obtained the wisdom that accomplishes the deeds of all the tathāgatas. He was the pure expanse of reality of all the tathāgatas, the domain of the wisdom of the equality of all phenomena, the perfected, wholly pure wisdom that perceives the nondual equality of all the tathāgatas. Through emptiness, suchness, the limit of reality, signlessness, wishlessness and the four divine abidings, he had become the domain of the wisdom of the buddhas, and he perceived the equality of the domain of the buddhas. Having consecrated all the tathāgatas as the very Great Lord of Vajra Yoga, he abided everywhere throughout the directions and intermediate directions.

Then again the Blessed One, the Lord of Vajra Yoga, spoke to Mañjuśrī Kumārabhūta: "Mañjuśrī, invite all the buddhas and the vajra holders!"

When the Blessed One had spoken thus, Mañjuśrī Kumārabhūta, the great universal monarch, his face beaming with delight, snapped his vajra fingers in order to invite all the tathāgatas and the vajra holders, and uttered this great heart mantra that gathers all the tathāgatas: buddha samājaḥ āḥ hāḥ oṃ hūṃ vajra samājaḥ.

When he had uttered this, all the tathāgatas and the vajra holders clearly beheld the clouds of the boundless oceans of the successions of world systems arrayed with the ornament of the essence of the flower ground.

Then again Mañjuśrī Kumārabhūta, together with all the tathāgatas, united with the Blessed Vairocana, fully generated the wisdom of the vajra mind of all the tathāgatas, and abided in every place.

Then the Blessed Vairocana, the Lord of Yoga whose nature is the vajra of all the tathāgatas, consecrated himself as all the tathāgatas, and spoke to those tathāgatas and to the vajra holders: "O all you tathāgatas, I shall explain the heart of the great universal monarch of omniscient wisdom which is within me. All you tathāgatas, bestow your blessing! bhruṃ."

Then the Tathāgata Akṣobhya,
The Tathāgata Ratnasambhava,
The Tathāgata Amitābha,
And the Tathāgata Amoghasiddhi,

together with the other tathāgatas as well, entered into the śrīvatsa at the heart of all the tathāgatas. The moment they entered, all the tathāgatas fully revealed every bliss in the wheel.

Then the Blessed Vairocana took on four faces, and with his four faces he melodiously proclaimed this very examination of the way of the Dharma, the domain of all the buddhas, the expanse of reality and the infinite realm of space, as follows:

"Whatever is the state of the purity of utmost delight, that is the state of a bodhisattva. Whatever is the state of the purity of view, that is the state of a bodhisattva. Whatever is the state of the purity of joy, that is the state of a bodhisattva. Whatever is the state of the purity of craving, that is the state of a bodhisattva. Whatever is the state of the purity of attachment, that is the state of a bodhisattva. Whatever is the state of the purity of aversion, that is the state of a bodhisattva. Whatever is the state of the purity of delusion, that is the state of a bodhisattva. Whatever is the state of the purity of pride, that is the state of a bodhisattva. Whatever is the state of the purity of the unfree states, that is the state of a bodhisattva. Whatever is the state of the purity of contentment of mind, that is the state of a bodhisattva. Whatever is the state of the purity of light, that is the state of a bodhisattva. Whatever is the state of the purity of the body, that is the state of a bodhisattva. Whatever is the state of the purity of speech, that is the state of a bodhisattva. Whatever is the state of the purity of mind, that is the state of a bodhisattva. Whatever is the state of the purity of form, that is the state of a bodhisattva. Whatever is the state of the purity of sound, that is the state of a bodhisattva. Whatever is the state of the purity of smell, that is the state of a bodhisattva. Whatever is the state of the purity of taste, that is the state of a bodhisattva. Whatever is the state of the purity of touch, that is the state of a bodhisattva. Whatever is the state of the purity of all things, that is the state of a bodhisattva.

"If one asks why this is so: it is because all phenomena are pure by their intrinsic nature. Since all phenomena are pure by their intrinsic nature, the Perfection of Wisdom is completely pure."

Having spoken thus, the Blessed One, the lord of all secret mantras, the guide of all secret mantras, who has become the master of all mantras, the universal monarch of all the crown protuberances, whose teaching cannot be overridden, uttered the heart of the universal monarch: bhruṃ.

"Mañjuśrī, whoever hears even once this heart of the Perfection of Wisdom, which gathers together all phenomena, will never fall into the lower realms such as the hells, until he reaches the seat of awakening, even though he may have amassed all the obscurations and impediments, the obscuration of the afflictions, the obscuration of phenomena, and the great obscuration of karma. Even if he has committed evil deeds, he will be without suffering, and they will be exhausted. Whoever holds this, recites it each day, reads it, and continually attends to it properly, will in this very life attain the vajra samādhi of the equality of all phenomena and will become lord of all phenomena. He will experience delight, satisfaction and great joy in all phenomena. As a great bodhisattva, within sixteen births he will attain the state of a tathāgata or the state of a vajra holder. hūṃ."

Then the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Vairocana, once again taught this way of the Perfection of Wisdom, the accomplishment of the manifest awakening to the peaceful nature of reality of all the tathāgatas.

It is the great awakening of being fully and perfectly awakened to the equality of the vajra through vajra firmness; the great awakening of being fully awakened to the equality of meaning through oneness of meaning; the great awakening of being fully awakened to the equality of the nature of reality through purity of intrinsic nature; and the great awakening of being fully awakened to the equality of all actions through conceptuality and nonconceptuality.

"Mañjuśrī, if anyone listens to, holds, recites or meditates on these four bases of the Dharma, then, for as long as he has not yet come to dwell at the seat of awakening, even though he may engage in every kind of evil, he will pass beyond all evil, and he will swiftly and fully awaken to unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening. āṃ."

Then again the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Śākyamuni, tamer of all the wicked, spoke the realization of the Perfection of Wisdom called The Equality of All Phenomena Gathered by Complete Victory:

"Because attachment is free of elaboration, attachment is without elaboration. Because aversion is free of elaboration, aversion is without elaboration. Because delusion is free of elaboration, delusion is without elaboration. Because all phenomena are free of elaboration, all phenomena are without elaboration. Because the Perfection of Wisdom is free of elaboration, the Perfection of Wisdom should be known to be without elaboration.

"Mañjuśrī, if anyone listens to, holds, recites and reads this way of the Perfection of Wisdom, then even if, having taken up the power of taming, he were to slay the beings born in the three realms, he would not go to the lower realms, and he will swiftly attain unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening. hūṃ."

Then again the Blessed One, the Tathāgata who has realized the nature of reality that is pure by its intrinsic nature, spoke this way of the Perfection of Wisdom called The Wisdom Seal of Avalokiteśvara, Lord of All Phenomena:

"Because all attachment is purified, in the world all objects will come to be pure. Because all aversion is purified, in the world all evil will come to be pure. Because all phenomena are purified, in the world all phenomena will come to be pure. Because omniscient wisdom is purified, in the world omniscient wisdom will come to be pure. Because the Perfection of Wisdom is purified, in the world the Perfection of Wisdom will come to be pure.

"Mañjuśrī, if anyone listens to, holds, recites and meditates on this way of the Perfection of Wisdom, then even though he dwells amid all sensual pleasures, he will, like a lotus, not be stained by the adventitious taint of the fault of attachment, and he will swiftly and fully awaken to unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening. hrīḥ."

Then again the Blessed One, the Tathāgata who is lord of all the three realms, spoke the way of the Perfection of Wisdom called The Essence of Wisdom, Source of the Empowerment of All the Tathāgatas:

"By bestowing empowerment, one will fully obtain sovereignty over all the three realms. Through the gift of what is of benefit, the wishes of all will be completely fulfilled. Through the gift of the Dharma, one will attain the equality of all phenomena. Through the gift of material things, one will obtain bliss of body, speech and mind for all. trāṃ."

Then again the Blessed One, the Eternal Tathāgata, who has realized the wisdom seal of all the tathāgatas and holds the fist of all, spoke the way of the Perfection of Wisdom called The Vajra Consecrated by the Wisdom Seal of All the Tathāgatas:

"By fully taking up the seal of the body of all the tathāgatas, one becomes all the tathāgatas themselves. By fully taking up the seal of their speech, one comes to obtain all the teachings. By fully taking up the seal of their mind, one comes to obtain all samādhis. By fully taking up the vajra seal, one comes to accomplish all body, speech and mind as the supreme vajra.

"Mañjuśrī, if anyone listens to, holds, recites, explains and meditates on this way of the Perfection of Wisdom, he will attain every accomplishment, every excellence, every wisdom, every activity, and every accomplishment of the supreme Vajrasattva of body, speech and mind, and he will swiftly and fully awaken to unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening. aḥ."

Then again the Blessed One, the Tathāgata for whom all phenomena are free of elaboration, spoke the way of the Perfection of Wisdom called Turning the Wheel of Vajra Letters:

"Through the mode of absence of intrinsic nature, all phenomena are emptiness. Because the signlessness of all phenomena is grasped, all phenomena are devoid of signs. Because they are fully freed from wishing, all phenomena are wishless. Because the Perfection of Wisdom is completely pure, all phenomena are by their intrinsic nature luminous. aṃ."

Then again the Blessed One, the Tathāgata who dwells within the wheel of all the tathāgatas, spoke the way of the Perfection of Wisdom called Entering the Great Wheel:

"By entering the equality of the vajra, one enters the wheel of all the tathāgatas. By entering the equality of meaning, one enters the wheel of the great bodhisattvas. By entering the equality of all phenomena, one enters the wheel of the sublime Dharma. By entering the equality of action, one enters all the wheels."

hūṃ.

Then again the Blessed One, the Tathāgata who is the vessel of the vast rite of worship of all the tathāgatas, spoke the way of the Perfection of Wisdom called The Sublime Worship of All the Tathāgatas:

"Generating the awakening mind is the vast rite of worship of all the tathāgatas. Fully protecting all the realms of sentient beings is the vast rite of worship of all the tathāgatas. Fully taking up all the teachings is the vast rite of worship of all the tathāgatas. And the action preceded by asking about the Perfection of Wisdom, having others ask about it, holding it, reciting it, proclaiming it and meditating on it, is the vast rite of worship of all the tathāgatas. oṃ."

Then again the Blessed One, the Tathāgata who has the power to tame all the wicked, spoke this way of the Perfection of Wisdom called The Essence of Wisdom That Tames All Sentient Beings:

"Because all sentient beings are equality, wrath is equality. Because all sentient beings are taming, wrath is taming. Because all sentient beings are the nature of reality, wrath is the nature of reality. Because all sentient beings are vajra, wrath is vajra. If one asks why this is so: it is the awakening that tames all sentient beings. haḥ."

Then again the Blessed One, the Tathāgata who is firmly established in the equality of all, spoke this way of the Perfection of Wisdom called The Supreme Equality of All Phenomena:

"Because all is equality, the Perfection of Wisdom is equality. Because all is meaning, the Perfection of Wisdom is meaning. Because all phenomena are equality, the phenomena of the Perfection of Wisdom are equality. Because all is action, the Perfection of Wisdom should be known to be action. hrīḥ."

Then again the Blessed One, all the tathāgatas, spoke this way of the Perfection of Wisdom called The Blessing of All the Tathāgatas:

"Because the great bodhisattva Samantabhadra is the self of all, all sentient beings have the essence of the Tathāgata. Because they are empowered by the vajra essence, all sentient beings have the vajra essence. Because they are empowered by all jewels, all sentient beings have the jewel essence. Because they fully enter into all speech, all sentient beings have the Dharma essence. Through the application that performs all actions, all sentient beings have the action essence. hrīḥ."

Then again the Blessed One, the Tathāgata who has passed beyond extremes and reached the end of the limitless, in order to consecrate the Dharma that is limitless and perfected, and again its conception and its final consummation, spoke this way of the Perfection of Wisdom called The Vajra Consecrated by the Final Consummation of the Equality of All Phenomena:

"Because the Perfection of Wisdom is without end, all the tathāgatas are without end. Because the Perfection of Wisdom is without limit, all the tathāgatas are without limit. Because the Perfection of Wisdom is oneness, all phenomena are oneness. Because the Perfection of Wisdom is the final consummation, all phenomena come to their final consummation.

"Mañjuśrī, if anyone listens to, holds, recites and meditates on this way of the Perfection of Wisdom, then, by abiding in the perfected conduct of a pure and perfected bodhisattva, he will be liberated from all obscurations, and so will swiftly attain the state of a tathāgata or the state of a vajra holder. bhyoḥ."

Then again the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Vairocana, who has realized the secret nature of reality of all the tathāgatas, since all phenomena are free of elaboration, spoke this supreme and sublime gateway of the Perfection of Wisdom of the equality of vajra phenomena, free of beginning, end and middle, called The Commitment of Great Bliss, the Unfailing Vajra:

"Through the supreme accomplishment of the great attachment of the great bodhisattvas, great bliss comes to be supremely accomplished. Through the supreme accomplishment of the great bliss of the great bodhisattvas, the great awakening of all the tathāgatas comes to be supremely accomplished. Through the great bodhisattvas' supreme accomplishment of the awakening of all the tathāgatas, the utter defeat of the great hosts of Māra comes to be supremely accomplished. Through the great bodhisattvas' supreme accomplishment of the utter defeat of the great hosts of Māra, lordship over all the three realms comes to be supremely accomplished. Through the great bodhisattvas' supreme accomplishment of lordship over all the three worlds, the supreme benefit and happiness of all sentient beings, the exceedingly great bliss that fully protects the realms of sentient beings entirely and without exception, comes to be supremely accomplished. svaṃ.

"If one asks why this is so:

For as long as the supreme among the wise
Remain within the state of saṃsāra,
So long, not passing into nirvāṇa,
Can they work the incomparable good of beings.

The means of the Perfection of Wisdom
Is accomplished through the blessing of wisdom;
Because all actions are completely purified,
Existence itself comes to be pure.

The world, to be tamed through attachment and the rest,
Ever commits evil throughout existence;
In order to purify such beings,
One tames oneself for as long as existence lasts.

Just as a lotus of lovely red
Is not stained by any taint of color,
So in existence, with its faults of habitual tendencies,
One who always benefits beings is never stained.

Great attachment, completely pure,
Is great bliss, is great wealth;
Having gained lordship over the three realms,
One firmly establishes their aims.

haḥ.

Here, then, are the vajra stations. Because they are by nature indivisible, one's own body is the station of Vajrasattva; this vajra station utterly destroys all the thickets of views. Because it leads into knowledge, ignorance is a vajra station; through this vajra station one comes to enter into full understanding of the objects of focus. hūṃ

Because of the forming of conditioned activity, the limit of the deeds of immediate retribution is a vajra station; through this vajra station one comes to know with certainty all that is seen. oṃ

Because of the sameness of the limit of freedom from attachment, the limit of attachment is a vajra station; this vajra station leads to freedom from attachment. ha

Because of the sameness of the limit of loving-kindness, the limit of aversion is a vajra station; this vajra station leads to the cutting off of all faults. maiḥ

Because of the sameness of the lamp of wisdom, the limit of delusion is a vajra station; this vajra station dispels the darkness. raṃ

All sentient beings are a single sentient being; this vajra station serves for entering into the sameness of sentient beings and for realizing the nature of sentient beings. hrī

All sentient beings are of a single mind; this vajra station serves for entering into certainty about the mind and for fully realizing that the mind is by nature luminous. a

All the buddhas are a single buddha; this vajra station serves for following all the tathāgatas and for realizing the wisdom of sameness. oṃ

All phenomena are the qualities of a buddha; this vajra station serves for entering into the realization of omniscient wisdom and for realizing the vajra-like meditative absorption. āṃ

All the deeds of Māra are the deeds of a buddha; this vajra station serves for entering into the realization of all actions and for passing wholly beyond all actions. hūṃ

All speech is the speech of the Tathāgata; this vajra station serves for entering into the realization of all sounds just as they are and for realizing the Dharma that is beyond expression. rdhaṃ

All phenomena are unborn; this vajra station serves for entering into deathlessness and for passing beyond the workings of birth, aging, and death. maṃ

All phenomena have never come into being; this vajra station serves for entering into non-cessation and for realizing that no phenomenon ceases. khaṃ

As for the words "Then one should train in that wisdom whose nature is the Dharma letters of all the tathāgatas": because they serve for discernment, they are the gateways to all phenomena, and they should be understood as serving for analysis. oṃ paṃ

The letter a is explained as a gateway to all phenomena because all phenomena are unborn. oṃ a ā aṃ aḥ

The letter pa is a gateway to all phenomena: one should see all the perfections of all phenomena as ultimately brought to completion. oṃ paṃ

The letter na is explained as a gateway to all phenomena because it masters the names, forms, wisdom, and qualities of all phenomena. oṃ naṃ

The letter ḍa is declared a gateway to all phenomena because it brings to completion the perfection of taming and of peace. oṃ ḍaṃ

The letter ṣa is established as a gateway to all phenomena because, having passed completely beyond what is unobstructed, the aggregates, elements, sense sources, and so on, which are taken up in relation to ultimate truth by way of thorough affliction and purification, of the contaminated and the uncontaminated, are not observed. oṃ sruṃ

The letter ta is to be seen as a gateway to all phenomena because it enters the Dharma realm, which accords with suchness. oṃ taṃ

The letter ka is to be made clear as a gateway to all phenomena because it is free from the ripening of karma. oṃ kaṃ

The letter ga is to be seen as a gateway to all phenomena because it is profound and hard to fathom. oṃ gaṃ

Two wheels are set,

Within the central maṇḍala,
Well arranged at the place of the hub,

Most beautiful, filled with anthers.
In the center of its hub
Is the all-pervading lord, the great lord of yoga,
Vairocana of great diligence,
Loving toward all sentient beings.

Garlands are to be arranged,

The goddesses Vajralāsyā and the rest
Should be set out with care.
Draw Vajradhūpā and the rest,
And likewise Rūpavajrā and the rest.
Vajrāṅkuśa and the rest,
The gatekeepers, should be drawn with care.
Abiding in the outer maṇḍala,
Draw Samantabhadra and the rest in order,
With the retinues of their own mudrās,
And the retinues of messengers,
The wrathful ones and the servants,
And likewise the male and female messengers.
The maṇḍala of the ocean
Is a bright maṇḍala of light.

Outside, the outer ones with vajra names
Should be drawn with care in the maṇḍala.
Beginning from the direction of Īśāna,

Brahmā, Viṣṇu, the one named Mahendra,
All the gods, Indra and the rest,
All the mātṛkās and the sisters,
The Four Great Kings and the nāgas,
And likewise all the gods,
And the garuḍas of great vigor
Should be drawn with care.
Having drawn such a maṇḍala,
One should make special offerings.
With flowers, incense, music and the like,
Likewise with food, feasts and the like,
And with various ornaments of garlands,
One should worship the tathāgatas.
By the rite of all the maṇḍalas,
With every offering, in every way,
By the rite of all the wheels,
One should worship all the buddhas.
Then all the disciples are to be brought in,
Especially those who are thoroughly pure,
Who, having generated the mind of awakening,
Follow the training for awakening.
The mantrin who knows the rite should bring them in
And have them make offerings to the Guides.
Through the application of the vajra empowerment
One then confers the empowerment.
Upon a lion throne
Adorned with silk and clusters of flowers,
With a white parasol and great jewels,
And a vast and blazing canopy,
At its right side
The wise one should set them.
Banners of various kinds
And lavish offerings of various cloths,
All of these, at the left side,
Should be set with care.
Having properly set out vases
Filled with all jewels and medicines,
With the empowerment of the vajra name
One should oneself empower him.
The empowerment with the vajra name
The wise one should perform once more.
With the crown empowerment of all,
Empower him again and again.
The Dharma crown empowerment,
The protector of all the buddhas:
Confer the empowerment of varied action,
Which is equal to the dharmadhātu.
The empowerment of the Dharma body,
The lamp of all the buddhas: confer it.
Thus the empowerment of the disciple
The mantrin should perform with special care.
Then, by the rite of the pledges,
Join him to the mantras and mudrās.
Do not despise the master,
And do not disparage any practitioner.
Toward all the conduct of mantra,
Do not transgress it and do not disparage it.
Toward the worldly gods
And all the reasonings of the tenet systems,
Do not disparage them and do not adopt them;
Give up delighting in them.
Abandon all wrongdoing entirely,
And likewise gather all merit.
Of the many foods and feasts,
Do not eat them and do not disparage them.
Through desire the Blessed One is mind;
I am born as the son of all the buddhas.
The peace that has no beginning and no end
Should never be cast aside.
All the buddhas are born from passion,
Likewise born from aversion and delusion.
All phenomena, equal and unequal,
Abiding in the state of the middle:
Having known this very pledge,
Never forsake the awakening of the buddhas.
Do not harm any embodied being.
You must not be miserly,
And you must not be jealous.
Do not abandon the true Dharma.
The mind of awakening is supreme bliss.
Give up drinking liquor,
And completely avoid the wives of others.
Never cast off omniscience.
Never kill a living creature.
Toward all sentient beings, cultivate
Compassion and loving-kindness as well.
Benefit for sentient beings
Should be gathered again and again.
These very pledges,
Which follow and protect all the buddhas,
The wise one, out of kindness to the disciples,
Should teach.
Then the binding of the mudrās
Should be taught in proper sequence.
I shall fully and rightly explain
The specific characteristics of these mudrās.
Press the palms firmly together,
Lovely in the manner of a closed flower;
All the buddha heroes
Proclaim this the lotus palms.
With all the tips well extended,
It is called the lotus mudrā.
Bound firmly, each finger in the spaces between,
That is known as the vajra palms.
All the fingers firmly clasped
Are well bound in the vajra bond.
Extending the fingers and pointing them upward
Invokes the awakening of the buddhas.
Having made the fists inward,
By joining both, it is known
As the mudrā of the Universal Monarch and the others.
It is said to bestow the sublime.
Of all the buddha heroes
This is the pledge mudrā.
With a fist with the ring fingers placed inside,
It is known as the Summit of All.
Joined well in the shape of a vajra,
It is proclaimed the Great Vajra.
From a fist of the little and ring fingers,
Extending the middle fingers well,
And joining the thumbs with the index fingers,
It invokes the Universal Monarch.
Joining the tips of the extended fingers
Is renowned as Sitātapatra.
Join the ring fingers and the thumbs,
And arrange the two middle fingers:
The great mudrā of Tejorāśi
Invokes Tejorāśi himself.
That same, joined in the form of a sword,
Is renowned as Vijaya.
That same is called the Victor,
The tathāgata possessed of the vajra.
By making the fist with its core,
It is the source of the forms of the uṣṇīṣa;
Invincible, it is called by all the buddhas
Going Upward.
Fully joining the vajra bond
Is the source of all the pledges.
Join the tips of the middle fingers supremely,
Extend the little fingers and the thumbs;
With the application of the hook and the arrow,
One should form the "Excellent!"
The middle fingers at the tip are Vajraratna;
Spread open, it is the supreme of supreme splendor.
Join them firmly at the place of the banner,
And join them at the place of laughter.
For the great mudrā of Vajradharma,
Bend the middle fingers like a lotus.
Holding a sword is Mañjughoṣa.
Joined as a wheel is the Universal Monarch.
The great mudrā opened at the mouth
Becomes Vajrabhāṣa.
The great mudrā of the action vajra
Is like a dance, bearing the crossed vajra.
By the application of gathering in all phenomena,
It is known as Vajrarakṣa.
Baring the two fangs
Is called Vajrayakṣa.
Holding the fists, the vajra palms of the fists
Are placed at the heart:
This is renowned as Vajralāsyā.
Fully joined and placed on the crown of the head,
It is called Vajramālā.
Joined palms released at the face
Are proclaimed as Vajragītā.
By the application of spreading and bowing,
That is said to be Vajranṛtyā.
Open the lotus palms at the face,
Join them fully and place them on the crown of the head.
At the heart, the thumbs held level
And fully extended: this is the unguent.

The hands fully extended upward
Become the mudrā of form.
The two hands placed at the passage of the ears
Are said to be the mudrā of sound.
Scent, by the gesture of anointing,
Is called the mudrā of scent.
Cupped hands opened at the face
Are proclaimed the mudrā of taste.
By a swift movement in the sky,
Touch is said to be the mudrā of touch.
Join the two little fingers fully;
From the index fingers in the gesture of the hook,
That same in the shape of a noose,
And in the form of a chain, it is to be known.
The fourth, called the vajra bell,
Is known as the vajra goddess who causes descent.
All the mudrās and the subsidiary seals
The intelligent one should discern for himself.
Join the palms fully and clasp them,
And, bending the two index fingers,
Bind them in the form of a jewel:
This is Samantabhadra's, for the wise one.
From that, bend the tips, tip to tip,
Having meditated in the mind on Maitreya.
Joining the index fingers and inserting them,
It is known in the form of a sword:
This all the buddhas have praised
As the mudrā of Ākāśagarbha.
Then, abiding in the form
Of the weapon that is a sword,
This mudrā is called by all
The mudrā of Viṣkambhin.
By the application of the gathering dance,
Extend the vajra palms fully,
And it is known as lotus-like:
For the wise, it is Padmapāṇi.
That same, in the manner of a closed flower,
Lovely in the form of an utpala:
This is the mudrā of the Lotus Savior,
Renowned as the Lotus Protector.
Arrange it well to resemble a mouth
With bared fangs:
This is the mudrā of Hayagrīva;
Place it near the mouth.
The lotus palms are applied
At the frown of the forehead.
The vajra palms lowered are the great one,
Śrīgarbha, possessed of jewels;
They hold the form of a wish-fulfilling gem.
With the palms well and fully extended,
It is the great mudrā of Akṣayamati,
Determined by all the buddhas.
By the application of the gathering dance,
Place the jewel vajra at the heart:
This is the mudrā of Gaganagañja;
It bestows the very treasury of the sky.
Extend the great vajra well,
Bind the little fingers and thumbs firmly,
And arrange the two hands firmly:
It becomes the mudrā of Sāgaramati.
Raise the vajra palms upward
And join the two index fingers:
This is the mudrā of the Vajra Essence,
As the Vajra Essence has declared.
Having made the fists inward,
Make points of the two index fingers:
This is the mudrā of Mañjuśrī,
Known as the vajra sword.
That same in the form of a spear
Belongs to the wise Mañjughoṣa.
Yamāntaka becomes the spearhead;
It is known as the great spearhead.
Having bent the index fingers,
Place them fully upon the middle fingers;
Being fully in the form of a noose,
It terrifies even Yama.
That same made into a sword
Is said to be Keśinī.
Binding all the fists firmly,
Bend one index finger:
This, as the mudrā of Upakeśinī,
Mañjughoṣa has declared.
Binding the vajra bond upward,
Set the two middle fingers apart,
Make the index fingers in the form of a vajra,
And extend the little fingers and the thumbs:
This is the mudrā of the great Vajradhara,
Which accomplishes every aim.
The index fingers clasped by the thumbs
Are said to be Māmakī.
From the index fingers in the form of a chain,
Make the middle of the middle fingers a vajra:
This is the mudrā of Vajraśṛṅkhalā,
Said to be like a vajra.
The great five-pronged vajra
Is said to be the wrathful Splinter goddess.
Making the supreme tip single-pointed
Is said to be that of the Vajra Point.

Extend both hands well,
And place the fingers in the expanse of the lotus:
It is said to be the Urging Goddess.
From the palms fully joined,
Join the index fingers and the thumbs
In the manner of an eye:
It is said to be the form of Buddhalocanā.
Take up the auspicious cross-legged posture;
The one who knows yoga straightens the body,
With the two hands as if holding a begging bowl:
This is called the meditation mudrā.
This is the mudrā of Vairocana,
Which accomplishes the aims of all the buddhas.
Touching the earth is that of Akṣobhya.
Granting the supreme boon is said to be that of Ratnaketu.
The meditation mudrā is Amitābha's,
Which realizes the meaning of essential nature.
The mudrā of Amoghadarśin,
Itself fearless, grants freedom from fear.
The wise one, abiding in the yogic posture,
Whose self is the nature of all the buddhas,
Resting in equipoise, should bind all the mudrās
Or have them bound.
For the mudrās of the worldly ones,
One hand is fully extended:
This is the trident of Īśāna.
Press the little finger with the thumb:

Place the thumb upon the middle finger.
The vajra staff of Yama:
Join the index finger and the thumb.
That same is the discus of Viṣṇu;
Set out the letters of the wheel.
A skull cup held in one hand
Is rightly said to be that of the mātṛkās.
Two fingers raised as a sword
Belong to the great rākṣasa.
Joining the index finger and the thumb
Is said to be the noose of Varuṇa.
The index and middle fingers
Are extended well, like a banner:
The banner mudrā of Vāyu
Is formed with both hands.

Is called the mudrā of the club;

The great Kubera.

Is rightly said to be that of Brahmā.
Know the stake to be Rudra's;
The goddess's is called the baṭesa.
Gaṇapati's is said to be the trunk.
The moon's is the disk of nectar.

The thousand rays of the sun
Should be shown with both hands,
By forming a disk of light.
Like the vajra weapon of Indra,
This is the seal of all the gods,
Fully sanctioned by all the heroes.
The mudrā of the gandharvas
Is proclaimed to be like singing.
The great mudrā of Nandīśvara
Blazes with the sound of the clay drum;
For the sake of worshiping all the buddhas,
The Great God (Śiva) devised it.
By the gesture of holding the cymbals,
It is to be called that of the kinnaras.
By the gesture of raising the lute,
It is said to be that of Sarasvatī.
Ever sounding the kettledrum
Is called that of the vidyādharas.
The two hands made in the manner of a serpent's hood
Are rightly said to be those of the nāgas.
By the application of the gathering dance,
Likewise of spreading out and drawing together,
And by firmly binding the noose of the hands,
That is called a mudrā.
These the tathāgatas have rightly declared
To be the pledge mudrās.
Through diligent application of action,
These too are called mudrās.
Having released the mudrā,
Contemplate again and again the jewel of the mind.
Nonconceptual, at peace from the very beginning,
The dharmadhātu, equal and unequal:
Having known it to be empty, like space,
The wise one should meditate.
All of these are unborn
And abide like a dream;
They appear like a city of gandharvas.
The world of the living, again and again,
Like a magical illusion,
All beings appear vividly.
In brief, all existence,
Impermanent, suffering and empty,
Having been seen as selfless,
By all the buddhas and heroes:
Like an illusion, a mirage, an echo,
Foam, a bubble on water,
And a reflection is the world;
Thus do the buddhas regard it.
All is like a whirling firebrand,
Just like the circling of a waterwheel,
Like the setting of a dance;
View it as like a double moon.
It is ever like a garland of lightning,
And abides like the humming of bees.
Saṃsāra, intensely aroused,
Abides like the mouth of the mare.
Having known and realized it thus,
The yogin who, by the power of meditation,
Has himself well accomplished the mind:
For him the mudrā is taught.
From the nature of emptiness,
Signlessness and wishlessness,
Through body, speech and mind
The three pledges are established.
Mudrā, mantra and the deity:
These three realities fully abide.
Through the yoga of uniting with oneself,
They abide as a single characteristic.
This the buddhas have declared
To be the sublime mudrā.
The mudrā is free of letters,
Formless and without appearance.
For phenomena that are without entity,
It is said to be the very reality of entities.
The absence of entity is the nature of entity;
The absence of entity itself is also entity.
Fully freed from entity and nonentity,
The wisdom of yoga is supreme bliss.
Without speculation, without appearance,
Without confusion and without letters,
The mind is empty and like space.
When it has become just so,
Then the mudrās and mantras
Are also to be regarded as the deity itself.
Not seeing the mudrā apart from the mantra,
Nor the mantra apart from the mudrā,
The yoga of oneself as the deity
Is known as nondual mantra and mudrā.

From the Great King of Tantras called the Ornament of the Vajra Essence of All the Tathāgatas, the chapter on the stages of mudrā.

Then all the blessed tathāgatas gathered once more and supplicated the great Universal Monarch of all the tathāgatas, the great Lord of Vajra Yoga: "Please generate the family of all the tathāgatas."

Then the Blessed Śākyamuni, having heard the words of their supplication, came forth from the abode of Akaniṣṭha and dwelt in the abode of Tuṣita. There he taught the Dharma to the sons of the gods of the Tuṣita realm, and he looked upon the clan of Śuddhodana. Having gone forth into homelessness, he pressed down upon the seat of awakening and sat there. Having shown his victory over the four māras, he came to sit at the seat of awakening, and, having sat there, he entered into absorption in eighty-four hundred thousand koṭis of samādhis, preceded by the all-radiating samādhi. Having entered into absorption, with the sound of the vajra finger snap of all the tathāgatas he invited all the tathāgatas and spoke this secret mantra:

oṃ sarvva tathāgata vajra samājaḥ ākarṣaya sarvva tathāgatāna hūṃ

Then all the tathāgatas, the great maṇḍalas together with their retinues, duly arrived. Emanating into the dharmadhātu, clouds of vast oceans pervaded all the world systems of the boundless ten directions, and all who dwell in the realm of space came to be arrayed in Jambudvīpa like a pod full of sesame seeds.

Then the Blessed Lord of Vajra Yoga summoned them, drew them in, bound them and brought them under control, and placed them firmly in his own heart. Then the Blessed great Lord of Vajra Yoga, blessing them through nondual union, discerned his own mind with this mantra:

oṃ citta prativedhaṃ karomi

Immediately after that, in the heart of the Blessed Vairocana, from the enjoyment body and the emanation body, the Dharma body, naturally luminous, the precious mind, emerged for all the buddhas, and they caused it to radiate. That very mind is luminous, pervaded by great compassion, characterized by the sameness of all the tathāgatas, free of the turbidity of grasped object and grasping subject, and primordially at peace like space. Turning from that toward great compassion, he gazed upon all the realms of sentient beings. Seeing the world pervaded, through ignorance, by aging and death, he once again set forth the generation of the awakening mind with this mantra:

oṃ bodhi citta utpādayāmi

Immediately after the generation of the awakening mind, he blessed that luminous mind in the hearts of all the tathāgatas to be like a full moon disk, and set it in the hearts of all the tathāgatas.

Having made it stable like a mirror, stainless like perfectly pure space and free of all elaboration, he remained so, and over the great awakening mind he recited this mantra:

oṃ jñāna rasmi visṛja hūṃ

Immediately after that, rays of light of various kinds were sent forth from the moon disk. Having been sent forth, they entered the palace of the dharmadhātu and illuminated every maṇḍala of the knowable. They leveled the mountain peaks of the minds of all sentient beings and brought them to full realization. Then they merged into one, and the great vajra bodies entered the heart of the Blessed One and came to rest in the center of the moon disk.

Then the Blessed great Lord of Vajra Yoga blessed it with this mantra:

oṃ tiṣṭha vajra

Again he caused it to radiate with this mantra:

oṃ sphara vajra

Again he drew it back with this mantra:

oṃ saṅhara vajra

Then all the tathāgatas proclaimed this essence of the wisdom vajra:

oṃ jñāna vajra

Then the Blessed great Lord of Vajra Yoga blessed it with this mantra:

oṃ sarvva tathāgata jñāna vajra hūṃ

Then, having attained the sameness of the body, speech and mind of all the tathāgatas, he blessed it with this mantra:

oṃ vajra ātmako'haṃ

Then all the tathāgatas became stable.

Then the Blessed Vairocana, having looked upon the body, speech and mind of all the tathāgatas, blessed them with this mantra:

oṃ yathā sarvva tathāgata stathā ahaṃ

Then the Blessed One displayed throughout all the realms of sentient beings the body of all the tathāgatas, the cause of accomplishing the aims of all sentient beings, the body that is the very nature of all the buddhas. Then Śakra, Brahmā and the world protectors saw this, and gods and humans raised the cry, "He has attained full awakening; the Blessed One is fully awakened!" Then Brahmā made his request. Turning the wheel of Dharma, he tamed the beings to be trained by each particular means, through which they became irreversible. These are the stages of meditating on the awakening of a buddha.

Then the Blessed Vairocana arose from that samādhi. Having shown the awakening of a buddha and accomplished the aims of all sentient beings, he drew forth a vajra from his heart and placed it in his own hand. Then he sent forth rays of light that illuminated all world systems; they returned and entered the heart of the Blessed One. He blessed them into the body of the great bodhisattva Samantabhadra, complete with every characteristic, adorned with every ornament, empowered with silk and garlands, and blessed to have rays like the color of the moon; and so he remained.

Then, resting upon the moon disk drawn forth from the heart, in front of, behind and beside all the tathāgatas, Vajrasattva, Vajradharma, Vajraratna, Vajrakarma and the others stood arrayed as a garland of great bodhisattvas. Likewise he specially generated Lāsyā and the others and the goddesses of incense and the rest, and he generated the goddesses of form and the rest and the Hook goddess and the others. Then he declared their maṇḍala:

Next I shall rightly explain
The supreme great maṇḍala,
Which all the buddha heroes
Have called the Vajra Essence.
It is square, with four gates,
Adorned with silk and garlands of flowers,
Each embellished with gate wings,
Facades and archways.
In its center is a great throne,
Square on every side;
Upon it is a great lotus,
With anthers, exceedingly beautiful.
In the very center of its hub,
An image of the Buddha is to be set.
All around the Buddha,
All the buddhas are to be set.
In the surroundings of all the buddhas,
Draw the Universal Monarch and the rest.
In all the outer places,
Arrange the sons of the buddhas.
Yellow in color, of great splendor,
The Mahendra maṇḍala blazes brightly;
All are adorned with various ornaments:
Those adorned ones are to be set out.

From the Great Tantra of the Ornament of the Vajra Essence, the rite of the Mahendra maṇḍala.

Then the Blessed Vairocana again declared the great maṇḍala of Varuṇa, the water god:

Next I shall rightly explain
The supreme great maṇḍala.
Because it resembles the wheel of Dharma,
It is called the Wheel of Dharma.
Its second, outer part
Is to be understood as circular.
With many and various lines,
The gates and gate wings are to be laid out.
With archways of various kinds,
It is adorned with silk and garlands of flowers.
In its center is a great lotus,
With anthers, exceedingly beautiful.

Then the Blessed One, the great Lord of Vajra Yoga, taught the extensive rite for the accomplishment of all the tathāgatas.

There is no lunar date and no constellation to observe, and no rite of fasting observance. Having taken up the vows of the observance according to the rite as explained, make a maṇḍala, in whatever way is suitable, in any place that delights the mind. Scatter loose flowers, perfume it with incense, and make offerings to all the tathāgatas with offerings of flowers and so on, as far as one is able. With this mantra confess one's misdeeds, mentally as well:

oṃ sarbbapāpātakāyikabācikamanasodbhūtān rāgadveṣamohamadamānadhiṣṭhena yaṅ kṛrtaṃ tantrekālikaṃ hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ phaṭ phaṭ sarbbapāpāṃ sphoṭayāmi svāhā oṃ sarbbapābāṃ pratideśayāmi svāhā

Immediately after that, one should offer one's own person:

oṃ sarbbatathāgatānān ātmabhāba nirayātayāmi sarbbatathāgataḥ pratigṛhṇaṃtu māṃ svāhā

Then the rejoicing in the merit of all the tathāgatas:

oṃ sarbbatathāgatapuṇyajñānasaṃbhārān anumodayāmi svāhā

Again and again one should generate the awakening mind, with the mantra that is accomplished by its own nature:

oṃ sarbbatathāgatasamantabhadramahābajrasamayaṃ mahābodhicitta utpādayāmi svāhā

Then, immediately after generating the awakening mind, one should imagine oneself as moon-colored, seated upon a moon disc, with the accumulations of merit and wisdom perfected, the lord of the ten grounds, upon a lion throne in the heart of a lotus of great precious jewels:

oṃ buddhohaṃ

With this mantra one should purify oneself:

oṃ svabhābaśuddhāḥ sarbbatathāgatāḥ svabhābaśuddho'haṃ

With this mantra one should settle oneself firmly in the vast expanse of reality, in the radiance of luminosity:

oṃ dharmmadhātusvabhāba ātmako'haṃ

Then one should bless oneself as adorned with the thirty-two marks of a great being, and with this mantra establish oneself in the Dharma body of all the tathāgatas, which wholly transcends every world:

oṃ prabheśvarohaṃ

Likewise, by this sequence, expanding into the great and vast expanse of reality, one should generate the pride, "I myself am the Buddha," make oneself the ally of all beings without exception, and abide preeminently in the vast and luminous expanse of reality. Then, more extensively, one should begin recitation in four sessions. In three sessions, at the appropriate times, with a mind intent upon the syllables, one should recite two million times, reciting eight thousand, or one thousand, each day, until signs are obtained.

Make a maṇḍala with sandalwood, camphor, saffron and the like, and make offerings with all the requisites. Then, on the full moon of the Month of Miracles or of Vaiśākha, light eight thousand butter lamps, sit in the vajra posture, and make offerings to all the tathāgatas and to a stūpa with jasmine flowers or lotus flowers; then recite until one hears the roar of thunder. After that, one should know that the body of the Tathāgata will become manifest to oneself. Then, by completing a hundred thousand fire offerings, all the signs will become clear. By reciting forty million times, all beings, Maheśvara (Śiva) and the rest, will come under one's power.

Then one who wishes to perform the great practice should make stūpas of the Tathāgata, each the size of a tathāgata's span, and recite a thousand times before each stūpa; or else recite seventy million and a hundred thousand times beside a stūpa made of fragrant substances. One should make one stūpa, the size of a sugata's span, each day, until the recitation of seventy million is complete.

Finally, when the light of the blazing stūpa enters the practitioner's body, the gods, from Śakra (Indra), Brahmā and the world guardians up to Maheśvara of the Pure Abodes, will come and rain down flowers. Gods, nāgas, yakṣas, gandharvas, asuras, garuḍas, kinnaras and the rest will also come and sing praises, and all the hell beings, as far as Avīci, the Hell of Ceaseless Torment, will be cooled. The hungry ghosts too will be completely freed from the sufferings of hungry ghosts. Those who suffer will no longer torment sentient beings. Then all the tathāgatas will look upon the practitioner.

After that, the practitioner will have a body like the color of refined gold and the appearance of a sixteen-year-old; will attain samādhi and wisdom; will be endowed with qualities such as complexion, long life, strength, vigor and mastery; will roam the world systems; will be as great as a universal monarch; will be praised by all the buddhas and go upward; will become a king of vidyādharas, a king of the vidyādharas of the great trichiliocosm, surrounded by many hundreds of thousands of koṭis of vidyādharas and surrounded by many koṭis of divine maidens; will travel by miraculous power to the boundless world systems of the ten directions, making fine offerings to all the tathāgatas with clouds of great offerings; and all the siddhas will fall down before his light. Śakra, Brahmā and the world guardians will give him half of their seats. In splendor, form and heroism he will outshine all the vidyādharas. He will also attain, one by one, the grounds of a bodhisattva. He will become skilled in taming all sentient beings. Blessing his life until the final end, he will see all the buddhas come into being. He will surely display the deeds of all the bodhisattvas. All the clouds of offerings will arise. He will become equal to the great bodhisattvas Samantabhadra, Maitreya and Mañjuśrī.

This is the first, called the means of accomplishing the body of all the tathāgatas.

The second means of accomplishment is this. One should make offerings to all the tathāgatas, place bezoar or orpiment in a lotus vessel, and recite until the three kinds of accomplishment appear. One will bring all beings under one's power, become invisible, and hold every kind of knowledge. Having become a vidyādhara, one will have the color of a thousand rising suns and the appearance of a sixteen-year-old, with hair curling and coiled, wearing bracelets, surrounded by many koṭis of divine maidens and surrounded by all the vidyādhara siddhas; one will live until the end of a great eon and become the lord of all the vidyādharas.

If one accomplishes the trident by this sequence, one becomes Maheśvara. If one accomplishes the implement carried for worship, one becomes Brahmā. With the discus one becomes Viṣṇu. If one takes earth from the top of an anthill, makes an elephant of it, mounts it and recites, one becomes Indra. If one makes a disc of the sun and sits upon it, one becomes equal to the sun. With a disc of the moon, the moon likewise. By this very method, whatever particular god's emblem one takes up, one will enjoy that god's riches. Likewise, by this sequence, whatever one sets out to accomplish, whether mercury or an elixir, will be accomplished. If it is not accomplished, one should then perform the practice seven times. By doing it seven times it will certainly be accomplished.

Through the letters one practices awakening,

Without letters and indivisible;

The grounds and the perfections.

The bodhisattva Vajrasattva,

On the pure letter of Mahendra,
Adorned with the dot and the sound,

She steals away every evil.
The third of the first class
Is declared to be the Eye of Space.

What need to mention other malicious ones?

Acala in particular accomplishes these.

Summons all beings.

Abiding on the vajra, in the center
Of the wheel, it paralyzes all beings.

Entering the heart of Acala,

Joined above conduct,

Abides upon the letter of earth;

Will be accomplished in this very life;
Have no doubt about this.

Are distinguished by the divisions of their combination.
The measures of words and speech,

Then, as the blessed ones had assembled once again, they requested of the Blessed One, the great Lord of Vajra Yoga, the maṇḍala of speech. Then, immediately after the words of request offered by the Blessed One Vairocana, for the sake of the complete perfection of the voice of the great Brahmā, the well-proclaimed sound of boundless thunder, a sky treasury (gaganagañja), vast and vajra-like in every way, and for the sake of the individual full realization of the maṇḍala of the wheel of speech of all the tathāgatas, the great maṇḍala called the Accomplishment of the Speech of All Tathāgatas was declared:

Next, what is to be correctly explained:
The great maṇḍala of the wheel of speech
Is round and excellent all around;
It appears like the disk of the moon.
Upon it is a great lotus
Of eight petals, with anthers.
In the very center of its hub
Is the state of the supreme letter;
There one should meditate on one's own accomplishment.
The Lord of Speech, of great light:
At the heart a moon, on it a lotus,
And upon that one should imagine a conch.
In the middle of the conch is the tongue,
Virtuous, of the nature of vajra.
Upon it is a jewel lotus,
Or else an image of the Buddha,
Blazing and pervading to the edge of the palate,
Pouring down a stream of nectar.
The letter ba, turned upside down,
Rests above the buddha;
Its dripping drops of nectar
Should be imagined in their sequence.
For half a month, for three months,
For four months, or for a single month,
Meditating supremely for eight months,
One should accomplish the excellent maṇḍala.
Seated in the cross-legged posture,
The lord of the first yoga is born.
Throughout the whole of the dharmadhātu,
Filling it completely everywhere,
One should meditate on images of the Buddha.
Equal to the dharmadhātu and unequaled,
All the vidyās there are
That follow and protect all the buddhas,
Those mudrās of body, speech and mind,
Supreme in their nature:
Imagine all of them entering
The maṇḍala of one's own body.
Imagine the clear sound of the conch,
Resting on the element of the lotus.
From the drop on the lotus anthers,
The particles whose nature is sound
Reach the throat and become sound;
That sound is the vowel itself, preeminent.
And when it reaches the tongue once more,
It is the letter, supremely virtuous.

The letter of the tongue, blazing greatly,
One after another, in the manner of vajra,
Utterly luminous all around:
Vajra speech is the sacred meaning.
Until its sound fills the whole of space,
The practitioner should recite, condensing it in brief.
Likewise, if sound at the heart,
Or light rays surely radiating on the tongue,
If light rays shining brightly
From the lips or teeth are seen,
Then know that it is accomplished.
Accomplished speech is the sacred aim.
Or else, the supreme great sādhana
Of speech of another deity:
Imagine one's own tongue as having the nature of vajra,
And oneself as Vajrasattva,
Like a blazing lamp.
In the central part of one's own heart
Is the supremely subtle letter,
The letter a, supreme sovereign,
Shining brightly like the sun.
Equal to the culmination of its own class,
Letter merges wholly into letter.
The culmination of subtle equality,
Blazing and like a drop,
Illuminates the maṇḍala of the heart;
Its exceedingly subtle blaze
Reaches as far as the palate.
Again and again it illuminates
All the letters once more,
And the blazing vajra on the tongue,
As though making them like atoms,
Sends forth thousands of blazing vajras
Again and again.
They go out to all the worlds,
Equal to the dharmadhātu and unequaled,
Shining with the nature of letters,
And by themselves they illuminate beings.
Then imagine them coming back again
And entering their own place.
The letters that have gone to all directions
Enter into the mouth.
On the body, the image of the embodied one,
The mudrā of the mind: contemplate it well.
Then the sound and the letters
Go to the navel and abide there.
At the navel is a supreme lotus;
On the lotus a stainless vajra, beautiful;
Upon it a great conch
Abides, radiating intensely.
From it a sound in blazing form
Emerges from the region of the chest;
Then, reaching the throat once more,
It touches the vajra tongue.
In the middle where the jaws join
Is a stainless image of the Buddha,
White and shining like snow or curd,
Like drops of the nectar of the elixir.
The vajra tongue in its entirety
It illuminates again and again.
Reaching the peak of meditative absorption,
It goes back again to the heart itself.
From the drop arises the hooked stroke;
The hooked stroke has a long line.
From the gathering of these four parts,
Know that it is a letter.
From the letter arises sound;
From sound, the nāda; from that, the vowel;
From the vowel it turns into speech.
From letters, collections of parts
Become lines and sentences;
From lines come verses,
And likewise gāthās,
And likewise continuous garlands.
In this way, mantras and vidyā-mantras
Enter fully into one's own heart.
Then the great yoga of recitation:
The wise one, having made it stable,
Should accomplish it through summoning, drawing in,
Binding, and bringing under control.
Through the practice of this recitation
One should be steadfast in every way:
Speech that fulfills every wish is accomplished,
Marvelous and supremely hard to find.
Or else, of another deity:
The single letter of all the protectors
For accomplishing speech, the great secret,
The supremely virtuous sādhana of speech.
The nature of all the buddhas
Is to be meditated on in well-settled equipoise.
At one's own heart, on a moon, is a great vajra,
With light of swirling garlands of flame.
From it, all the treatises
Will certainly be recited.
Through this wisdom of accomplished speech
One attains omniscience.
One recites all the treatises
And also explains their very meaning.
Whatever thing, however slight,
All sentient beings anywhere have thought of,
All of that one knows,
And speaks of it in a mere instant.

From the Ornament of the Vajra Essence, the sādhana of speech is complete.

Is the sādhana of the Universal Monarch.
Draw a wheel of eight spokes,
Or else one of sixteen spokes.
At the hub place the Tathāgata;
Adorn it with a garland of wheels;
At the rim the skilled one
Should set the seven kinds of precious things in alternation.
At its hub, oneself
As the Universal Monarch of great strength,
The great body of all the buddhas,
Supreme, equal to the dharmadhātu.

In a single instant they emerge.
Having illuminated all beings,

The accomplishment of the wheel, of great splendor:
One oneself becomes the Universal Monarch.
Minuteness and greatness, mastery,
Independence, enjoying whatever one desires,

One attains freedom from aging and death,

A precious wheel of gold,
Likewise of silver or of crystal,

Held in one's own hand:

The Universal Monarch, of great splendor,
Adorned with the major marks:

One becomes the Universal Monarch himself.

Wisdom, life, power and vigor,
Or else all the sublime lordships:

What need to mention other accomplishments?

This is the sādhana of the Universal Monarch.

Bearing the thirty-two major marks,

Abides like the dawning of light.
Contemplate his mudrā always,
Abiding well in the form of space,
Boundless to the limits of the dharmadhātu,
Pervading all with garlands of light rays.
Meditate on him and oneself as vajra:
It fully accomplishes the awakening of the buddhas.

It will arise from all things.
Wisdom, life, power and vigor,
The power of going everywhere,
The state of all the buddhas,
The state of all the vajra holders:
Having attained all supreme splendor,
One becomes like Tejorāśi.

oṃ tejorāśi svāhā
This is the heart mantra.
oṃ jvāla tejorāśi hūṃ phaṭ svāhā

Through these pure mantra words,
The wisdom of the sun shines brightly.
One should accomplish the power of going everywhere,
And one will attain the state of a vidyādhara.

This is the sādhana of the secret mantra of Tejorāśi.

The supreme one called the All-Illuminating,
Of three syllables, of great power,

oṃ āḥ hūṃ

Turn the wheel of Dharma.

Pervading the dharmadhātu:
Meditate on it in well-settled equipoise.
Beings, both stable and moving,
All of these arise in dependence.
This is the great mudrā of the Dharma;

oṃ dhuna pātaya cchinda cakreṇa bajriṇi hūṃ

oṃ kleśa aśani hūṃ phaṭ

oṃ bijaye haḥ

oṃ bākye daṃ namaḥ

The samādhi of the speech of the sattva of all the tathāgatas: oṃ vajrabhāṣa

Likewise, in the maṇḍala of Vajra Amogha, the samādhi of manifold action: oṃ vajrakarmma

The samādhi of the perfection of diligence: oṃ vajrarakṣa

The samādhi of the wrath of all the tathāgatas: oṃ vajrayakṣa

The samādhi that binds the body, speech, and mind of all the tathāgatas in a fist: oṃ vajrasandhi

Then the Blessed One Vairocana entered absorption in the samādhi called blessed by the vajradhātu, the commitment of the wisdom mudrā of all the tathāgatas, and drew this forth from his own heart: oṃ vajradhātu hūṃ

His own supreme vidyā: oṃ vajradhātvīśvari hūṃ vajriṇi

Then the Tathāgata Akṣobhya entered absorption in the samādhi called the blessing of the maṇḍala of the wisdom mudrā of the commitment of Vajrasattva of all the tathāgatas, and drew forth from his own heart this supreme vidyā of his own: oṃ akṣobhya aṃ oṃ jñānamuṣṭicittavajre vajriṇi hūṃ

Then the Tathāgata Light Rays entered absorption in the samādhi called the blessing of the maṇḍala of the wisdom mudrā of the commitment of the vajra jewel of all the tathāgatas, and spoke from his own heart this supreme vidyā of his own: oṃ ratnasaṃbhava trāṃ oṃ vajrajñānagarbhasti hūṃ

Then the Tathāgata Amitābha entered absorption in the samādhi called the blessing of the maṇḍala of the wisdom mudrā of the commitment of the vajra Dharma of all the tathāgatas, and spoke from his own heart this supreme vidyā of his own: oṃ amitābha hrīḥ oṃ vajradhāriṇi hūṃ

Then the Tathāgata Subjugator entered absorption in the samādhi called the blessing of the maṇḍala of the wisdom mudrā of the commitment of the vajra action of all the tathāgatas, and spoke this supreme vidyā of his own: oṃ amoghasiddhi aṃ oṃ vajramahābale hūṃ

Then the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Vairocana, gazing upon the domain of all the tathāgatas, spoke this essence that unfolds the sameness of all the tathāgatas: oṃ sarbbatathāgatasamatājñānavajra hūṃ

Then, by the mere speaking of these essences of all the tathāgatas, all the maṇḍalas of all the tathāgatas became visible.

Then Vajrapāṇi, having realized himself as the essence of all the tathāgatas, spoke the essence that unfolds vajra sameness: oṃ vajrasamatājñānabuddha hūṃ

Then, by the mere speaking of this, the essences of all the tathāgatas were seen as Vajrapāṇi.

Then from the heart of Vajrapāṇi this essence that unfolds the sameness of all phenomena was drawn forth: oṃ sarbbadharmmasamatājñānavajra hūṃ

Then, by the mere drawing forth of this, once again in the heart of Vajrapāṇi all the tathāgatas blessed all the maṇḍalas, and they were seen seated in all the maṇḍalas.

Then from the hearts of all the tathāgatas this essence that unfolds the sameness of all the tathāgatas was drawn forth: oṃ sarbbasatvasamatāprapañcabuddha hūṃ

Then, by the mere drawing forth of this, Vajrapāṇi was drawn forth from the hearts of all the tathāgatas, and once again, standing before all the tathāgatas, he sat ready to receive their command:

"Blessed tathāgatas, all of you,
How shall I accomplish this?"

All the tathāgatas said, "Bless the maṇḍalas of all the tathāgatas!"

He replied, "So be it," and the Blessed One Vajrasattva, from the hearts of all the tathāgatas, blessed all the maṇḍalas so that they became stable. He then drew forth the goddesses Vajralāsyā and the rest, and those goddesses likewise took their places at the corners of all the maṇḍalas. At the doors are the female door guardians, and at the junctures, it is taught, are form, sound, smell, taste, and touch.

Their mantras are these: oṃ vajrimeghe ṭāṃ ṭāṃ This is to be placed at the junctures of the wheel. oṃ snehavajriṇi haṃ oṃ śaradvajre aṃ aṃ oṃ vajriṇi kili kili hūṃ

As the offering of great delight to all the tathāgatas, at the corners and junctures of the outer maṇḍala the goddesses Vajra Scent and the rest should be set out, dwelling close to the tathāgatas: oṃ vajragandhe hūṃ oṃ madhuvajriṇi ghuṃ ghuṃ oṃ manodbhavavajriṇi hūṃ oṃ vajriśeśire ho ho

The goddesses of the great play of all the tathāgatas, those of form and the rest, at the junctures of the doors: oṃ vajrirūpe oṃ vajriśapde oṃ vajrigandhe oṃ vajrirase oṃ vajrisparśe They bring delight.

The vajra essence of the supreme joy of all the tathāgatas: oṃ samaya hoḥ surastvaṃ

"Through this vajra essence of great joy and bliss, may all the tathāgatas, wholly pleased, become stable in all the maṇḍalas, and please grant all accomplishments!"

Summoning, and excellent consecration,
And joining each to its place as is fitting:
All the buddhas, wholly delighted,
Bestow every accomplishment, auspicious.

The mantra words are these: oṃ ehyehi bhagavān vajra aṃkuśa jaḥ oṃ praveśaya hūṃ sarbbatathāgata oṃ samaye bandha tiṣṭha bhagavān oṃ tuṣya ho bhagavān vajra āveśa aḥ

These are the activities at the doors of all the maṇḍalas.

In drawing such a maṇḍala, the bodhisattvas Maitreya, Mañjuśrī, Samantabhadra, Avalokiteśvara, and the rest should be set out. Having had the disciples enter by the maṇḍala rite explained before, and having performed the empowerment and so on, one should, together with the disciples, worship all the tathāgatas with every requisite.

There, first of all, the master of all the maṇḍalas, one who knows all the commitments, who has been blessed with all accomplishments by the Blessed One, the great Lord of Vajra Yoga, who has obtained all the empowerments, and who is like the Lord of Vajra Yoga himself, should train, through the wisdom of omniscience, in the commitment mudrās of all the tathāgatas.

Then, as for the binding of the commitment mudrās here: sitting cross-legged and anointing both hands with scents and the like, one should carry out the binding of the mudrās.

Forming the two hands into the vajra bond, the fingers interlaced with each other, and placing the vajra bond in the middle of the crossed legs, is the mudrā of all the tathāgatas. Those of Vajrasattva and the rest are done by applying the sequence of the dance.

Holding that same vajra bond at the waist is the mudrā of Vajrasattva. From the vajra bond, holding the index fingers like a hook is the mudrā of Vajrarāja. That same, applied as though shooting an arrow, is the mudrā of the vajra arrow. From that same, placing it behind while giving the gesture of "Excellent!" is that of Vajrasādhu. These are the mudrās of the awakening mind.

Placing that same vajra bond at the forehead is the mudrā of Vajraratna. From the vajra bond, applying a circle of light is the mudrā of the vajra sun's circle of light. Joining the vajra bond at the crown of the head is the mudrā of the banner. Circling it before the mouth is the mudrā of Vajrahāsa.

Circling that same vajra bond before the mouth is the mudrā of Vajradharma. That same, made at the heart, is the mudrā of the vajra sword. That same, circled about like a wheel, is the mudrā of Vajrahetu. That same, raised at the heart, is the mudrā of Vajrabhāṣa. These are the Dharma mudrās.

Displaying that same vajra bond as the dance itself is the mudrā of manifold action. Placing the two hands at the crown, the heart, and the throat is the mudrā of Vajrarakṣa. Placing the vajra bond at the two cheeks is the mudrā of Vajrayakṣa. Pressing that same vajra bond at the heart is the mudrā of Vajramuṣṭi.

Likewise, by the applications of Lāsyā, Dhūpā, and the rest, one should apply all the mudrās of all the goddesses.

Playful, with flirtatious movement and laughter,
With the moods of grace and of compassion,
The moods of heroism, wrath, and the rest,
And the mood whose very nature is peace:
All the mudrās fully assembled.
If you desire supreme accomplishment,
Either perform the vajra dance yourself,
Or apply the binding of the mudrās
Of all the buddhas, the protectors.
For those who wish to accomplish everything and the rest,
The cross-legged posture of all the buddhas.
Therefore, nondual, the vajra holder:
The upright body of the buddhas,
The vajra holder in graceful application;
The peaceful eyes of the buddhas,
The vajra holder's understood as proud.
The eyes of the buddhas and the vajra eyes
Are to be known from the divisions of samādhi.
The wrathful ones have a turbulent gaze;
The consorts, playfulness itself.
The worldly gods
And evildoers gaze upward.
The gaze that follows after all phenomena
With force is a mudrā.
The hosts, the male and the female messengers
Have gazes of manifold kinds.
Fully endowed with the lip mudrā,
Unwavering and unmoving,
The supreme mudrā of the buddhas
Bestows the awakening of a buddha.
The lips slightly joined,
Without change of expression, it abides beyond compare;
Endowed with the syllables of play,
This is the lip mudrā of the vajra holder.
With changing expression, eyes rolling,
The teeth clenched tight in wrath:
This is declared the wrathful mudrā.
The lip mudrā of beautiful form
Is the lip mudrā of the worldly ones.
Expression breaking up, devoid of form,
With desire very hard to bear,
And with hatred and delusion, in all actions,
Playful, with flirtatious movement,
Moving and radiating out:
This too is to be known as a lip mudrā.
For the goddesses, the messengers, and the hosts as well,
There are these and others, infinite and supreme.
The names of the gazes, the lips, and the rest,
All the mudrās in summary,
Should be known in full by the protectors.
As many as the divisions of the body
And the divisions of the faculties and the rest,
So many are those infinite, supreme mudrās,
Discerned by all the buddhas.
The manifold body of great splendor,
Source of immeasurable qualities,
The supreme mudrā of the buddhas,
Bestows the awakening of a buddha.

From the great Tantra of the Ornament of the Vajra Essence, the great maṇḍala called the Secret Reality of the Great Commitment is complete.

Then the Blessed One Vairocana once again spoke this great maṇḍala, called the Empowerment into the Sameness of All Tathāgatas.

Next, the supreme great maṇḍala
Is to be fully explained.
It is square, with four gates,
Made beautiful with four porticoes,
With manifold elements, inconceivable and supreme,
Adorned with silks and garlands of flowers,
Embellished with vajras and jewels,
And likewise with various lotuses and other things.
In the center of that maṇḍala
Is a lovely image of the Buddha's body,
The Protector with the thirty-two marks,
Adorned with a supreme blaze of light.
His mantra is to be known by its signs:
The first vowel and the second.
All around him, in his surroundings,
Eight wheel-bearers should be drawn.
In the center of that maṇḍala
A wheel blazing all around should be placed.
In the middle of the wheel's hub
The king of lotuses abides.
Lāsyā and the others, and all the buddhas,
Should be drawn just as before.
With all the requisite offerings
One should worship the Tathāgata.
The ritual applications, summoning and the rest,
Are just as in the previous maṇḍala.
For all the activities of the maṇḍala
This rite will come into use.

This is the ritual of the great maṇḍala.

Next, the supreme great maṇḍala
Is to be fully explained,
Called Victory over the Three Worlds,
Renowned as that of the vajra hūṃ maker.
It is square, with four gates,
Made beautiful with four porticoes.
Within it is a wheel,
Like an eightfold maṇḍala.
In its center is the great wrathful one,
Trailokyavijaya, Victor over the Three Worlds,
Scowling, with twisted brows,
His clear eyes greatly blazing,
His mouth slightly baring its fangs,
Laughing aloud and yet wrathful.
With his extended left leg
He tramples Maheśvara, who defied the command.
He is adorned with garlands of buddhas and the rest,
His radiance like sapphire,
Four-armed and three-eyed,
Four-faced and hard to overcome.
A club and a noose are declared;
The first hand is said to hold a vajra,
The second, the noose, with threatening forefinger.
The two hands in the supreme, excellent fist:
The two fists should be carefully formed,
The two little fingers joined evenly,
Interlinked like the links of a chain;
Having set the mudrā well at the heart,
He threatens with the two forefingers.
This is the one called Trailokyavijaya,
The supreme mudrā, hard to overcome.
The mantra carries the essence of his name,
Densely massed in a garland of flames.
In the places of that maṇḍala,
In every part and on all sides,
All the mudrās should be drawn evenly,
Resting alike on moon disks:
Sword, wheel, bow and arrow,
And likewise the axe;
These and others, infinite and supreme:
The mudrās are to be set out in brief.

Having thus arranged the whole maṇḍala of the wrathful one, one should worship with all the requisite offerings. This is the maṇḍala of Trailokyavijaya.

"The four applications of mindfulness are these. With regard to the inner body, the outer body, and the body both outer and inner, one dwells contemplating the body, diligent, possessed of vigilance and mindful, having dispelled worldly attachment and mental unhappiness. One dwells contemplating origination in the body. One dwells contemplating passing away in the body. One dwells without abiding in the body, and grasps at nothing whatsoever in the world. With regard to inner feelings, outer feelings, and feelings both outer and inner; inner mind, outer mind, and mind both outer and inner; inner phenomena, outer phenomena, and phenomena both outer and inner, one dwells contemplating phenomena in phenomena, diligent, possessed of vigilance and mindful, having dispelled worldly attachment and mental unhappiness. One dwells contemplating origination in phenomena. One dwells contemplating passing away in phenomena. One dwells without abiding in phenomena, and grasps at nothing whatsoever in the world. One constantly abides in the Perfection of Wisdom through the equality of body, speech, mind, and phenomena."

oṃ kaṃ oṃ catvāri smṛtyāpasthanānā hūṃ

"The four right efforts are these. So that evil, unwholesome dharmas that have not arisen may not arise, one generates resolve and strives, in a manner that does not apprehend them; one undertakes diligence; one firmly holds the mind; one rightly makes aspirations. For the abandoning of evil, unwholesome dharmas that have arisen, one generates resolve and the rest; and in order to generate wholesome dharmas that have not arisen, one generates resolve, makes effort, undertakes diligence, firmly holds the mind, and rightly makes aspirations. For the sake of the increase, growth, and broadening of wholesome dharmas that have arisen, and for the complete perfection of their cultivation, one generates resolve, makes effort, undertakes diligence, firmly holds the mind, and rightly makes aspirations. One should realize all dharmas as, in the ultimate sense, neither abandoned nor not abandoned." Thus spoke the Blessed One, the great Lord of Vajra Yoga.

oṃ samyaka prahāṇa sarvva tathāgata samatā adhiṣṭha aḥ raḥ hūṃ

This, called the Declaration of the Right Efforts, is a gateway of the Perfection of Wisdom.

"The four bases of miraculous power are these. One cultivates the basis of miraculous power endowed with the samādhi of resolve and the formative force of exertion. One cultivates the bases of miraculous power endowed with the samādhi of diligence, of mind, and of investigation, together with the formative force of exertion. In the Perfection of Wisdom, all dharmas of the gateways of the ultimate and the relative enter into the defining mark of illusion, of illusion." Thus spoke the Blessed One, the Universal Monarch of All Dharmas.

oṃ ṛdhi pāda pāramite labāpara hūṃ hūṃ

This, called the Perfection of the Bases of Miraculous Power, the Wisdom of Equality, is the Perfection of Wisdom.

"The five faculties are the faculty of faith, the faculty of diligence, the faculty of mindfulness, the faculty of samādhi, and the faculty of wisdom." These faculties free of elaboration were spoken by the Blessed One, who has mastery over the equality of all dharmas.

oṃ indriyā babodha prajñe iśeñca nāmanya nāya gatāya āḥ hūṃ

This, called the Equality of the Faculties, is the Perfection of Wisdom.

"The five powers are the power of faith, the power of diligence, the power of mindfulness, the power of samādhi, and the power of wisdom," he said. The Blessed One, who abides in the equality of all dharmas, who has realized all the powers, who is free of all elaboration and pure from the very beginning, spoke this:

oṃ sarvva tathāgatā bala aḥ hūṃ hūṃ baḥ

This, called the Increase of Power, is the Perfection of Wisdom.

"The four abodes of Brahmā are loving-kindness, compassion, joy, and equanimity."

oṃ brahma bihara dhiṣṭhāna gaṃ maṃ druṃ kkhaṃ hūṃ trāṃ

He spoke this Perfection of Wisdom called the Equality of All Dharmas. It is the blessing of the abodes of Brahmā.

"The four analytical knowledges are the analytical knowledge of eloquence, the analytical knowledge of definitive words, the analytical knowledge of meaning, and the analytical knowledge of dharmas." So spoke the Blessed One: "Because it discerns all dharmas as having the defining mark of illusion, it is the Perfection of Wisdom."

oṃ pratisambidaḥ sva hūṃ

The Perfection of Wisdom is equality.

"What are the six recollections? Recollection of the Buddha, recollection of the Dharma, recollection of the Saṅgha, recollection of generosity, recollection of moral discipline, and recollection of the gods. The single recollection of all dharmas is called the emptiness of all dharmas in the ultimate sense." This, called the Gateway of Equality through engaging with characteristics and the absence of characteristics, was spoken by the one who has mastery over samādhi.

oṃ sarvva anusmṛti buddhaṃ saṃ padādiśi hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ

This is the wisdom of the six recollections.

"What are the seven branches of awakening? The branch of awakening that is mindfulness, the branch of awakening that is discernment of dharmas, the branch of awakening that is diligence, the branch of awakening that is joy, the branch of awakening that is pliancy, the branch of awakening that is samādhi, and the branch of awakening that is equanimity." Thus he spoke the Perfection of Wisdom called the Equality of the Branches of Awakening, the Defining Mark of Non-arising.

oṃ sarvvatā bodhyaṃga samatā supratiṣṭhite dhruvaṃ

What is the noble eightfold path? Right view, right thought, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right effort, right mindfulness, and right samādhi. It is the wisdom of omniscience that, entering the great awakening mind, the path that knows no exhaustion, discerns well.

oṃ āryāṣṭhāṃga mārga maṭa

This, called the Equality of the Noble Eightfold Path, is the Perfection of Wisdom.

The three samādhis are emptiness, signlessness, and wishlessness. Among these, what is the samādhi of emptiness? It is the one-pointed mind that realizes all dharmas as having a single characteristic, through the aspect of emptiness, the aspect of hollowness and vacuity, and the aspect of lacking intrinsic nature. This is declared to be the samādhi of emptiness. Among these, what is the samādhi of signlessness? It is the one-pointed mind in the aspect of making nothing into a sign, of the absence of desire, of the absence of cognition, of peace, and of separation. This is called the samādhi of signlessness. Among these, what is the samādhi of wishlessness? It is the one-pointed mind in the aspect of impermanence and the aspect of suffering. That is called the samādhi of wishlessness. Having realized all dharmas, one makes no wish. One does not seek after any god, or anything else besides a god, as one's aim, nor does one wish to make supplication, with regard to any thing whatsoever, even through the gateways of all the samādhis. Through this very Perfection of Wisdom one should come to understand the three gateways of samādhi.

oṃ khasamaḥ sarvva dharmmāḥ khaṃ

Among these, the ten powers are the power of knowing what is possible and what is impossible, the power of knowing the maturation of karma, the power of knowing the various elements, the power of knowing superior and inferior faculties, the power of knowing the various inclinations, the power of discerning the cessation of suffering, the power of knowing affliction and purification, the power of knowing the recollection of former lives, the power of knowing the five superknowledges, and the power of knowing the exhaustion of the defilements. Because it discerns the equality of all the tathāgatas, it is the Perfection of Wisdom.

oṃ daśabala samatā thā

This, called the Equality of the Ten Powers, is the Perfection of Wisdom.

What are the eight liberations? Possessing form, one sees forms: this is the first liberation. With the perception of having no inner form, one sees outer forms: this is the second liberation. Being intent on the beautiful: this is the third liberation. Completely transcending perceptions of form, with perceptions of resistance having subsided, and not attending to perceptions of variety, thinking, "Space is infinite," one attains and dwells in the sphere of infinite space: this is the fourth liberation. Completely transcending the sphere of infinite space in every way, thinking, "Consciousness is infinite," one attains and dwells in the sphere of infinite consciousness: this is the fifth liberation. Completely transcending the sphere of infinite consciousness in every way, thinking, "There is nothing at all," one attains and dwells in the sphere of nothingness: this is the sixth liberation. Completely transcending the sphere of nothingness in every way, one attains and dwells in the sphere of neither perception nor non-perception: this is the seventh liberation. Completely transcending the sphere of neither perception nor non-perception in every way, one attains and dwells in the absorption of the cessation of perception and feeling: this is the eighth liberation. He declared this: all dharmas have the characteristic of freedom from elaboration; they are without affliction and without purification, without meditative concentration and yet not without meditative concentration, without meditative absorption and yet not meditative absorption.

oṃ samanta bhadre sarvva svabhāva biśuddhe rdhaṃ ṣaṃ khaṃ

This, called Free of the Possible and the Impossible, is the Perfection of Wisdom.

What are the four fearlessnesses? When I declare, "I am perfectly and completely awakened," I see no ground at all on which any ascetic, brahmin, god, māra, Brahmā, or any other assembly of beings in the world could challenge me in accordance with Dharma, saying, "You have not fully awakened to these dharmas." Since I see no such ground, I dwell having attained selflessness, having attained freedom from anxiety, and having attained fearlessness. I claim the place of the great leader of the herd. I roar the lion's roar in the midst of the assembly. I set fully turning the wheel of Brahmā, which no ascetic, brahmin, god, māra, Brahmā, or any other assembly of beings whatsoever in the world can turn in accordance with Dharma.

When I declare, "My defilements are exhausted," that any ascetic, brahmin, god, māra, Brahmā, or any other assembly of beings could say to me, "These defilements of yours have not been completely exhausted," and so forth, up to "in accordance with Dharma in the world."

"Those things that I have taught to be obstructive dharmas do not become obstructive dharmas when one relies on them": this should be applied in full.

"The path that I have declared to be the path of the noble ones that leads to deliverance does not lead to deliverance; how could it bring about the true exhaustion of suffering and the true ending of suffering?" Here, seeing no ground on which any ascetic, brahmin, god, māra, Brahmā, or any assembly of beings in the world could challenge in accordance with Dharma, and all the rest, should be stated in full just as before.

Fearlessness is the equality of all dharmas, the characteristic of illusion, because it is free of grasping at "I" and grasping at "mine."

oṃ baiśāradya samatā prāpta a a aṃ aḥ

What are the nine absorptions of the equality of the successive abidings? Secluded from desires, secluded from evil, unwholesome dharmas, with thought and with analysis, possessing the joy and bliss born of seclusion, one attains and dwells in the first meditative concentration. With thought and analysis stilled, inwardly fully clear, the mind's continuum having become unified, without thought, without analysis, possessing the joy and bliss born of samādhi, one attains and dwells in the second meditative concentration. Being free of attachment to joy, one dwells in equanimity, mindful and vigilant, experiencing bliss with the body; this the noble ones describe as "equanimous, mindful, dwelling in bliss." Without joy, one attains and dwells in the third meditative concentration. Having abandoned bliss and abandoned suffering as well, mental happiness and mental unhappiness having already subsided, with neither bliss nor suffering, with equanimity and mindfulness completely pure, one attains and dwells in the fourth meditative concentration. Completely transcending perceptions of form in every way, with perceptions of resistance having subsided, and not attending to perceptions of variety, thinking, "Space is infinite," one attains and dwells in the sphere of infinite space. Completely transcending the sphere of infinite space in every way, thinking, "Consciousness is infinite," one attains and dwells in the sphere of infinite consciousness. Completely transcending the sphere of infinite consciousness in every way, thinking, "There is nothing at all," one attains and dwells in the sphere of nothingness. Completely transcending the sphere of nothingness in every way, one attains and dwells in the sphere of neither perception nor non-perception. Completely transcending the sphere of neither perception nor non-perception in every way, one attains and dwells in the absorption of the cessation of perception and feeling. Having attained the eight liberations and equality, one does not conceive of things as existent, nor conceive of them as nonexistent; one realizes all dharmas as nondual. This is the equality of the nine absorptions of the successive abidings.

oṃ anantaḥ bimokṣa mukhe śuṃ

This, called the Equality of the Liberations of the Successive Abidings of All Things, is the Perfection of Wisdom.

The calves are like those of the eṇeya antelope: the Blessed One's calves are rounded, like those of the eṇeya antelope. His proportions are like those of the nyagrodha tree: the height and the breadth of the great being are equal. The head has an uṣṇīṣa: his head is rounded and covered above by an uṣṇīṣa like a parasol, and the uṣṇīṣa is very high. The tongue is long: he touches as far as the rims of his ears with the tip of his tongue, and with his tongue he covers his forehead up to the hairline. He has the voice of Brahmā: in an assembly of many thousands his voice is heard outside just as it is within, and it is as sweet as that of the kalaviṅka bird. The jaws are like a lion's: his jaws are rounded like those of a lion. The teeth are white: his teeth have the color of conch, of kunda flowers, and of the moon. The teeth are even: his teeth have tips that are neither too low, nor too high, nor uneven. The teeth are well set: his teeth have no gaps. He has forty teeth: twenty teeth below and twenty above. The eyes are deep blue: the blue of his eyes is perfectly pure, because it is unmixed with red lines, and the white is perfectly pure, white alone. Through the four means of attracting disciples, namely Dharma, material things, consistency of aims, and acting for others' aims, he benefits beings, and having benefited them, he establishes them in great awakening.

Through the equality of the thirty-two marks of a great being, all dharmas are equal, utterly equal. The marks of the tathāgatas are to be regarded as accomplished, but this is not the accomplishment of signs. All the tathāgatas, awakening, and all dharmas have a single characteristic; that is, they have the characteristic of space, the characteristic of emptiness, the characteristic of peace.

Translator's notes

The long series of epithets beginning "the awakening mind, the unfailing one, the one who gathers all in" appears to allude in sequence to the sixteen vajra bodhisattvas of the Vajradhātu maṇḍala (Vajrasattva, Vajrarāja, Vajrarāga, Vajrasādhu, Vajraratna, Vajrateja, Vajraketu, Vajrahāsa, Vajradharma, Vajratīkṣṇa, Vajrahetu, Vajrabhāṣa, Vajrakarma, Vajrarakṣa, Vajrayakṣa, Vajrasandhi), followed by further epithets. They are translated as descriptive epithets. "Great king, radiant one, banner" is written without separators in the Tibetan and could also be read as a single epithet. The closing sentence is ambiguous: it may instead mean that all the tathāgatas, having consecrated the Blessed One as the Great Lord of Vajra Yoga, abided throughout the directions and intermediate directions. The world system "arrayed with the ornament of the essence of the flower ground" corresponds to the Kusumatalagarbhavyūhālaṃkāra world system familiar from the Avataṃsaka; it is rendered descriptively. The word rendered "impediments" (chod pa) is unclear as written and is translated tentatively. The litany of purified states closely parallels the opening of the Adhyardhaśatikā Prajñāpāramitā, but the items are given as they stand in this text. The phrase rendered "having taken up the power of taming" is terse in the Tibetan; the point, as in the parallel Adhyardhaśatikā, is that one disciplined by this teaching does not fall to the lower realms even after such an act. The third verse is terse; "one tames oneself" renders rang 'dul byed, which might also mean that the bodhisattva tames them naturally, of his own accord, throughout existence. The text reads ཕྱ་ན་རྡོ་རྗེ, which makes no sense; it has been read as ཕྱག་ན་རྡོ་རྗེ, Vajrapāṇi. The line rendered "there comes the maṇḍala of nectar" (དཀྱིལ་འཁོར་བདུད་རྩི་ཡི་ནི་འོང་) is terse and its construction uncertain. The five uṣṇīṣa deities are given their standard Sanskrit names; the Tibetan list (White Umbrella, Uṣṇīṣa of the Victor, Mass of Splendor, Radiating, Victorious) corresponds to Sitātapatra, Jayoṣṇīṣa, Tejorāśi, Vikiraṇa and Vijaya.
