About this text

The Sequence of the Fire-Offering Rite, in Tibetan Jinsek gi Lé kyi Rimpa, is a short ritual manual for performing the fire offering, or homa, within the practice of the deity Hevajra; no Sanskrit title is given in the source. Opening with homage to the Blessed One Hevajra, it gives precise measurements, shapes, colors, emblems, directions, and times of day for the hearths used in the four kinds of rite: pacification, increase, subjugation, and the fierce rite. It goes on to list the offering substances, kindling sticks, and firewood suited to each, and then sets out the ritual itself, from kindling the fire and visualizing the fire god to inviting the wisdom beings, offering each substance with its own mantra, and concluding with the disciples' rejoicing and the final offerings. Its colophon attributes it only to a glorious lord of yogins, without a clearly identifiable personal name. The work is Toh 1303 in the Tengyur, where it belongs to the tantra commentaries, among the ritual texts of the Hevajra cycle.

Translation

Here is contained the Sequence of the Fire-Offering Rite.

Homage to the Blessed One Hevajra.

In the sequence of the fire-offering rite, before conferring empowerment, one performs the rite of increase. For a hearth one cubit in measure, dig down half a cubit. Spread it four finger-widths outward. Encircle the rim with four finger-widths, and make it four finger-widths in breadth and in height. Leaving four finger-widths outside that, lay down a garland of lotuses. On the central rim lay down a vajra fence. Overall it is square, laid out with an outer breadth of three cubits. As for the principal's hand emblems placed inside the hearth, make them of clay or the like, eight finger-widths across and one finger-width high: in the center a jewel, in the east a vajra, in the south a wheel, in the west a lotus, and in the north a sword. Paint it yellow. For the rite of increase, face east.

The hearth for pacification is made round, one cubit across, and dug half a cubit deep. Its rim should accord with that for increase and the rest. On the central rim lay down a vajra garland, and outside that lay down lotuses. As for the principal's hand emblems placed in the hearth: in the center a vajra, in the east a wheel, in the south a jewel, in the west a lotus, and in the north a sword; make them eight finger-widths across. Paint it white. The master faces north.

For the rite of subjugation the hearth is a half-moon. Lay out the length of the half-moon at forty finger-widths, the rim at fully forty finger-widths, and dig it ten finger-widths deep. The rim is as in general. On the rim lay down a vajra fence, and in the outer ring lay down a ring of fire mountains. Paint it red, smearing it with red sandalwood. In the center lay down the principal's hand emblem; the lotus and the outer parts are as in general. For subjugation and for expulsion, face west.

In the sequence of the fierce rite the hearth is triangular, twenty finger-widths on each side. Spread the rim three finger-widths outward, encircle the rim with three finger-widths, make it three finger-widths in breadth, and outside that lay down three finger-widths. On the rim lay down a three-pronged vajra, and in the ring outside that lay down fierce hand emblems such as skull cups, bow and arrow, and trident. As the principal's hand emblem in the center, a sword. The rest is to be learned from the characteristics of the rite and from the guru's own mouth. Paint it black. Face south.

Increase is performed in the morning, pacification at midday, subjugation at sunset, and the fierce rite at midnight. That is the sequence of the fire hearth.

In the sequence of the offering substances and the substances to be burned: to the left of the mantra master place a conch bowl filled with scent and flowers. Place a vessel for the offering water, of gold or the like according to the particular rite, filled with water; if that is not to hand, one made of earth will also do. Place the filling ladle, the pouring ladle, and the butter in front. To the right, in a copper basin filled with scented water with kuśa grass set in it, place the vessel for pro kṣa, the sprinkling. Place also one action vase. Place also the substances to be burned, such as sesame. As for the sequence of substances: for increase, curd; for pacification, sesame; for subjugation, red utpala flowers; for the fierce rite, thorny wood; the rest follow in order. Butter comes before all, and at the end there is butter again. That is the sequence of arranging the substances.

As for blessing the substances: place a vajra over all the basins, and by reciting the mantra of the three realities, clear away all the conceptions of all the worlds, and imagine those very substances as excellent.

In the sequence of the kindling sticks: the kindling sticks for pacification come from milky trees, fresh and with their tips intact, a finger in length and as thick as a finger joint, soaked in honey, milk, and water of white sandalwood; offer them one-pointedly for the rite of pacification. The kindling sticks for increase are a cubit in length, their thickness as for pacification, taken from the branches and the middle part, soaked in curd, butter, and yellow scent; offer them one-pointedly for the rite of increase. The kindling sticks for subjugation are five finger-widths long, with leaves, and a finger joint thick, soaked in mustard oil, nectar, and the like; offer them for the rite of subjugation. The fierce kindling sticks are four finger-widths long, from a tree with sharp points, most auspicious for the rite of killing. Soak human bone, charcoal from a charnel ground, great ash, bitter salt, and pungent substances in water, and with intense concentration offer them for the rite of sorcery. That is the sequence of the kindling sticks.

In the sequence of the firewood: use palāśa, nyagrodha, and the like. If there are none, use the dry wood of milky trees, and make it accord with the rite.

Then the master, having completed the samādhi of the chosen deity, performs pro kṣa ṇaṃ, the sprinkling, with kuśa grass. Then perform añcamānaṃ argha vidhi, the offering of the five kinds of offerings, and the offering of the inner mudrā, and prostrate. Then with kuśa grass perform pro kṣa ṇaṃ on everything.

Then stack the wood on a new vessel of iron or the like, take fire made by friction or the like, fan it with a fan, and kindle the fire. The mantra for kindling it is: oṃ j la jva la hūṃ phaṭ. Then, when it is free of smoke and blazing well, pour it onto the wood stacked in the hearth. That is the sequence of kindling the fire.

Then place two medium bundles of kuśa grass with their tips facing east, and set them likewise to the south, the west, and the north. The mantra for spreading the kuśa: oṃ vajra satva ā uṣe svāhā. That is the sequence of spreading the kuśa.

Then the master, endowed with the meditation of the chosen deity, imagines inside the hearth a variegated lotus of eight petals, and in its center, by means of the five manifest awakenings, meditates on Hevajra, father and mother. By the power of the great passion that arises they melt and remain like quicksilver, and that becomes the two syllables hūṃ and jaṃ. From the complete transformation of the two syllables the fire god is generated as father and mother. The fire god has three faces and six arms, is pot-bellied, and has a topknot of matted hair; he holds the implements for the offering and embraces a wisdom consort like himself. His middle right hand holds a gundhe, and the lowest grants boons. His middle left hand holds grain, and the lowest a rosary. His body color accords with the particular rite. In the heart of the one so imagined, upon a variegated lotus, is a triangle of fire; upon it imagine bhrūṃ. Imagine jaṃ. On the tongue imagine raṃ. That is the sequence of meditating on the fire god as the pledge being. For him too perform the pro kṣa ṇaṃ and the rest, up to the dance.

Then the wisdom being is invited: the master, performing the dance, invites it with jaḥ and the hook mudrā. Then draw it also from the space in front and set it at the mouth of the hearth. Then perform the prokṣaṇaṃ, the añcamānaṃ, and the rest, up to the prostration. Then again perform the three, the prokṣaṇaṃ and the rest. Performing the dance, with the noose dissolve it so that the two are not separate; bind it with the chain; and please it with the bell. Then again perform the three, the prokṣaṇaṃ and the rest.

Then in each of the two ladles imagine a hūṃ, the heart syllable of the chosen deity. Then bring the two elbows together and offer seven ladlefuls of butter: oṃ namaḥ samanta buddhānāṃ agnaye svāhā purṇa titi mantra oṃ agnaye puṣṭiṃ kuru svāhā. Then offering should be made with the pouring ladle, and the filling ladle is set down.

oṃ sarva saṃpade svāhā is for curd and porridge. Then oṃ sarva pāpaṃ dahana vajrāya amuka sarva pāpaṃ daha svāhā is for sesame. oṃ vajra āyuṣe svāhā is for dūrvā grass. oṃ vajra puṣṭaye svāhā is for unbroken rice. oṃ apratihata vajrāya svāhā is the mantra for kuśa grass. oṃ vajra vijāya svāhā is for puffed rice. mahā vegāya svāhā is for barley. oṃ mahā balāya svāhā is for cooked rice. oṃ vajra ghasmariye svāhā is for wheat.

For the kindling sticks: oṃ vajra yajñāya svāhā is for the udumbara. oṃ bodhi prikṣāya svāhā is for the bodhi tree. oṃ vajra rtatāya svāhā is for the nyagrodha. oṃ vajra sahakarāya svāhā is for the mango.

Then again offer full filling ladles of butter, up to a thousand. Then again perform the prokṣaṇaṃ and the rest. When all of that is complete, hold the vajra at the hip, ring the bell, and recite the verse "Existence is pure by its intrinsic nature." Then praise the fire god and prostrate. Then, with the water of the action vase, sprinkle the hearth three times with kuśa grass and purify it.

Then imagine in the heart of the fire god the circle of the deities of the maṇḍala, and imagine the fire god's mouth as the opening for the oblations. Then, performing the dance, invite the wisdom beings with jaḥ and request them to be seated in the space in front. When the prokṣaṇaṃ and all the rest are complete, again with the four mudrās summon them, draw them in, bind them, and delight them, and dissolve them so that the two are not separate. Then again perform the prokṣaṇaṃ and the rest, up to the prostration. Then offer ladlefuls of butter, up to twenty-one. Then offer to the deities the sequence of substances, curd and the rest. Again offer fire offerings of butter, up to a hundred and eight. Again perform the prokṣaṇaṃ and the rest. Hold the vajra at the hip, ring the bell, recite the verse "Existence is pure by its intrinsic nature," and make praises. Then, with the dance, prostrate to the deities of the maṇḍala. With oṃ vajra muḥs request them to depart, and ask their forbearance.

Then offer to the fire god whatever substances remain. With oṃ vajra agnaye svāhā offer with the pouring ladle until all the substances are used up.

Then all the disciples, holding flowers in their hands, rejoice: the master fills the filling ladle with butter, all the disciples put their flowers into it, and the master too offers it, saying vajra agnaye svāhā. The disciples too should rejoice, saying, "May it increase!" or "May it be pacified!" If it is pacification, they rejoice in a hundred and eight fire offerings of butter with the ru ru sphu ru mantra.

In the sequence of the subsequent offerings: with the prokṣaṇas one purifies in general. With the añcamā one takes flowers between thumb and finger and offers them to the face, the heart, and the hands. The argha is offered in front three times, by the palmful, from the conch bowl. For the pādyaṃ, take flowers from the conch bowl between thumb and finger, lower them so that they touch the foot of the hearth, and offer cooling water for the mouth three times. The action vase is for purifying. Liquid substances are offered to the mouth. The sticks are offered to the light. Rice and the like are offered to the hands. Scent is offered to the heart. Flowers are offered to the head. Incense is offered to the nose. Scented water is offered to the feet. The argha and the lamps are offered in front. The reality of the fire is to be learned from the guru's own mouth.

To please the deities,
To make the splendor of the mantras arise,
And to burn away misdeeds,
The fire offering is even more auspicious than recitation.

This explanation by the glorious venerable lord of yogins is complete.

Translator's notes

The thickness measure chag (gang) is uncertain and is rendered "a finger joint". In the description of the fire god, the implement written gundhe and the item ze 'bru, rendered "grain", are uncertain; "grants boons" reads mchod sbyin as a slip for mchog sbyin, the boon-granting gesture; and the upper hands are not described in the text as it stands. "Bring the two elbows together" renders gru mo gnyis sprad, apparently the posture for holding the ladles. "Puffed rice" for 'bras kyi lag pan is uncertain. "Flowers are offered to the head" reads me tog dbul dbul as a slip for me tog dbu la dbul. The closing line names its author only by the epithets "glorious lord of yogins" with the honorific -pāda; no personal name can be identified with confidence.
