About this text

The Dhāraṇī Called the Essence of the Unobstructed Wish-Fulfilling Wheel, the Secret Treasury of the Noble Avalokiteśvara is known in Tibetan by a title beginning Phakpa Chenrezik Wangchuk gi Sangwé Dzö Tokpa Mepé Yishin, and it belongs to the textual tradition centered on Avalokiteśvara's wish-fulfilling wheel. It is a dhāraṇī text, a scripture centered on a sacred incantation. It opens on the Mountain of the Sages, where the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara rises before the Buddha and a great assembly of bodhisattvas and asks permission to teach a great dhāraṇī called the Wheel of the Sandalwood Essence of Many Great Lotuses, said to bring to full accomplishment all that a practitioner wishes for and undertakes. The work thus presents the compassionate bodhisattva as the revealer of a potent spell, empowered by the Buddha's blessing, for fulfilling wishes and aims. It is Toh 898 in the Kangyur, where it is found among the collected dhāraṇī texts.

Translation

The Dhāraṇī Called the Essence of the Unobstructed Wish-Fulfilling Wheel, the Secret Treasury of the Noble Avalokiteśvara

Homage to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas.

Thus did I hear at one time. The Blessed One was dwelling on the Mountain of the Sages together with a great assembly of bodhisattva great beings. Then the bodhisattva great being Avalokiteśvara rose from his seat, arranged his robes, knelt with his knee on the ground, joined his palms, and said this to the Blessed One:

"Blessed One, I have a great dhāraṇī called the Wheel of the Sandalwood Essence of Many Great Lotuses, which brings to full accomplishment all that one wishes for and undertakes. If the Tathāgata, in his great compassion, permits me to explain it, then through the power of the Blessed One's blessing I wish to explain it in order to benefit all sentient beings. And why? Blessed One, because this dhāraṇī, endowed with the power of great splendor, fulfills every wish, just like a great precious wish-fulfilling jewel or a wish-fulfilling tree."

Then the Blessed One praised the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara, saying, "Excellent, excellent!" and continued: "Since you have set out in this way for the sake of sentient beings out of loving kindness, I grant you permission to explain this unobstructed dhāraṇī."

When the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara had thus been given permission by the Blessed One's command, he rose, joined his palms, paid homage, and sat down again on his seat. With a one-pointed mind he gazed at the gathered assembly, and with a mind endowed with great compassion he spoke this mantra:

namo ratna trayāya nama āryābalokiteśvarāya bodhisatvāya mahāsatvāya mahākāruṇikāya tadyathā oṃ cakrapati cintāmaṇi mahāpadme ru ru tiṣṭhate vajrabhara ākarāya hūṃ svāhā

This is the body mantra.

oṃ padme cintāmaṇi mahāvajrabhara hūṃ

This is the heart mantra.

oṃ bharata padme hūṃ

This is the innermost heart mantra.

At that time, when the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara had spoken this dhāraṇī of the Wish-Fulfilling Wheel, the great earth shook violently in six ways. The dwellings of the gods, nāgas, yakṣas, gandharvas, asuras, garuḍas, kinnaras, mahoragas, and others also shook violently. The king of the māras and the retinue of the māras were utterly terrified, and great fear arose in them. The celestial palace of the king of the māras blazed with fire. The yakṣas and all the various other fierce spirits and demons, panic-stricken and terrified, covered their faces and fell to the ground. All the gates of the hells were thrown open, and the suffering beings were set free and experienced the supreme bliss of the gods.

At that time the gods also sent down a rain of precious flowers and of various precious ornaments and articles. Divine music, too, resounded in the sky with many sounds, as an offering to the Tathāgata.

Then the Blessed One, with the voice of Brahmā, spoke in verse to the bodhisattva great being Avalokiteśvara, praising him: "Son of noble family, excellent, excellent! Because, out of loving kindness for sentient beings, you have explained this dhāraṇī, sentient beings will obtain great benefit."

Then the Blessed One again spoke to the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara, saying, "Son of noble family, for the sake of sentient beings, explain the rite of this dhāraṇī, the great king of miraculous power!"

Then the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara said this to the Blessed One:

"If any son of noble family or daughter of noble family, any monk or nun, layman or laywoman, boy or young girl, wishes to experience its ripening directly in this very life, and if, day and night, with a one-pointed mind and earnest diligence, they keep this dhāraṇī without forgetting it, and, without choosing the day or the hour, whether they are clean or unclean, recite it steadily, all their undertakings will bear fruit.

"Whatever it may be, if there is some aim one undertakes and pursues, reciting it one hundred and eight times will accomplish many hundreds of thousands of aims. Therefore there is no other vidyā-mantra whatsoever that can compare with this dhāraṇī of the Wish-Fulfilling Wheel. And why? Because it utterly destroys the evil karma of the past and present, and heavy obscurations.

"For whoever recites this dhāraṇī, even those destined to fall into the Avīci hell will be freed, and even sins such as the five deeds of immediate retribution will be purified. What need, then, to speak of evil karma, grahas and obstructors, and all other ailments?

"Those who suffer from fevers of the one-day, two-day, three-day, or four-day kind, or who are afflicted day and night, or who are ill from wind, bile, phlegm, and the like, if this vidyā-mantra is recited for them, all will be cleared and they will be healed.

"Whoever has been struck by poison or sorcery, or suffers from ulcers, itching sores, leprosy, blisters, wind disease, madness, or pain in the brain, or whose ears, nose, lips, tongue, throat, mouth, face, crown, brain, chest, ribs, heart, belly, waist, back, legs, hands, and so on are all in pain, it heals them all. However many ailments of the body there may be, it heals them all.

"Nor will yakṣas, rākṣasas, vināyakas, the evil Māra, or demonic grahas do them harm. Nor will weapons, armies, water, fire, wind, rain, or fierce hail bring them fear. Nor will enemies, bandits, fierce kings, or fierce thieves do them harm.

"They will not die an untimely death. They will not dream bad dreams. Nor will lizards, snakes, scorpions, crawling insects, centipedes, savage and vicious beasts of prey, lions, tigers, or wolves do them harm. Whenever they fight at the front line of battle, they will be victorious everywhere. Even if they become embroiled in disputes over affairs of state, these will be settled. If one recites this dhāraṇī even once, all of the things taught above will be accomplished just as one wishes.

"If anyone recites this dhāraṇī one hundred and eight times each day, the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara will say to them, 'Child of noble family, do not be afraid! What do you wish for, and what do you seek? I shall give you everything.' The Tathāgata Amitāyus will also reveal his body to them directly. Just as it is taught in the sūtra, they will also see the features of the array of the world realm Sukhāvatī. They will also see the hosts of bodhisattvas of the world realm Sukhāvatī. They will also see all the buddhas of the ten directions. They will also see Mount Potala, the abode of the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara. Their own bodies will become wholly pure, and so kings and ministers will always honor and serve them. Many people will love and honor them. In all their births they will not be born from a mother's womb, but will be born upon a lotus. They will be endowed with supreme adornment. In all their births they will remember their former lives. From now until they attain perfect and complete awakening, they will never fall into the lower realms, and will always be born in the presence of the buddhas. This is called the dhāraṇī that destroys all evil deeds."

This is the first chapter, on the dhāraṇī that destroys all evil.

Then the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara, out of compassion for sentient beings, again taught the secret rite of the heart dhāraṇī of the Wish-Fulfilling Wheel: "If anyone at all has a cherished aim to be accomplished, everything will be settled of itself. That is of two kinds, namely worldly wealth and wealth beyond the world. Of these, worldly wealth is precious things such as gold and silver. Wealth beyond the world is being endowed with the twofold array of merit and wisdom; great happiness of body and mind; being loved and honored by many people; protecting all suffering sentient beings; having expanded the mind of loving-kindness, bestowing happiness on those who possess understanding; the increase of the necessities of life; and constant power and strength.

"This domain of the secret treasury should not be told to others. Whoever wishes to obtain the supreme accomplishment of my dhāraṇī of the Wish-Fulfilling Wheel should recite it with a one-pointed mind, at all times and in all places, whether pure or impure. Since there is no fault at all in reciting it constantly, one should complete the required number of recitations, and then, in addition, utter the name of the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara, the name of the dhāraṇī of the Wish-Fulfilling Wheel, and the name of the person concerned. If one wishes to attend upon and win the friendship of a king, a prince, a queen, a minister, a princess, a brahmin, a kṣatriya, a śūdra, a caṇḍāla, a man, a woman, a boy, a young girl, or any of the various non-Buddhists (tīrthikas), one should utter their names and always complete the number of recitations at dawn.

"If, seeking the supreme accomplishment, one wishes to attend upon a king, one should recite one thousand and eight times at dawn every day for seven days, and one will meet him. If one wishes to attend upon a queen, one should recite nine hundred times. If one wishes to attend upon a prince, one should recite eight hundred times. If one wishes to see ministers, one should recite seven hundred times. If one wishes to attend upon a princess, one should recite six hundred times. If one wishes to attend upon a brahmin, one should recite five hundred times. If one wishes to attend upon a kṣatriya, one should recite four hundred times. For a śūdra it is three hundred. For a caṇḍāla it is two hundred. For monks and nuns it is one hundred times. For laymen and laywomen it is ninety times. For young boys and young girls it is sixty times. This is called the rite of attending upon and winning friendship, and it brings about wealth, servants, horses, and every comfort in abundance.

"If anyone wishes to meet a dear and beloved friend who lives far away, that friend will come like the wind, just as they had in mind. Whatever one wishes to do, once the number of recitations is complete, those aims will be accomplished. If one wishes to see the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara, by reciting one thousand and eight times one will see his true body, and he will fulfill all one's wishes. If one wishes to see Vajrapāṇi, by reciting ten thousand times he will show his body directly, cherish that person as a father loves his son, and grant the wishes of their heart. If one wishes to see the Buddha together with a great gathered assembly, by reciting thirteen thousand times one will see them accordingly. If one recites without interruption for seven days and nights, the vidyādharas will appear directly, each will grant that person whatever power their own mantras possess, and they will protect them completely. On the seventh day, the lords of the great trichiliocosm and Śakra (Indra), lord of the gods, will come with their retinues and grant that person's wishes. If one performs the rite of recitation as taught above, one's wishes will be completely fulfilled. This is called the rite, from the secret treasury of the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara, by which all become loving and devoted."

This concludes the second chapter, the chapter on the rite by which all become loving and devoted, from the Secret Treasury of the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara.

Then the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara, once more out of compassion for sentient beings, taught a rite. In it one prepares a medicine that makes those who see one loving and joyful, and if one goes about carrying it on one's body, one attains supreme accomplishment and all one's wishes are fulfilled just as one desires.

Take bezoar, white sandalwood, saffron, camphor, musk, nutmeg, clove, cardamom, lotus, blue utpala, and gold leaf, in equal parts. Pound them and knead them with white honey. Recite the earlier mantra one thousand and eight times. Then, if one fumigates the body with it, or fumigates one's clothes with it, or anoints the eyes with it, or makes a mark with it on the forehead, or smears it on the body, then kings, queens, ministers, servants, men, women and others will become loving and joyful. They will honor the Dharma of the path and give rise to the mind set on awakening. They will give the flesh of their own bodies and their wealth without stinginess and will serve one. It is beyond description. Just as everyone delights in seeing the sun and moon, so the aims one strives for will come about and be accomplished.

Whoever carries this medicine, the obscurations of misdeeds will melt away, and that person will be freed from all obstructors. Such a person will be freed from seizure by force through a king's power, from being swept away by water, from being burned by fire, from weapons of every kind, from poison, and from all the afflictions of bondage. If one performs this rite of preparing the agada medicine with heartfelt resolve, all will become loving and joyful. Having fumigated with this medicine, one should give it out freely, as a loan. This is called the rite of the medicine by which others become loving and joyful, taught by Avalokiteśvara.

This concludes the third chapter, the chapter on the rite of preparing the agada medicine by which others become loving and joyful, from the dhāraṇī of the Wish-Fulfilling Wheel of the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara.

The bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara, once more out of compassion for sentient beings, taught the rite of the medicine held in the mouth, which makes one pleasing to all and makes them joyful.

Grind camphor, musk and saffron finely. When they have been mixed with bezoar, recite each of the three kinds of mantra taught above one thousand and eight times. Knead the mixture with pure water and make pills the size of the fruit of the hollow-crowned tree. Again recite the three earlier mantras one hundred and eight times and dry the pills in the shade, taking care that neither sun nor wind touches them. Over each pill, again recite the three kinds of earlier mantra seven times apiece.

If one holds one pill in the mouth and then speaks to a king, to ministers and attendants, or to great gatherings of people, they will pay one honor and, without stinginess with their wealth and jewels, will grant whatever one wishes. Whatever words one speaks, all will believe and heed them. And if one gives a command, all will heed it and carry it out. While holding the medicine in the mouth, one should always recite the mantra, and Avalokiteśvara will bring one's aspirations to fulfillment.

Before an image of the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara, make with scented water a square maṇḍala measuring four cubits in length and width all around. Inside the maṇḍala scatter whatever flowers of trees and plants of every kind are available. Burn white sandalwood, take the pills of medicine made earlier, and place them inside the maṇḍala. Plant four victory banners, and hang a curtain and a white canopy. On the maṇḍala also draw four white lotuses. After making offerings to the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara, one should recite the heart mantra and the mantra of the heart of the heart one hundred and eight times. Having also recited the body mantra one hundred and eight times, burn white sandalwood and scatter flowers. At that moment all aspirations will be fulfilled.

If one takes the medicine that lies within the maṇḍala and ties it on one's body, then whatever direction one goes in, whatever one pursues will be accomplished. Whatever words one speaks, all will believe and heed them. Every aim one works toward will be accomplished. The only exception is one who has not made the mind one-pointed. This is called the rite of the medicine held in the mouth, from the Wish-Fulfilling Sandalwood Wheel.

This concludes the fourth chapter, the chapter on the rite of the medicine held in the mouth, from the Wish-Fulfilling Wheel of the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara.

Then the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara, once more out of compassion for sentient beings, taught a rite by which, when the whole multitude of people see one, they become loving and joyful.

Take the medicine śirikakuśu, lotus stamens, blue lotus, sea foam, the bones of a black thief-fish, orpiment, kakunbansya, bezoar, saffron, Chinese saffron, long pepper, black pepper and dried ginger, in equal parts. Pound them and sift them finely. If there is one tola of this medicine, add half a tola each of musk and camphor and grind it fine. Prepare it before an image of Avalokiteśvara, recite each of the three kinds of earlier mantra one thousand and eight times, give rise to a mind of loving-kindness and compassion toward all sentient beings, and place the medicine at the feet of the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara.

After that, touch it, take it up with a bronze probe and apply it to the eyes. All the afflictions of the eyes and of the brain, all cataracts that obscure sight, white clouding, flowing tears, red clouding, blindness, and every ailment of the brain will be healed. If one applies this medicine to the eyes every day without fail, all eye diseases will be cleared away. If one applies it for two days, all illnesses of the body will be cleared away. If one applies it for three days, the eighty-four kinds of illness of madness will be cleared away. If one applies it for four days, one will not be obscured by any outer or inner obscuration.

If it is applied for five days, one will be victorious in every battle with enemies. If it is applied for six days, all evil karma, the afflictions, the four grave offenses, the five deeds of immediate retribution, bad dreams, and all sorcery are destroyed, and one will never fall into the lower realms. If it is applied for seven days, the king, the great ministers, and the whole multitude will follow one, show one honor, trust one, and feel loving-kindness toward one.

If it is applied for two weeks, one will gain great sovereign power. If it is applied for three weeks, one will become a friend of the king and the great ministers. If it is applied for four weeks, the yakṣas together with their retinues will wait upon one. If it is applied for five weeks, the asuras, nāgas, yakṣas, and rākṣasas will act as one's servants. If it is applied for six weeks, the garuḍas and the rākṣasas of great might will guard one until one fully and completely awakens, so that sorcery, spirits, and grahas cannot harm one. If it is applied for seven weeks, the god Mahākāla and the eight classes of spirits and demons will come near, follow one, and act as one's servants. If it is applied for eight weeks, one will be able to become invisible. If it is applied for nine weeks, one will see all hidden treasures. If it is applied for ten weeks, the doors of the asuras' dwellings will open by themselves, one will see everything that is within them, and one will go in and out without hindrance. If it is applied for eleven weeks, all the medicinal herbs that blaze like fire will openly delight in one, so that if one wishes for long life, or wishes to be endowed with great power, that too will be accomplished accordingly. If it is applied for twelve weeks, many sages will open treasuries of jewels and show them to one, and one will enjoy them as one wishes. If it is applied for thirteen weeks, the doors of the nāgas' palaces will open by themselves, and nothing will hinder one from looking within. If it is applied for fourteen weeks, the doors of the palaces of the gods of the desire realm will open by themselves, and one will see everything. If it is applied for fifteen weeks, even in the pitch darkness of night it will be as bright for one as day. If it is applied for sixteen weeks, one will see even the vajra ground beneath the earth and the maṇḍala of wind. If it is applied for seventeen weeks, one will see all the hell beings there are in the four continents, and through its power all those suffering beings will be set free. If it is applied for eighteen weeks, that person will gain a power like the sun. If it is applied for nineteen weeks, one will see the true body of Vajrapāṇi, and he will fulfill one's aspirations. If it is applied for twenty weeks, one will see Avalokiteśvara, endowed with great compassion, and he will fulfill all one's aspirations. If it is applied for twenty-one weeks, one will attain the bases of miraculous power. The doors of the palaces of the gods of the form realm will open by themselves, and one will see everything. One will also see the buddhas and bodhisattvas of the ten directions and the utterly pure buddha fields. If it is applied for one year, one will attain the five utterly pure eyes.

Whoever wishes to accomplish this rite of practice should have intense faith in this teaching and should feel compassion for sentient beings. If one is free of doubt, everything will be accomplished just as taught above. If one comes to have doubts, the rites will be difficult to accomplish. This is called the rite of eye medicine from the Sandalwood Jewel Wheel of Avalokiteśvara.

This concludes the fifth chapter, on eye medicine, from the Mind Wheel of the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara.

At that time the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara, once more, out of compassion for sentient beings, taught the auspicious rite of the homa fire offering. It accomplishes every aim of one's practice. It destroys the afflictions, the obscuration of wrongdoing, and all bad karma. It subdues all enemies. It pacifies the wrathful minds of the vināyakas, so that they no longer harm the many people and loving-kindness arises in them.

Dig a pit and make a square fire hearth one cubit across. Mix in equal parts mālencasu flowers, white mustard seed, curd, honey, and ghee. Cut aloeswood, sandalwood, and fragrant woods, each into sticks twelve finger-widths long and one finger-width wide, and burn them in the hearth. Take a little of the mixture, empower it once with the mantra, and cast it into the fire. If one completes this one thousand and eight times, all karmic obscurations will be destroyed and one will be able to live for a hundred and twenty years. If one performs it in accordance with the homa rite for seven days without interruption, one will be able to live a thousand years, and one's body too will become utterly pure. If one performs it according to the rite for two weeks, kings, princes, great ministers and courtiers, and ordinary people as well will come to one for refuge and pay one homage. If one performs it according to the rite for three weeks, the gods of the Heaven of the Thirty-Three with their retinues, the gods of the sun and moon, and the Four Great Kings with their retinues will come near and protect one. Vajrapāṇi too will bestow the great accomplishment, and the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara too will fulfill one's great aspirations.

If rain is scarce in the land, empower white mustard seed and ghee with this mantra and burn them in the fire for three days, and rain will fall. If the rain is heavy and does not stop, take the ash from that hearth, empower it with the mantra one hundred and eight times, and scatter it into the sky, and the rain will stop.

"If a destructive hailstorm falls, or a violent wind suddenly arises, then too one should take that ash, empower it with the mantra one hundred and eight times, and scatter it toward the quarter where the clouds are. It will then be pacified.

"If one constantly recites this mantra, one's power and strength will become like those of a mighty one. Therefore, on leaving this body, one will be born in the world realm Sukhāvatī. In every life, until one attains perfect and complete awakening, one will have the recollection of former lives."

Then the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara said this to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, if any monk, nun, layman, laywoman, man or woman holds this dhāraṇī of the wheel of the sandalwood essence even once, in the way I have taught it, it will without doubt be accomplished. Therefore one should not give rise to doubting thoughts. Since one will attain every aim one has in mind, one should have very great faith and should not give rise to doubt."

Then the Blessed One praised the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara, saying: "Out of great compassion you have rightly explained this splendid rite of the dhāraṇī of the Wish-Fulfilling Wheel, and you have brought benefit and happiness to all the sentient beings of Jambudvīpa. Excellent, excellent! Whoever generates the resolve and recites it aloud will attain accomplishment. Although this is so, you should instruct sentient beings again and again, with earnest diligence, just as I have taught, and manifest your body so that they may attain accomplishment. Do not go against my word. I too rejoice."

Then the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara said this to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, for immeasurable eons I have instructed sentient beings with a mind of love and affection, have constantly protected them, and have granted them accomplishments. The Blessed One himself knows this, and it is within his power. Since this dhāraṇī of the Wish-Fulfilling Wheel has been explained for the sake of sentient beings, if anyone takes it up and holds it, and always personally completes the full number of recitations, their aspirations will be fulfilled. In this way, through the power of the Blessed One's blessing, I shall fully protect suffering sentient beings."

At that time, when the bodhisattva mahāsattva Avalokiteśvara had spoken this dhāraṇī of the Wish-Fulfilling Wheel, the whole great assembled retinue rejoiced greatly. With full faith they rightly took it up and put it into practice.

This concludes the sixth chapter, on the hoṃ fire offering rite of combining medicines, from the dhāraṇī of the Wish-Fulfilling Wheel of the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara.

Here ends the noble dhāraṇī called The Essence of the Wish-Fulfilling Wheel, the Unobstructed Secret Treasury of the Bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara.

It was translated from a Chinese manuscript, then edited and finalized, by the chief editor-preceptor and translator, the monk Chödrup, a follower of the Blessed One's tradition.

Translator's notes

"The Mountain of the Sages" renders དྲང་སྲོང་གི་རི literally. It may correspond to Ṛṣigiri near Rājagṛha, but this is not certain. The phrase གཡར་སྟོན་དུ་བཟླས་པ is obscure, and "recite it steadily" is a contextual rendering. ཀླད་རྒྱས, the brain or its membrane, is rendered simply as "brain". The term rendered "crawling insects" is uncertain in the Tibetan and may name a specific kind of venomous creature. The Tibetan at the start of the second chapter reads "Avalokiteśvara and Īśvara," evidently a copyist's slip for the single name Avalokiteśvara, which is how it is translated. The clause rendered "one should give it out freely, as a loan" is obscure in the Tibetan and the rendering is tentative. "Gold leaf" renders an uncertain term for a gold substance. The "hollow-crowned tree" is a literal rendering of an unidentified plant name. In the fifth chapter several ingredients are uncertain: śirikakuśu and kakunbansya are given as the Tibetan transcribes them; "lotus stamens" and "black thief-fish" are tentative; "sea foam" is the traditional cuttlefish bone of Indo-Tibetan pharmacology. The fourth chapter's closing line reads "Wish-Fulfilling Sandalwood Wheel" as written, though elsewhere the text is called simply the Wish-Fulfilling Wheel. In this chapter "day" renders the honorific word for the sun, used here to count days of application. "Become invisible" renders ཨནྟྲད་ན, read as Sanskrit antardhāna, "disappearance, invisibility." The flower name མ་ལེན་ཙ་སུ could not be identified with confidence and is given as "mālencasu" from its Tibetan spelling. The sense of the eleventh-week result, that the fire-like medicinal herbs "openly delight in one," is somewhat obscure in the Tibetan. "A mighty one" renders the Tibetan "mthu bo che."
