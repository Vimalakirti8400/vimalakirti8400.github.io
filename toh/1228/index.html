About this text

The Rite of the Fire Offering from Glorious Hevajra, known in Sanskrit as the Śrī Hevajra Homavidhi and in Tibetan as Dpal kye rdo rje las sbyin sreg gi cho ga, is a concise ritual manual for performing the fire offering, or homa, within the practice of Hevajra, attributed in its colophon to the master Bodhigarbha and translated into Tibetan by the Indian preceptor Kamalaśrī with the monk Sherab Kyab. Written largely in verse, it sets out the four kinds of ritual action, pacifying, increasing, subjugating and fierce, and for each prescribes the shape, size, color, emblems, direction and time of the hearth, together with the offering substances, kindling sticks and firewood suited to it. It then guides the practitioner through kindling the fire, spreading the kuśa grass, visualizing the fire god and inviting the wisdom deities into him, making the oblations with their mantras, and finally dismissing the deities, closing with a summary of the whole sequence. The work is Toh 1228 in the Tengyur, where it belongs to the section of tantra commentaries among the ritual texts of the Hevajra cycle.

Translation

In the language of India: śrī hevajra homa vidhi. In the language of Tibet: The Rite of the Fire Offering from Glorious Hevajra.

Homage to Hevajra.

Having paid homage to the glorious ḍāka, protector of beings,
And to the circle together with its goddesses,
I shall correctly explain
The stages of the fire offering.

In the action tantras the Victor
Taught that the actions are of four kinds:
Pacifying, increasing, subjugating, and the fierce rite.
Without error in their order,
I shall explain the stages in brief.

For performing the face-warming rite
The increasing action is supreme;
So, beginning with increasing,
I shall fully explain the stages of the actions.

I shall explain the stages of the hearth.
For increasing it is square;
For pacifying it is held to be round;
For subjugating it has the shape of a bow;
For killing a triangle is auspicious.
Or else the best is one that serves for all actions.
One cubit and two cubits
Are prescribed for the top and bottom of the increasing hearth.
One cubit and half of that
Are prescribed for the top and bottom of the pacifying hearth.
The bow is forty, with an opening of twenty,
And ten finger-widths in depth: this is for subjugating.
Three sides of twenty finger-widths,
And half that in depth, are prescribed for killing.
For increasing the color is yellow;
Pacifying should be known as white;
For subjugating red is well known;
Likewise for killing black is prescribed.
The action of summoning is to be done as subjugating;
Under the fierce rite come aversion and the like.

For increasing, in the center is a jewel,
In the east a vajra, in the south a wheel,
In the west a lotus, and a sword in the north.
Having made it spread four finger-widths,
Encircle the rim, four finger-widths, with vajras,
And on the outer four finger-widths as well
Draw a garland of lotuses.
The arrangement of the hand emblems for pacifying:
In the center a wheel, in the east a vajra,
In the south a jewel, in the west a lotus,
In the north a sword, it should be known.
It is smeared with white sandalwood.
I shall explain the arrangement of the hand emblems for subjugating.
The rim accords with the general scheme;
In the center an excellent vajra, and
In the center make an eight-petaled lotus.
The outer part accords with the general scheme; smear it with red.
I shall explain the characteristics for the fierce rite.
The rim of three finger-widths is encircled with vajras;
In the center a sword; the outer part
Is as known from the guru's own mouth; smear it with black.

Increasing is done facing east;
For pacifying, north is prescribed;
For subjugating and expelling, west is prescribed;
For wrathful actions, the south.
Increasing is done in the morning;
Pacifying at midday;
Subjugating at sunset, and the wrathful at midnight.

What has been taught thus is the stage of the hearth.

Now the stage called that of the offering substances and the substances to be burned.
To the left of the mantra practitioner
Place a conch bowl filled with scent and flowers.
The vessel for the offering water, of gold and the like,
Is set down filled with water and flowers;
If there is none, one made of clay is also acceptable;
Wood is inauspicious and should be avoided.
The filling ladle, the pouring ladle and the butter are placed in front.
On the right, in a vessel filled with scented water,
Put kuśa grass and set it down.
The vase for all actions, too,
Should be set down in the proper way.
Set down also an excellent torma for the bhūtas.
Set down in order the substances to be burned, sesame and the rest:
For increasing, curd; for pacifying, sesame;
For subjugating and summoning, red utpala lotuses;
For the wrathful and the like, thorny wood,
The last as explained below.
Butter first, and butter last.
This is the stage of the offering substances and the substances to be burned.

To consecrate them,
One touches them with the vajra, and by reciting that very mantra
Their stains are cleansed,
And one imagines them pure like a sun crystal.
This is the stage of consecrating the substances.

Green wood with its leaves, taken from the tip of a branch,
A finger's length long and one measure thick,
Smeared with honey and butter
And soaked in white sandalwood water,
With a single tip, is offered for the pacifying action.
A cubit in length, and in thickness
One measure, is excellent;
Taken from the middle part of the branch,
Soaked in curd, butter and yellow scent,
With a single tip: this is for increasing.
Five finger-widths long, with leaves, one measure thick,
Soaked in mustard oil, the five nectars and the like,
Is auspicious for the subjugating action.
Four finger-widths long and thorny,
Soaked in water with human bone, the great ash of the charnel ground,
Poison and the like:
This is fit to be offered for the fierce rites.
This is the stage of the kindling sticks.

Next I explain the stage of the firewood:
pālaśa, nyagro and the like,
Chosen to accord with the action.
Or else the best is wood with milky sap;
That too should accord with the action.
This briefly teaches the stage of the firewood.

Then the master, endowed with the five mudrās, beginning with the worship of the guru, completes the meditative absorption, and performs the purifying sprinkling for his own maṇḍala with kuśa grass. He makes offerings of water for rinsing the mouth, arghaṃ, water to cool the feet, and the five kinds of offerings, and then the offering of mudrās, and he prostrates. This is the stage of generating one's own splendor.

Then, over all the substances,
Having performed the purifying sprinkling three times,
Take fire in accord with the action
And kindle it with a fan.
The mantra for kindling is this:
oṃ jvāla jvāla hūṃ phaṭ
Reciting it many times and kindling it well,
Into the hearth stacked with firewood
Place the fire as the scripture says.
Raise its splendor with butter,
And examine the signs of good and bad.
This is the stage of kindling the fire.

oṃ vajra āyuṣe svāhā
With this one consecrates the kuśa grass.
First it is laid with its tips pointing east;
Likewise with its tips pointing south, west and north
It is laid out: this is the order of spreading.
This briefly teaches the stage of spreading the kuśa grass.

The stage of the preliminary meditation:
In the realm of space visualize a bhaga.
In its center, generated from hūṃ, visualize a stūpa.
In its center visualize a hearth
In accord with the action.
In its center, from vaṃ, generate a lotus,
And in its center visualize a sun maṇḍala.
Upon that, through manifest awakening,
Meditate on Vajrasattva, father and mother.
Through the power of passion they melt into light;
The light becomes hūṃ and raṃ.
These transform, and one visualizes the fire god, father and mother,
Of a color in accord with the action,
Three-faced and six-armed,
Embracing a wisdom consort like himself.
In the right hands he holds a gundhe and makes the gesture of supreme giving;
In the left, a pomegranate and a counting rosary, and so on;
Pot-bellied, with a crown of matted locks,
He bears the lord of the family on his crest, for worship.
In his heart one visualizes
A triangle of fire upon a multicolored lotus;
Upon it hūṃ and jaḥ;
On his tongue visualize the syllable raṃ.
Beginning with the purifying sprinkling, through prostration and the rest,
Offerings should be made to him.
This is the stage of the fire god as the commitment being.

oṃ agnaye mahāteja sarva karmā bra sādhaka karuṇi kṛta sarva āsmin sanni hitvā bhava. With this the wisdom being is summoned.

Looking toward it in front of oneself,
From the purifying sprinkling through to the prostration,
Then with the hands one should cause it to dissolve completely.
With this mantra one requests it to remain with delight:
sarvā nyai par than ca si ti haṃ ga ca ha bya ba ka a ka mu ye tha ka le sarva siddhi kuru śa me me jaḥ hūṃ vaṃ hoḥ.
With this command one should urge it on, accompanied by dance.
Gaurī summons it with the hook,
Caurī leads it in with the noose,
Vetālī binds it with the chain,
Ghasmarī delights it with the bell.
Having dissolved it so that the two are not two,
Make offerings, beginning with the purifying sprinkling.
This is the stage of causing the wisdom fire god to enter the commitment fire god.

Then the practitioner, in accord with the action, having meditated on the tantras,
Visualizes inside the two ladles
The seed syllable of the chosen deity.
With the two elbows joined, seven ladlefuls
Are offered in accord with each action.
For increasing and pacifying, offer with
puṣṭiṃ and śāntiṃ kuru.
For the two, subjugating and wrathful,
Offer with vaśaṃ and śatrūṃ mara.

I shall explain the order of offering. It is done with this: oṃ namaḥ samanta buddhānāṃ agnaye svāhā puṇa hi manta agnaye puṣṭiṃ kuru svāhā. The others should be offered with the pouring ladle. oṃ sarva saṃvade svāhā: this is for the curd and grain. oṃ sarva pāpiṃ dahana vajraya amukaṃ bya sarva pābaṃ dahana svāhā: this is for the sesame. oṃ vajra āyuṣe svāhā: this is for the unbroken dūrba grass. oṃ vajrāyuṣe svāhā: this is for the unbroken rice. oṃ vajrāpratihata vajraye svāhā: this is for the kuśa grass. oṃ vajra bajiye svāhā: this is for the lakpen. oṃ vajra mahā vegaye svāhā: this is for the barley. oṃ mahā balāya svāhā: this is for the large beans. oṃ vajra ghasmari svāhā: this is for the wheat.

The order of offering the kindling sticks is this. oṃ yajñāya svāhā: this is for the udumvā. oṃ bodhi bṛkṣāya svāhā: this is for the wood of the tree of awakening. oṃ vajra nadathāya svāhā: this is for the nyagrodha. oṃ vajra hasakarāya svāhā: this is for the mango.

Then, with agnaye puṣṭiṃ kuru, one should offer full filling ladles of butter, a thousand or more. Then, beginning with the purifying sprinkling, one makes offerings and praises the fire god. The hero who holds the vajra and bell Recites the verse on the intrinsic nature of existence. Up to this point is the stage of worshiping the fire god.

Then, with the water of the vase for all actions,
One performs the purifying sprinkling on the fire god, and in his heart
One purifies the circle of the maṇḍala.
Having purified the fire's mouth, the hearth, in accord with the action,
One should make offerings into it.
With this command of jaḥ hūṃ vaṃ hoḥ
The deities of the maṇḍala are dissolved into the maṇḍala.
Make offerings, beginning with the purifying sprinkling,
And carry the offering, the praise and the nectar through to the end.

Then one should offer twenty-one or more ladles of butter. Then, having offered the substances such as curd, one should offer a butter fire offering of a hundred or a thousand, with eight added.

Beginning with the purifying sprinkling, worship with butter;
Praise, prostrate, and request it to depart.
The hero who holds the vajra and bell
Recites the verse of natural purity.
With the hundred syllables, ask for forbearance.
Adding muḥ at the end, gather it into the heart.
This is the stage of the fire offering to the deity.

Then with the pouring ladle alone,
Make offerings to the fire god with what remains:
It is done with oṃ agnaye svāhā.
Then, having offered all the substances
And filled the filling ladle with butter,
The disciple then casts a flower
And speaks the words of good fortune in accord with the action.
Offer it with oṃ agnaye svāhā.
śantiṃ kuru is for pacifying;
For increasing, puṣṭiṃ; paśaṃ is for subjugating.

When performing the pacifying fire offering, the stages to be carried out by the yogin are these: first, determining the kind of hearth; its measurements and color; drawing the marks; the time and the direction; the characteristics of the substances; consecrating the substances; generating the yoga; the characteristics of the kindling sticks; the characteristics of the firewood; the characteristics of kindling the fire; spreading the kuśa grass; consecrating the hearth; generating there the commitment fire god; inviting the wisdom fire god into him; the purifying sprinkling; again, generating there the circle of the deities of one's own deity; summoning the wisdom circle into it and causing it to enter; offering there with the filling and pouring ladles and so forth; having presented the offerings and the rest, requesting the wisdom deities to depart and gathering in the commitment circle; again making offerings and the rest to the fire god, urging the wisdom deity to depart, and gathering in the commitment fire god; performing the fire offering with the remaining substances; and oneself remaining at ease.

For the fierce rite, with māraya,
A hundred and eight butter fire offerings.
Having made the offerings to the fire god,
With the offerings and the water to refresh the mouth as above,
And having pleased the fire god
With tooth-cleansers, garments and the like,
Recite the verse of natural purity
And request the fire god to depart into space.
This is the stage of the worship of the fire god.

The profound meaning is a matter of oral instruction, and so it is not taught here.

By whatever virtue has been gained
Through clarifying the stages of the fire offering
Of what is called Hevajra,
May sentient beings swiftly be liberated.

This completes The Concise Rite of the Fire Offering, composed by the master Bodhigarbha. It was translated by the Indian preceptor Kamalaśrī and the monk Sherab Kyab.

Translator's notes

"Face-warming rite" renders zhal bsro, apparently a fire offering performed as part of a consecration; the exact referent is not stated here. "Under the fierce rite come aversion and the like" refers to rites such as sowing enmity (vidveṣaṇa). In the subjugating hearth the line "in the center an excellent vajra, and" seems to lack its continuation; it is given as it stands. "Wood is inauspicious" reads mi shis for the written mi shes ("not know"), which makes no sense here. "Touches them with the vajra" reads reg pa for the written rag pa. "One measure thick" renders chag gang, a small unit of thickness whose exact size is uncertain. The gundhe held by the fire god is probably a water pot (kuṇḍikā); the final word of the line on the left hands (mtha') is unclear and is rendered "and so on." "Pot-bellied" reads the unclear gsus 'dir as a description of the belly. "Having meditated on the tantras" renders rgyud rnams bsgoms; rgyud may here mean the practitioner's own mental continuum. "Unbroken" for the dūrba grass reads mi grugs pa for the written me grugs pa. "Lakpen" renders lag pan, a substance offered in the fire that could not be identified with confidence. "With eight added" means 108 or 1,008 oblations. The "hundred syllables" are the hundred-syllable mantra recited to ask the deities' forbearance for faults in the rite.
