About this text

The Noble Dhāraṇī Called the Torch of the Jewels, a Mahāyāna Sūtra (Sanskrit: Ārya-ratnolkā-nāma-dhāraṇī-mahāyāna-sūtra; Tibetan: 'phags pa dkon mchog ta la la'i gzungs zhes bya ba theg pa chen po'i mdo) is a Mahāyāna sūtra centered on a dhāraṇī, a formula of protective and purifying power. As the opening lines indicate, it is set at Vulture Peak Mountain in Rājagṛha, where the Buddha is gathered with a great assembly of monks and a retinue of bodhisattvas, including Samantabhadra. It is Toh 847 in the Kangyur, where it belongs to the general class of tantra, as the colophon-style note on the "third tantra" at the head of the text suggests, even though it is framed as a sūtra and contains a dhāraṇī.

May the teaching be protected. The third tantra is complete.

In the language of India: Ārya-Ratnolka-nāma-dhāraṇī-mahāyāna-sūtra. In the language of Tibet: The Noble Dhāraṇī Called the Torch of the Jewels, a Mahāyāna Sūtra. First chapter.

Homage to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas.

Thus did I hear at one time. The Blessed One was dwelling in Rājagṛha, on Vulture Peak Mountain, together with a great assembly of the Saṅgha, one thousand monks in full. All of them were perfect in the white factors, roaring the great lion's roar, skilled in seeking the immeasurable accumulations of wisdom, and they were all great and eminent monks.

There was also a great assembly of bodhisattvas, namely: the bodhisattva, the great being Samantabhadra; the bodhisattva, the great being Ratnamudrāhasta (Jewel Seal in Hand); the bodhisattva, the great being Nityodyukta (Constantly Diligent); the bodhisattva, the great being Guṇālaṃkṛta (Adorned with Qualities); the bodhisattva, the great being Puṇyaprakāśa (Proclaimer of Merit); the bodhisattva, the great being Mahāmati (Great Intelligence); the bodhisattva, the great being Guṇavyūha (Array of Qualities); the bodhisattva, the great being Vajramati (Diamond Intelligence); the bodhisattva, the great being Vajragarbha (Diamond Essence); the bodhisattva, the great being Vajraprabha (Diamond Radiance); the bodhisattva, the great being Vajrāyudha (Diamond Weapon); the bodhisattva, the great being Vajradṛḍha (Diamond Firmness); the bodhisattva, the great being Dharaṇiṃdhara (Earth-Holder); the bodhisattva, the great being Dhāraṇīmati (Intelligence of Dhāraṇī); the bodhisattva, the great being Sarvārthadarśin (Seeing All Aims); the bodhisattva, the great being Avalokiteśvara; the bodhisattva, the great being Mahāsthāmaprāpta (Attainer of Great Power); the bodhisattva, the great being Sthiramati (Steadfast Intelligence); the bodhisattva, the great being Vajrapāṇi; the bodhisattva, the great being Mañjuśrī, the Youthful; the bodhisattva, the great being Apāyajaha (Abandoner of the Lower Realms); the bodhisattva, the great being Sarvaśokatamonirghātana (Dispeller of All Sorrow and Darkness); the bodhisattva, the great being Pravaravikrāmin (Overwhelmer through Excellent Prowess); the bodhisattva, the great being Anupādānāvisarjana (Neither Grasping nor Discarding); the bodhisattva, the great being Candanagarbha (Sandalwood Essence); the bodhisattva, the great being Sāgaramati (Ocean of Intelligence); the bodhisattva, the great being Durlabhaprabhava (Rare Arising); the bodhisattva, the great being Nandotpāda (Arising of Joy); the bodhisattva, the great being Caryāmati (Intelligence of Conduct); the bodhisattva, the great being Pratibhānakūṭa (Heap of Eloquence); the bodhisattva, the great being Śīghragarbha (Swift Essence); and the bodhisattva, the great being Maitreya.

These and other bodhisattvas, great beings, were present. They abided in the inconceivable liberation. They had come forth through the absorption of heroic progress. Their voices were unobstructed. They were skilled in overpowering countless buddha fields. They abided in fearlessness, free from attachment and from resentful anger. They were endowed with pleasing voices, unattached to the three realms, and endowed with unbroken retinues. They were born from omniscience itself, endowed with limitless absorptions and meditative attainments, and they fulfilled all hopes.

They had realized and fully comprehended the Perfection of Wisdom. They were devoted to making their actions of body, speech, and mind meaningful. They strove toward omniscience itself. They were blessed with immeasurable absorptions and conduct. They had attained fearlessness, they ranged in the domain of emptiness, and they abided in the signless. They were such as these, and many others besides.

Then the bodhisattva, the great being named Samantabhadra, who had come to be seated in that very assembly, bowed his head to the feet of the Blessed One and spoke these words to the Blessed One. "Blessed One, how should the dharmadhātu, the basic space of phenomena, be realized?"

The Blessed One said, "Son of good family, the basic space of phenomena is to be realized as without inherent existence. Son of good family, the basic space of phenomena is like space itself. It is free of elaboration, it is unelaborated, there is nothing to take up and nothing to cast away, it is without inherent existence, and it is without abiding. It should be realized in just this way."

The bodhisattva Samantabhadra asked, "Blessed One, does the basic space of phenomena arise?"

The Blessed One said, "Son of good family, the basic space of phenomena is without arising. Son of good family, the basic space of phenomena is inconceivable. Son of good family, the basic space of phenomena is realized as without inherent existence, and so it cannot be expressed or demonstrated as any thing whatsoever."

The bodhisattva Samantabhadra asked, "Blessed One, how many kinds of awakening are there?"

The Blessed One said, "Son of good family, the kinds of awakening are immeasurable, and they cannot be taught as having any limit."

The bodhisattva Samantabhadra asked, "Blessed One, does one conceptualize the basic space of phenomena?"

The Blessed One said, "Son of good family, the basic space of phenomena is free of conceptualization."

The bodhisattva Samantabhadra asked, "Blessed One, if the basic space of phenomena is free of conceptualization, why do ordinary childish beings conceptualize?"

The Blessed One said, "Son of good family, all ordinary childish beings arise from conceptual thought, from discursive conceptualization, and from thorough imputation."

The bodhisattva Samantabhadra said, "Blessed One, this awakening of the Tathāgatas is profound."

The Blessed One said, "Son of good family, so it is. It is just as you have said. Moreover, son of good family, the absence of elaboration in all phenomena is called awakening."

Then Mañjuśrī, the youthful prince, who had come to be seated in that very assembly, bowed his head to the feet of the Blessed One and spoke these words to the Blessed One. "Blessed One, please teach the dhāraṇī called Tala-tala of the Jewel."

The Blessed One said, "Son of good family, ask the bodhisattva Samantabhadra here, who abides in unobstructed eloquence regarding all phenomena. He will teach you."

Then Mañjuśrī, the youthful prince, sat facing the Blessed One, joined his ten fingers in homage, and spoke these words to the Blessed One. "Blessed One, you who know all and see all, why do you yourself not teach it?"

The Blessed One said, "Son of good family, where bodhisattvas endowed with such qualities as these are present, there the Tathāgatas rest in equanimity."

Mañjuśrī said, "Blessed One, the Tathāgatas do not rest in equanimity."

The Blessed One said, "Son of good family, I do not abandon the realm of sentient beings to ruin. Nevertheless, son of good family, the teaching of the bodhisattvas is a teaching without measure, a teaching beyond conception."

Then Mañjuśrī, the youthful prince, again spoke these words to the Blessed One. "May the Blessed One teach the dhāraṇī called the Jewel Talala."

The Blessed One said, "Son of good family, ask the bodhisattva Samantabhadra here, and he will teach you. Why is this? Son of good family, this supreme being abides in the gathering of wisdom."

Mañjuśrī said, "If the Tathāgata is mindful of me, I shall ask this supreme being."

The Blessed One said, "Mañjuśrī, you have attained meditative absorptions as numerous as the particles of dust in the finest atom. Why then must you make requests of the Tathāgata? Son of good family, you are fortunate in all the qualities of a buddha."

Mañjuśrī said, "Blessed One, whatever qualities of a buddha I have relied upon, meditated upon, and practiced extensively, I shall understand all of them as arising through the power of the Tathāgata's own being."

The Blessed One said, "Excellent, excellent, son of good family! You have spoken these words well. Nevertheless, Mañjuśrī, I make this request of you: ask the bodhisattva Samantabhadra."

Then Mañjuśrī, the youthful prince, spoke these words to the Blessed One. "Blessed One, this bodhisattva Samantabhadra teaches the exceedingly profound; he has fully comprehended and realized the Great Vehicle."

The Blessed One said, "Son of good family, you too are the sovereign king of power over all phenomena, so do not say this of me. Moreover, son of good family, you possess the inconceivable meditative absorption that abides in emptiness."

Then Mañjuśrī, the youthful prince, joined his ten fingers in homage to the bodhisattva Samantabhadra, and spoke these words to him. "O son of the Victor, if you grant me the opportunity, I shall ask you two syllables."

The bodhisattva Samantabhadra said, "Son of good family, if you know how to bring it to its proper time, ask."

Then Mañjuśrī, the youthful prince, spoke these words to the bodhisattva, the great being Samantabhadra. "O son of the Victor, what is the meaning of the ground of the word 'buddha, buddha'?"

The bodhisattva Samantabhadra said, "O son of the Victor, the meaning of the ground of a buddha is the meaning of being without ground. The meaning of the ground of a buddha is the meaning of being without real entity. The meaning of the ground of a buddha is the meaning of the inconceivable. The meaning of the ground of a buddha is the meaning of the unequal and the equal. The meaning of the ground of a buddha is the meaning of being without elaboration. The meaning of the ground of a buddha is the meaning of non-elaboration. The meaning of the ground of a buddha is the meaning of being without grasping. The meaning of the ground of a buddha is the meaning of being without rejecting. The meaning of the ground of a buddha is the meaning of being like space. The meaning of the ground of a buddha is the meaning of the inexpressible. Son of good family, such is the intrinsic nature of the qualities of a buddha."

Mañjuśrī said, "O son of the Victor, if all phenomena are free of elaboration, then how are the qualities of a buddha taught as knowable?"

The bodhisattva Samantabhadra said, "O son of the Victor, this teaching is a teaching that cannot be taught."

Mañjuśrī said,

"O son of the Victor, if there is nothing to be taught, then what is taught?"

The bodhisattva Samantabhadra said, "Mañjuśrī, what cannot be taught is taught through designation."

Mañjuśrī said, "O son of the Victor, what does designation designate?"

The bodhisattva Samantabhadra said, "Mañjuśrī, designation designates neither things nor the absence of things."

Mañjuśrī said, "O son of the Victor, if designation designates neither things nor the absence of things, then how are the three vehicles taught?"

The bodhisattva Samantabhadra said, "Mañjuśrī, do not cling to the dharmadhātu, which is free of clinging. Why is this? Mañjuśrī, it is because the Tathāgata has taught that all phenomena are without substantial reality. And why are they without substantial reality? Because the five aggregates are not apprehended as objects."

Mañjuśrī said, "Is awakening accompanied by elaboration, or is it free of elaboration?"

The bodhisattva Samantabhadra said, "Son of good family, awakening is neither accompanied by elaboration nor free of elaboration. For awakening, that which is accompanied by elaboration, and that which is free of elaboration are not two; they cannot be divided into two."

Then the Blessed One gave his approval to the bodhisattva, the great being Samantabhadra, saying, "Son of good family, well done, well done, this teaching of the inconceivable." And by this discourse, the world with its gods was bewildered.

Mañjuśrī, the youthful one, said, "Blessed One, a teaching of Dharma such as this cannot be conceived in any of its aspects."

The Blessed One declared, "Son of good family, so it is. It is just as you have said."

Then Mañjuśrī, the youthful one, said to the Blessed One, "Blessed One, are all phenomena designated by this designation?"

The Blessed One declared, "Son of good family, so it is. It is just as you have said."

Then the bodhisattva Samantabhadra said to the Blessed One, "Blessed One, this gateway of purity is difficult to have faith in."

The Blessed One declared, "Son of good family, so it is. It is just as you have said."

Then the bodhisattva Matisāgara (Ocean of Intelligence) said to the Blessed One, "Blessed One, the bodhisattva Samantabhadra has spoken well in teaching this gateway of purity."

The Blessed One declared, "Son of good family, so it is. It is just as you have said. Moreover, son of good family, this is in accord with the nature of all phenomena. This is the great rain of Dharma."

Then a brahmin, like a great śāla tree, abiding in equality, said to the Blessed One, "Blessed One, awakening is equality, and it is inconceivable. In it, no letters are found, and no words are found."

The Blessed One declared, "Son of good family, so it is. In the dharmadhātu, no words appear, and no meaning appears either."

Then Mañjuśrī, the youthful one, said to the Blessed One, "Blessed One, what is that which accords with emptiness? What are its aspects? What are its signs? What are its marks?"

The Blessed One declared, "Mañjuśrī, emptiness is inexpressible and unspeakable. Whatever faith there is in this inexpressible nature of reality is called emptiness. Emptiness is without letters, and for that reason it is called emptiness. Emptiness is unspeakable, and for that reason it is called emptiness."

Son of good family, all phenomena are empty of the intrinsic nature of phenomena.

Then the venerable Śāriputra said to the Blessed One, "Blessed One, behold the miraculous display of those bodhisattvas who abide in the inconceivable Dharma."

The Blessed One declared, "Venerable Śāriputra, the knowledge of a bodhisattva who has just generated the first thought of awakening is supreme, and the knowledge of an arhat is not like that. Why is this? The arhat remains far from the qualities of a buddha, whereas that bodhisattva will become a buddha, a Blessed One."

Then the bodhisattva, the great being Sovereign King of All Phenomena, said to the Blessed One, "Blessed One, as I understand what the Blessed One has said, the śrāvaka has not attained the qualities of a śrāvaka."

The Blessed One declared, "Son of good family, it is not that the śrāvaka has failed to attain the qualities of a śrāvaka. Rather, how could a śrāvaka possibly have the capacity or the power to answer questions put together with a bodhisattva, or to uphold the blessing of a bodhisattva's conduct?"

Then Mañjuśrī, the youthful one, said to the Blessed One, "Blessed One, if the Tathāgata has taught that this Śāriputra is foremost among those endowed with wisdom, to whom, then, is this teaching taught?"

The Blessed One declared, "Mañjuśrī, what I teach is without anything taught."

Then Mañjuśrī, the youthful one, spoke to the elder Śāriputra. "Elder, have you attained the qualities of a śrāvaka?" He replied, "That is not so." Mañjuśrī said, "Then are you an ordinary person?" He replied, "That is not so." Mañjuśrī said, "Then are you a trainee?" He replied, "That is not so." Mañjuśrī said, "Then are you the foremost of those endowed with wisdom?" He replied, "That is not so." Mañjuśrī said, "Venerable Śāriputra, if you are not an ordinary person, nor a trainee, nor the foremost of those endowed with wisdom, then you yourself have become one who has a fixed reference point."

Śāriputra replied, "Son of good family, I am a supreme person, as hard to fathom as the ocean, and I will not compete with you."

Mañjuśrī said, "Venerable Śāriputra, you are the eldest and the foremost of the elders. Venerable Śāriputra, do not speak in that way."

The elder replied, "Son of good family, by the mere fact of being old, one attains nothing and realizes nothing. Son of good family, in order that you may understand this very point, I will give you an example. Son of good family, just as a mere thunderbolt can shatter even a great mountain of rock, so too, son of good family, the wisdom that dwells in a single pore of your skin is not found in beings like me, even as many as the specks of dust in a fine particle. Son of good family, so that you may understand this very point, I will give you another example. Just as a great elephant, an exceedingly unruly and mighty bull elephant, is tamed with a small goad by a man who has strength, so too, son of good family, you are the one who has strength and I am the weak one. How could I contend with one such as this great elephant?"

Then the elder Śāriputra said to the Blessed One as follows.

Blessed One, it is like this. Suppose a blind man travels along a path. If he cannot even perceive a city, how could he possibly find the way from house to house within it? That is simply impossible. In just this way, in the presence of these great elephants, I see myself as like a blind man. For these qualities of the buddhas are so vast. I have not become a vessel for the qualities of the buddhas, and there is nothing at all that I can do.

The Blessed One said: Son of Śāradvatī, do not speak in this way. Son of Śāradvatī, it is like this. However many beings are touched by the light of the Tathāgata, all of them obtain the qualities of the buddhas. Son of Śāradvatī, in just this way, you have attained a meditative absorption that is boundless and beyond conception.

When the Blessed One taught this discourse on the Dharma, ninety-two thousand beings among the gods and humans came to realize the Dharma directly.

Then the bodhisattva Dharmamati entered the bodhisattva absorption called Boundless Application, Engagement with the Jewel Ṭ ala. As soon as the bodhisattva Dharmamati had entered it, in each of the ten directions, beyond as many world-systems as there are specks of dust in a fine particle of ten thousand buddha-fields, there appeared before him as many buddhas, blessed ones, as there are specks of dust in a fine particle of ten thousand buddha-fields. All those buddhas, blessed ones, were in complete accord in bearing the name Vajragarbha. As it was in one direction, so it was in all ten directions. Those buddhas, blessed ones, spoke to him as follows.

Dharmamati, excellent, excellent, that you have entered the bodhisattva absorption of Boundless Application. Son of good family, it is thus. By the former aspiration and former blessing of this very Blessed One, the Tathāgata Vairocana, and supported by the roots of virtue that are your own, all this is so: in order that all phenomena be taught; that the wisdom of the buddhas be purified; that the wisdom of the buddhas be increased; that beings enter the dharmadhātu; that the realm of sentient beings be liberated; that you enter and fully penetrate the wisdom without attachment; that you be completely joined to it; that all languages be spoken; that you enter the wisdom of omniscience; that you be free of obscuration regarding all phenomena; and that you engage in teaching the Dharma that fully knows all three times. For these reasons, from the ten directions, all the tathāgatas, as many as the specks of dust in a fine particle of ten thousand buddha-fields, in complete accord in name, bless you in all these respects. Son of good family, drawing on the ten settings of the bodhisattva, by the power of the buddhas and the blessing of the buddhas, give rise to eloquent confidence in teaching this Dharma.

Then those buddhas, blessed ones, bestowed upon the bodhisattva Dharmamati the radiance of unobstructed wisdom; freedom from obscuration; freedom from interruption; the nature of reality that is never forgotten; undifferentiated wisdom; freedom from fault; that which cannot be overpowered; freedom from timidity; the nature of reality that cannot be stolen away; and the accomplishment of excellent speech. Why was this?

This was because they had attained the very nature of reality of this samādhi.

Then those buddhas, blessed ones, stretched out their right hands and stroked the head of the bodhisattva Dharmamati. As soon as those buddhas, blessed ones, touched the bodhisattva Dharmamati, he rose from that samādhi and spoke to those bodhisattvas.

"O sons of the Victor, this is the family of the bodhisattvas: it is as vast as the realm of phenomena is pervasive, and as the limit of the realm of space is far-reaching. O sons of the Victor, in this way, the bodhisattvas, the great beings, are born into the family of the buddhas, the blessed ones, of the past. They are born into the family of the buddhas, the blessed ones, of the future. They are born into the family of the buddhas, the blessed ones, of the present."

Then those bodhisattvas said this to the bodhisattva Dharmamati: "O son of the Victor, who are those bodhisattvas, those great beings, who are born into the family of the buddhas, the blessed ones, of the past, the future, and the present? With what in mind do you say this? O son of the Victor, please tell us what those ten settings of the bodhisattva are, the settings of the bodhisattvas."

When they had said this, the bodhisattva Dharmamati spoke to those bodhisattvas as follows: "O sons of the Victor, the settings of the bodhisattva, of the bodhisattvas, are these ten. They were taught by the buddhas, the blessed ones, of the past and the future, and of the present. They are taught, they were taught, and they will be taught. What are the ten? They are these: the setting of the bodhisattva who has just generated the first thought of awakening; the beginner; the one engaged in yoga; the one who has taken birth; the one perfect in application; the one perfect in intention; the one who cannot turn back; the one who has become a youth; the heir to the throne; and the setting of the bodhisattva who has received consecration. O sons of the Victor, these are the ten settings of the bodhisattva, of the bodhisattvas. They were taught by the buddhas, the blessed ones, of the past and the future, and of the present. They are taught, they were taught, and they will be taught."

"O sons of the Victor, what is the setting of the bodhisattva who has just generated the first thought of awakening? O sons of the Victor, here the bodhisattva who has just generated the first thought of awakening, as soon as he sees the buddhas, the blessed ones, sees a beautiful form, or a handsome one, or one of flourishing and radiant complexion, or one that is vast, or one of great splendor; or sees a miraculous display of magical power, or a miraculous display of discourse, or a miraculous display of instruction; or sees suffering beings; or hears the Tathāgata's praises extensively recounted, then, desiring the wisdom of omniscience, he generates the thought of unsurpassed, perfect, complete awakening from the very first. As soon as he has generated that thought, he takes hold of the ten difficult-to-approach grounds of understanding. What are the ten? They are these: knowing what is possible and what is impossible; knowing the actions and the undertakings of phenomena, past, future, and present; knowing the paths that lead everywhere; knowing the many and varied elements; knowing the many kinds of inclinations and liberations; and..."

knowing the superior and inferior faculties; knowing the meditative concentrations, the liberations, the absorptions, and the attainments, with all their defilement, purification, and emergence; the knowledge that recollects former abodes; the knowledge of the divine eye; and knowing the exhaustion of defilements. He takes hold of these ten difficult-to-approach grounds of understanding.

Sons of the Victor, this is the account of the bodhisattva who has just generated the first thought.

Sons of the Victor, that bodhisattva, that great being who has just generated the first thought, should be applied to honoring the buddhas and to the comforts and requisites of a bodhisattva. It should also be said of him that he becomes a lord of the world, a supreme one, one who cannot be overpowered; that he meets countless buddhas; that he practices the meditative absorption of perfect peace; that he turns back the wheel of saṃsāra; that he sets in motion the wheel of the sacred Dharma; and that he protects suffering beings. Why is this so? Because from the very moment the thought is generated, he is for the most part joined to all the qualities of a buddha, and he applies himself even to those grounds above, of which he has heard, having gained the ability not to leave the matter to the shoulders of others. Sons of the Victor, this is the account of the bodhisattva who has just generated the first thought.

Sons of the Victor, what then is the account of the beginner bodhisattva? Sons of the Victor, the beginner bodhisattva first generates ten kinds of thought toward sentient beings. What are the ten? They are these: the thought of benefiting, the thought of bringing happiness, the thought of compassion, the thought of tenderness, the thought of affection, the thought of helping, the thought of protecting in every way, the thought of equanimity, the thought of a teacher, and the thought of a guide. He generates these ten kinds of thought. Sons of the Victor, this is the beginner bodhisattva.

Sons of the Victor, that beginner bodhisattva should apply himself to receiving the scriptural transmissions and to reciting them from memory. Having heard much, he should apply himself to complete solitude. Having become secluded, he should apply himself to delighting in spiritual friends. Delighting in spiritual friends, he should apply himself to being easy to instruct. Having become easy to instruct, he should apply himself to knowing the right time. Knowing the right time, he should apply himself to fearlessness. Having become fearless, he should apply himself to knowing the meaning. Knowing the meaning, he should apply himself to following the Dharma. Following the Dharma, he should apply himself to the nature of phenomena without forgetfulness. Having become free of bewilderment, he should apply himself to the analysis of phenomena. Why is this so? Because from the very moment the thought is generated, he for the most part strives earnestly toward all the qualities of a buddha, and he applies himself even to those grounds above that are to be attained, having gained the ability not to leave the matter to the shoulders of others. Sons of the Victor, this is the account of the beginner bodhisattva.

Sons of the Victor, what then is the account of the bodhisattva who practices yoga? Sons of the Victor, the bodhisattva who practices yoga realizes phenomena through ten kinds of focus. What are the ten? They are these: he realizes all phenomena as impermanent; all phenomena as suffering; as without self; as empty; as without movement;

without breathing; without abiding; without conceptual thought; without effort; and without deliberate formation. Because he has come to follow the Dharma, he does not strive, and yet he does not fail to strive either. Sons of the Victor, this is the bodhisattva who practices yoga.

Sons of the Victor, that bodhisattva who practices yoga should apply himself to examining the realm of sentient beings. He should apply himself to examining the dharmadhātu. He should apply himself to examining the realms of the world. He should apply himself to examining the earth element. He should apply himself to examining the water element, the fire element, the wind element, the space element, the form realm, and the formless realm.

Why is this so? Because he for the most part enters into the appearance of phenomena, and because, even regarding those grounds above that he has heard about, he applies himself directly and has gained the ability not to leave the matter to the shoulders of others. Sons of the Victor, this is the account of the bodhisattva who practices yoga.

Sons of the Victor, what then is the account of the bodhisattva who is born by rebirth? Sons of the Victor, here the bodhisattva who is born by rebirth is born into the noble lineage through ten purifying practices. What are the ten? They are these: irreversibility from the nature of reality; the complete accomplishment of unshakable faith in the buddhas that reaches its culmination; certainty in contemplating the Dharma; discernment concerning sentient beings; discernment concerning buddha fields; discernment concerning the world; discernment concerning karma; discernment concerning karmic maturation; discernment concerning saṃsāra; and discernment concerning nirvāṇa. Through these ten purifying practices he is born into the noble lineage. Sons of the Victor, this is the bodhisattva who is born by rebirth.

Sons of the Victor, that bodhisattva who is born by rebirth should apply himself to discerning the equality of the qualities of the buddhas of the past. He should apply himself to discerning the equality of the qualities of the buddhas of the future. He should apply himself to discerning the equality of the qualities of the buddhas of the present. He should apply himself to discerning the equality of the qualities of all the buddhas.

He should apply himself to discerning the genuine accomplishment of the qualities of the buddhas of the past. He should apply himself to discerning the genuine accomplishment of the qualities of the buddhas of the future. He should apply himself to discerning the genuine accomplishment of the qualities of the buddhas of the present. He should apply himself to discerning the genuine accomplishment of the qualities of all the buddhas.

He should apply himself to discerning the equality in the complete perfection of the qualities of the buddhas of the past. He should apply himself to discerning the equality in the complete perfection of the qualities of the buddhas of the future. He should apply himself to discerning the equality in the complete perfection of the qualities of the buddhas of the present. He should apply himself to discerning the equality in the complete perfection of the qualities of all the buddhas.

Why is this so? Because he for the most part realizes the equality of the three times, and because, even regarding those grounds above that he has heard about, he applies himself directly and has gained the ability not to leave the matter to the shoulders of others. Sons of the Victor, this is the account of the bodhisattva who is born by rebirth.

Sons of the Victor, what then is the account of the bodhisattva who has perfected application?

Sons of the Victor, here the bodhisattva who has perfected application undertakes whatever virtuous deeds he begins for the sake of fully protecting all sentient beings; of wishing benefit for all sentient beings; of joining all sentient beings with happiness; of having compassion for all sentient beings; of liberating all sentient beings completely; of making all sentient beings free from harm; of guiding all sentient beings rightly; of inspiring faith in all sentient beings; of taming all sentient beings; and of leading all sentient beings fully beyond sorrow into nirvāṇa. Sons of the Victor, this is the bodhisattva who has perfected application.

Sons of the Victor, for that bodhisattva who has perfected application, ten abodes should be properly expounded. What are the ten? They are these: that sentient beings are limitless, that sentient beings are immeasurable, that sentient beings are innumerable, that sentient beings are inconceivable, that sentient beings are incomparable, that sentient beings are beyond measure, that sentient beings are empty, that sentient beings are unmoving, that sentient beings are nonexistent, and that sentient beings are without intrinsic nature.

Why is this so? Because he for the most part rests his mind in the absence of fixation, and, even regarding those grounds above that he has heard about, he applies himself directly and has gained the ability not to leave the matter to the shoulders of others. Sons of the Victor, this is the account of the bodhisattva who has perfected application.

Sons of the Victor, what then is the account of the bodhisattva who has perfected intention?

Sons of the Victor, here the bodhisattva who has perfected intention is one who, upon hearing ten things, has a settled intention toward the qualities of the buddhas. What are the ten? They are these. Whether one speaks in praise of the buddhas or speaks not in praise, his intention toward the qualities of the buddhas remains settled. Whether one speaks in praise of the Dharma or not in praise of it, whether one speaks in praise of the bodhisattvas or not in praise of them, whether one speaks in praise of the conduct of bodhisattvas or not in praise of it, his intention toward the qualities of the buddhas remains settled. Whether one says that the realm of sentient beings is small or says that the realm of sentient beings is vast; whether one says that the realm of sentient beings is wholly afflicted or says that it is not wholly afflicted; whether one says that the realm of sentient beings is easy to tame or says that it is difficult to tame; whether one says that the dharmadhātu is small or says that the dharmadhātu is vast; whether one says that the world systems are destroyed or say that they are not destroyed; whether one says that the dharmadhātu exists or says that the dharmadhātu does not exist, his intention toward the qualities of the buddhas remains settled. Sons of the Victor, this is the bodhisattva who has perfected intention.

Sons of the Victor, for that bodhisattva who has perfected intention, ten ways of following the Dharma should be properly expounded. What are the ten? They are these: it should be properly expounded that all phenomena are without signs; that all phenomena are without defining characteristics; that they are without real entity; that they are nonexistent; that they are false; that they are secluded; that they are without essential nature; that they are like an illusion; that they are like a dream; and that they are without conceptual thought. Why is this so?

This is so because, being unable to be robbed of this, he becomes one whose nature is, for the most part, to increase his superior intention; and even regarding those matters he has heard of above, he applies himself directly and has gained the state of not resting on the tracks of others. Sons of the Victor, this is the presentation of the bodhisattva who has perfected intention.

Sons of the Victor, what is the presentation of the bodhisattva who does not turn back? Sons of the Victor, here the bodhisattva who does not turn back is one who, on hearing ten objects of focus, does not turn back from the qualities of the buddhas. What are the ten? They are these. Even on hearing that there is a buddha or that there is no buddha, he does not turn back from the qualities of the buddhas. Likewise, on hearing that there is Dharma or that there is none; that there are bodhisattvas or that there are none; that there is the conduct of bodhisattvas or that there is none; that a bodhisattva will emerge definitively through the conduct of bodhisattvas or will not emerge definitively; that the Tathāgatas have passed away or have not passed away; that the Tathāgatas have come or have not come; that the Tathāgatas have now appeared or have not now appeared; that the wisdom of the buddhas is exhaustible or inexhaustible; and that the three times have one defining characteristic or do not have one defining characteristic. Even on hearing these, he does not turn back from the qualities of the buddhas. Sons of the Victor, this is the bodhisattva who does not turn back.

Sons of the Victor, for that bodhisattva who does not turn back, ten streams of Dharma should be properly expounded. What are the ten? They are these: it should be properly expounded that all phenomena are one and also that they are different; that they are many and also one; that meaning is superimposed upon letters; that letters are superimposed upon meaning; that nonentities are superimposed by means of entities; that entities are superimposed by means of nonentities; that the signless is superimposed by means of signs; that signs are superimposed by means of the signless; that the characteristicless is superimposed by means of characteristics; and that characteristics are superimposed by means of the characteristicless. Why is this so? Because, being for the most part skilled in all phenomena, he emerges definitively in the manner of full maturation, and even regarding those matters he has heard of above, he applies himself directly and has gained the state of not resting on the tracks of others. Sons of the Victor, this is the presentation of the bodhisattva who does not turn back.

Sons of the Victor, what is the presentation of the bodhisattva who has become a prince? Sons of the Victor, here the bodhisattva who has become a prince is endowed with ten realizations of the Dharma. What are the ten? They are these. He is endowed with the fact that bodily actions have results. He is endowed with the fact that verbal actions have results. He is endowed with the fact that mental actions have results. By the mere generation of the mind he accomplishes birth as he wishes. He knows the intentions of other sentient beings and other individuals, and discerns their inclinations. He thoroughly knows the diversity of the elements of sentient beings. He thoroughly knows the diversity of the desire realm. He thoroughly knows the diversity of the form realm. He thoroughly knows the diversity of the formless realm. And because he proceeds in accord with the times, his higher knowledge is swift. Sons of the Victor, this is the bodhisattva who has become a prince.

Sons of the Victor, for that bodhisattva who has become a prince, ten ways of accomplishing the Dharma should be properly expounded. What are the ten? They are these:

Thorough knowledge of the buddha-fields; the shaking of the buddha-fields; the blessing of the buddha-fields; the discernment of the buddha-fields; going to the buddha-fields; going to innumerable world-systems; asking innumerable questions; the accomplishing of the mind-made body; the application of immeasurable skill in languages; and the perfect accomplishment of innumerable buddha-fields through the generation of the awakening mind: these should be properly expounded. Why is this so? Because for the most part he applies himself to accomplishing what is acquired through skill, and, in the case of those stages of which he has heard above, he has attained the capacity to practice them himself without leaving them to others. Sons of the Victor, this is the classification of the bodhisattva who has become a prince.

Sons of the Victor, what is the classification of the bodhisattva who is a crown prince, the heir? Sons of the Victor, the bodhisattva who is a crown prince is skilled in ten things to be realized. What are the ten? They are these: he is skilled in realizing the births of sentient beings; skilled in realizing the movements of the afflictions; skilled in realizing the connections of habitual tendencies; skilled in realizing the application to fields of activity; skilled in realizing the nature of reality; skilled in realizing close engagement; skilled in realizing the distinctions among world-systems; skilled in realizing knowledge of the past limit, the future limit, and what arises in the present; and skilled in realizing the definitive understanding of conventional truth. Sons of the Victor, this is the bodhisattva who is a crown prince.

Sons of the Victor, ten stations should be properly expounded for that bodhisattva who is a crown prince. What are the ten? They are these: skill in the retinue of the royal palace should be properly expounded; and likewise the full engagement with the retinue of the royal palace; entering the retinue of the royal palace; discerning within the retinue of the royal palace; the consecration of the Dharma king; the blessing of the Dharma king; the overcoming of others by the Dharma king; the station of the Dharma king; and the command of the Dharma king. Why is this so? Because his mind, for the most part, enters into the accomplishing of unobstructedness regarding all phenomena, and, in the case of those stages of which he has heard above, he has attained the capacity to accomplish them himself without leaving them to others. Sons of the Victor, this is the classification of the bodhisattva who is a crown prince.

Sons of the Victor, what is the classification of the bodhisattva who has received consecration? Sons of the Victor, the bodhisattva who has received consecration possesses ten accomplishments of knowledge. What are the ten? They are these: he frequently causes innumerable world-systems to shake; he frequently blesses innumerable world-systems; he frequently carries innumerable world-systems across in a single thought; he frequently purifies innumerable world-systems; he frequently makes innumerable sentient beings know in a single thought; he frequently beholds innumerable world-systems; he is skilled in examining, in a single thought, the mental conduct of innumerable sentient beings; he is skilled in comprehending, in a single thought, the faculties of innumerable sentient beings; and he is skilled in disciplining innumerable sentient beings.

And he is skilled in causing innumerable sentient beings to enter into the mind of omniscience.

O sons of the Victor, no bodhisattva below the stage of the crown prince can know the bodily actions of that bodhisattva who has received consecration. They cannot know his actions of speech. They cannot know his actions of mind. They cannot know his miraculous powers. They cannot know his surveying by miraculous power. They cannot know his surveying of the past, nor can they know what he intentionally sets in motion. They cannot know the workings of his intellect. They cannot know the range of his mind. They cannot know the sphere of his wisdom. O sons of the Victor, such is the bodhisattva who has received consecration.

O sons of the Victor, concerning that bodhisattva who has received consecration, the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, proclaim ten dwellings. What are the ten? They are these: knowing the three times; knowing the qualities of the buddhas; knowing the inseparability of the dharmadhātu; the dharmadhātu being without limit or center; completely filling all world-systems; illuminating all world-systems; blessing all world-systems; fully knowing all sentient beings; directly knowing all phenomena; and proclaiming the boundless wisdom of the buddhas. Why so? Because for the most part his mind has entered into omniscience. O sons of the Victor, this is the establishing of the bodhisattva who has received consecration.

Hardly had the bodhisattva Dharma Intelligence finished expounding these ten establishings of bodhisattvas, the great beings, when, in that very moment, through the power of the buddhas, in each of the ten directions, as many world-systems as there are particles of dust in ten thousand buddha-fields shook in six ways: they shook, shook greatly, shook all over and greatly; they trembled, trembled greatly, trembled all over and greatly; they heaved, heaved greatly, heaved all over and greatly; they roared, roared greatly, roared all over and greatly; they resounded, resounded greatly, resounded all over and greatly; they quaked, quaked greatly, quaked all over and greatly.

Through the power of the buddhas, and through the attainment of the nature of reality, a rain of clouds of divine flowers also fell. A rain of clouds of divine incense also fell. A rain of clouds of divine perfumes also fell. A rain of clouds of divine powders, a rain of clouds of divine garlands, a rain of clouds of divine robes, a rain of clouds of divine jewels, a rain of clouds of divine lotuses, a rain of clouds of divine necklaces, and a rain of clouds of divine ornaments also fell. Divine cymbals sounded, though no one struck them. Divine radiance also appeared, and divine voices rang out.

Just as in this world of the four continents, in the palace of the lord of the gods (Indra) on the summit of Mount Meru, so too in all world-systems this teaching of the Dharma pervaded and arose, without addition or deficiency. These very words show this very meaning.

Through the power of the buddhas, from beyond as many world-systems as there are particles of dust in ten thousand buddha-fields, bodhisattvas, as many as the particles of dust in ten thousand buddha-fields, came, filled the ten directions completely, and spoke thus: "O son of the Victor, excellent, excellent, that you thus teach this nature of the bodhisattvas!"

"O son of the Victor, we too are called by the name Dharma-Intelligence, and we have come here from the world-system called Cloud of Dharma, from the presence of the Tathāgata named Excellent Dharma-Intelligence. Through the blessing of the buddhas, this teaching of the Dharma arose in all those places, in assemblies just like this one, and with these very words it shows this very meaning, without addition or deficiency. O son of the Victor, we have come under your influence, and through the power of the buddhas we have come to this world-system. Just as we have come to this world-system, so too in all the four continents of all the world-systems of the ten directions, and in the palaces of the lord of the gods (Indra) on the summit of Mount Meru, bodhisattvas, as many as the finest particles of dust in ten buddha-fields, have come."

End of the first volume.

Second volume.

Then, through the power of the buddhas, the bodhisattva Dharma-Intelligence looked out over the ten directions, and over the entire assembly, and gazed upon the dharmadhātu as well. At that moment he spoke these verses:

Seeing one of self-arisen, utterly pure intelligence,
Beautiful, endowed with the power of a radiant form,
Adorned with marks hard to approach,
One sets the steadfast mind upon awakening.

Seeing incomparable powers of miraculous display,
Excellent renown, and the teaching that follows,
And seeing beings of the world worn down by enduring suffering,
One sets the steadfast mind upon awakening.

Having heard the qualities of Samantabhadra,
The Tathāgata, the source of all good qualities,
One sets the steadfast mind upon awakening,
Which is unchanging, like the expanse of space.

In order to realize all the bases that exist,
And the other bases that abide by their own nature,
As nothing other than the very things themselves,
One sets the steadfast mind upon awakening.

In order to realize the causes of all the virtuous
And non-virtuous actions that exist
In the past, the future, and the present times,
One sets the steadfast mind upon awakening.

In order to realize the stages of meditative absorption,
Concentration, liberations, and conduct,
And likewise the pure, final attainments of equipoise,
One sets the steadfast mind upon awakening.

In order to realize, step by step, in each and every case,
The world's diligence, powers, and faculties,
Of whatever kind they may be,
One sets the steadfast mind upon awakening.

In order to realize the many treatises,
From the various inclinations of all the world
And the three kinds of differing superior intentions,
One sets the steadfast mind upon awakening.

In order to realize the nature of the elements,
Of every kind and every variety,
That exist in these three worlds,
One sets the steadfast mind upon awakening.

In order to realize the true nature of whatever
Goes everywhere within this Dharma,
And of whatever enters into virtuous practice,
One sets the steadfast mind upon awakening.

In order to open fully the unattached eye
That sees how all the buddha-fields come to be,
And how beings arise from the grounds,
One sets the steadfast mind upon awakening.

In order to realize the classes of beings,
And whatever defining marks they have,
In past times and in the present,
And their former states,
One sets the steadfast mind upon awakening.

In order to realize the exhaustion of the afflictions,
The fetters that bind beings in the world
As they wander through the destinies,
One sets the steadfast mind upon awakening.

Whatever conventions are designated in the three worlds,

Whatever pathways of speech, modes of mantra, and conduct there may be,
In order to realize the truth of conventional designation,
One sets the steadfast mind upon awakening.

All phenomena are inexpressible,
Empty, unmoving, their very nature and essence;
In order to realize the ultimate truth,
One sets the steadfast mind upon awakening.

In order to reveal the earth trembling in the buddha-fields,
And the oceans themselves thrown into turmoil,
And thus to show the miraculous powers of the Tathāgatas,
One sets the steadfast mind upon awakening.

From each single tip of a ray of light, rays of light
Spread out in the same way through all ten directions;
In order to realize that these are one light,
One sets the steadfast mind upon awakening.

However many buddha-fields there are, many and beyond thought,
In order to manifest them all in one's own hand,
And to realize that illusory manner of being,
One sets the steadfast mind upon awakening.

All the buddha-fields, and likewise all sentient beings,
Placing them without harm in the palm of the hand,
In order to realize that phenomena are without a life-force of their own,
One sets the steadfast mind upon awakening.

However much water there is in all the oceans,
One causes it to enter into a single drop on a hair-tip;
In order to realize the complete and final limit of all things,
One sets the steadfast mind upon awakening.

Of all the atoms of fine dust within all the buddha-fields,
However many there may be, one counts them all without remainder;
In order to realize the number of the atoms,
One sets the steadfast mind upon awakening.

In this world, the aeons that have passed,
And likewise those to come, fearful with the burning of the aeon;
In order to realize the exhaustion of the aeons,
One sets the steadfast mind upon awakening.

The Tathāgatas of the three times,
And the Teachers, the solitary buddhas, and those born from hearing;
In order to realize the nature of the Dharma,
One sets the steadfast mind upon awakening.

The inexpressible world-systems,
Bound up with a single hair, one places them all on the scale;
In order to realize the nature of things,
One sets the steadfast mind upon awakening.

The encircling mountain ranges, beyond thought,
Bound up with a single hair, one places them all on the scale;
In order to realize the coarse and the subtle,
One sets the steadfast mind upon awakening.

In all the world-systems without exception,
In a single instant one makes oneself understood with one voice;
In order to show the pure aspects of that voice,
One sets the steadfast mind upon awakening.

Whatever sounds there are in all the worlds,
In a single instant one speaks in an instant's speech;
In order to realize their nature of peace,
One sets the steadfast mind upon awakening.

One realizes all three worlds without exception,
Fully known by all sentient beings;
In order to realize the broad, vast tongue,
One sets the steadfast mind upon awakening.

In all the buddha-fields, just as it is spoken,
In a single instant one dispels wrong views;
In order to realize phenomena as conceptual constructs,
One sets the steadfast mind upon awakening.

Here, in all the buddha-fields without exception,
In whatever field the Tathāgata is present, the self-arisen
Phenomena in a single instant, in order to realize them,
One sets the steadfast mind upon awakening.

Like fine, separate particles, the world-systems
One manifests in every possible form;
In order to realize those that arise from the mind,
One sets the steadfast mind upon awakening.

The buddhas of the past, and likewise those yet to come,
However many dwell in the world, all of them,
In order to realize them in a single instant of mind,
One sets the steadfast mind upon awakening.

Though one speak but a single phrase, beyond thought,
Even as the aeons are exhausted, it suffers no decline.

In order to realize the divisions of speech,
One sets the steadfast mind upon awakening.

Whatever the destinies and whatever the reversals
Of all the worlds of the ten directions,
In order to realize them in a single instant of mind,
One sets the steadfast mind upon awakening.

Though one travels in every direction,
With body, speech, or mind one clings to nothing at all.
In order to realize the three times as empty,
One sets the steadfast mind upon awakening.

As the awakening mind is generated,
So one travels through the directions for countless aeons,
Making offerings in the presence of the buddhas.
This is the teaching of the irreversible ones.

In the differing worlds of the ten directions,
Becoming a lord of the world, a child of the Victor,
One proclaims whatever is excellent and holy.
This is the teaching of the irreversible ones.

While practicing in existence for the sake of awakening,
Whatever is the ease and union of the bodhisattva,
Of that one constantly speaks the praise.
This is the teaching of the irreversible ones.

The supreme Dharma, the Dharma that is sublime and has gone beyond,
And whatever distinctive, noble, unsurpassed Dharma there is,
These one teaches to the bodhisattvas.
This is the teaching of the irreversible ones.

The life of the Tathāgata, endowed with all qualities,
Exists in this very place and nowhere else;
In this the bodhisattva engages.
This is the teaching of the irreversible ones.

How it is to meet the buddhas,
Immeasurable, countless, inconceivable,
This one shows to that bodhisattva.
This is the teaching of the irreversible ones.

Whatever meditative absorptions there are, however many,
And likewise the varieties of the continuum of absorptions,
These one explains to the child of the Sugata.
This is the teaching of the irreversible ones.

Turning back the wheel of saṃsāra,
And setting in motion the wheel of the sacred Dharma,
Which no one in the world has ever turned before:
This is to be taught to the bodhisattvas.

For all the worlds in utmost suffering,
And for those harmed in the realms of the lower destinies,
One who becomes a refuge, a shelter, and an ally:
This is to be taught to the bodhisattvas.

One who sets the mind upon awakening
Is taught to be a bodhisattva of the first kind.
The instruction on the training, correctly determined,
And its proper arrangement, belong to such a one.

As in the teaching of those freed from sickness,
In order to benefit all the worlds,
Whoever first generates the mind in this way,
This one is the second kind, a beginner.

The mind that brings benefit, the mind of joy and ease,
The mind of kinship, of affection, and likewise the mind that gathers,
The mind of complete protection, the mind of equanimity,
The mind of the teacher, and the mind that teaches impartially:
For one who abides in minds such as these,
Study and recitation of the scriptures are to be joined.

Having heard much, let one be placed in the wilderness;
Having become secluded, let one be set to rely on spiritual friends;
Toward the spiritual friends let one be easy to instruct;
Let one know the proper time and be endowed with vigilance.
Knowing the time, let one become utterly fearless;
Being free from fear, let one strive for the meaning;
Having understood the meaning, let one speak the Dharma accordingly;
Let one be free from bewilderment about all phenomena.
Having become free of bewilderment, O child of the Victor,
Let one abide in the well-spoken Dharma.
This is the account of the beginner,
The bodhisattva who has entered upon awakening.
This is the instruction, this is the teaching;
Whoever trains in this is a child of the buddhas,
The third kind of bodhisattva.

Let one apply oneself fully to the teaching of the King of Dharma.
Impermanence, suffering, emptiness,
Selflessness, and the absence of any movement;
So too all these phenomena are not a self,
Without deliberate making, and utterly secluded;
All of this is free of conceptual thought.
In this way the bodhisattva reflects.

Concerning the realm of sentient beings and the basic space of phenomena as well,
He is inspired and praised, for the sake of examining them.
"The world-systems are boundless and without limit,"
Thus he sets his mind to investigate.
Here, as far as there is earth, water, and likewise fire,
Wind and space, and in every direction
The desire realm, the form realm, and the formless realm,
He sets himself to investigate all of these.

The vast phenomena in their entirety, and the elements,
Their intrinsic nature without remainder, to be examined to the very end:
This is taught, in due order, for the benefit
Of the buddhas' child who is engaged in practice.
The bodhisattva who is born into the lineage of awakening,
Born into the teaching, goes forth.
That wise one realizes things and the absence of things;
Born into the abode of the sacred Dharma, he is well born.
He never turns back from his own ground.
He is born with the conviction that buddhahood is the final goal,
And always has a mind certain about the Dharma.

He examines thoroughly what the nature of sentient beings is,
And karma, and worlds, and buddha-fields,
Karmic maturation, saṃsāra, and nirvāṇa.
The Victor's child knows the distinctions among them;
He is called the bodhisattva born in the heart.
Having examined all phenomena of the omniscient ones,
Past and present, and those yet to come as well,
Whatever has been accomplished, and whatever is well born, and the final end,
And how the accomplishment of the self-arisen ones comes about,
He knows that all those holy buddhas
Have entered into the sameness of the three times.
They never set apart the particulars of that sameness;
They enter the three times without distinction.
One who has such benefits as these
Is the fourth kind of bodhisattva.

The bodhisattva who has well accomplished these
Will certainly go forth for the sake of awakening.
Thus the fifth kind of bodhisattva
Is described as one perfect in practice,
Following the way of skillful means,
Who brings to completion what he has undertaken through meritorious deeds.
Whatever merit he strives for in this way,
He becomes a support for the whole world,
Wishes to benefit, brings about happiness,
Is compassionate, and becomes a liberator of beings.
For the sake of taming a world gone to harm,
He leads and brings beings to serene faith.
Wishing to tame the many sentient beings, all of them,
With a mind that aims at nirvāṇa, his power is great.
He knows that all worlds are without end,
Likewise immeasurable and innumerable,
Incomparable, inconceivable, unequaled, and beyond measure,
And empty, unmoving, and without intrinsic nature.
This is the fifth kind of bodhisattva,
Endowed with perfect practice, benefit, and compassion.
For him the instruction is of this kind,
Taught by those who possess every quality.

He has no bewilderment about the endless sentient beings
Or about the essential nature of phenomena.
Exceedingly wise, free of thought, without conceptualization,
He is not set apart from the world with its gods.
Even if he hears praise or blame
Of the buddhas, the Dharma, the bodhisattvas, and their conduct,
Or that sentient beings are few or many,
His thought is not shaken.
Even if he hears that sentient beings are afflicted or pure,
Easy to tame or hard to tame,
That the basic space of phenomena is small or vast,
Will be destroyed or will be established,
That the basic space of phenomena exists or does not exist,
That bodhisattva who understands the three times
Reflects with certainty and examines everything,
And his intention is utterly settled in this buddha-Dharma.
Without sign, and without defining characteristic,

Things without essence, false in nature, and utterly secluded,
Of an illusory nature, like a mirage,
Such is the supreme, peaceful Dharma he is able to hear.
Thus the bodhisattva who does not turn back
Examines with certainty whether the buddhas, the Dharma, the bodhisattvas,
And their conduct exist or do not exist.
Whether they arise or do not arise, still he does not turn back.
Whether the Tathāgatas have passed away or have not passed away,
Whether they have not come, or are not "not come,"
Whether they have arisen or not arisen, are exhausted or are inexhaustible,
Whether suchness has one defining characteristic or none,
Whether it is one or different, of many aspects,
Whether the ultimate is the letters of speech, or is real, or is unreal,
All of this is a superimposition, or else something quite other than this;
The bodhisattva who does not turn back has faith in this.
Without sign, with sign,
Without defining characteristic, or marked by a mark,
Differentiated, or mutually combined:
Hearing these, he enters into this as the final goal.

The bodhisattva who has become a youth
Performs deeds that bear fruit by body, by speech, and likewise by mind,
And, just as he wishes, he displays his birth here.
He examines the intentions of others,
And acts in accord with their inclinations.
He knows the conduct of sentient beings, the Dharma that suits them,
The destruction and the formation of the realms.
As he wishes, he gives, and swiftly attains the superknowledges.
He directs his mind into all directions.
Though he hears the Dharma that praises the Sugata,
He does not become conceited about it.
He knows fully, and he shakes the realms;
Likewise he bestows blessing and engages in discernment.
Moving in an instant across countless realms,
He is to be described as going to the realms.
He answers countless questions in the same moment,
And displays bodies of every kind, even mind-made bodies.
In regard to this he voices boundless sounds, words, and phrases,
And in a single instant speaks of countless buddhas.

The bodhisattva who is the crown prince
Knows the entrance of sentient beings just as it is:
Their afflictions, their continuum, their habitual tendencies,
What their domain is, what their application is,
Their diverse dharmas, how they move,
The classification of worlds, the former limit,
The conventional truth and the ultimate truth;
He is skilled in realizing all of this without exception.

Skilled in the palace and its retinue, he brings forth the assembly.
There too, as to how he behaves in all ways,
The dwelling, and likewise how to enter,
And what the palace and its retinue are like when examined,
What the royal empowerment of the sacred Dharma is like,
What blessing and overcoming of others are like,
Entering the Dharma and proclaiming the transmitted word:
This is the crown prince's entry into the grounds.
With just this, his course reaches its end,
And as it has been taught, it becomes nothing other.
When at some time he lets go of the formations of life,
The crown prince, endowed with the proper way, turns directly toward the Victor.

The one who is empowered is the sole son of the Sugata,
Exalted, supreme, a master who has reached the end.
He can take up the measure of water in the ocean on a single hair-tip.
Here his intention cannot be grasped by counting.
Such is the conduct of the Victor's sons.
Here, all the realms with their sentient beings and their deeds,
Ground into the finest dust, he can count completely.
Whatever is reckoned as "so many countless ones,"
All of that the bodhisattva knows here.

Buddhas of the past, likewise of the future,
And those who dwell in the ten directions,
Pratyekabuddhas and likewise śrāvakas:
The awakening mind, which is the seed of all these, arises.
When one first knows the awakening mind, whatever is said of it
Is countless in every way.
Were it to fill even all these world-realms,
What could be said beyond that?

Then the bodhisattva Samantabhadra spoke to the bodhisattva Dharmamati, saying: Son of noble family, you have spoken well in expounding the dhāraṇī of the Dkon mchog ta la la. Son of noble family, the qualities of the buddhas are inconceivable. If those beings who merely hear the name of this teaching draw near to omniscience, what need is there to speak of those who hold this teaching, recite it, fully understand it, and practice it just as it is? Such beings will surely become buddhas.

Dharmamati said: O son of the Victor, it is just so. It is exactly as you have said. Those who possess profound wisdom should be known as having been empowered by the Tathāgata.

Samantabhadra said: Son or daughter of noble family, whoever has such teachings as these resound in their ears, those excellent persons should be known as having received prophecy regarding the qualities of the buddhas. If that is so, what need is there to speak of those who have this teaching in their very hands?

Then the venerable Śāriputra bowed his head at the feet of the Blessed One and said: Blessed One, please look upon me. Since I have not understood such teachings as these, I am like one who is blind. Blessed One, it is not only I who have become like one blind. Blessed One, all those beings who have not heard this teaching have become like the blind.

The Blessed One said: Elder, it is just so. It is exactly as you have said.

Śāriputra requested: Blessed One, please explain the inconceivable.

The Blessed One said: Śāriputra, go and urge Indra, Brahmā, and all the world protectors. Proclaim the command of the Tathāgata as well. Proclaim also to that assembly this dhāraṇī called the Dkon mchog ta la la, the supreme seal of the Dharma. Then the venerable Śāriputra, having heard the Tathāgata's command, went out of eagerness for the sake of the meaning of this dhāraṇī, and spoke of the matter to Indra, Brahmā, and the world protectors, saying: Come together, all who wish to listen to the Dharma. The Tathāgata will expound the dhāraṇī of the Dkon mchog ta la la and will also reveal the inconceivable. Assemble! Otherwise you will later regret it, saying: O friends, such precious sūtras are extremely rare to appear in the world.

Then in that very instant Indra, Brahmā, and all the world protectors gathered. They circumambulated the Blessed One three times, sat down before the Blessed One, joined their palms, and made their request to the Blessed One: Blessed One, please expound the dhāraṇī of the Dkon mchog ta la la.

Then the Blessed One remained silent, which was a sign of permission. Though Indra, Brahmā, and all the world protectors requested a second and a third time, the Blessed One still remained silent.

Then the venerable Śāriputra said to the Blessed One: Blessed One, please expound the dhāraṇī called Dkon mchog ta la la. Sugata, please expound it. Then at that time the Blessed One extended the organ of his tongue from his mouth.

Sons of good family, all those who petition the Tathāgata for the sake of this dhāraṇī of the Dkon mchog ta la la will become irreversible from unsurpassed, perfect, complete awakening. With these words he made his voice heard throughout the great world-system of a billion worlds. Then the Blessed One spoke again to the venerable Śāriputra, saying: Śāriputra, go and request the youth Mañjuśrī to teach the Dharma.

At that time Mañjuśrī, the youth, was sitting cross-legged and motionless beneath a flowering śāla tree, in a place apart. In a pavilion of great splendor, with a radiance surpassing hundreds of thousands of millions of suns, he sat encircled by Indra, Brahmā, and the world protectors, who gazed toward him. His body was the color of gold, and he shone blazing and brilliant with glory.

Then the venerable Śāriputra went to where the youth Mañjuśrī was, and on arriving he said to him: Son of good family, the Tathāgata requests you to teach the Dharma.

Then the youth Mañjuśrī said to the venerable Śāriputra: Venerable Śāriputra, what is the basis and meaning of this thing called the Tathāgata?

Śāriputra replied: Son of good family, your wisdom is profound. I cannot compete with you in analysis.

Mañjuśrī said: Then do not make yourself small in heart. Whatever you are able to bear, I will teach accordingly.

Śāriputra said: Son of good family, I will listen. Please speak.

Then the youth Mañjuśrī arose from that absorption and made his voice heard throughout the great world-system of a billion worlds. And at that time, all the sons of the gods dwelling in that world-system, from the class of the Pure Abodes down to Akaniṣṭha, came together. The four great kings, with their armies and attendants, also came together, along with many millions of yakṣas. Indra, Brahmā, and the world protectors also gathered. Both those who desired the Dharma and those who did not desire it also gathered. Many goddesses also gathered.

The assembly of monks, the assembly of nuns, the assembly of laymen, and the assembly of laywomen gathered. So too did the assemblies of the gods of the Thirty-Three, of Yāma, of Tuṣita, of those who delight in creation, and of those who wield power over the creations of others, the assembly of Brahmā's realm, and likewise the assemblies of gods up to Akaniṣṭha.

The company of hearers (śrāvakas) also gathered, namely: the venerable Subhūti, the venerable Mahākāśyapa, the venerable Mahāmaudgalyāyana, the venerable Śāriputra, the venerable Mahākātyāyana, the venerable Aniruddha, the venerable Gayākāśyapa, the venerable Mahākauṣṭhila, the venerable Cūḍapanthaka, the venerable Nadīkāśyapa, the venerable Uruvilvākāśyapa, the venerable Pūrṇa son of Maitrāyaṇī, the venerable Rāhula, the venerable Bhadrika, the venerable Revata, the venerable Ānanda, and other great hearers besides these, and five hundred nuns headed by Yaśodharā also gathered.

Kings of the realms and wheel-turning kings of power also gathered. Retinues of nobles, brahmins, and householders also gathered.

Then the venerable Śāradvatīputra (Śāriputra) circumambulated the Blessed One three times and said: "Blessed One, by whose power has this great assembly gathered today? How should this be understood?"

The Blessed One declared: "Venerable Śāradvatīputra, this is through the power of the dhāraṇī of the Jewel Talala."

Śāradvatīputra said: "Blessed One, I wish to hear the dhāraṇī of the Jewel Talala."

The Blessed One declared: "Śāradvatīputra, go and make your request to Mañjuśrī, the youthful, and to the bodhisattva Samantabhadra."

Then the venerable Śāradvatīputra spoke to Mañjuśrī, the youthful: "Son of noble family, please teach this meditative absorption, the precious teaching, the Jewel Talala."

Mañjuśrī said: "Reverend Śāradvatīputra, who is it that will hear the dhāraṇī of the Jewel Talala?"

Śāradvatīputra replied: "These gathered four assemblies, and Indra, Brahmā, and the world-protectors who have assembled here. I too have come in order to listen."

Then Mañjuśrī, the youthful, said to the elder Śāradvatīputra: "Elder Śāradvatīputra, if all these listeners to the Dharma are an illusory creation, then to whom am I to teach the Dharma? Who is the listener? Who is the speaker?"

The elder said: "Excellent being, you speak, and I will listen."

Mañjuśrī said: "Reverend Śāradvatīputra, this word 'teaching,' what is it a synonym for?"

He replied: "Mañjuśrī, 'teaching' is a synonym for emptiness."

Mañjuśrī said: "Śāradvatīputra, of what is emptiness a synonym?"

He replied: "Mañjuśrī, emptiness is wordless."

Mañjuśrī said: "Reverend Śāradvatīputra, if emptiness is wordless, what is there to teach? Reverend Śāradvatīputra, all phenomena are indemonstrable; they cannot be shown, and they cannot be heard."

The elder said: "Son of noble family, all that you have taught is a teaching of emptiness, a teaching of signlessness, a teaching of wishlessness, a teaching of nothing to be taken up, a teaching of nothing to be abandoned, a teaching of freedom from conceptual elaboration. It is a teaching of the unelaborated."

Then that whole assembly of bodhisattvas, together with Indra, Brahmā, and the world-protectors, rejoiced greatly, and with one voice they praised Mañjuśrī, the youthful, and the venerable Śāradvatīputra, saying, "Excellent! This dhāraṇī of the Jewel Talala has been well taught."

Then the venerable Subhūti spoke to Mañjuśrī, the youthful: "Mañjuśrī, how should a bodhisattva hold this dhāraṇī of the Jewel Talala? How should he recite it, fully awaken to its meaning, and explain it extensively and correctly to others?"

Mañjuśrī said: "Subhūti, this dhāraṇī should be contemplated as unarisen. It should be contemplated as without entities, without defining characteristics, without conceptual elaboration, and without anything to be abandoned."

As Mañjuśrī, the youthful, taught this exposition of the Dharma, ninety-two bodhisattvas in that assembly attained the meditative absorption called Heroic Progress. Sixty-two gods and humans attained the acceptance of the unarisen nature of phenomena.

Then the bodhisattva Samantabhadra rose from his seat, arranged his upper robe over one shoulder, and with palms joined together,

he addressed the Blessed One with these words: "Blessed One, what is the great compassion of bodhisattvas, the great beings?"

The Blessed One declared: "Noble son, the great compassion of bodhisattvas, the great beings, is this: not abandoning the three realms entirely. This is great compassion. Showing the buddha-fields to all sentient beings is great compassion. Embracing sentient beings of broken discipline is great compassion. Not forsaking the Perfection of Wisdom is great compassion. Giving up body and life for the sake of all sentient beings is great compassion. Speaking words of true loving-kindness to beings who are given to attachment is great compassion. And loving-kindness toward powerful beings is likewise the great compassion of bodhisattvas, the great beings.

"Noble son, furthermore, a bodhisattva, a great being, should cultivate a mind free of anger toward all sentient beings, and should practice at length being free of hostility."

Then the bodhisattva Samantabhadra addressed the Blessed One with these words: "For the sake of the healing and the happiness of the many, out of compassion for the world, for the benefit and healing and well-being of the great multitudes, of gods and humans, may the Blessed One please expound the dhāraṇī called Tala Tala, the Jewel. May the Blessed One, who holds healing and compassion for all sentient beings, expound it."

Then the Blessed One, in the voice of Brahmā, addressed those bodhisattvas with these words: "Among you, who is willing, in the later time, in the last five hundred years, when the true Dharma is vanishing, to teach this exposition of the Dharma correctly and thoroughly?"

Then the following bodhisattvas came forward: the bodhisattva Samantabhadra; the bodhisattva Mañjuśrī, the youthful; the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara; the bodhisattva Great Power Attained; the bodhisattva Abandoner of the Lower Realms; the bodhisattva King of Medicines; the bodhisattva Heaped Eloquence; the bodhisattva Sovereign Who Is Noble Through All Phenomena; the bodhisattva Inexhaustible Intelligence; the bodhisattva Ocean of Intelligence; the bodhisattva Radiant Jewel; the bodhisattva Jewel Light; the bodhisattva Jewel-Crested; the bodhisattva Beholder; the bodhisattva Unseen by Sight; the bodhisattva Ever-Beholding; the bodhisattva Vajrapāṇi; the bodhisattva Jewel Heap; the bodhisattva Source of the Jewel; the bodhisattva Glory of Dharma; the bodhisattva Glory of the Crest; the bodhisattva Glory of Wealth; the bodhisattva Glory of Merit; the bodhisattva Glory of Sandalwood; the bodhisattva Intelligence of Dharma; the bodhisattva Nectar Intelligence; the bodhisattva Boundless Intelligence; the bodhisattva Adorned with Merit; the bodhisattva Adorned with Qualities; the bodhisattva Adorned with Marks; the bodhisattva Ever-Laughing, Delighting All the Faculties; the bodhisattva King Who Fells the Mountain Peaks; the bodhisattva Skilled in Eloquence; and the bodhisattva Ever-Outstretched Hand.

the bodhisattva Earth-Holder; the bodhisattva Swift Eloquence; the bodhisattva Essence of Space; the bodhisattva Essence of the Moon; the bodhisattva Essence of the Sun; the bodhisattva Essence of the Stainless Moon; the bodhisattva Essence of the Sun-Parasol; the bodhisattva King of Superknowledge; the bodhisattva Great Mountain; the bodhisattva Steadfast Intelligence; the bodhisattva Courageous Eloquence; and the great bodhisattva Maitreya. These and other bodhisattvas, numbering sixty-two thousand hundred-thousand billions, spoke together with a single voice:

Blessed One, in the final age, in the last of the five hundred years, when the sacred Dharma is vanishing, all of us will gather and assemble, and we wish to rightly expound the dhāraṇī called Alala of the Jewel.

The Blessed One declared: Excellent, excellent, sons of good family! This is a difficult thing to do. A rite such as this is exceedingly difficult to perform.

Then the Blessed One spoke to the bodhisattva Samantabhadra: Son of good family, listen to the dhāraṇī of Alala of the Jewel, which benefits many beings and brings happiness to many beings.

Then the Blessed One, seated on the lion throne of great splendor, spoke the mantra:

Homage to all the tathāgatas, arhats, perfectly awakened buddhas. Tadyathā: Oṃ maṃ, bharate, bharate, bharabharanate svāhā.

Then the Blessed One uttered an inspired utterance two and three times: "Alala dharma! Alala dharma! Alala dharma!"

Then the bodhisattva Samantabhadra said to the Blessed One: Blessed One, what is the meaning of the ground of this "dharma, dharma"?

The Blessed One declared: Son of good family, the meaning of the ground of all phenomena is the meaning of groundlessness. The meaning of the ground of all phenomena is the ground of the absence of intrinsic reality. The meaning of the ground of all phenomena is the ground of space. The meaning of the ground of all phenomena is the ground of there being nothing to take up. The meaning of the ground of all phenomena is the ground of there being nothing to discard.

Then the bodhisattva Samantabhadra asked: Blessed One, then why are they called "all phenomena"?

The Blessed One declared: Son of good family, what is called "all phenomena" is the eye, the ear, the nose, the tongue, the body, and the mind; likewise the elements, the sense sources, and dependent origination. Son of good family, this is what is called "all phenomena." Furthermore, son of good family, all phenomena are empty because they have never been born from the beginning.

Then Mañjuśrī, the youthful one, said to the bodhisattva Samantabhadra: O child of the Victors, how should this dhāraṇī of Alala of the Jewel be held?

When he had spoken thus, the bodhisattva Samantabhadra replied to Mañjuśrī: This dhāraṇī should be held as the teaching of suchness; as the suchness of non-arising; as the suchness of non-origination; as the suchness of the absence of defining characteristics; as the suchness of space; as the suchness of the absence of intrinsic reality; as the suchness of essential nature; and as the suchness of the essential nature that lacks intrinsic reality. Son of good family, this dhāraṇī of Alala of the Jewel is to be held, fully upheld, meditated upon, and thoroughly examined,

It is to be designated, established, explained, analyzed, and clarified in this way. Son of good family, this is the meaning of the meditation upon, and the thorough examination of, the dhāraṇī of Alala of the Jewel.

Then the venerable Śāriputra said to Mañjuśrī, the youthful prince: Mañjuśrī, how long is it since you entered fully into this Dharma teaching?

Mañjuśrī replied: Śāriputra, it is possible to say how long it is since attachment, aversion, and delusion entered it.

Śāriputra said: Son of good family, how long is it since attachment, aversion, and delusion entered it?

Mañjuśrī replied: Śāriputra, it is possible to say how long it is since the earth element entered it.

Śāriputra said: Son of good family, how long is it since the earth element entered it?

Mañjuśrī replied: Śāriputra, it is possible to say how long it is since knowledge and ignorance entered it.

Śāriputra said: Son of good family, how long is it since knowledge and ignorance entered it?

Mañjuśrī replied: Śāriputra, it is possible to say how long it is since the element of space entered it.

Śāriputra said: Mañjuśrī, how long is it since the element of space entered it?

Mañjuśrī replied: Śāriputra, it is possible to say how long it is since all phenomena, whose essential nature is the absence of real entities, entered it.

Then the venerable Śāriputra said to Mañjuśrī, the youthful prince: What capacity do I have to converse with such superior beings? Son of good family, it is like this: even if all the dogs and cats, or all the foxes, gathered together, they could not demolish Meru, the king of mountains. In the same way, son of good family, even all hearers such as I cannot fathom the very edge of a single teaching of a bodhisattva, let alone the teaching of Mañjuśrī, the youthful prince.

Then the bodhisattva Samantabhadra said to Mañjuśrī, the youthful prince: Mañjuśrī, go forth, and request the Tathāgata to grant his protection and full care to those Dharma teachers who, in the later time, in the later age, uphold this king of sūtras.

Then Mañjuśrī, the youthful prince, rose from his own Dharma seat and said to the Blessed One: Blessed One, please explain the special qualities of those Dharma teachers who uphold, read, and fully understand this Dharma teaching, and who also teach it extensively and correctly to others.

The Blessed One declared: Mañjuśrī, those monks who uphold this king of sūtras and speak the Dharma will certainly come to belong to my Dharma body. They will attain the awakening of a buddha. They will draw near to the qualities of the buddhas. Māra, and the sons of the gods belonging to Māra's retinue who watch for an opening, will find no opening against them.

Son of good family, those who give rise even for the span of a finger-snap to an unloving thought toward such a monk, a Dharma teacher, will become far removed even from rebirth as human beings, so what need is there to speak of the awakening of a buddha? Moreover, Mañjuśrī, those who give rise to a faithless thought toward monks who uphold this king of sūtras will have foul, uneven teeth. They will be without tongue or nose. Their hips and arms will be twisted backward. Their bodies will be afflicted by disease. They will be blind. They will be of confused and disordered discernment. They will be of weak diligence,

Mañjuśrī, these are the faults that befall such deluded people. In brief, Mañjuśrī, at the time of their death, in each and every pore of their bodies they will enter immeasurable sufferings of the beings in hell. Even if they are born as humans, they will always be blind, and they will be without a tongue.

Moreover, Mañjuśrī, those who reject this Dharma discourse will in this very life become lepers. Their mouths will look hideous and frightening. Their bodies will be withered, naked, and dried up. They will come to resemble hungry ghosts (pretas). Moreover, Mañjuśrī, as for the places where those deluded people will be born, I know them through the inconceivable wisdom of a buddha.

Then Mañjuśrī, who had become a youth, made this request: "May the Blessed One make a prophecy. May the Sugata make a prophecy."

The Blessed One said: "Mañjuśrī, do not ask in that way, like a babbler. On hearing this, the world with its gods will become intoxicated, will become terrified, will tremble, and will tremble greatly."

Mañjuśrī said: "So that these beings, having heard such a thing, will never again reject the sacred Dharma, may the Blessed One, thinking with compassion, explain it."

The Blessed One said: "Mañjuśrī, beneath the earth there are hells for sentient beings, named as follows: Hot, Extremely Hot, Black Line, Totally Blazing, Intensely Blazing, Hard to Touch, Fierce, Sharp Beak, Iron Mortar, Iron Cauldron, Dark Pit, Head-Praising, Pillar-Fastening, Monkey-Faced, Ever-Blazing, Putrid, Ever-Stinking, Utterly Crushing, Thoroughly Crushing, and Utterly Harm-Crushing. Mañjuśrī, these are the hells of sentient beings in which those deluded people will be born."

Then the bodhisattva Samantabhadra said this to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, when those who speak the Dharma die and pass from here, where will they be born?"

The Blessed One said: "Samantabhadra, it is excellent that you have asked this. Samantabhadra, when such a son or daughter of good family who speaks the Dharma dies and passes from here, he or she will be born in the buddha field, the world realm, called Adorned with Inconceivable Confidence, of the Blessed One, the Tathāgata. In that world realm there is a vast multitude of bodhisattvas who are altogether beautiful. At the time of death, one hundred and twenty million buddhas will appear before that son of good family. In brief, son of good family, the benefits of that son of good family are immeasurable and beyond reckoning. They could not be told even in thousands of eons."

The bodhisattva Samantabhadra said: "Blessed One, in the final time, in the final age, what kinds of beings will there be who will reject this Dharma discourse?"

The Blessed One said: "Samantabhadra, those beings who will reject such teachings as these will for the most part be renunciants, who will say: 'These are poetic compositions. These were not spoken by the Tathāgata. These are fabrications of one's own devising. We have never before heard of these sūtras.' Saying this in their arrogance, they will reject this Dharma discourse. And having rejected it, they will not confess it or make amends for it,

Because they have rejected the Dharma, at the time of death those beings will be born in the hell called Blazing Everywhere, a place of unbearable torment. As soon as they are born, an iron wheel will cut off the heads of those deluded people, and their heads will fall to the ground. Even after this, for many eons, for many hundreds of thousands of eons, for many thousands of eons, they will be blind. And if, after hundreds of eons have passed, they should be born as humans, then in every lifetime they will be born blind. They will be tongueless, with faces turned backward, hunchbacked, with sunken necks and crippled legs, with voices like the barking of dogs. They will be constantly hungry and tormented by thirst, with withered bodies and mouths parched and numb, and they will be born so that all beings who see or hear of them find them repulsive.

Then the entire assembly spoke with one voice: "Blessed One, we confess our fault as a fault: we who have rejected such sūtras, and we who have looked with anger upon those who teach the Dharma. Blessed One, however childish we have been, however deluded, however confused in our discernment, however small-minded, we confess it all. May the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, be mindful of us."

Then those great hearers, together with Śakra (Indra), Brahmā, and the world-protectors, and the monks, nuns, laymen, and laywomen, trembled, and said: "Blessed One, having heard such a teaching, we are utterly terrified."

The Blessed One said: "Friends, it is so. It is just as you say. It is fitting that you should be afraid. Friends, I have attained omniscient wisdom, and even I am afraid. How much more so should my disciples be! Sons of noble family, this Dharma is profound. Not even a human, a nonhuman, a god, a nāga, a yakṣa, or a gandharva is able to disturb it. How then could any other ascetic or brahmin possibly do so? Sons of noble family, this Dharma discourse has become a place of veneration for the world with its gods. Sons of noble family, just as one regards a jewel as a precious treasure, so too should one regard this king of sūtras."

Then the youthful Mañjuśrī said to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, this has been well spoken. But Blessed One, how will such monks fully digest the offerings given in faith?"

The Blessed One said: "Mañjuśrī, those who reject this Dharma discourse and harbor an unloving mind toward those who teach the Dharma, I do not permit them to enjoy offerings given in faith for even a single day, or for even the time it takes to milk a cow. I do not permit them even to spit a single gob of spittle on ground that has been offered to the Saṅgha."

Then the venerable Śāriputra said to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, are the five heinous crimes close to this, or are they far from it?"

The Blessed One said: "Elder Śāriputra, do not ask such a harsh question. Why? Śāriputra, the five heinous crimes are never like rejecting this supreme Dharma discourse. Śāriputra, one who commits the five heinous crimes will exhaust them quickly and will be born again as a human. But a person who rejects the sacred Dharma will never attain the qualities of a buddha. Such a person falls himself and also causes others to fall."

Then the venerable Śāriputra, in the presence of the Blessed One, said this: "Alas! Those beings who reject the sacred Dharma are in suffering. May I never even see in a dream such karmic obscurations as these."

Then the Blessed One said this to the venerable Śāriputra: "Śāriputra, do not think that the Dharma I have taught is something that makes things come about. The world has karma as its owner; it lives on the share of its own karma; it rests upon its own karma. Beings experience the feelings of suffering through their own respective karma, and in that the Tathāgata has no part. Śāriputra, I have taught the path that leads to well-being: the path of going to happiness, going without fear, going to nirvāṇa, going to the deathless. And yet these beings are endowed with wrong conduct of body, wrong conduct of speech, and wrong conduct of mind. They slander the noble ones and hold wrong views. Through the karma of wrong views and through the cause of having rejected the true Dharma, when their bodies break apart and they die, they fall into the lower realms and wretched destinies and are born among the hell-beings. In that, the Tathāgata has no part.

Śāriputra, toward all beings such compassion arises in me that I think: for the sake of even a single being I would undergo the sufferings of the hells for an eon, or for more than an eon, but I would not abandon even one being. Śāriputra, such is the great compassion of the Tathāgata.

Śāriputra, it is like this. Suppose a man has an only son, handsome and beautiful, lovely to behold, of fine complexion, flourishing and endowed with the best qualities. If that son met with some misfortune and his time of death came, the man, thinking of his son, would be pained and unhappy, and would weep and utter lamentations. In just the same way, Śāriputra, the Tathāgata, the arhat, the perfectly complete Buddha, regards suffering beings as an only son. Yet the Tathāgatas do not dwell together with the afflictions. Śāriputra, it is like this: the great ocean does not remain together with a corpse. In just the same way, Śāriputra, the Tathāgatas do not remain together with the afflictions.

Śāriputra, it is like this. Suppose a magician, or a skilled apprentice of a magician, conjures up a great crowd of people at a crossroads. Though they appear to perform various actions, those actions do not exist anywhere in the land and do not exist in any direction; they have not arisen, have not ceased, and will not cease. In just the same way, Śāriputra, the Tathāgatas bring beings to full maturity and even display the conduct of desire. Though they display such deeds, that conduct does not truly exist. Just as space is free of conceptual thought and free of elaboration, so too the body of the Tathāgata is free of conceptual thought and free of elaboration. Nevertheless, in accord with beings, the Tathāgatas act in accord, and in whatever way beings may be tamed, in that way the Tathāgatas engage in accord with them.

Śāriputra, the Tathāgata has no confusion. The Tathāgata has no ignorance. The Tathāgata has no forgetfulness. Śāriputra, I am the elder of the world with its gods, its chief, its foremost, its supreme, its most excellent,

the supreme, the unsurpassed, the unexcelled, the unequalled, and the equal of the unequalled.

Śāriputra, these words are in accord with the truth. Whether they be renunciants or householders, those beings who abandon this exposition of the Dharma will all come to experience such sufferings as those of the hell beings.

Then the venerable Subhūti, having heard this teaching of the Blessed One, joined his palms and said to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, those beings have made a wretched gain. For those who come to abandon this exposition of the Dharma, what sort of tongue will they have?"

The Blessed One declared: "Subhūti, the tongues of those beings will become a hundred thousand leagues in length. Upon each of them, burning, blazing iron ploughs, blazing intensely, blazing and flaring up on every side, a single mass of flame, will plough them five hundred million times. Why is that? Because they did not guard their speech. Subhūti, such is the karmic obscuration of those deluded people."

Then the whole assembly, by the power of the Buddha, joyfully exclaimed: "What the Tathāgata has taught is a great marvel!"

Then Śakra, lord of the gods, said to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, I will carry those monks who teach the Dharma upon my shoulders. I will honor them with flowers, incense, perfumes, garlands, and unguents. Three times by day and three times by night I will bow to them. I will attend upon them and revere them. I will hold them in high regard. I will treat them as my teachers. I will adorn them with splendor. I will make offerings to them. I will serve them with respect. I will make requests of them. I will esteem them, regard them as my teachers, and guard them with reverence. Why is that? Because they are the sons of the Blessed One, born from the body of the Dharma.

"Blessed One, it is like this. A king's son of the warrior caste, anointed upon the crown of his head as heir, of fine form, handsome and pleasing to behold, is worthy of the offerings and homage of all those beneath him. In the same way, those who teach the Dharma are worthy of the offerings and homage of the world with its gods."

Then the Blessed One said to the bodhisattva Samantabhadra: "Noble son, Śakra, lord of the gods, has spoken these words well, in order to hold those Dharma-teaching monks dear, to nurture them, to protect them on every side, and to guard them."

The bodhisattva Samantabhadra said: "Blessed One, I too will protect on every side those sons or daughters of noble family. I will guard them well, I will uphold them, and I will bring them peace and well-being. I will keep watch over them all around, for a hundred leagues in every direction."

Then the Blessed One said to the bodhisattva Samantabhadra: "Noble son, you have spoken these words well. Excellent! Excellent!"

The Blessed One said: "Noble son, if bodhisattvas, great beings, possess one quality, they will obtain the dhāraṇī of the Jewels, Tālala. What is that one quality? It is this: being free of resentment toward all sentient beings.

"Samantabhadra, there are two further qualities of bodhisattvas, great beings. What are the two? They are these: being free of envy, and being free of arrogance. Samantabhadra, these are the two qualities of bodhisattvas, great beings.

Then the venerable Ānanda rose from his seat, arranged his upper robe over one shoulder, set his right knee on the ground, and, bowing with palms joined toward the Blessed One, said to him: "Blessed One, this exposition of the Dharma is profound."

The Blessed One said: "So it is, Ānanda. Form is profound because it is profound. Feeling, perception, formations, and consciousness are profound because they are profound. Emptiness is profound because it is profound. Space is profound because it is profound."

Ānanda said: "Blessed One, I have directly heard eighty-four thousand sections of the Dharma from the Tathāgata, yet I have never before heard an exposition of the Dharma like this one." The Blessed One said: "This exposition of the Dharma is extremely difficult to hear."

Ānanda said: "In the last time, in the last of the five hundred years, when the true Dharma is vanishing, may the Blessed One bless this exposition of the Dharma, so that it may perform the work of the buddhas for many sentient beings."

The Blessed One said: "Ānanda, I have already blessed this exposition of the Dharma. I, the Tathāgata Śākyamuni, have also blessed those who speak this Dharma. Ānanda, whoever writes down this exposition of the Dharma will not suffer illness of the eyes, nor of the tongue, nor of the nose, nor of the teeth. They will not suffer illness of the hands or feet, nor of the head, nor of the ears. Their body will not become wretched, and their body will not become foul-smelling. They will not meet an untimely death through the harm of others. All humans and non-humans will protect them. When they die and pass from here, having wholly abandoned all conceptual elaboration, they will be born among the gods in a happy destiny, in the higher realms."

Ānanda said: "Blessed One, what is the cause and what is the condition by which Māra does not create obstacles when this Dharma is taught?" The Blessed One said: "Ānanda, no Māra is able to obstruct this exposition of the Dharma.

"Ānanda, when the wicked Māra conceives the thought, 'I will go and create obstacles to the teaching of the dhāraṇī of the Jewel-Supreme Ala-la,' at that very moment the wicked Māra sees his own home everywhere ablaze. Ānanda, this exposition of the Dharma is that profound. Ānanda, any place where this exposition of the Dharma is practiced will become a shrine, and it will also become worthy of homage."

Ānanda said: "Blessed One, suppose this exposition of the Dharma also abides in the very place where the Tathāgata dwells, and I come to see the Tathāgata. Having come, if I first pay homage to the Tathāgata and afterward pay homage to this exposition of the Dharma, what fault would there be in that?"

The Blessed One said: "Ānanda, if you were to set aside an exposition of the Dharma such as this and pay homage to me, then you would be at fault. Furthermore, Ānanda, although I am the eldest, the chief, the foremost, and the most excellent in the world with its gods, it was only because I first heard this exposition of the Dharma that I afterward became fully and perfectly awakened to awakening."

Ānanda said: "Blessed One, from whom did the Tathāgata hear this teaching of the precious meditative absorption, the Jewel-Supreme Ala-la? Who is there above the Tathāgata?"

The Blessed One said: "Ānanda, in the world of humans, in the world of gods, and in the world of gandharvas, there is no one above me. Nevertheless, when I was practicing the conduct of a bodhisattva, I heard it directly from the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Unobstructed Wisdom, Heap of Essences, Excellent Light, whose center expands with many inconceivable rays of light. Ānanda, after enduring great hardships for many millions of hundreds of thousands of ages, I heard this exposition of the Dharma. At that time that Tathāgata gave me the prophecy. Therefore, Ānanda, first pay homage to this exposition of the Dharma, and afterward pay homage to me."

Then Śakra (Indra), Brahmā, and the world-protectors all said together in one voice these words: "Ala-la, the Dharma! Ala-la, the Dharma! The magical display of phenomena is Ala-la! Those beings who merely hear the name of this exposition of the Dharma are worthy of homage. How much more so, then, those who uphold it, recite it, fully realize it, and extensively and rightly teach it to others! Such beings should be called Tathāgatas."

Then the Blessed One said to Śakra, Brahmā, and the world-protectors: "Friends, you have spoken these words well. Excellent! Excellent!"

Then the Blessed One extended the organ of his tongue from his mouth, and having illuminated the world-system of the great trichiliocosm with the organ of his tongue, he spoke these words to the bodhisattva Samantabhadra: "Those beings who do not hear this exposition of the Dharma are like a blind man who cannot see the light of the sun, like a merchant who does not know the proper means, and like a poor man who looks to the faces of others for help.

"If for their sake I give this example, then in order that beings may not be kept far from a heap of precious Dharma such as this, I now make this request of you for the sake of this exposition of the Dharma and for the sake of all beings: son of good lineage, are you willing?"

Then the bodhisattva Samantabhadra rose from his seat, arranged his upper robe over one shoulder, set his right knee on the ground, and bowed toward the Blessed One with palms joined, weeping. It trembled, trembled greatly, trembled all over. It quaked, quaked greatly, quaked all over. It resounded, resounded greatly, resounded all over. It rumbled, rumbled greatly, rumbled all over. It was convulsed, convulsed greatly, convulsed all over. And a great light appeared in the world.

Then the bodhisattva Samantabhadra, joining his ten fingers in homage and weeping, spoke these words to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, I am willing. Sugata, I am willing. Blessed One, you are omniscient, you wish only the benefit of all beings, and you are endowed with great compassion. Moreover, I am one born from the Tathāgata. Why then should I not act in accord with the Tathāgata's word? Blessed One, the Tathāgata's word is a word of nectar. How could I possibly have the power to act against the Tathāgata's word? Blessed One, I will make amends for what I have done; I will not fail to make amends."

Then the Blessed One said to the bodhisattva Samantabhadra: "Excellent! Son of the Victor, excellent, excellent! Among the sons of the Tathāgatas you are the eldest, the foremost, the supreme, the holiest, the most excellent. I entrust this exposition of the Dharma wholly to you. This is my Teacher, and therefore you must guard it. From time to time, air it in the sun. Keep it in a precious vessel and carry it upon your shoulder. In the later time, in the later age, do not trust those sinful monks. Son of good lineage, this is my instruction to you."

The bodhisattva Samantabhadra asked: "Blessed One, what kind of renunciants will appear?"

The Blessed One said: "Son of good lineage, do not ask that, you crow."

The Blessed One said: "Samantabhadra, listen to what I will explain. Such is the magical display of the samādhi of the sages.

Through the wisdom that gathers beings, they accomplish magical emanations and the supreme samādhis. Through every door of activity and of honoring others, through a thousand means, they tame sentient beings.

Some tame through the door of making offerings to the Tathāgatas, through the door of giving that is beyond thought, through the doors of the qualities of ascetic purification and of every kind of moral discipline, and through the doors of patience that is inexhaustible and unshakable.

Some tame through the doors of austere vows, hardship, and diligence, through meditative concentration and the door of perfect peaceful abiding, through the doors of wisdom that ascertains the meaning in every aspect, through the doors of skillful means that embrace every kind of conduct, through the brahmavihāras and the door of the higher knowledges, through the door of benefiting others by the means of gathering disciples, through the doors of accumulating merit and of wisdom, through truth, reliance, and the door of liberation.

Some tame through the doors of the powers, the faculties, and the path, through the door of liberation by the vehicle of the hearers, through the door of purification by the vehicle of the pratyekabuddhas, and through the door of the magical display of the supreme vehicle.

Some tame through the doors of impermanence and suffering; some through the doors of selflessness and the absence of a life-force; through the door of the perception of the unpleasant and freedom from attachment; and through the door of the samādhi of peace and cessation.

However many doors of conduct beings have, they enter just as many doors of Dharma. Through all those doors of liberation, they tame the beings of the world according to their dispositions. Those who, through all the doors of liberation, deliberately tame the beings of the world, cannot be grasped by anyone as a fixed characteristic. Such is the magical display of samādhi.

Now there is the supreme samādhi called the Array of Joy, which brings all beings to full maturation. Filled with joy, happiness, delight, and great gladness, they become steadfast in intention and tame sentient beings.

Wherever there is famine, and the world's necessities, everything that brings happiness, have become exceedingly scarce, there they act with deliberate intent for all. They give gifts and accomplish the aims of beings. By completely giving away the best food and drink and the finest flavors, clothing and garments, precious things of many kinds, kingdoms, wealth, and even their own cherished possessions, they tame beings who delight in giving.

Those steadfast ones adorn their bodies with the supreme marks, wear garments and the finest ornaments, are decked with garlands and anointed with perfumes. By displaying such forms, they tame sentient beings, for those who delight and are satisfied at the sight.

Those supreme in intellect reveal forms that are supreme and beautiful, the most excellent of forms, and so tame sentient beings who delight in form.

With the sweet voice of the kalapiṅka bird, and the cry of the cuckoo and the swan, with the sounds of drums, of Brahmā, and of the kiṃnaras, they teach the Dharma as it is to all who are inclined to hear.

Through the eighty-four thousand gateways of Dharma, which are the great sweep of the Victors' work for beings, they tame the beings of the world according to their dispositions.

They also become companions in happiness and in suffering, companions in what is beneficial and in what is harmful. They become companions in every undertaking, and through companionship they tame sentient beings. For the sake of their companions they endure suffering, harm, and the faults of conditioned existence. For the benefit and happiness of all beings, they accept harm for the sake of their companions.

Where no buddha appears and there is no Dharma, where one goes to the wilderness and no liberation is perceived, there they wholly renounce kingship and wealth, and with a mind at peace they appear in a place of no abode.

Those... home, bondage, and the realms of existence.

In order to free all beings, they dwell in none of the pleasures of desire. They go forth and teach liberation well. Explaining those ten practices, they proclaim the Dharma-conduct of the great beings and the entire conduct of the seers, and they accomplish the aims of beings.

Wherever there are boundless beings who live long, are happy, and have few afflictions, there they show beings oppressed by old age, afflicted by sickness and children, without power over themselves, fallen under the power of death. Where desire blazes and aversion blazes fiercely, where the world is ablaze with the great fire of delusion, and blazes with old age, sickness, and death, having shown such a world, they tame sentient beings.

By means of the ten powers of a buddha, the four fearlessnesses, and the eighteen distinctive qualities, they declare the greatness of the buddha's nature. Through the buddha's qualities they work for the good of beings. Teaching all of this, and demonstrating miraculous power, through the force of the blessing of the form body they display the miraculous power of the Tathāgata everywhere, and by miraculous transformations they tame sentient beings.

By many kinds of skillful means they move through the world and accomplish the aims of beings. Like a lotus unstained by water, unattached to the world, they move among beings, bringing joy and inspiring faith. They become panegyrists, bards, and composers of praise; dancers, drummers, acrobats, and musicians; they dance adorned with garlands and ornaments; as masters of illusion they display many forms everywhere. They become villagers and village headmen, ministers of state, likewise caravan leaders, merchant chiefs, and householders. They become kings and ministers, escorts and envoys, physicians, and experts in worldly treatises and rites. In the wilderness they become great trees, medicines, inexhaustible treasures of jewels, wish-fulfilling gems, and trees that grant desires. For those who have lost their way and wander in pits, they become guides who show the road.

Soon after they have appeared in the world, they come to know, and those who do not understand the ways of karma among beings, to all of them they teach farming, trade, the dharma of commerce, and the various crafts, and they converse with the world about these things. Whatever practice is harmless and without injury, which the wise praise as bringing happiness to beings, the arts of strength and lineage, medicine, and the many treatises, all these those seers declare.

Whatever supreme and holy conduct of the seers the world with its gods reveres, and all the foremost austerities, vows, and hardships, those wise ones proclaim these too. They go among all the wandering non-Buddhists (tīrthikas), those who practice the way of Gautama, the way of silence; to naked, unclothed ascetics who devote themselves to such ways, they appear as skilled teachers of the tīrthikas. To all those who uphold a livelihood by their teaching, those with a superior or with none, those with long matted hair and young ascetic vows, to these too they become teachers. To the followers of the sun, those with the five austerities, those who practice the vows of dogs, cows, and deer, those with the Naked Ones and the thirty tīrthika sects, to these too they become teachers.

To those who delight in entering the knowledge of the gods, who wander in lands near the tīrthikas, who live on roots, fruits, and water, they appear as supreme in the Dharma of inconceivable meditations. To those who sit in a squatting posture, who wander alone, who lie on thorns, grass, and ashes, and who dwell on pestles and beds of wood, to these too they become teachers. For all the outer tīrthikas, as many as there are, they discern their dispositions and inclinations, and by fierce and unbearable austerity, hard to follow, they tame them, leading the tīrthikas to abandon suffering.

Knowing that the world is troubled by wrong views, for the tīrthikas who abide in bad views he shows the truth through a subtle foundation and by skillful means. To some he teaches the truth through the foundation of the mantras of those who move through the sky, through a most secret foundation. To some he speaks in clear and direct words; to some in the secret words of the gods; to some in words that distinguish the letters; to some in the words of vajra-like definitive meaning; to some in words that know how to tame opponents; to some in treatises, in words of irrefutable liberation. To some he speaks in the languages of humans, in words of entry open to all and in etymological explanations. To some he speaks in the words and etymologies of the gods, in the etymologies of the nāgas and the yakṣas. To some he speaks in the words of rākṣasas, spirits, and flesh-eaters, of gandharvas, kumbhāṇḍas, and great-bellied serpents, of kinnaras, asuras, and garuḍas. By these he teaches the truth and leads beings to liberation. Knowing the truth and the way of etymologies, he thus teaches, through his knowledge of the inconceivable pathways of speech, the nature of all the Victor's teachings without remainder. Such is the magical display of their meditative absorption.

Here ends the fourth volume, the last.

Their meditative absorption is the supreme joy of beings. It accomplishes manifest benefit for all beings, and it sends forth rays of light, inconceivable and vast. Having sent forth the rays, it tames sentient beings. Whoever sends forth the rays of "Good to See," and whoever, among sentient beings, knows those rays, their seeing is not without meaning, for it is the cause of the supreme, unsurpassed wisdom. To all humans one fully shows the buddha and fully shows the Dharma, and by making images of the Victor's body and showing stūpas, the rays of "Good to See" come to be accomplished.

One sends forth the rays called "Light-Maker," which outshine the light of the gods and dispel all dust and darkness. The world is brightly illumined by that light which benefits it. Those sentient beings who are inspired by that light take up lamps to honor the Victors. By holding lamps to honor the Victors, they become Victors who make lamps for the world. Having offered to the Victors lamps of grain, butter, ghee, grass and wood, torches and shining lamps, incense, essences, and jeweled lamps, they obtain the Light-Maker.

One sends forth the rays called "Deliverer." Those sentient beings who are inspired by that light put on armor for the sake of delivering beings from the broad ocean of craving and existence. Those who put on armor for the sake of delivering beings from the broad ocean of craving and existence are themselves fully delivered from the four rivers, and they show the city of happiness without sorrow. By building many bridges and causeways, and launching boats to ferry people across rivers and roads, and by praising peace and rejecting the conditioned, the rays of "Deliverer" come to be accomplished.

One sends forth the rays that quench thirst. Those sentient beings who are inspired by that light fully abandon the thirst for the qualities of desire and seek as their goal the taste of delight in the Dharma. Having fully abandoned that thirst, and having come to seek the taste of delight in the Dharma, they become buddhas who rain down the nectar of immortality and completely dispel the world's thirst of craving. They dig ponds, rivers, wells, and springs, and make lakes for the sake of awakening, and by disparaging desire and praising meditative concentration, the dispelling of craving comes to be accomplished.

One sends forth the rays that bring joy. Those sentient beings who are inspired by that light, with joy increasing, generate the mind, resolving, "For the sake of supreme awakening may I become self-arisen." They make images of compassionate bodies adorned with the major marks, seated upon lotus thrones, and by constantly speaking of the buddhas' qualities,

Thus the ray that brings delight comes to be accomplished. One sends forth the rays that bring contentment, and those sentient beings who are inspired by that light are always delighted and content with the Buddha, delighted and content with the Dharma, delighted and content with the Saṅgha. Always delighted in the Buddha, and delighted in the Dharma and the Saṅgha, they come to keep constant company with the buddhas, the Dharma, and the noble assemblies, and they attain unsurpassed patience. Urging many beings, and bringing to their minds the recollection of the Buddha, the supreme Dharma, and the Saṅgha, and proclaiming the qualities of the awakening mind, thus the ray that brings contentment comes to be accomplished.

One sends forth the rays of accumulated merit, and those sentient beings who are inspired by that light, seeking unsurpassed awakening, give many varied gifts. When they make offerings without stinginess, they fulfill the wishes of those who ask, and by giving whatever is wished for, the ray of accumulated merit comes to be accomplished.

One sends forth the rays that possess wisdom, and those sentient beings who are inspired by that light, from the gateway of a single teaching, grasp in an instant the many gateways of the Dharma. They encourage beings to hold the distinctions of the Dharma, they unfold the wisdom that is certain about meaning, and they expound the words and meanings of the Dharma. Thus the ray that possesses wisdom is accomplished.

One sends forth the rays of the lamp of discernment, and those sentient beings who are inspired by that light enter into the teaching that is empty, selfless, unborn, unarisen, and without intrinsic nature. They teach that phenomena have no master, are empty and without agent, like an illusion, a mirage, the moon in water, like a dream, like a reflection. Through such teaching the lamp of discernment is accomplished.

One sends forth the rays of the Dharma's wondrous display, and those sentient beings who are inspired by that light attain the dhāraṇī of inexhaustible patience and uphold the treasury of all the Tathāgatas. They benefit the holders of the Dharma, protect the sages through the Dharma, and, by guarding beings with the Dharma, the ray of the Dharma's wondrous display comes to be accomplished.

One sends forth the rays that possess generosity, and those sentient beings who are stricken with poverty, inspired by it, come to know that wealth is impermanent and unstable, and they come to delight always in giving. Beings who are untamed, hard to tame, and miserly, knowing riches to be like a dream or a cloud, expand their giving with joyful minds. Thus the ray endowed with generosity is accomplished.

One sends forth the rays that are free of remorse, and those sentient beings of broken discipline who are inspired by it come to abide in utterly pure moral discipline and generate the mind, resolving, "May I become self-arisen." They purify the paths of virtuous action, lead many beings into moral discipline, and cause them to hold the awakening mind rightly. Thus the ray free of remorse is accomplished.

One sends forth the rays of the array of patience, and those impatient sentient beings who are inspired by it abandon anger, contempt, and pride, and come always to delight in patience. They bear with the faults of the wicked, and are not disturbed, for the sake of awakening, and by constantly praising the qualities of patience, the array of patience is accomplished.

One sends forth the rays that possess ascetic discipline, and those lazy sentient beings who are inspired by it become diligent in the Three Jewels, making offerings with tireless effort. Having made offerings to the Three Jewels with tireless effort, they pass utterly beyond the paths of the four māras and swiftly touch unsurpassed awakening.

Having caused many sentient beings to take up diligence, and having made offerings to the Three Jewels, they uphold the sacred Dharma in the time of its decline. Through this one obtains the light that possesses ascetic discipline.

One sends forth the rays that bring peace, and those sentient beings who are disturbed and agitated, when inspired by them, become free of attachment, free of anger, free of delusion. Having come to realization, they become possessed of a mind in meditative equipoise. Having utterly abandoned evil companions and those of faulty conduct, and having praised the peaceful meditative concentration of the wilderness, one accomplishes the light that brings peace.

One sends forth the rays that array wisdom, and those sentient beings of confused understanding, as many as are inspired by them, come to comprehend the truths, steadfastness, the modes of liberation, and the knowledge of the faculties. Having comprehended these, they attain the meditative absorption called the Lamp of the Sun, and become a Victor who makes wisdom shine. Having wholly given up kingdom, wealth, and possessions, and having sought the Dharma for the sake of awakening, one reveres that Dharma and teaches it well. Thus the light that arrays wisdom is accomplished.

One sends forth the rays that possess buddhas, and those sentient beings who are inspired by that light see many thousands of inconceivable buddhas seated upon sacred lotuses. By telling of the greatness of the buddhas, the liberation of the buddhas, and the boundless emanations of the buddhas, and by proclaiming the powers and miraculous abilities of the buddhas, one accomplishes the light that possesses buddhas.

One sends forth the rays that bestow fearlessness, and those sentient beings in danger and terror who are touched by them are freed from all fears: from spirits, from baleful planets, from killing, beating, and bondage, and from plague and epidemic. Whoever has protected sentient beings by giving fearlessness, has turned others back from taking life, and has given refuge to those who seek refuge from fear, by that the light that bestows fearlessness is accomplished.

One sends forth the rays that bring all happiness, and those who are sick and ill, when touched by them, are freed in every way from the suffering of illness, and also attain the joys of meditative concentration and absorption. One obtains this by healing the sick, and by giving the fruits of medicine, roots, precious restorative elixirs, incense and ointments, molasses, honey, milk and butter, grains, oil, food, and drink.

One sends forth the rays that show the Buddha, and those who, at the very end of their lives, are inspired by them come to recollect the Buddha and to see the Buddha. When these ones pass from this life, they go to where the buddhas dwell. One obtains this light by recollecting the Buddha at the time of death, by showing the joy-giving image of the Victor, and by declaring, at the moment of death, one's going for refuge to the Buddha.

One sends forth the rays that proclaim the Dharma, and those sentient beings who are inspired by that light listen to the Dharma, read it, and ask for it in writing. They come always to delight in the Dharma and to have devotion to it. In times of Dharma famine, one teaches the Dharma, fulfills the aspirations of those who seek the Dharma, arouses enthusiasm, and understands the Dharma through diligence. Through such teaching one obtains the light that proclaims the Dharma.

In order to inspire all the sons of the Buddha, one sends forth the rays endowed with melody, and whatever strains of sound there are in the three worlds, all are heard as the voice of the Tathāgata. One praises the great sages with secret hymns of praise, and for the sake of the meaning of the Victor's words, one offers cymbals and sweet music, great drums and bells, proclaiming them to all beings. Through this the light endowed with melody is accomplished.

One sends forth the rays that bestow the deathless, and those sentient beings who are inspired by that light wholly abandon all heedless conduct and devote themselves to practicing all good qualities. Conditioned things are never a source of happiness; they are completely filled with the many torments of suffering.

By proclaiming that cessation is ever blissful, peaceful, and secure, one obtains the gift of the deathless.

One sends forth the rays of distinction, and those sentient beings who are inspired by that light come to know the distinction of the Victors' moral discipline, the distinction of their meditative absorption, and the distinction of their wisdom and learning. Because one praises and extols, for the sake of awakening, the Great Sage, the king of the Dharma, as supreme in discipline, supreme in absorption, and supreme in wisdom, the light of distinction is accomplished.

One sends forth the rays of arrayed jewels, and those sentient beings who are inspired by that light obtain inexhaustible treasuries of jewels. They honor the great seer with the finest jewels. They offer jewels to the Victors and to the Victors' stūpas, though people are gathered in by the jewels of the afflictions. Because one has offered jewels to the Victors, the rays of arrayed jewels are accomplished.

One sends forth the rays of radiant fragrance, and those sentient beings who are inspired by that light come to sense a delightful fragrance, such as does not exist among humans, and they become certain of the qualities of the buddhas. With the incense and perfumed unguents of humans and gods they make fine offerings to the chief of men. Because one has made images of the Victor and stūpas from fragrant substances, that radiant fragrance is accomplished.

One sends forth the rays of varied adornment: banners, pennants, and many kinds of supreme parasols, the sound of cymbals resounding, and fragrant incense burning. They beautify the sublime palaces and scatter flowers. With cymbals they welcome the Victors, and they honor them with flower garlands, incense, powders, banners, pennants, and supreme parasols. Thus the varied adornment is accomplished.

One sends forth the rays of great serenity, and the grounds are made as level as the palm of a hand. Because one has cleansed the stūpas and the dwellings of the seers, the light of great serenity is accomplished.

One sends forth the rays endowed with clouds, and clouds raining fragrance abide everywhere. Because one has sprinkled the surroundings of stūpas with fragrant water, the light endowed with clouds is fully obtained.

One sends forth the rays of flowing garments, and the naked come to be clothed and adorned. Because one has given clothing, robes, and many kinds of ornaments and garlands, the flowing garments are accomplished.

One sends forth the rays endowed with supreme flavor, and the hungry come to obtain food of supreme flavor. Because one has given food, drink, and many kinds of supreme flavors, the light endowed with supreme flavor is accomplished.

One sends forth the rays that reveal wealth, and the poor obtain treasuries of jewels. Because one has presented to the Three Jewels an inexhaustible store of treasure, the light that reveals wealth is obtained.

One sends forth the rays that purify the eyes, and then the blind see forms of many kinds. Because one has offered lamps to the Victors and to the Victors' stūpas, the rays that purify the eyes are accomplished.

One sends forth the rays that purify the ears, and those whose hearing was impaired come to hear many sounds. Because one has offered cymbals to the Victors and to the Victors' stūpas, the rays that purify the ears are accomplished.

One sends forth the rays that purify the nose, and those who never smelled before come to perceive sweet fragrance. Because one has offered fine incense to the Victors and to the Victors' stūpas, the rays that purify the nose are accomplished.

One sends forth the rays that purify the tongue, and with pleasing and melodious sounds they praise the buddhas. Having abandoned unpleasant and harsh speech, one speaks gently, and thus the rays that purify the tongue are accomplished.

One sends forth the rays that purify the body, and those whose faculties were impaired come to have sound faculties. Because one has prostrated one's body to the Victors and to the Victors' stūpas, the purification of the body is obtained.

One sends forth the rays that purify the mind, and all who are mad regain their minds. Because one has led the mind through the power of samādhi, the rays that purify the mind are accomplished.

One sends forth the rays that purify form, and the lords of men behold the bodies of the buddhas, beyond all thought. Through the many and varied painted images of the Buddha's body, and through adorning stūpas, the rays that purify form are accomplished.

One sends forth the rays that purify sound, and sound is fully known as not empty of sound. Because one has taught that sounds arise from conditions and are like an echo, the rays that purify sound are accomplished.

One sends forth the rays that purify smell, and every foul odor becomes a supreme fragrance. These are the rays of the awakened buddha-field for one who has bathed the Victors' stūpas with the finest perfumed water.

One sends forth the rays that purify taste, and what is poisonous becomes poison-free and a supreme flavor. These are the rays of one who has offered every supreme flavor to the buddhas together with the hearers, and to one's father and mother.

One sends forth the rays that purify touch, and what is rough to the touch becomes soft and pleasant. Showers of short spears, three-pointed lances, swords, and great arrows turn into soft garlands, lotuses, and blue lotuses. These are the rays of one who has spread out soft and pleasant-to-touch cloths of many kinds as a path for the Victors to walk upon, and who has offered flowers, ointments, soft garments, and garlands and crowns.

One sends forth the rays that purify phenomena, and from every pore of all the Victors who benefit the world, inconceivable teachings of the Dharma are heard, and all who have faith are satisfied. Those of excellent intelligence, of vast intelligence, of unerring intelligence, of boundless intelligence, possessed of supreme wisdom and of distinguished mind: it is the supreme liberated lives of such beings that are taught in this way.

Whoever hears these sacred accounts of liberation, holds them in trust, and proclaims them to the world, such persons are possessed of wonder and marvel in the Dharma. They bring delight in the human world. Those who have faith in a Dharma such as this are rare among the multitudes of ordinary beings. Those who have gathered virtue and made merit have faith in this through the power of their causes.

Even those people who trust the Vehicle of the Hearers are few in the human world. Fewer still are those who trust the vehicle of the solitary buddhas, and fewer than those are those in the Great Vehicle. Rare is the one who has entered the Great Vehicle. If even faith in this Dharma is so rare, what need is there to speak of entering it and holding it, or of practicing accordingly?

Suppose someone were to carry the three-thousandfold world, with its mountains and oceans, upon the head for the whole of a future aeon. However difficult that deed, it does not compare to the difficulty of having faith in this Dharma.

Suppose someone were to serve with every happiness, for aeons, as many beings as there are dust motes in the three-thousandfold world. The merit of faith in this Dharma surpasses that; no such distinction of merit is found in that service.

Suppose someone were to hold ten buddha-fields, with their mountains and oceans, in the palm of the hand for an aeon. However difficult that deed, it does not compare to the difficulty of having faith in this Dharma.

Suppose someone were to serve with every virtue, for aeons, as many beings as there are dust motes in ten buddha-fields. The merit of faith in this Dharma surpasses that; no such distinction of merit is found in that service.

Suppose someone were to honor with every happiness, for aeons, as many buddhas as there are dust motes in ten buddha-fields. The merit of one who has taken up this chapter of the Dharma is more excellent than that.

Neither māras, nor the gods of the retinue of māras, nor those who have gone forth, nor householders, nor likewise kings, nor great ministers, nor lay patrons can harm them. So they cried out, with a clamor of shouts and uproar.

Then the venerable Subhūti said this to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, it is rare for sūtras as precious as these to appear in the world. And yet, by the power of the Tathāgata, such a Dharma has appeared in the world, and by the very power of the Tathāgata, those beings have also been able to hear it."

Then the Blessed One extended his tongue, and said to the venerable Ānanda: "Ānanda, you must hold this Dharma discourse. Retain it. Master it. Fully comprehend it."

Then the venerable Ānanda circumambulated the Blessed One three times and said this: "Blessed One, I have taken hold of this Dharma discourse, and from now on I shall not let it lapse."

When the Blessed One had spoken thus, that entire retinue, together with the bodhisattvas, the noble bodhisattva Samantabhadra, Mañjuśrī, the youthful one, the venerable Śāriputra, and the other great hearers, and the world with its gods, humans, asuras, and gandharvas, rejoiced and praised what the Blessed One had spoken.

Thus ends the Dharma discourse called the Noble Dhāraṇī of the Precious Talala.

It was translated, revised, and finalized by the Indian preceptor Surendrabodhi and the great editor-translator, the venerable Yeshé Dé.
