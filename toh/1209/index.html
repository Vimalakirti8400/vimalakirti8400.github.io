About this text

The Stages of Clear Realization, known in Sanskrit as the Abhisamayakrama and in Tibetan as Ngönpar Tokpé Rimpa, is a short tantric treatise devoted to the meditative practice of the Hevajra Tantra. Opening with homage to glorious Hevajra, it presents itself as a teaching on the precious clear realization of that tantra, which is said to have come from glorious Uḍḍiyāna. Its opening passages turn to the subtle body, describing the thirty-two channels through which the awakening mind flows and focusing on the three principal channels, Lalanā, Rasanā, and Avadhūtī, which it correlates with wisdom, skillful means, and nondual reality, and with both conventional and ultimate truth. The work is Toh 1209 in the Tengyur, where it belongs among the Indian commentarial and practice texts of the tantra section associated with the Hevajra cycle.

Translation

In the language of India: abhisamayakrama nāma. In the language of Tibet: The Stages of Clear Realization.

Homage to glorious Hevajra.

I shall teach the precious clear realization of the Hevajra Tantra, which came from glorious Uḍḍiyāna.

The thirty-two channels through which the awakening mind flows are of the nature of both the conventional and the ultimate. Their chief ones are three: Lalanā, Rasanā, and Avadhūtī. Lalanā is of the nature of wisdom. Rasanā is of the nature of skillful means. Avadhūtī is the form of nondual reality in the cakra of the place of origin. The one through which Akṣobhya flows is Lalanā. The one through which blood flows is Rasanā. The one through which the awakening mind flows is Avadhūtī. The extreme subtlety of all phenomena is Lalanā. The freedom of all phenomena from conceptual elaboration is Rasanā. That whose nature is indivisible is Avadhūtī.

I shall now state the locations and natures of these thirty-two channels. At the big toe of the left foot is Abhedyā, because she is without intrinsic nature. At the shin, Sūkṣmarūpā is of the nature of the sphere of space. On the left thigh is the Loving One, because she embodies the five sense pleasures. At the channel on the left side of the place of origin is Vāmā, because she is the abode of the wisdom that holds the excellent reality. At the navel, Vāmanī embodies what is concordant with the Great Victor. On the left portion of the pistil of the heart is Kūrmajā, because she is the form of existence and nirvāṇa joined in union. At the left breast, Bhāvakī is the meditation on great bliss. On the left side of the neck is Sekā, because she makes one stainless. In the palm of the left hand is Doṣāvatī, because she brings about transformation through slaps and the like. She Who Enters Water is so because great bliss enters. At the cheek is Sumo, because she reveals the basis of all. At the left eye, Śarvarī has as her nature the bathing of great bliss. At the root of the left ear is Śītadā, because wind arises abundantly. On the left side of the brain is Uṣmā, because she possesses fire. At the crown, on the left side of the great Vajradhara, is Lalanā, because her nature is play. In front is Avadhūtī, because she is free from elaboration. On the right is Rasanā, because she wishes to taste. Behind is the Measure, because she is the measure of the body and so on. On the right side of the brain is Kṛṣṇavarṇā, because Akṣobhya is black. At the root of the right ear is Sāmānyā, because she pervades everything. At the right eye is Hetudāyikā, because she has become the efficient cause. At the cheek is Viyogā, because joy is abandoned. At the upper lip is Premaṇī, because she makes all places cherished. At the throat is Siddhā, because her nature is beginningless. At the right hand is Pāvakī, because she heightens the flavor of erotic grace. At the right breast is Sumanā, because one of good mind attains the arising of the fruit of the body. On the right side of the heart is Trivṛttā, because she embodies body, speech, and mind. In the region of the right side of the navel is Kāminī, because she is lovely in desire. At the place of origin is Gehā, because she is the abode of all the elements. At the thigh is Caṇḍikā, because she pervades everything with great bliss. At the shin is Kātyāyanī, because she cuts off obstructors. At the right big toe is Māradārikā, because no suffering whatsoever enters.

This is because, in accordance with their locations in the thirty-two channels, the sixteen letters, and the days as well, beginning with the first lunar day, run in forward order and in reverse order.

These channels are the three realms of existence transformed, transformed by great bliss whose nature is the body and so on. āli is the aspects of ā and the rest. kāli is the aspects of kā and the rest. These are vajra and lotus, method and wisdom; the Dharma body, the enjoyment body, the emanation body, and the innate; the joy of cessation and the supreme; mind, speech, and body.

As for e vaṃ ma ya: the aspect of e is the source of phenomena, emptiness, joy, and the goddess Locanā. The aspect of vaṃ is the vajra, signlessness, supreme joy, and Māmakī. The aspect of ma is greatness, wishlessness, the joy of cessation, and Pāṇḍaravāsinī. The aspect of ya is moistness, the unconditioned, the innate, and Tārā.

At the place of origin is the emanation cakra of sixty-four petals. The thirty-two letters beginning with ka, in forward and reverse order, make sixty-four letters. The letters of the sixteen emptinesses of each of the four lotus petals and so on, purified and by nature transformed into Locanā and the others, arisen in the form of sixty-four petals: this is the cakra of the abode.

At the heart is the Dharma cakra of eight petals, with eight letters, a ka ca ṭa ta pa ya śa: the elements and what derives from the elements, extremely subtle form. It is the eight-petaled lotus.

At the throat is the enjoyment cakra of sixteen petals, with ā i ī u ū ṛ ṝ ḷ ḹ e ai o au aṃ aḥ. The sixteen emptinesses purified are the sixteen-year-old wisdom consort in the aspect of enjoyment. That itself is the sixteen-petaled enjoyment cakra.

At the crown is the cakra of great bliss of thirty-two petals, with ka kha ga gha ṅa, ca cha ja jña ña, ṭa ṭha ḍa dha ṇa, ta tha da dha na, pa pha ba bha ma, ya ra la va, śa ṣa sa. The marks of the qualities of a great being, purified, are the thirty-two petals.

According to the number of the four cakras, the four moments are variety, maturation, consummation, and freedom from characteristics. The Four Truths are suffering, origin, cessation, and the path. The four realities are the reality of self, the reality of mantra, the reality of deity, and the reality of primordial wisdom. The four joys are joy, supreme joy, the joy of cessation, and innate joy. The four schools are the Sthaviras, the Sarvāstivādins, the Saṃmitīyas, and the Mahāsāṃghikas.

These abide distinctly in the four places by way of the divisions of the moments, the truths, and the realities, and by way of the nature of the schools, as small, middling, greater, and greatest.

The moon is the left, the sun is the right; āli is subtle, kāli is gross.

The sixteen transitions are the Ram, the Bull, the Pair, Karkaṭa the Crab, the Lion, the Maiden, the Scales, the Scorpion, the Bow, the Makara, the Vase, the Fish, the moon seized by Rāhu, the sun seized by Rāhu, the cessation of the drop, and the cessation of space. These, the sixteen transitions and so on, are the gross. Or else, by the division of the leftward and rightward circling of the seminal fluid, it comes to shift through sixteen places.

On the first lunar day it is in the big toe in the form of ā; on the second in the calf in the form of ā; on the third in the thigh in the form of i; on the fourth in the place of birth in the form of ā; on the fifth in the navel in the form of u; on the sixth in the heart in the form of ū; on the seventh in the breast in the form of ṛ; on the eighth in the throat in the form of ṝ; on the ninth at the tips of the hands in the form of ḷ; on the tenth in the cheeks in the form of ḹ; on the eleventh in the eyes in the form of e; on the twelfth at the root of the ears in the form of i; on the thirteenth in the forehead in the form of o; on the fourteenth at the crown of the head in the form of au; and on the full moon, in the left and right armpits, as aṃ aḥ. It comes to shift in the extremely subtle forms of each of these and of their portions.

The sixty-four units are taken from the divisions of time's aspects: the twenty-eight lunar mansions, the fifteen days, the nine planets, and the twelve portions. By these divisions of time's aspects the units are to be taken. In the night and the day there are sixty water-clock periods, and in these same, by waning and waxing, four more. For that very reason, the sixty-four units are to be counted by time. Or else, with respect to wisdom and means, by the division into ultimate and conventional, the units are taken through the sixteen transitions, purified as being the very places in each of these, by the division of the intoxicating one.

These are the thirty-two channels. All of these are gathered into the four cakras. And again, the four dissolve into the great mudrā called the caṇḍālī blazing at the navel.

The Stages of Clear Realization is complete.

Translator's notes

The channel names are matched to the Sanskrit list of the Hevajra Tantra where the correspondence is clear. The channel at the upper lip (sdu gu ma) is read as sdug gu ma, the Tibetan rendering of Premaṇī in the Hevajra Tantra, which also fits its position in the list. Four names could not be identified with confidence and are translated from the Tibetan: "the Loving One," "She Who Enters Water," "Sumo," and "the Measure." "The Loving One" and "Sumo" stand where the Hevajra Tantra has Divyā and Mātarā. "She Who Enters Water" stands where it has Mahiṣā. "The Measure" (tshad ma, perhaps from a reading Pramāṇā) stands where it has Pravaṇā. This list also does not follow the canonical list exactly: Surūpiṇī is absent, and Kātyāyanī appears.

"Akṣobhya" in "the one through which Akṣobhya flows" stands, as in the Hevajra Tantra, for semen.

"The basis of all" reads kun gzhi for the written kun bzhi.

In the sentence on Trivṛttā, the written "and" is taken as the topic marker. The term rendered "units" (Tibetan dbyi gu) is obscure. The context shows it is a set of sixty-four time divisions (28 + 15 + 9 + 12, or 60 + 4), probably corresponding to the sixty-four petals or channels of the navel cakra. "Water-clock periods" renders sbu gu can, literally "the tubed ones," which are taken here as the nāḍikās (ghaṭikās), sixty of which make a day and night. In the phrase "of their portions," the Tibetan reads chang ("liquor"), which is taken here as a slip for cha ("portion"). In the first sentence, "greater" renders lhag ma, which is read as lhag pa. The sense of "the intoxicating one" (myos byed) in the final technical sentence is uncertain.
