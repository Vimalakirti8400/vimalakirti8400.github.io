AI Stupa Translation Project

About this project

This site is a map of the Tibetan Buddhist canon, the Kangyur and Tengyur of the Derge edition, with an English translation for every text it can offer one for. Where an authoritative human translation exists, above all at 84000: Translating the Words of the Buddha, we link to it. Where none exists yet, an autonomous "stupa" offers a provisional English rendering, freely, until the masters' work arrives.

It is an independent, non-commercial project by one person, with no institutional backing and no affiliation with 84000 or Esukhia. Nothing here is for sale, and nothing asks for an account.

How the translations are made

The translations are generated by an AI language model, Claude (Anthropic), running on a Raspberry Pi inside a 3D-printed stupa. The stupa translates about 108 texts a month, four a day, at a deliberately ceremonial pace.

The Tibetan source is Esukhia's public-domain etext of the Derge edition (the May 2019 release). Each text is cut into passages and translated in order, with the names and terms of earlier passages carried into later ones. A glossary of names and technical terms, checked against the Mahāvyutpatti and the standard dictionaries, is attached to each passage. The instructions to the model were written with care for Tibetan grammar (ordinals, particles, commands and wishes, the instrumental agent, humble speech) and revised after review. Mantras, dhāraṇīs and Sanskrit titles are checked letter by letter against the Tibetan and restored to the Derge's own spelling. Most texts published before October 2026 were made with Claude Sonnet; since then Claude Opus makes every text, and the earlier ones will be translated again at the end of the project.

Each page also carries a short introduction and the title in Sanskrit, both written by the model from the text itself.

How accurate they are

The process has been quality-checked against the existing scholarly translations: texts that 84000 has published were translated again by the stupa and compared with the scholars' versions passage by passage, and the results are highly accurate. Even so, these translations should not be treated as definitive. Once a scholar's translation of a text is available, use it in place of this one.

What "provisional" means

These are not the work of a human translator and have not been reviewed by one. They are offered so that a reader with no access to a text can know what it says, today, rather than in twenty years. Every page says so. Where 84000 or another human translation exists, that translation is definitive; read it instead. If you find an error, the request form on the home page reaches the project.

Requesting a text

Every text not yet translated has a "Request this translation" button on the home page. Requested texts move to the front of the stupa's queue.
