The Tantra Called the Mirror of All Secrets, the Net of Magical Display of Vajrasattva (Vajrasattvamāyājālaguhyasarvādarśa-nāma-tantra)

The Tantra Called the Mirror of All Secrets, the Net of Magical Display of Vajrasattva, known in Sanskrit as the Vajrasattva Māyājāla Guhya Sarva Ādarśa Tantra and in Tibetan as Dorjé Sempé Gyutrul Drawa Sangwa Tamché kyi Melong, is Toh 833 in the Kangyur, where it belongs to the section of Ancient Tantras of the Nyingma school and to the Māyājāla, or Net of Magical Display, cycle of Mahāyoga tantras. It opens with homage to the buddhas Vairocana, Akṣobhya and Amitābha, after which Vajradhara, the Lord of Secrets, praises the assembled Victors and requests a teaching that gathers all ritual systems of body, speech and mind. Across thirteen chapters the tantra sets out the setting and occasion of the teaching, the view, conduct, maṇḍala, empowerment, sacred commitments, practice, the distinctive features of the path, and the spontaneously accomplished result, described in terms of the enlightened bodies, speech, mind, qualities and activities. It closes by entrusting this secret to Vajradhara with strict warnings about who may and may not receive it, and its colophon attributes the translation to the Indian preceptor Vimalamitra and the Tibetan monk Jñānakumāra.

In the language of India: vajrasatva māyājala guhya sarva ādarśa nāma tantra. In the language of Tibet: The Tantra Called the Mirror of All Secrets, the Magical Net of Vajrasattva.

oṃ Homage to Vairocana! hūṃ Homage to Akṣobhya! aṃ Homage to Amitābha!

Then glorious Vajradhara,
lord of the secrets of all the victors,
holder of the treasure of empowerment by them all,
joyful, with wide-open eyes and with reverence,
praised and circumambulated all the victors,
sat respectfully before them, and made this request:

Emaho! Speech of all the buddhas,
king of all ritual systems,
tantra that teaches the rites in full,
stainless, profound and hard to fathom,
Vinaya, Sūtra and Abhidharma,
accomplishing the sacred commitments and attainment,
tantra of the rites of body, speech and mind:
this that settles everything without exception,
how is it Vajrasattva?
What is it by which the Magical Display is taught?
For what reason is it taught as the tantra of the Net?
For what reason is it called scripture?
For what reason is it explained as general?
How is it called secret?
For what reason is it the meaning of the essence?
How is it called suchness?
For what reason is it called definitive?
To which persons was this name spoken?
These are the questions asked about the name.

In the common and the special modes,
for what reason is one called the Teacher?
What are the place and the retinue like?
What are the time and the compilation like?
How is it that he is Akṣobhya?
How is it that he is called Vairocana?
For what reason Ratnasambhava?
For what reason Amitābha?
What is Amoghasiddhi like?
Locanā and Ākāśadhātvīśvarī,
Pāṇḍaravāsinī and Māmakī:
for what reason is one called Tārā?
For what reason Kṣitigarbha?
Ākāśagarbha, mighty Avalokiteśvara,
and Vajrapāṇi: for what reason are they so named?
What are Lāsyā, Mālā, Gītā and Nṛtyā?
Maitreya, Sarvanivaraṇaviṣkambhin, Samantabhadra:
for what reason is one called Mañjuśrī?
The four times, Dhūpā, Puṣpā,
Ālokā and Gandhā: for what reason are they so named?
What are Vijaya, Yamāntaka,
Hayagrīva and Amṛtakuṇḍalin?
Aṅkuśa, Pāśa, Sphoṭa
and Ghaṇṭā: for what reason are they so named?
For what reason the two, Mind and Excellent Dharma?
Lord, tell me definitively!
For what reason, from the uncommon
inner and secret introductions,
how are the teacher, the teaching, the place and the retinue,
the time and the compilation, spoken of?
From the renowned common introduction,
how are the teacher, the retinue, the place and the time,
and what are the teaching and the compilation like?
Great being, please explain!
This is the verse of questions on the introduction.

Why does the opening of the discourse become twofold,
common and uncommon?
Who opens the discourse to whom,
and what means and result does it show?
The stages of the five minds and the five wisdoms,
common and uncommon,
and with what they are connected: please explain!
This is the verse on the opening of the discourse.

Great being, chief of teachers,
who has gained mastery over the Magical Net,
perfect lord of Dharma,
perfect mass of splendor:
what is to be viewed, and for what reason?
By whom is it realized, and what comes of it?
What is the difference between realizing and not realizing?
How many doors of realization are taught?
What are the reasons, the modes and the result?
This is the question on the view.

For what reason is there common conduct?
Common and uncommon: which, and for what reason?
What are the conduct of wisdom, of the accumulations and of compassion,
of realization, the partial conduct,
the conduct of connection and the conduct of the vidyādharas?
By whom is it practiced, toward whom, and how?
For what reason does its result come about?
And where one is not endowed with it,
tell me the faults of that practice!
This is the question on the particulars of conduct.

Of the common, the general and the particular,
of the intrinsic nature and of spontaneous presence,
for what reason is it called a maṇḍala, and which is it?
How does it become outer, inner and secret,
and two means of taming?
Which are the three stages of the samādhi maṇḍala?
What is called superior samādhi?
In the two, what are the union of body, speech and mind,
and the stages of outer, inner and secret?
What is the maṇḍala of images like?
For what reason is it called an image?
The image of what appears in what?
How does it become outer, inner and secret?
For what reason is it called superior,
and what is that called an image like?
Taught as two, as three and as six,
how are ultimate and relative bodhicitta
joined as outer, inner and secret?
What are the two maṇḍalas of the gathering?
Who gathers what, where,
and practicing how, accomplishes what?
What is the intent behind teaching these?
With which occasions are they connected?
With what are the roots and the branches connected?
How are the center and the surround to be examined?
Speak, great supreme being!
That about which certainty has not been found,
and which has not become the practice of all,
explain it well, great supreme victor!
This is the question asked about the maṇḍala.

By whom, to whom, and in which,
how is the perfect empowerment conferred?
How shall I come to understand it?
With what, where and how is it connected?
What are its faults and qualities like?
Please explain the stages of the result!
How many are the rites of entry here?
What are the stages of the activity like?
Vajrasattva, great bliss,
fully perfected in the Magical Net,
through the nonduality of means and wisdom,
please dispel my doubts!
This is the question asked about all the topics of empowerment.

For what reason is that called a sacred commitment
which is relied upon by whom, toward whom?
How many root sacred commitments are taught?
For what reason are they to be called roots?
Tell how many branches are taught!
How are these connected with the path, with approach and accomplishment,
and with which persons and which aims?
What are the faults and qualities of keeping and transgressing them?
How are they spoken of as outer, inner and secret?
In view, conduct and practice,
in the knowable, samādhi and sacred commitment,
what is accomplished, what not abandoned, what guarded?
Supreme victor, great being, please explain!
Of the common and the uncommon,
of the supreme and the not supreme,
what are the distinctions of accomplishment like?
By whom, where and how is it accomplished?
What are summoning, binding, healing and killing,
dividing, expelling, defeat and victory?
How are treasure, keen eyes, swift feet,
and the level of a vidyādhara accomplished?
Supreme victor, please explain!
This is the question asked about accomplishment.

What, and which, are the stages of enlightened activity?
By whom, and where, are they to be undertaken?
What are the hearth, the substances and the kīla?
What are the samādhi, the posture and the fire pit?
What are the stages of those to be tamed?
How are the stages of the distinctions explained?
How do the result and the qualities come about?
Speak, speak, supreme chief of victors!
Cut through my doubts completely!
This is the question asked about enlightened activity.

By what marvelous person,
and in what manner, is the path entered at first?
Why are there outer, inner and secret paths?
The yoga of emptiness, great compassion,
what are the single mudrā and the elaborate?
The levels of maturation, of mastery, of mudrā,
and what are the characteristics of the spontaneously present?
How many kinds of culmination are taught?
What is the result of the outer, inner and secret brought to culmination,
the very aim of oneself and of others?
How is the meaning of the twenty-five to be understood?

That which is the king of ritual texts,
the unsurpassed Net of Magical Display (Māyājāla),
comprehending all paths and results,
is the tantra that explains the rites in detail.
The secret, the great secret, not realized by those of poor fortune,
the collections of sūtras and the Vinaya, the heaps of Dharma,
all the teachings, reaching to the limits of space,
have emanated from the Secret Essence (Guhyagarbha);
therefore this is the essence of all,
of all the paths of generation and completion, as many as there are, without exception.
To those of the lesser vehicle, to the untrained,
to those whose lineage is cut off, and to those lacking empowerment and pith instruction,
however they look, it does not appear.
Since it is the domain of supreme persons,
to individuals trained for eons as numerous as atoms,
because it is secret, through secret means
it is taught, so that limitless accomplishments may be attained.

All phenomena, outer and inner, the whole vessel and its contents,
together with support and supported,
however they are known and have come to be known,
all of them without exception, though of a single cause,
appear distinctly through the power and potency of conditions.
Whatever abides in stages of distinction,
the nature, those to be tamed, and the sugatas,
appears distinctly through the force of delusion;
in the uncontrived state there is no difference.
All the particulars that are taught
as distinct through self-appearance and karma,
in order to establish them in suchness,
Supreme Victor, you who are wholly filled with great bliss,
please answer the meaning of these questions,
and establish us in the secret meaning
where skillful means and wisdom are joined.

This completes the king of ritual texts, the explanation called the Root Tantra of Questions, from the Tantra of the Net of Magical Display, the Secret Mirror of Vajrasattva.

Then the Lord of Secrets, Vajradhara,
a being fully endowed with the major marks and minor signs,
surrounded by countless victors,
in the self-arisen, pure celestial palace,
skillful means and wisdom in embrace and union,
and all the others likewise in accord with him,
smiling, and beaming his approval,
spoke in a manner of delight:

"What countless victors have spoken,
speech, speech transmitted, and the tantra of the mind,
virtuous in the beginning, the middle and the end,
the rite of skillful means and wisdom inseparable:
O supreme one who has asked of it in detail,
listen! Now, O sattva, I shall explain.

All phenomena without exception are nondual,
self-aware, the very mind of awakening;
this intrinsic nature without distinction
is what is called vajra.
Phenomena whose nature has no limit,
comprehended and directly realized
through the union of skillful means and wisdom:
this is explained as the outer, inner and secret sattva.
The nature, the means, and the meaning of the result,
the stages of potency appearing as display,
and the meaning of the union of means and wisdom:
these four are explained as magical display.
That which, from the undifferentiated, is connected
as differentiated essence, means and identity,
and through the force of blessing,
is what is explained as the meaning of net.

The teaching renowned without being spoken,
free of superimposition and denial, and spoken from the mouth,
is itself stainless scripture.
Being the universal of action and wisdom,
and because it is complete through completeness, it is scripture.
The nature of the general teaching,
being the nature of all things whatsoever,
though it abides neither as one nor as many,
is an ocean of tantras of individual teachings.
It teaches this, teaches by this, teaches for the sake of this;
since all meanings are fully complete in it,
it is called the general scripture.

Why is it called secret?
It is the nature of body, speech and mind;
primordially it is the essence of the nature,
and it has become the means and the result:
this is stated as the meaning of secret.
Those inferior by disposition, the untrained,
those lacking empowerment and sacred commitment,
and, obscured by their own imputations, the distinctions of the grounds:
owing to each of these,
because they are without its taste, it is explained as secret.

The phenomena of nature, means and result
are the meaning of the great sphere;
the transcendent and the mundane
are all, without exception, of one taste and inseparable.
All the distinctions of vehicles
and the particular teachings that exist
are gathered here, are indicated by this means,
and this alone is their root.

The mind of awakening, means and wisdom nondual,
great bliss, self-arisen wisdom without beginning or end,
is the nature of Vajrasattva.
The brilliant blessings of the victors,
the inconceivable greatnesses,
are taught from this; therefore it is the essence,
as with a core and its light.
Because the quintessence of cause and result is gathered in it,
it is called the essence.

Because all things are the mind of awakening,
in no way cut off at edge or center,
pervaded by the three things,
it is the cause of perfect completeness.
Knowing through the vajra, as suchness,
skillful means and wisdom inseparable,
the capacity for one's own and others' aims complete:
this is the reality of the result.
Because the appearances falsely imagined
as two distinct things
are, like light in darkness and transmutation into gold,
of one taste, this is the reality of means;
therefore it is called reality.

Various things, inseparable,
are neither different in meaning nor one in meaning.
The certainty of that very secret
is taught as threefold: ground, path and essence,
each made certain by certainty.
Where four vehicles are taught,
they are not definitive, being for guiding beings;
to abide definitively in the vehicle of one taste
is the unsurpassed definitive vehicle.
From the uncontrived, self-arisen nature,
naturally free of agent and action,
the diverse illusory maṇḍala is magically displayed:
this is the self-appearing Net of Magical Display."

From the nature that has no edge or center,
through causes, conditions and karma, there is delusion into separateness.
That itself is the very mind of awakening,
abiding in evenness, without edge or center.
Thus the tantra that appears as separate
is like space and the sectors marked off within it.
By meditating directly on the two paths joined
of the great tantra, the Net of Magical Display,
the sphere of great bliss, skillful means and wisdom inseparable,
manifestly appears.
This very path of entry and of liberation
is called the path of the Net of Magical Display.
What is wholly pervaded by the nature,
joined by means with the power of things,
having grown in strength stage by stage,
is the magical display of special means.
The beginningless vajra mind,
the very essence of all phenomena,
the profound teaching, the general scripture,
skillful means and wisdom inseparable,
is the tantra of the ritual texts, detailed in its rites.

For those who from countless eons before
have trained in wisdom and possess compassion,
purified through faith, diligence, sacred commitment
and ritual, it is the domain of those of the highest faculties.
This vehicle of the vajra secret
is accomplished as essential nature, as blessing,
as symbol and as vajra:
the vajra vehicle, great bliss.
Because it is secret from those of bad nature and the inferior,
the perverse and those without interest,
and those without empowerment and sacred commitment,
it is called the secret vehicle.
If a master who lacks skillful means
teaches the Secret Essence
to a student who is not a vessel,
self and others all will suffer,
and remain long in burning and obscuration.

From the great Tantra of the Vajra Mirror of the Net of Magical Display, this is the first explanation, the explanation of the meaning of the title.

Then, further, what is to be explained
is the uncommon secret meaning.
To the uncommon retinue,
Vajradhara, victor of the ten directions,
having made the maṇḍala manifest,
through the union of vajra and lotus
confers the empowerment of the mind of awakening.
Then, in a single instant, he clears away
the common and uncommon
afflictions, views and objects of knowledge,
and since he definitively teaches the two paths,
the common and the special secret,
he is called the Teacher.

His supreme place is now to be explained,
as the place of arising, of dwelling, of support,
and of symbol, because of blessing.
Through the special features of means and path,
the lord of all, the great Vajradhara himself,
in that great supreme place, Akaniṣṭha,
which transcends the Pure Abodes,
there the perfect buddha attained awakening;
in order to tame beings without fortune,
the emanation attained awakening here.
The place where all the buddhas dwell,
which transcends the Pure Abodes,
the culmination of the worlds produced by karma,
is the blazing celestial palace of Akaniṣṭha.
Its expanse unbroken in the ten directions,
it is inlaid with special qualities.
There, in the bhaga of the secret consort,
the great Vajradhara, lord of the thirteen grounds,
together with countless others,
dwells, packed like sesame seeds in a pod.
The suchness of all phenomena,
without beginning, end or middle,
is the Akaniṣṭha of nature, of the true meaning,
since it is the palace of all the victors.
The place on which all the victors
of past, present and future rely,
and that which closely symbolizes them,
the palaces arising from mind and from signs,
meditated, drawn, constructed and so forth,
celestial palaces resembling that one,
are, through the victors' mantras and blessings,
places of symbol, for the sake of their dwelling there.

The great perfect enjoyment,
arisen from the various accumulations
of special merit and wisdom,
both common and uncommon,
is in the supreme place of great Akaniṣṭha,
which transcends the Pure Abodes.
The lords of the families, spontaneously present bodies of the mudrā,
free of being one or many,
are the universal form of all the buddhas.
Because from the very beginning they are the treasury of the supreme vehicle,
for those to be tamed who have abandoned the extremes of obscuration
they bring about abandonment in a single instant.
However many various phenomena there are,
they are not different things; therefore the palace is round.
Because it is pervasive, inlaid and immeasurable,
and because of the four wisdoms, it is shown as a wheel.
Because the dharmadhātu has no meeting or parting,
it is encircled all around by a round enclosure.
Because there is nothing not pervaded
by body, speech, mind, blessing and compassion,
it is shown in a form of expanding qualities.
Because self-arisen wisdom is free of limits,
and for the sake of the mind that is free of measure,
not abiding in coming and going in the ten directions,
its foundation is shown as an immeasurable wheel.
Because from the beginning it has the essence of blessing,
it is a treasury, unaccumulated, spontaneously complete;
because it fulfills the hopes and wishes of beings,
it is a celestial palace of blazing jewels.
All phenomena, of various things and of no things,
abide by themselves in their essence;
because they are free of self and other, of apprehended and apprehender,
it has no outer and inner and no obstruction.
The result of means that relies on the common
is exalted through the means of wisdom;
because it appears manifestly to the minds of those to be tamed,
its four projecting bays are surpassingly beautiful.
All the stages of those to be tamed, high and low,
common and uncommon,
enter emptiness, wishlessness, signlessness and luminosity;
therefore wisdom dawns as four doors.
Since its many means are inconceivable,
by traversing the eight paths in sequence
all the qualities of the result are completed;
therefore sixteen porticoes of cause and result are shown.
Because it is free of the paths of clinging and of rejection,
the five wisdoms play as the sense pleasures;
since it arises from the self-offering that delights all,
it is shown thronged with goddesses of sense pleasures.
Through possessing skillful means and wisdom,
possessing the means of non-attachment,
and the spontaneous completeness of the qualities of greatness,
there are seats of sun, moon, lotus and jewel.
Through karma, compassion and aspiration,
bodhisattvas of great power
dwell there by the force of their aspirations,
in order to gather an ocean of learning.

At that time, though it is beyond the limits of time,
it is the time when the four times are equal in essence;
because it is directly realized at the end of time,
it is taught as the uncommon time.
At that time, as the victors of the ten directions
had prophesied for ten million eons,
when the time for taming had come, the Lord of Secrets,
Vajrapāṇi, made the compilation.
Because the victors of the three times had blessed him,
the one of supreme fortune within the retinue,
the secret Vajradhara, having compiled it,
taught it to the retinue to be tamed.

The name of all the ocean of victors,
past, future and present,
by whatever means have gone, go and will go
into suchness, which is the certainty of the three bodies,
of body, speech and mind,
endowed with the three liberations, with wisdom
and with the six perfections,
whose buddhafields and qualities are inconceivable,
in whom all eighty particulars are gathered:
because he has comprehended the vajra,
and is not shaken by actions and conduct, by austerities,
or by the mountain of existence,
he is called the supreme Akṣobhya.
Because whatever body, speech and mind appear
through the union of skillful means and wisdom
appear distinctly
through the particulars of inclination and of means,
he is the supreme Vairocana, the Illuminator.

The qualities of all the victors
are the essence in which the accumulations are gathered;
because qualities arise like jewels,
he is called Ratnasambhava, Source of Jewels.
The doors of immeasurable speech,
through the particulars of faculties and means,
the doors of meaning, sign, speech and letter,
appear limitlessly; therefore he is the supreme Amitābha, the Limitless.
Through immeasurable compassionate activity,
through compassion without coming or going,
he accomplishes the aim of protecting beings;
therefore he is shown as Amoghasiddhi, Accomplisher of the Aim.
These kings of awareness, too,
are the five ordinary ones purified in their very ground,
and having been made manifest from them by that means,
they master them and appear as themselves;
therefore they are called kings.
The stages of union, of absorption and control, and of the winds,
made manifest from above and below,
whatever is made manifest from that means,
the form of bliss, its feeling,
perception, formations
and consciousness, with the taste of undefiled bliss,
having mastery, are explained as the kings of wisdom.
Because the colors of what appears, what is meditated and what is drawn,
of the mudrā, and of the channels, essences and drops,
are white and red and yellow,
blue and green, in sequence.

The bliss and happiness of all the victors,
the mothers from whom conduct arises,
the five from whom all things are produced,
the purity of ordinary clinging,
the nature of the five queens of mastery,
are what is distinguished as solidity, moisture, warmth and motion,
and the element of space,
made manifest from the means of above and below.
Object and cognition being inseparable,
through the union of compassion and wisdom
she sees saṃsāra and nirvāṇa as nondual:
the supreme Locanā, the Eye.
Because the great expanse of undefiled emptiness
produces, gathers and sustains,
suchness, free of edge and center,
is what is called Ākāśadhātvīśvarī, the Expanse of Space.
Because by realization she cleanses and burns away
the boundless stains, common and uncommon,
of karma, views, afflictions
and objects of knowledge,
she is called Pāṇḍaravāsinī, the White-Clad.
Because with the water of karma and samādhi,
of wisdom and compassion, without interval,
she washes and satisfies them all,
water is explained as Māmakī.
Because through realization and means,
and through the winds of the means of samādhi,
she ferries beings across to the realm that needs no journey,
she is explained as Tārā, the Savior.

The essence, self-arisen wisdom itself,
since it is the support and ground of all and all-pervading,
in the nature of the mind of awakening,
is called Kṣitigarbha, Essence of the Earth.
The self-arisen essence, the mind of awakening,
like space, beyond thought and expression,
which transcends edge and center, superimposition and denial,
is itself Ākāśagarbha, Essence of Space.
Because with immeasurable compassion
he watches beings without weariness
until they have crossed the river,
he is Avalokiteśvara, chief of the lotus.
With the vajra of realization and inseparability
he destroys the signs of grasping at extremes and at self;
therefore he is Vajrapāṇi.

The four secret consciousnesses,
both common and special,
the common and special objects,
from the union of skillful means and wisdom,
through symbol, realization and meditation,
appear manifestly as the four goddesses.
Because she is supremely graceful
with the mind of awakening, with bliss,
and with the bliss of means,
this goddess is explained as Lāsyā, the Graceful.
All the particulars of outer and inner sound
of saṃsāra, liberated and unliberated,
she utters like echoes;
because it is without interruption, she is Mālā, the Garland.
What is taught as the essence of satisfaction
is the very specialness of what is fragrant;
because the melody itself satisfies and gladdens the mind,
she is Vajra Gītā, the Song.
Pervaded by taste, like the movements of a dancer,
the dance of the four great blisses
pervades, is beautiful and satisfies,
and unfixed, it releases; therefore she is Nṛtyā, the Dancer.
Ornament, the sounds of passion,
the garland of the mind, and the dance of bliss
are the nature of the four goddesses.

For the boundless beings who have not realized,
through acting with natural compassion
he benefits and protects them all;
this is called Maitreya, the Loving One.
Through the special culmination of realization and familiarization,
he clears away the stages of karma, afflictions and views;
this is Sarvanivaraṇaviṣkambhin, Remover of Obscurations.
When the abiding of skillful means and wisdom is realized,
because there is no dwelling in rejecting or accepting,
all things outer and inner, vessel and contents without exception,
being without division, are Samantabhadra, the All-Good.
Because with undefiled wisdom
he cuts through the net of views,
all grasping at self and other,
he is undefiled wisdom, the body of Mañjuśrī.
The faculties, together with letters and drops,
are spoken of because they appear manifestly
through their place, their realization and their meditation.

The four times are the stages, twice eight;
they are spoken of for the sake of releasing that very thing,
of producing relish, and of purifying the domains.
Because she satisfies speech with fragrance and with bliss,
and the mind is without reversal, she is Vajra Dhūpā, the Incense.
Because she arranges flowers blissfully as drops
in the maṇḍala that pervades outer and inner,
she is the unsurpassed Vajra Puṣpā, the Flower.
To clear away the darkness that dwells
in the mind, outer and inner,
the unsurpassed light of wisdom and primordial wisdom
is Vajra Ālokā, the Lamp of clarity.
Because she abides pervading all,
with the bliss of self-arisen samādhi,
in all maṇḍalas outer and inner,
she is the pervading Vajra Gandhā, the Scented Water.

Because, with his intent on suchness,
he has vanquished all the phenomena of saṃsāra without exception
at the seat of awakening,
he is called Vijaya, the Victorious.
Wisdom free of apprehended and apprehender
is the antidote to the conception that grasps at two;
because it destroys that through realization and familiarization,
he is yamāntakṛta, Yamāntaka.
Because from the ocean of saṃsāra,
through miraculous power joined with means,
he destroys and liberates,
he is explained as the king Hayagrīva.
On the five poisons, the māras of saṃsāra,
by clearly familiarizing them as the five wisdoms,

Seizing, embracing and binding,

Great bliss, unborn,

hells, pretas, animals,
humans, gods and asuras,

The gods and the humans,
the asuras, pretas and animals,

When they meet according to their own inclinations,

The vehicles taught are various;

The single vehicle, in the manner of sameness,

Through the activities of body, speech and mind,

Each with their own portion, as an emanated body,
the first of the retinue abiding on the grounds

gathered into mind, words and letters,

The tantras of body, speech and mind,

All the phenomena that are thus renowned

Then, further, what is to be explained:
the person and the means, in common,
whatever has become an object of the intellect,
that is the common object.
The person, the means and the Teacher himself,
what has not become their domain,
and is endowed with the great means and with secrecy,

That very one, for the sake of the two retinues of beings,
makes himself appear in two ways.
The aggregates, elements and sense sources themselves
are spontaneously present as the victors, father and mother,
as the male bodhisattvas and as the female bodhisattvas.
Because they destroy without exception the secret objects, faculties, consciousness and contact,
they are the wrathful ones;
because they are born from signs, the marks and the rest,
they are the consorts, explained as not abiding through karma.
Mind and phenomena are Excellent Dharma and his consort, the Excellent One.
The six afflictions, attachment and the rest,

That itself, in the ten directions and four times,
is the aggregates, elements, consciousness and objects,
the faculties, the times, and the body with its four phenomena;
the signs and afflictions are the consorts themselves.
Since that itself, being mind, is unborn,
from the union of the superior two truths
the five minds of awakening are magically displayed.
All phenomena, things, appearances and sounds,
appear as unborn and unceasing,
as nonabiding and without reference,
as without coming or going.
Though for oneself wisdom appears clearly,
because others have not realized it,
the nature and essence are secret from the beginning:
the meaning of the secret, utterly hidden and concealed.
When that itself is well explained,
in the secret that is primordial sameness,
through the power of the conditions of ignorance and wrong thought,
the suffering of saṃsāra appears, as if left behind there.
That itself is the essence of the sugatas,
the essence of things that has no thing.
In order to ripen beings,
from the nature of body, speech and mind
the emanations are magically displayed.
With various bodies, speech and minds
they turn the wheel of Dharma.
In those maṇḍalas of the victors, too,
the Teacher and retinue, one to another,
father and mother and the nondual,

Through the projecting union of the winds,

the light of passion, the nondual union,

having made the wisdom of space manifest,
the clear taste of the wisdom of bliss
comes to be known from arising, joy, abiding,
and coming and going themselves.
Whoever requests whatever of whomever,
by the power of whatever condition, from those

appearance and existence together with bodies and things,
the wisdom of space appears as anything at all:
this is the inner request, the great means.
Because the means urges wisdom
with songs of passion and with union,
and wisdom in reply also urges the means
with the songs and dances of śa da pa,
and through the stages of mantra and union
they are directly joined through the four mudrās,
through the union of the moving winds
of skillful means and wisdom nondual,
the very means of summoning and having summoned,
bringing down, holding and drawing up,
reversing, holding and joining,
there come generation, completion and the great completion.
All that arises from the ultimate stages,
common and uncommon,
appears as the five minds:
the drop that arises from means,
the secret drop of wisdom,
and what arises from nondual bliss:
the five minds come to appear in sequence.

Means and wisdom in equal union,
arising from body, speech and mind,
the eighty particulars all gathered,
the forty-two maṇḍalas,
through body, speech, mind and the five wisdoms,
through the union of means and nature,
set their seal upon all beings.
Through the nature of bliss of the vessel and its contents,
all, without exception, abide as coemergent.
Movement, rising, aspiration, entry, abiding and culmination,
the ground of birth and the means,
and, after birth, the culmination, the five wisdoms,
the very nature of all the buddhas,
abide just as they are.
Through the particular inclinations of beings,
forms, colors, mantras and lights,
maṇḍalas and the rest abide separately,
taught in accord with the minds of individuals.
Arising from support and supported,
self-arisen great bliss, nonconceptual,
abiding bodiless in the body,
pervading, nondual, in the manner of fullness.
Because the special bliss of samādhi,
the undefiled bliss,
through the deities of nature, imagination, appearance
and the power of blessing,
outer and inner, vessel and contents, body, speech and mind,
all arise without exception from the three unions.
The channels, the winds and the drops
are relied on in sequence, and union,

The stages of the drops of bliss

The paths, mundane and transcendent,

not a thing, not a nonthing,

Then, further, what is to be explained
is the explanation of the object, the knowable for all:
all phenomena, without exception, every one,
free of both one and many,
are suchness without edge or center.
From equality free of all superimposition and denial,
through the difference between realizing and not realizing,
various phenomena are magically displayed.
Beyond ground and root,
the indivisible nature is suchness.
Beyond the domain of thing and nonthing,
the various essences of saṃsāra and nirvāṇa
are self-appearing:
the nondual great means, vajra itself.
How wonderful! From nondual great bliss
various abodes and enjoyments,
families, colors and lifespans,
and the distinctions of pleasure, pain and faculties,
various natures, are magically displayed.
Like an illusion, the moon in water, a rainbow,
they abide as the essence of the mind of awakening (bodhicitta).
Whatever appears through karma and conditions,
through the differences of each one's own clinging,
through various distinct faculties,
is seen as the various powers of conceptuality.
The expanse whose nature has neither ground nor root,
through coemergent and imputed
non-seeing and wrong clinging grown strong,
appears as the force of the perverted wheel of delusion.
Without dispelling saṃsāra, the pure
fields, spheres of activity and enjoyments,
samādhis, powers, strengths and miraculous displays,
immeasurable higher perceptions and wisdoms,
various bodies, speech and minds,
qualities and activities are self-appearing:
the spontaneously present benefit of self and other.
Like a wish-fulfilling jewel with its variety,
the unaccumulated, spontaneously present body,
indivisible from all variety,
appears to all in accord with their wishes.
All of it is the self-arisen mind of awakening,
appearing as the meaning of the indivisible truths.
For like the nature of space,
though it appears in that way to each,
it cannot truly be grasped as one.
Uncontrived, self-arisen, peace itself,
it is the completely pure essence;
in saṃsāra itself is nirvāṇa,
known as the completely pure expanse.
From the self-appearing essence of all,
free of all superimposition and denial,
all variety without exception is magically displayed.
Neither thing nor nonthing,
that in which suchness is beyond objects,
immeasurable like space,
without partiality, edge or center, its extent immeasurable:
just as one thing pervades another,
the essence of the elements, space and the rest, free of particles,
is the undefiled essence itself.
Whatever appears in the faculties of all embodied beings,
connected with the refined essences,
is altered by the power of wrong seeing;
it is self-appearance without self or other.
All without exception, indivisible,
the stream of the five essences of means and wisdom,
enjoyment enjoined as the means,
abides pervading all beings.
Whatever there is of means and wisdom
is the very nature of the Net of Magical Display.
The mind of awakening, indivisible in nature,
is the very essence of great bliss.
What is to be realized by all without exception,
the countless victors and vidyādharas
of the past, the present and the future,
abides as the essence that pervades without direction.
Whatever appears, without exception, is suchness,
uncontrived, unmade, peaceful;
beyond one and many,
it is the very nature of all buddhas.
Like the nature of space, free of limits,
all of it abides so; yet
because, through the faculties' differing realization,
through pure and impure ways of seeing,
that nature has not become an object of experience,
through outer, inner, secret and other
empowerments and connection with the oral instructions,
it is taught for the sake of seeing and realizing suchness.
For this reason the vajra master,
possessing complete qualities and ability,
to one who has faith, diligence and the observances,
and is endowed with special wisdom,
gives empowerment, sacred commitment and instruction;
and through the power of the connection of cause and conditions,
bliss, clarity and nonconceptuality
are brought to perfection through means.
The essence, the great secret of secrets,
is the accomplishment of body, speech and mind:
outer and inner, self and other without exception,
all are indivisible suchness.
For those who lack means and set their seal on everything,
it has turned into a mass of darkness.
Just as darkness and light, poison, and the elixir that turns metal to gold
surely change their nature through skill in means,
so, through the view, conduct and samādhi of this,
it becomes so at once.
Just as, in the indivisible nature of space,
color, shape, substance and the rest
are all without distinction,
so through skill in means all becomes thus.
Ordinary people and the perverse,
ascetics and solitary realizers (pratyekabuddhas),
those of outer and inner, those who hold to distinctions,
those concerned with clean and unclean, and those of partial realization:
whoever strays from the view of equality,
through wrongness of essence and means,
of faculties and the rest,
that very thing becomes a harmful force.
The illness by which one is seized,
through excellent means and diligence,
through the distinction of such striving,
is itself, without being abandoned, completely purified.
Because, in suchness as it truly is,
through the difference of realizing or not realizing,
whatever appears as distinct phenomena
does not in reality abide that way.
The vajra is unborn wisdom;
the mirror is means itself, in which variety appears.
The equality of their nondual union
is taught as the sublime indivisibility of all.
Next, the doors of realization are to be explained.
Since there is nothing to be gained by anything for anyone,
view, conduct, path and fruit
are this alone; there is no other aim to pursue.
There are those who, for that aim,
through words, mantra substances and austerities,
through the blessing of whatever has been taught,
wish to benefit themselves and others.
There are those who hold that when that from which body and mind arose has perished,
they lie latent in the elements,
and since thereafter there is no factor of benefit or harm,
austerity and effort are not needed.
There are those who, because of the four causes of action,
in order to bring about happiness by pleasing and purifying,
through rites and fierce austerities,
appear to wish to be released and liberated.
Through the four truths, their sixteen aspects and the distinct paths,
by way of abandoning through seeing and through meditation in the three realms,
free of all defilements for their own sake,
one gains liberation for oneself, nirvāṇa.
The solitary realizers, through the awakening of their habitual tendencies,
having examined for themselves the twelve links as non-arising,
exhaust ignorance, the wheel of delusion, its cause and effect,
and reach the stage of supreme bliss, as they wish.
The mind of awareness without distinction,
however one regards the difference of true and false,
is the essence, clear and nonconceptual,
appearing as the force of the potency of self and other.
Phenomena are like illusions, mirages,
echoes and the moon in water;
their suchness, free of extremes and center,
the union of the two truths, is the way of the Middle Way.
Setting out the three sublime vajras,
freed from the illusion of things,
and relying on pure interdependence,
the illusion of wisdom dawns directly.
Since, through activity and ritual action,
relying on ablution and cleanliness,
through the power and strength of mantra and mudrā,
one reaches the stage of holding the vajra of the three doors.
Distinguished by being in accord with both of these,
having realized the four suchnesses,
through becoming familiar with action and samādhi together,
one abides at the stage of Vajradhara.
Making samādhi and awareness primary,
through mantra, mudrā and contemplation,
one actually transforms into the deity's guise just as it is,
and through familiarity the aims of self and others are accomplished.

Through the distinction of means and liberation,
the faculties, objects and consciousnesses,
all the spheres of activity of the world of appearance and existence,
outer and inner, vessel and contents, however they change,
being sealed by the secret means
of indivisible means and wisdom,
all without exception becomes vajra itself.
That great means, the hero of appearance and existence,
of a nature indivisible and pure:
since its various displays are self-appearing,
all without exception is nondual wisdom.
For whoever has grasped the means and awareness
of nondual means and wisdom,
the phenomena of saṃsāra that others reject
become the very essence of the nectar of wisdom.
In the essence that accords with things just as they are,
free of all one and many,
they do not abide as separate entities.
Therefore, just as the generation, deity and celestial palace,
the completion, the wisdom like space,
and things as coemergent
are appearances transformed by conditions,
all without exception becomes its essence.
Through differences in how these are viewed,
whatever appears as each one's own inclination, intention
and degree of capacity,
just as from the nature of clear space
sun and moon, planets and stars, clouds, mist and vapor arise,

all phenomena, however they appear,
have not moved from their meaning by the force of reality;
since they appear just so,
they gather into the expanse of space itself.

all beings without exception,

the individual common reasonings,
the uncommon ones and the rest,

Cause, condition and result:

The single cause, the way of the syllables,
blessing and direct perception:

Whatever appears from the unborn state
is one, like water and its waves;

The root of saṃsāra and nirvāṇa

Whatever appears there as particular and general,

Vessel, contents and mind streams, infinite,
through the two equalities and the two superior equalities,

abide uncontrived as suchness.
All without exception, the essence and the bindus,

The first, abiding; the second, the two;

Through the bindu, its location and the syllables
mingling with their blessing,
great bliss appears manifestly.
Just as from water come distinct rivers,

Whatever has arisen from the five refined essences,

Then, further, what is to be explained:
all the distinctions of conduct
of the manifest perfection of the Net of Magical Display,
these are to be explained.
Through nature, means and manifestation,
all conduct is taught.
How wonderful! From the essence of self-awareness
come pleasure and pain, abodes and enjoyments,
lifespans, colors and so on.
In some there is manifest attachment;
in some, fierce conduct of aversion;
in some, being as if crushed by a mountain;
in some, the darkness of blindness;
in some, jealousy appears;
in some, avarice and strife;
in some, immorality and the abandoning of activity;
effort and effortlessness and so on:
such things are found without limit.
In the infinite, boundless dharmadhātu,
in the infinite expanse of space,
that is the distinction of conduct.

The self-arisen conduct of means itself,
just as it is thought of and examined,
because its nature is without division,
is the inconceivable acting and conduct of the three doors.
Although by the nature of means and wisdom
all without exception have the abode of the buddhas,
because they do not realize it and are without means,
their various pleasures and pains are inconceivable.
For one who possesses the great means,
desire, aversion, delusion,
pride and great jealousy
are taught to be bliss on the path of awakening.
People who lack means,
through various methods and activities,
through various hard deeds and practices,
gain no liberation beyond men and gods.
For example, as with something that contains poison,
according to the distinction of means
and according to the distinction of realization,
there are various streams of pleasure and pain.
From the pure, nondual nature,
through the particular distinctions of imputation,
discordant and various pleasures and pains are experienced,
yet there is no movement from the uncontrived expanse.
Those who, from the common and the real,
by way of action and activity,
have acted, act and will act:
therefore, by this conduct, it is called conduct.
Although by the nature of the conduct of the Victors
it pervades like water and its waves,
in the various activities of rejecting and accepting
there is no conduct held by means.
Because, through means and wisdom,
and through various sounds and realizations,
it has not become the object of the common,
the conduct of means is uncommon.
In some cases it is the common itself:
the liberated and the unliberated,
wandering ascetics and non-Buddhists (tīrthikas),
women and the lowly,
kings, heroes and ascetics,
children and the rest, each distinct.
In some it is shown as laughter and as ferocity,
scolding, terror and peace;
in some, likewise, great attachment,
deceitful conduct and wantonness;
peace, theft and jealous conduct,
desire, the torment of enjoyments,
song, dance and performance, playfulness and charm,
and likewise terror and threat,
savagery and harm,
pure fields and miraculous conduct,
many marvels and miracles:
whatever deeds are done by whomever,
in order to tame all beings without exception,
infinite deeds are displayed.
For the peaceful, Brahmā; for the wrathful, the wrathful;
for the liberated, the young and beautiful at play;

The reflection maṇḍala of nondual skillful means and wisdom

Self-arisen wisdom without arising or ceasing,
the indivisible vajra form of all,
whose aspects have no end:
since its spontaneously present qualities appear of themselves,
its reflections dawn naturally.
The self-arisen maṇḍala free of extremes,
because it appears as anything at all yet is not apprehended,
and does not abide as truly stable,
dawns entirely as the reflection maṇḍala.
Great bliss, self-arisen and spontaneously present,
like a jewel perfect in qualities,
because it appears as immeasurable phenomena,
is the reflection of great bliss.
For the higher and lower grades of those to be trained,
beings pure and impure,
in order to arouse their enthusiasm for the conduct of awareness,
from the beginning to the very end,
according to each one's own disposition,
it appears in just that way to them.
The reflection is the outer stage,
because it has become a common object.

The body, undivided and without remainder,
complete with outer and inner, vessel and essence,
the abode rich in essence that brings about bliss,
the indestructible bindu, the substance of all,
the form of self-arisen great bliss,
through the potency of the link between the refined and the dregs,
appears as the phenomena of two kinds of things,
the defiled and the undefiled.
In the intrinsic nature of the causal bindu,
through the distinct particular potencies,
the elements, the consorts,
great bliss, and the rest
appear from the power of the bindu's potency.
The very heart of all the cakras,
the blazing bindu, the form of bliss,
together with the particular channel letters,
the bindus of gathered essence, filled with flavor,
are the maṇḍala of all that is supported,
appearing everywhere as the whole of vessel and essence.
The secret stage of great skillful means:
the bindu itself, dependent on the four elements,
moves and drips, abides and dissolves;
proceeding through these three distinctions,
like the domain of embarking on a boat and drinking the illusory water of a mirage,
or like reaching the summit of a mountain clear as a mirror
and gazing upon everything,
it appears as the very reflection of great bliss.
The bindu of the four stages:
whatever formation of bliss is stirred,
the distinctions of the bindu in the four places are clear,
like pearls strung on a garland.
The dripping and blazing bindu,
like sesame seeds ripened in the fruit,
like a whole heap of sesame,
abides as the body of bindus.
When experience blazes clearly,
trembling, swimming particles, the hair standing on end,
leaping, uttering sounds, the dance of heroes,
appear as a host of displays.
When the world of appearance and existence, great bliss itself,
with the ordinary set, has become stable,
the bindu is refined by the power of space.
When, by the power of one-pointed seeing of space,
the bindu dissolves into emptiness,
it is ascertained as the form of wisdom space.
All of appearance and existence, vessel and essence without exception,
as the reflection of great bliss awareness,
like gazing out from the depths of the ocean,
appears in just that way, without exception.
Because it is experienced by awareness
and its domain is pure, it is explained as inner.
Self-arisen great bliss itself,
in order to train secret individuals
and to reveal secret experience directly,
is endowed with all the ritual activities
of the union of skillful means and wisdom,
and, with empowerment, blessing, and purification,
by completing the three hundred and sixty

like a sun crystal giving forth light,

the jewel drips water without control,
and all is gathered into the bindu of awareness.
When the moving wind ceases,
awareness dissolves into the bindu of emptiness.
The stages of the bindu that rest on a ta,
from the nature of moving and not moving,
appear as saṃsāra and nirvāṇa.
By relying on the secret bindu,
one becomes the universal monarch of great bliss over all.
First is the bindu of the body,
then the bindu of awareness,
then the bindu of wisdom itself,
and finally it dissolves into the bindu of the nature of reality.
Then, without meeting or parting in the three times,
the very space element of great bliss,
profound and without movement,
free of all clinging and attachment of one's own continuum,
the reflection of great bliss dawns.
When it is not realized, it is the pervading reflection;
through realization, it is the reflection of experience;

those without empowerment and pith instructions,
ascetics, the unripened,
and the fortuneless who have fallen away;

Next, what is further to be explained
is the superior reflection maṇḍala.
To arouse delight in ordinary beings,
I shall explain the rite in detail.
Although the great maṇḍala, the light of realization,
has not become manifest just as it is,
since through this one comes to realize it in a superior way,
it is taught as "superior."
Because what symbolizes what for whom
comes especially close to symbolizing it,
and because whatever is accomplished by relying on whatever
is accomplished especially swiftly,
this is called superior.
Because by this means, whatever arises from whatever
is manifestly superior, it is superior.
The body, speech, and mind of all the Victors
are manifestly shown by that means,
and because it is not the actual thing itself,
it is taught as a reflection.
Just as, through the means on which one relies,
that very thing appears in likeness,
so for those to be trained who have not seen it but have faith,
it is a means resembling it;
because it is not the actual thing but resembles it, it is a reflection.
It is the intrinsic nature of all the Victors.
The form of the five self-arisen wisdoms,
spontaneously complete with immeasurable wisdom,
appears to undefiled trainees.
Through the reflection of its qualities,
for defiled persons who have faith,
the very aspects of body, speech, and mind
are symbolized in likeness; therefore it is a reflection.

Because, although in the essence of the dharmadhātu
no division into distinct things abides,
under the sway of devotion and of ferocity,
one is not tamed by just anything at all;
and because compassion pervades all,
the maṇḍalas of enjoyment and emanation are taught.
To subdue by skillful means and by ferocity
those who cling and those who are proud,
they are suited to each in this way;

Next, what is further to be explained
is the maṇḍala of the awakening mind:
all without exception is the awakening mind itself,
and I shall explain it so that this may be realized.
All phenomena without exception
and all maṇḍalas without exception
do not abide as distinct divisions;
they are the very maṇḍala of the awakening mind.
Self-arisen, without high or low,
body, speech, and mind, and body, voice, and mentation,
are the ground, root, and heart of all without exception,
gathered in the self-arisen awakening mind.
That itself is self-aware luminosity,
not a thing and not a non-thing,
not both and not other than both,
free of all extremes, center, and parts,
profound, vast, and expansive;
it is the domain of the Tathāgatas,
and since it is not the domain of logicians,
the awakening mind is the secret of secrets.
How wonderful, the marvelous, astonishing Dharma,
the secret of all the perfect buddhas:
from the unborn all is born,
yet in being born there is no birth.
How wonderful, the marvelous, astonishing Dharma,
the secret of all the perfect buddhas:
from the unceasing all ceases,
yet in ceasing there is no cessation.
How wonderful, the marvelous, astonishing Dharma,
the secret of all the perfect buddhas:
from the nonabiding all abides,
yet in abiding there is no abiding.
How wonderful, the marvelous, astonishing Dharma,
the secret of all the perfect buddhas:
from the nonreferential all is referenced,
yet in referencing there is no reference.
How wonderful, the marvelous, astonishing Dharma,
the secret of all the perfect buddhas:
from no coming and going there is coming and going,
yet in coming and going there is no coming and going.
The unborn awakening mind of awareness,
nonexistent, yet with the nature of things,
is the very essence of Vajrasattva.
In all outer and inner, vessel and essence without exception,
together with the moving and the unmoving,
it is the very maṇḍala of the self-arisen mind.
Its natural abiding, its realization,
contemplating, referencing, and growing familiar,
the signs of body, speech, and mind,
gods, asuras, and secret ones,
piśācas, bhūtas, kinnaras,
kumbhāṇḍas, pretas, and nāgas,
all moving creatures without exception,
plants, trees, branches, and fruits,
and every excellence without exception,
are the unfabricated deity of the ultimate meaning itself,
without distinction in the awakening mind.
That they are taught elsewhere as "deities"
is merely a verbal convention.
In the awakening mind, nondual,
they are taught as one in the meaning that is realized.
Whatever is the nature of great compassion,
because it does not meet with any meaning that abides,
has become the abode of compassion for all without exception;
compassion is the awakening mind.
Applied thoroughly to the meaning that is without contradiction,
it is called the relative awakening mind.
All phenomena with forms, however many,

like a miracle in the sky,
though various things appear, there is no thing.

seeing her brings bliss to body and mind,
meeting her makes suffering decline,
and intimacy with her brings stable bliss.
If she is practiced with skillful means,
the conceptual clinging that is not overcome
even by training for an eon is reversed,
and one ripens into great bliss right now.
The yogin who wishes to stabilize what has been attained
sees, hears, and dwells together with her;
through embrace, meeting, and practice,
coemergent wisdom is born.
Because it is not the domain of those without empowerment,
the ill-fated, and the lowly, who do not realize it,
it is explained as "secret."
Next, what is further to be explained:
having consummated the samādhi of the stages,
one should meditate on the maṇḍala of thirty,
endowed with three groupings,
endowed with body, speech, and mind.
Where it has come under the sway of the extensive,
endowed with the five families and the five wisdoms,
from body, speech, mind, and qualities,
one should meditate on the sequence of seventy.
One of little devotion who wishes to practice in secret
uses the maṇḍala of a single grouping;
of ten or else of eighteen,
one should meditate on the final grouping.
Here one should meditate on the feast gathering,
in which all maṇḍalas are gathered.
Where there is the maṇḍala for taming the vicious,
the sequence is the same:
the wrathful ones, the sequence transposed,
should be meditated on merely so that the sequence is not mixed.
Through the gathering of the yogins' requisites,
the gathering of maṇḍala, samādhi, and conduct,
since all is gathered without exception, it is called a "gathering."
First, faith, diligence,
samaya, view, and the rest go before,
and through consummating samādhi in sequence,
a yogin who has the capacity,
fortunate, with equal measures of heat,
or, if lacking that, accomplishes it by training in emanation and gathering.
In an utterly secluded and pleasing place,
a mountain peak, a secret slope, or an island,
a charnel ground, a mountain hollow, or a forest edge,
free of gatherings of people,
in that place free of obstacles,
since the yogin is like a king,
one should construct a circular house
adorned beautifully with precious and pleasing things,
with outer, inner, and middle parts,
together with a place for walking.
Yogins and yoginīs equal in number,
complete in view, conduct, samādhi, and samaya,
with the requisites of dance, performance, gathering, and emanation,
the requisites of songs and sounds,
the requisites of food and drink,
and the requisites of ornaments and clothing,
joining the vowels and consonants in union,
are seated in the essence of the stacked sun and moon.
Because deities, humans, requisites,
place, and conduct are all gathered,
these are explained as the "gathering."
When skillful means and wisdom are joined,
in the samādhi of the clear father and mother,
all the endless Victors
are summoned like rain from the sky;
upon the father and mother mingled nondually,
empowerment, blessing, and ornaments are bestowed.
With the five nectars and the five meats,
food and drink, the deity delights in the deity,
all are satisfied with light,
and many beings are to attain bliss.
Through the five dances, the five performances, the five steps and words,
dance and performance, play, and utterance,
the light rays of the families are clear in emanation and gathering,
the outer and inner darkness of beings is dispelled,
and one is trained from one vidyādhara ground to the next.
Through the five songs, the five words, and the five families,
uttered while looking in each direction,
all beings attain bliss,
and the sound of the great conch of Dharma resounds.
The requisites of outer and inner ornament,
precious crowns and earrings,
bracelets, anklets, and necklaces,
whatever adorns with clothing and ornaments,
make samādhi pleasing and clear
and make one worthy to be beheld by beings.
Endowed with the power of wind and capacity,
the habitual tendencies of the three doors are exhausted;
through gaining mastery over the wind of blessing,
joining the vowels and consonants in union,
through joining at the time when the sixteen are complete,
when the lord meets the one of passion,
when sun and moon rise at the same time,
all the capacities of the domains,
common and uncommon, are reversed,
and the defining character of the path of seeing itself is born.
For a month or a half month, at the junctures of time and so on,
the experience of great bliss is unbroken.
Through resting evenly in the meaning that has been seen,
it becomes stable and certain, and one gains confidence.
The body is the great mudrā,
great bliss, undefiled samādhi;
even for hundreds of thousands of eons
great bliss is unbroken.
Through enjoyments emanated,
the beings of many world systems
are then joined to the expanse of beings.
From the time the vajra body is attained onward,

is the everlasting svastika, free of birth and death.
Through the twelve, the fourteen and the sixteen,
the sacred family of mastery is accomplished.
Then, up to the sixteenth,
within that very bindu of wisdom
the bindu of wisdom itself appears,
inconceivable, infinite, supreme.
Persons who enter gradually,
by first seeing the actual meaning,
are to traverse the grounds in this very life;
in order to attain spontaneous accomplishment of all that is intended and done,
through view, conduct and samādhi linked in sequence,
they strive on the uninterrupted path.
However many stages of circumstance there may be,
ordinary people, those of wrong conduct,
those of mere aspiration and the like are not its domain;
by attaining capacity and accomplishment
one should link the secret with the path.
Abiding in the five great yogas,
becoming stable stage by stage,
one who attains the clear and unmoving
is endowed with fearless strength;
like a lotus in the mud,
by abiding in the various kinds of conduct,
the root is the yoga with elaboration;
practice abides in the gatherings of the appointed times,
and the branches are taught as the conduct of the gathering.
As for why the maṇḍala is definitively taught:
self-awareness without edge or middle is the center;
the four wisdoms radiating in the manner of a wheel are the circle.
What transcends the world is itself the center;
the manifold world is explained as the retinue.
The very meaning of awareness is the center;
self-arisen miraculous displays are taught as the retinue.
Vajrasattva is the center;
the gods and goddesses of the four families,
the wrathful males and wrathful females, are explained as the retinue.
The samādhi of the deity is the center;
conduct and implements are explained as the retinue.
Whatever is sealed by whatever,
through the force of habituation and growth,
all phenomena, as they appear and resound,
become the very essence of that.

This is the sixth chapter, the complete explanation of the maṇḍala, from the Tantra of the Secret Mirror of Vajrasattva's Net of Magical Display (Māyājāla).

Then, what is further to be explained:
the perfect rite of empowerment,
the ritual procedure that joins one to the grounds of skillful means,
I shall explain.
The great secret of all the Victors,
the very ocean of all without remainder,
the universal form of all the Victors,
in the assembly of the retinue, the ocean of wisdom,
together with the natural maṇḍala,
relying on the wisdom goddess,
through the empowerment of great wisdom,
the Lord of Secrets becomes the great Vajradhara.
By the lord of the ocean of wisdom,
from the first ground up to the tenth,
in the maṇḍala of blazing samādhi,
relying on the goddess of awareness,
through empowerment by the river of samādhi,
one advances through the grounds, the wisdoms, the buddhafields,
and the stages of miraculous deeds.
Having attained the vajra body on the path of seeing,
for the yogin endowed with the experience of penetrating insight,
in the maṇḍala of the experience of awareness,
relying on the dhāraṇī through the dynamic power of awareness,
one is joined to the undefiled vajra body.
The yogin who has attained stable awareness,
for the yogin who has the karma and the good fortune,
in the maṇḍala of the exalted image,
relying on empowerment and blessing
through the beautiful mudrā of an awareness consort,
confers the empowerment of ritual means;
and through the instructions of the lineage of empowerment
the yoga endowed with the experience of union is attained.
Wise with an ocean of wisdom,
protecting beings with an ocean of compassion,
having obtained samaya, empowerment and the warmth of awareness,
and the empowerment handed down stage by stage through the lineage,
skilled in the meaning of the profound intent,
a seed able to withstand conditions, of great splendor,
not confused about rites and activities,
undistracted, with diligence free of interruption,
like the lion, king of beasts,
of great splendor, never intimidated by others,
possessing the power of gathered blessings,
accomplishing the blessing of word and truth:
such a one should undertake the rite of empowerment.
One endowed with faith and samaya,
with great compassion, eager to learn,
perfect in diligence and wisdom,
who for eons as many as the atoms of the realms
has been well established in hearing, reflection and meditation,
with an unchanging, steady intention,
having abandoned ordinary thoughts,
should regard the master as the Tathāgata,
as the lord of taming, skilled in means.
That master, the buddha, in turn,
should think of the disciple with compassion, as of a patient,
abandoning faults, harshness and deceitful intent.
Those endowed each with their own capacity,
with diligence able to accomplish the aims of self and others,
fully endowed with the three disciplines,
seeking for the sake of accomplishment,
certain to belong to the family of the great secret,
and not falling under the sway of others:
to them one should give empowerment and instructions.
On a mountainside or the like, rich in resources,
where trees and flowers flourish,
a mountain fragrant with the scent of sandalwood,
a place of one's own control, free of obstacles,
a mountain hermitage, a place of good qualities
outer, inner and secret:
in such a hermitage where all is gathered without remainder,
once it is found, there one should dwell.
For the person who has perfected the ten grounds,
in a place of buddhafields without limit,
the Victor, the great Vajradhara,
relying on the wisdom goddess,
through the instantaneous empowerment of wisdom,
the eighteen qualities spontaneously present
are spontaneously complete in that very instant,
and one abides on the ground of the Lotus Eye
and ripens on the ground of the Wheel of Syllables.
With great bliss that makes no division of self and other,
one's intent is impartial;
through a nature without increase or decrease,
one benefits others like a wish-fulfilling jewel.
For the being who has attained the ground, habituated and stable,
in the palace of the awareness of samādhi,
the regent, the being who is lord of the frontiers,
with an ageless goddess of awareness,
beyond family, accomplished through the power of samādhi:
the disciple, relying on her with certainty,
just as water is poured into water,
through the empowerment of the blessing of samādhi,
the empowerments free of any form
arise by themselves as the empowerment of experience.
Through attainment, stability and habituation,
in a hundred worlds, ten thousand,
a hundred thousand and more, the aims of self and others are perfected;
since this depends on but a single instant,
the regent is explained to be a king of Dharma.
For one who has entered the path, whose family has been determined and who has obtained the signs,
the master who abides on the path near its end,
over a krośa, a yojana and so on,
with the vajra measuring lines, joined and declared,
makes the maṇḍala of mudrās arise vividly:
this is taught as the supreme manifold maṇḍala.
In the maṇḍala of a thousand buddhas
that holds sway through manifold awareness,
relying on a powerful maiden,
through the empowerment of means and samādhi,
one gains mastery over vajra life
and comes to possess miraculous powers and higher knowledges.
First, the supreme child certain in family,
endowed with every good quality,
one of unchanging, stable lineage
endowed with faith, diligence and discipline,
is granted permission when the warmth is present and receives empowerment;
by the master who is like a king,
in the exalted image endowed with blessing,
the mind of faith, devotion, diligence and engagement
abides in an irreversible and steady manner.
Crown ornament, diadem and garland,
parasol, vase, food and drink,
armor, mudrā and victory banner,
and the empowerment by the five essences:
upon the way things abide by their nature and essence,

through empowerment by substances, means and samādhi,
the blessing of capacity becomes manifest,
and one comes to belong to the vajra family of the Great Vehicle.
Then that fortunate disciple,
reversing all ordinary perceptions,
for the clarity in which each is made into the deity,
matching the measure of capacity with experience,
receives the empowerment of listening, of meditation,
of holding, of performing enlightened activity,
and the empowerment of the vajra king master.
For one who abides by nature and essence,
like water placed in water,
through the empowerment of means and samādhi,
the measure is whether one lacks capacity or has attained it:
it is not conferred on one who lacks capacity;
it is conferred in stages on those who meet the measure.
The awareness consort, the action mudrā,
aged sixteen or eighteen,
fragrant and endowed with good qualities:
in the non-duality of means and wisdom,
in which the essence of all the Victors is gathered,
the channel wheel of vajra and lotus,
endowed with the taste of the five wisdoms,
the supreme vessel of means and wisdom,
by dissolving into the tip in the manner of the three means,
all the Victors of the three times,
as Vajrasattva the size of a mustard seed,
dissolving from tip to tip,
the three kinds of negative tendency are purified;
the very essence of channel, vital essence and bindu
becomes clear like the light of a lamp,
and the experience and signs of great bliss are attained:
they arise right now, without doubt.
One fully endowed with the vajra sun,
who has well reached fourteen or sixteen,
with the qualities of outer, inner and secret,
having made the awareness consort a goddess through blessing,
the very methods of generating passion
are to be made manifest by one endowed with intelligence.
Then, when all the rites are complete,
in order for the master to entrust them as non-dual,
having again performed the union of blessing,
taking hold of the ring fingers of both,
and entrusting her well to this disciple,
one should give the secret permission:
"This person you have taken is excellent.
From this day on, you, supreme child,
by joining your three doors evenly as the deity,
abandon the inferior, ordinary view!
By experiencing great bliss,
abandon the view of the vehicle of austerities!
Through the non-duality of means and wisdom,
make the experience of bliss stable!
By meditating stably, without interruption,
you will go to the ground of Vajradhara.
Therefore, O fortunate one of good family,
never be separated from her!"
Through the means of union and samādhi,
and by gaining mastery over the moving wind,
there is no obstruction in the work of the bindu.
As many maṇḍalas of the Victors as there are atoms:
the bindu itself is where all of them gather without remainder.
The ground, too, is the thirteenth, endowed with both.
Relying on experience and joining non-dually,
the bindu supported by the wind
arises as one taste, simultaneous,
neither forceful nor slow,
neither the experience of contemplation nor clinging attachment.
The mind of the melting of the vowels and consonants (āli-kāli),
the garland of buddhas, is without duality.
Vajra and lotus are vigorously stirred,
and from the mudrā of the embrace and the dance,
when the tips of sun and moon meet and come together,
the act is to hold the bindu of the wind,
cut off its movement and press the bindu down,
and set consciousness in the state of space.
According to greater or lesser faculties and wisdom,
until it has become stable three times,
or until it is attained, it should be given.
The vajra consort, beautiful as moonlight,
avoiding those who are base or of bad family,
untouched by the vajra organ,
not enjoyed while under another's control,
of twelve, fourteen, or fifteen also:
by means, food and conduct,
the rite of approach should be performed according to the scripture.
With the vajra tongue, the ring finger,
an artificial ring finger and the like,
through the branches of means and anointing,
the secret palace should be opened wide.
Having found the channel that overwhelms saṃsāra,
join it fully with the longing lord.
When signs of the bindu, fast or slow, arise,
hold its movement to the measure of the wind.
When sun and moon arise at one time,
the great means that overwhelms saṃsāra:
by experiencing it, one attains the river-like samādhi.
If it parts from the channel,
with phaṭ it will be emitted;
by equalizing the pure essence and the wind it will be held.
Know it by the hūṃ that reverses and draws up;
by the hūṃ of gathering it is bound and remains,
and the bindu pervades the body.
The first places the seed,
the second generates capacity,
the third generates experience,
the fourth makes that very thing stable,
and the fifth, practicing with stability,
accomplishes the supreme siddhi in this life.
The vajra master who has obtained permission,
endowed with capacity, should undertake the rite of consecration.
Then the disciple should be admitted in the proper way.
Having imposed the samaya of the vows,
free of all the faults of the three doors,
one should give the stages of empowerment.
In order to stabilize the experience of empowerment,
one should give the corresponding instructions.
Instructions, stable experience and the stages of the path:
the path brought to perfection is the result itself.
At the culmination of warmth, at the eighth ground,
and at the pure grounds of the greatly renowned buddhas,
through the signs of self-awareness and through compassion,
the guiding powers appear as three ḍākinīs,
of space, of sound and of form.
In the one known as the Lotus Woman (Padminī),
union and songs appear,
and by stabilizing mere form one is liberated.
The root maṇḍala of empowerment
is the uncontrived maṇḍala of the vajra expanse.
The maṇḍala of the wrathful ones is ancillary,
for accomplishment and for taming the vicious.
First, having obtained the root empowerment,
one should obtain the empowerment of the later maṇḍala.
For disciples, empowerment is firmly set according to their inclination;
it is not fixed as merely common.
The first, second and third
are the empowerments of Vajrasattva;
the fourth and the fifth
are the maṇḍala of Heruka.

Those who do not please and honor
the master who teaches the unmistaken path,
blessed by the Victors of the three times,
and who, without being granted empowerment and permission
for each to engage according to their capacity,
explain and listen to the secret,
meditate on it, practice it and so on,
will not attain the result
for countless tens of millions of eons,
but will be born in the realms of hell.
Because they have scorned all the holy ones,
many illnesses and sufferings,
ḍākinīs and grahas will harm them,
and then they will surely die.
When the master endowed with blessing
empowers and blesses through skillful means
the supreme person, certain in family,
fully endowed with faith, diligence and discipline,
the Victors, the ḍākinīs,
and the accomplished male and female vidyādharas
fulfill all one's wishes in this life,
and one becomes the master of perfect
blessing, empowerment and accomplishment.
When one is empowered in this way,
ordinary clinging is purified,
and having actually entered the path of great bliss,
one is unhindered in any accomplishment;
whatever actions one undertakes are accomplished,
and by enjoying the aims one desires,
one perfects the stages of the ten grounds,
attains the ground of the Lotus Eye,
and abides on the ground of the Wheel of Syllables.
From body, speech, mind, qualities and activity,
the twenty-five are spontaneously present:
the result of empowerment dawns from within oneself.
Whoever is apart from this great means,
though they seek the result with diligence
for eons as many as the sands of the Ganges,
will find the result they desire empty.
Because of the master, the disciple,
and the maṇḍala, in their proper sequence,
through action, samādhi and blessing,
the rite of entry is explained as threefold.
The great secret of the body, speech and mind
of all the buddhas of the three times,
the great path of the Net of Magical Display,
the vast rite, complete without remainder,
of the glorious Vajra Mirror:
according to the stages of entry,
all the secrets should be known.
That very vajra master,
beautiful in respectful attire,
holding flowers, makes the wheel manifest,

the deities recall their samaya and turn their attention.

Then, what is further to be explained:
the unsurpassed vajra samaya,
the firm vows that have been pledged:
these I shall explain well.
By all the buddhas of the three times,
in the past, the future, all three times,
it has been decreed like a royal law or an oath,
decreed in stages as a curse:
its nature as blessing is hard to transgress,
and it is explained as what all must guard.
For with regard to this great samaya
blessed by the holy ones,
the Victors of past, present and future with their heirs,
the vidyādharas and the ḍākinīs,
the gods and humans,
the nāgas, non-humans and hosts of bhūtas,
even the great gods endowed with miraculous powers,
by transgressing the sacred will be burned.
As for this very nature of the sacred:
persons who practice the intent of all the Victors
according to the mere sound of the words
will be burned by practicing it wrongly.
It is decreed by the holy ones,
and its blessing is naturally established as a curse:
whoever transgresses this sacred pledge,
even one who holds the vajra, will be burned.
If, having obtained empowerment and blessing
in the intent of the holy ones,
one practices with certainty in the manner of the sacred,
all adverse factors will be burned.
Therefore the buddhas of the three times
proclaimed it as samaya.
The unsurpassed samaya
is the self-aware mind of awakening, complete without remainder,
free of edge and center, of going and coming,
the very nature of mind, without base or root.
Whoever realizes this meaning is transformed:
this is called the ultimate mind of awakening.
The unborn mind of awakening itself
appears as the very nature of birth;
one's own body is the mudrā of the Victor,
and all that appears there is of one essence.
Clearly realizing the three doors to be like oneself
is explained as the relative mind of awakening.
The one who explains the secret, confers empowerment,
gives instructions and scriptural transmission, restores what is broken,
bestows accomplishments supreme, common and the rest,
and brings liberation,
is equal to the buddhas;
therefore do not let your devotion lapse for even an instant.
Being non-dual with one's own deity,
the yogin clear in the four mudrās
keeps the three doors in evenness,
without interruption, like a river.
The secret of all the buddhas
is the very secret of the Great Vehicle;
therefore to those who are not vessels, to the inferior and the like, to the perverse,
the ten secrets and so on should not be spoken.
First, being of one life with the secret,
and never parted from the essence of awakening,
it would be easier to cut off one's own life oneself:
do not part from the samaya of love and devotion.
These are the root of the path and of practice,
and the root of all accomplishments;
if these are impaired, all is impaired,
and so they are taught as the root.
The branch samayas, common
and uncommon, I shall now explain.
First, having received the secret scriptural transmission,
until capacity becomes manifest,
for the sake of oneself and others, in common ways,
going beyond all contradiction, exaggeration and denial,
and pleasing everyone's minds,
not contravening the common samaya,

is explained to be the common samaya.
For the uncommon person,
the uncommon great means,
the five afflictions and the five conducts,
the five nectars and the five consciousnesses,
the very dharmas of the five accomplishments,
are the domain of the being whose family has been determined;
since they are not the domain of the common yogin,
they are explained as uncommon.
From the very first until the signs are attained,
with body and mind undistracted and one-pointed,
the root and branch samayas
are to be taken up together with their forms.
When the ground is attained, habituated and perfected,
and one abides in the meaning of samādhi,
since the three doors no longer act contrary,
in the samaya of continuous samādhi
all the samayas of form are gathered without remainder.
When one rises from that meaning, there is compassion,
and the root and branches are carried by their own momentum.
When one abides on the ground of a buddha,
it is the samaya that cannot be transgressed:
like the moisture of the ocean, there is nothing to gain or abandon;
it is the samaya of wisdom that needs no guarding.
This samaya is most wondrous.
Until approach is accompanied by signs,
one should guard the general samaya;
and when whatever practice one undertakes is to be accomplished,
without the specific samaya of each ritual procedure
it will not be accomplished.
In order to establish each person in the secret meaning
through the samaya and austerity in which they delight,
infinite kinds of samaya have been taught.
To the yogin who is a beginner
the root and the common samayas are taught.
To the person who has relied on these and is endowed with experience,
the uncommon samaya is taught.
To the fearless person of realization
the samaya without transgression or impairment is taught.
To one who has attained the ground, habituated and perfected,
the samaya of continuous samādhi is taught.
Connected with all accomplishments and realizations,
this samaya is the great vajra.
This samaya with its root and branches,
blessed by all the buddhas:
whoever obtains it and guards it as sacred
is said to accomplish it even with few good qualities.
All the ritual procedures for accomplishment,
supreme, common and the rest,
whatever rite, whatever samādhi,
whatever practice it may be,
by striving greatly to keep it unimpaired
will be swiftly accomplished.
If one is not blessed,
even Vajradhara himself would come to ruin.
Since the very qualities of all the Victors
arise from the vajra samaya,
whoever relies on this sacred pledge
becomes like a wish-fulfilling jewel.
This sacred austerity to be guarded,
of the body, speech, mind and branches
of all the Victors of the three times,
is not to be transgressed by anyone;
it is the very sacred abiding.
Persons of inferior karma and fortune,
of inferior family and great laziness,
and of evil nature:
whatever fool transgresses it,
turns his back on the two accomplishments,
and curses the holy ones,
having wronged all the buddhas,
the king of vajra holders becomes wrathful
and destroys him and his line for up to seven generations.
The ḍākinīs and vidyādharas
and the hosts of fierce, flame-mouthed, raging and terrifying ones
cut off his life at once,
and he is born in the realms of hell.
Even if that world is destroyed,
he will be born in another world,
and until space is destroyed
or the outer ocean runs dry,
he will be freed elsewhere never at all.
Abandoning the holy guru,
quarreling with vajra brothers and sisters,
not remembering the mind of awakening itself,
neglecting mantra and mudrā,
explaining the secret to those who are not vessels,
and denigrating the secret meaning:
for these one will experience suffering here too,
and the instant one ceases, immediately,
without even a body of the intermediate state,
one will be born in the vajra hell.
The dharmas to be guarded and imposed,
the root together with the branches,
connected with the common empowerment
and forming the common, general domain:
these are to be guarded in common,
and this is the outer samaya.
The mind joining means and wisdom,
the stages of the path of the five minds of the bindu:
when awareness is clear without remainder,
the non-dual bindu arises in experience.
By transforming the essence of the bindu of bliss,
the six collections, self-appearing, become companions of the bindu.
Since all is blessed by the bindu,
everything is clear as the mudrā of great bliss.
The bindu of awareness is not to be released outward.
Through the bliss of the union of the wheels,
ordinary bliss is transformed into the faculties.
The intent and conduct of the Victors are experienced through means,
and ordinary clinging is reversed.
For one endowed with means, the meaning is the secret meaning:
the five maṇḍalas of light of one's own heart,
by the stages of arising and setting,
like the secret bindu of the great means,
once known, abide as nothing to abandon.
The five self-arisen essences that abide
appear as the meaning of one's own body, the support;
by the ways in which they pervade and move,
they abide as both refined and dregs.
The concepts of one's own body, refined and dregs,
with their colors, limbs and lesser limbs,
are known as the five wisdoms of bliss
and are taught to be great bliss itself.
Born from the essence of the five elements,
they are entirely the nature of the bindu;
endowed with the taste of the five wisdoms,
they are truly taught to be the five Victors.
Means and wisdom, vajra and bell,
the luminosity of the stages of sun and moon:
when the five minds become clear in space,
the supported and the support are non-dual,
and coemergent wisdom appears.
What is not retained is taken up by the vajra tongue.
All the concepts of the two
are blessed as the single taste of great bliss.
The mind of bliss, not parted even for an instant,
abides firmly, unparted throughout the three times.
Through the recitation of wind and bindu,
everything is clear as the mudrā of great bliss.
Great bliss comes through means and experience;
it is not reached by speech and words.
Through the bliss of the meeting of the two in union,
the ordinary is exhausted and one is joined to great bliss.
What no one is able to give,
one now experiences directly by one's own capacity.
This meaning, which for others is false,
is shown through means, directly, as true.
In order to abandon ordinary clinging,
it is free of misdeed and of shame.
At the times of arising, capacity and abiding,
the five natures are accomplished as a matter of course.
From each of one's own channels and essences,
the nectar abides inseparably.
The five elements, the faculties, form and so on,
through the blessing of union endowed with means,
are known as coemergent great bliss.
Sun and moon held by the wind:
from the essence free of signs,
through the great means free of clinging,
from the coemergent nature,
being filled with the taste of the five wisdoms,
all are accomplished as the five Victors.
This is the great samaya of the nature
of outer, inner and secret.
The samayas, infinite in kind,
in the most condensed form are of two kinds;
in the middling form, they are to be known
as five root samayas and ten branches.
The most extensive samayas
number three hundred and sixty in general:
luminosity, the nature itself,
the appearance of one's own maṇḍala,
the supreme Victors of the four times,

perfection in evenness, with nothing to accomplish,
the samaya of abiding in spontaneous bliss,
utterly pure awakening free of apprehended and apprehender,
the samaya of holding the meaning once realized,
the samaya of abiding in the mind,
the samaya uncontrived from the very beginning,
the samaya of the complete purity of the conditioned,
the samaya of the inseparability of phenomena,
the samaya of fearlessness in all things,
the samaya of the svastika that increases life,
and that in which there is nothing at all to guard:
these are the samayas of the view.
The samaya of uniting with all,
the samaya of liberating all without remainder,
the samaya of taking what is not given,
the samaya of speaking falsehood,
the samaya of enjoying all things,
and the samaya of enjoying the sacred substances:
these are the samayas of conduct.
The three kinds of samaya of practice are:
the samaya of the vajra birthplace,
the samaya of the power of the place that guides,
the samaya that shows the path,
the samaya that brings full realization,
the samaya of transforming awakening,
the samaya of mastery over all,
and the samaya of the power of the vidyādhara state:
these are the samayas of realizing what is to be accomplished.
The samaya of the mother, rich in the expanse,
the samaya of joining means itself with wisdom,
and likewise of joining wisdom with means,
the samaya of overpowering the majesty of the elephant,
the samaya of letting nothing be wasted,
the samaya of protecting all,
and the samaya of the mudrā of samādhi:
these are the samayas of the means of practice.
The samaya of the gathering offering,
the samaya of the gathering maṇḍala,
the samaya of the gathering activities,
and the samaya of satisfying the piśācīs:
these are the samayas of the activities of practice.
The samaya of showing the true meaning,
the samaya of the infinite supreme speech,
the samaya of meeting all languages,
the samaya of meeting without letters,
the samaya renowned throughout the three-thousandfold world,
the samaya of the supreme speech of wisdom,
the samaya of the inconceivable vajra speech,
and the samaya of the unchanging vajra:
these are the samayas to be known by oneself.
The four samayas of samādhi on what is to be known:
the samaya of space that illuminates,
the samaya of earth that supports,
the samaya of fire that burns,
the samaya of water that stirs,
and the samaya of the great one that scatters:
these are the samayas of the five wisdoms;
and the samayas of the five aggregates
are the samayas of the five supreme means.
The samaya of phenomena as reflections,
the samaya of knowing them as like sound,
the samaya of knowing them as like scent,
the samaya of knowing them as like taste,
and the samaya of knowing them as like touch:
these are the samayas of non-duality.
The samaya of the five colors,
the samaya of the five directions,
and the samaya of the five empowerments:
these three are the samayas that pervade generally.
The samaya of the very state of bliss,
the samaya of spontaneously present wisdom,
the samaya of the fearless supreme Victor,
the samaya of spontaneous perfection in evenness,
the samaya of the inseparability of directions and times,
the samaya of one's own maṇḍala,
the samaya of faultlessness in the Dharma,
the samaya beyond expression and beyond elaboration,
the samaya of the non-duality of directions and times,
the samaya of the non-dual mind of awakening,
the samaya of the unconditioned,
the samaya of sealing all,
and the samaya of obstructing nothing:
these are the samayas of the samādhi of essence.
The five to be accomplished are taught:
the samaya of the wheel of Dharma,
the samaya of the source of jewels,
the samaya of the lotus free of attachment,
the samaya of accomplishing all aims,
the samaya of the terrifying vajra,
the samaya of the body of suchness,
and the samaya of being endowed with all riches:
these are explained as the seven samayas of body.
Showing the true meaning and the rest,
the eight samayas of speech themselves,
are to be known as complete within oneself;
they are to be accomplished by guarding the result.
The samaya of mastery of self-awareness,
the samaya of the utter purity of the great and supreme,
the samaya of natural luminosity,
the samaya that binds awakening,
and the samaya of mastery over all,
six in all, are explained as the samayas of mind.
The samaya of arraying the fields of awakening,
the samaya of the abode where wisdom dwells,
the samaya of showing the celestial palace,
the samaya of transforming all the manifold,
the samaya of possessing the inexhaustible treasure,
the samaya of inexhaustible practice,
and the samaya of the great inexhaustible treasure:
these are explained as the seven samayas of qualities.
The samaya of blessing all,
the samaya of cutting down the hosts of Māra,
and the samaya of acting for the benefit of sentient beings
are explained as the three samayas of activity.
The samaya of the vajra of abandoning,
the samaya of the wisdom of desire,
the samaya of great delusion,
the samaya of great jealousy,
the samaya of pride as supreme bliss,
and the samaya of the five afflictions:
these are the samayas of not abandoning.
What is guarded, never distracted at all:
the samaya of the five equalities,
the samaya of the inseparability of the truths,
the samaya of uniting body, speech and mind,
the samaya of completing the five paths,
the samaya of uniting all in bliss,
the samaya of the vajra that crushes the perverse,
the samaya of overpowering saṃsāra,
the samaya of the uncontrived and spontaneously present,
and the samaya of union in the bliss of the bindu:
these are the samayas of continually guarding the view.
The samaya of continually meditating in samādhi,
the samaya of binding the mudrās,
the samaya of recitation with melodious sound,
the samaya of evenness of the three doors,
and the samaya of union with wisdom in bliss:
these are the samayas of continuous samādhi.
The samaya of overpowering conventions,
the samaya of ease and richness in one's conduct,
the samaya of the direct leap of awareness,
the samaya of the uncontrived pure place,
and the samaya of the self-arisen, free of place:
these are the means of guarding that abide all at once.
The samaya that generates all,
the samaya that joins the six continua,
the samaya that satisfies through realization,
the samaya of pervasive space,
and the samaya of accomplishing the benefit of others as a matter of course:
these teach the greatness of samaya.
The great hells and the ordinary hells,
pretas, animals, rākṣasas,
yakṣas, womb-spirits, the flame-mouthed and flame-tongued,
gods, asuras, Rudra,
Brahmā and the rest, saṃsāra:
these are taught as the births that are the penalty.
This is the great samaya of guarding.
If one realizes the meaning of emptiness,
there is no such thing as restoring or impairing;
it has passed beyond the domain of penalties.
It is the domain of only a few persons.
If a root samaya is impaired,
before a hundred gurus and companions
one should make a hundred thousand confessions,
perform seventy million recitations,
offer seven hundred thousand tormas,
and perform one million one hundred thousand fire offerings.
Then one should take it again,
or remain once the guru has given permission.
If that object of confession is not present,

or if three years or more have passed,
or if, on seeing the manner of confession,
thoughts of faithlessness and craving arise,
it has passed beyond the limit of restoration.
Even confessing for eighty thousand great eons,
one will not be purified;
in what is called the Vajra Hell
one experiences unbearable suffering,
and for as long as space endures
one whirls about like a wheel of weapons.
If a branch samaya is impaired,
one should keep silence for seven months,
perform one thousand and eight fire offerings,
recitation to the measure of ten million and one hundred thousand,
and offer ten thousand tormas.
Then one should take it again,
and proclaim oneself pure and cleansed.
Teaching and explaining the secret
to one who is not a pure vessel,
transgressing the command without having obtained empowerment,
going beyond the word of the Sugatas,
proclaiming the secret at full measure
to one who is not a qualified vessel,
and, without practicing the secret oneself,
spreading it to everyone out of vanity:
such is a person who has gone beyond all limits.
Because he shows contempt for all the buddhas,
the buddhas of the three times, enraged,
destroy him down to the seventh generation.
When guru and companions,
father and mother, relatives and friends, king and ministers,
and the king's subjects turn hostile without any fault,
this is explained as a sign that samaya has been impaired.
Obstacles arising to the profound,
practice not bringing about what is desired,
the protecting deities growing negligent,
and disgrace arising without any wrongdoing
are explained as signs that samaya has been impaired.
When, having first received the guru's transmission,
one's own awareness senses no impairment,
wishes for happiness and well-being are fulfilled,
and the particular aims of oneself and others
are accomplished as before, like a wish-fulfilling jewel,
and praise is received,
these are the signs of being unimpaired and meaningful.
Such a one is a king, a lord of yogins.
Even if signs of being unimpaired arise,
quickly undertake the rite of restoration and repair.
If it is impaired yet there are no signs
and all appears well, that is not right:
one's own seed has been burnt,
happiness is used up, and one suffers forever.
If, when signs of being unimpaired arise, one quickly restores it,
former habitual tendencies will be exhausted.
This is the eighth chapter, explaining samaya without exception, from the Tantra of the Secret Mirror of Vajrasattva's Net of Magical Display.

Next, what is to be rightly explained:
for all the varieties of accomplishment,
supreme, common, and so on,
I shall explain the ritual procedures of the manuals in brief.
From all the kings of tantras without exception,
those who have entered through devotion to the common,
by unbroken training in the common requisites, samādhi,
mantra, and mudrā,
and by bringing signs and power to completion,
accomplish whatever they aim at:
this is the contaminated, common accomplishment.
I shall explain what is other than that.
When a yogin of the supreme
obtains empowerment, samaya, and samādhi
in the stages of outer, inner, and secret,
he attains the irreversible, stable yoga.
From seeing onward, on the path of meditation the family is not fixed;
whatever samādhi he cultivates is without obstruction.
In this very life he gains mastery and is free from fear,
and then attains the nature of the five kāyas.
Because the one endowed with the supreme attains the supreme through the supreme,
the supreme accomplishment is not common.
One who has gained faith, samaya, wisdom,
diligence, discipline, and patience,
who is dependable and loving, complete in empowerment,
who knows the practice and the application of the path,
who has mastered his mind and is irreversible and steadfast,
and who has gained experience and gladly takes it up:
such a person, alone, or in groups of five or three,
in mountains, in hidden mountain hollows,
on river islands, in ravines and rock fortresses,
in charnel grounds, mountain caves, and at river confluences,
in places pleasing, auspicious, and delightful,
completes the rite and its blessing.
Whatever rite of activity there may be,
performed by oneself or by another,
is endowed with one's own mastery and perfect auspiciousness.
Through completing the preliminaries
of samaya, empowerment, and view,
whether one has first attained the three yogas
or is simply one whose mind is steady from the start,
whatever the mind focuses upon
is itself linked with rite and samādhi,
according to each one's own ritual manual.
Ordinariness, laziness, and doubt
are overcome by the power of faith;
if one is not endowed with these,
it does not succeed even for one who has attained the grounds,
so what need to speak of ordinary persons?
If one performs the rite of summoning,
oneself is the powerful and fierce wrathful one,
deep red, the color of smoke.
Abiding as above, what radiates and gathers
is nothing but nooses and hooks.
With steady focus, having gained the signs,
until it becomes manifest,
recite one's own mantra, and at its end,
"so-and-so jaḥ hūṃ."
Enemies, obstructors, gods, and dhāraṇī holders,
even if endowed with power and miraculous ability,
are summoned helpless like ordinary beings.
If its purposes
are not quickly accomplished,
until they are well accomplished,
the skilled one should bind whoever it may be.
Through speech, method, and samādhi
it will be accomplished, without any doubt.
Upon the tongue of oneself, vivid as the powerful fierce one,
imagine the wheel of magnetizing,
with nine arranged, bearing the three syllables.
Its light fills the realm of space
like molten ruby;
whoever it dissolves into comes under one's power.
Declaring the truth, bind them with speech.
Or else, just as someone long desires
the substance to which he is attached,
make a pledge to fulfill his wish,
and with the samādhi of pleasing taming,
attend to him with pleasing means.
If he wishes to go and stray away,
threaten, bind, and torment him with the powerful fierce mudrā.
Having captivated his mind with pleasing means,
like people gathered and enthralled
by musicians and conjurers,
parted from their tasks by such means,
he will remain without parting.
Through the power of stable samādhi,
view that sentient being, whoever it may be,
as bound by various wrathful males and females
with light and blazing mudrās
and remaining before you.
Pleasing, threatening, and at last the substances:
through stable samādhi these certainly succeed.
If things are certainly produced from nothing,
and the eight adverse ones and the rest are certainly accomplished,
what need to mention bringing under control
beings who exist and appear?
If one performs the rite of healing,
the rite is twofold, accomplished by undertaking it.
Having continually generated oneself,
and absorbed all empowerments and blessings,
in a crystal vase in front
generate from the heart the goddess of water.
The Victors of the ten directions become her,
and the goddess melts into light in the vase.
Imagine disease and suffering purified.
Milk of a reddish-yellow cow,
medicines, jewels, incense, and grains,
and the ingredients of the nectar essence
are consecrated with the mantra of Māmakī:
oṃ māmakī sarba paba duru duru svāhā
A body afflicted with disease and suffering,
when washed with it, will doubtlessly be restored to health.
Oneself, endowed with the bliss of discipline,
having first completed the preliminaries,
in a hermitage, a remote and utterly solitary place,
an uninjured, undecayed, properly qualified

great corpse from the charnel ground, bearing the marks,
having performed all the washing, anointing, and fumigation,
whether it is male, female, or otherwise,
that rite will surely be accomplished.
Having placed it well
in the center of a clean maṇḍala,
oneself vivid as any wrathful one whatever,
summon even Brahmā or Śakra (Indra)
with one's own mantra, the life-essence, and jaḥ,
and bring them down with the four mudrās.
If trembling and laughter,
weeping and terrifying sounds occur,
display the samaya mudrā.
Give it the oath-water, bind it under oath,
and dispatch it to whatever task one wishes.
If, being worldly, one falls into doubt,
one will be devoured by it, will faint,
and will oneself die at once.
When one with stable samādhi
practices without interruption,
becoming lustrous and lovely is the first sign;
a sweet fragrance and a glow is the second;
uttering sounds of joy is the third;
brilliance and splendor are the reviving of its vigor.
If hri comes from the mouth of the great corpse,
cut it off with the vajra teeth of samādhi.
If the vetāla is struck, it turns to gold.
When that is cut, the sword of the tongue
is armor and weapon nothing can withstand;
since it defeats even the armies of the asuras,
what need to mention powerful heroes?
In its five sense faculties
lie the accomplishments of the five superknowledges.
If the skilled one practices
according to his own ritual manual, it will be accomplished.
If one washes and anoints the beautiful corpse
of a maiden of seventeen or eighteen,
adorns it well with ornaments and garments,
and then, into that fragrant one with pure faculties,
oneself through the yoga of the powerful fierce one
summons and binds with mantra and mudrā
a goddess, either of wisdom or of the world,
lovely and blazing with light,
one should dissolve her in until signs appear.
Fragrance, sound, and light,
trembling, stirring, and laughter
are explained to be the first signs.
The deity's presence and speech are the second;
actually rising up is the full measure.
Then bind her by pledge and oath and bring her under control.
If, when she is awakened for union,
she displays great miracles and turns perverse,
with the great pride of the powerful fierce one
bring her under control and overwhelm her.
If one enjoys play with them in passion,
in an instant one gains the four miraculous powers:
abiding without birth or death,
blazing entirely with great bliss,
and traveling to all realms
are accomplished without hindrance.
Having sought out and obtained the corpse of a child,
clean, unwounded, lovely, and fine,
purify it and anoint it with nectar and scents,
anoint it well with dahūmata,
and bind it with a five-colored dhāraṇī cord.
Summon Brahmā, Śakra,
or any goddess with the heart mantra,
continually dissolve them in with the four mudrās,
and, vividly meditating on oneself as any wrathful one,
Hayagrīva or Yamāntaka and the rest,
summon forcefully and dissolve them in.
When the signs arise and culminate in manifestation,
it will speak of good and ill.
If it trembles and does not speak,
empower its tongue skillfully
with the light of oṃ and with amṛta,
while Hayagrīva threatens it with laughter;
it will doubtless speak well.
If one performs the secret rite,
the yogin who has gained clarity and stability,
at a charnel ground, a solitary tree, a crossroads,
or a place where gods and demons manifestly dwell,
should imagine oneself as Dveṣavajra,
smoky black, blazing with light,
eyes glaring, weapons blazing like fire,
with emanations raging like a rainstorm.
In front, a homa pit a cubit in size,
triangular, smeared with charnel charcoal and human blood.
Inside it, place the liṅga and the five nectars,
made with the cause of attachment, the heat,
and a support to which the consciousness clings.
Have messengers summon the target with name and clan,
dissolve it in, and bind it with mudrā.
With a fierce kīla made of iron or of black wood,
strike the three places.
With the fierce hriḥ ṣṭīḥ from right and left,
burn it with a wrathful mind;
burning fierce thorny wood within it
at midnight, naked,
even a god will be destroyed.
If one performs the rite of separation,
meditate on oneself as Yamāntaka,
fearsome, vicious, savage, and proud,
utterly terrifying and menacing.
At the hour fearsome by daylight
and at midnight fearsome with darkness,
draw whoever it is and wrap it up,
and entrust it to the two, the gods and the asuras.
Let it be driven by that toward the quarter of the wind.
Imagine emanated messengers waging battle,
fighting and quarrelling in every way;
between them a hawk and a crow fight.
If one imagines fire, water, and wind blowing upon them,
whoever they are, whoever they may be,
they will part and fall to fighting.
In order to drive away kings, brahmins, and ministers,
and the vicious and harmful kinds,
so that they cannot remain,
meditate on oneself as Aparacitta
in a fierce and wrathful manner.
Beneath any enemy or obstructor
draw the wind seed syllable,
and on top mount them, with their name and clan.
Imagine vividly that the one to be tamed, present before one,
rides upon the camel of Vāyudevī,
led and driven on by endless emanated messengers,
and imagine him led into the presence of Yama.
If by this even one without breath, in a faint,
is forcibly driven elsewhere,
what need to mention one who is alive?
If one wishes to defeat an enemy army,
meditate thoroughly on oneself as the protector Acala,
just as he abides.
Having drawn his image as well,
bless it there with the rite,
and over a sword of seven kinds of iron,
twelve finger-widths in measure, together with the heart mantra,
recite the mantra seven hundred thousand times and more,
clearly, until those signs arise.
When there is light, sound, and trembling of the earth,
and the sword gives forth a cry,
drawing it turns back the battle,
and brandishing it puts the army to flight.
If one wishes oneself to be victorious and great,
having become clear and stable,
visualize oneself vividly as Dveṣavajra,
and write the name and clan on charnel-ground cloth.
Summon and dissolve in with mantra and mudrā,
and when certain and stable omens appear,
press it down with the feet of Heruka,
threaten it with the fierce laughter of Hayagrīva,
overpower it with the garuḍa-wing mudrā,
enclose it in a box with oneself and a dog, and expel it.
Press it beneath one's own threshold,
or else press it beneath oneself.
With the crossed-vajra mudrā
press down from above and declare the truth:
one will become their king.
If a poor person wishes to find treasure,
one who is trained in the rite
visualizes the special deity of the five families,
and imagines each one's hand emblem, the size of a mustard seed,
until it is seen with the senses,
reciting the mantra and declaring the truth.
When the sign is stable and clear,
go toward the direction where it lies.
Through the power of truth, samādhi, and mantra,
whether it rests, speaks, or is seen,
the tiny emblem will fall there.
Then one meets with the treasure
and is freed from all poverty.
Then, if the vajra yogin
wishes to have the eye of superknowledge,
oneself, a yogin of the deity,
in an utterly solitary place,
with the five kinds of eyes and the fat of the seventh,
their rendered fat together with soot,

should be mixed with magical milk.
Practice with the vajra pra divination for seven days.
When experiences and the signs of warmth are attained,
anoint the eyes well with it, along with the mantra:
in an instant one sees with superknowledge
the ten directions together with the trichiliocosm.
In order to examine the mindstreams of persons,
to reverse clinging through fierce suffering,
and to bring about mental happiness
through the various pure realms,
accomplish superknowledge well by means of the signs.
One who wishes to attain swift-footedness,
having meditated on oneself as the special deity
and attained stability without interruption,
should sit upon a moon disk.
From yaṃ comes a wind maṇḍala
that travels a hundred yojanas in an instant.
By imagining oneself abiding on it, without distraction,
just as the wind pervades,
one will traverse many hundreds of yojanas
in a mere instant.
If one wishes to go to the level of a vidyādhara
by means of the four miraculous powers,
having first attained the pure meaning,
when the mudrā has become clear,
on a moon in the space liṅga,
from hūṃ a vajra, by the threefold rite,
one gradually gains mastery of stability.
Relying on the mudrā of the refined essence,
relying on the wind together with the vajra and the moon,
one gains the ground of traveling to whatever realm is fitting:
the vajra vidyādhara
accomplishes the activity of the pure realms.
On a moon in the space before one,
transform oṃ by way of the rite.
By the stable wheel, with moon and wind,
the body abiding through the power of the refined essence,
the Tathāgata vidyādhara
travels to the various realms
and should accomplish his own wishes.
On a moon in the expanse of space,
by the rite of what arises from traṃ,
the jewel together with wind and moon,
by the force of intense samādhi that moves it,
with refined body and mind,
one travels to all the pure realms
and one's special intention will be accomplished.
At the center of the maṇḍala of space,
meditate on a moon together with a lotus.
From hriḥ, the mind of supreme Dharma in manifold forms,
clear and stable, its power displayed by the wind:
whatever refined body and mind there may be
travels to the realms, and its wishes are accomplished.
At the center of the expanse of space,
meditate on it together with the various consonants.
From a or ha, train in oneself
as the action mudrā.
Once stable, by the power of the refined wind,
whatever realm one has in mind,
the wish one desires will be accomplished.
Through this the elements have no power over one;
having gained mastery over the deathless wind,
overpowering through the four miracles,
and taming the vicious through superknowledge,

in all the kings of ritual manuals
the stages of the activity rites
are clearly taught as four.
First is the nature of pacifying;
after that, enriching, for the sake of increase;
through increase, magnetizing;
and, to liberate those who will not come under control, the wrathful:
thus the common and special qualities are complete.
Unconfused about the rites to be performed,
with the nature of stable power,
one should undertake them in their proper order.
White snowy slopes and pleasant hillsides are for pacifying;
trees, flower groves,

Charnel grounds, solitary places, crossroads,

Milky trees, golden trees,

Pungent butter and sour beer,

For those of gold or of yellow wood,

Next, what is to be explained is the secret.
From the union of method and wisdom
arise the four secret activities,
in order to turn back ascetics and ordinary people
from the common path.
One who clings to desire, whose faculties are ripe,
together with a qualified consort,
in the hermitage of great bliss, the abode of the bindu,
has all the special qualities complete.
The nature of nondual embrace
is the source from which great bliss arises.
The expansive secret bhaga,
possessing the power and blessing

Whatever secret fire offering is offered
into the secret hearth of the lotus,

The refined and dregs, the essences and imprints
arising from method and wisdom,

clinging and thought, these very attachments:

into the secret object,
in coemergent samādhi

The manner of play and the two bindus,
the channels of the lotus and the vajra essence,

are to be examined at the time of secret union.
The ways the channels abide
differ in color, shape, and so on.
By offering all the offering substances together with sun and moon
into the hearth with the two ladles,
it will descend as the great bindu.

who practice the discipline of awareness,
whose faculties are vigorous and ripe,
who are devoted to the meaning of the bindu,

all yogins, male and female,

appears manifestly, here and now, through method.

Then, what is further to be explained:
for those who delight in the secret path,
the uncommon secret path
I shall explain in its stages.
A person of great faith and great discipline,
whose family is certain in the Great Vehicle,
endowed with compassion, empowerment and samaya,
diligent in practice,
with body, wealth and livelihood,
with children, wife and kingdom,
first pleases the guru,

The whole of the outer and inner, the vessel and its contents,
is the self-appearance of self-arisen wisdom;

Through blessing, scripture and reasoning,
self-arisen wisdom manifestly appears.
The self-abiding wheel is inconceivable,
not the domain of dialecticians.

for increase, for joy and bliss, and for wealth,

peaceful or wrathful as the occasion requires,
should be meditated on as is fitting.
Either with one face and two arms,
or meditate on three faces and six arms.
In one place, three places or five places,
meditate on the limb deities complete.
Having grown familiar, dissolve them, and all domains of experience
should be clearly visualized in that way.
To meditate on the deity of a single root
is the yoga of the single mudrā.
By striving greatly in meditation with the three doors,
one passes beyond distracting religious activities.
It is the conduct that is the root of all,
and is called the conduct of a single focus.
Then the conceptual branches,
both of them, as method and wisdom,
having been visualized, should be meditated on in that way.
The three syllables radiating light,
or else the five wisdoms themselves,
should be placed in one place, as the heart essence.
Likewise, the principal lord and lady,
the male and female sattvas,
and the male and female wrathful ones, meditate on as the retinue;
and know this for mind and mental objects too.
Faces and arms and the number of deities:
when the clusters are elaborated, it is with elaboration.
From body, speech, mind, qualities and activity,
meditate on five clusters, together with the wrathful ones.
Or else, with three clusters
endowed with body, speech and mind,
meditate on the male and female wrathful ones as the retinue.
The lord of the family, the supreme of mind,
together with the host of wrathful ones:
by way of devotion and application,
meditate on the deities of a single cluster.
The master who abides on the path close to its end,
with maṇḍalas of a krośa, a yojana and so forth,
merely by casting the vajra measuring lines,
has the mudrā maṇḍala in its clarity;
this is declared the supreme manifold maṇḍala.
First is the maṇḍala of a krośa,
then the maṇḍala of a yojana;
in the inconceivable expanse of space
meditate on the manifold maṇḍalas of the buddhas.
Then, endowed with the five requisites,
with people in harmony in samādhi, devotion and conduct,
just enough in number to match,
one should accomplish the desired aim as a cluster.
With method and wisdom joined,
males and females gathered in equal number,
endowed with an assembly of gathered requisites,
build a circular house in a solitary place.
Each one, visualized as method or wisdom,
enjoying the requisites,
by means of vajra songs, dances and movements,
for six sets of thirty days,
or twelve, fourteen or sixteen,
one gains mastery over life and attains the undefiled.
Then, by sixteen hundred,
the five kāyas become spontaneously accomplished.
The coming together of all friendly people
is called the gathering of the yogic assembly.
The completeness of the enjoyments of wealth
is explained as the gathering of blissful enjoyments.
Because all gods and oath-bound ones assemble,
it is declared the gathering of the great assembly.
Because no conduct at all is grasped,
and because it continually completes the two accumulations,
it is definitively explained as the great gathering.
By completing the uninterrupted path,
the awareness-being cannot be overcome.
Though wearing the guise of gods and humans,
he gains mastery over the miracles of the five elements.
Through pure realms and enjoyments,
he joins in evenness, higher and ever higher.
The body of a vajra vidyādhara
abides in all realms.
If, through the force of clinging to signs,
the power of realization becomes weak,
then even if the mudrā of awakening, the vajra body,
the major and minor marks and so forth,
appear manifestly in that guise,
one does not see peace, suchness itself.
The consorts with whom one is joined,
on the common path and on both occasions,
by the greater or lesser distinctions of their capacity,
are explained according to the four activities.
A white one, short in stature and loud of voice,
her face and body fair, her topknot coiling to the right,
her limbs slender and soft, her voice clear,
heavy of breath: with her one performs pacifying.
One with many companions and limbs,
slender, lithe and tall,
her face and body yellow and squarely built,
with pendulous flesh and full breasts:
these should be taken at the time of increase.
One whose face is lotus-colored, broad of shoulder,
with long brows, her upper and lower body in proportion,
her body, fluids and breath fragrant,
endowed with bliss, her limbs not prominent:
she becomes supreme for drawing in.
A black one, triangular, with bristling hair,
with wrathful frowns and a childlike body,
fierce, harsh of speech and without restraint,
free of notions of clean and unclean, is explained as for killing.
One who has attained stable capability,
training in these from ground to ground,
is the holy awareness-being,
endowed with the transmission of the stages of yoga,
master of the unborn evenness,
who has brought everything to full clarity.
Because self-aware wisdom is the meaning of tantra,
one who has actualized self-appearing awareness
is the supreme vidyādhara, spontaneously accomplished.

Even if he lacks the might of samādhi's capability,
he is a sattva who trains through the grounds, with nothing to be produced;
sharing one domain with the Sugatas themselves,
he has obtained the transmission of the awareness-being.
If the body of elements perishes,
viewing himself, his three doors, as the deity,
he is freed from the body amassed from the elements
and ripens into the great mudrā of samādhi.
Like a garuḍa chick freed from its shell,
the whole supreme mudrā of illusion:
that is what is called "matured."
Defilements exhausted, a vajra body,
from then on free of the qualities of change and destruction;
having obtained the transmission of great bliss,
he is free of the qualities of defiled training.
An emanation body with mastery over realms,
the enjoyments of his samādhi immeasurable:
whoever's capability from practice is stable
becomes a vajra body, indestructible.
Free of the five destructions, defilements exhausted,
having gained power over the three masteries,
with immeasurable bliss and enjoyment,
he acts for the benefit of beings for countless eons,
and through seeing he is likewise of the nondual family.
The regent, the being who trains through the grounds:
the whole of the outer and inner, vessel and contents,
is immeasurable like space,
beyond the stream of thoughts of edge and center.
Free of fear, relieved, gone beyond sorrow,
seeing the faces of immeasurable self-appearance,
he is free of any object to look at elsewhere.
The might of familiarity with the awareness of samādhi
passes beyond the domain of wretched signs.
Mastering thousands of worlds,
of the family of the nondual great mudrā,
just as space has no limit,
through the prowess and power of meditation and capability,
into the thicket of habitual tendencies and signs
the fire of samādhi-awareness has broken out.
Whoever, in boundless, immeasurable realms,
has trained through samādhi-awareness,
and through meditation and samādhi-awareness
has obtained the prophecy of Vajradhara,
is the vidyādhara of the great mudrā.
Then, the prowess of awareness perfected,
through the play of the wisdom goddesses,
he is the consummate awareness-being;
gaining mastery over ten million realms,
the very son of Vajradhara,
having obtained all empowerments and miracles,
he pervades with his realization to the limits of space.
Through the distinctions of meditative equipoise and post-meditation,
he receives the prophecy of the Victors and abides by their command.
Though he moves through all the pure realms,
and though he acts within the wondrous secret nature,
through the nature of the view
he does not see directionless space,
and so reaches culmination through empowerment and realization.
Empowerment, samaya and discipline:
first, having brought the training to completion,
one who wishes to perfect the bindu
applies himself to the inner secret meaning.
Having completed the common activities,
the body together with its domains of experience,
and the whole of deity yoga,
one should view as the very bindu of great bliss.
By uniting deity and bliss in evenness,
appearance sinks into the bindu as bliss.
The four kāyas, the maṇḍala, the elements and the bindu,
by uniting them in evenness as the five pure essences,
through their essence and through conditions,
the stages of method are known from arousal.
Experiencing the bindu of bliss
is nonconceptual bliss without parts.
The indestructible bindu, the self of all,
bearing the pattern joined with emptiness,
free of all ordinary activity,
is taught as conduct itself.
Through the nature of the melting bindu,
the bliss of dripping, blazing, dissolving and filling,
the thirteen grounds too are adorned with bliss.
The bindu seeps through the whole body;
as a sesame seed is pervaded by its oil,
it is filled with an all-pervading taste.
Like light and its essence, without exception,
appearance and existence are the great bindu itself,
clear as pervaded by great bliss.
The bindu in which all is gathered
is the essence of the five elements mingled;
the indestructible bindu, the elixir of bliss,
sealing all with bliss,
the wisdom of space is clear in every way.
It is the realization of the Victors of the three times:
since a single bindu pervades all,
great bliss and compassion swirl together as one.
That is the mudrā of great bliss,
explained as the single inner mudrā.
Since that becomes truly workable,
together with the bindu of great bliss,
in all eighty particulars without exception,
bodies radiate in each separate bindu,
with the five elements and the taste of wisdom;
the seed bindus are adorned with bliss;
the nonconceptual bindu is the sound of supreme mind.
One's own body having ripened, they radiate as appearance.
From whatever to whatever they radiate,
so far extends the mudrā of the bindu;
the bindus of bliss are beyond number.
The five wisdoms and body, speech and mind,
being pervaded by one indivisible taste,
having completed countless accumulations of great bliss,
all appears as the mudrā of bliss.
Whatever is sealed by that
ripens into the bindu of great bliss.
Forms, outer and inner, with their dregs,
ripen into the essence of the bindu of great bliss.
Passing beyond the body of the dregs of the elements,
a body of essence without interruption,
since the bindu fills the lowest vessel,
all ripens into great bliss.
Then, endowed with method and samādhi
and with the power of guiding the winds,
one enters the bindu of the precious transmission.
Ripening in an instant into the five clairvoyances,
one becomes free of the classes of beings.
The bindu dissolves into the indestructible a;
one obtains the wind without exit or entry,
abides in a body without change or destruction,
in bliss itself without increase or decrease.
Appearance becomes the bindu itself;
the bindu dissolves into the short a:
nondual bliss free of parts.
This is undefiled, self-arisen,
great bliss itself, free of conditions,
like fire and its heat,
or the ocean and its moisture.

Over wind, bindu and awareness,
over life, wealth and the elements,
and over all five sense pleasures, one gains mastery.
The great mudrā of bliss
is the wholly undefiled bindu,
the boundless bindu of awareness itself.
When the defilements of the dregs and the winds are exhausted,
samādhi is clear as the bindu of great bliss.
Since awareness is clear as the taste of great bliss,
whatever is seen is the mudrā of great bliss itself.
Like domains of experience transformed through union,
or as darkness is transformed, or poison into elixir,
the boundless pure realms
become the great mudrā of great bliss.
For those persons who have meditated,
ordinary things and covetous attachments,
all the elaborations of forms,
without meditation and without deliberate effort,
are spontaneously accomplished as the bindu of great bliss.
Like water and its waves,
or like the perception of having reached the ocean,
through familiarity the clarity of great bliss reaches its culmination.
All conditions and harms,
even through touch, sight, hearing and recollection,
blaze in experience as the bindu of great bliss.
Remaining for many eons,
one accomplishes the mingling of the five elements
and all domains of experience into the bliss of one's own body.
If one trains in the secret path,
through the illustrative bliss of awareness
one realizes coemergent joy,
and the stages of the path are accomplished to the end.
The yogin whose faculties are in their prime and passionate,
devoted to great bliss,
abiding in e and vaṃ:
awakening arises from vajra and bell.
Through the coemergent substance,
having realized all as bliss,
through the rite of outer and inner union,
when one is endowed with the signs,
possessing the four secret mudrās,
one should play in supreme great bliss.
With the sun and moon of wisdom joined in evenness,
the bindu that cuts off hope and fear arises.
Without falling, abiding and dissolving,
from the meeting of the lord and the yearning one,
like a light free of day and night,
when sun and moon rise at the same time,
and all becomes pervaded by bliss,
the unmoving bindu dawns.
If signs of decline appear,
through method, experience and play,
one should train in the proper sequence.
The bindu of sun and moon mingled,
of method and wisdom nondual,
coemergence itself, free of substance,
is the mudrā of all the Victors.
When abiding in coemergence,
since all is the one taste of great bliss,
one abides as the single mudrā of great bliss.
The very bindu of vajra and lotus,
with channels numbering twice thirteen million,
by the dance of sun and moon mingled,
in each palace of bliss,
when it radiates to fill them with bindu,
body, speech and mind and the five wisdoms
are sealed with the taste of experience.
Having perfected the bliss of the body,
one should abide in the bliss of awareness,
which is beyond cause, condition and dependent origination.
If it declines, then by thinking,
by looking, listening and so forth,
one should meditate without interruption.
Since awareness ripens as bliss,
the three doors ripen into great bliss.
The domains of experience, together with winds and bindu:
by mastering the method of familiarization,
one masters the bindu and the winds,
together with support and supported,
and is able to bring under control
goddesses, human women, yakṣiṇīs and ḍākinīs.
One masters lowering, holding,
reversing and spreading the bindu.
If it is emitted into space,
one is parted from the path of accomplishment.
From then on, apart from offering and bestowing capability
on one's own proper occasions,
the bindu does not fall elsewhere,
and one should train in the path of mastery.
When the bliss of sun and moon mingled
dissolves into the undefiled essence,
it self-appears as the bliss of mind,
and the bliss of mind is uninterrupted.
When one is free of the force of what illuminates,
all is the mudrā of great bliss,
body and mind abiding in evenness,
beyond measure, bounds, expanse and taste.
The play of immeasurable bliss
is the great delight of all the buddhas:
the bindu, the seed, free of defilement,
the experience of great bliss unceasing.
Since one experiences oneself as the bindu,
one is never parted from the bindu of the essence.
Subtle, free of dust, without stain,
the vajra bindu is the bell itself,
self-appearing without effort,
abiding as an essence with neither parting nor attaining.
Where it flourishes richly with hosts of qualities,
arising from a stream like a flowing river of great bliss,
the two accumulations are complete and self and other spontaneously accomplished.
It is not elsewhere: it is only one's own appearance.
Because the nature abides, nothing comes from elsewhere.
By reversing clinging, I see my own face myself.
This is the eleventh chapter, explaining all the special features of the path, from the Great Tantra of the Secret Mirror of Vajrasattva's Net of Magical Display.

Then, what is further to be explained:
that which is to be accomplished by all,
the great qualities, as numerous as the atoms
of all the ten directions, I shall explain.
The nature of the five culminations,
though it abides as realization and as fruit,
seeing, familiarization and accomplishment
are rightly explained as common fruits.
As for the truly consummate fruit,
arisen from great method,
it is the precious wish-fulfilling jewel,
spontaneously complete with immeasurable qualities,
beyond measure and reference,
inconceivable and spontaneously accomplished.
Immeasurable like the limits of space,
the wisdom of omniscience
has become the treasure of all that is supreme,
a jewel without depletion or increase,
abiding equally in all.
The nature of all the buddhas,
beyond the measure of bounds and expanse:
whatever appears, pure or impure,
has no accepting or rejecting and no partiality at all.
In the self-arisen luminous maṇḍal
all is of a single nature;
through the measureless blessing of compassion
it fulfills all wishes.
Self-arisen wisdom, spontaneously present,
being the essence of all phenomena,
though it self-appears everywhere, without direction,
has no clinging and no deliberate intent.
The fruits of all the vehicles,
with their grounds, paths and so forth,
though each is a culmination relative to its own,
are not equal to the great and supreme spontaneous presence.
The immeasurable qualities of buddhahood,
spontaneously complete in a single instant:
the lord of the family, great Vajradhara,
through the play of the wisdom goddess,
manifestly attains the bliss of sun and moon
and establishes beings on the ground of Vajradhara.
Of all the immeasurable Victors,
because the beings to be tamed are limitless,
body, speech and mind are likewise limitless,
and their modes of presence appear immeasurably.
Spontaneously complete in great qualities,
the nature of the sixth buddha,
with marks reaching to the limits of space,
abides with the wisdom of great bliss expanding.
Like the moon made full by the sixteenth day,
wisdom itself, complete in all,
free of all the faults of the limited grounds,
abides as the very self of all qualities.
Throughout the limitless buddha realms,
to the very limits of space,
for as long as beings to be tamed, as numerous as atoms, remain,
it abides without meeting or parting for an instant.
The great bindu in which the essences gather
is the undefiled bindu of bliss,
joined in bliss with ā,
present in an all-pervading way.
The channels of the eighty particular bindus,
the taste of the secret bindu of gathered essences,
are transformed within the unborn evenness,
and by way of filling all,
the limitless realms of the ten directions,
being of one taste in the undefiled bindu,
are suchness itself, having the form of space.
The element-bindu in which the four kāyas gather,
possessing the essence in which the five pure essences gather,
is the unchanging essence in which the three gather.
The forty-two, filled with bliss,
opened by the taste of the bindu,
immeasurable body, speech, qualities and mind,
are an inconceivable self-appearance:
the mudrā of the bindu is inconceivable.
All the outer and inner essences and dregs
are the self-arisen bindu of pure essence itself,
a self-appearance with no other and no direction,
directionless great bliss itself.
The great secret bindu
is the essence of perfect qualities;
from the essence of great bliss
immeasurable qualities are magically displayed.
The bindu of great bliss,
free of gathering and radiating, is undefiled;
beyond partiality, there is nothing it does not pervade;
it is evenness without beginning or end.
It is the nature of all the Victors.
By resting evenly in the taste of the bindu,
all phenomena, to the limits of space,
are clearly experienced as great bliss.
The major and minor marks beyond measure
are the bindu complete in itself;
all the play of bliss, however much there is,
abides as complete in itself.
The bliss of all the buddhas
is the great self-arisen bindu;
from the melting of the vowels and consonants
arises bliss pervading space.
The secret bindu, the cause of all,
the bindu of space, the secret state,
great pervasion without part or direction,
abiding in the body, its nature unborn:
the body, speech and mind of the lord of bliss
are immeasurable like space.
The abode where the secret bindu
of method and wisdom inseparable resides,
together with all the Victors,
immeasurable, as many as sesame seeds and atoms,
the whole taste of great bliss,
through its coemergent nature,
conveyed in experience by awareness without being spoken,
is the abode of great enjoyment without grasping.
The taste of the self-arisen support of sun and moon,
gathered without being abandoned, has no meeting or parting.
The secret bindu in which the essences gather,
the taste of bliss filling space,
arises from the power of the nondual embrace.
The expanse of space, the mind of awakening in its richness,
the great bindu neither released nor emanated:
like the limits of vast space,
the measure of bliss too is immeasurable.
The number of the father-mother pairs abiding there
is equal to the limits of space, as many as the atoms of the realms,
and the maṇḍala of bliss of each one
is as numerous as atoms, equal to the limits of space.
Of father and mother joined nondually,
method and wisdom with their essences gathered,
the taste of the bindu, obscured by nothing,
beyond the signs of the three appearances,
the elixir of all the precious buddhas:
together with all of them,
numberless, ten millions as many as atoms,
through the distinctions of inclination and passion,
limitless guises appear:
some gaze with desire,
and likewise speak gently;
some embrace and gaze;
some use nail-marks and kiss;
some caress, strike and blow;
spreading apart, bowing and swaying proudly,
showing the teeth and showing the navel,
kissing, squeezing and laughing.
Through these manifold plays
those without passion are made passionate,
and passion is swiftly spent;
the parched is swiftly made moist,
and the harvest of great bliss is grown.
With gods and goddesses and their retinues,
numbering ten millions as many as atoms,
dancing and smiling together,
they play and sing this song:
How wonderful! Great bliss, the nature of all the buddhas,
coemergent, free of dust like space!
When the vajra is placed in the lotus and moved, imagined appearances set.
Though it is made joyful, pervading all like space, the faculties are stirred by the wind,
and the seeds of bliss, the outflows, rise up again.
The great bliss of union in which all movement is cut off
equals the expanse of space and endures for just as long.
Goddess, the five sense pleasures are complete in you;
relying on you, in joy I raise my eyes and behold all the realms of the Victors.
When ordinary beings, in pleasure, enter into attachment to the objects of the senses,
entering fully into joy, bliss, one-pointedness and lovely desire,
the two organs play, sun and moon meet, and great supreme bliss is born, vast;
yet though they taste birth, aging, sickness and death, hope and fear, shame and shamelessness, and the eight worldly concerns such as renown, the childish do not know it.

Whoever enters the secret meaning while lacking method and wisdom
will not see the desired fruit arise, as with seed sown in the ocean.
Śrāvakas, pratyekabuddhas and bodhisattvas fall short;
even a Victor, lacking method and wisdom, would find the sprout of awakening wholly withered.

When this was taken up in song,
the assembled retinues, as numerous as atoms,
each cast off their ornaments and garments;
the heroes took on the manner of alluring women,
and in maṇḍalas as numerous as the atoms of the ten directions,
with manifold songs and dances,
they cut off ordinary thoughts of the mind
and captivated the mind with great bliss.

Likewise inconceivable maṇḍalas,
realms, means, and wondrous bliss,
and the manifold methods of taming,
appear as anything at all, immeasurable and endless;
yet from the essence of the single sphere,
self-arisen wisdom, the mind of awakening,
through the manifold dance of enlightened deeds,
anything at all is emanated according to those to be tamed.

Since method is mastered through method,
the great method arising from method
is a marvel, a wonder, a great astonishment,
the nature of all the buddhas:
the great method, illusion, has wondrously arisen.

Illusion is the expanse without birth or cessation.
Body, speech, qualities, activity and mind,
all gathered without remainder, are utterly pure.
Everything is the self-aware mind of awakening,
the maṇḍala that encircles all,
the very source of the nature of all.
It performs deeds, yet nothing is done.
Without depletion and without increase,
it is the great treasure of accomplishments, beyond thought.
The sphere of wisdom, self-appearing,
is inconceivable, endless and supreme.
Wisdom without edge or center
cannot be described as "one" or as "this."
Like space, self-arisen and permanent,
it is without transition or change, and so it is.
Just as space has no edge or center,
self-arisen wisdom has no beginning or end.
The mind of awakening, unarisen and unborn,
is clear like the light of the sun of wisdom.
Buddhas, sentient beings, liberation and freedom, all of them,
are without remainder self-aware luminosity;
because self-arisen wisdom is self-appearing,
there is not the slightest knowing or obscuring.
Within the single wisdom that knows,
since it is free of any phenomena that are parts of itself,
it knows all phenomena, to the very limits of space,
in a single instant, without mixing them.
From the self-aware mind of awakening itself,
no high or low, nothing to adopt or reject, is found;
so although it appears of itself as the essence of saṃsāra,
it is unattached and faultless, like a lotus.
The essences of immeasurable qualities,
being self-arisen awareness appearing of itself,
are all the oceans of wonders without remainder:
they appear of themselves, with nothing to be gained from another.
Blessings, miracles and wondrous qualities
are emanated from the self-arisen itself.
Their modes are manifold,
differing as pure and impure;
though they never stir from the one essence,
they are seen according to each one's inclination.

Speech pervades space;
its sound resounds pervading the ten directions;
according to inclinations, in manifold ways,
inconceivable and manifold methods of taming appear.
Mind is the mind that knows all without remainder;
loving, bringing benefit and happiness,
it emanates the higher knowledges, the wisdom that knows;
in the wisdom mind there are no distinctions.
Qualities, free of measure and distinction,
hold the treasure of the jewel of space;
to fulfil immeasurable wishes,
their blessing is like a wish-fulfilling jewel.
Activity is blessing appearing of itself;
in the manner of the meaning without coming or going,
like the wish-granting vase and the precious jewel,
it fulfils all aims without agent or act.

All elaboration, without remainder, is utterly pure;
self-arisen awareness without edge or center
is the source of everything without remainder:
great bliss, the Dharma body.
All the enjoyments of desire
are all complete in the state of nonduality;
having gained boundless mastery of that very state,
it is the body of perfect enjoyment.
From the essence of the single body, the sphere,
throughout all the world realms,
in whatever way suits those to be tamed,
it displays magically: the emanation body.
Through realizing the meaning of the unarisen and unborn,
all without remainder is indivisible, vajra itself,
fearless, free of all antidotes:
this is taught as the vajra body.
That in which manifest awakening takes place,
luminosity, space without edge or center,
untouched by the limits of the four bodies,
is the primordial manifest awakening.

Having abandoned the extremes of all divisions,
it abides thus, equal with all;
by its meaning it comes to pervade all without remainder:
this is the speech of inexpressible meaning.
Through bodies and mudrās and ways of gazing,
through the intent of meditative absorption, for those to be tamed,
what is shown in an inexpressible way
is taught as the speech of symbols.
When anything whatever is seen or heard,
just as a hero's gaze dispels a battle,
awakening is realized as space through the thing itself:
this is called the speech of blessing and realization.
Whatever appears for whomever's sake,
where antidotes and obstacles do not remain,
and which others cannot defeat,
is declared the overpowering speech.
By uttering a single word, endless words arise;
finding what each inclines to, they are liberated,
endowed with an ocean of qualities:
this is the speech of spoken words.

However much it is elaborated in many forms,
in the expanse of phenomena there is no distinction;
even in conceiving, the concepts and signs
are nonconceptual wisdom: the liberated mind.
Through nondual great bliss
it plays among the five enjoyments of desire;
when all without remainder appears as bliss,
this is the mind of great bliss.
Though it has no self or other, outer or inner,
being self-arisen and free of the two sides,
it knows all the intentions of those to be tamed:
the mind that frees all beings without remainder.
However many objects of awareness appear,
in the essence of all without remainder,
arising, destruction and antidotes are empty from the beginning:
this is called the unchanging vajra mind.

The immeasurable realms of space,
the seats that show the distinctions of greatness,
the undefiled ornaments,
the palaces blazing with wisdom,
and the masses of light rays, pervading without edge or center,
all appear.
Further, the great qualities,
like space without edge or center,
cannot be measured or weighed;
the undefiled qualities of greatness appear.
Further, the enlightened activities performed,
as many as the domains of those to be tamed,
through inconceivable gateways of deeds
appear without limit.
To explain them in summary,
they are gathered into the four that are endless,
pacifying, enriching, magnetizing, wrathful and the rest,
and all are accomplished spontaneously.
In short, the categories of the result
are summed up as twenty-five.

This concludes the twelfth chapter, explaining spontaneous accomplishment without exception, from the great tantra Vajrasattva's Net of Magical Display (Māyājāla), the Mirror of All Secrets.

Then the Lord of Secrets, Vajradhara,
empowered by immeasurable Victors,
the Lord of Secrets, treasury of mantra,
the treasure-holding king of great strength,
joyful and delighted, brandished
the very vajra in his own hand,
and, together with koṭis as numerous as atoms,
whirled it all around.
The worlds were shaken in six ways:
he made them quake and tremble,
quake all over and tremble utterly.
The worlds of the great trichiliocosm with their encircling ranges
he filled with light and sound,
and wondrous miracles of marvelous body, speech and mind
were displayed.
With the wondrous, marvelous miracles
of outer, inner and secret offerings
and the five qualities of desire he pervaded all,
and pleased the whole host of Victors.
All, joyful and devoted,
the assembled retinues, equal in number to the atoms of the realms,
took up this praise in song
with manifold sounds:

oṃ āḥ hūṃ a la la ho
The mind of the Net of Magical Display is the unborn mind of wisdom and bliss;
the nonconceptual, clear mind abides pervading all;
it is wholly indivisible, neither conditioned nor unconditioned, without arising or perishing;
in the sublime mind there is no arising or sensing, no stirring or ceasing at all, and so it is bliss, ho!
The body of the Net of Magical Display is the body of bliss, blazing with manifold light;
however it appears, manifold and endless, it is void of essence;
through the means by which that very nature gathers, it is thus the captivating, satisfying body;
however clearly the endless manifold distinctions appear, in the state of space there is no distinction, ho!
The qualities of the Net of Magical Display are a precious wish-fulfilling jewel;
endowed inexhaustibly, the wheel of ornaments appears immeasurably vast;
it captivates beings, so beautiful that they never tire of beholding it;
it appears with the qualities of all the Victors complete, and these have no end, ho!
The speech of the Net of Magical Display is profound and vast, like space;
understood by all and satisfying, it sends forth the vajra sound, proclaiming melodiously;
the great vajra sound, endless, appears accordingly as each is inclined and as befits them, ho!
The activity of the Net of Magical Display spontaneously accomplishes manifold deeds;
for aeons as many as the atoms of the realms it tames all beings without remainder;
this manifold miraculous display of method and wisdom, never joined and never parted, is at play, ho!

Then the supreme Victor, the Great Being,
entrusted this secret
to Vajradhara and the others,
together with koṭis as numerous as atoms:
"This secret essence of mine
is the nature of all the teachings
of all the buddhas of the three times,
and appears as the method of all without remainder.
It is the Secret Mantra, the Vajra Vehicle,
the glorious tantra of the mind of magical display,
in which all phenomena without remainder appear,
the Secret Mirror, the tantra of method."
All the Victors, as numerous as atoms, assembled
and praised this sublime teaching.
This great tantra of the Net of Magical Display,
the definitive meaning of the profound scripture,
is like a mirror for all;
yet persons of severed or inferior lineage,
though they look into it, see no reflection appear.
They turn their backs on the two teachers,
cling to outer doctrines they have devised,
hold the master in contempt,
bear great hatred toward their vajra siblings,
deny the profound meaning,
scorn the meaning of the secret tantras,
are miserly and greatly attached to their own side,
and possess neither faith nor sacred commitment.
To persons who will not give up kingdom and jewels,
holding the dualistic view of outer and inner,
even if, for aeons as numerous as the atoms of the realms,
one's body and mind were to be torn apart,
one must not show the secret volume;
for them there is never empowerment or accomplishment.
If, out of delusion, one should give it to them,
one falls into the Hell of Unceasing Torment oneself.
One who has faith and sacred commitment,
who gives up all abundance,
who comes with the sublime requisites,
and who has no regard even for body and life,
never meets with meager enjoyments.
Devoted to the Great Vehicle,
respectful, with discipline and loving compassion,
serving and honoring the vajra master,
patiently bearing faults and rebukes,
undisturbed in mind though keeping company for an aeon,
holding no notion of wonder or weariness
toward anything at all, whether bad or good,
of good nature, knowing proper conduct,
in whose company the disease of poison does not arise:
to such a sublime one give the secret.
Those without the master's oral instructions,
unaccustomed to the secret meaning,
using as their valid cognition their own mistaken reasons,
try to settle the hidden secret meaning.
Fools cut off by darkness,
following their wrong attachments,
ripen into hell in this very life.
This great secret essence,
the mudrā of all the tathāgatas,
is the secret of all without remainder;
so whoever, having realized it, makes it clear
has empowerment and maṇḍala complete,
and accomplishes the aims of self and others spontaneously.
This great secret is the fruit
of having served and honored as many realms as there are atoms
and of having relied on the master of the gathering.
Therefore it is the domain of the Victor's heirs alone;
those mighty in fame, who live by gain and honor,
are utterly rejected.
Aho! It is set in the secret vajra words.

This concludes the thirteenth chapter, on the consummation, from the tantra of Vajrasattva's Net of Magical Display, the Secret Mirror.

The great tantra Vajrasattva's Net of Magical Display, the Secret Mirror of Vajrasattva, is complete.

It was translated, edited and finalized by the Indian preceptor Vimalamitra and the monk Jñānakumāra. Later the nun Zemo requested it again and again from Vimalamitra himself. If this is spread to others, the sacred commitment is broken.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

"The two, Mind and Excellent Dharma" (ཡིད་དང་ཆོས་བཟང) most likely refers to the central couple Samantabhadra and Samantabhadrī, as mind and phenomena; the names are rendered by meaning. "The four times" refers to the four offering goddesses who represent past, present, future and indeterminate time.  The questions on "maturation, mastery, mudrā" and "the spontaneously present" refer to the four levels of vidyādhara (of maturation, of mastery over life, of mahāmudrā, and of spontaneous presence). In the colophon རྫོགས་སྷོ is read as རྫོགས་སོ ("completes"). The phrase འཛུམ་ཞིང་སྟེག་པས་སོས་འདེབས is obscure; it is rendered loosely as "smiling, and beaming his approval." In the explanation of the title words, སྩལ is read as རྩལ ("display, dynamic expression"), and དྲང་བའི་དོན ("provisional meaning") is read as དྲ་བའི་དོན ("the meaning of net"), since the verses gloss the words of the title in turn (vajra, sattva, magical display, net). Similarly ཡོངས་སུ་རྫོགས་པའི་རྒྱུ ("cause of perfect completeness") may be a slip for རྒྱུད ("tantra, continuum"), which would fit the gloss "not cut off"; it is translated as written.  གཙང་མ་རིས་དག་སྤངས་པ (once written རིགས་དག) is read as "transcending the Pure Abodes" (Śuddhāvāsa), Akaniṣṭha here being placed beyond them. In "sixteen porticoes of cause and result" (རྟ་བབས), the sense is that the eight paths, as cause and result, are symbolized by the gateways. དུས་ཀྱི་ཐ་བྲལ is read as དུས་ཀྱི་མཐའ་བྲལ, "beyond the limits of time." The name སྣང་མཛད is taken as a short form of Vairocana (རྣམ་པར་སྣང་མཛད), as the gloss "appear distinctly" (རྣམ་པར་སྣང) shows.  The opening རྒྱ་བ is read as རྒྱལ་བ, "victors." The phrase སྦྱོར་དང་འཇུ་འདུལ་རླུང་གི་རིམ, describing yogic practices of the winds, is obscure; "absorption and control" is a tentative rendering of འཇུ་འདུལ. རྡོ་རྗེ་དྲི is taken as the incense goddess Vajra Dhūpā, since the four outer offering goddesses (incense, flower, lamp, scented water) follow in sequence and Gandhā is named separately as དྲི་ཆབ་མ. The verses gloss each deity's name by its function, which the translation keeps by adding the English sense after the Sanskrit name.  The opening section describes the secret Akaniṣṭha and its teacher and retinue within the yogin's body (the indestructible drop, winds, mind and mentation). The Sanskrit-letter syllables (e, artha, mana mata, mani manu mata, vaṃ) are code words of this esoteric reading and are left untranslated. The six "teachers" listed are the emanations for the six classes of beings; the one for the hells is described as "the king of Dharma," possibly an allusion to Yama as Dharmarāja. གདོང་བར is taken as the facades or projecting fronts of the palace.  ས་ཡ་བཅུ་གསུམ is read as "the thirteen grounds" (as ས་ཡི་བཅུ་གསུམ, matching the earlier "lord of the thirteen grounds"); read literally it would be "thirteen million." The term śa da pa, describing the songs and dances with which wisdom answers, is obscure and is left as written. Much of this chapter describes the esoteric yoga of union with a consort and of channels, winds and drops in deliberately terse terms; renderings of the sequence "summoning, bringing down, holding, drawing up, reversing" follow the literal Tibetan.  Here མཐའ་ཡས in "the countless victors and vidyādharas" means "endless, infinite" and is not the name Amitābha. The last part of the passage seems to run through the views of the vehicles in order: worldly religious practice, the nihilists, the eternalists, the śrāvakas, the pratyekabuddhas, Mind Only, Madhyamaka, and then Kriyā, Ubhaya (Caryā) and Yoga tantra. The brief lines make it hard to say exactly where one view ends and the next begins.  In this passage མཐའ་ཡས means "infinite" every time and is not the name Amitābha. The phrase translated "by the force of reality" (དངོས་དབང) is read like the logicians' "force of fact" (dngos stobs). The line on the channels (ལྔ་བདག་བརྒྱད་ཅུ་རྩ་གཉིས་རྒྱུ། ས་ཡ་བཅུ་གསུམ་རྩ་ཡན་ལག) and "known from their own account" (ལོ་རྒྱུས) are terse, and the renderings are tentative. "The single cause, the way of the syllables, blessing and direct perception" are the four realizations (rtogs pa bzhi) of Mahāyoga.  Here གླུ, གར and བྱམས are the ordinary words "song", "dance/performance" and "love", not the goddess names used elsewhere. In "for the peaceful, Brahmā" ཚངས may mean "pure" rather than Brahmā. The lines about the "one, five and three", the "four" called the wheel, and the "eleven and nine extra ones" are terse technical terms of the generation stage, and the renderings are literal.  Here "the expanse of space" (ནམ་མཁའི་དབྱིངས) is the ordinary phrase, not the name of the consort Ākāśadhātvīśvarī, and མི་གཡོ་བ is "unmoving", not Acala. ཨུ་ཐའི་རླུང is given as "the utha wind", following the Tibetan spelling. It may stand for the udāna wind. Much of the channel terminology here is terse and technical, and some renderings are literal.  Here "dancers" (གར་མཁན་མ) belongs to a list of women of various castes and is not the goddess Nṛtyā. "Flower" (མེ་ཏོག) and "the essence of space" (ནམ་མཁའི་སྙིང་པོ) are ordinary words here, not the names Puṣpā and Ākāśagarbha. The numbers "twelve, fourteen, sixteen, eighteen", "twenty" and "twenty-five" are given as bare numbers, as in the Tibetan. "The offering to the male" (སྐྱེས་མཆོད) is tentative. The doubled ལེ in ཐིག་ལེ་ལེ་ལས is read as ཐིག་ལེ་ལས.  The opening list of maṇḍalas is terse: it names the natural, samādhi and reflected-image maṇḍalas, then "the further two, samādhi and reflected image", then those of the awakening mind and the assembly; it is rendered as it stands. In "all-pervading through the five wisdoms" the text reads ཀུན་ཁྱད་ཅན ("distinguished in all"); this is taken as a slip for ཀུན་ཁྱབ་ཅན ("all-pervading"), which the parallel line just above uses. The sentence beginning "Because its nature, too" is elliptical in the Tibetan and is kept so. The passage ends mid-sentence; the list of haughty gods continues in the next piece.  The heruka forms (Great Glorious, Vajra, Ratna, Padma, and the Karma family "with the various") follow the five-family scheme; ཁྲག་འཐུང ("blood-drinker") is rendered Heruka. In the list of wrathful modes, སྡང་ཁྲ is taken as a slip for སྡང་ཁྲོ ("wrath of hatred"), matching the neighbouring terms. "Not left under any power of their own" renders the terse གང་གི་དབང་དུ་མ་བཏང་བར. The "seats of the fearless" are presumably the lion and other animal thrones. The architectural terms (ཕ་གུ, དྲ་ཕྱེད, ཟ་ར་ཚགས) are rendered by their usual maṇḍala-palace senses. In this passage, གཟུངས་མ means "consort", not dhāraṇī.  The four directional buddhas are named by their wisdoms (Dharmadhātu, Equality, Discernment, Accomplishment of Activity), as the Tibetan does. The emblems of the six sages (པི་ཝང་གོ་ཆ་གསེག་པར་སྒྲོམ་པོ་ཏི་མེ་ཆུ) are terse; གསེག་པར is rendered "bowl" tentatively. "Akṣobhya and the other agents are adorned with Vajradhara" refers to the crown ornaments of the families; the line with Yamāntakṛt is elliptical. In "heat is fire", དྲོད་མེད is read as དྲོད་མེ ("heat, fire"), which the element list requires. "The emptiness vajra" refers to the emptiness mantra and its accompanying meditation.  After the long run of mantra syllables, one Tibetan verse is set off by the syllable oṃ ("The nondual Net of Magical Display ... may it be inseparable right now!"). That verse is translated, and the oṃ is kept at the end of the preceding marked run. Elsewhere in the passage the Sanskrit word citta stands untranslated in the Tibetan, where it means the heart or mind as the seat of the maṇḍala, and it is kept as written.  The attendant goddesses (Lāsyā, Mālā, Gītā, Nṛtyā) are evoked here only by their functions, grace, garland, song and dance, and are translated as such. ta kri ta (ཏ་ཀྲིཏ) stands untranslated in the Tibetan, apparently a truncated "-antakṛt", the name element of the four gatekeepers. The lists of emblems are terse, and their attribution to particular deities follows the maṇḍala described in the previous chapter. In the list of the eight goddesses, the last item, མ་ཚོགས་མ, is rendered literally as "the Mother Host". Śmaśānī would be expected in that position, but this cannot be confirmed. The bowl in the sages' emblems renders གསེག་པར tentatively, as before.  The line on the four animal-headed gatekeepers lists horse, sow, snake and monkey faces as the Tibetan gives them. The line rendered "the gate of jhaṃ, the ground gate of movement" is obscure in its syntax and the rendering is tentative. In "Bliss is seated, victorious, upon sun and moon," rgyal may instead be an abbreviation for a lion throne; the plain reading is kept.  The second line reads literally sa ya bcu gsum, which could be taken as "thirteen million"; in view of the preceding passage's "thirteen grounds" (sa yang bcu gsum), it is read here as the thirteen grounds. The short verse after oṃ in the mantra sequence is in Tibetan and is translated.  Several emblem lines in the closing list are terse and their exact distribution among the goddesses is uncertain. "Great club" renders sti ra chen po, whose meaning is not certain. "Hold out the lotus and blood" reads stobs ("power") as stob ("offer, hold out"), which the context requires. "Hidden heart" renders gab tse snying po, where gab tse may refer to a casket or diagram. As in the preceding piece, sa ya bcu gsum is read as the thirteen grounds.  Several goddess names are rendered by their Sanskrit equivalents as identified in the list of twenty-eight sovereign goddesses (for example Lobhā, Indrāṇī, Saumī, Vasurakṣā, Agnāyī, Varuṇī); sna tshogs ma is translated literally as "the Manifold One" since its Sanskrit form is not certain. Some emblems are uncertain: "drinks sandalwood" (tsandan 'thung), "draws out entrails" (ba su 'dren), "winged staff" (dbyug gshog), "cudgel" (be raṃ) and "club" (sti ra). The songs of the śadapa are left untranslated; the word may stand for ṣaṭpada, the humming bee. In the mantra the ninth syllable of the kro series is written kra and is kept so.  The line rendered "phenomena imputed without true aspect" reads rnam par ma med brtags pa'i chos, whose wording is unusual; the rendering is tentative.  The line rendered "trembling, swimming particles" reads literally "trembling and dust (rdul)"; the sense of rdul among these signs of experience is uncertain. The syllables "a ta" on which the stages of the bindu rest are unclear and have been left as written. "The three hundred and sixty and the sixty-four" likely refers to the channels or practices of the completion stage and the sixty-four arts of union; the text does not specify. "Gazing out from the depths of the ocean" renders mjings, whose precise sense here is uncertain.  "The eight great supreme ones" (mchog chen brgyad) are not named here; in Mahāyoga they are a set of eight great worldly deities, and the line may mean the skulls of Brahmā, Indra and others form these eight. "The five sources" (byung khungs lnga) is rendered literally; it likely means five colored substances from natural sources. "Garlands of sense organs" renders dbang po, which could also be read as Indra.  ལྷ་ཆེན here is the Great God (Mahādeva, Śiva), on whose upturned skull Heruka's maṇḍala rests, not the goddess named earlier. "The supreme fields, the ones to be liberated" refers to the classic objects of wrathful "liberation" (zhing bcu, the ten fields). "Great conches, great cloths" (dung chen ras chen) are listed without further explanation.  "The jewel grid of the buddhas" renders sangs rgyas rin chen gab tse; gab tse ordinarily means a divination diagram or grid, and its exact sense here is uncertain. The "five elements, the cause, the self, and space ... cast out each in turn" in the rite for the accumulation of merit is rendered literally; the list is terse.  "Endowed with the thirteen millions and the two" renders sa ya bcu gsum gnyis ldan pa literally; its reference in this description of the secret empowerment is unclear. The five minds, five dances, and similar sets are not enumerated in this passage.  The text plays on the two halves of the word maṇḍala in Tibetan, "center" and "circle" or "retinue"; the verses identify what is the center and what is the surrounding circle. In "the samādhi of the deity is the center," the text reads lha ya, evidently a slip for lha yi, "of the deity." The phrase rendered "the lord of the ocean of wisdom" uses the word earlier rendered Īśvara, but here it is an epithet of the presiding buddha, not the god Śiva. In "relying on the dhāraṇī," the term may here denote a consort (as in gzungs ma), in keeping with the parallel stanzas. "Lord of the frontiers" for the regent's epithet (mtha' bdag) is uncertain. In the earlier lines, "mālā," "flowers," "scent" and "unmoving" occur as ordinary words, not as the goddess or deity names listed earlier.  This section describes the sexual yogic practice of the third empowerment with an action mudrā (karmamudrā), in the technical language of channels, winds and bindu. The ages given for the consort are rendered exactly as the text states them. "The thirteenth, endowed with both" is obscure; it may refer to the thirteenth ground reached through the union of the two (means and wisdom). "First, having obtained the root empowerment" reads dang la, evidently a slip for dang po, "first." The "vajra sun" and "longing lord" are cryptic terms of the union practice and are given literally. "Lamp," "garland" and "dance" occur here as ordinary words, not as the offering goddesses named elsewhere.  "Even the great gods endowed with miraculous powers" renders lha chen, here a generic term for mighty gods rather than the goddess named elsewhere in this text. "One who holds the vajra" (rdo rje can) may also be read as Vajrin, an epithet of Indra. "Keeps the three doors in evenness" reads mnyams par, taken as mnyam par.  This section describes the inner and secret samayas in the language of the completion stage and sexual yoga: the bindu, winds and channels, and the five nectars. "Bell" here renders dril bu as a ritual implement, not the goddess Ghaṇṭā. "He will be freed elsewhere never at all" renders a terse line (thar te gzhan du nam yang min) whose sense is that there is no release elsewhere before space is destroyed or the ocean dries up.  "Pride as supreme bliss" renders bde mchog, here describing the transformed affliction of pride rather than the deity Cakrasaṃvara. "Transforming all the manifold" uses sna tshogs as an ordinary word, not the deity named the Manifold One elsewhere. "The great one that scatters" is evidently the wind element, completing the list of elements. Rtsangs pa in the list of births is taken as a variant spelling of tshangs pa, Brahmā.  "Recitation to the measure of ten million and one hundred thousand" renders the terse bye ba tshad 'bum gcig; the intended figure is unclear. In the signs of impairment, the line listing guru, companions, parents and others ending in "hostile without fault" is read as these people turning against the practitioner without cause; it could also mean the practitioner hating them. The line "From seeing onward, on the path of meditation the family is not fixed" (mthong nas bsgom lam ma nges rigs) is obscure. The lines on ordinariness, laziness, doubt and faith are elliptical; they are read as faith overcoming those three, with faith and the other qualities as what one must possess. "Dhāraṇī holders" renders gzungs in a list of beings; the precise class intended is uncertain. "The eight adverse ones" (mi mthun brgyad) is not identified with certainty. The passage on the signs of being "unimpaired" calling for restoration follows the Tibetan as written.  "Reviving of its vigor" renders the unclear nyams sod; it may be a corruption for "the fourth" (bzhi pa), completing the numbered series of signs. "Dahūmata" (Tibetan 'da hū ma ta) is an unidentified anointing substance, given as spelled. "Aparacitta" follows the Tibetan spelling a par tsitta; it may stand for Aparājita, but this is not certain. In the rite of separation, "the hour fearsome by daylight" renders the obscure dang rung, and "wrap it up" renders the unclear blan. "Enclose it in a box with oneself and a dog" is a literal rendering of a difficult line. "Twelve finger-widths" supplies the unit implied by "twelve" for the sword. "The fat of the seventh" is literal; it may refer to the fat of a seven-times-born person, or to a seventh day.  "Vajra pra divination" renders rdo rje pra, a pra rite (prasena, mirror divination). The passage on the stages of bindu, wind, and channels is highly condensed completion-stage instruction, and several lines (for example "the space liṅga," "the three bindus," "the eighty particulars," "the eighty-two particulars," and "meeting of the tips and reversing the two") are rendered literally without the commentarial explanation they presuppose. "The various consonants" renders gsal byed sna tshogs, read as the letters from which the following syllables arise. "Tārā" here may refer to the consort.  "Unites with" renders the unclear spongs mdzad. In the fire-rite substances, "elephant flesh and blood" reads glang chen for the written klung chen ("great river"), which makes no sense here. "The goat-born one" (ra skyes) is uncertain; it may refer to the fire god, whose mount is a goat. The four fire deities are given as Pāvaka (written papaka in the Tibetan), and, translated from their Tibetan names since their Sanskrit forms are not certain, the Mighty One, the Splendid One, and the Destroyer. "Gundhe flask" keeps the Tibetan's transcribed Sanskrit; it is probably a water vessel. The objects planted with knotted heads and differently shaped points are the four kīlas mentioned below.  "Sweating" reads rngul for the written rdul ("dust"), which makes no sense among the signs of bliss. "The upper door" (steng sgo) contrasts with the lower door of the secret practices; the passage is terse. The lines on short life, illness and poverty appear to introduce the enriching activity in its inner sense without an explicit statement.  The ritual sequence here follows the four activities (pacifying, enriching, magnetizing, wrathful). "Receives what is desired" renders an elliptical line, probably meaning that the disciple receives the desired instructions. The line on those who "have not realized it" though empowered and trained is compressed in the Tibetan and its construal is somewhat uncertain. Rāgavajra and Dveṣavajra here name the vajra samādhis of desire and aversion, kept consistent with earlier usage.  The descriptions of the four types of consort contain several rare or obscure physiognomic terms, and some lines are uncertain: "heavy of breath" for rlung khar lci, "many companions and limbs," "lithe and tall," "broad of shoulder" for sog ka, and "limbs not prominent" for yan lag med. Here dkar mo is a type of consort, "a white one," not the goddess Gaurī; likewise the black one. The numbers of days are given literally (six sets of thirty days, or twelve, fourteen or sixteen); the text does not specify the unit of the last three. Khra mo is read as khro mo, female wrathful ones.  "The bindu of the precious transmission" renders rin chen lung gi thig le; lung may be an error for rlung, "precious wind," which would fit the context of guiding the winds. The opening lines on the bodhisattva "with nothing to be produced" are compressed and their construal is uncertain. "Space" (mkha') in the line on emission is likely also a veiled term for the consort's lotus.  Several terms in the description of amorous play are rare and their sense uncertain, notably dkar 'dzin ("showing the teeth"), and tha skam ... bengs ("the parched is made moist"). The twenty-five categories of the result are evidently the five bodies (Dharma body, enjoyment body, emanation body, vajra body, and primordial manifest awakening), five kinds of speech, five kinds of mind, five qualities (realms, seats, ornaments, palaces, light rays) and five activities, as set out in the verses. Two couplets in the passage on unworthy and worthy recipients are terse: "for them there is never empowerment or accomplishment" and "never meets with meager enjoyments" render the Tibetan "never empowerment and accomplishment" and "never inferior enjoyments" literally, and their exact force is open to interpretation.
