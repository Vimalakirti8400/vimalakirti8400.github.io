The Means of Accomplishing Kurukullā (Kurukullesādhana)

The Means of Accomplishing Kurukullā, known in Sanskrit as the Kurukullā Sādhana and in Tibetan as Kurukullé Drubpé Tab, is a short tantric ritual manual devoted to Kurukullā, the red goddess of magnetizing and subjugation who is regarded as a form of Tārā. Opening with homage to the noble goddess Tārā, it guides the practitioner through dispelling obstructors, taking refuge, and visualizing Kurukullā as a red, three-faced and six-armed deity holding a bow and arrow, a hook, a noose and a red utpala, surrounded by a maṇḍala of eight Tārās and four fierce gate goddesses. It then gives the mantras for invitation, offerings, the torma and the deity's departure, and closes with a brief rite for bringing others under one's power, followed by a colophon naming its Indian paṇḍita and Tibetan translator, Shönnu Ö. The work is Toh 1318 in the Tengyur, where it belongs to the tantra commentaries section among the many sādhanas, or practice texts, for meditation deities.

In the language of India: kurukulle sādhanaṃ. In the language of Tibet: The Means of Accomplishing Kurukullā.

oṃ Homage to the Blessed Lady, the noble goddess Tārā.

First of all, seated in the cross-legged posture of a hero (sattvaparyaṅka), one should dispel the obstructors. This is the mantra: oṃ amṛti hūṃ phaṭ svāhā.

Then, visualizing the buddhas and bodhisattvas in the midst of the sky, one should immediately go for refuge: oṃ ratnatrayaṃ sma śaranaṃ kritva. oṃ svabhāva śuddhaḥ sarva dharmāḥ svabhāva śudho'haṃ. oṃ śūnyatā jñāna vajra svabhāva ātmako'haṃ.

Then, from the syllable paṃ, a lotus; upon it, from the syllable a, a moon disk; on that, the syllable ṭaṃ, which transforms completely into an utpala. After that one should invite her: oṃ bhagavati samayaśvare sarvottamati tajaḥ adhiṣṭhanaṃ mi kuru svāhā.

From that arises the Blessed Lady, with three faces and six arms. Her right and left faces, and her root face, are black, yellow, and red. Of her right hands, the first is in the gesture of granting fearlessness, marked with a white wheel; the second holds an arrow; the third a hook. Of her left hands, the first holds a red utpala with a cluster of jewels; the second a bow; the third a noose. Her body is red in color, and she sits on a seat of a red lotus. She is crowned with the five Tathāgatas, and her body is adorned with every ornament.

In the east is Prasanna Tārā; in the south, Niṣpanna Tārā; in the west, Dīpa Tārā; in the north, Karṇa Tārā; in the southeast, the corner of Agni, Ratna Tārā; in the southwest, the corner of Nairṛti, Jaya Tārā; in the northwest, the corner of Vāyu, Gaurī Tārā; and in the northeast, the corner of Īśāna, Vīra Tārā. These are four-armed, holding the gesture of supreme giving, an utpala, an arrow, and a bow. Their bodies are red in color, and they sit on seats of red lotuses. They are crowned with the five Tathāgatas, and their bodies are adorned with every ornament.

Their mantras are: oṃ kurukulle hūṃ hrīṃ svāhā. oṃ prasanna tāri hūṃ hrīṃ svāhā. oṃ nesanna tāri hūṃ hrīṃ svāhā. oṃ dīpa tāri hūṃ hrīṃ svāhā. oṃ karṇa tāri hūṃ hrīṃ svāhā. oṃ maṇi tāri hūṃ hrīṃ svāhā. oṃ jaya tāri hūṃ hrīṃ svāhā. oṃ gaurī tāri hūṃ hrīṃ svāhā. oṃ vīra tāri hūṃ hrīṃ svāhā.

At the eastern gate is Vajravetālī, her fangs bared, her face contorted, of terrifying appearance. She is four-armed, holding an arrow, a bow, a vajra hook, a noose, and a lute (vīṇā), and she is crowned with Akṣobhya.

At the southern gate is Vajragandhārī, her face snarling, her hair coiled, her belly hanging. She is four-armed, holding a vajra, a bell, a hook, and a noose, and she is crowned with Ratnasambhava.

At the western gate is Aparājitā, her hair bristling upward, her belly hanging. She is four-armed, holding the threatening gesture, a hook, and a noose, and she has Amitābha as her head ornament.

At the northern gate is Ekajaṭī, her hair bristling upward, her belly hanging. She is four-armed, holding a hammer, a vajra, an axe, and a noose, and she has Amoghasiddhi as her head ornament. She stands with the right leg extended and the left drawn in.

The four gate goddesses are red in color and sit on lotus seats. Their mantras are: oṃ vajra vatili hūṃ phaṭ. oṃ vajra ghandhari hūṃ phaṭ. oṃ vajra aparījita hūṃ phaṭ. oṃ vajra ekajaṭā hūṃ phaṭ.

oṃ āryāvalokiteśvarāya bodhisatvāya mahāsatvāya mahākāruṇikāya tadyathā oṃ āgaccha āgaccha bhagavati tāri āryāvalikiteśvara satyena svāhā. Having recited this seven times over guggulu incense, one should invite her.

namaḥ āryāvalokiteśvarāya tadyathā oṃ sori soravate hera hera svāhā. This is the mantra of the welcoming water (arghya).

namaḥ āryāvalokiteśvarāya tadyathā oṃ tari taribati sarva caturtha sādhane taṣṭha tiṣṭha yad karyāṃ siddhi me kuru svāhā. This is the mantra of the flowers.

namaḥ āryāvalokiteśvarāya tadyathā megha megha svāri doga svā kuru kuru bhara bhara svāhā. This is the mantra of the incense.

namaḥ āryāpalikiteśvarāya tadyathā oṃ bokka bokka bhamane jvāla jāla dīpa svāhā. This is the mantra of the lamp.

namaḥ āryāvalokiteśvarāya tadyathā oṃ hara hara horu horu gandha vasani svāhā. This is the mantra of the perfume.

namaḥ āryāvalokiteśvarāya tadyathā oṃ svabhāva diha diha kuru kuru deśaṃ baliṃ hūṃ phaṭ svāhā. This is the mantra of the torma.

namaḥ āryāvalokiteśvarāya tadyathā here here hara hara sarva satva kāruṇi śīghraṃ āgaccha devatā bhagavān āryāvaloketeśvara samayamanusmarana svāhā. Joining the palms with flowers, one should settle her into the torma.

The mantra for requesting her to depart is: oṃ prasanna nespan pratiprakarana mane jaya gure savire tara vetale gandhare sa vajrahasta aparājita ekajāṭaṃ namamiḥ oṃ tara taraṇe hūṃ hara hara svāhā.

If one takes clay from the horn of a lead bull, recites over it twenty-one times using the left hand alone, and applies it, one will bring the target under one's power.

The Means of Accomplishing the Blessed Lady Kurukullā is complete. It was translated by the paṇḍita Bimpa and the translator Shönnu Ö.

In the description of the main face colors, སེར་བ is the color yellow, not hail. The southern Tārā, rendered literally "the Perfecting Tārā," is given as Niṣpanna Tārā following the parallel Sanskrit sādhanas; her mantra here is spelled differently. The southeastern Tārā is called "Jewel Tārā" (Ratna Tārā) in the description and Maṇi Tārā in the mantra. Vajravetālī is said to be four-armed but is listed with five implements as the text stands. The paṇḍita's name Bimpa is given as written; its Sanskrit form is uncertain.
