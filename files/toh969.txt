The Noble King of Vidyā-Mantras Called "Great Breath" (Āryamahāśvāsanāmavidyārāja)

The Noble King of Vidyā-Mantras Called "Great Breath" is a short scripture whose Tibetan title opens with the words 'phags pa rig sngags kyi, "noble... of vidyā-mantras." Its full Sanskrit original is not securely attested, so it is best known by its English rendering. The text is a protective incantation, set as a dialogue in which Daṃṣṭrākarālapiṅgala, the fearsome lord of the bhūtas and sovereign of the four continents, addresses the Buddha. He describes his power to harm and bind spirits and to send forth a great hungry breath that saps the vitality of humans and nonhumans and spreads fevers through the world. He then turns to the protective efficacy of this king of vidyā-mantras, which guards against such afflictions wherever it is present. The work is Toh 969 in the Kangyur, where it belongs among the collected dhāraṇī and mantra texts valued for healing and protection from harmful spirits and disease.

The Noble King of Vidyā-Mantras Called "Great Breath"

Homage to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas.

Then the great lord of the bhūtas, the sovereign of the four continents, the one called Daṃṣṭrākarālapiṅgala, said this to the Blessed One:

"Blessed One, I am the lord of all the bhūtas. I harm, strike and bind all the bhūtas who dwell in the world-system of the four continents. I hurl them from this world-system into other world-systems, and I cut off their limbs, both major and minor. Blessed One, whenever I grow hungry, I let out a breath, and that breath fills the whole world-system of the four continents. Blessed One, I steal the vitality of all humans and nonhumans, and as soon as the great breath touches sentient beings, all kinds of fevers arise.

"Blessed One, wherever this king of vidyā-mantras called Great Breath is practiced, my breath will not prevail there, and the bhūtas will not cause harm. Any son or daughter of good family who pays homage to the Buddha, the Dharma and the Saṅgha, and then ties this on the head or at the neck and wears it, will not be struck by fevers arising from wind, from bile, from phlegm, or from their combination. From this day on I make this pledge in the presence of the Tathāgata."
