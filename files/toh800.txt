The Mantra That Dispels Illnesses of Bile

The Mantra That Dispels Illnesses of Bile is a very short text, and the Tibetan title line names it as "the mantra that dispels illnesses of bile" (mkhris pa'i nad sel ba'i sngags); the Sanskrit title is not evident from the material at hand. It is a protective incantation of the dhāraṇī type, offering a brief spoken remedy for ailments traditionally attributed to bile, such as yellowing of the eyes, nausea, and vomiting. After a homage to the Three Jewels and the mantra itself, the text prescribes reciting it while fanning the body with a cooling fan, and it closes with a note on a companion dhāraṇī for pacifying the stinging pain of burns from fire. It is Toh 800 in the Kangyur, belonging to the class of dhāraṇī texts within the Tantra division, where short spells for healing and protection are gathered.

Recite it eleven times and anoint the spittle upon the affected place, and the stinging pain of a burn caused by fire is pacified. Here ends the dhāraṇī that thoroughly pacifies the stinging pain of fire.

The mantra that dispels illnesses of bile.

Homage to the Three Jewels.

Namo hetu hege svāhā.

If one fans the body with a cooling fan while reciting it, it dispels illnesses of bile, yellow eyes, and the sickness of nausea and vomiting. Here ends the dhāraṇī that dispels illnesses of bile.
