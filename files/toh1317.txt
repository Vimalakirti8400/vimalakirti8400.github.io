In Praise of the Noble Tārā Kurukullā (Āryatārākurukullāstotra)

In Praise of the Noble Tārā Kurukullā, known in Sanskrit as the Āryatārākurukullāstotra and in Tibetan as Pakma Drolma Kurukulle Töpa, is a brief hymn of praise to Kurukullā, the red, four-armed form of Tārā associated with magnetizing and bringing beings under the sway of the Dharma. In seven short verses it describes her arising from the syllable hrīḥ, her blazing red body, her arrow and bow, hook and utpala, her dancing posture, skull crown and tiger skin, and her power to draw beings out of the mire of afflictions. The colophon attributes it to the great master Ḍombi Heruka, one of the Indian tantric adepts. It is Toh 1317 in the Tengyur, where it is found among the texts of the tantra section as a devotional praise.

In the language of India: āryatārakurukullestotra. In the language of Tibet: In Praise of the Noble Tārā Kurukullā.

Homage to the noble Tārā of great power!

Though you stir from the state of changeless sameness,
You act for the benefit of beings through discerning wisdom.
Radiating red light, with the splendor of a thousand suns,
Homage to you, one-faced and four-armed!

Goddess arisen from hrīḥ,
Red in color and four-armed,
Your hands holding arrow and bow,
Holding the hook: homage and praise to you!

Your light blazes like the mass of fire at the aeon's end;
You have one face and three eyes,
Your red hair blazing upward;
Holding the utpala: homage and praise to you!

You are seated in a dance with half-crossed legs,
Fierce in your wrathful manner, majestic,
Crowned with an ornament of five skulls,
Wearing a tiger skin: homage and praise to you!

Red, baring your fangs, of unbearable form,
Adorned with the five great fierce seals,
With a necklace garland of fifty,
Vanquisher of māras, I pay homage to you!

From the union of āli kī,
Ever delighting in bliss and awakening,
Your head adorned with Amitābha,
Delighting in the dance, I pay homage to you!

With arrow and bow you threaten;
With the utpala you bring under your power;
With the hook you draw beings from the mire of affliction:
Four-armed one who quells the māras, homage to you!

The praise of the noble Tārā, the goddess Kurukullā, composed by the great master Ḍombi Heruka, is complete.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The union named in the sixth verse is evidently that of the vowels and consonants (āli and kāli), the source of the deity's syllables. The last line of the final verse reads literally "māras, four arms: homage"; "who quells the māras" supplies the evident sense. The "five great fierce seals" are the five bone ornaments, and the garland of fifty is the necklace of fifty heads.
