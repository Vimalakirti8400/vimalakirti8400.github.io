Praise of Glorious Hevajra (Śrīhevajrastotra)

Praise of Glorious Hevajra, in Sanskrit Śrī Hevajrastotra and in Tibetan Pal Gyepa Dorjé la Töpa, is a short hymn in verse to the tantric deity Hevajra and his maṇḍala. It praises Hevajra, eight-faced and sixteen-armed, as the union of emptiness and compassion who crushes the māras and the afflictions, then offers homage to his consort Nairātmyā and to each of the eight goddesses of his retinue, from Gaurī in the east to Ḍombinī in the northwest, describing the color, posture, and position of each. According to its colophon it was composed by Ratnavajra, an accomplished master from Kashmir, at the request of his son, and was translated into Tibetan by the Indian preceptor Kumārakalaśa and the monk translator Jangchub Sherab. The work is Toh 1304 in the Tengyur, where it belongs to the tantra commentaries, among the texts of the Hevajra cycle, and it is a hymn of praise in form.

In the language of India: śrī he vajra sto tra. In the language of Tibet: Praise of Glorious Hevajra.

Homage to the Blessed One Vajraḍāka.

Arisen from both entity and non-entity,
Great marvel, vast of body,
Gone beyond the body, great bliss:
Homage to the protector of beings.

Great compassion, great in wrath,
You who utterly crush all the māras,
Delight of Nairātmyā's heart,
Roused to wakefulness by the host of goddesses,

You who give rise to pure joy,
You who strike terror into the great terrors,
Eight-faced and with a retinue of eight,
Endowed with arms that number twice eight,

Terrifying, your faces turned
Toward devouring every disease without remainder,
Emitting and gathering back with hūṃ,
Unbearable guide;

With the thunder of the clouds at the end of time
You crush the afflictions.
Moist with the moisture of compassion, lightning flashing,
You let fall the stream of the rain of the true Dharma.

Those of the three realms,
Overpowered by the sleep of ignorance, you awaken.
Fully showing the path of liberation,
You grant light to the worlds.

To all beings fallen into error
You stretch out your many arms,
And through the full working of your divine eye
You clear away the cataract of delusion.

Utterly luminous great secret,
Accomplishing every aim in an instant,
Through secret mantra wholly freed beyond the human,
Loving toward all sentient beings,

Vajra essence of awakening,
Whose very nature is emptiness and compassion,
Like a blue rain cloud,
You dwell in the charnel ground in the manner of a dance.

With the great blood of the four māras
You are intoxicated together with the goddess.
Supreme fangs like the tips of the crescent moon,
You enjoy the beautiful maṇḍala.

With a garland of great skulls strung on entrails
Your body is fully adorned.
With the great laughter of your play
You put the terrifying ones to flight.

Through right mindfulness, for the hosts of beings
You fulfill completely whatever their minds desire.
Surpassing the wish-fulfilling jewel,
With many vast and supreme praises

The great Lord of Speech praises you.
Wholly beyond the domain of sound,
Fully abandoning the net of concepts,
Utterly crushing great delusion,

Endowed with the major marks of a great being
And with all the minor marks and limbs,
At the sight of you, joy increases.
For the mind-streams of those supremely accomplished

You are the fine moonlight that truly satisfies.
Without beginning, middle, or end,
Sun, perfect buddha of the three times,
Great fire that surely burns the firewood of the afflictions,

Vanquishing misdeeds,
With rays of sun, moon, and fire,
Three eyes in each face,
A host of twenty-four eyes,

You crush the three kinds of delusion.
Abandoning both attachment and freedom from attachment,
You bring full liberation through great passion.
Possessed of the supreme accomplishment of self-awareness,

You show directly the means of bliss.
Gone beyond existence and nirvāṇa,
You abide within existence and nirvāṇa.
Tasting the lips of the Great Seal,

Well sealed with the five mudrās,
With the enjoyment of close embrace
You sport in the joy of full accomplishment.
Thoroughly smeared with the ash of corpses,

Adorned with ornaments of bone,
Great in play in the time of night:
To glorious Heruka I bow.

She who, apart from the Lord,
Does not sport for even an instant,
Of excellent fortune, lovely, well favored:
To Nairātmyā the yoginī I bow.

In Indra's quarter, the east, she stands
Pressing Brahmā down with a stamp of her foot,
Lovely, endowed with a blue body color:
To Gaurī I bow with the crown of my head.

On the right side she sits
Upon Indra, crushing him,
Sounding the small drum, all her limbs
Madder-red: to Caurī I bow.

In the western quarter she sits
Dancing upon Upendra (Viṣṇu),
Yoginī the color of refined gold:
To supreme Vetālī I bow.

In the north she sits upon Rudra,
Holding a frightful snake in her hand,
Like the color of the precious emerald:
To fierce-devouring Ghasmarī I bow.

In Īśāna's quarter, the northeast,
She sits truly pressing down Yama, the king,
Like a blazing sapphire:
To fortunate Pukkasī I bow.

In the quarter of fire, the southeast,
She sits dancing upon a yakṣa,
White as the autumn moon, a sovereign lady:
To supreme Śavarī I bow.

In Nairṛti's quarter, the southwest,
She tramples the god Nairṛti, her supreme hand marked with a wheel,
Endowed with a body like the cloudless sky:
To Caṇḍālī I bow.

In the quarter of the wind, the northwest, upon Vemacitrin,
She sits dancing in the half-crossed posture,
Lovely, endowed with varied body colors:
To Ḍombinī too I bow with reverence.

Where is this supremely profound one,
And where are such words of mine?
Where is the vast and supreme protector,
And where this brief composition with which I praise him?

Yet because my son,
The youth called Prātimokṣa,
Made the request, for that reason
I have made a praise such as this.

By whatever virtue I have gained
Through this condensed praise of mine
To the Protector together with the eight goddesses,
By that may I too become like him.

This praise of the glorious and venerable Hevajra, composed by the glorious Ratnavajra, the accomplished master learned in all the fields of knowledge without exception, who was born in the land of Kashmir, is complete.

It was translated, edited, and finalized by the Indian preceptor Kumārakalaśa and the translator, the monk Jangchub Sherab.

"Through secret mantra wholly freed beyond the human" renders gsang sngags mi las rnam par grol; the sense of mi las is uncertain. The name of the author's son, so thar, is the standard Tibetan rendering of prātimokṣa, and is given in that form; whether it renders a personal name exactly cannot be confirmed. The eight goddesses are given in the standard Sanskrit forms of the Hevajra maṇḍala.
