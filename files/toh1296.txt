The Rite of the Maṇḍala of Glorious Hevajra (Śrīhevajramaṇḍalavidhināma)

The Rite of the Maṇḍala of Glorious Hevajra, in Sanskrit Śrī Hevajramaṇḍalavidhi and in Tibetan Pal Gyepa Dorjé Kyilkhor gyi Choga, is a concise tantric ritual manual for conferring the empowerments of Hevajra. It describes how the vajra master prepares the maṇḍala, vases, offerings and tormas, generates Hevajra and the eight Nairātmyā goddesses, and confers the vase, secret, wisdom knowledge and fourth empowerments on disciples, and it also teaches a way of continually receiving these four empowerments from deities visualized on one's crown, culminating in resting in the luminous great seal, before closing with the feast, dedication and aspiration prayers. Its colophon attributes it to the wisdom ḍākinī Niguma and says it was translated by her together with the Tibetan translator Len Darma Lodrö. It is Toh 1296 in the Tengyur, where it belongs among the tantra commentaries as a ritual work of the Hevajra cycle.

In the language of India: śrī hevajra maṇḍal vidhi nāma. In the language of Tibet: The Rite of the Maṇḍala of Glorious Hevajra.

Homage to glorious Hevajra.

Having completed one hundred and ten thousand recitations of the approach to the Blessed One Hevajra, one should ripen a disciple of the right lineage in a secluded place. There one makes a maṇḍala of a red eight-petaled lotus encircled by vajras, together with a courtyard, and sets out nine heaps of flowers. Two vases, furnished with the substances and ornaments, are placed in the center and in the northeast, and the place is adorned with a canopy, curtains and so on. On the four sides of the maṇḍala one should surround it with four sets of the five offerings, five tormas, and the provisions for the feast.

Then the vajra master bathes, and on a comfortable seat meditates on the stages of generation and completion. In an instant, from within the state in which the vase is empty, from hūṃ arises a celestial palace, and in its center, upon a lotus stalk arisen from paṃ, is a red eight-petaled lotus. At its center, upon a sun and the corpses of the four māras, one generates, by the five rites, or by the three, or in an instant, Heruka with eight faces and sixteen arms, or with one face and two arms, together with his consort. On the eight lotus petals, from a, one generates eight Nairātmyā goddesses, each with one face and two arms.

Then, with the light of one's own hūṃ, one invites the wisdom beings. Having made offerings and praises and done as much of the approach recitation of the four mantras of father and mother as one can, one makes offerings again, and the deities melt into nectar. One generates swirling nectar in the action vase, performs the approach recitation, and with it bathes the disciple.

Then, for the disciples seated in a row, one carries out, as the scriptures teach, the going for refuge and the generation of the mind of awakening, the supplication, the granting of permission, the gathering of thoughts, the performance of the five limbs, the taking of the vows, and the proclamation of the sacred pledge; and then one should cause the wisdom to descend.

Then comes the supplication to the master requesting empowerment:

Just as the empowerment of glorious Heruka,
The majestic one, for the protection of beings,
Is thus the perfect source of all qualities,
So grant it to me today, I pray.
Just as the great offering was bestowed
Upon Bodhivajra, the Buddha,
So for the sake of protecting beings,
Grant me today the vajra of space.

When this supplication has been made, from the hearts of the maṇḍala and of the hosts of deities in space come goddesses holding vases of nectar water. As they confer empowerment with the stream of secret water, one imagines that the misdeeds and obscurations of the body are purified. Then, as one says:

The empowerment of the great vajra,
To which all the three realms pay homage,
Springing forth from the three secrets of all the buddhas,
Shall now be given,

one imagines that one has obtained the vase empowerment. Likewise, at the throat is the secret empowerment, at the heart the wisdom knowledge empowerment, and at the navel the fourth empowerment. This is the empowerment solely for the sake of others.

Next I shall explain the continual conferral of the four empowerments upon oneself and others. On the crown of one's head, upon a lotus stalk transformed from paṃ, at the center of a red eight-petaled lotus, upon a sun and a corpse, one imagines, by the three rites or in an instant, Hevajra alone, without consort. On the eight lotus petals, upon a sun and a corpse, one imagines eight Nairātmyā goddesses arisen from a, swelling with bliss.

Then, supplicating as before, measureless light rays come forth from the hosts of deities on one's crown, and one's whole body, inside and out, is pervaded with light. By this all the obscurations of karma and the afflictions are cleansed away, and one imagines one's body to be of the nature of light. Becoming oneself, through this, Hevajra alone without consort is the vase empowerment.

Then, by supplicating again, the goddesses on the crown, swelling with bliss, gather into light and embrace the father. As father and mother join in union, the white and red bodhicitta flow from the place of union in blissful trickling drops and fall upon the tongue. The bliss that arises from this is the secret empowerment.

When one again supplicates the hosts of deities, a single Nairātmyā splits off from the principal's consort and comes forth. She embraces oneself as Hevajra, and through the union of churning, the great undefiled bliss arises in the body. This is the wisdom knowledge empowerment.

Then, when one makes offerings to the hosts of deities on the crown, the deities together with their seats dissolve into one's crown. Vivid in possessing the deity's body, one rests in equipoise in great bliss without grasping, the union of clarity and emptiness. In brief, one should rest solely in the great seal (mahāmudrā), the completely pure essence of all phenomena comprised by appearance and existence, saṃsāra and nirvāṇa, free of all conceptual elaboration and luminous by nature. That is the fourth empowerment.

Then, within a vessel arisen from yaṃ raṃ kaṃ and a, one thoroughly purifies the various substances into the five meats and the five nectars, makes offerings to the hosts of deities, and the yogins should enjoy them. The five tormas are purified as before, and with the mantra for all the bhūtas one satisfies the gurus, Hevajra, the Dharma protectors, the lords of the place, and the harm doers. Then one sings vajra songs, makes offerings, praises and confession to the hosts of deities, and dedicates the merit with these words:

By the merit of the giving and the rest that I have done,
May I please the Blessed One Hevajra,
And having brought the six perfections to completion,
May all beings attain the state of the Vajra King.

Then the master and everyone together, with one voice, make this aspiration:

Born into the family and keeping the pledges,
Teaching the incomparable Hevajra,
Devoted to the guru and endowed with compassion,
May I be so in birth after birth.
Hands jingling vajra and bell,
Reading the profound Dharma,
Partaking together of the consort's melting bliss,
May I be so in birth after birth.

Then one circumambulates the maṇḍala, asks forbearance, recites verses of auspiciousness, requests the wisdom beings to depart, gathers the pledge beings into one's crown, and pleases the vajra master with an offering fee.

The Abridged Rite of Empowerment through Glorious Hevajra, composed by the wisdom ḍākinī Niguma, is complete. It was translated by that same Vajra Wisdom Ḍākinī and the translator Len Darma Lodrö.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The line rendered "Upon Bodhivajra, the Buddha" follows the Tibetan as it stands; the well-known Sanskrit verse behind it speaks of Bodhivajra bestowing the great offering upon the buddhas, and the Tibetan may intend that sense. The eight goddesses of the retinue are all called Nairātmyā here, as the text names them.
