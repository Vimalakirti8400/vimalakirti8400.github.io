The Rite of the Torma (Balividhi)

The Rite of the Torma, in Sanskrit Balividhi and in Tibetan Tormé Choga, is a short tantric ritual manual for offering torma, the ritual cake, to all the bhūtas and other spirits, so that the harm done by obstructors is pacified and every rite can be accomplished. It guides the practitioner through purifying all phenomena with mantras, arising as one's chosen deity, inviting the gods, world protectors, earth deities and nāgas, transforming them into the forms of Vairocana, Akṣobhya and Amitābha, and then preparing the offering as wisdom nectar in a skull cup before satisfying the guests, requesting their departure and dedicating the merit. Its colophon names its author as the great master Dīpaṃkaraśrījñāna, the Bengali monk better known as Atiśa, who says he received the rite by pleasing his guru. It is Toh 1295 in the Tengyur, where it belongs among the tantra commentaries and ritual works.

The Rite of the Torma.

In the language of India: baliṃ vidhiḥ. In the language of Tibet: The Rite of the Torma.

Homage to the bodhisattva Mañjuśrī, the ever-youthful.

To pacify the harm done by obstructors
And to accomplish every rite,
I shall properly set down the rite
Of giving torma to all the bhūtas.

Here, first, the yogin sits on a comfortable seat and recites three times the mantra called oṃ svabhāva śudhaḥ sarva dharmāḥ svabhāva śuddho'haṃ, and firmly resolves that all phenomena are completely pure by their intrinsic nature. Then one recites the mantra called oṃ śūnyatā jñāna vajra svabhāva ātmako'haṃ. One apprehends the three realms as, from the very beginning, unborn, luminous by nature and free of concepts, as nothing but the appearance of one's own mind, which is nondual suchness. In the middle of the vast element of space one visualizes the seal of the source of phenomena, standing point upward. In its center, upon a vast variegated lotus and a moon and the other maṇḍalas, one should generate the yoga of one's own chosen deity.

After that, having first illuminated all beings without exception with light rays arising from the seed syllable in one's heart, one properly invites the beings: Brahmā and the others who move in the form realm, lords of the great trichiliocosm; the king of the Vaśavartin gods and the others; all the world protectors, such as the Moon, the Sun and Upendra (Viṣṇu); all humans and non-humans; Vemacitrin (the lord of the asuras), the earth deities and the others; and the nāgas and the others. One imagines them in the center of the source of phenomena.

Here, first, those of the upper direction:
The gods who dwell in the form realm
Are white, with matted locks and diadems,
With the mudrā of meditative absorption,
And garlanded with white light rays.
Those who dwell in the desire realm,
The Lord of the Desire Realm and the others,
Are gods who wear jeweled diadems,
With various ornaments and radiance.
Above, the Sun, the Moon and the others likewise,
And the lords of each of the directions,
Each with their own color, hand emblems and so on,
Should be visualized in their proper order.
Vemacitrin, the Earth and the others,
And the eight lords of the nāgas as well,
Through the three families that arise from the seed
In the heart of one's own wisdom being,
Should be accomplished, one after another,
In the forms of Vairocana of wisdom and the rest.

Now the rite that thoroughly purifies all sentient beings will be taught. From the seed syllable in the heart of one's own wisdom being, one imagines above one's head a white syllable oṃ, like the light of ten million moons, resting on a moon disc and radiating light in every direction. By its light rays one should accomplish the gods who move in the form realm, Brahmā and the others, in the form of Vairocana.

After that, from that same seed syllable in the heart of one's own wisdom being comes the yogin's syllable hūṃ, the color of sapphire and radiating sapphire light,

Resting in the middle of a moon disc.
Imagine light radiating from it.
Having drawn them in with that cloud of light rays,
The gods who move in the desire realm
And the world protectors are purified,
And one should fully meditate on them
As accomplished in the forms of the buddha Akṣobhya.

Immediately after that, from that same heart of one's own wisdom being comes a red syllable a, intensely radiating masses of red light rays and resting on a moon disc. As its red light rays spread out everywhere, they thoroughly purify Vemacitrin, the earth deities and the eight nāga kings, and one should accomplish them in the form of the Tathāgata Amitābha.

All of these, the world protectors and the others together with their retinues, are accomplished in the forms of the tathāgatas of their own families. Here, those who move in the form realm belong to the family of the Tathāgata Vairocana. The gods who move in the desire realm and the world protectors belong to the family of Akṣobhya. Vemacitrin and the others belong to the family of Amitābha. Therefore, having accomplished all of them, from those who move in the form realm down to the eight nāga kings, in the forms of the tathāgatas of their respective families, one empowers them with the empowerment of all the tathāgatas, places the Vajrasattva mudrā on their heads, and with these blesses their body, speech and mind.

Then one gathers before them whatever food, edibles and other things are at hand. First one imagines a wind maṇḍala, transformed from yaṃ, shaped like a bow and marked with a banner. Above it is a fire maṇḍala, transformed from a red raṃ, triangular and blazing intensely. Above that one meditates on a skull cup transformed from the syllable ā. In the center of that skull cup one imagines the five nectars, blessed by the seed syllables of the five tathāgatas, bhrūṃ āṃ jṝṃ khaṃ hūṃ. After that one properly brings about the kindling, the warming and the rest, and sees it as blessed by the syllable oṃ. Above it one imagines a vajra arisen from the syllable hūṃ. Then, with the light rays of the syllable oṃ, one draws forth the wisdom nectar of all the tathāgatas and dissolves it completely into those five nectars. One imagines that at once the blaze and the warmth, the syllable oṃ and the vajra all melt into it. One offers arghaṃ and the rest, and satisfies the whole assembly of tathāgatas as they draw up the nectar so prepared through tubes of light rays streaming from their vajra tongues.

Then one properly takes up the vajra and bell, blesses those substances up to seven times with the torma mantra for all the bhūtas, and then offers them to those guests.

Swiftly brandishing the vajra,
And joining the vajra, one must clear away
All the mountains of the imputed.
Then, ringing the bell,
With its sound of supreme delight,
By which the Victors come to realize emptiness,
One recites this verse three times:
Existence is pure by its essential nature;
By its essential nature it is freed from existence.
The supreme being, pure by intrinsic nature,
Brings about the supreme state of existence.

Here the mantra for blessing the torma is this: oṃ akāro mukhaṃ sarva dharmāṇāṃ ādyanutpannatvāta oṃ āḥ hūṃ phaṭ svāhā. One recites this mantra of suchness three or seven times.

Recollecting the pure mind of perfect awakening,
One blesses the torma.
Just as with the flavors of nectar,
So should one satisfy them all.
Having properly given the scattered offering and drink,
One should ask their forbearance and request them to depart.

Here the mantra for requesting departure is this: oṃ

You have accomplished every aim of sentient beings;
Grant the accomplishments that accord with them.
Having gone to the realm of the buddhas,
Please come back once again.

oṃ āḥ hūṃ muḥ

Unborn and unceasing,
Pure by intrinsic nature from the very beginning:
Having seen all these beings in this way,
This great sending forth is to be done.

By whatever merit I have sought and gathered
Through writing well this rite of giving torma,
May all these beings
Become vessels for perfect awakening.

The monk born in Bengal,
Dīpaṃkaraśrījñāna,
Having received it by pleasing his guru,
Has fully explained this very torma.

The Rite of the Torma for All the Bhūtas, composed by the great master Dīpaṃkaraśrījñāna, is complete.

ཐགས་བཟངས་རིས is taken as Vemacitrin, the asura lord, here standing for the asuras of the lower region alongside the earth deities and nāgas. The verse on the bell is terse: the line "by which the Victors come to realize emptiness" follows the instrumental as written, though it may mean the sound that expresses the Victors' realization of emptiness.
