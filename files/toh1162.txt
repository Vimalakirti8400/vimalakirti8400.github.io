Praise of the Blessed One, the Song of Glorious Vajradhara (Śrī Vajradhara Saṃgīti Bhagavān Stotraḥ)

Praise of the Blessed One, the Song of Glorious Vajradhara, known in Sanskrit as Śrī Vajradhara Saṃgīti Bhagavān Stotraḥ and in Tibetan as Chomden Dé la Töpa Pal Dorjé Dzin gyi Yang, is a short devotional hymn in verse. Opening with homage to glorious Vajrasattva and bearing the name of Vajradhara, the tantric form of the Buddha, it belongs to the tantric strand of Buddhist devotional poetry. Its verses extol the Blessed One as unequalled, unmoving and stainless, an embodiment of compassion who dispels the suffering of beings, whose qualities no example in all of space can match, and who ever bestows supreme accomplishment on the realm of sentient beings. It is Toh 1162 in the Tengyur, where it is placed among the hymns of praise (stotra) that make up the Praises section.

In Sanskrit: śrī vajradhara saṃgīti bhagavān stotraḥ
In Tibetan: Praise of the Blessed One, the Song of Glorious Vajradhara.

Homage to glorious Vajrasattva.

Unequalled and unmoving one, endowed with the supreme quality of sameness,
Embodiment of compassion, you dispel the suffering of beings.
Equal in all qualities, you bestow endless accomplishment;
Stainless, unmoving, unequalled, the supreme and most excellent Dharma.

You ever bestow supreme accomplishment on the realm of sentient beings,
Equal and endless in accomplishment, beyond all example.
Nowhere in space is there an example to equal you;
None can match even the tiniest fraction of your qualities.

Ever stainless, you arise from the force of compassion;
Your aspirations fulfilled, you possess the unceasing nature of reality.
Boundless one, devoted to accomplishing the benefit of beings,
Embodiment of compassion, you ever shine forth.

Immeasurable and inestimable, your very state perfectly complete,
Though you have gone to the abode of bliss, your compassionate conduct never wavers.
You bestow supreme accomplishment on the three worlds,
Never subject to cessation, the wondrous and excellent nature of reality.

Bestower of the supreme, abiding in supreme generosity itself, you have attained the nature of reality;
Ever bestowing the sublime on the three worlds, you accomplish the supreme.
Sugata (one gone to bliss), whose knowledge of the three times is free of obscuration,
Grant me the supreme accomplishment of the three sacred commitments of all!

This concludes the Praise of the Blessed One, the Song of Glorious Vajradhara.

It was translated, edited, and finalized by the Indian preceptor Vidyākaraprabha and the chief editor-translator, the monk Paltsek.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

In the fourth verse, the line rendered "Never subject to cessation" can also be read "unchanging even in cessation." The first line of the final verse is highly compressed; it is read here as "abiding in supreme generosity itself."
