An Explanation of the Eight Charnel Grounds (Aṣṭaśmaśānākhyāna)

An Explanation of the Eight Charnel Grounds, known in Sanskrit as the Aṣṭaśmaśānākhyāta, is a short tantric work that describes the eight great charnel grounds, the cremation sites that ring the protective circle of many tantric maṇḍalas. Opening with homage to the Blessed One Lokeśvara, it moves through each direction in turn, naming each charnel ground, such as Caṇḍogra in the east and Gahvara in the north, along with the tree that stands there, the great being who dwells in it, the guardian deity of that direction with his color, gestures, and mount, and the associated nāga and cloud. These are the visual details a practitioner would use to imagine the charnel grounds in meditation. The text is Toh 1216 in the Tengyur, where it belongs among the commentarial and practice literature on the tantras.

In the language of India: aṣṭaśmaśanākhyatanāma. In the language of Tibet: An Explanation of the Eight Charnel Grounds.

Homage to the Blessed One, Lokeśvara.

Now I shall explain the eight charnel grounds.

The charnel ground of the east is called Caṇḍogra. There, in a myrobalan tree, is the great being called Caṇḍogra, white in color, with the color of an elephant. At the foot of the tree is the lord of that direction, Indra, yellow in color, holding a vajra in his right hand and making the threatening gesture with his left, riding the white elephant that guards the earth. There too are the nāga Vāsuki, yellow in color, and the cloud called Thundering, blue in color.

The charnel ground of the north is called Gahvara. There, in a bodhi tree, is the great being called Bagara, with a human face, yellow in color. At the foot of the tree is the lord of that direction, Kubera, yellow in color, his right and left hands showing the gesture of fearlessness and holding a gha ha. He is short and rides upon a treasure. There too are the nāga Takṣaka, red in color, and the cloud called Stupefying, white in color.

The charnel ground of the west is called Jvālākulakaraṅka. There, in an aśoka tree, is the great being called Jvālākulakaraṅka, with the head of a makara, yellow in color. At the foot of the tree is the lord of that direction, Varuṇa, white in color, riding a makara, his right and left hands making the threatening gesture and holding a noose. There too are the nāga Karkoṭa, green in color, and the cloud called Fierce, white in color.

In the south is the charnel ground called Terrifying Host. There, in a bibhītaka tree, is the great being called Terrifying Host, with a human face, black in color. At the foot of the tree is the lord of that direction, Yama, black in color, his right and left hands holding a club and a noose, riding a buffalo. There too are the nāga Padma, white in color, and the cloud called Massing, yellow in color.

The charnel ground of the intermediate quarter of the fire god is called Lakṣmīvana. There, in the tree called karañja, is the great being called Lakṣmīvana, with the face of a goat, white in color. At the foot of the tree is the lord of that direction, Agni, red in color, holding a rosary in his right hand and a water pot in his left, riding upon a goat. There too are the nāga Mahāpadma, white in color, and the cloud called Swirling, red in color.

The charnel ground of Nairṛti is called Ghorāndhakāra. There, in an udumbara tree, is the great being called Terrifying, with the face of a buffalo, black in color. At the foot of the tree is the lord of that direction, the rākṣasa Nairṛti, black in color, his right and left hands making the threatening gesture and holding a human head, riding a corpse. There too are the nāga Ananta, blue in color, and the cloud called Thundering, yellow in color.

The charnel ground of the intermediate quarter of the wind is called Kilikilārava. There, in the tree called Accomplisher of Existence, is the great being called Kilikilārava, with the face of a buffalo, the color of smoke. At the foot of the tree is the lord of that direction, Vāyu, god of the wind, white in color, holding a wind banner with his right and left hands, riding a deer. There too are the nāga Kulika, multicolored, and the cloud called Raining, green in color.

The charnel ground of the northeast is called Aṭṭahāsa. There, in a nyagrodha tree, is the great being called Aṭṭahāsa, with the face of a bull, white in color. At the foot of the tree is the lord of that direction, Īśāna, pale blue in color, his right and left hands holding a trident and a skull cup, riding a bull. There too are the nāga Śaṅkhapāla, yellow, and the cloud called Caṇḍī, of various colors.

All the lords of the directions have human faces and two arms. The nāgas of the directions have seven hoods, slightly bare their fangs, and take whatever form they wish. The clouds too are of various colors.

Caṇḍogra and Gahvara,
Jvālākulakaraṅka,
And Terrifying Host, beginning in the east,
Abide encircling in the directions.
Then there is Aṭṭahāsa;
In Lakṣmīvana is Agni;
Ghorāndhakāra is Nairṛti's;
In the wind's quarter is Kilikilārava.
These are the eight charnel grounds.
The glorious vajra yogin
Should contemplate them with great effort
Within the vajra fence.

This completes An Explanation of the Eight Charnel Grounds, composed by the master Yogin. It was translated by the Indian preceptor Buddhākaravajra and the Tibetan translator Gö.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The charnel-ground names follow their standard Saṃvara-cycle Sanskrit forms wherever the Tibetan matches them: Caṇḍogra, Gahvara, Jvālākulakaraṅka, Lakṣmīvana, Ghorāndhakāra, Kilikilārava and Aṭṭahāsa. The southern ground is rendered literally as "Terrifying Host" because its Sanskrit form could not be identified with confidence.

The following are also translated literally: the names of the clouds, the northwestern tree ("Accomplisher of Existence"), and the southwestern great being ("Terrifying"). "Caṇḍī" for the northeastern cloud reflects the drafts' rendering.

The northern great being "Bagara" is kept as the text transliterates it. It may be a garbled form of Gahvara.

The object Kubera holds, "gha ha," is unidentified and is left as written.

The text says the eastern great being is "the color of an elephant" (mdog), not elephant-faced. This may be a scribal slip for gdong, "face," since every other great being is described by its face. It is kept as it stands.

The nāgas' "'dod pa'i gzugs can" is taken as "take whatever form they wish"; it could also mean "of pleasing form."

"The master Yogin" may be a personal name or simply an epithet.
