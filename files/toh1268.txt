The Means of Accomplishing Glorious Hevajra, Called The Precious Lamp (Śrīhevajraratnapralokesādhana-nāma)

The Means of Accomplishing Glorious Hevajra, called The Precious Lamp, known in Tibetan as Dpal dgyes pa rdo rje'i sgrub thabs rin po che'i sgron ma, is a sādhana, a manual of deity practice, whose Sanskrit title survives in the Tibetan text in a somewhat irregular spelling naming it a Hevajra sādhana called the Precious Lamp. Written in verse, it guides the practitioner through the full meditation of Hevajra with his consort Nairātmyā and their circle of eight goddesses: the preliminary worship and confession, the protection circle and the generation of the maṇḍala palace, the empowerment and nectar offerings, the completion-stage practices of the inner channels and the fire of Caṇḍālī, mantra recitation, the torma offering to the world's gods and nāgas, and the symbolic meaning of each feature of the deity and palace. Its colophon attributes it to the great master Dīpaṃkaraśrījñāna, and says it was translated into Tibetan by the Indian paṇḍita Dānaśīla and the translator Gan Darma Lodrö. It is Toh 1268 in the Tengyur, where it belongs to the tantra commentaries, among the practice texts of the Hevajra cycle.

In the language of India: śri he bajra ratna pra lo ke sā ṅha naṃ nā m. In the language of Tibet: The Means of Accomplishing Glorious Hevajra, called The Precious Lamp.

Homage to glorious Vajrasattva.

Paying homage to Heruka together with Nairātmyā
And to their Dharma retinue,
For those who long for him, and for those who seek that aim,
I write this Lamp, the means of accomplishing him.

One free of attachment, endowed with the awakening mind,
Intelligent, having received empowerment and taken the scriptural transmission,
In a charnel ground or some other pleasant place,
Should practice, diligent in yoga.

At dawn, rise from bed with loving-kindness.
Having washed the face and so forth with this,
Sit on a seat marked with a vajra,
And place a nectar pill upon the tongue.

Having taken all mothers into one's care with compassion,
Meditate on oneself with the pride of the Eight-Faced One.
With rakṣa purify the ground and protect the place,
And meditate firmly on the mountain of fire, the net and the tent.

In the heart of Heruka,
From paṃ and raṃ, clearly,
Meditate on a lotus and a red sun.
On that, a black hūṃ, with five rays of light,
Radiates to the ten directions, invites and purifies;
Having done so, all abide in the sky.
Visualizing clearly the guru, the buddhas, oneself and the deities,
Emanate bodies and prostrate.

Moreover, the Blessed One,
With the body color, arms, ornaments
And hand emblems to be taught below,
Is seated united with the consort Nairātmyā.

Before him, black Gaurī
Offers the white awakening mind, the hare-marked moon.
On the right, red Caurī
Offers the red awakening mind, the light of the sun.
Behind, yellow Vetālī
Offers a begging bowl filled with fine water.
On the left, green Ghasmarī
Offers a skull cup filled with fine scent.
In the northeast, blue Pukkasī
Offers a skull cup filled with the five meats.
In the southeast, white Śavarī
Offers various flavorful drinks.
In the southwest, red Caṇḍālī
Offers the sounds of the resounding ḍamaru drum.
In the northwest, Ḍombī, of various forms,
Offers worship with her hands crossed in mudrā.
The guru Akṣobhya is seated on the crown of the head.

Having meditated well, clearly and steadily,
Through the radiating and gathering of the seed at the heart,
The outer goddesses, the host of offerings,
Offering water, flowers, incense and so forth,
Make offering with the mantras of the chapter on consecration.

Emanating the seed, the host of Gaurī and the others,
Arrayed, having come from their abodes,
In the order in which they stand before one,
Offer the five nectars and the five meats.
Urged by the seed of the principal deity,
Ḍombī offers the secret offering.
All the offering goddesses
That were emanated are gathered back from their places.

Then, before them once more,
Kneeling, palms joined, with a fearful mind:
"The evil accumulated in saṃsāra after saṃsāra
From beginningless time until this day,
What I have done, what I have caused to be done,
And what I have rejoiced in, I confess, and henceforth I shall refrain."

In the virtue of the buddhas and bodhisattvas,
Of pratyekabuddhas and śrāvakas,
And of individual beings, rejoice.
Go for refuge to the three and generate the two kinds of mind.
I too make requests and supplications,
And dedicate everything to great awakening.

Then meditate for a long time
On loving-kindness, compassion, joy and equanimity.
By the three suchnesses and by saying muḥ,
Those invited in front depart to their abodes.

Knowing all phenomena to be empty,
With the mind settled, recite this mantra:
oṃ sva bhā va śuddhaḥ sarba dharmā sva bhā va śuddho'haṃ.
By reciting it, all phenomena that are grasped
Are mere dreams, magical displays, mirages,
Moons in water, optical illusions and illusions;
They do not exist apart from mere appearances of mind.

oṃ śū nya tā jñā na bajra sva bhā va ātma ko'haṃ.
By reciting it, all those appearances of mind
Are as the center of the sky at midday in autumn,
Free of the faults of dust, smoke, mist and clouds; so settle the mind.

From the nature of reality, free of elaboration,
With aspiration and loving-kindness, remember beings.
In the center of the sky, from raṃ and hūṃ,
A sun disk blazes with the fire of the end of time.
Then, arising from hūṃ,
A ground of crossed vajras,
An enclosure, a tent and a canopy,
And a blazing vajra wall radiate forth.

Then from e, the sphere of phenomena,
A triangular source of phenomena, vast as space.
Within what is thus meditated, yaṃ raṃ baṃ
laṃ, the four, blue, red, white and yellow.
yaṃ melts into a blue, bow-shaped
Wind maṇḍala bearing a banner.
On that, from raṃ, a red triangle,
The fire maṇḍala, marked with raṃ.
On that, from baṃ, a white circle,
The water maṇḍala, marked with a vase.
On that, from laṃ, a yellow square,
The earth maṇḍala, marked with a vajra.

Through the light of mind these melt,
And as the light spreads the celestial palace is formed,
Made of precious substances,
With four corners, four doors and four walls,
Four porticoes and eight pillars,
Beautified with various offerings and ornaments;
A wheel of Dharma with deer, parasols,
Banners, bells, pennants and so forth;
Pervaded by the light of many buddhas;
Nets and half-nets, archways,
Vases and vajra garlands,
The door frames beautified with half-moons and vajras.

In the eight charnel grounds are eight lakes and nāgas,
Eight trees, field protectors, eight seats and heads,
The guardians of the directions with their wives, sons and eight retinues,
And above, the eight gods with the retinues of gods;
Eight stūpas and hosts of yogins,
Yogins who act as companions, vetālas and spirits,
Birds and various beasts of prey and snakes,
Piśācas, malevolent beings and hosts of corpses.

On the sides of the doors, from the east onward in order,
The goddesses of song and of dance, of offering water,
Of flowers, incense and lamps,
Of scented water, food and various sounds,
Of form, sound, smell, taste, touch and phenomena,
And of ornaments and garlands, of various colors.

Within, white, yellow, red and green;
The center of the palace blazes dark blue.
A vajra wall furnished with a lotus;
On its anthers, the four māras pressed down by a sun.
On the eight petals draw the eight deities,
Pressed down at the heart by a moon.

Within that beautiful celestial palace,
Upon the sun over the four māras at the center,
From hūṃ and aṃ, below and above,
From the doubled āli, a moon disk,
From the doubled kāli, a sun disk;
The skull cup and the curved knife, the hand emblems,
With the seeds resting on them, radiate and gather;
From all of these becoming one,
Meditate clearly, as the underlying basis,
On the Eight-Faced One in union with the consort.

To protect those caught in attachment, through compassion,
From the seed at one's own heart
Buddhas go forth to the ten directions and come to the crown,
And at the heart the seeds abide each in its place.

gaṃ caṃ baṃ ghaṃ paṃ śaṃ laṃ ḍaṃ,

From the doubled āli, a moon

Above it, from the doubled kāli,
A sun disk, the wisdom of equality;
gaṃ transforms into a black curved knife;
The moon and the seed, each distinct, are discriminating wisdom;
The seed emanating, purifying and becoming one
Is the wisdom that accomplishes activities;
And the body of Gaurī arising
Is the wisdom of the sphere of phenomena.

The principal deity meditated on before
And the seven goddesses below
Are likewise bodies of the five wisdoms;
The yogin should meditate on them steadily.

In the east, arising from the curved knife,
Upon Brahmā, black Gaurī,
A curved knife in her right hand, a fish in her left;
Meditate on her as the nature of the aggregate of form.

In the south, upon Indra, red,
Caurī arises from a ḍamaru,
The ḍamaru in her right hand, a pig in her left;
Meditate on her as the nature of the aggregate of feeling.

In the west, from baṃ, Vetālī,
Golden in color, is seated upon Upendra,
A tortoise in her right hand, a skull cup in her left;
Meditate on her as the nature of the aggregate of perception.

In the north, from ghaṃ, Ghasmarī,
Upon Rudra, the color of emerald,
Holds a snake in her right hand and a skull cup in her left;
Meditate on her as the nature of the aggregate of formations.

In the northeast, from paṃ and a lion,
Pukkasī, blue, upon Yama,
A lion in her right hand, an axe in her left,
Is the nature of the earth element.

In the southeast, from śaṃ and a monk,
Śavarī, white, upon a yakṣa seat,
A monk in her right hand, a rattle in her left,
Is the nature of the water element.

In the southwest, from laṃ and a wheel,
Caṇḍālī, the color of the sky, pressing down a rākṣasa,
A wheel in her right hand, a plow in her left,
Is the nature of the fire element.

In the northwest, from ḍaṃ and a vajra,
Ḍombī, of various colors,
A vajra in her right hand, the threatening gesture in her left;
Meditate on her as the nature of the wind element.

These goddesses have three eyes,
Red and round, and bare their fangs;
Their tawny hair stands beautifully upswept.
Bone wheels, earrings,
Necklaces and the rest, the five mudrās,
A garland of fifteen dry human heads;
Naked, left leg extended, they stand in dancing pose,
In the attitude of bowing to the principal.

The body, speech and mind abiding in the intermediate state,
oṃ, āḥ and huṃ, white, red and black,
And the two hoḥ, relying on method and wisdom,
Are seen by the circle as an illusion;
They enter the crown of the principal, and the principal melts.
The consort too melts, and the two seeds huṃ and aṃ
Remain at the center of the maṇḍala,
Abiding in nonconceptual great bliss.

Because the lord has vanished, the goddesses,
Their minds tormented with intense desire,
The four goddesses, Pukkasī and the rest,
Sing the vajra songs:

"Venerable one, arise with a mind of compassion!
I am Pukkasī: protect me!
Abandon the very nature of emptiness
And grant me union in great bliss.
If you do not remain, I shall die;
Do not cut off the stream of your compassion: arise!"

"Blessed One, why do you remain in emptiness?
For Śavarī, bring the fruit to fulfillment!
Lord of joy, invited as the guest of the world,
Do you remain in emptiness?"

"When I, Caṇḍālī, make supplication,
Blessed Hevajra, arise!
I long for your heart;
Arise like a magical display!"

"I, Ḍombī, am losing my senses:
Blessed Hevajra, arise!
By the sweet sound of the vajra song,
Lord, arise as in a dream!"

From the wisdom of the five manifest awakenings,
The Blessed Hevajra himself
Abides at the center of the maṇḍala in the nine moods of dance.
The Eight-Faced One has sixteen arms.
The upper face is smoke-colored and bares its fangs,
With tawny hair and a bone wheel,
The hair adorned with a crossed vajra.
The first face matches the body in color;
The right face is white, the two other right faces match the body;
The left face is red, the two other left faces are as the body.
The hair blazes upward, with a crown of five skulls;
Three eyes, red and round; fangs bared;
Earrings, necklace and armlets,
Bracelets and anklets, and a girdle ornament;
A tiger-skin skirt bound on;
A garland of fresh heads; smeared with ashes.
Upon Brahmā, Śakra, Upendra and Rudra
He stands with four legs, the right extended.
The first right hand holds an elephant
In a skull cup, and embraces the consort.
Likewise a horse, a donkey, an ox, a camel,
A human, a lion and a cat are held,
The right hands holding skull cups in order.
The first left hand, within a skull cup,
Holds the earth goddess Pṛthivī, and embraces the consort.
Above and below in order, abiding in skull cups,
Are Water, Fire, Wind, the Sun,
The Moon, Yama and Dhanada.

His consort, one-faced and two-armed,
Black, embraces him with curved knife and skull cup;
Adorned with the five mudrās, with a garland of dry skulls,
Her yellow hair blazing upward, her three eyes red,
Savoring the lord, her left leg extended,
She abides with him in union upon the four māras.
Then meditate on oneself and that retinue
Clearly, well and steadily.

Bliss and clarity, the supreme king of maṇḍalas:
These are taught as the three samādhis.
To make them clear, at the heart of each
Meditate on its hand emblem upon a seat.
Its seed radiates to the ten directions;
Having invited and purified, the seeds dissolve each into its place.
With hūṃ summon the wisdom circle;
It abides in the eastern sky and is worshipped.
With jaḥ hūṃ baṃ hoḥ, by the four goddesses,
It is summoned, drawn in, made nondual and delighted.

Then bless the sense bases.
Of the principal and of the whole circle,
In the eyes, white Mohavajrā;
In the ears, black Dveṣavajrā;
In the nose, yellow Mātsaryavajrā;
On the tongue, red Rāgavajrā;
In the body, green Īrṣyāvajrā;
In the mind, dark blue Nairātmyayoginī.
They stand on Brahmā and the rest, upon lotus and moon,
With curved knife, skull cup and the five mudrās.

Body, speech and mind are blessed as well:
At the head, the mouth and the heart,
oṃ, āḥ and hūṃ, white, red and black;
Bhūcarī, Khecarī and Nairātmyā,
Holding curved knife and skull cup,
Meditate on them upon lotus, moon and Brahmā.

Again, by the seed at one's own heart,
Invite the gurus and the victors of the ten directions,
And the wrathful ones and goddesses of the circle;
Having worshipped them, make supplication:
oṃ sarba ta thā ga ta a bhi ṣiñca tu māṃ.
By this recitation they consent, and the five families
Confer empowerment with vases filled with nectar,
And the lord of the family marks the crown.
Oneself is marked by Akṣobhya Vajra.
For the four goddesses of the directions
Meditate on the four: Akṣobhya, Vairocana,
Ratnasambhava and Amitābha.
Likewise, by the four of aversion, delusion,
Avarice and desire,
Meditate on Pukkasī and the others in the four intermediate directions
As marked on the crown, in order.
oṃ sarba ta thā ga ta a bhi ṣe ka bajra sva bhā ba ātma ko'haṃ.

Now one should taste the nectar.
Meditate on yaṃ raṃ āḥ huṃ oṃ,
And bless with the three suchnesses:
Wind and fire, a hearth of skulls,
And the skull cup on which the seeds abide;
Purified by hūṃ, set ablaze by āḥ,
By oṃ it descends as wisdom nectar.
The five, bhrūṃ jrīṃ āṃ khaṃ hūṃ,
And the five, naṃ haṃ śaṃ ṣaṃ ghaṃ,
Having become nectar, are tasted with the vajra tongue,
And the circle is worshipped with bliss.
For that, make it steady with this mantra:
oṃ sarba ta thā ga ta a mṛ ta sva da bajra sva bhā ba ātma ko'haṃ.

Summoned by the seed hūṃ,
All the buddhas enter the mouth.
With the offering goddesses radiated outward,
They are worshipped in order, and the goddesses dissolve into their places.
oṃ sarba ta thā ga ta pū jā bajra sva bhā ba ā ko'haṃ.

Those goddesses, with the family consorts,
Praise in harmony with one voice:
"Blessed One, surrounded by eight goddesses,
With a heart of compassion you free beings.
The vessel of the world and its beings are the Blessed One's mind.
Blessed One, be pleased!
You who purify the aggregates, the elements, the sense bases
And the five poisons, we praise you."
oṃ sarba ta thā ga tastu ti bajra sva bhā ba ātma ko'haṃ.

With these steady and very clear,
The yogin should practice yoga.
The purity of the palace is Vairocana;
Passion is Vajrasattva;
The empowerment is the purity of Akṣobhya Vajra;
The tasting of nectar is Amitābha;
The worship is Amoghasiddhi;
And the praise is Ratnasambhava.
By meditating endowed with these six limbs,
Accomplishment will swiftly be attained.

First, it is like the sun among clouds;
Second, it appears like an illusion;
Attaining appearance, it is like a dream;
Luminosity has no equipoise and post-meditation.

When oneself has thus become stable,
Looking upon the suffering of beings,
The deities of the circle of loving-kindness and compassion
Radiate unceasingly in every direction;
Having purified their respective disciples,
They gather back, and one meditates on the circle within.
Arising from method and wisdom,
The awakening mind radiates and gathers.
All the colors, marks and attire
Meditate on clearly and steadily, just as they are.

Thus the generation is complete. In the completion stage,
Gather the goddesses in order into the faces.
The faces and arms, in order from the outermost,
Are gathered into the root face and arms;
The two legs dissolve into the consort,
And one seals with the seal of the four cakras.
At the navel, heart, throat and crown,
Meditate on the cakras of emanation, of Dharma, of enjoyment
And of great bliss;
These are the essence of the four bodies.

At its center aṃ blazes, and on the channel petals
The kāli, four and sixty, abide,

At the heart are eight channel petals;
At the center a black hūṃ, and on the petals
bhrūṃ jrīṃ āṃ khaṃ e vaṃ ma

At the throat, on sixteen petals,

At the crown are thirty-two channel petals;
At the center a white haṃ, and on the petals

raṃ blazes from below the navel;

From the purified cakras, all filled,

Meditate on the undefiled nectar of the ultimate.

Become accustomed for a very long time to non-abiding,
Free of stain like the center of the sky,
Without beginning, end or middle,
Neither saṃsāra nor nirvāṇa,
Where neither self nor other exists.
This is the king of great bliss,
Wholly free of grasped and grasper.

If one is weary of training the mind,
Generate the body stage by stage,
Father and mother both with faces and arms complete.
Around the seed letter at the heart

Recite as much as one wishes.
By the distinctions of meditation and color,

To gather the two accumulations, to bring others under one's power,
And to protect oneself and sentient beings,
To the gods, the Dharma protectors and sentient beings
Give nectar, offerings, gifts and torma.

"To bring others under one's power" renders a phrase whose second element in philosophical contexts means "the dependent nature"; in this ritual setting it is read as subjugation.
