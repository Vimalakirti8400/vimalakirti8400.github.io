The Means of Accomplishing Glorious Connate Kālacakra (Śrīkālacakrasahajasādhana)

The Means of Accomplishing Glorious Connate Kālacakra, in Sanskrit the Śrī Kālacakra Sahaja Sādhana and in Tibetan Pal Dükyi Khorlo Lhenchik Kyepa Drubpé Tap, is a short Indian ritual and meditation manual for the practice of Kālacakra in his connate form, united with his consort. Written largely in verse, it moves from preparing the site, offering torma and expelling obstructors, through the visualization of the elemental disks, the jeweled palace and the deity, to the inner yogas of the drop and the clear light, the stages of approach and accomplishment, mantra recitation, the midnight torma offering, and a closing verse that reads the six perfections in terms of this practice. Its colophon names its author as the master Kālapāda and records that it was translated into Tibetan by the great paṇḍita Buddhaśrījñāna and the translator Jampé Pal. It is Toh 1361 in the Tengyur, where it belongs among the tantra commentaries, in the group of works connected with the Kālacakra Tantra.

In the language of India: śrī kāla cakra saha ja sādhanaṃ. In the language of Tibet: The Means of Accomplishing Glorious Connate Kālacakra.

Homage to glorious Kālacakra.

Most terrifying one, protector of the world from fear,
Who keeps pure conduct in union with a woman,
In whom compassion and wisdom are inseparable:
Bowing to him, I write in brief how he is to be accomplished.

In a highly praised piece of ground,
The mantrin, with a mind most virtuous,
Having made the maṇḍala and the rest,
And having first given the torma,
Should expel the obstructors.

Then one imagines a multistoried vajra house arising from bhrūṃ. In its center, upon a lotus and a sun, light radiates from a brilliantly white hūṃ, summoning the buddhas and the rest, and one should worship them with every kind of offering.

Upon emptiness, wind and fire and water,
Earth and ocean, taste, and lotus,
And in the midst of moon, sun, and fire,
Meditate on a jeweled multistoried palace radiating light.

Upon the moon and sun,
Abiding on a seat of Rudra and Kāmadeva,
Holding vajra and bell, embracing,
White as a snowy mountain, together with his queen,
Adorned with the purity of the six perfections,
Possessing, since all his qualities are immeasurable,
The qualities of the pure immeasurables,
He is the embodiment of the five bodies of a buddha.

At the navel the buddhas, with their consorts, are burned;
At the crown the excellent moon lets fall a stream.
The two sources of phenomena joined, bearing the vajra:
So one should rightly meditate upon the three realms of existence.

From the equal union of vajra and lotus,
The drop of mind enters the lotus of the wisdom consort;
Through the power of that connate bliss
The indestructible clear light will be meditated.

Approach is the five nectars and the rest;
The four lords of the families,
Through mantra recitation and the rest,
Become individual withdrawal and the rest.
The nectar born from vajra and lotus
Is the near accomplishment.
Joy and the rest, the three vajras,
Having gone evenly into the lotus, become the accomplishment.
Not to let fall in union with the wisdom consort
Is the great accomplishment itself,
Through the yoga of the subtle.
Likewise, the others arise in other ways.

By resting one-pointedly in equipoise
Upon the supremely secret all-pervading lord,
One sees the common and uncommon signs:
Mirage, sky, and the rest.

oṃ kāla cakra āḥ hūṃ hoḥ svāhā
oṃ hraṃ hrīṃ hṛṃ hruṃ hrḷṃ hraḥ svāhā
oṃ vajra dhā tvi śva re āḥ hūṃ svāhā

When one tires of meditative absorption, one should do the recitation in modes such as the one called dolī. One should meditate on the suchness of the sun, the moon, time, taste, the ocean, fire, and the sky.

The torma should be given at midnight. Having blessed it with the letters of time and taste, one should give it with the ocean, the mudrā of the eye, and meditative absorption, using this mantra:

oṃ brahme viṣṇu nai ri tye pāyu yama agne samu treṃ īśva rendra yakṣe bhyaḥ sa pa ri vā re bhyaḥ idaṃ baliṃ gandhaṃ puṣpaṃ dhūpaṃ dīpaṃ akṣa taṃ dadāmahi te ja a ka tya sa pa ri vā re śīghāṃ idaṃ baliṃ gṛhṇa saṃ tṛpta hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ vajra dhara ājñā pa ya ti svāhā

Giving away the supreme treasure is generosity;
Not letting fall in union with the consort is moral discipline;
Not engaging with sound and the rest is patience;
Abandoning duality, free of laziness, is diligence;
Being wholly undistracted is supreme concentration;
The clear light of blissful union is wisdom.
Its fruit is inconceivable and marvelous:
It is what is called glorious Heruka.

Thus, by drawing out a little
Of this hidden essence of the Tantra of the Arising of Time,
For practitioners who rightly engage in union
The great fire of wisdom will blaze.

The Means of Accomplishing Glorious Connate Kālacakra, composed by the master Kālapāda, is complete. It was translated by the great paṇḍita Buddhaśrījñāna and the translator Jampé Pal.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

In the Kālacakra literature words such as sun, moon, time, taste, ocean, fire, sky and eye also serve as number words and can encode syllables or quantities; in the verse on the palace, the passage on the suchness to be meditated, and the blessing and offering of the torma, they are translated literally, and their coded sense is not resolved here. In the verse on the palace, "taste" (ro) may be a slip for "mountain" (ri, Mount Meru), which usually follows the element disks in this visualization. The extra syllable ma in "upon the moon and sun" is unclear. "Will be meditated" reads bsgom for the written bsgo. "When one tires" reads skyo na for the written skyon. Dolī is an unidentified term (mdo li) for a mode of recitation. "The Tantra of the Arising of Time" presumably refers to the Kālacakra Tantra.
