The Pacifier of Poison

The Pacifier of Poison, known in Tibetan as Dug zhi bar byed pa, is a very short dhāraṇī, a protective incantation meant to be recited to neutralize poisons of every kind. It opens with homage to Vasudhārā, here called the goddess of nectar medicine, and then gives a brief mantra followed by a declaration of truth: by the truth and splendor of the buddhas, the poisons of nāgas and snakes, concocted poisons, the poison of breath, the poison of roots, and the venom in the fangs of beasts of prey are all destroyed. Its closing line names it the dhāraṇī that pacifies all poisons, which marks it as a practical, healing and protective formula rather than a doctrinal discourse. It is Toh 1051 in the Kangyur, where it belongs among the dhāraṇī texts, the class of short ritual spells and invocations gathered in the canon for protection, healing, and blessing.

The Pacifier of Poison.

Homage to the goddess of nectar medicine, Vasudhārā.

tadyathā oṃ suru suruka vara varake viri hiri śuddhe viśuddhe svāhā

One should recite: "By the truth and splendor of the buddhas, the blessed ones, the poison of nāgas, the poison of snakes, concocted poison, the poison of breath, the poison of roots, and the poison of the fangs of beasts of prey are destroyed. The poison is utterly destroyed."

The dhāraṇī that pacifies all poisons is complete.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The words "the poison is utterly destroyed" are taken here as part of what is to be recited, since the instruction "one should recite" closes the passage. "Poison of breath" renders a phrase that may also mean poisonous vapor or exhalation.
