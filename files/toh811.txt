It opens with homage to the Three Jewels and then proceeds in verse: the speaker, moved by the boundless suffering of sentient beings and fearing that cyclic existence offers no lasting happiness, resolves to generate the mind of awakening in order to rescue beings from the lower realms and free them from the mental afflictions. The verses go on to make aspirations to become a refuge and protector for all who lack one, and to dedicate whatever virtue has been gathered, in this life and others, for the welfare of beings. Within the canon it belongs to the Kangyur, the collection of translated words of the Buddha, and its character is that of a practice text or ritual aspiration of the sort recited in the cultivation of bodhicitta, the altruistic intention at the heart of the bodhisattva path.

Homage to the Three Jewels.

For the sake of the boundless realm of sentient beings,
that their suffering may be fully pacified,
that they may be rescued from the lower realms,
and delivered from the mental afflictions,
fearing that existence brings no happiness,
and seeing the many kinds of suffering that there are,
in order to protect them completely from these,
I generate the mind of awakening.

May I always be a refuge
for all sentient beings in need.
May I be a protector for those without protectors,
a support for those without support,
a refuge for those without refuge,
one who turns suffering into happiness.
May I bring about the pacification
of the mental afflictions of all sentient beings.

Whatever virtuous actions
I have gathered, in this life and in others,
may they become the accumulation
called merit and wisdom, the two kinds of accumulation.

Whatever diligent effort I undertake
through the gateway of the six perfections,
may that too bring benefit
to all sentient beings without exception.

Whatever, however slight, I do
to extend the limits of their lives,
in short, I strive in order that all the afflictions
of all sentient beings may be pacified
and that they may be liberated,
and I joyfully exert myself for awakening.

Then, having generated the awakening mind in its fullness,
in accordance with the ritual, in every respect,
I should cultivate compassion
and loving-kindness toward all sentient beings.

In order to train the mind well,
by various methods, through the six recollections,
with an inner self at peace,
I should meditate single-pointedly.

Just as the former sons of the Victors
made their aspirations,
so too I, with a virtuous mind,
make aspirations in just the same way.

Settled in meditative equipoise
upon the various riches of the Dharma, I rejoice.
May all sentient beings be happy,
at peace, and free from illness.

May I be capable in all that I do,
and may I possess good qualities as well.
May I be wealthy, generous, courageous, and wise,
patient, and first to take up virtue.

And in all the lives of sentient beings,
may I remember my births and be endowed with love.
