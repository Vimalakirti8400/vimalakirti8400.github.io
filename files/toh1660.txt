The Ornament of Union: A Commentary on the Union with All Buddhas, the Saṃvara of the Ḍākinī's Illusion (Sarvabuddhasamāyogaḍākinīmāyāsaṃvaravṛtti Samāyogālaṃkāra)

The Ornament of Union, known in Sanskrit as the Sarvabuddhasamāyogaḍākinīmāyāsaṃvaravṛtti Samāyogālaṃkāra and in Tibetan as Sangye Tamché Dang Nyampar Jorwa Khandroma Gyuma Dechok gi Drelpa Nyamjor gyi Gyen, is a commentary on the Union with All Buddhas, the Saṃvara of the Ḍākinī's Illusion, an early tantra of the great yoga class. Opening with homage to Vajraḍākinī and to Vajrasattva as supreme bliss, it explains the tantra chapter by chapter for yogins who seek the suchness of union with all buddhas, treating its maṇḍalas, mudrās, the deities of the six families, the four joys, and the meditation of calm abiding and insight that culminates in great bliss and luminosity. Its closing colophon attributes the work to Pramuditavajra, who says he wrote it because the tantra had not yet been fully and clearly explained. It is Toh 1660 in the Tengyur, where it belongs among the commentaries on the tantras.

In the language of India: sarbba buddha sa ma yo ga ḍā ki nī mā yā sambā ra vṛtti sa ma yo gā laṃ kā ra nā ma. In the language of Tibet: A Commentary on the Union with All Buddhas, the Saṃvara of the Ḍākinī's Illusion, called The Ornament of Union.

Homage to the glorious Vajraḍākinī.

You whose nature is the very being of all buddhas,
Vajrasattva, supreme bliss,
Illusion Saṃvara, to you I bow,
and explain the Union with All Buddhas.

First of all, so that yogins who set out to accomplish their own aims and the aims of others, without clinging to anything whatsoever, may come to understand the suchness of union with all buddhas, this exposition of the meaning of the tantra is undertaken.

What, then, is its subject matter? What is its purpose? What is its connection? And what is the purpose of its purpose? I shall explain. In order to teach the root tantra, the later tantra and the later-later tantra, the tantra of the cause, the tantra of the method and the tantra of the result are the subject matter; and the root tantra of ten sections, the later tantra of seven sections and the later-later tantra of five sections are what express it. As for the connection, between the subject matter and what expresses it the relation is one of identity, while between the subject matter and the purpose the relation is that the one arises from the other. The purpose is that persons who wish to practice, who would take up the accomplishments, and who would benefit others come to understand it. The purpose of the purpose is the attainment of the common, worldly accomplishments and of the supreme accomplishment.

Having thus explained the meaning of the purpose, the summary meaning is this: the explanation, together with their ancillary practices, of how to accomplish the six sections that bring one's own aims and the aims of others to perfection; the explanation of the ninth section of the later tantra, which accomplishes the supreme aim; and, through the tantra of questions and answers, the explanation of the practice of the root tantra and of the later tantra, and of the accomplishments and the rites for attaining them. Because the yogin, with a nondual mind and mental factors endowed with such a summary meaning, is perfectly joined in equality with the minds of the tathāgatas of the ten directions and the three times, as numerous as the finest particles of the buddha fields, it is the tantra called the Union with All Buddhas.

Having explained the summary meaning together with the name, I shall now explain the meaning of its parts. Having praised the Blessed One, the Ḍāka Saṃvara, made offerings to him and paid homage, they made this request:

Protector, we entreat you:
for the sake of all sentient beings,
emanate through illusion the yoga of Saṃvara,
and make us victorious over all afflictions.

Immediately upon this request, the Blessed One spoke:

The being who is the reality of all buddhas,
Vajrasattva, supreme bliss,
in the delight of the supreme secret,
the self of all, abides forever.
This is the self-arisen Blessed One,
the sole deity, great in his very self,
the Union with All Buddhas,
the Ḍākinī's Illusion, supreme bliss.

Thus the Teacher himself spoke the introduction. Here one may ask: Who is the Teacher? What is the place? With what retinue? At what time? And what Dharma was taught? The Teacher is the embodiment of the three bodies. His nature as the nirmāṇakāya is what is meant by "the being who is the reality of all buddhas," for he embodies the six universal monarchs. The saṃbhogakāya is what is called "Vajrasattva, supreme bliss." The place is what is called "in the delight of the supreme secret." The retinue is "the self of all." The time is what is called "abides forever." The dharmakāya, then, is what is called "This is the self-arisen Blessed One, the sole deity, great in his very self": that is, the sole great deity whose nature is the nonduality of the mind's self-awareness and luminosity. The Dharma that is taught is what is called "the Union with All Buddhas, the Ḍākinī's Illusion, supreme bliss."

If all this was already directly evident to the retinue of the five perfections, why was the introduction spoken? It was spoken to the bodhisattvas Kṣitigarbha, Avalokiteśvara and Mañjuśrī for the sake of those in the future who wish to engage in the conduct by way of secret mantra, so that they might meditate on form at the lesser level, on mantra at the secret level, and on the Dharma at the ultimate level. These three points should be looked up in the commentary on Cakrasaṃvara.

Having thus spoken the introduction, now, wishing to teach how the knowable abides by way of the three, cause, method and result, he teaches the first chapter, called Entering the Domain of the Sublime Appearance of Suchness:

It is not desire, nor is it free from desire;
nor does it abide in the middle way.

This teaches that the very nature of the tantra of the method is free from the eighty natural conceptions. If one wonders by what that method is realized, the text says:

The illusory net of all women.

"All women" are the action seal, the Dharma seal, the pledge seal and the great seal. The "illusory net" is the assemblage of causes that makes calm abiding and insight directly manifest. If one asks whether that alone is the one vehicle, the text says:

This is the supreme nondual vehicle.

That is, it is the assemblage of causes and conditions in which method and wisdom are nondual. If one asks what those causes and conditions are like, the text says:

Of all illusions whatsoever,
the illusion of woman is most exalted;
through the power of essential nature
it is accomplished by its very nature.

"All illusions" are the assemblages of causes and conditions for the feeling of suffering, for that of equanimity and for that of happiness; this seal, by its very nature, is especially exalted above those causes and conditions. If one asks how, without habituation, calm abiding and insight can be directly accomplished, the text says, "through the power of essential nature." The words "among the assemblages of causes and conditions that make calm abiding and insight directly manifest, it is accomplished by the very nature of the seal" mean that, like an alchemical elixir that turns things into gold, the meditative absorption of meditative concentration and undefiled wisdom are accomplished through the power of the assemblage of the seal's causes and conditions.

Next, the result of the three seals is explained:

Here in these three worlds, through women,
they come to be held in reverence.

That is, through the three, the great seal, the pledge seal and the Dharma seal, although one is not yet free from the stains of the three worlds, they bring forth the directly experienced bliss and emptiness, and so they are held in reverence. As the root tantra says:

It swiftly brings forth accomplishment,
and at once inspires conviction.

The result of the fourth, the action seal, is stated in the words:

Through gain, through bliss, and through every joy,

"Gain" is joy, and supreme bodily bliss; "bliss" is coemergent joy, mental bliss. "Every joy" means through the meditative absorption of the three joys.

Even women of base conduct will bring accomplishment.

This means that accomplishment comes even with lowly women of base conduct, women other than the goddesses sanctioned by purification, by empowerment, by the hero and by authorization. If one asks how, then, this comes about for a beginner, the text says:

If one's own nature is wholly transformed,
it is accomplished through the illusion of all women.

Transforming one's own nature means that, having caused the wisdom being to enter, the nature of the ordinary woman is transformed, and it is then accomplished through the illusion of all women, such as Buddhalocanā, Māmakī, Pāṇḍaravāsinī and Tārā. If one asks why it says, "In a human body, why Buddhalocanā?" and so on, the text says:

This wondrous illusory mudrā
is indicated by the convention "ḍākinī."

That is, just as with the sages of the non-Buddhists (tīrthikas), or with the meditative concentration of the absorptions that serve as causes in the Vehicle of Characteristics, the wisdom being descends upon a human woman, and since she has become, how wondrously, an illusory action mudrā, she is indicated by the convention of the buddhas and bodhisattvas as "ḍākinī."

She is "one who goes in the sky":
so it is construed from the verbal root.

Here, the verbal root of the word ḍā ki ni is its has ga ma, which means "going in the sky." The sky is one: it is the sky of nonconceptual calm abiding and the sky of insight, free from apprehended and apprehender as one. Going there means that one goes by means of the meditative absorption of the bliss of calm abiding and the nonconceptual wisdom of insight. Therefore it is said:

Accomplished in moving throughout the sky,
she is widely renowned as "ḍākinī."

All, without exception, through the seal:

these are the action seal, the Dharma seal, the pledge seal and the great seal.

All, without exception, through Saṃvara:

this is knowing the methods of the Dharma seal, the pledge seal and the great seal.

She who unites with all buddhas:

this is meditating on union by joining in equality the four methods and the four wisdoms.

She is widely renowned as "ḍākinī":

these are the emptiness of the great seal; the pledge seal, which possesses the heat of wind and fire; the Dharma seal, which is the five qualities of sensual pleasure; and the action seal, which is the ḍākinīs born of wisdom, born of mantra and born of the sacred fields.

She is called "ḍākinī,"
construed from the root of awakening.

This is the language of the gods of the Pure Abodes. The mind of calm abiding and insight, which has become the convention of the buddhas and bodhisattvas, is called "ḍākinī."

Accomplished in going everywhere:

the great seal, whose nature is the Dharma, is the ḍākinī of all buddhas.

śaṃ is explained as bliss:

this is undefiled bliss, the great bliss that is the nature of the wisdom of all buddhas and bodhisattvas.

Union of all illusions,
supreme bliss of supreme bliss:

this is the illusion of the wisdom of bliss, in which the bliss of calm abiding and the bliss of insight come together directly: the supreme bliss of calm abiding, and the supreme undefiled bliss of insight.

Thus, by explaining the name "the Ḍākinī's Illusion, supreme bliss, which unites with all buddhas" together with its meaning, the tantra of the method, which brings understanding, has been taught.

Everywhere, from every side, all:

these words and those that follow explain the meaning of union with the whole of the vessel and its contents. "Everywhere" means at all times. "From every side" means in all directions. "All" means in all things. "Always" means in all places and at all times. "In every way" means in all circumstances. From "buddhas" onward are the four root vidyā-mantras. The words "and so on" refer to the families such as vajra and vajra-holder. The stable is the celestial palace, and the moving is the circle of deities.

I have become the reality of all.

This shows that the very nature of great bliss pervades both peace and existence. Having thus taught in brief, the extensive teaching says:

Mind and the meditative absorption of mind.

Mind is reckoned as the eight collections of consciousness. Mental factors are set out as the fifty-one formations. Meditative absorption is the meditative concentrations and the formless absorptions.

The very accumulation of the conduct of awakening:

this is from entering the womb up to the conduct of awakening.

Likewise Māra and victory over Māra:

having tamed Māra at dusk, he awakened as a buddha at dawn.

That is the awakening of the buddha:

that which pervades all is itself the nature of the buddhas of the four families. Vajra is the family of Vajrasattva. Vajra-holder is the family of Heruka. Lotus is the family of Padmanarteśvara. Lotus-bearer is the action family. Jewel is the family of Vajraratna. Jewel-holder is the family of Vairocana. Within that, "desire" means having the form of a householder, and "free from desire" means having the form of a self-arisen one. "Desire" is worldly accomplishment, and "liberation" is the supreme accomplishment.

Having now explained the union of the buddhas and bodhisattvas, the text explains union with all buddhas in the state in which the mind's self-aware luminosity is impure, beginning with the words:

The three times, existence, the three supreme ones.

The three worlds are above the earth, beneath the earth and upon the earth. The three realms are the desire realm, the form realm, the formless realm and so on.

Various and different aspects:

this is all the bases, such as the aggregates, the elements and the sense sources.

All beings without exception:

these are the six classes of beings.

This becomes all things:

this is the mind of luminosity.

These and others, endless and supreme:

this is the nature of the whole of the vessel and its contents.

The dharmadhātu, equal and unequal:

unequal as illusion-like, and equal as luminosity.

The boundless reaches of the expanse of space:

this is all things that are the domain of the buddha's wisdom.

Glorious in every region of space:

having broken open the gold of emptiness upon the great seal, then, by the instruction of Indrabhūti, like a crescent moon, like a frolicking lion, or like a window, Vajrasattva, the Tathāgata, the union with all buddhas, is called "the Ḍākinī's Illusion, supreme in union."

Having thus explained that the nature of great bliss is the nature of Vajrasattva, in order now to explain the method for accomplishing it, the text says:

For countless tens of millions of eons.

This states a promise to explain the assemblages of causes of the four seals by which buddhahood, which the Vehicle of Characteristics does not attain even in countless eons, is attained in this very life.

The buddha himself, supreme bliss:

by these words is meant that, having made the bliss of calm abiding directly manifest through the three seals, and having thoroughly accomplished the great seal, bliss and emptiness, one attains the supreme bliss of the eleventh ground, the very nature of all buddhas.

Having thus explained the completion stage, the generation stage is explained. The text says:

The mind that is not the genuine yoga.

In the six sections:

The minds of the respective generation stages,
which take the deities as their focus:

these are the deities of the maṇḍalas of the six chapters.

The yoga whose nature is a reflected image:

this means that if, through the yoga that follows the reflected image of the nirmāṇakāya, one meditates on a cast image as the body of the being, signs of seeing, hearing and touching will arise.

Having thus explained the yoga of the beginner, the meditative absorption of the yogin who has become supreme is taught in the words beginning:

The yogin of genuine and supreme mind.

That is, the supreme yogin who has obtained the signs and who takes the deities of the nirmāṇakāya as his focus becomes, through the practice of the pledge of his own dharmadhātu, unchanging in the supreme great seal, and he accomplishes the sublime perfection of the aims of others.

If one asks why meditating on form alone does not become the supreme accomplishment: reflected images, forms and the like do not bring forth the yoga; but when one fully undertakes the suchness of the deity of the awakening mind, great bliss, the yogin becomes a buddha and a bodhisattva.

The bodhisattva is vajra:

this is the vajra of the mind's luminosity, which is nondual, and it is the nature of all buddhas. Therefore buddhahood is attained also by all yogins joining themselves to the nature of all deities.

Having taught this, the extensive explanation says:

I myself am all buddhas,
and I am all heroes.

That is, the self that is the mind's luminosity is the very nature of all buddhas, of the heroes, and of all beings.

Therefore, joining oneself to the deity:

by these words, by joining oneself to the nature of the deity, in which illusion and emptiness are of one taste, one should join oneself to the nature of the deity. For that reason the five aggregates will be accomplished in this very life as all the buddhas, as the heroes and all the beings, and as all the vajra-holders of the saṃbhogakāya.

The commentary on the first section.

Having thus explained the section that chiefly teaches suchness, now the many methods whose nature is Vajrasattva are explained, in the words beginning:

This is the yoga, the bha ga vān.

The words "Vajrasattva, the Tathāgata" and so on have already been explained. If one asks what that yoga is like, the text says:

By joining oneself with Vajrasattva,
every descent will be accomplished.

That is, if one generates oneself as the deity through the six sections, invites the wisdom being, and brings down the wisdom of all body, speech and mind, then, like an alchemical elixir that turns things into gold, the body, speech and mind of a buddha will be accomplished. Therefore this is union with all buddhas by the path of blessing: by it, the great lord of yoga of the tantra of the cause, through the path of blessing of the tantra of the method, by bliss itself attains all the bliss of buddhahood, the result.

Having thus explained meditation, conduct is explained. The text says:

Union with all buddhas.

This says that once one has meditated on the conduct of union, in this yoga of the method, ablutions, vows and the like are not needed. The rest is easy to understand.

Having explained conduct, the observance is explained in the words beginning:

Standing and sitting.

That is, when yogins, in the four modes of conduct, seal themselves with the pride of the deity of the six sections and practice the observance of the deity, then even if they have not entered the wisdom maṇḍala set before them, a yogin who meditates on himself as his own deity will succeed, even if his merit is slight.

Having thus taught the observance, suchness is explained in the words beginning:

By this yoga of suchness.

That is, when, having obtained the fourth empowerment from the maṇḍala of the dharmadhātu, one meditates on suchness, the yogin who through unelaborated conduct makes everything, whether virtuous, nonvirtuous or morally neutral, into the nature of great bliss will not be harmed by any fault. And the grounds which the Vehicle of Characteristics does not attain in countless eons: if one accomplishes, by the path of the buddhas and bodhisattvas, the great seal that is directly perceived, accomplishment will be attained.

Having explained meditation on suchness, now, to teach the practice performed while wearing a mask, the text says, beginning:

Union with all buddhas.

That is, if one wears a mask joined in likeness to the garb of a buddha and accomplishes the circle of deities through the six sections, all the buddhas will bless one and gather there. Therefore all buddhas will be accomplished.

Having explained the wearing of the mask, in order to explain the feast gathering the text says, beginning:

Whoever, having seen this hidden intent,
makes offerings in every way.

That is, when, with the reflected image of the contrived yoga, one meditates on the circle of one's own deity of the sections and shows devotion in every way with the five qualities of sensual pleasure, and the yogin makes himself inseparable from the wisdom being, then he sees the buddha and has made pū ja.

To this one and to all buddhas:

that is, by making pū ja to this yogin himself and to all buddhas with what is seen, forms, what is heard, sounds, what is smelled, odors, what is tasted, flavors, and with tangibles and the like, one will see the wisdom of the bliss of the first ground, even without having entered a maṇḍala of colored powders.

Its benefits are taught in the words beginning:

By seeing and by touching.

That is, for the yogin who travels the path, accomplishment comes even through laughing, gazing and holding hands.

The path of passion is taught in the words beginning:

This one, toward all buddhas.

"This one" refers to the yogins and yoginīs who meditate on the feast gathering. "Toward all buddhas" means that through the union of the wisdom being with the yoga, even the goddesses of purification, of empowerment, of the heroes and of authorization by command become fully liberated and touch awakening.

Having thus explained the path of passion, the conduct of the feast gathering is explained in the words beginning:

Everywhere, from every side, in all things.

That is, in the four modes of conduct, in the main practice of turning the circle of deities and in what accords with it, the yogin who has obtained the signs always meditates, in all places and at all times, on the nature of all things, and so attains awakening through all of them.

Having explained the conduct, the meditation of the four yogas is explained in the words beginning:

Since the self of this abides in all.

That is, having practiced the yoga of oneself as Vajrasattva, one generates as goddesses the three kinds of forms, pleasing, displeasing and the rest, and the three pleasing ones, form, sound, smell, taste and touch; and through pū ja na with all the clouds of pū ja, one attains supreme bliss.

Having thus explained what the yogin needs, the yoga of all is explained in the words beginning:

By closely applying every yoga.

That is, one generates the whole of the three realms as the nature of deities, and with the bliss of enjoyment with all the yoginīs, embracing, kissing and the like, one should make abundant pū ja. In the subsequent yoga, accomplishment comes through pū ja na with the bliss of uniting the circle of method and the circle of wisdom. The utmost yoga is taught in the words beginning:

Of the nature of all buddhas.

That is, having sealed bliss with emptiness, one accomplishes the bliss of the ra sa ya na of all buddhas, and so accomplishes the supreme bliss of union with all buddhas.

The commentary on the second section.

Having thus explained the many methods, the profound is now explained in the words beginning "the yoga, the Blessed One." The sense is that this is the profound union with all buddhas.

The yogin of Vajrasattva:

this is the tantra of the cause; and

The glorious, excellent, virtuous pledge:

this is the union that, through union with the seal, makes calm abiding and insight directly manifest.

Through the union of Vajrasattva:

with these words and those that follow, it is said that through union with the four seals of the delusion family, great bliss is accomplished in each one individually by the very power of the assemblage of dependent origination.

The explanation of the pledge of Heruka:

Through the nature of Samantabhadrī:

the four seals: if each of the four mudrās, which have the very power of dependent origination, is sealed with bliss and emptiness, they are accomplished as bliss and emptiness. The explanation of the pledge of Padmanarteśvara: bliss and emptiness are accomplished individually in the four seals that have the nature of Pāṇḍaravāsinī. The explanation of the pledge of Vajrasūrya: by sealing with emptiness the four seals that have the nature of Māmakī, great bliss is accomplished in each individually. The explanation of the pledge of Paramāśva: the words "the union of glorious Vajrasattva" mean that by the very power of the assemblage of causes that makes meditative concentration and the undefiled vow directly manifest, great bliss is accomplished individually from the two seals that have the nature of Tārā, and then the six worldly accomplishments are accomplished, with rejoicing.

Having thus explained the seals of the individual deities, the Dharma seal common to the six families is taught in the words:

Its pledge arises.

That is, whatever is the pledge of the six methods, which is the nature of wisdom, arises.

The pledge of the lovely one follows me:

"the lovely one" is the goddess; "the pledge" is the self-appearing Dharma seal; "follows me" means that her body color accords with that of the method. "Thorough union" is knowing it to be like an illusion. "Great union" is knowing it to be of the nature of luminosity.

Supreme pledge, the self of all:

these are the six self-appearing goddesses, whose nature is the dharmadhātu.

Practicing as one, just like one:

this is generating oneself as god and goddess through the four stages of approach and accomplishment. "Practicing together" is the two joys. "Subsequently lovely" is supreme joy.

The Ḍākinī's Illusion blazes supremely:

this is coemergent joy.

The seals of all joys without exception,
all of them, are great bliss:

this is the wisdom of great bliss. "Section, pledge goddess" refers to the nirmāṇakāya in female form, such as Buddhalocanā.

And the union of Vajrasattva:

the three joys, free from the eighty natural conceptions, and the knowing of bliss as emptiness, are the union of Vajrasattva. "All the goddesses assembled" are the goddesses of the feast gathering.

All the fortunate ones without exception:

these are those born of wisdom, those born of the sacred fields, and those born of mantra.

Bliss accomplished in each individually:

this is making calm abiding and insight directly manifest in those goddesses.

Having thus explained the Dharma seal, the great seal is explained. The text says:

Sealed with this seal.

That is, if the yogin of Vajrasattva meditates having sealed his own mudrā with great bliss, he becomes the lord of desire, holding sway over all the goddesses to the boundless reaches of the realm of space. If one wonders how this comes about, the text says:

All are intoxicated with the bliss of yoga.

That is, being intoxicated with the bliss of the wisdom intention toward all fortunate yoginīs, even though he enters into union, there is no wrongdoing.

With the face of power held high:

this means "directly, through its power." Because he is free from the eighty natural conceptions there is no wrongdoing, and because calm abiding and insight arise directly, his face is held high. The yogin of Vajrasattva, having attained the supreme accomplishment, performs the activities of the accomplishment of the lord of desire.

The benefit of accomplishing the great seal of Vairocana is taught in the words beginning:

Arising from all the senses.

That is, he comes to be honored with pū ja through the five qualities of sensual pleasure of the world systems.

The great seal of Heruka is taught in the words beginning:

All the bliss of yoga.

That is, through the empowerment of great bliss, it is the empowerment in which Heruka is of one taste in the essence of the dharmadhātu. It is explained thus:

he arises from great bliss;
ru is the precious one, free from attachment;
ka purifies utter delusion:
the empowerment of all bliss.

The great seal of Padmanarteśvara is taught in the words:

In the enjoyment of all goddesses.

These are the goddesses born of the sacred fields, of mantra and of karma. Having shown them as they are by way of the four joys, one meditates on that as emptiness.

The great seal of Vajrasūrya is taught in the words:

With all yoga and with bliss.

That is, through the conduct in which method and wisdom are nondual, one experiences great bliss at all times by way of the two yogas.

The great seal of Paramāśva is taught in the words beginning:

Through the self of all buddhas.

That is, through that bliss of the ra sa ya na of the four joys, Vajrasattva will be accomplished.

Having thus explained the six great seals, what is taught of them here is five: body, speech, mind, pū ja and qualities. In brief, these five are the nature of Vajradhara as mind, of Lokeśvara as speech, and of Ratneśvara as body.

If one takes delight with them:

that is, if one practices meditative absorption through the two joys with those goddesses of the five or the three families, then one attains in this very life the accomplishment of a king of the gods, which is called "going and flying as one wishes," and the supreme accomplishment of the buddhas and bodhisattvas, which is called "equal and unequal with the dharmadhātu."

This teaches the following. One may take up accomplishment after obtaining the signs in the maṇḍala of colored powders of the generation stage; or, in the completion stage, one may accomplish it through the maṇḍal of the body, of the bha ga and of the dharmadhātu, through the feast gathering, and through the two, the peaceful drink and the purity of the messengers. In either case, the means of accomplishment are the first path, the Appearance of Suchness; the second, the Many Methods; and the third, the Profound. Having taken up accomplishment through these, the aim of beings is what is called Enlightened Activity.

The commentary on the third section.

Having thus taught the profound, the supreme accomplishment, intended for those of sharp faculties, is explained in the words beginning:

This is the yoga, the Blessed One.

That is, this yoga intended for those of sharp faculties is the Saṃvara of union with all buddhas.

Union with all goddesses:

this is union with the seal.

Desiring the supremely fortunate:

this is the empowered person's desire for accomplishment.

Experiencing bliss through perfect union:

this is experiencing the undefiled bliss of the meditative absorption of the four joys, free from the eighty natural conceptions.

Desire, enduring and permanent:

"desire" is undefiled bliss. "Enduring" means that the object experienced through direct perception is realized just so and alone. And since there is no past that has ceased and no future that has not yet arisen, it is permanent.

Its conduct is explained in the words beginning:

In the great form of all buddhas.

This is enjoying undefiled forms, sounds, smells, tastes and tangibles.

The buddha accomplished in the perfections:

that is, if even the accomplishment of three countless eons is attained through the perfections, the dhāraṇīs and the grounds, what need is there to speak of attaining it through the fortunate women born among gods and humans, those born of wisdom, of mantra and of the sacred fields?

The accomplishment of the gods, and joy:

this is worldly accomplishment.

The awakening of a buddha, great bliss:

this is the supreme accomplishment.

The fortunate one, bliss and emptiness:

that is, through the desire for undefiled bliss, undefiled wisdom will be accomplished by means of each bliss of the seal.

Having thus taught in brief, the extensive explanation follows. The conduct of Vajrasattva for the beginner is taught in the words beginning:

Arising from all things.

That is, one should practice with the four seals that accord with the family of Samantabhadrī.

The conduct of Heruka:

The supreme accomplishment through the vajra-bearer:

this is generating undefiled bliss with the four seals that accord with the family of the wrathful Dhātvīśvarī.

The conduct of Padmanarteśvara is taught in the words:

The hero of the supreme lord of the world.

That is, one meditates on bliss with the four seals that accord with Pāṇḍaravāsinī.

The conduct of Vairocana is taught in the words beginning "sublime bliss in the buddhas," that is, one meditates on bliss with the four seals that accord with the family of the buddha seal.

The conduct of Vajrasūrya is taught in the words beginning:

In all the three realms without exception.

That is, one meditates on bliss with the four seals that accord with the family of Māmakī.

The conduct of Paramāśva is taught in the words beginning:

The great accomplishment of all buddhas.

That is, one meditates on bliss with the four seals that accord with Tārā.

Having thus explained the wisdom goddesses of the supreme accomplishment, the meditative-concentration goddesses of worldly accomplishment are explained in the words beginning:

From the Mighty One, the Steadfast Auspicious One.

"The Mighty One" is Viṣṇu. "The Steadfast Auspicious One" is his consort (Lakṣmī). "From the Ravisher" refers to Maheśvara (Śiva). "The Accomplished One" is the goddess Umā. "From the Wicked One" refers to the god of desire (Kāmadeva). "Delight" is his consort (Rati).

Having seized them by the power of the face held high:

that is, having summoned them directly, one should enjoy them oneself, and give them to the faithful for holding the mind, for empowerment and for performing the feast gathering.

Having thus taught the accomplishment of the precious queen, the accomplishment of kingship is taught in the words beginning:

Kingship over all the three worlds without exception.

That is, having seized all the enjoyments of the lord of the three realms, one enjoys them oneself and gives them to the faithful.

Having taught the accomplishment of kingship, the accomplishment of lordship is taught in the words beginning:

Of Brahmā, Viṣṇu and Mahendra.

That is, having seized the lordship of the kings of the gods, one enjoys it oneself and also gives it to the faithful.

Having thereby taught worldly accomplishment, the transworldly accomplishment is taught in the words beginning:

All that is to be done for the sake of the buddhas.

That is, there is nothing that the yogin who, having sealed with great bliss, meditates on the great seal cannot do.

If even the wisdom mantras of the buddhas and bodhisattvas are seized by the three methods of tantra and yantra and given to others, what need is there to mention worldly accomplishments? Through the power of having sealed with great bliss, one attains the supreme accomplishment, conquers all the Māras, gains the great accomplishment of perfect wisdom, and fully accomplishes the state of a universal monarch (cakravartin) as well.

That emanated universal monarchs abide in all the world-systems is taught in some places in the words beginning:

The great bodhisattva.

That is, it is the accomplishment of the methods for taming those to be tamed.

The fruit of the saṃbhogakāya is taught by this, in the words beginning:

Supreme among all great accomplishments.

That is: the form of a god of the fourth dhyāna, and the life of a body inconceivably transformed; the coloring of the thirty-two major marks and the eighty excellent minor marks; the powers, the ten powers and the rest; the undefiled radiance that is the nature of Vajrasattva; being attended upon by all good beings; and making offerings to all the tathāgatas.

If one wonders whether that saṃbhogakāya is necessarily single: Vajrasattva abides in the center of all four goddesses; in the manner of a wheel of the six families he seals the bodhisattvas; the worldly gods and the bodhisattvas of definitive goodness also give to sentient beings; and for as long as saṃsāra remains, for many eons, he continually enters into existence for the sake of sentient beings.

The commentary on the fourth section is complete.

Having thus explained the chapter on the methods of accomplishing the siddhis, which teaches the suchness of accomplishing all the rites in common, and which is set forth with regard to many methods, to profundity, and to those of sharp faculties, now the rites specific to the various kinds of disciples are taught in the words beginning:

This is the yoga, Bhagavān.

That is, in order to tame persons of great attachment, this yoga of Vajrasattva is what is taught by the Union with All Buddhas of all the tathāgatas, the vajra-accomplishment rite of twenty-one deities, the Saṃvara of the Ḍākinī's Illusion. And this is a single teaching: it is explained as the emanation body of the perfectly complete Buddha who appeared in Jambudvīpa in the past; and with regard to the yogins who follow after, it teaches, after meditation on the wheel of images, the accomplishments of the beginner, the supreme yogin's taking up of accomplishment, and the maṇḍala for conferring empowerment on persons who benefit others.

The sequence of the way of the emanation body that appeared in Jambudvīpa in the past is taught in the words:

The single one, perfectly accomplished.

That is, having awakened in Akaniṣṭha, he attained the dharmakāya.

Applied just as one wishes.

That is, at the fifth awakening, it is applied to the whole host of rites that appear as the causal Vajrasattva of the saṃbhogakāya, or the causal Vairocana, or the causal Vajrasūrya, or the causal Padmanarteśvara, or the causal Heruka, or the causal Paramāśva.

The Dharma of the supreme, sublime accomplishment.

By this, the supreme, sublime, undefiled path accomplished in the past on the path of the buddhas is taught right there; and, as it appears in the Saṃpuṭa Tilaka, through the moon, the single moon, the vajra, the entering of the elements, and the transformation into the body, one should meditate on all six rites at the time of awakening in Akaniṣṭha.

Through the application of the various paths.

By this: the buddhas of the ten directions, abiding in Akaniṣṭha, bestow blessing, confer empowerment, and establish him in samādhi and in enlightened activity.

And the taming of beings who wish for the various rites.

That is, in order to tame the disciples of the six rites, those with great attachment, with delusion, with aversion, with wrong view, with avarice, and with great laziness, and in order to accomplish the bliss of the six wisdoms. As for "Bhagavān," the fruit of the emanation body in Sukhāvatī in the past: the sixth, Vajradhara himself, emanates the magical displays of the buddhas for the sake of sentient beings, and through devotion to the four mudrās, accomplishment comes swiftly. By what method is it swift? It is accomplished by causing the wisdom being to enter through the path of blessing.

Having thus explained the deeds of the buddhas that took place in the past, the teaching for those who follow after is given in the words:

Every day, or else every month,
Or else likewise every year,
Having blessed it as is proper,
One should perform the dance of Buddha Saṃvara.

That is, having done the approach by meditating on the four branches of approach and accomplishment, one should draw a maṇḍala kept for one day, or one kept for one month, or one kept for one year. "Having blessed it as is proper" means that the yogin who performs the maṇḍala, once he has generated it through the stages of lesser, middling and great approach and accomplishment as will be explained below, should perform the pūjā and meditate on the drama of Buddha Saṃvara.

Or else, if one does not wish to meditate on the maṇḍala of samādhi, accomplishment by putting on the attire is taught in the words beginning:

With all the substances of the minerals.

That is, gold, silver, copper, iron and the like.

Likewise with what comes from living beings and from plants.

That is, with things made of ivory and horn, which come from living creatures, and with wood, which comes from plants, carved in relief, one should make the attire of one's own deity. What is it like? This is stated in the words:

The mudrā of accomplishment, perfectly.

That is, one should make them in the forms of Vajrasattva, Vairocana, Heruka, Vajrasūrya, Padmanarteśvara and Paramāśva.

Whatever maṇḍala is taught,
With offerings of the various mudrās.

These words mean: whichever wheel of the six rites is taught for the yogins of the six families, whether householders or renunciants, men or women. With whom, then, is one to meditate? It is taught in the words:

With the whole assembly of disciples, in each place,
Having made offerings, accomplish it.

That is, together with the assembly of disciples who have received empowerment, and wearing masks, one should perform the feast gathering.

Having thus explained the tantra of what took place in the past and of those who follow after, now the introductory account of the past origins of all the rites is taught in the words:

Then, further, of all the rites,
the six families, emanations of all the buddhas, are to be emanated,
The accounts I shall fully explain.

If one asks who the Teacher was, in what place, together with whom, and at what time it was heard, it is said:

The Teacher, the being who is the very reality of all buddhas,
Vajrasattva, the supreme of bliss.

This is the essential nature of the saṃbhogakāya and the nirmāṇakāya. The place is in the delight of the supreme secret. The retinue is the very self of all. The time is that he abides always. The nature of the dharmakāya that abides there is stated in the words:

This is the yoga, the Blessed One,
Permanent, sublime and steadfast,
Always as though it had come about just now,
By its own nature hard to transgress.

The Dharma that is taught is:

For those who wish for the various rites,
Through various applications of the rites of action,
The buddhas, Vajradhara and the rest,
Were made for the sake of those to be tamed.

This explains the emanation of the conceived forms of the wheel of the six families, and the conceived sounds.

The buddhas and the rest, all that is stable and moving,
This becomes the substance of them all.

By this, the container world is generated as the celestial palace, and the world of its contents as deities.

The supreme aims of all sentient beings
Are accomplished through the taming done by the buddhas and the rest.

This explains that one possessed of the five perfections emanates for the sake of sentient beings.

This is the commentary on the Vajrasattva chapter.

Its meaning shows the following. Having thus explained the stages of practicing the method by which the emanation body descended into the human realm, now the meaning of the limbs is to be stated: the meaning of the limbs of the Vajrasattva chapter and the rite for practicing the six sections have been explained. Now the meaning of the limbs of the Vairocana chapter is to be stated:

What took place before, in time past.

This is the way the deeds of the emanation bodies of the buddhas of the past were generated, emanated and gathered back, and the way they abide and are supported. The great self is permanent.

From the scriptures of the Tathāgata.

This means: in the past, in the time of the Perfect Age, the Blessed One, at the very summit of the four dhyānas, united in the manner of Vajrasattva and taught the yoga tantras such as the Paramādya. Because of that teaching, sentient beings came to make superimpositions by clinging to the form body and the like; so Krakucchanda, Kanakamuni, Kāśyapa and others taught the vehicle of the Middle Way and dispelled those superimpositions. Then, because deluded beings clung to emptiness alone, in order to turn them back from superimposition and denial, the tantras of unsurpassed method and of unsurpassed wisdom were taught. This is explained in its proper order in the first chapter of the Vajraḍāka, so one should have no doubt about it.

With reference to the eighteen-thousand-verse Union tantra, unsurpassed by virtue of wisdom: from the great self, the permanent dharmakāya, from the scriptures of the Tathāgata, the causal Vairocana, in the presence of the lords of the ten grounds, bestowed blessing, conferred empowerment, established them in samādhi, and performed enlightened activity.

Formerly, in the first eon of the Perfect Age, at the very summit of the fourth dhyāna, in the abode of the Paranirmitavaśavartin gods, and on the peak of Mount Meru, there took place the Paramādya, the Compendium of Reality (Tattvasaṃgraha) and the rest, the wheel of the scriptures of the Tathāgata for the purpose of taming the gods, the rites in the form of the gods among beings. And in Jambudvīpa, in this Good Eon, from the scriptures of the Tathāgata in the teachings of Krakucchanda, Kanakamuni and Kāśyapa, they appeared in the manner of renunciants, in keeping with the beings of Jambudvīpa, and taught the piṭakas.

At that time all sentient beings, through the "extreme emptiness" and the like, brought to an end the stream of consciousness that engages the dharmatā of cessation, in which the stream of wisdom is cut off, and so passed into nirvāṇa without remainder of the aggregates. Knowing that this yoga was to be abandoned, the Blessed One Vajrasattva, in order to free from emptiness those tathāgatas and beings who clung one-sidedly to emptiness, and to turn them back from nirvāṇa without remainder of the aggregates, generated the womb-born maṇḍala of Vairocana.

If one wonders what that maṇḍala of Vairocana teaches as a view beyond this, the words beginning "neither empty nor non-empty" are spoken. That is: ultimately it is neither empty in the manner of the illusion-like or the utterly non-abiding, nor non-empty in the manner of those with aspects and those without aspects; and since it is not apprehended even as the middle, the nondual union, it is fully understood as the pure worldly wisdom arising from the nonconceptual dharmakāya, whose nature is wisdom, emptiness, and method, compassion.

For the yogin devoted to nonconceptuality, the devotion is this too: he should regard the whole wheel of deities in the manner of an echo or a resounding sound. In order to show the intention that deluded beings should not superimpose or deny through clinging to the bodies of the deities, the wheel of the maṇḍala and the reality of the deities were taught. And those in the future who wish to engage in conduct through the three doors by way of mantra should, at the time of the Supreme King of the Maṇḍala, praise all the tathāgatas at the three times with the song that expands the qualities of the Great Vehicle.

That is: from "Equality, unmoving" up to "possessing the supreme Dharma" is praise by way of the mirror-like wisdom; from "even in the subtlest part of the qualities" up to "certainly request" is praise by way of the wisdom of equality; from "Ever stainless mind" up to "ever fully" is praise by way of the discriminating wisdom; from "Immeasurable, free of measure" up to "never changes" is praise by way of the wisdom that accomplishes activities; and from "How wonderful, utterly excellent" up to "I and the rest" is praise by way of the wisdom of the dharmadhātu.

The master Nāgārjuna holds that at the time of approach, when one is absorbed in the subtle samādhi, one is roused by the songs of the goddesses. Indrabhūti holds that, in order to generate the resultant Vairocana from the melting of the causal Vairocana, one is roused by the song of the four goddesses. Praśāntamitra and Ānandagarbha hold that one is roused after completing the meditation on the maṇḍala, upon arising from the samādhi of emptiness. For Jñānapāda there is no doubt that the praise is made at the Supreme King of the Maṇḍala.

The passage beginning "This, of all the buddhas" is not explained, since it is easy to understand.

Then its accomplishment.

These words explain the meaning for those who follow after. Accomplishment is meditating on the wheel of deities through the two, approach and accomplishment, until the signs are obtained.

The lord of well-examined accomplishment.

That is, once the signs have been obtained, one meditates on the worldly accomplishments, the five clairvoyances.

In this very existence
One obtains the most enduring accomplishment.

This is the supreme accomplishment, the achievement of the wisdom of buddha-awakening. If one asks what the method for accomplishing it is, it is said:

By snapping the vajra fingers,
One should gather all the buddhas.

That is, the yogin who meditates on the wheel of the maṇḍala of Vairocana causes the wisdom being to enter by the path of blessing.

With the palms well joined,
Clap the palms three times without fail.

That is, having paid homage to the wisdom being who has come, one causes it to enter the heart, and at the end of the empowerment one should clap and so please it.

Whichever sense faculty becomes the path,
One should make it of that one's nature.

That is, one meditates on the faculties of the buddha and one's own as of one taste in union.

According to one's devotion and attachment.

That is, when one meditates with the devotion of lucid faith in the deity, one should sing the heart mantras such as śajava and the songs of mantra, and offer praise. It abides everywhere: "entirely" means entities, and "all" means the varieties of aspect.

The self, always, in all aspects.

This refers to time.

Holding all the buddhas and the accomplishments,
Seeing them fully, that is accomplishment.

That is, one will obtain the signs of seeing, touching and hearing one's own body and the deity as inseparable.

When accomplishment is not obtained, the practice to be repeated is taught in the words beginning "By all the buddhas." That is, if the yogin of the family praises the deity the whole night through with the king of praises explained earlier, reality is roused, and there is no doubt that accomplishment will be achieved.

This is the commentary on the Vairocana chapter.

Having thus explained the Vairocana chapter, the Heruka chapter is taught in the words beginning "From time past." The words "from time past" and the rest have already been explained.

At that time all sentient beings.

That is, after the teaching of Kāśyapa, all sentient beings became vicious and fierce. If one asks, "They merely became vicious; what harm came of it?", the words beginning "extremely wrathful" are spoken. That is, through the ripening of that, they were born in the Wailing hell. Then, passing from that life, one was born as the bhūta called Virtuous, son of the bhūta called Eager to Belong. He became the black Maker of Goodness, and in order to destroy the Triple City he carried out the work of vināyakas, fevers, poisons, mineral poisons and the like; he destroyed the three worlds, harmed the vidyādhara practitioners, caused the gods and others to die, and also destroyed the teaching of the Sugata.

Then, unable to bear the viciousness of that fierce Bhairava, the gods together with Indra went for refuge to Brahmā. Brahmā in turn went for refuge to Viṣṇu. Viṣṇu too went for refuge to the great son of the gods of the Paranirmitavaśavartin heaven. That great god, knowing the nature of the Blessed One Vajradhara, went for refuge to him.

Then the Blessed One Vajradhara himself snapped his vajra fingers and performed the vajra gathering: he invited all the buddhas, and all the world-systems became full of them, like a sesame pod full of seeds. Vajrasattva, seeing that peaceful means would not benefit vicious and fierce beings, entered the samādhi of wisdom and method, and by uttering hūṃ, the heart of method and wisdom, he himself and all the tathāgatas became wrathful and assumed wrathful forms. Then came the wrathful utterance:

If one of wrathful manner, in his wrath,
Burns up even the three worlds,
Then by the wrath of all the buddhas,
What need to speak of the three realms without remainder?

Then on the tip of the nose
Hold the mind perfectly steady.

That is, the mind is fixed on the drop of the melted buddhas of the ten directions, appearing in the form of the syllable hūṃ in the secret space of glorious Heruka, father and mother, the father and mother in whom method and wisdom are nondual. From the breath at the tip of the nose of the secret vajra, made long and warm by the thirty-two syllables, the mudrās of the wheel of Heruka, Gaurī and the rest, came forth by birth from the womb.

That those in the future who wish to engage in the conduct of the door of secret mantra should meditate on the stages of the womb-born wheel of the maṇḍala is taught in the words beginning "That Blessed One, the yogin." If one asks what the color of that Blessed One's body is like, the words beginning "the blue, blazing sattva" are spoken. He is four-faced and eight-armed. The right face is peaceful and white; the left is red with passion; the central face is fierce; the rear face, bewildering, is yellow. His first two hands hold a vajra and bell; the lower two hold the skin of the terrifying one; the third pair hold arrow and bow; and the fourth pair hold a spear and a skull cup. Or else he has one face and four arms.

If one asks what activity he performs, it is taught from "fierce as the time of the great destruction" up to "having defeated them all, he again revives those turned to ash." That is, having brought suffering upon all the wheels of the unruly, he again revives those who had died and turned to ash. If one asks what more he does, it is said: "From his blazing sun." That is, he emanates the guardians of the charnel-ground fields, who arise from the blazing rays of his own sun, and makes them emanated forms like shadows. Then those renowned as surpassing all the māras, able to devour the blood of the vicious ones and others who obstruct the yogins of the future, sport together with Heruka, king of the charnel grounds. Therefore, having purified the buddha-fields and placed all beings in awakening, he purifies all the realms of sentient beings.

This is the commentary on the Heruka chapter.

Having thus explained the Heruka chapter, now the chapter of Padmanarteśvara is to be explained. From "What took place before, in time past" up to "took place before in the manner of beings" has already been explained.

At that time all sentient beings.

That is, after the teaching of the Buddha Kāśyapa, all sentient beings, under the sway of delusion, came to hold wrong views. Then, under the sway of attachment, they were born in the Great Wailing hell. In order to purify the very wisdom of those who held wrong views, the Blessed One Vajradharma saw that, just as this red lotus, though born from the mud, is not stained by the faults of the mud, so the luminosity of the beings of the three realms is not stained by the faults of adventitious defilements; and so he generated himself in the body of Avalokiteśvara.

If one asks who that is, the words "Vajrasattva, the Tathāgata" are spoken: it should be understood that the maṇḍala of the six families and Vajrasattva himself are the root emanation body. Therefore that Blessed One Vajrasattva, in order to tame the attachment arising from wrong views, made the sacrifice of discriminating wisdom, attachment purified: by the stages of womb birth in which Padmanarteśvara unites method and wisdom, and in the manner of the maṇḍala of Padmanarteśvara, he became Amitābha, king of Avalokiteśvara who looks upon all, for the taming of the manifold.

This is the commentary on the Padmanarteśvara chapter.

Having thus given the commentary on Narteśvara, now the Vajrasūrya chapter is to be explained. The one verse "What took place before, in time past" has already been explained.

At that time all sentient beings.

That is, after the teaching of the Buddha Kāśyapa, they lived with avaricious minds and dwelt mostly on wealth; and because they had become extremely miserly, they fell into the realm of the hungry ghosts. Then, passing from that life, they were born as poor people, and they suffered hunger and thirst and killed one another. In order to purify that very yoga of the extremely vicious and avaricious, the Blessed One Vajrasattva rested evenly in the samādhi of the great wealth of equality and took the form of Vajrasūrya. Then that Blessed One Ākāśagarbha, Vajrasūrya, in order to fulfill the hopes of all sentient beings of the ten directions, sent down a rain of the precious queen and the other treasures, and bestowed upon all sentient beings the kingdom of a universal monarch.

Because peaceful means do not benefit those who are wrathful with the pride of the vicious and avaricious, from Vajrasūrya of wisdom and method, by the stages of womb birth, the avaricious were thoroughly scorched by the rays of the sun and established as gods free of avarice. "Doing" means bringing body, speech and mind under control. "Turning back" means the dissolving of the life breath and consciousness.

And especially by carrying off.

This means enjoying the queens and enjoying the feast gathering. Having thus been victorious over others, he gathers in all the hosts of wealth that are in his own power in all three places, below the earth, upon the earth and above the earth, and he is completely victorious over the three worlds.

This is the commentary on the Vajrasūrya chapter.

Now the Paramāśva chapter is explained. The śloka "In time past" has already been explained.

At that time all sentient beings.

That is, after the teaching of Kāśyapa. Knowing that their diligence had become very slight, they were full of laziness and envy. If one wonders what comes of that laziness, the words "they fall into the hell of Avīci" are spoken. Avīci stands as an example, meaning the eight hells. Then, dying and passing on, even when born as humans they became adulterers, bandits and thieves, and did deeds that obstruct the side of virtue. The Blessed One Vajrasattva, knowing that very yoga, generated in the form of Paramāśva the manner of fierce application whose nature is the wisdom that accomplishes activities. Through devotion to all the sentient beings to be tamed as pure, by the power of a compassion free of evil, he acted wholly in direct perception.

With the prowess that overpowers others.

That is, he overpowered them by acts of bringing under control and of dissolving their enjoyments. If one wonders how he overpowered them: by the stages of womb birth of Paramāśva, father and mother in union, he emanated the wheel of the maṇḍala in order to bring in all the worlds of the many kinds of beings who were vicious through great laziness and fierce through envy. If one asks what he did by that, the words "by adulterers, bandits and thieves" are spoken. An adulterer is one who goes to another's wife. A thief is one who steals wealth unseen. A bandit is one who steals wealth openly.

Deceit and trickery.

Deceit is by words. Trickery is a crooked mind, a way of concealing one's own faults.

He joins them all thoroughly.

That is, he joins those to be tamed through the wheel of emanation.

Likewise by the power of boldness.

That is, openly, by the power of diligence, he brings them under control and dissolves their enjoyments.

Having overcome them with the five weapons.

That is, having subdued them with vajra, sword, bow and arrow, single-pointed spear, and club, he then becomes victorious over all the worlds. Then, it is said, taking the central chief Paramāśva as the basis, he protects his own kingdom by various methods of taming those to be tamed.

With reference to the two Paramāśvas of the elements, he taught: in order to tame the lustful, and in order to tame through lust the bodies and the wrathful ones, a wrathful body of fierce rites; in order to tame the peaceful, a peaceful body; and in order to tame the violent, a fierce body of increase.

If one asks whom Paramāśva subdued in those four, it is this. Having pressed them down directly by the power of a diligence free of evil, he carried off the goddess called the Seductress. In his might he pressed down Viṣṇu, carried off the goddess called the Sixth, and enjoyed her. He pressed down Brahmā, Prajāpati, the Lord of Creatures, carried off the goddess Utterly Peaceful, and enjoyed her. He pressed down the god of desire (Kāmadeva), carried off the Sovereign of Humans, and enjoyed her. Thus in the time of the past, having pressed down all the unruly and brought their body, speech and mind under control, he carried off all the goddesses and the feast gatherings and enjoyed them.

For them too, when conceptual thought has ceased, the life breath dissolves; and when nonconceptual wisdom has arisen, wisdom dissolves. Therefore, from "These and the others, infinite and supreme" up to "displaying the great vajra activity," acting for the benefit of sentient beings by means of the emanation body is taught.

If one wonders who it is that tames those beings, by what method it is attained, and what fruit is attained, the tamer is shown by the words:

In the expanse of all space, the glorious
Vajrasattva fully abides.

By accomplishing through method and prowess.

That is, accomplishing by the method of bringing under control and the prowess of enjoyment, through the power of the application of fierce rites, that Vajradhara, in the form of Paramāśva, attained mastery over the entire kingdom of the three realms, and by the stages of womb birth he is surrounded by a fourfold retinue that gathers in the families. Emanating infinite and supreme bodhisattvas and accomplishing through the violent application of fierce rites: that is the method.

As for the fruit: from "The ground of devotion, all completely pure" up to "fully explains the awakening of the buddhas" is the supreme accomplishment; and from "The accomplishment of the gods, joy" onward is the achievement of worldly accomplishment. From "The awakening of the buddhas, great bliss" up to "having attained it by one's own yoga, knowing" teaches the dharmakāya; from "All sentient beings, cleansed of stains" up to "gives the teaching to the realm of beings" teaches the saṃbhogakāya; and from "Paramāśva is an emanation" up to "also acts for the benefit of beings" teaches the nirmāṇakāya.

This is the commentary on the Paramāśva chapter.

Having thus explained the six chapters, their conclusion is stated in the words:

These and the others, infinite and supreme.

That is, the wheel of empowerment is sixfold, and the wheel for receiving accomplishment is the one hundred and eight deities; or else, emanating one hundred and eight of the solitary hero, those tamed in the past realized, through the self-arisen accounts, pure worldly wisdom. If one asks which root deity of the initial yoga emanated those one hundred and eight maṇḍalas, it is said:

In the expanse of all space, the glorious
Vajrasattva abides everywhere.

The one hundred and eight yogins who receive accomplishment are also lords of accomplishment, fully accomplished: that is, in the common worldly and the supreme accomplishments.

He causes all, the self, to engage.

That is, the yogin who has attained accomplishment engages in all activities. "The seal of the accounts" means that the history of the Paramādya in the first eon, the history of the three buddhas in the middle eon, and, in the last eon, the emanation of the rites of the six maṇḍalas of the chapters of the Union, together with the meditation of the six future yogins who follow after them, have been taught.

This is the commentary on the fifth section.

Having thus explained the six maṇḍalas of the chapters on what took place in the past and on those who follow after, now, in order to show the assembly of samaya and empowerment as endowed with good fortune, the words beginning "Then, further, in all" are spoken. Because without samaya and empowerment in the maṇḍala, accomplishment will not be attained, this is joined to "the Saṃvara of the Ḍākinī's Illusion is explained." "Further" means that ripening the fortunate disciples, in relation to the six preceding maṇḍalas, is a further matter. "In all" means in all directions, for fortunate persons devoted to the six rites. "Supreme in various aspects" means that those to be tamed are tamed by the explanation of the wheel of the six families. If one wonders by what method this is done, it is said:

Union with all buddhas,
The Ḍākinī's Illusion, supreme of bliss.

That is, by uniting the undefiled wisdom of the buddhas of the six families with ordinary yogins who still have adventitious stains, there is the assembly of the causes and conditions of the illusion of the ḍākinī of calm abiding and insight. That is to say, calm abiding is bliss, and insight is emptiness; and through the assembly of causes and conditions there is supreme bliss, Saṃvara: the assembly of the causes and conditions of the illusion of undefiled bliss is what will be explained.

If one wonders in what place that Saṃvara is accomplished, what the thing is, and by what kind of sādhana it is accomplished, the words beginning "Glorious" are spoken. Its place is one, outer and inner: the outer place is a delightful spot and the like; the inner is the three maṇḍalas, the maṇḍala of the body, the maṇḍala of the bhaga, and the maṇḍala of the dharmadhātu. Drawing a maṇḍala of colored powders at a delightful outer place and conferring the vase empowerment will be taught in the seventh section. Here, with reference to the supreme yogin, three will be explained: conferring the secret empowerment on the maṇḍala of the body, conferring the wisdom-knowledge empowerment on the maṇḍala of the bhaga, and conferring the fourth empowerment on the maṇḍala of the dharmadhātu.

Wishing to explain the maṇḍala of the body and the maṇḍala of the bhaga, he states the place in the words:

The glorious one, the reality of all buddhas,
Vajrasattva, the source of bliss,
In the delight of the supreme secret.

The bliss of the dhyāna of calm abiding and the undefiled bliss of insight are Vajrasattva, the reality of all buddhas. The place where that bliss arises is the maṇḍala of the body and the maṇḍala of the bhaga, in the delight of the supreme secret. "The self of all" is the self endowed with the qualities of a perfect retinue; at the time when the two winds are stopped, at the time of the four joys, he abides always. With reference to the maṇḍala of the dharmadhātu, it is construed thus: Vajrasattva of nonconceptual wisdom, the dharmadhātu, the delight of the supreme secret, the self of all five wisdoms, abides always at an inconceivable time.

If one asks what the thing to be accomplished is: that which is not to be transgressed by all the buddhas, the glorious, is the essence of bliss appearing as that Vajrasattva, in whom emptiness and the illusion-like are of one taste. "The distinction of all" means adorned and ornamented with the armlets, necklaces and other ornaments of the four jewels of the tathāgatas. As the Saṃvara also says:

Adorned with fragrant perfumes, flowers and incense,
Ringing bells of most sweet sound.

"Endowed with vajra, bell and supreme banner" means endowed with the retas of the springtime of a girl of the śūdra caste who has reached sixteen years of age. "When a fine canopy is spread" means wearing new clothes. "With vajra words, offerings and so forth" means worshipping with the offerings of karna: signs, embraces, kisses and so forth. "Song and music, in manifold display" means a place where the four songs are sung, surrounded by ten kinds of musical instruments such as the flute and the vīṇā. Or else it is explained according to the literal sense, and is easy to understand.

Adorned with flowers, incense, lamps, perfume, food offerings and so forth, one should accomplish the Illusion Saṃvara in the place, in the maṇḍala of the body and the bhaga. Or else, in a pleasant place such as a pleasure garden, with reference to the maṇḍala of Vajrasattva, one arranges twenty-one seats. They are covered with cloth adorned with lotuses over a set of cushions with inner stuffing, soft and pleasant to the touch. On all those seats, by the first stage of the four of approach and accomplishment, one generates Vajrasattva, and afterwards one emanates the deities of the retinue from all those supports. Moreover, the twenty heroes and yoginīs, each in the sequence of womb birth, are set to meditate on their own deity.

If one asks how the celestial palace of the feast gathering is to be meditated on, it is taught by the passage from "Square, with four doors" up to "and it should be furnished with gatekeepers." If one asks what their colors and shapes are like, it says, "like one's own deity." "The women are excellent" means that, having been purified by the four of approach and accomplishment, they meditate each on her own goddess; or else, with face ornaments and necklaces, one should envisage, through one's own mudrā, the feast gathering of the signs.

As for the substance for the feast gathering and for conferring the secret empowerment, it says:

Human vermilion, when accomplished,
Is explained as being called the Great Red.

That is, self-arisen blood. If one wonders why it is needed, it says:

By the powers of essential nature,
And by intrinsic nature, it is accomplished.

The principal things, which make the root and life increase, called kamatatva and beśāntatva, are the power of essential nature; and the four joys, free of its eighty intrinsic natures and nonconceptual, called nereñjatattva, are what is accomplished by intrinsic nature.

If one wonders how it is tasted, it says, "With vajra water and with vajra." With excrement and urine, the five nectars, together with bodhicitta and great vermilion, that is, flesh, endowed with potency by the power of their intrinsic nature, are placed in a skull vessel, and are realized, in the vajra lady, as the samaya taste of the Blessed One Vajrasattva, like an illusion. This is to be tasted not by one person alone but by all the yogins.

Teaching the drink of peace, it says, "Its vermilion and semen." Vermilion is the self-arisen flower; semen is the seed. Accomplishing the great vermilion is accomplishing the nectar. Why so? If one asks by what distinctive name, in "Among the five nectars, the great vermilion," it is designated: because it arises from the great passion of meditating on bliss and emptiness, it is explained as "the Great Red." Its bodhicitta and red sandalwood, together with the great flesh, placed in the center of the gathering, become what is called the rasayana that raises the dead, tasted by all the deities.

If one asks how it is tasted, it says, "Endowed with the union of one's own deity." When the yogins of the feast gathering, endowed with the yoga of their respective deities, taste it with the tips of the ring finger and the thumb, like the drink of peace, they will always obtain the accomplishment of undefiled wisdom. Moreover, the Catuṣpīṭha says: having generated the thumb as the ground and imagined the ring finger as Mount Meru, one meditates on it as a great ocean of nectar, stirs it and emanates; and just as they hold that sins are purified by drinking the soma grass gathered from Mount Badira, pounded in the hide of a yellow cow that bears bezoar while reciting the mantras of the Vedas, so should one taste it.

Its result is taught in "the nature of all buddhas" and so forth, which is easy to understand.

Having thus explained the secret empowerment, the conferral of the wisdom-knowledge empowerment is taught by saying, "Then bathed, the body fragrant." Having bathed, anointed the body with fragrances, put on whatever clothes and ornaments one can afford, worn garlands of flowers and filled the mouth with fragrance, one is with a woman of any caste endowed with good fortune; and having joined the door of the avadhūtī, the knowing of the wisdom of emptiness arises in the bliss of touch. While the disciple remains as if blue and fallen asleep, the goddesses of the maṇḍala make wisdom descend, and when he is absorbed in the samādhi in which conceptions have subsided, the master, the master of the maṇḍala of raising the dead that is described below, takes him up; and with the cloth of the bhaga of the queen consort, through the union of the joy of the secret vajra, he should stimulate his own bodhicitta. In the center of the space channels of both method and wisdom, the perfect buddhas called sun and moon, that is, the bodhicitta of method and wisdom, having passed beyond the mountain of the four cakras, rise in the center of the space of the śaṅkhinī. Drawing forth certainty about them by the power of the guru's pith instructions, and experiencing them without sinking, one will be freed from all sins, and all merit will be accomplished.

Explaining this very thing in detail, it says "the definite path" and so forth. "The definite path is endowed with all" means endowed with the path in which calm abiding is ascertained as nonconceptual bliss and insight is ascertained as bliss and emptiness. "Fully adorned by transcendence" means that, being beyond the eighty intrinsic natures, it is adorned by the four joys. That which arouses from the great bliss thus accomplished is called "complete arousal"; it is awakened by singing the song of raising the dead. That song is taught from "Emptiness, supreme yogin" up to "Glorious one, you are the self." Its benefits are taught from "Having sung these very ones as songs" up to "what need to mention."

If one wonders whether the disciple who has obtained the wisdom-knowledge empowerment is endowed with the path and its times, it says:

Being equally endowed with the four paths,
Made beautiful by transcendence.

At the time of joy one is endowed with emptiness; at the time of supreme joy, with utter emptiness; at the time of coemergent joy, with great emptiness; and at the time of luminosity, with the emptiness of all aspects. This is being endowed with the four paths.

Moreover, by passing beyond the eighty intrinsic natures there is nonduality; dualistic imputation having subsided, and being free of the conceptions of the imputed and the dependent, nonduality is made beautiful as the intrinsic nature. As the Saṃvara also says:

The offering, and likewise the yoginī,
The union of the two faculties:
That union too holds reality itself.

The Vajraḍāka also teaches that the three appearances of mind enter into the nature of luminosity. And the latter-latter tantra of this very tantra also says:

Beings greatly exalted in merit,
The two aspects unmoving: that itself is the self.
The formations of mind are movement,
And appear as all conditioned things.

Explaining the times of the four joys, it says, "Following the manifold, thoroughly accomplished." Following the manifold aspects, there are maturation, consummation, and freedom from characteristics. "The three worlds thoroughly accomplished" means that the three appearances of mind are accomplished as the nature of luminosity. "Homage paid by the three worlds" is explained:

Just as water placed in water,
Just as butter in butter,
One's own wisdom by oneself:
Whatever is seen here, this is homage.

"Teacher of the three worlds": if one knows the suchness of mind, one will know all dharmas in brief and in full. "Bestowing growth on all the world" means thorough pliancy, the genuine limb of awakening, the bliss of body, speech and mind. "Accomplish the three worlds for me" means that all is accomplished as the nature of luminosity.

Vajrasattva, possessed of glory,
Keeps company with all the goddesses;
Fortunate one of all the manifold,
All arali will be accomplished.

This means that through union with the action mudrā on the path of passion, and through union with the Dharma mudrā and the samaya mudrā on the path of liberation, the play of the three joys will be accomplished as all arali.

"Through devotion in bliss to all the goddesses throughout the boundless reaches of the element of space" means that through devotion to the goddesses of emptiness, the great mudrā, the nature of the dharmadhātu, the thirty-nine factors on the side of awakening, and to the illusion-like as one taste with whatever bliss there is, the undefiled glorious Vajrasattva, the nature of luminosity, will be perfectly completed.

Thinking, "Having experienced it once through the wisdom-knowledge and the fourth empowerments, how will one know emptiness at all times?", it says:

Of the sun rising clearly,
Vajrasattva perfectly joins.

When the bodhicitta, clear in the manner of sun and moon in the four cakras, rises at the door of the avadhūtī, "the Ḍākinī's Illusion Saṃvara is taught." That is, for the arising of the ḍākinīs of calm abiding and insight, the assemblage of the illusion of bliss and emptiness, once one has drawn forth certainty by the power of the guru's pith instructions and focuses on it, thereafter all the accomplishments of bliss are taught, at all times, in all things and in all aspects.

If one wonders what to do when, focusing solely on bliss and emptiness, the life wind enters the avadhūtī and becomes stable and accomplished, it says, "one arouses." By singing the songs of the manifold, maturation, consummation and freedom from characteristics, namely the songs that appear below, the gandharbha, called "Possessing Gandharvas," and the sarja, called "Endowed with Six," one arouses the samādhi of great bliss. Then, just as in the yoginī tantras wisdom is made to descend and is then withdrawn, so should it be viewed here too.

The passage from "Manifold passion" up to "please accomplish bliss" explains the mirror-like union. From "O supreme one" up to "Enjoy, ho!" it explains the union of equality. From "O supremely secret one" up to "know clearly" it explains the discriminating union. From "All the samayas" up to "having thoroughly accomplished, the teacher" it explains the union of diligent activity. From jaḥ hūṃ baṃ hoḥ up to a la la ho it explains the union of the wisdom of the dharmadhātu.

Thus, in the maṇḍala of the dharmadhātu, aroused by songs like echoes, the fourth empowerment, the luminous nature of consciousness, enters; and therefore one makes the supplication, "Please admit the disciple through the manifold Ḍākinī's Illusion of all the tathāgatas." Thus, aroused by the echo-like song, in order to realize through the dharmadhātu fourth empowerment the reality of one's own mind and, having realized the emptiness of all dharmas, to seal it with great bliss, the king of songs called "The Appearance, Without Remainder and Without Omission, of the Magical Display of All the Buddhas" is spoken.

"Vajrasattva, abode of glory" means the wisdom of the luminosity of mind. "Saṃvara of all illusions" means the following. From the illusion of the maṇḍala of colored powders there is the Saṃvara in which appearance and existence are realized as a wheel of deities. From the illusion of the secret empowerment in the maṇḍala of the body there is the Saṃvara in which appearance and existence are realized as illusion. From the illusion of the wisdom-knowledge empowerment in the maṇḍala of the bhaga there is the realization of the Saṃvara of the consort's emptiness. And from the illusion of the fourth empowerment, the dharmadhātu of one's own mind, there is the realization of the nonconceptual, undefiled Saṃvara.

Now, the song that shows the all-pervading Saṃvara of the first ground, the Very Joyful, which pervades all dharmas, is as follows. The passage from "Manifold, the tantra of passion" up to te na te te hūṃ teaches the wisdom in which appearance and existence are united as mirror-like. From "Manifold, the tantra of utter joy" up to hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ it explains the wisdom in which appearance and existence are united in equality. From "Manifold, the tantra of the wrathful" up to ru lu ru lu hūṃ bhyo hūṃ it teaches the union of the discriminating wisdom of appearance and existence. From "Manifold, the tantra of life" up to hūṃ śi ya ā be śa hūṃ it teaches the union of the all-accomplishing wisdom of all appearance and existence.

In the language of the gods of the realm of the Pure Abodes, the mirror-like reality is the tantra of passion; the reality of equality is the tantra of utter joy; the discriminating reality is the tantra of the wrathful; and the accomplishing reality is called the tantra of life. This is known as the mudra of song for all the self-arisen.

"Supreme in boundlessness, and so forth" means that, through boundless echo-like songs that enter the dharmadhātu, the lord who accomplishes boundless disciples, having accomplished the luminous mind, accomplishes all appearance and existence as the nature of luminosity. "Thoroughly urged on by all the goddesses" refers to the two songs explained before. These very songs are the urging by song by the four: Saṃvarā, Wondrous Bliss, Illuminatrix and the Disciple Lady.

Thus the yogin himself, having obtained the secret, the wisdom-knowledge and the fourth empowerments, now performs for the disciples six different feast gatherings of the six maṇḍalas; and the empowerment of six disciples in the six maṇḍalas of the body and of the bhaga, the dharmadhātu, is taught by the words "drawn forth from all the faculties." With an arrangement of the eight offering goddesses, the Flute Goddess and the others and the Flower Goddess and the others, arising from the secret space of the nondual method and wisdom of the six causal Herukas, one worships oneself as father and mother. Blessing the bhaga and the vajra, the Saṃvara of the Ḍākinī's Illusion, the Union with All Buddhas, is accomplished as a body that is the self of all things of the four families, all appearance and existence without exception.

Having thus taught in brief the generation of the feast gatherings of the six families, it explains in detail, saying, "By bringing down the mudrā of the Teacher." One generates them in the form of the mudrās of the wheels of the six families, causes wisdom to descend, and by the four of approach and accomplishment accomplishes the women as goddesses; they then become goddesses holding vajra, wheel, skull cup, jewel and crossed vajra. When they too are accomplished by causing wisdom to descend, like one's own goddess, wisdom descends, and unprecedented music, cymbals, dance and magical display will also be accomplished. When wisdom has descended, whatever unprecedented bliss there is of divine forms, sounds, smells, tastes and objects of touch, when one sees oneself as the deity with mental devotion, one will be making offering.

"The divine offering in union" means that the one of supreme good fortune, nonconceptual and free of the eighty intrinsic natures of the bliss of meditative concentration, possessed of the passion of bliss and emptiness, accomplishes the bliss of good fortune for all the disciples. Through the blessing of illusion by the wisdom of the buddhas, the blisses of all the maṇḍalas of the six ritual systems, in union with the bliss of the undefiled Vajrasattva, are accomplished as follows: by the very power of experiencing the bliss of the four joys from seeing the six maṇḍalas, the one hundred and eight fortunate yogins who meditate on the one hundred and eight deities of the six maṇḍalas accomplish it through the very bliss of body, speech and mind of their respective deities.

Having taught the samādhi of the four joys in accomplishing the feast gathering, it teaches the Dharma mudrā, the object of the feast, saying, "Possessing the form of all buddhas." By meditating on the bliss of the four joys in oneself, the delightful embodiment of the divine forms, sounds, smells, tastes and objects of touch, all accomplishments without exception will be accomplished.

Thus, when the feast gathering is complete, what is to be recited for the sake of the one hundred and eight yogins is this:

oṃ Secret Saṃvara of the Ḍākinī's Illusion,
In union with all buddhas,
The dance, the self of all buddhas,
Appears as supreme playful display.

So one should recite.

Then the praise of the six wheels with the songs of their respective four consorts is taught, saying, "Then the perfect, utterly secret." One imagines the maṇḍala of Vajrasattva being praised by the four women Saṃvarā, Wondrous Bliss, Illuminatrix and the Disciple Lady. One imagines the maṇḍala of Vairocana being praised by the four, Sarasvatī, Vīrā, Samantabhadrā and Sulocanā. One imagines the maṇḍala of Heruka being praised by Gaurī, Caurī, Pramoha and Vetālī. One imagines the maṇḍala of Padmanarteśvara being urged on by the four, Great Earth, Tārā, the Surveyor and Bhṛkuṭī. One imagines the maṇḍala of the Sun being praised by the four, the Empowering Lady, the Treasure Holder, the Banner Lady and Supreme Action. One imagines the maṇḍala of Paramāśva being urged on by the four goddesses of pacifying, enriching, magnetizing and fierce sorcery. Their song is set out from "Very joyful" up to "please accomplish everything."

The benefits of those songs are these: with this song-mudrā one praises even when abundant in vast activities, and one praises when accomplishments have not come about; and if one performs it when the requisites are lacking, they will be accomplished. Also, at the time of admitting the disciples, offering should be made with these applications of the offering of song, and with the four goddesses explained before holding flowers and so forth, when all the disciples are admitted into the maṇḍala.

Having thus explained the master's rite for the six feast maṇḍalas, the rite for admitting the disciple is now explained. The supreme yogin who desires accomplishments makes regular confession, takes the vows and generates the mind. Externally, having been empowered with the vase of all activities, he is made, as appropriate, to circle all around with the proud dance of the six families and to take on the binding of the conduct, and he should be admitted with his face covered by a cloth of the colors of the six families. Then, for the disciple who thus wishes to attain accomplishments, wisdom is made to descend, the garland is cast and tied, the face covering is removed, and he should look at the maṇḍala. Then, having received the supreme offering fee, one should confer the empowerments of water, crown, vajra, bell and name.

Having thus conferred the vase empowerment, the conferral of the secret empowerment is taught in "With great red and camphor" and so forth; it is the same as for the master. The conferral of the wisdom-knowledge empowerment is taught in "Union with all buddhas" and so forth: the bhaga maṇḍala wheel of the assembly of the Ḍākinī's Illusion, the Union with All Buddhas. "Not sealed even by that" means overwhelming the eighty intrinsic natures in nonconceptual bliss. "Delusion" means not being deluded by the stain of apprehended and apprehender in conceiving bliss and emptiness. "It will not be overpowered by that" means that it will not harm the realization of the fourth empowerment, the emptiness of mind.

Then, after the disciple has been empowered, the pith instruction of the fourth empowerment is taught by the words, "Then the pith instruction should be proclaimed." The proclamation of the pith instruction is taught from "In harmony with all buddhas" up to "one who drinks great blood at the heart." The giving of relief is taught from "For the sake of the whole realm of sentient beings" up to "Vajrasattva declared."

Then, in teaching the training in the wisdom of the mudrās of the four families, the wisdom of the mudrā of Vajrasattva is taught in "With yoga and with secrecy" and so forth. "With yoga" is the feast gathering. "With secrecy" is the drink of peace. "I accomplish the mudrā" is the purity of the messenger.

By dancing in the manner of the deity,
One bestows bliss upon the disciples.

This teaches the pith instruction of the three purities. That yogin, the supreme disciple, from then on takes up mudrās born of mantra, born of the field and born of wisdom, and, like Vairocana and Buddhalocanā, will accomplish the nature of all buddhas. Then, by the passage from "With taste" up to "one offers to self and others whatever they desire," it teaches that if one has become joyless, one should make offering to the buddhas and bodhisattvas. As the Saṃvara also says:

With the drop of one's own substance,
One should make offering to the buddhas and bodhisattvas.

Then, having accomplished the three purities oneself and being firmly established as the master of all, one should declare to the disciples the Dharma of truth, namely the manner in which they are to enter nonconceptual bliss and emptiness, free of the eighty intrinsic natures. That is the accomplishment of the mudrā of the three purities of Vajrasattva.

Now the accomplishment of the mudrā of the three purities of Vairocana is taught by the words, "The glorious manner of Vajrasattva." Accomplishing the three purities of Vajrasattva and meditating on oneself as the deity of all the ritual systems, one who wishes to perform the great feast gathering, by the four of approach and accomplishment and the application of various activities, and by the manifold mudrās whose nature is to tame by the path of passion and the path of liberation, in order to tame the boundless inclinations of sentient beings, the maṇḍala of Vairocana, other than that maṇḍala of Vajrasattva, is taught. That very Blessed One Vajrasattva, having trained all those to be liberated who are tamed by a peaceful body, generates the maṇḍala of Vairocana, the ḍākinī of liberation, and blesses the male and female yogins to be like their own deity. Having blessed the secret space, one should accomplish, in accordance with one's own mudrā, the feast gathering of the emanations of the buddhas that accords with it, the drink of peace, and the purity of the messenger.

The three purities of Heruka are taught by the words, "Moreover, the yoga of the Blessed One." The three purities of Heruka are taught in order to purify aversion.

The purity of Padmanarteśvara is taught in "Another, the supreme yoginī" and so forth. In order to purify the desire stirred up by wrong views, the three purities of Padmanarteśvara are taught to the yogin of supreme good fortune.

The three purities of Vajrasūrya are taught by the words, "Moreover, the yoga of the Blessed One." The three purities of Vajrasūrya are taught in order to purify the miserly.

The purity of Paramāśva is taught by the words, "That very yoga, the Blessed One." The three purities of Paramāśva are taught in order to purify persons who are violent.

Having thus explained the purities, in order to teach that the one hundred and eight yogins of the six ritual systems should meditate on the one hundred and eight solitary heroes, bless their own one hundred and eight action mudrās, and accomplish them, it says:

Like one's own deity,
Accomplished by one's own blessing,
Through union with the essence of the reflection,
One should dance the union of the buddhas.

Taking on the pride of the one hundred and eight deities and blessing one's own one hundred and eight deities, by thus enjoying the four joys through the manifold samayas of the feast gathering, the drink of peace and the purity of the messenger, the Saṃvara of the Union with All Buddhas will be accomplished.

In order to teach that one who wishes to accomplish the maṇḍala of the condensed families, like the maṇḍala of Paramāśva, should accomplish it by placing the maṇḍala of the Tathāgata in the center, the maṇḍala of Vajrasattva in the east, that of Padmanarteśvara in the west, that of Vajrasūrya in the south and that of Paramāśva in the north; or else that one should place Heruka, Padmanarteśvara or Vajrasūrya there, it says "Everywhere, from all sides, in all" and so forth. In all places and at all times, all the retinues emanated by all the buddhas, through all their selves, in all aspects: for each buddha there is the feast gathering in a maṇḍala condensed from the nature of the support, the celestial palace of the wheel, and the nature of the moving, the deities.

Having taught it in brief, it explains in detail. The placing of the maṇḍala of Vairocana in the center is taught in "In the center, of all the buddhas" and so forth. The maṇḍala of Vairocana is with all the magical displays of all the buddhas. The magical display of the self of Vairocana, surrounded by eight goddesses, is shown; and the retinue goddesses, the Flute Goddess and the Flower Goddess and the others, and the Horse-Faced Goddess and the others, twelve goddesses in all, accomplish the buddhas' emanations in all the outer buddha fields without exception. Likewise, in the center of the eastern pillar one places the maṇḍala of Vajrasattva, the emanation-body mother of the buddhas, the deeds of the buddhas, surrounded by eight goddesses; the twelve retinue goddesses are placed in common. Likewise, the emanation of the maṇḍala of Padmanarteśvara in the center of the western pillar is "Through the magical display of the lotus illusion" and so forth. The emanation of the maṇḍala of Vajrasūrya in the center of the southern pillar is what is taught in "Of the three realms" and so forth. The emanation of the maṇḍala of Paramāśva in the center of the northern pillar is taught in "The great kingdom of the three realms, through the conduct that tames me."

"Supreme in boundlessness, and so forth" means that making any one of the one hundred and eight the principal in the center becomes the condensed maṇḍala. "The glorious magical display of delighted play" means the boundless emanation of condensed maṇḍalas. "Playing with all the manifold" means the manifold wheels that tame. "Manifold, the fortunate ones" means the manifold disciples. "By the manifold Saṃvara" means the manifold three purities.

By the manifold kinds of disciples,
The realm of sentient beings is thoroughly accomplished.

This means that they are accomplished nonconceptually by boundless supremely accomplished yogins. "Supreme in boundlessness, and so forth" teaches that, by the sequence of transmission from one guru to another, through meditating on the wheels of the maṇḍalas of the six ritual systems, on the individual heroes, and on the boundless maṇḍalas that condense the ritual systems, the self of all is accomplished.

This is the commentary on the sixth chapter.

Thus, having shown the abiding nature by the introduction, "by entering the domain where suchness appears," and having caused the signs to be attained by the means of the generation stage and the completion stage of the three chapters, which concern many methods, profundity and those of sharp faculties, there is the giving of relief. In accord with the deeds of the emanation bodies of the buddhas of the past of the six ritual systems, the yogins who follow after meditate on the wheel of the maṇḍala, whether one is a beginner who wishes to accomplish, or a supreme yogin, or a yogin possessed of great compassion who benefits others. Having taught the number of the six givings of relief, and the time, and the approach with its signs, it teaches that after the approach has been accomplished, the activities are to be accomplished by the heroes and yoginīs of the feast gathering arrayed in the attire of the deities, and it teaches the receiving of accomplishments and the benefiting of others.

Now, having performed the approach of the six givings of relief before an image cast in metal, or carved in relief, or a maṇḍala of colored powders drawn as a painting, according to one's inclination, the drawing of the image maṇḍala is taught in "Then, further, what is to be explained" and so forth. Since Vajrasattva, supreme of all the manifold, wishes to emanate image maṇḍalas of the six wheels, the delight of the supreme secret is taught as having the nature of colored powders. Since it says, "The secret, the glorious supreme Heruka; Padmanarteśvara; the delighted Vajrasūrya; my Paramāśva; the ritual system of Vairocana; and the subsequent accomplishment, Vajrasattva," this is the accomplishment of the six colored-powder maṇḍalas.

For these, moreover, the particular places are taught. From "A solitary place" up to "one should accomplish as one pleases," the place of Vajrasattva is taught. From "A shrine and a hermitage" up to "one should accomplish," the place of Vairocana is taught. From "A charnel ground" up to "one should accomplish," the place of Heruka is taught. From "A lotus" up to "it will be accomplished," the place of Padmanarteśvara is taught. From "A king" up to "one should accomplish," the place of Vajrasūrya is taught. From "A retinue" up to "one should accomplish," the place of Paramāśva is taught.

Having thus explained the places of the four maṇḍalas, the attire of the yogin and the gathering of substances are shown from "Attire" up to "by Paramāśva, the vajra holder."

"What are the places and retinues like?" The places are the six wheels that are the support of the place. As for the retinue, if one does not make the maṇḍala of Paramāśva into a maṇḍala condensed by families, there are one hundred and eight deities for the six families. If the families are condensed, there are nine principals in each of the four directions, thirty-six in all; and since the four retinues, the Flute Goddess and the others, the Flower Goddess and the others, and the Horse-Faced Goddess and the others, are also arranged six by six, the deities of the condensed maṇḍala increase to one hundred and eight. The four activities, Paramāśva, and the three father-mother pairs are not to be reckoned by counting them separately.

The arrangement is shape: the shape of the celestial palace, the body colors of the deities, and the shapes of the hand emblems. If "the arrangement" is joined with "the principals of the six families, what are they like?", they are called Sugatas. "From the retinue" means the maṇḍala of the past. "Making such a supreme wheel" explains the maṇḍala of drawn images. "The drawn maṇḍala, endowed with the merit of seeing the emanation bodies of the wheel of meditative concentration of glorious Vajrasattva and the others, also becomes merit" explains the purpose of the image maṇḍala.

Having thus explained the purpose, the rite of the lines is stated in "The maṇḍala" and so forth. If one asks what is to be laid down, it says, "the lines of the maṇḍala are to be laid down," that is, of the six. If one asks by whom, by the wise. If one asks with what thought, by focusing on all directions as equality. If one asks into how many parts the central maṇḍala is to be made, it says:

In the east, the west, the north and the south,
By laying four lines, eight maṇḍalas.

By laying the lines of the eight pillars, it becomes eight parts, and with the center, nine.

If one asks how large the maṇḍala is: what the maṇḍala of emanations of the appearance of the buddhas and bodhisattvas is like cannot be gauged. As for the six image maṇḍalas, according to the difference in the greater or lesser number of one's own deities, that maṇḍala of measure is accomplished even if it is conceived through one's inclination.

If one asks how a maṇḍala with something excessive or deficient is to be purified, this is shown from "Oneself" up to "it will be accomplished." "Everywhere manifold, the fortunate one" is the tantra of the cause. "Everywhere manifold, possessing the wheel" is the tantra of method. From "In order to accomplish the manifold everywhere" onward, a half verse teaches the tantra of the result.

If one asks what the inner maṇḍala is like, although there is no difference in the outer maṇḍala: the maṇḍala of Vairocana is white, has eight pillars, and is encircled by a round vajra garland. That of Heruka is black and encircled by nine crescent moons. That of Padmanarteśvara has eight lotus pillars between eight lotus petals, is red in color, and is encircled by a garland of lotuses.

That of Vajrasūrya has eight jewel pillars, is eight-cornered, and is encircled by a garland of jewel pinnacles. That of Paramāśva is green, has eight crossed-vajra pillars, and is encircled by a garland of crossed vajras.

"As one wishes": one draws Paramāśva as a solitary hero, or with nineteen deities, or the condensed maṇḍala of one hundred and fifteen deities. "Whatever is available" refers to the substances and implements, whether great or small. "At such a time": for pacifying, at dawn, one places the pacifying Paramāśva in the center; for increasing, in the morning, one places the increasing Paramāśva; for the fierce rite, at midday, one places the Paramāśva of the fierce rite; and for subjugating, at dusk, the subjugating Paramāśva is in the center of the maṇḍala. "At ease" means: having meditated, as before, through the four phases of approach and accomplishment, and having generated oneself as Paramāśva through the stages of awakening, one generates the maṇḍala in front in an instant and performs the pūja and the rest. "Abiding throughout the maṇḍala" refers to the maṇḍala of the condensed Paramāśva.

Having thus laid out the lines of the maṇḍala and drawn it, one applies the substances, makes good whatever is excessive or lacking, and sets out the offering substances such as the vases. One enters oneself, just as one enters the feast gathering. Then, with the mantras and mudrās that come later, one draws in the wisdom being, blesses body, speech and mind, confers empowerment, and makes extensive offerings. With this the activity of the master is complete, and now I shall explain the activity for the disciple.

The persons who are recipients to be rejected are taught in one verse. One verse teaches the commitments and the vows. The four verses from "Everywhere manifold, the fortunate" up to "All sages, the unfortunate" explain the six persons who are recipients to be accepted. The two verses from "All bliss" up to "will be accomplished" explain the characteristics of the master who receives the accomplishments.

The supreme disciple who receives the accomplishments is stated in the passage beginning "master of all." "By abiding as master of all" means meditating as the principal of the six maṇḍalas. "When the master leads into the wheel" refers to the six disciples. To the disciple entering that maṇḍala one should put the question: "Among the boundless, genuine secrets of the six maṇḍalas, the worldly, the common and the supreme accomplishments, in what do you most delight?" And the disciple should say, "I desire it." The supreme yogin, having correctly recognized the signs of the four secrets, then engages each in this accomplishment, according to who desires it and in what way.

Having thus explained the rite for the supreme disciple, the rite for the beginner is explained in the passage beginning:
"If one does not truly know the secret,
One should enter after offering the fee."
After the disciple has made the request, there follow the ninefold regular confession, the taking of the vows, the generation of the mind set on awakening, the generation as the deity, and empowerment with the water of the outer vase; after this the fee should be offered to the master. The ten verses from "Having thus entered the maṇḍala" up to "is the fee of Paramāśva" teach the offering of the fee together with its result.

Having thus entered through the door, the proclamation of the deity's commitments, the proclamation of the master's commitments, the descent of wisdom, the casting of the garland, the removal of the blindfold, and the viewing of the maṇḍala are done as explained for the maṇḍala of the feast gathering. Then, having generated the disciple as the deity through the four phases of approach and accomplishment, one should confer empowerment.

"And all the essences of the elements" refers to the four crowns made of gold and the like. "Living" refers to those made of ivory, bone and the like. "Root" refers to those made of sandalwood, juniper and the like. "Having a jeweled diadem with skulls" means a jeweled diadem inlaid with skulls. "The buddha crest jewels" means that one should place Vairocana, with the mudrā of supreme awakening, on the crown of the head. "Everywhere manifold, the guru" means that if one is empowered with the crown of the five families, one becomes a master of the six maṇḍalas. "Master of all" means of the six maṇḍalas. "To all" refers to one who meditates on the principal goddess; "kingship" and so forth, from empowerment with the crown of the five families, is the result. Or else it refers to the time when the one on whom the flower fell is meditated upon as a deity of the retinue, such as Saṃvarā.

"Made of a single wonder of jewels" means that one confers empowerment with the crown of the five families made of gold or whatever else is obtained, and on top of that confers empowerment with two garlands of vajras, jewels, lotuses and crossed vajras. Since one whose flower fell to the east is to be empowered with the buddha, one to the south with the jewel, one to the west with the lotus, and one to the north with the crossed vajra, it says:
"Likewise adorned with manifold ornaments,
By empowering with buddha and lotus,
All the disciples are empowered."

Having explained the empowerment of the maṇḍala of Vajrasattva, now the explanation of the empowerment of the maṇḍala of the Tathāgata: it is marked with a jeweled half-vajra. The empowerment of the maṇḍala of Heruka is the five wrathful vajras, with the five buddhas seated at the hub. That of Padmanarteśvara has Amitābha seated in the middle of two lotus petals. That of Vajrasūrya is of jewel water. For that of Paramāśva, one should empower the disciple with five garlands: of buddhas, jewels, lotuses, vajras and gems.

The empowerment of vajra and bell is taught in the one verse beginning "Made by hammering, or." In order to teach that, when performing the feast gathering, or when, having obtained the signs, one receives the accomplishments by holding the hand emblems of the deities of the six maṇḍalas and practicing, and also for a maṇḍala of images, these great seals should be drawn as follows, it says:
"Everywhere manifold, the fortunate,
The seal of the signs of the self-arisen ones."
This refers to the disciple who receives the accomplishments and to the deities of the feast gathering. And:
"This is the seal of the signs
Of all the buddhas, the self-arisen ones.
It rests in the hands of the self-arisen ones,
Bound by the manifold fortunate one."
With oṃ sarbba tathāgata eṣa stvaṃ dharayāmi bajrasatva hi hi hi hi hūṃ one should bestow the observance of the hand emblems of all the deities.

If one asks whose seal it is, it says:
"Of all the goddesses, to the very limits of the expanse of space,
The supreme of bliss."
The arrow and bow are Saṃvarā's.

"The fortunate, and likewise the fortunate one": the lotus and the vajra are Wondrous Bliss's. The skull cup, the khaṭvāṅga and the crossed vajra are Illuminatrix's. The crossed lotus and the image of Amitāyus are the Disciple Lady's. The arrow and bow and the four heads of Brahmā are the Buddha-Awakening Lady's. The eight-spoked wheel is the Dharma-Wheel Lady's. The five-pronged vajra and the trident are Trailokyavijayā's. The wish-fulfilling tree and the vine are Kāmeśvarī's. The lotus, Hayagrīva, and the even hook held high are the Horse-Faced Goddess's. The boar's tusk is the Noose Goddess's. The chain marked with the sun and moon is the Chain Goddess's. The crossed vajra, the bell, and the skull cup in which she takes up the essence of the five buddha bodies melted down are the Bell Goddess's. The flute belongs to the goddess of the southeast, the lute to the southwest, the mukunda drum to the northwest, and the muraja drum to the northeast. The four beginning with the Flower Goddess are at the four outer corners. "These and the rest, boundless and supreme" intends that, by making the retinue the principals, there come to be twenty-one maṇḍalas.

Having explained the observance of the hand emblems of Vajrasattva, the observance of the hand emblems of Vairocana is this: the Dharma robes, the alms bowl, the ringed staff and the book, these four, are the observance of the hand emblems of the principal. The Essence of All says that they are to be arranged in the four directions of a drawn maṇḍala. The tongue is the Sealing Lady's. The cymbals are Vīrā's. The vajra is Samantabhadrā's. The image of Akṣobhya is the Embodied Lady's. The mirror and the fan are the Fair-Faced Lady's. The vajra hail is the Light-Ray Lady's of the Lamp Clouds. The eye is the Sky-Eyed Lady's.

Having explained the observance of the Tathāgata, the observance of Heruka is this: holding a blazing vajra in the right hand and a khaṭvāṅga and a skull cup in the left is Heruka's. The Essence of All says that the great red and the great heart, resting within the skull cup, are also the Blessed One's. The arrow is Gaurī's; the hook of summoning is Caurī's; the cymbals, Pramoha's; water, Vetālī's; the hook, Pukkasī's; a new cloth stirred by the wind, Caṇḍālī's; holding a blazing vessel of fire, Ghasmarī's. She whose emblems are like those of Heruka is Ḍombinī; in the Definitive Utterance it is explained that, because her hand emblems match, she is called She Who Appears as Heruka.

The observance of Padmanarteśvara is explained thus: the lotus of manifold colors is the Lady of the Bhaga's. The lotus heart is the Lady of Clear Vision's. The triangular source of phenomena is Tārā's. The wheel is the Banner Lady's. The torch is Bhṛkuṭī's.

The observance of Vajrasūrya: the arrow and bow, the blazing sword, and the four fires are the principal's. The diadem is the Empowering Lady's. The vase is the Samādhi Lady's. The victory banner is the Lady Endowed with Thought's. The offering of flowers is the Great Hidden Lady's.

The observance of Paramāśva is that of the crossed lotus. With one or two crossed vajras one obtains all the accomplishments, such as kingship. The hand emblems of the consort are the sword, and also the vajra and the lotus.

The fourth: the commentary on the seventh section.

Having thus explained the wheel of colored powders, the commitments, the empowerment, and the rite of the vajra observance, now the worship of the four maṇḍalas with the mudrās of offering is explained in the passage beginning "Then, further, what is to be explained." It says that the beginner yogin, as a limb of practice, should emanate all the offerings, of manifold forms, of the six families of the Bhagavān Vajrasattva.

Having thus taught it in brief, it is explained in detail in the passage beginning "With both the right and left hands." Pressing the little fingers of the right and left hands with the thumbs, with a swaying of the body, gazing with smiling eyes and dancing, one bows with the mind stirred: this is the homage to the buddha family, the vajra family, the lotus family, the Vajrasūrya family and the karma family. Likewise, by paying homage with joined palms to the fierce worldly god Mahādeva (Śiva), to Indra Śakra, to Upendra Viṣṇu, to the guardians of the directions such as the sun, the moon and Yama, and to Brahmā and the others, one comes to accomplish all the activities of pacifying and increasing.

The joined palms for paying homage to the Tathāgata are taught in the passage beginning "Join the palms of the glorious vajra": binding the vajra palms and dancing, one places them at the heart and should touch the forehead to the ground. The vajra palms for paying homage to Heruka are taught in the passage beginning "Moving the eyebrows": dancing, holding them at the crown of the head and raising them, one should pay homage while laughing rulu rulu hūṃ bhyo hūṃ. The joined palms for paying homage to Padmanarteśvara are taught in the passage beginning "Palms like a lotus opening from the bud": making the palms like an unopened lotus bud, and bringing level the four, the thumbs and little fingers, one should pay homage. The vajra palms for paying homage to Vajrasūrya are taught in the passage beginning "All the limbs of the body": stretching all the limbs of the body out on the ground, one should pay homage. The joined palms for paying homage to Paramāśva are taught in the passage beginning "The joined palms of the glorious swaying vajra": gazing with smiling eyes and dancing, one should pay homage. The mantra of homage common to all is done by reciting oṃ namo te hūṃ nama mi hūṃ namo nama hūṃ.

Having thus explained the mudrās of homage, the teaching that one should make offerings by dancing with the nine moods of dance is in the passage beginning "However many the nine moods of dance." Grace, heroism and terror are the moods of body; laughter, scolding and fierceness are the moods of speech; compassion, peace and wonder are the moods of mind. With these, together with a swaying mood, one should turn in the dance and offer it.

Having thus explained the dance of the principal, the dance of the retinue is taught in the passage beginning "With both the right and left hands": making the left hand a vajra fist and the right the three-banner gesture, one dances; one should dance in the manner of the moods of Saṃvarā, Wondrous Bliss, Illuminatrix and the Disciple Lady. The dance of the Flute Goddess and the others is taught in the passage beginning "Holding the great pride": dancing with pride, one places the palms on the two cheeks and at the crown of the head, and then, lowering them, the twelve goddesses from the Flute Goddess to the Clapping Lady perform the dance.

The dance of the Tathāgata is taught in the passage beginning "With the right and left hands": binding the Tathāgata fist, one should perform the dance of all the deities of the maṇḍala of the Tathāgata. The dance of Heruka is taught in the passage beginning "With both the right and left hands": dancing with two vajra fists, one opens the vajra palms upward and releases them; this is performing the offering mudrā of all the deities of Heruka in the manner of a dance. The dance of Padmanarteśvara is taught in the passage beginning "With both the right and left hands": dancing with two lotus fists and turning the lotus palms, one performs in dance the pūja mudrā of all the deities of the maṇḍala of Narteśvara. The dance of Vajrasūrya is taught in the passage beginning "With both the right and left hands": binding the jewel fist, one should dance; turning the jewel palms, one should make the offering of dance in the manner of the mudrās of all the deities of the maṇḍala of Vajrasūrya. For Paramāśva, one performs the dance that arises from the karma fist and, by turning the karma palms, makes the mudrā of the dance.

As for the dances of the goddesses arising from the maṇḍala: scattering upward is the Flower Goddess's; pressing downward is the Incense Goddess's; circling is the Lamp Goddess's; anointing is the Perfume Goddess's. When showing any hand at all, one fills the hand with flowers or whatever else is available; if one makes offerings through the practice of dance in harmony with the commitments of one's own deity, they will be pleased.

The single verse beginning "If one binds these mudrās" teaches the benefits for as long as the mudrās are bound. The passage from "Equal and unequal to the dharmadhātu" up to "There is nothing that is not accomplished by it" means that if one makes offerings by way of body, speech and mind, there is nothing among the three kinds of accomplishment that is not accomplished by it. Moreover it is said:
"Just as water placed in water,
Just as butter in butter,
Whoever rightly sees
One's own wisdom by oneself: that, here, is homage."

The commentary on the eighth section.

Having thus explained the limbs of practice for the beginner, now the twenty-eight mudrās, such as the great seal, are taught, which are the limbs for practice by the supreme yogin, one who has obtained the signs and has mastery over some small measure of wisdom: practice of the worldly accomplishments, the qualities of the six classes of beings; of the common accomplishments, the eight qualities of the meditative concentrations and the formless states; and of the supreme accomplishment, that of the eleventh. They are taught in the passage beginning "Then, further, what is to be explained." If one asks who is the teacher of these twenty-eight methods that are limbs of practice, where the place is, with whom the retinue, when the time, and what the sublime Dharma taught, then: the teacher is taught by the passage from "Vajrasattva" up to "great bliss." The place is taught by "the delight of the supreme secret." The retinue is taught by "the very self of all." The time is taught by "ever engaged." The Dharma is taught by the passage beginning "Attached to all bliss." "All bliss" is attachment to the bodies of the deities of the maṇḍala. "In order to purify all accomplishments" means that when it is sealed with emptiness, great bliss is realized. If one asks what that is, it says, "Vajrasattva, supreme of bliss": it is the samādhi of meditating on bliss and emptiness.

Having thus taught it in brief, it is explained in detail. The passage from "White like the moon, a body with red veins" up to "beautiful with the finest of diadems" teaches the body of Vajrasattva, the great seal, together with the crown. "Not the wealth of others, not to be stolen": what is not the wealth of others is the skull cup; it is not to be stolen because others have no use for it. The single verse from "Within the skull vessel, together with the pure water of whichever deity" up to "of that practitioner" teaches the signs of the water empowerment: the diadem with its garland of skulls in the four directions and the buddha placed on the crown of the head. This is the empowerment as regent of the universal monarch of the afflicted world, the trichiliocosm, the Sahā world.

The one śloka from "Of the union of Vajrasattva" up to "The ḍākinī accomplishes all" explains Vajrasattva surrounded by goddesses. The buddha, the vajra, the lotus and the sun bring under their power the beings to be trained in the three realms by sealing them, enjoy them through bewilderment, take them in through dissolution and then restore them, and, giving them life, apply them to all the activities. If one asks who does this, it says, "If one seals with this mudrā": the emanation body of Vajrasattva surrounded by goddesses seals those to be trained, and Vajrasattva is fully accomplished.

The accomplishment that comes from Vajrasattva binding the mudrā with the feet is explained in "By the union of right and left": that is, by the sattva cross-legged posture.

Having explained the great seal of Vajrasattva, the great seals of the four consorts are taught by the passage from "Moon-colored, with a smiling face" up to "as though adorned with Sahā and the rest." In particular, making the mudrā of Saṃvarā is grace; turning back is heroism; seizing is terror; and doing it to the utmost is possessing the three moods of dance. It says:
"By the mudrā of embracing oneself,
The seizing of all the limbs is accomplished."
Through practices such as embracing the practitioner she grants accomplishment; she holds arrow and bow.

The passage from "Of manifold forms" up to "like the mudrā called Play" teaches the mudrā of Wondrous Bliss, embracing oneself and the liṅga. The passage from "In the manner of the serakha" up to "the mudrā of Saṃvara's play" is the mudrā of Illuminatrix, holding a crossed vajra in the right hand and a khaṭvāṅga in the left. The passage from "The begging bowl of the perfect Buddha is accomplished" up to "mudrā" teaches the mudrā of the Disciple Lady, holding a crossed vajra in the right hand and a begging bowl in the left.

"These and the rest, boundless and supreme" means that although the mudrās of these goddesses, the mudrās of the hands, the mudrās of the feet, the mudrās of the eyes, the mudrās of speech and so on, are boundless, the wisdom will descend through the very nature of the body, speech and mind of Vajrasattva and the others. "The assembly of the root mudrās": since Vajrasattva, father and mother, is the principal of the peaceful maṇḍala, it is called "the root."

The passage beginning "As the treatise, in accord, the life" teaches the mudrā of the Buddha-Awakening Lady. The passage beginning "Raising and lowering the foot" teaches the great seal of the Dharma-Wheel Lady. The passage beginning "Doing and turning back" teaches the mudrā of Trailokyavijayā. The passage beginning "Taking hold of any vine whatever" teaches the mudrā of Kāmeśvarī. The passage beginning "By acting as if inhaling a fragrance" teaches the mudrā of Paramāśva. The passage beginning "By inhaling with the nose's sense of smell" teaches the mudrā of the Boar-Faced Goddess. The passage beginning "By gazing with eyes drawn inward in longing" teaches the mudrā of the Chain Goddess. The passage beginning "The very self of all, tasting" teaches the mudrā of the Clapping Lady. The mudrās of the Flute Goddess, the Lute Goddess, the Small-Drum Goddess and the Clay-Drum Goddess are made clear by their names. The one śloka from "With the offering of the mudrā of dance" up to "is accomplished entirely, without remainder" teaches the mudrās of the Flower, Lamp and Perfume Goddesses. The passage from "Fevers, poisons and spells of poison made" up to "All will be accomplished as one's very self" teaches the benefits of the mudrās of these goddesses.

Having thus explained the mudrās of Vajrasattva for receiving the accomplishments, now the song common to the twenty-one deities is explained: the passage from "The union of all the buddhas" up to "obtains the unsurpassed accomplishment" teaches the common song. The one śloka beginning "Then" teaches the benefit of accomplishing the enlightened activities. The passage from "Glorious Vajrasattva, great bliss" up to "please accept" teaches the common song. The passage beginning "The union of all the buddhas" teaches the benefits of the common practice.

Having thus explained the mudrās and songs for receiving the accomplishments of the maṇḍala of Vajrasattva, now the great seal for receiving the accomplishments of the maṇḍala of Vairocana is taught. The passage from "That very yoga, the Bhagavān" up to "the mudrā of the glorious knower of the commitments" teaches the great seal holding the Dharma robes, the alms bowl and the ringed staff. The passage beginning "Its mudrā of the feet: by the gathering of the right limbs" teaches the sattva cross-legged posture of Vairocana.

Having taught the mudrā of Vairocana, the great seal of the Buddha-Melody Lady is taught in the passage beginning "Holding the vajra fist." The mudrā of Vīrā is taught in the passage beginning "Likewise the glorious Heruka." The mudrā of Samantabhadrā is taught in the passage beginning "The lotus woman." The mudrā of the Inexhaustible Lady is taught in the passage beginning "And the fist holding the jewel." The mudrā of the Embodied Lady is taught in the passage beginning "Holding the buddha fist." The mudrā of the Beautiful Lady is taught in the passage beginning "Having bound the mudrā of the lotus woman." The mudrā of the Great Cloud Lady is taught in the passage beginning "And the glorious fist of Paramāśva." The mudrā of the Sky-Eyed Lady is taught in the passage beginning "And the mudrā of the glorious accomplished eye."

Then the eight names in common are taught in the two ślokas beginning "First, the Buddha, guru of all." The song taught as a limb of accomplishing the accomplishments after binding the great seal is taught in two verses, beginning "Of these that are called mudrās," which give the method of singing the song together with its result. "From that, the clay drum has six." devata is the song of Tilottamā, the goddess of the Thirty-Three, and of the sequence. The passage from "Beings of many dispositions" up to "will be accomplished this very day" teaches the song common to all the goddesses of Vairocana. The meaning of the song of the goddesses of Vajrasattva and the song of the goddesses of Vairocana is: "Show the body of the deity to me, a yogin who has obtained the signs, and enter into the heart." The passage from "By singing these songs" up to "without doubt" teaches the benefits of the practice. "From that, the four women" means that one should worship in the manner of the great seals of the four beginning with the Flute Goddess, the four beginning with the Flower Goddess, and the four beginning with the Horse-Faced Goddess, and should practice for the accomplishments.

Having thus explained the great seal for receiving the accomplishments of the Tathāgata, now the great seal for receiving the accomplishments of Heruka is taught by the passage from "That very yoga, the Bhagavān" up to "of the Lord of the Vajra Dance." In it, "Abides with the glorious seal of the charnel ground" means adorned with ornaments of bone. The one śloka "Not by others, not to be stolen" teaches the water empowerment. The great garment is human skin. The great empowerment is the five skull crowns. The great flesh and great honey: it is the honey held in the left hand, filled with great flesh and blood. If one asks why the Bhagavān holds such things, it is in order to tame the bhūtas of the eight charnel grounds: the skull cup, the khaṭvāṅga, the garland of skulls, and Akṣobhya made into the crest jewel. "With the khaṭvāṅga" teaches the hand emblem. If one wonders what that hand emblem does: the beings to be trained by the Tathāgata, and those to be trained by the vajra, the lotus, the jewel and the crossed vajra, are sealed with the three of bringing under control; they are bewildered by enjoying the queen; they are devoured by enjoying the feast gathering; and through the dissolving of their life they are reduced to ash.

Having thus explained the four-faced, eight-armed form, in order to explain the one-faced, two-armed form it says, beginning "In the manner of the serakha": holding a skull cup in the left hand, he holds the khaṭvāṅga in the left armpit in the serakha manner, and in the right hand, in the three-banner gesture, he holds a five-pronged vajra.

"Women and the rest, the supreme jewels" means that he emanates the seven precious things, such as the precious queen, and enjoys them. "Slaying Indra, Rudra and Upendra" means that he slays the four māras and all the guardians of the directions and eats them.

The dance common to both the solitary lord and the four-faced, eight-armed one is taught in the passage beginning "By the union of right and left": dancing with both hands, in the sattva half cross-legged posture and standing, he brings under control all the māras, such as Rudra, enjoys them, and consumes them. In the passage beginning "By the union of that very one," he breaks up the dance of the half cross-legged posture and takes the right-extended stance; this is enjoying all the women among the goddesses of the Fortunate Eon. Planting the big toe of the right foot on the ground and placing the left on the thigh, he stretches out fully; moving and releasing is the reversal of the bodhicitta. This teaches the yantra of the body. Through those very illusions of the bodily yantra he enjoys gods and humans. Thus is taught the great seal for receiving the accomplishments of Heruka.

The great seals of his retinue are taught in the passage beginning "Then, further, what is to be explained." "Bringing down the glorious Heruka" means that, having brought down the wisdom into the feast gathering or into a maṇḍala of images, he abides in manifold illusions according to the inclinations of those to be trained. Among them, the moods of the retinue are these: the peaceful is Gaurī; the fierce is Caurī; the bewildered is Pramoha; the joyful is Vetālī; the one who dances and transforms is Pukkasī; the descending is Caṇḍālī's; the laughter of death is Ghasmarī's; abiding with the seal of bliss is Ḍombinī's. Further, having peaceful eyes is Gaurī's; having fierce eyes is Caurī's; having bewildered eyes is Vetālī's; gazing upward is Pukkasī's; the gaze of passion is Pramoha's; the gaze while dancing is Caṇḍālī's; the wrathful gaze is Ghasmarī's; the loving gaze is that of Ḍombinī, who grants the supreme accomplishment.

The hand emblems are taught: the flower is Gaurī's; incense, Caurī's; liquor, Pramoha's; water, Vetālī's; the vine, Pukkasī's; cloth, Caṇḍālī's; fire, Ghasmarī's; embracing the accomplished one is Ḍombinī's.

The mudrās of their minds are taught in the passage beginning "By one-pointed union." Sealing bliss through one-pointedness is Gaurī, the first. Raising both hands in praise, snapping the fingers, breathing out upward, gazing to the left, with the hand drawing out the heart: this is the second. Embracing with kisses and bewildering by dancing is the third. Raising the body with the intention of eating the one who is the target of the rite is the fourth. Performing within the skull of Brahmā the dance of the nectars' victory over the three worlds is the fifth. Likewise, drawing out from the earth, piercing the heart and showing it in the manner of her own mudrā is the seventh, the Slayer. Feeding into the mouth the one who is the target of the rite of the nāga maṇḍala is the Devourer's. Burning with an exceedingly terrifying fire is the mudrā of Heruka and Ḍombinī.

Then the responses for accomplishing the accomplishments through the answering mudrās are taught in the passage beginning "Then, by all these mudrās": at the time of accomplishing the accomplishments, when the master makes the mudrā of Gaurī, the disciple makes the mudrā of Ḍombinī. When the master makes Caurī's, the disciple makes Ghasmarī's; when the master makes Pramoha's, the disciple makes Caṇḍālī's; when the master makes Vetālī's, the disciple makes Pukkasī's mudrā.

Thus I shall explain the dance and the answering dance, the characteristics of the mudrās, and the special features of the mudrās, whose very nature is to crush the afflictions. "The glorious vajra asking for the lotus" means: first, by making with both hands a mudrā like a lotus in bloom, one seals, bewilders, devours, reduces to ash, and restores to life. The five actions are accomplished through the eight answering mudrās.

Having thus explained the karma mudrās, the song of the maṇḍala of Heruka is explained: the passage from "Then, of the knowing of mudrā" up to "I shall explain in particular" teaches the pledge to explain. The passage from "Seeing in manifold ways" up to "make it clear" is the song for receiving the accomplishments of Heruka. The one śloka "By singing these songs" teaches the benefits. This was the song for receiving the accomplishments of the mudrā of Heruka.

Now the mudrās of the maṇḍala of Padmanarteśvara are explained. The passage from "That very yoga, the Bhagavān" up to "mudrā" teaches the great seal of Padmanarteśvara. "By the union of right and left" teaches the mudrā of the feet. The mudrās of the goddesses of the retinue are taught in the passage beginning "With the proud left hand." Holding a lotus proudly in the left and opening it with the right is the Surveyor's. Holding a sharp lotus proudly in the left and drawing the right to the heart is Tārā's. Holding a lotus proudly in the left and turning the wheel of the earth with the right is the Earth-Holding Lady's. Holding a lotus panel proudly in the left and dancing with the right in the three-banner gesture is Bhṛkuṭī's. Holding a yellow lotus proudly and at ease in the left, and raising and holding a makara banner in the right, is Caṇḍeśvarī's. Holding a red lotus in the left and drawing the right to the heart is the Manifold Lotus Lady's. Holding a manifold lotus or a pale red lotus is the Lotus-Eater's. Holding a blue-white lotus in the left and opening it at the heart with the right is Vajreśvarī's.

Having thus explained the karma mudrās, the great seal of the body is taught by the passage from "The pure seer" up to "the great goddesses." Having explained the mudrās, the pledge to explain the song for receiving the accomplishments is given in the one śloka "Then, mudrā." The passage from "By all the tathāgatas" up to "Dance!" is the song for receiving the accomplishments of Padmanarteśvara. The one śloka "By singing these songs" is the benefit.

Having thus explained the mudrā and the song of Padmanarteśvara, now the great seal for receiving the accomplishments of Vajrasūrya is taught by the passage beginning "That very Union, the Bhagavān." As for
"For one who is extremely vicious and fierce,
peace brings no benefit":
Vajrasūrya, with a somewhat wrathful mood, has the form of the divine Sun, displaying a wrathful form, wrathful conduct, a wrathful manner, and great splendor. The rest is easy to understand. The one śloka "Binding the two vajra knees" teaches the foot mudrā.

Having shown the principal, the retinue is shown by "The Empowering and the treasures," and so on: the Empowering Lady, the Lady of Treasures, the Supreme Banner Lady, and the Lady of the Supreme. By "At the time of offering" are taught the one set beginning with the Arrow-Holding Lady, the four beginning with the Flute Goddess, and the four beginning with the Flower Goddess; these twelve are called the great offerings. By "Those who teach in accordance with how it is," the four gatekeepers beginning with the Horse-Faced Goddess are taught.

Having explained the mudrās, the song is explained. In "Then, mudrā," and so on, the one śloka is the pledge. The passage from "The kingship of all the three realms" up to "all go" teaches the song for receiving the accomplishments. The one śloka "By singing these songs" teaches the benefit.

Having explained the mudrā of Vajrasūrya, the mudrā of Paramāśva is taught: the passage from "That very yoga" up to "is the mudrā called Vajra Rousing" teaches the great seal of the body. "His foot mudrā: by the union of right and left," and so on, is taught. Standing on the four feet in the two postures, extended and drawn in, is the wrathful seated posture. The maṇḍala of his retinue is not taught, since it is considered easy.

Having thus explained the mudrā, the song of accomplishment is taught. "Then the mudrā": this one śloka teaches the pledge. The single line "A thousand intoxicators" teaches the body of magnetizing. The single line "Above, by a thousand Yamas" teaches the body of the fierce rite. The single line "From a thousand Brahmās" teaches the body of pacifying. The single line "maṇḍala" teaches the body of enriching. Having shown this, what does he do? He performs the four activities: to the four kinds of disciples who are devoted to the complete purity of all things, having subdued them by the force of their arrogance, he teaches the path of direct perception with the four syllables hi. And who are they? Having shattered all the lords, he made the four gods swoon. With the sound hulu hulu he subdued Maheśvara and carried off the Lady of Triple Accomplishment. With the sound mata mata he subdued the Mighty One and carried off Śrī. With the sound smanata he subdued Brahmā and carried off the Powerful Lady, and he subdued Kāmadeva, the god of desire, and carried off Rati.

Having taught the four activities of Paramāśva, it is through union with the four goddesses that he said "a la la ho." Future practitioners who practise by the door of secret mantra, too, having subdued the lords by the power of wrath and fierceness, say "a la la ho" at the time of union with the goddesses. The mantra by which later yogins make the four lords swoon is taught from "oṃ suṃbhani" up to "hūṃ phaṭ."

If one asks how, then, one becomes a buddha through liberating by fierce rites and through the union of desire, it is taught by
"Toward a friend or one who is not, what is to be done or not done;
even with no conceptualizing of them at all, it is easy,"
and so on. Being free of the eighty natures, when the wisdom without apprehended and apprehender arises, one is certain that all is like an illusion, and so there is no one at all who is a friend or not a friend. Likewise, knowing opponents to be like illusions, there is no one at all to be treated as an enemy. The one śloka "By singing this song" teaches the benefit.

Having thus taught the great seal, the pledge seal is explained by "Pressing the thumb with the index finger," and so on. Joining the palms in the vajra gesture, one presses the tips of the joints of the index fingers and thumbs, and the tips of the middle fingers are called the Eye of Glorious Accomplishment; this is the pledge seal for accomplishing the accomplishments of the maṇḍala of Vajrasattva. The mudrā of the maṇḍala of Vairocana is taught by "Joining the vajra palms evenly," and so on: having joined the two vajra palms, one bends the two index fingers and joins them to the tips of the thumbs, and places the thumbs of the palms on top. The mudrā of the maṇḍala of Heruka: opening the vajra palms upward, one bends the tips of the two index fingers and presses them with the two thumbs. Having subdued the Reposing One, he carried off the yakṣiṇī called Śrī and came; this is done by generating the four joys. The mudrā of Padmanarteśvara: joining the vajra palms evenly, one extends the middle fingers, and the thumbs and little fingers in the manner of a four-petalled lotus. The mudrā of the maṇḍala of Vajrasūrya: joining the vajra palms evenly, bending the two index fingers and covering them with the thumbs, is called the Glorious Jewel. The mudrā of Paramāśva: joining the vajra palms, interlacing the little fingers and ring fingers and extending them, and bending the tips of the two index fingers, is the mudrā.

Having thus taught the pledge seals, meditation on the great pledge seal of the colored-powder maṇḍala is taught by "Body, speech, and mind are vajra," and so on. One meditates on the yogin's body, speech, and mind as the deities of the colored-powder maṇḍala; holding the mudrā of signs, in the manner of the four stages of approach and accomplishment, one generates everything through the yoga of the deity of speech and meditates on oneself as the deity. One should meditate on the deities of the six maṇḍalas, each in its own maṇḍala. Why is it called a pledge? Because, having sealed body, speech, and mind with emptiness, one does not pass beyond the dharmadhātu of illusion and emptiness, it is called the great pledge.

Now the great pledge of the feast gathering is explained, by "Vajrasattva yoga," and so on: one meditates as endowed with the form and attire of the deity. As for "By bliss itself, the self of all": by the very bliss of coemergent joy, as the very nature of the bliss of all sense pleasures, one should engage in the five sense pleasures.

If one asks how that is the great pledge:
"Without attachment, and without non-attachment,
and the middle too is not apprehended":
this is the union of Vajrasattva, in which the emptiness of object and subject is meditated.
"It is not existent, nor is it nonexistent,
and in the middle too it is not apprehended":
since by meditating on the emptiness of mind the samādhi of the fourth empowerment arises, one meditates on the body of Vairocana as like luminosity, and so this is the mudrā of the union of Vairocana.
"All has the characteristic of space,
and space too has no characteristic,"
and so on, is the nature of luminosity. Having meditated in the form of Heruka, by enjoying the mantra-born, the field-born, and the wisdom-born women, the precious women and the rest, one attains the accomplishment of the lord of desire. This is the union of Heruka. "Just as this red lotus," and so on, is the union of Padmanarteśvara, of the nature of luminosity. "For one extremely vicious and fierce," and so on, is the appearance of luminosity, the union of Vajragītā. "Of those devoted to the purity of all," and so on, is meditation in the forms of Vajrasūrya and Paramāśva, the nature of luminosity: the wisdom of the mudrā of the union of Paramāśva.

Thus, of the twenty-eight mudrās for accomplishing the accomplishments, the great seal is the great pledge of meditating on the pledge colored-powder maṇḍala. The wisdom of the mudrā of the union of meditating on the deities of the feast gathering has now been taught.

Now the mudrā of the union of all is taught by "All the unions that there are," and so on. The yogin who emanates the maṇḍala, having meditated just as it is through the four of approach and accomplishment, through the many kinds of division of the four activities, wears the masks of the six maṇḍalas of Vajrasattva and the rest: white for pacifying, yellow for enriching, red for magnetizing, and black for the fierce rite. Like a dancer's performance, when meditating on the deities, one casts off the costumes as one meditates, and one then attains the accomplishments of Vajrasattva and the others.

"Acting and reversing," and so on, teaches the accomplishments of the activities of the thorough union of the six maṇḍalas. Acting is benefiting, and reversing is cutting off. In particular, stealing is subduing Īśvara and the others and carrying off the goddesses. "By acting with all the limbs": this is carrying off also the wealth of the limbs, which brings body, speech, and mind under control. Enjoying is sealing the three realms by kissing, embracing, and so on. Dissolving is liberation through the dissolving of wisdom, killing through the dissolving of the life force, and raising one up again immediately after death. Thus the mudrās of the six maṇḍalas are explained.

The facial mudrā is taught by
"The beautiful face of Vajrasattva,
the great illusion of all women,"
and so on: it is endowed with the flirtatious mood of the twenty goddesses beginning with Saṃvarā. The mudrā of the nature of the Tathāgata maṇḍala is
"Uniting all the buddhas,
gazing and smiling with supreme joy,"
and so on. The facial mudrās of Heruka are nine:
"Endowed with the moods, the erotic and the rest,"
and so on. The facial mudrā of Padmanarteśvara is taught by
"Dancing with flirtatious glances,"
and so on. That of Vajrasūrya is taught by
"The mouth with fangs slightly bared,"
and so on. That of Paramāśva is taught by
"Dallying through devotion to passion,"
and so on.

Having explained the facial mudrās, the smile mudrā is
"The supreme bliss of all the goddesses,"
and so on. The smile mudrā of Saṃvarā is
"Dallying with the bliss of passion,"
and so on. That of Wondrous Bliss is
"The supreme bliss of all unions,"
and so on. That of Illuminatrix is
"Tasting the flavor of the great flesh,"
and so on. That of the Disciple Lady is
"Tasting the flavor of all bliss,"
and so on. That of the five maṇḍalas of the Tathāgata and the rest is taught in common by the one and a half ślokas
"This supreme smile mudrā
devours even the three realms,"
and so on.

As for the wisdom of the gaze mudrā, the passage from "Richly endowed with bliss and emptiness" up to "makes a living" teaches the gaze of Vajrasattva. The one śloka "The skilled woman" is for Saṃvarā and the rest. The one śloka "The secret union of the buddhas," and so on, teaches the Tathāgata maṇḍala. The one śloka "A wrathful frown between the brows" explains the gaze of Heruka. The one śloka "Wrathful and transformed" explains the gaze common to Vajrasūrya and Paramāśva. The one śloka such as "Striking and the rest" teaches that of Padmanarteśvara. Thus the mudrā of the union of all, and the mudrās of the face, the smile, and the gaze, are of eight kinds.

As for the Dharma mudrā, "Make all the mother's bodies into child-spirits," and so on, teaches the Dharma mudrā of the maṇḍala of Vajrasattva. "ru ru sphu ru," and so on, is that of Vairocana. "su ba ga ste," and so on, is that of the maṇḍala of the Great Glorious One. "oṃ tāre," and so on, the mantra of Tārā, is that of Padmanarteśvara. "traṃ ba ba ta," and so on, the mantra of Māmakī, is that of Vajrasūrya. "śrī vajra padma bha ba ta mu kha," and so on, is that of the maṇḍala of Paramāśva. This is the wisdom of the Dharma mudrās of the six maṇḍalas.

As for the wisdom of the song mudrā: "The secret of the voice, high and low," and so on. With the sound "hūṃ hūṃ" and the rest, recited to the measure of thirty-two syllables, the "ka ka la," which is a sweet melody; and with "śarja ba" and the rest, meaning "beat the clay drums and the rest," one sings. This is of the maṇḍala of Vajrasattva. "The song mudrā of oṃ": with the melody of Brahmā, oṃ is recited in thirty-two syllables; one should sing the song and beat the long drum and the rest. This is of Vairocana. "Vajra Fierce Lady, great laughter," and so on: singing with the sound "rulu rulu," with hi and a single hūṃ, and beating the great drum, is that of Heruka. "te na te," and so on, the song mudrā, together with beating the netted drum, is that of Padmanarteśvara. hi is recited to the measure of sixteen syllables. The song mudrā with the beating of the round drum is that of Vajrasūrya.

"haṃ" is the raised voice of Paramāśva. "ka ka" means that there is a threefold modulation; "ka twice" means that there is a dissimilar modulation; "well-intoned and melodious" means reciting the endings of the song clearly; and "ka hi la" means singing an Indian song like a copper horn. This is the wisdom of the song mudrā of the six maṇḍalas.

The mudrā of instrumental music is explained by "The flute and the one-stringed," and so on. The flute is the one with ten holes, or with the five elements. The one-stringed is the single-stringed lute. The many-stringed is the nine-stringed zither. The dense-stringed is the one with twenty-one strings. Instrumental music here means the lute and the clay drum. If, having put on the attire of the deities of the six maṇḍalas, one plays music and sings as in the drama of the deities, accomplishment will be achieved in four months, or in one month. The heart is the mantras of the lords of the four families. The mudrā is the mantras of the goddesses. The mantra is the recitation mantras. The vidyā is the offering mantras. The various kinds of action are the dance of the feet and the dance of the hands, together with the glances of the eyes. The means, dispelling and the rest, are means such as laughing, looking, holding hands, and embracing.

Having thus explained the mudrā of instrumental music in common, the particulars are taught. The passage from "jaḥ hūṃ baṃ hoḥ ruṭ," and so on, up to "the drum of continuity," is the song for beating the drum of the maṇḍala of Vajrasattva. From "aṃ aṃ" up to "the drum of the three pledges" is the song of Vairocana. From "He comes" up to "of Heruka's drum mudrā" is that of Heruka. From "padma padma tri" up to "the drum of the clapping of palms" is that of Padmanarteśvara. From "traṃ traṃ" up to "the king's drum" is that of Vajrasūrya. From "ta ki hūṃ" up to "the swift drum" teaches the peaceful gait of Paramāśva, and the gaits of enriching, magnetizing, and the fierce rite. The wisdom of the mudrā of instrumental music is taught in common.

As for the explanation that pairs each syllable with a deity, as in the All-Heart, it does not come from a tantra, and there is no consistent scriptural reasoning for it either, such as taking the first and last letters of names. Indrabhūti said that, having played music with these songs:
"Abiding nowhere, from the experience of the vajra of sound,
insight is knowing the reality of sound.
Whoever knows the emptiness
of the sounds and forms of music,
by the gathering of their deeds
the wisdom of bliss will arise."

Having thus taught the wisdom of the mudrā of instrumental music, the mudrā for gathering the wisdom is taught by the passage from "By the self-union of Vajrasattva" up to "One should gather the maṇḍala with the palms." After the gathering has been done, by performing song, music, and dance, the nine moods are performed in order to please the tathāgatas. It is taught that each maṇḍala too should be worshipped with the nine moods of dance. The erotic is Vajrasattva. The heroic is the Tathāgata. The compassionate is Heruka. The comic is Lokeśvara. The furious is Vajrasūrya. The terrifying is Paramāśva. The repulsive, the meditation on impure substances, is Śākyamuni. The marvelous is the ḍākinīs of the four maṇḍalas. The peaceful is the great Vairocana, the six causal Vajradharas. As for "endowed with the moods, the erotic and the rest," by performing the dance of the deities of the six wheels, the wisdom of the four wheels will arise.

"The erotic of Vajrasattva," and so on, is explained in full. The passage from
"oṃ, Vajrasattva in the erotic,
in the heroic, the Tathāgata of heroes,"
up to
"and grants even what is supreme. svāhā"
teaches the singing of the vajra song. Its benefit is taught by the passage from "The self of all" up to "will grant the sublime." It is taught by the passage from "By these mudrā unions of the goddesses Saṃvarā and the rest" up to "thoroughly apply the offerings." The mudrā of the maṇḍala of Vairocana is taught by the passage from "Universally various, fortunate one" up to "all the buddhas will arise."
"Then, in reply, once again,
one becomes the great vajra-holder":
this is that of Heruka.
"Then, in reply again, in due order,
one transforms supremely into the lotus-dance-born":
this is that of Padmanarteśvara.
"Then again, in reply,
one transforms supremely into Vajrasūrya":
this is Vajrasūrya. "Then, by transforming again" is that of Paramāśva. The passage from "Then again" up to "one perfectly accomplishes arising as oneself" teaches the common accomplishment.

That the offering of the nine moods of dance is added in this way even without instrumental music is taught together with
"Then, by the word 'mudrā,'
teaching all the vows,
the glorious song of Vajrasattva
I shall explain in particular."
What song is it? "Somewhere, the stainless" explains the songs of Vajrasattva and Vairocana. "Somewhere, the greatly terrifying" teaches the song of Heruka. "Somewhere, of beautiful radiance" teaches that of Padmanarteśvara. "Somewhere, all beings greatly" teaches Vajrasūrya. "In order to accomplish Vajrasattva" teaches that of Paramāśva. "Tame them in many ways!" and so on, teaches the retinues of the six maṇḍalas. "By singing these songs," and so on, teaches the benefit. By "Having thus danced intensely," and so on: since not knowing the dance leads to defeat and transgression, one should offer the food of the feast gathering together with its attendant offerings.

"By these rites of offering": one makes offering having arranged the lords and yoginīs of the feast gatherings of the four families explained above; or else a beginner makes offering having arranged drops of scent or heaps of flowers, together with syllables, hand emblems, and great seals. It is also said in the Definitive Statement that one is freed from all evil and that all merit increases. The Dharma mudrā, the song mudrā, the mudrā of instrumental music, and the dance mudrā: these make twelve.

As for the explanation of the mudrās, the passage from "Supremely joyful" up to "a la la ho" explains the song of the maṇḍala of Vajrasattva. The passage from "Ḍākinī of all the tathāgatas" up to "without doubt" is the song of Vairocana.

The passage from "Because Vajrasattva is gathered" up to "Homage to the one who rings the vajra bell intensely" is that of Heruka. The passage from "Somewhere, thoroughly recollecting" up to "shows accomplishment" is the song of Padmanarteśvara. The passage from "Empowering all" up to "make it accomplished!" is the song of Vajrasūrya. The one śloka "Somewhere, of the wise" is the song of the maṇḍala of Paramāśva.

Having taught the wisdom of the song offering, the wisdom of flowers and the rest is taught by "To white and red flowers," and so on. This teaches the flowers, incense, lamps, scented water, and fragrant balls of food of the maṇḍala of Vajrasattva. "Red flowers," and so on, is easy to understand.

Having taught the offering mudrā, the substances such as the torma at the time of the feast gathering are taught by "The flirtatious lady and water," and so on. Connecting them with the vajra wisdom that arises later, it teaches the flirtatious lady, water, and the rest; this is easy to understand. This is that of Vajrasattva. The passage from "Offering food to the buddhas," and so on, up to "offering a horse," is the rite of offering and torma for the five wheels of the buddhas and the rest.

Having thus taught the wisdom of the torma mudrā, the empowerment mudrā is taught by "The thumbs in the sattva cross-legged posture," and so on. Crossing the thumbs, one bends the tips of the two index fingers, joins the joints of the two middle fingers evenly, and joins the rest in the vajra palms; touching the five places beginning with the crown of the head, one confers the empowerment of the five families. There is also the empowerment of the jeweled crown for the Tathāgata. The crown mudrā of Vajrasattva at the center of the maṇḍala of the feast gathering is taught by "Interlacing all the fingers inward," and so on: binding the vajra binding inward, joining the tips of the index fingers and thumbs, and setting them like the mouth and eye sockets of a dry skull, is the empowerment of the principal who gathers the families of the lords Heruka, Padmanarteśvara, Vajrasūrya, and Paramāśva. "Joining the vajra palms evenly," and so on, teaches the empowerment mudrā of the Tathāgata. "With the hands fully extended," and so on, teaches the empowerment of Heruka. "The thumbs in the hero's cross-legged posture," and so on, teaches the mudrā of Padmanarteśvara. "The vajra palms joined evenly," and so on, teaches the empowerment mudrā of Vajrasūrya. "Buddha, Jewel, Lotus, and," and so on, is the wisdom mudrā of the empowerment of Paramāśva.

Having thus taught the wisdom mudrā of empowerment, now the wisdom mudrā of all the vajras:
"Interlacing all the fingers
is explained to be all the vajras,"
and so on, explains the cause from which the common pledge mudrās arise. As for the common mudrā of invitation, "From that, the middle fingers as a hook," and so on, is stated. For the Tathāgata, "Having made the vajra binding," and so on, is taught. For Heruka, "From the binding of the vajra mudrā," and so on, is taught: the two mudrās of the descent of the vajra and of the vajra heart are the mudrās of Heruka. "Remaining in the vajra binding," and so on, teaches the mudrā of Padmanarteśvara. "Making the vajra mudrā into a lotus" teaches the mudrā of Vajrasūrya. "Fully binding the vajra mudrā," and so on, teaches the mudrā of Paramāśva.

If one asks what knowing that mudrā of all the vajras accomplishes: for a person who wishes to practise and to receive the accomplishments, binding the vajra palms, inserting the two middle fingers inside, and extending the thumbs and little fingers causes the wisdom to descend; fully transforming the vajra mudrā is meditating on oneself as the body of the deity; and making the middle fingers into hooks invites the wisdom being. These become the accomplishment of Vajrasattva and the limbs of the practice. In the same way, for the five, the Tathāgata and the rest, one should see to it that the wisdom is made to descend, the wisdom being is invited, and one meditates on oneself as the deity. Moreover, the first Vajrasattva, the central Heruka, and the descent of wisdom are said to be the wisdom being.

The mudrā of the mother, together with praise:
"Having made the vowels and consonants equal,
one should bow directly toward the mouth."
Of the sixteen vowels and the thirty-four consonants, for one who meditates on any of the deities of the six maṇḍalas, imagining a sun and moon stacked one upon the other at the heart is what is called "having joined the vowels and consonants together."

Here there are two: the conceptualization of form, which is the letters, and the conceptualization of sound. Taking it in terms of the conceptualization of sound, the vowels and consonants are the outgoing and incoming breath. Remaining equal is the wind that abides in the center of the navel. As for "one should bow directly toward the mouth": the three, navel, heart, and throat, by their action strike into the narrowing pipe, and the sounds are differentiated by the action of the eight places of sound.

Below that, the drawn shapes of sound that can be grasped by the fourth are to be set at the base like the trunk of a wish-fulfilling tree. The vowel signs of the sixteen vowels are made like vines, and by being placed as letters above the consonants they become stacked at the head, like sprouts growing from a vine. Then, like flowers appearing, there is the crescent of the deity's half moon; like fruit ripening, there is the drop of the anusvāra; and like leaves falling, there is the closure by the two dots of the visarga.

In the conceptualization of form, making the vowels and consonants equal and winding them all in rows without straying from the ruled lines is called "directly toward the mouth." Because all the vowels and consonants look toward the one before, it is called "one should bow." By "Below that, the drawn shapes," the vowels and consonants, held with the pen of method and wisdom, the thumb and index finger, are written so as to be visible to the eye. "With the curves one should make the vines," and so on, corresponds to the conceptualization of form and is easy to understand.

Moreover, the shapes of the letters are taught in the Tantra in a Hundred Thousand:
"a has the shape of a rooster's head.
ī is like an elephant's trunk.
ī resembles Maheśvara.
ū is just like a camel's head.
ū resembles a deer's mouth.
ṛ resembles a hare's head.
The letter ṝ is like a lion.
The letter ḷ is an elephant's mouth.
ḹ is like the face of Īśāna.
e resembles a buffalo's mouth.
ai is like a peacock's mouth.
o is like a cat's mouth.
au is like a dog's mouth.
aṃ resembles a makara's head.
The visarga aḥ is like a tiger's head."
So it is said. Likewise, the consonants are said to resemble a tortoise, a bee, a rhinoceros, a heron, a tsakśa, a parasol, a drum, a great drum, a rkenyek, an owl, a hoopoe, a ḍākinī, a patron's balance, a fanged bat, a bow, a partridge, a crane, a marsh bird, a magpie, a monkey, a pigeon, a ruddy goose, a cuckoo, a crow, a hare, a peaceful bird, a wolf, Hārītī, and a rākṣasa ḍākinī.

By "This, of all the buddhas," and so on, the conceptualization of form and the conceptualization of sound are taught. "The letters too are the supreme unchanging": by writing the name of Vajrasattva's letters, "vajra svāhā," the supreme unchanging, the essence of the perfectly established luminosity, is indicated; therefore the mantra letters a na ha are called "unchanging." If one asks how they indicate it: because they have become the signs of the buddhas and bodhisattvas. Moreover, the Later Guhyasamāja says, "The nature of the five syllables is the very essence of the five elements of the body."

As for the Tathāgata: if one praises having written the name of the Tathāgata, "Great Passion, Glory of Beings," at the center of the hand emblem upon the sun and moon at the heart, one attains accomplishment. Likewise, at the heart of Heruka, upon a moon, on the wrathful vajra, one writes the name "Stainless Compassion, Glory of the Wrathful," and praises. At the heart of Padmanarteśvara, upon sun and moon, one writes the name "Pure Glory of Lotus Passion," and praises. At the heart of Vajrasūrya, upon sun and moon, at the center of a jewel, one sets "Vajrasūrya, Radiant Glory," and praises. At the heart of Paramāśva, at the center of the crossed vajra upon sun and moon, one writes "Paramāśva, Glory of the Subduer of Adversaries," and praises. Accomplishment will then be swiftly achieved.

Now the correlation with other deities is taught by "Whichever are the additional deities," and so on. Whichever deity the flower strikes, whether Saṃvarā of Vajrasattva, Buddha Voice of Vairocana, Gaurī of Heruka, Vipaśyinī of Padmanarteśvara, the Empowering Lady of Vajrasūrya, or the peaceful Hayagrīva and the rest of Paramāśva, the master of the four maṇḍalas should write the name of that one and praise it.

When one benefits others, having made the disciple enter and having conferred empowerment, at the end one confers the name empowerment. If one asks whether that name will be stable, it is said:
"Through those unions of the mudrā,
until the heart of awakening,
one abides in the vajra name,
and then one becomes a buddha too."

Just as one knows the deity's family and gives the name by the omen of where the flower strikes, so one writes the letters of the vowels and consonants in four groups: the four groups of consonants leaving aside śa ṣa sa ha, with the first and last vowels, as four equal groups. For the four groups up to where ka stands, in which the letters of each five are incomplete, one fills them out with the four, śa ṣa sa ha, and with the neuter letters ṛ and ḷ counted as two, making four groups of eight. On the four spokes of a wheel the fifty-eight letters are arranged, and by touching, or by throwing a flower, one knows the family, or gives the name.

If, also by knowing the marks of color, shape, and deportment, and the signals of body, speech, and limbs, one recognizes the mantra-born, the field-born, and the wisdom-born heroes and yoginīs, one makes them the deities of the feast gathering, and one will know the characteristics of empowerment in the colored-powder maṇḍala. As it is said in the Sūtra of the Ten Dharma Practices, "From smoke one knows fire," and so on.

Wondering how the sign mudrā is known, it is taught by "Whatever is anointed on the crown," and so on. At the time of holding a feast gathering, or when, having obtained the signs at the colored-powder maṇḍala, one receives the accomplishments, the limbs and minor limbs that have become the signs of the buddhas and bodhisattvas are the signals of the body. Since ordinary people do not utter them, and the bodily signals of ordinary people are not made either, they serve as the conventional language of heroes and yoginīs.

Having explained the sign mudrā, the sign mudrā of undefiled words is explained: "a bi pu," and so on, is taught as a sign of something. "a bi pu ta pra ti pu ta" belongs to the homage to the maṇḍala. As for the signs and conventions of the undefiled feast gathering: "Vajrasattva, supreme of bliss," and "How wonderful, bliss without beginning or end," these four are the names of Vajrasattva. Saṃvarā has two names, with "the Sealing Lady." Wondrous Bliss has two names, with "the Deluding Lady." Illuminatrix has two names, with "the Profound." The Disciple Lady has two names, with "the Life-Increasing Lady." The remaining fourteen goddesses have one name each.

By moving the eyebrows, Saṃvarā holding the four arrows is indicated. By showing the bhaga and liṅga, Wondrous Bliss is indicated. A skull cup and khaṭvāṅga indicate Vairocana. The buddha mudrā, two skull cups filled with barley on the ground, indicates the Disciple Lady. Showing a wheel indicates the maṇḍala. Wearing the ornaments is itself explained to be the pledge. The gathering of holy persons wearing the attire of all the deities is the feast gathering. Those signs of the buddhas and bodhisattvas are said to be what is done when friends gather.

If one asks why it is a maṇḍala:
"The glorious union of Vajrasattva
is explained to be all maṇḍalas":
this teaches the maṇḍala of the body.
"All the goddesses, the fortunate":
this teaches the maṇḍala of the bhaga. The passage from "The glorious union of Vajrasattva" up to "is explained to be the master of all" teaches the maṇḍala of the dharmadhātu. This is also stated in the Definitive Statement, by "The maṇḍala is explained to be the body," and so on.

If one asks what kind of master has authority over the three maṇḍalas, the one śloka "The master of all, well established," and so on, gives the characteristics.

If one asks what kind of disciple of his is the companion in practice, the one śloka beginning "If his disciple is accomplished" explains that this is the master of the rites, the regent. If one asks what the remaining disciples are like, the one śloka "All the disciples other than that" explains that they are bodhisattvas of lesser, middling and highest capacity, who accomplish the worldly, the common and the supreme siddhis.

The one śloka "All the buddhas, precious" indicates, by the crown of the five buddhas, the master of the colored-powder maṇḍala. The four skull cups and the buddha crown indicate the master of the feast gathering. One who is accomplished as the nature of Amitābha, and empowered with four lotus petals, belongs to the lotus family. The crown of jewel sprouts indicates the master of the jewel family. For the family of Paramāśva, the five heart mantras are recited, and binding with five garlands of the supreme of riches indicates the master who confers the empowerment of Paramāśva. The passage beginning "Camphor, red sandalwood, and" gives the code words for the five nectars of the secret empowerment.

As for the empowerment of wisdom and gnosis: since the sequence is first to administer the oath, then to proclaim the commitments, and then to confer the empowerment, one first makes from dough the figure of go ku da ha na, fills its inside with blood, and smears its outside with red sandalwood. Since this is what is explained as "the king's food cooked in butter," one offers it, together with a torma, to the ḍākinīs. Then one places on the disciple's tongue the five meats, or, if the five meats are lacking, a drop of the figure's blood, and administers the oath. That is why, in all the tantras, "the rite of slaughtering cattle" refers to the dough figure.

If one asks what names are given to the goddess of the empowerment of wisdom and gnosis, four names are given: Saṃvarā, "Oh, Blissful One," and so on. These goddesses are mantra-born and field-born. One causes the gnosis of the bliss of Vajrasattva to descend, gives her the code name of the buddhas, and blesses her as the mudrā. All the practices of the four joys are then explained to be the nonconceptual calm abiding of the buddhas and the gnosis of insight. When one realizes that Sparśavajrā, the Vajra Lady of Touch, one comes to know all dharmas both in brief and in full; therefore afterwards, by enjoying that goddess born from gnosis, one comes to realize the emptiness of the three realms without remainder.

By the code word "Tathāgata," form is indicated: the form of the karmamudrā, who wears ornaments on her face and is painted with designs. Sound is her song. Smell is her scent. Taste is the sucking of her tongue and lips. Touch is the bliss of the four joys, free of the eighty natures. If one asks by the code of which mudrā the five sense pleasures are designated, it is said:
"By the mudrā of the buddhas' code,
it is explained to be the Dharma mudrā."
So it is to be taken as the dharmamudrā.

If one wonders what to do if, not having passed into nonconceptuality, one has passed into the joy of cessation, the one śloka "With all the enjoyments of union" teaches that one should make offerings. This is also stated in the Cakrasaṃvara:
"With the mind drawn inward,
one should meditate on the desired siddhi.
By joining in union the drops, one's own substances,
one offers to the buddhas and bodhisattvas."

If one asks which yoga such a samādhi is, the one śloka "If one offers through anuyoga" calls the supreme joy of entering union with the goddess anuyoga. If one asks what yoga is, the one śloka "Absorbed in mind alone" explains the joy called yoga. If one asks what atiyoga is, the one śloka "Through union with all the goddesses" teaches the innate joy, by joining the dharmamudrā with the five sense pleasures. By the code word "union," the anuyoga of supreme joy is explained as great bliss. The full application of the samādhi of the four joys is explained. Innate joy, since it is union with all the buddhas, is explained as the blessing of the buddhas.

If one wonders what to do when, the mind having been drawn inward, one has passed into the joy of cessation, it is taught in the passage beginning "The accomplishment of the conduct of joy is explained." The subsequent accomplishment of anuyoga is supreme joy, because the wisdom consort becomes blissful. "Acting like a man, the conduct of joy" is the bliss of method, that is, joy. "Again the subsequent accomplishment is explained" is mental bliss, that is, innate joy. The one śloka "By pouring bliss into all the goddesses" explains the joy of cessation, the bliss of extracting the essence of the joy of cessation. If one asks when it is designated the joy of cessation, the one śloka "All arising, going everywhere" explains the buddhas' code word "joy of cessation."

If one wonders what one should meditate on after supreme joy and innate joy, if one has not passed into the joy of cessation, the two ślokas "The great siddhi, supreme of all" teach what is called great bliss, by the code of all the buddhas, through eight names: the great siddhi, the supreme of all, Maheśvara, Supreme Bliss, the Union, the lord of yoga, great bliss, and the unsurpassed.

Thus it is clearly taught in this very tantra, and in the Hevajra, the Definitive Statement, the Vajraḍāka, the Herukābhyudaya, the All-Conduct of the Ḍākinīs, and the Catuṣpīṭha, that the yogin who meditates on the feast gathering meditates on the four joys with the mind drawn inward, and, when the mind has come forth outward, on the four joys of enjoyment. The All-Heart, not having understood this, explains that the deity is generated through the generation stage. That one meditates on the four joys of enjoyment is like this very tantra and the Emergence of Radiance.

Now, as for the feast gathering of these, the peaceful drink, and the pūjā substances and sense pleasures that serve as limbs of the purity of the messenger: the undefiled code words, set over the code words known in the world, which serve as limbs of siddhi, are these. What is known in the world as blood is called "flower" in the code of the buddhas and bodhisattvas. The radiant essence is called "incense." The rest should be understood in the same way. The one śloka "Of the union of Vajrasattva" gives the song and dance of the maṇḍala of Vajrasattva the code name "emanation of the buddhas."

The song and dance of the Tathāgata are given the name "emanation of peace." The song of Vajrasūrya is given the name "emanation of the lord of the radiant ones." The song and dance of Heruka are given the name "emanation of the charnel ground." The song and dance of Padmanarteśvara are explained as "emanation of passion." The song and dance of Paramāśva are given the name "emanation of utmost joy."

The verbal code that is thus taught: the two verses beginning "ṭi is called ḍākinī" are the code for the mantra-born, field-born and gnosis-born of the maṇḍalas of Vajrasattva and of the Tathāgata. The goddess of Heruka is called a ralli. That of Padmanarteśvara is explained as "supreme commitment." That of Vajrasūrya is called vajra a ralli. For Paramāśva it is the same.

The code for the yogins of the four families, Vajrasattva and the rest, who are mantra-born, field-born and gnosis-born, is taught in the passage beginning "All the buddhas in union." The yogin of Vajrasattva is explained as "fortunate one." The yogin of the Tathāgata is explained as ta ki. That of Heruka is explained as ki li ki la. That of Padmanarteśvara is explained as "the child with the makara banner." Vajrasūrya is explained as "supreme lord of desire." That of Paramāśva is explained as "supreme lord." The vajra, the bell and the six ornaments are explained as "commitment." Body, speech and mind are explained as oṃ bhu khaṃ. The four acts of summoning and drawing in are explained as jaḥ hūṃ baṃ hoḥ. "Come here" is e hi. "Go away" is a hi. u ti ta means "rise!" svāhā means "depart!" khā hi means "eat!" The rest is easy to understand.

In the Saṃvara, the passage from "the Sealing Lady" up to "the Lady of Great Teaching" is the code for the eight goddesses, Saṃvarā and the rest. By the passage beginning "The vajra-holder and the buddha," Vajrasattva is explained as "becoming knowing," and Lokeśvara is explained as "becoming joyful." Again, the buddha, Vajrasattva, Lokeśvara and the Sky-Born are called aṃ aṃ aṃ aṃ aṃ. The Omniscient One is called sarva vid.

In the mantra of Buddhalocanā, ru ru is saṃsāra. tiṣṭha siddhaṃ is "knows." lo ca na is the ultimate, utter pacification. The self-arisen buddha, the Bhagavān, is sarva artha. ki ta sa is the eye, Kṣitigarbha. ko ta ba is the ear, Vajrapāṇi. ko ta ba is the tongue, Avalokiteśvara. ka ti ra ka is the body, Sarvanīvaraṇaviṣkambhin. ko la śa is form, Buddhalocanā. ko la ba is sound, Māmakī. ko la is smell, Tārā. ko la ba is Pāṇḍaravāsinī. ko la ma is touch, Samantabhadrī. What is expressed by these names of Vajrapāṇi himself is the realization of the emptiness of the consciousnesses of the objects, from the eye up to the tangible.

The one and a half verses beginning "Then the king, the vajra-holder" give the benefits. The one verse "The eye and" gives the code names for one's own aim, stated above. The maṇḍala of mental consciousness is also called "vajra weapon," bi ti, "enemy," and Vajrapāṇi. The lord of the yakṣas is also called "exhaustion of all afflictions," "exhaustion of enemies," and Vajrapāṇi. The great Vajradhara is given the names Heruka, "Destroyer into Ashes," "Supreme," and "Wrathful One of All the Buddhas." That is why the name "Blazing Vajra" is explained as "Utter Destroyer."

The khaṭvāṅga is called "great banner." The skull cup is called "bowl." Great blood is called "honey." The eight, Lāsyā and the rest, are known as Gaurī and Caurī. The four gatekeeper ladies are called "Guard!", "Quickly," "Glory of Victory," "Four Ashes," and "Lightning." Or else, the Horse-Faced Goddess is haṃ, the Sun and Moon Lady is dra, and the Lady Who Reduces to Ashes is hūṃ. phaṭ means "play!" raṅ means "consider!" phaṭ means "play!" phaṭ means "stay!" These are the code of Heruka.

The code of the maṇḍala of Padmanarteśvara is taught in the passage beginning "Padma, Avalokiteśvara, lord." Vipaśyinī is called "joy." Tārā is called "accomplishment." The Ornamented Lady is called "intellect." Bhṛkuṭī is called "miraculous power." The Robed Lady is called "increase." Padmeśvarī is called "union." The Manifold Lotus Lady is called "jewel of the lotus." Ākāśagarbha is known as "Lady Each of All," "Perfecter," "Fortunate," and "Vajrasūrya, great jewel." His retinue is empowered by traṃ. ba ba ma is the Lady of Treasures. This belongs to the Lady of the Tathāgata's Banner. tra ta tra ta is the Lady of Great Offering.

The code of Paramāśva is taught in the passage beginning "Manifold, buddha, lotus eye," namely:
"Manifold lotus, Buddhalocanā,
Lokeśvara, all awakening,
great being, manifold vajra, unsurpassed, devoted to all,
terrifying all the worlds."
These, with Paramāśvā the glorious, possessing twelve names, are the code of Paramāśva. Therefore there are twenty mudrās.

Having thus explained the twenty mudrās for accomplishing siddhi, now the maṇḍala in which masks are worn and signs are produced is taught, saying "one should follow with passion," in the passage beginning "Acting in attire as one impassioned." Two ślokas teach putting on the attire of Vajrasattva and producing the signs of the first dhyāna. Each single śloka teaches putting on the attire of one of the five maṇḍalas, Vairocana and the rest.

Having thus taught the gnosis mudrā of the signs that are seen, heard and touched, the mudrā of the higher knowledges is explained in the passage beginning "The glorious riches of Vajrasattva." Higher knowledge is produced through a samādhi in which one has been trained for a long time, or it is produced by means of a yantra. The method is taught as follows: being endowed with form, color, shape and the nine moods of dance, and joining with the goddess as the mudrā of method, so as to accomplish the supreme, unchanging body of the deity, then in all objects, in all things, through all the distinctions of everything, all the aspects of cognition in oneself become the siddhi of Vajrasattva. This is what is taught.

Having thus taught the common higher knowledges, Vajrasattva is taught in particular. A yogin who has obtained the signs smears a patch of ground the size of a cowhide and makes it into drops of the five nectars, then worships with the five offerings. He gives the vows to a girl or a young boy who is unsullied by the village practice. He sets out the heart mantras of the four families in the four places, and on top of the maṇḍala draws, in a circle, the maṇḍala of Vajrasattva exactly as it is, and sets out the syllables. In the four intermediate directions are the recitation vases of the four consorts, Saṃvarā and the rest: into them one puts the five medicines, the five grains and the five jewels, binds their necks with new cloth, and adorns their mouths with sprigs of foliage. In the victory vase one places the principal deity, and recites the mantra a hundred thousand times.

Then the one, oneself or another, in whom one wishes to produce higher knowledge is empowered with the water of the victory vase and given protection, and is made to touch, on that maṇḍala, the syllable of whichever deity it is. At that time one says, "By the truth of the Three Jewels, by the blessing of the great truth, by the truth that all dharmas are like illusions and that the nature of reality is utterly pure, and by the blessing of truth, may it show things just as they are!" and has him touch it. Alternatively, without touching, flowers may be cast; or one blesses the eyes and has him look into a sword, a mirror, a wooden board, clear water or cow's urine, a smooth rock face, or smooth chambers in which reflections appear. One blesses the ears and has him listen. One blesses the mind and examines the minds of others. One sets the wheel of the four classes of beings at the heart and examines death, transmigration and birth. One meditates on the totalities and the spheres of mastery, and meditates on knowledge of the domain of miraculous powers.

By this, worldly affairs, what is to be adopted and what rejected, wealth lost and not lost, robbed by bandits and not robbed, gain and no gain, victory and defeat, success and failure of an aim, accomplishment and nonaccomplishment, happiness and unhappiness, the desire realm, the form realm and the formless realm, and even all the aims of the buddhas: having produced the five higher knowledges through the power of the yantra, one acts for the benefit of beings.

Having produced the higher knowledge of Vajrasattva, the yogin of Vairocana should produce higher knowledge with regard to earth, water, fire, wind and space. The yogin of Heruka should produce it with regard to all the sense pleasures. The yogin of Paramāśva should produce higher knowledge, as before, with regard to the ten media for calling down a prognostic image, with touching or without touching, and so on.

Having thus explained the gnosis of the mudrā of consciousness that knows the full extent of things, I shall now explain the gnosis of the protection mudrā that vanquishes the māra who is a son of the gods. Vajrasattva, glorious and rich: endowed with form and shape just as they are, the master of the colored-powder maṇḍala generates himself as the deity through the four phases of approach and accomplishment; or the master of the feast gathering may put on the costume and ornaments, either will do. Ash from corpses burned in a charnel ground, or the powder of skeletons long aged in a place of the mātṛkās, is mixed with the five nectars and a tilaka is made with it on the practitioner's forehead; or else three-finger lines are drawn in the three places, heart, throat and forehead. This will protect oneself and others. If that ash is scattered in the four directions, the four intermediate directions, the sky and the ground, it will give protection. In the same way, one should understand that this very protection serves for the five maṇḍalas too.

If one wonders what that ash is like: when one has done the approach upon ash mixed with the five nectars, and signs such as smoke, steam and leaping have appeared, it is the protection called "the protective commitment of vajra ash."

Having explained the protection by ash of the four families, the protection by water is explained in the passage beginning "Vajrasattva, glorious union." Meditating as the four deities wearing masks, on the shore of an ocean or of a flowing river, one meditates on the maṇḍala in the water. When one sees the water flowing upward and becoming motionless, then with that supreme peaceful water, the divine substance of all the deities melted, one sprinkles oneself and others, pours it on the crown of the head, and bathes. Drawing off that water and crossing it protects the world of inhabitants, and the world as container is protected likewise, as before.

Having explained the protection by water of the four families, the protection by growing mustard seed is taught in the passage beginning "Vajrasattva, glorious union." Having meditated as before, or having generated oneself wearing the costume of the deities, one plants mustard seed in a human skull cup filled with charnel-ground earth with the mantra of Buddhalocanā, pours water mixed with the five nectars with the mantra of Māmakī, weeds it with the mantra of Pāṇḍaravāsinī, and protects it with the mantra of Tārā. When it has ripened, one has it threshed by the hands of a girl of the śūdra caste, and ties it on the body or at the neck. One makes a vegetable dish of its leaves, anoints the head with mustard oil, and protects the directions and intermediate directions as before.

Having thus explained the protection by the mustard seed of the families, the protection of the body maṇḍala of the families is taught in the passage beginning "Vajrasattva, glorious union." The yogin who meditates on Vajrasattva generates in the eyes a sun and a moon from mā and ṭa, and on the pupils a five-pronged vajra from hūṃ, and should declare that the element of space is filled with the light rays of tiny vajras like lightning. When he has obtained the sign of seeing the tiny vajras, he presses his lower lip with his teeth, holds the vajra and bell, and walks with the vajra gait, swaggering to the left. With a clear gaze he circles the ten directions, blesses above and below, and seals the three levels: beneath the earth, above the earth, and upon the earth. Likewise the evil natures of the feet of the disciples who enter the maṇḍala become fit vessels. One should understand the same for the four maṇḍalas.

Having taught the common mudrās, the particular ones are taught in the passage "Vajrasattva, glorious union." Fourteen women are dressed in the costume of the goddesses, as described in the Barti, and in their midst one dresses oneself in the costume of Vajrasattva and meditates according to the four phases of approach and accomplishment. With the gazes, postures and mudrās of the goddesses, the dance of the mudrās, and the recitation of mantras, saying "rakṣa rakṣa so-and-so," one will protect oneself and others, bind the directions, and so on.

The yogin of Vairocana puts on the costume of the deity of the maṇḍala of Vairocana, and seventeen women put on the costume of the deities as described in the Barti; then, with gazes, with hands and feet, and with the recitation of mantras, one will give protection as before. If the yogin of Heruka acts as described in the Barti, with the magical display of Heruka, he will give protection. If the yogin of Padmanarteśvara puts on the costume as described in the Barti, with form and shape just as they are, and with the opening of the lotus, he will give protection as before. If the yogin of Vajrasūrya sits in the manner of the blazing vajra gaze and puts on the costume of the deities of the maṇḍala of Vajrasūrya as in the Barti, he will give protection as before.

The yogin of Paramāśva acts according to the manner of the maṇḍala of Paramāśva just as it is, and empowers with the mudrā that confers the empowerment of Paramāśva. He generates armor from haṃ khaṃ, ties three knots at the heart and two knots each at the breasts and the throat, one at the forehead, and one knot at the nape behind the two ears, and wipes away the remainder; this is called "in the manner of armor." Then he ties vajra garlands at the crown of the head, the forehead, the right and left sides and the back, and also ties garlands as one ties silk ribbons, and thus he will protect himself and others and bind the directions.

Having thus explained the protection mudrās for obtaining siddhi, the gnosis of the mudrās for pacifying disease and increasing life is now explained in the passage beginning "Showing the manner of the supreme being." Through the two phases of approach and accomplishment one generates oneself in the body of Vajrasattva and binds the two vajra fists. From the body of the sick person one imagines the fever spirit, three-eyed, three-armed, three-legged and black, and forms of diseases, scorpions, snakes, evils and the like. Binding the mudrā of Trailokyavijaya, one makes the index fingers into hooks, and so removes the disease and poison; if that very mudrā is placed between the eyebrows, it gives protection.

From the bound vajra, the tips of the two index fingers are bent and pressed on the belly of a pregnant woman, and the child will be born. If they are pressed on abscesses, tumors, dropsy and the like, and the mantra is recited a hundred thousand times, the stabbing pains will be healed.

For healing the eyes, there is what is called the Eye of Glorious Accomplishment: the index fingers are bent to the base of the thumbs, and the tips of the thumbs and middle fingers are joined; this is called the Eye of Glorious Accomplishment. If one recites the mantra a hundred thousand times and wipes the eyes, the blind will see.

For healing diseases of the brain: in the union of oneself with the deities, one draws out the disease of the brain with the vajra fist, the Tathāgata fist, the wrathful fist, the lotus fist, the jewel fist and the karma fist, and makes it come out through the mouth; the disease of the brain will then be healed.

For healing the ears: one generates two white five-pronged vajras from hūṃ in the ears, imagines a white five-pronged vajra on one's own tongue, recites ta thā ga ta hūṃ into the ears a hundred thousand times, and blows into the ears.

For healing children: from the bound vajra, one extends the two middle fingers, and extends the thumbs and little fingers separately; one places the middle finger of the right hand on the crown of the child's head and ties the topknot with sumbha, and the child's illness will be healed.

For healing poison: one imagines in the palm of the right hand a moon disk from a, and the five fingers arising from it as the spread hoods of snakes. At the practitioner's feet one imagines wind from yaṃ, and at the navel fire from raṃ, and above it a black figure in the form of a man riding a black goat. One imagines the poison burned by the blazing fire, summoned and subdued, bound with iron chains of light, and devoured by the fire god. Then those who have been seized by the fangs of venomous creatures and the like, by tigers, and by poisons in clothing and in food will recover.

For healing graha afflictions: with the wrathful threatening finger, one makes the right thumb and the two middle fingers like an eye, and does the same with the left wrathful fist. One joins the backs of the third joints of the right and left middle fingers. By separating the thumbs and index fingers, a triangle is formed between the index and middle fingers; and by placing the two middle fingers on the first joints of the thumbs, a square is formed between the index fingers, middle fingers and the two thumbs. Binding these mudrās of pacifying, increasing and fierce rites, one removes graha afflictions and poisons.

The mudrā for raising the dead: one holds the mudrā of Heruka and binds the two three-banner hands. Turning them inward, one knots the two middle fingers, and knots the two index fingers and the two little fingers each with its pair. By joining the tips of the right ring finger with the left thumb, and of the left ring finger with the right thumb, one forms what is called the five-pointed mudrā, and places it at the ūrṇā between the eyebrows. Thereby one subdues all the mātṛkās, seals the three worlds, and revives those who have been stupefied and reduced to ashes.

The mudrā for healing horses: one generates the horses from traṃ, generates a lotus seat from hrī, generates a moon disk from a, and generates Vajrasattva from hūṃ, or generates Paramāśva; they will then be free of disease and their vigor will grow.

For healing elephants: one generates the elephant from hūṃ ga hūṃ, generates a lotus seat and a moon seat, generates the deities of the four families, and imagines nectar dripping down; the vigor and strength of the elephant will then increase.

For healing birds: one generates the bird with hri maṃ hri and recites śrī vajra satva stvaṃ a gi ho into its ear; it will then be free of disease and will speak human language.

For growing trees: one practices the yoga of the four families, Vajrasattva and the rest, and from hūṃ maṃ hūṃ, under the asterism Puṣya and the planet Jupiter, generates all the fields with the mantra of Buddhalocanā. One waters them with the mantra of Māmakī, guards them with the mantra of Tārā, weeds them with the mantra of Pāṇḍaravāsinī, and protects them with the mantras of Sumbha and the others; they will then be free of disease, and their flowers and fruits will increase.

Having thus blessed with the gnosis of the healing mudrās, the gnosis of the weapon mudrās is explained in the passage beginning "thrown by a device and not thrown." What is thrown by a device is the arrow. What is not thrown is the spear. What is thrown by hand is the discus. What is thrown and then caught back again are the arrow, the spear and the noose. Wielding strength is the strength of the arm.

The same is explained in detail in the passage beginning "Shape, ease and application." There, there are the application of throwing by a device, full application, drawing, filling, clarity, and increase. They are explained as follows. Placing the feet side by side makes the even stance; spreading the feet makes the sa ga stance. "Those maṇḍalas also" means pressing the ground in a circle. "By stretching and bending also" means the right leg extended and the left bent. The right extended is the mudrā of shape.

The ease is explained in detail as follows: one keeps the left upper arm from slackening; when one aims at the target, one sets it like a pea; one braces by bending the right arm; and by drawing back to the nape behind the ear the limbs are braced. This is called "the body mudrā possessing ease," and the rest gives the benefits.

The application is explained in detail by the one śloka "In the manner of drawing to one's own heart." For the full application, the one śloka "The left hand well straightened" is the detailed explanation of drawing. The one śloka "Round, up to beside the ear" is the detailed explanation of filling. The one śloka "Just as knowledge is drawn" is the detailed explanation of speed, together with the one śloka "Having passed, one should cover the target."

Throwing is explained in detail in the words beginning "It is square, with four gates." Circling the arrow on the right side, one draws it full and lowers it toward the ground, and aims it at the target or at the ground. Bending the knee of the left leg, one plants the right on the ground: this is called "pressed by the focus." "By the supreme victor of the ten piercings" means that one generates the fingers of the right hand as the five buddhas, and imagines that by that supreme mudrā the arrow strikes the five aggregates and the five faculties.

Clarity is explained in detail; it teaches the common rite of the five weapons. The five, throwing forth, the even, throwing forth and so on, are the mudrās of method. "Fearful, startled, at ease and so on" are the five mudrās of drama. "Lifting all and lifting definitively" are the five mudrās of the garland. "Everywhere, entirely, in all ways" are the five mudrās of bone and the rest. The twenty is the term for what is called "clearly discerning all the enemy factions." These weapons are the mudrās for hurling at the deities in front and at the obstructing māras when one takes siddhi, and by which, when one performs activities, one will be victorious in battle.

Having thus taught the weapon mudrās, the mudrās of all actions are taught in "Inside the two ring fingers." One accomplishes the siddhi of the vajra fist of Vajrasattva, and of the fists of the Tathāgata, the wrathful, the lotus, the jewel and the karma, and having obtained the signs, one performs the actions.

Having thus explained the gnosis of the hand mudrās, the gnosis of the foot mudrās of the glorious vajra action is taught in the passage beginning "Extending the foot and pressing." The yogin of Vajrasattva, when he takes siddhi on the maṇḍala of the effigy, performs the actions, and practices the discipline of the conduct of the feast gathering, uses the eight-word line: the right half cross-legged posture, and "pressing," the left half cross-legged posture with the leg bent; then with the lifted vajra cross-legged posture he presses the effigy, and when it cries out he should issue his command. Likewise the yogin of Vairocana presses the Glorious One with the vajra cross-legged posture; Heruka, the vajra holder, extends the left leg; Padmanarteśvara presses with the lotus cross-legged posture; Vajrasūrya presses with the jewel cross-legged posture; and Paramāśva presses with the karma cross-legged posture.

If one wonders whom one enjoys after pressing: having pressed Īśvara, one summons the Lady of the Three Siddhis; having pressed Viṣṇu, one summons Vīrā; having pressed Brahmā, one summons the Liberated Lady; having pressed Kāmadeva, one summons Rati; and one should enjoy their retinues, the mātṛkās and the ḍākinīs. Likewise, having pressed Kubera, one summons the yoginī of the accomplishment of vidyā; having pressed a king of men, one summons his queen; and one should enjoy them.

Having thus explained the foot mudrās: when one knows the colors, shapes and deportment of the limbs of practice, the mantra-born, field-born and gnosis-born, one makes signs with the limbs and the minor limbs. Knowing them as limbs of practice, the mudrā of summoning is taught in the passage beginning "Binding the vajra fist at the heart." Having explained the common mudrā, the summoning of the goddess of Vajrasattva in particular is given by the one śloka "In the manner of swaggering to the left." That of Vairocana is the one beginning "Glorious holder of the manifold." That of Heruka is the one "Sounding the bell in the left hand." That of Padmanarteśvara is the one "Placing it on the left thigh." That of Vajrasūrya is the one "In the manner of drawing to my heart." That of Paramāśva is the one śloka "In the manner of drawing to my own heart."

Thus the ninth chapter makes the twenty-eight mudrās into twenty-eight limbs of practice. When a beginner practices, and when the signs have been obtained, in these two instances; when a yogin who has become supreme upon obtaining the signs takes siddhi; and when a yogin who, having obtained siddhi, has mastery over gnosis performs actions for the benefit of beings, they serve as limbs in a pure and proper way. This is the commentary on the ninth chapter.

Having thus explained the nine chapters, now is explained the meditation on the path of direct perception, through union with the mahāmudrā of the yogin who blesses himself, devoted to mahāmudrā, the nature of the dharmadhātu. "Then, in all the manifold" refers to the six yogins who meditate as the six deities. "This practice called mudrā" refers to the distinctions of the six families and the four mudrās. "Through manifold union in every way" refers to generating the gnosis of the four joys in the four mudrās.

"I shall explain the manifold in every way" refers to applying this to the twenty-four mudrās of the four yoginīs.

Having taught it thus in brief, the detailed explanation is given in the passage beginning "Eyebrows, eyes, nose and lips." The six mudrās that resemble oneself take the consort's eyebrows, eyes, teeth and so on as their adornment, and all the mudrās embrace, kiss and so forth. Having released the ripening, there are manifold aspects; and having released the ripening, it is free of characteristics. Moreover, with reference to the six goddesses who generate consciousness and the meditative absorption of meditative concentration, the single śloka "Of the glorious union of Vajrasattva" explains the characteristic of joy. "If one seals with this mudrā" gives the characteristic of supreme joy. The single śloka "Through union with all the buddhas" teaches the characteristic of the goddess of innate joy.

Having thus taught the bliss of calm abiding, the bliss of insight is taught. The single śloka "Of the glorious union of Vajrasattva" is the meditation on the great bliss of Vajrasattva, luminosity. The single śloka "A body one never tires of beholding" teaches the great bliss of Vairocana. The single śloka "Fulfilling every intention" teaches the great bliss of Heruka. The single śloka "Arising from all the senses" teaches the bliss of Vajrasūrya. The six lines beginning "Glorious, with qualities and grace" teach the great bliss of Padmanarteśvara. The six lines beginning "The accomplishment of soaring upward" teach the great bliss of Paramāśva, luminosity.

If one asks how the goddesses perform the mudrā, the passage beginning "In every way manifold, fortunate one" is spoken. When the channels of the four elements have become clear and the opening of the avadhūtī is joined, there is what is called "the eye of the glorious vajra and lotus soaring upward." The six mudrās of wisdom should be understood as the response to the six mudrās of method, by which the life-force of the four families enters the avadhūtī.

Having thus explained the meaning of the tenth chapter, its result is spoken in the passage beginning "Of the assembly of all the buddhas." Even if all the buddhas of the ten directions were assembled, possessed of immeasurable bodies reaching to the limits of the realm of space, and spoke in words of truth for a time, still, throughout the infinite and boundless buddha fields, all the tathāgatas could not anywhere express all the ritual procedures of the four classes of tantra, the power of the conceptions of form of the king of kalpas, and the qualities of the conceptions of sound. Why is that? Because of the power of the path of blessing, because the nature of the path of direct perception is accomplished, because the mind is ever accomplished as luminosity, the essential nature of the dharmadhātu, and because calm abiding and insight are each accomplished.

Though it is so, if one asks why it cannot be spoken, the passage beginning "This is the supreme of the supreme secret" is spoken. Since the object's own characteristic is not something words can express, the meaning experienced through direct perception is this: joy is "the secret," supreme joy is "the supreme," innate joy is "of the supreme," and since it is great bliss, it is "the Saṃvara," supreme bliss.

If one asks how the buddhas teach the Dharma to bodhisattvas through the saṃbhogakāya and the nirmāṇakāya, it says:
"Purifying the paths for the sake of sentient beings,
And complete purity, the Victor does not treat as existent."
Because, unlike the śrāvakas, they do not pass into the nirvāṇa without remainder of the aggregates, to the buddhas it appears in the aspect of gnosis, to bodhisattvas in the aspect of the form body, and to ordinary beginners as speech. Ultimately, there is no speech regarding the meaning of union.

If one asks why there is no obstacle to the meaning of union, it says: "Here there is not even a māra who makes obstruction." If one wonders why there is neither purified nor unpurified stain, it says: "It is not accomplished by conceptual thought, nor does it come to exist by polishing." If one asks why it is not mixed with the adventitious, it says: "I do not become unstable, nor a māra." If one asks why the root dharmakāya, the self-aware luminosity of mind, is not taught, it says: "Because it is stable, I do not teach it either." If one asks how, although ultimately there is no knowing, the path is realized conventionally, it says:
"One who has accomplished bliss through his own bliss
Becomes the support of the supreme accomplishment."

Having thus spoken the tantra of the result, the rejoicing and praise are given in the passage beginning "Manifold." "The manifold ones spoke thus" refers to the six teachers of the six families. "Bhagavān, the Tathāgata ever delighted" means that the listening buddhas, as many as the sands of the river Ganges, are ever delighted. "Spoken by Vajrasattva on the Glorious Mountain (Śrīparvata)": having explained the conceptions of form of the six families, the six conceptions of sound are the meaning of "the Blessed One Vajrasattva spoke." "Conceiving and becoming utterly delighted" is praise of the conceptions of form in summary, and praise of the conceptions of sound and the rest already explained. By this, future mantrins who practice the conduct of secret mantra praise the maṇḍala in summary, and after what has been explained at length, they rejoice and praise.

After the rejoicing and praise, it is taught that those of sharp faculties should realize the meaning of the ten chapters all at once. "Worshipping with manifold passionate joy" is the samādhi of calm abiding of the three joys. The meaning is: if one worships with the bliss of the yoga of Paramāśva, clearly and ever more clearly, one should meditate in direct perception on the accomplishment of great bliss. "Supremely blissful, holy yoga, fortunate one, supreme of joys" means: "Yogin who abides as the exalted deity yourself, meditate on great bliss all at once!" "The samaya, the nature of most supreme bliss, will then be accomplished" explains that one will attain great bliss, the three kāyas. By this teaching of the meaning of simultaneous meditation, future yogins, after they have made the concluding summary, are told: "Dedicate the roots of virtue." "The Blessed One Vajrasattva spoke thus" are the words of Vajrapāṇi.

All the buddhas of the ten directions, the domain of gnosis, and the assemblage of causes and conditions of the four mudrās of the ḍākinīs of union:
"Through the path of direct perception, by illusion,
The undefiled, the supreme of bliss."
This is the name of the ten chapters. The statement that by this tenth chapter, called "Turning the Wheel of All the Chapters," the meditation of the great fourth empowerment is complete means that it reaches its culmination. This is the commentary on the tenth chapter.

"The Union has not been explained
Completely and clearly by the commentaries":
So I heard it, as though asked in conversation,
Concerning the tantra of the Union.
Although it is hard for me to realize,
Since I am blessed by the feet of the guru,
There is no fault in composing its meaning.
Therefore, this light ray of words
That illuminates the meaning of the chapters of the king of tantras
Is for the sake of generating noble wisdom.
Whatever slight virtue has been gathered by it,
By that may omniscience be attained!

The commentary on the Union with All the Buddhas, the Ḍākinī Illusion Saṃvara, called Ornament of the Union, composed by Pramuditavajra, is complete.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The Sanskrit title is given as spelled in the Tibetan. "Sections" renders rtog pa (kalpa), the divisions of the Samāyoga tantras. The line "Here in these three worlds, through women, they come to be held in reverence" is syntactically ambiguous (it could also mean that women show reverence); the commentary's gloss suggests the three seals are what come to be revered, and the translation follows that reading. The phrase "for the lesser, form; for the secret, mantra; for the ultimate, Dharma" is terse; it appears to correlate three levels of practice with the three bodhisattvas named.  The list of goddesses "sanctioned by purification, by empowerment, by the hero and by authorization" is terse and its exact sense uncertain. The images "like a crescent moon, like a frolicking lion, or like a window" in the final sentence appear to describe forms or postures in Indrabhūti's instruction on the great seal; their precise reference is unclear. The verb "awakened as a buddha at dawn" follows the standard account of the Buddha subduing Māra in the evening and awakening at daybreak.  "The deities of the maṇḍalas of the six chapters" renders dbugs dbyung ba drug, apparently the six divisions (āśvāsa) of the root tantra corresponding to the "six sections" (rtog pa drug). In "to this yogin himself and to all buddhas," the Tibetan rang sangs rgyas could also be read as "pratyekabuddhas," but in context it seems to mean "oneself"; the translation follows the latter reading. The Tibetan mtshan nyid kyi theg pas bas contains a doubled syllable, read as mtshan nyid kyi theg pas. The four acts at the end (laughing, gazing, holding hands, and by implication union) echo the classical correlation of desire with the classes of tantra.  The "four yogas" here appear to be yoga, the yoga of all, the subsequent yoga (anuyoga) and the utmost yoga (atiyoga); the text's labels are terse. The phrase rendered "with rejoicing" (rjes su yi rang) at the end of the Paramāśva passage is unclear in context. Vajrasūrya (rdo rje nyi ma, "Vajra Sun") is a reconstruction for the lord of the family associated here with Māmakī.  "The Steadfast Auspicious One," "the Ravisher," "the Accomplished One," "the Wicked One" and "Delight" render the root tantra's words, which the commentary glosses as Viṣṇu's consort, Maheśvara, Umā, Kāmadeva and his consort; the Sanskrit identifications of the consorts (Lakṣmī, Rati) are inferred from these glosses. "The Appearance of Suchness," "the Many Methods," "the Profound" and "Enlightened Activity" appear to be the names of the tantra's sections. Dbang chen is taken as Mahendra (Indra).  "The three methods of tantra and yantra": the Tibetan names only tantra and yantra while speaking of three methods; the third (perhaps mantra) is not stated, so the wording is kept as it stands.
In this text the six families (rigs drug) are those of Vajrasattva, Vairocana, Heruka, Vajrasūrya, Padmanarteśvara and Paramāśva, so "six families" is used rather than "six classes of beings".
"The moon, the single moon, the vajra, the entering of the elements, and the transformation into the body" are the stages of the five awakenings (abhisambodhi) as listed after the Saṃpuṭa Tilaka (kha sbyor thig le); "the single moon" is a literal rendering of zla ba gcig pa.
'dri ba ("ask") before dkyil 'khor is read as 'bri ba ("draw"), as in the parallel clause that follows. The root line has rtsa ("root") where the commentary has rtswa ("grass, plants"); the commentary's reading is followed.
The last quotation interleaves root-text lines with a gloss ("the six families, emanations of all the buddhas, are to be emanated"); it is kept together as it stands in the Tibetan.  "Chapter" renders dbugs dbyung (Skt. ucchvāsa, a section of a text), here naming the Vajrasattva and Vairocana sections of the root tantra.
"The womb-born maṇḍala of Vairocana" (mngal skyes) probably refers to the Garbha (womb) maṇḍala associated with Mahāvairocana; the literal wording is kept.
"Dpal mchog de nyid bsdus pa" is read as two titles, the Paramādya and the Tattvasaṃgraha; it could also be taken as a single compound reference.
"Those with aspects and those without aspects" refers to the sākāra and nirākāra Yogācāra positions.  Several names in the Heruka origin story could not be identified with confidence and are translated literally: "the bhūta called Eager to Belong" ('byung po gtogs 'dod can), "the bhūta called Virtuous" ('byung po dge ba can), and "the black Maker of Goodness" (legs ldan byed nag po); "the fierce Bhairava" renders drag po 'jigs byed, which here appears to name that same malevolent figure. The Wailing hell (ngu 'bod) is Raurava.
"Mineral poisons" renders dbyig dug, which is uncertain. "The whole night through" reads mtshan thog thag. śajava is given as spelled. "The skin of the terrifying one" ('jigs byed kyi pags pa) may mean the skin of Bhairava or of an elephant.  The four goddesses carried off by Paramāśva could not be identified with confidence and are translated literally: "the Seductress" (lha mo byi ma), "the Sixth" (drug ma, possibly Ṣaṣṭhī), "Utterly Peaceful" (rab tu zhi ba), and "the Sovereign of Humans" (mi'i dbang phyug ma). In the first of the four, the one pressed down is not named in the Tibetan.
"The two Paramāśvas of the elements" (khams kyi rta mchog gnyis) and the clause about taming "the bodies and the wrathful ones" through lust are obscure and are rendered literally.
"Doing", "turning back" and "especially by carrying off" gloss short root-text words of the Vajrasūrya chapter.  "Retas" (re ta) is taken as the Sanskrit retas, generative fluid; "the springtime" of the sixteen-year-old consort likely refers to her menses. "Karna" (kar na) is an unidentified technical term for erotic offerings, perhaps karaṇa; rendered as spelled. In the list of five nectars the text reads byang chub sems dpa' ("bodhisattva"), evidently for byang chub sems ("bodhicitta", here semen); translated as bodhicitta. The image of the disciple "as if blue and fallen asleep" is literal and its sense is uncertain. Mount Badira is given as spelled and is not securely identified. The Catuṣpīṭha is the tantra rendered in Tibetan as "Four Vajra Seats." Śaṅkhinī renders dung can ma, the conch-shaped channel.  The four phases following "the manifold" are the four moments: the manifold, maturation, consummation (vimarda) and freedom from characteristics. The text reads "thirty-nine" factors on the side of awakening, not the usual thirty-seven; kept as written. Gandharbha and sarja appear to be the musical modes gāndhāra and ṣaḍja, glossed in the Tibetan as "Possessing Gandharvas" and "Endowed with Six." Arali is left as spelled; it denotes the rapture of union in Saṃvara songs.  Several goddesses' names could not be identified with confidence and are translated from the Tibetan: Wondrous Bliss, Illuminatrix and the Disciple Lady (with Saṃvarā) around Vajrasattva; Great Earth and the Surveyor around Padmanarteśvara; and the Empowering Lady, the Treasure Holder, the Banner Lady and Supreme Action around the Sun. "Six ritual systems" renders rtog pa drug, taken as six kalpas; it could also mean "six conceptions." The list of implements gives five items as written.  "The three purities" renders gtsang sbra gsum, the three observances of the feast gathering, the drink of peace and the purity of the messenger. "Six ritual systems" reads rtog pa drug for the text's rtag pa drug ("six permanences"), which makes no sense here, following the previous passage. The mudrās "born of mantra, born of the field and born of wisdom" are the classes of consort. "The emanation-body mother" renders sprul sku ma as written; the sense is uncertain.
