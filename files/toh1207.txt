The Reality of Song (Gītitattva)

The Reality of Song, known in Sanskrit as the Gītitattva, is a short tantric commentary that opens with homage to glorious Hevajra and explains, word by word, the hidden meaning of a vernacular song associated with the Hevajra tradition. Taking each phrase of the song in turn, it reads its seemingly ordinary words as references to yogic experience, such as the unfabricated awakening mind, the inner fire of caṇḍālī abiding at the navel, the drop that emanates and withdraws, the four abodes of Brahmā, and compassion whose nature is supreme joy. In this way it shows how the imagery of drums, battle, feasting, and goddesses encodes the practices and realizations of the completion stage. The work is Toh 1207 in the Tengyur, where it belongs to the collection of commentarial treatises on the tantras, and in particular to the literature surrounding the Hevajra cycle and its esoteric songs.

In the language of India: gīti tattva nāma. In the language of Tibet: The Reality of Song.

Homage to glorious Hevajra.

"kolla i re" means the head. "ṭhi a" means the unfabricated awakening mind. "bo la mummu ṇi" means caṇḍālī, abiding at the navel, having the nature of the drop, emanating and withdrawing; kakko lā. "re" is a vocative. "ghaṇi," by their coming together, means continuously. "kṛ pi ṭa" means the hand drum, and the drum, by being beaten: bājja i. "ho" is again a vocative. "kā ru ṇe sa" means the four abodes of Brahmā. For the host of goddesses of form: ki a i. "na ro lā": battle, that is, na ro lā. "ba la" means joy: ta hiṃ khājja. "gā ḍheṃ" means absorbed within. "ma ā ṇā" means compassion having the nature of supreme joy: bājji i a i. "ha le" is again a vocative. "kā liñja ra" means water, again and again: pā ṇi i i. "du ndu ra" means the moment that is without moments: bājji a. ca u sa ma kacchu rī. "sihla kappū ra" means the tathāgatas who are the nature of the aggregates: lā i a i. "mā la i" means fire, the eater of oblations. "irña na" means the aggregates. "sā liñja" means the supreme. "ta hiṃ bha ru" means much: khā i a i. "phreṃ kha ṇa" means the lovely. "khe ṭa" means play; "ka rante," through compassion: śuddhā śuddha na mu ṇi a. "ni raṃ śu aṃ ge" means moonbeams: ca ḍā vi. ta hiṃ ja sa rā pi pa ni a ī. "ma la a ja" means again becoming one. "ku ndu ru" means unceasingly: pā ṭa i. "ḍiṇḍa ma ta hinna" means: O, the beating of the drum of the whole Dharma: bājji a.

Further, "kolla i re" is at the crown of the head. The rasanā, abiding in the form of one's own right of the right, of the kā li, of the sun, of the undestroyed, of the downward mouth, of the stream of the speech vajra, and of method and the blazing lotus, is called "bo la." "mummu ṇi" is the navel. The lalanā, abiding as the nature of what is other than that, namely the left, the ā li, the moon, that which emanates and gathers, the drop, the lady of the body vajra, the upper mouth, and the maker of the vajra fluid, is called "kakko la." The avadhūtī, which through the mode of the mind vajra makes their designations become one, is the hand.

And of that, what is the thumb and what is the ring finger, these, and the rasanā and lalanā, waxing and waning, method and the yoginī Nairātmyā, and the waves of the conventional and the ultimate: "pressed" by these means pressing, kissing, embracing and so on, the yogin's union that makes them of one taste; in enjoyment, the two waves are what is to be eaten. This is explained as complete enjoyment. "Then gnosis will arise" refers to the supreme state. The meaning is: "like the joy of a youth," or "free of the two times."

Alternatively, the kolla i re of bo la, abiding at the jewel, is bo la, that is, the rasanā and the secret vajra. The mummu ṇi of kakko lā, abiding at the navel, is kakko lā, that is, the lalanā and the secret lotus. These themselves are bo la ko lā i re and kakko la mummu ṇi; and their becoming of one taste is their becoming the avadhūtī: lā ko lā i reṃ kakko la mummu ṇi, like butter in butter. Moreover, whatever in the avadhūtī is complete enjoyment, and she who possesses the emanation body, are also destroyed; and having gone beyond, by that end of the joy of cessation, by that beginning of the joy of cessation, that selflessness is ascertained as the Great Seal (mahāmudrā). This is called the bo lā ko i re kakko lā mummu ṇi of the Great Seal. The meaning is: having the nature of method and wisdom as they intrinsically are.

Contemplating its nature again and again, always and everywhere, together with that, one by whom this is swiftly ascertained becomes the chief of the host of Victors, and through that nature, which passes beyond the domain of the path of what is to be self-known, the self-knower, and self-knowing, comes to know the suchness of all gnosis.

Alternatively, kakko lā mummu ṇi abiding at the navel, and bo lā ko lā i re at the tip of the jewel: the awakening mind that has utterly abandoned attachment and non-attachment, great bliss, free of moon and sun, selflessness, the supreme vajra abode, incomparable, without the joy of pleasure, is kakko la mummu ṇi bo lā i reṃ: the becoming of one taste of attachment and freedom from attachment. Likewise it is said:

Because method is change itself,
Wisdom, the fearsome, brings existence to its end.
Therefore there is no great fear,
And toward that itself there is no attachment.
Some are destroyed by destruction;
There being no destructible thing, it is called inexhaustible.

Because all these are absent, that becomes absent; and because that is absent, everything becomes absent.

Likewise, just as that lord is, just as the mind vajra, having become external, appears separately as form and so on, the nature of the objects of the senses, so it is mind itself appearing as external. As it is said:

Great gnosis dwells in the body,
Wholly free of all conceptions;
It pervades all things;
Dwelling in the body, it is not born from the body.

And as it is said:

Having abandoned the mind, the body
Is not seen to abide elsewhere;
Having abandoned the body, the mind
Does not become mere play.

That itself is the supreme abiding. And as it is said:

Nowhere in any world realm
Is a buddha found elsewhere;
Mind itself is the perfect Buddha;
No buddha is taught to be elsewhere.

By these and other such passages, this is for now ascertained. Yet that is merely a distinction: everything has been distinguished in this way so that the heirs of the Buddha may enter; ultimately there is no distinguisher, no distinguishing, and nothing to be distinguished. In just the same way it is also taught of the meditator, the meditating, and what is to be meditated upon. In that very great awakening mind the liberations and the abodes arise, and that itself is the complete nirvāṇas.

Therefore the primordial Buddha is nothing other than the certainty of supreme bliss. By one's very own self-awareness there is self-awareness; by what is to be known through self-awareness there is what is to be known through self-awareness; by its nature there is its nature; and by the abode of that very thing there is its abode, the Great Seal (mahāmudrā). And I, the great being, know the unobstructed vajra consciousness. Self and other, persons, pots, clothing, food, drink, bathing, and all the other forms of existence are made equal to that. Just as they are, so one performs the activities of going, coming, and the rest, and again and again the one possessed of consciousness is roused. This is the meaning of "rousing" and "meditating."

"Wherever" means a fixed place or a place that is not fixed. "However" means with an action seal (karmamudrā), or alone, or likewise by one who is not a mendicant. If one asks what this teaches, it means naked, or, if not naked, in whatever way one wishes.

A yogin for whom reality is "this way" or "that way" will not attain accomplishment even in a billion eons. For it is said:

Because, without meditating by the mind,
One should meditate on all beings;
When all phenomena are fully known,
Meditation itself is not meditation.

Therefore:

The low, the middling, and the supreme,
And whatever others are there as well,
By meditating on that very equal taste,
One sees all this as sameness.

By this it is said:

From me all beings arise;
From me the three abodes arise as well;
By me all this is pervaded;
No other nature of beings is seen.
The yogin who, knowing it thus,
Grows accustomed to perfect equipoise,
Even if he is a person of little merit,
Will accomplish it, there is no doubt.

Therefore, having diligently come to know reality, with kakkolā mummuṇi abiding in the navel, and bolā kolla eraṃ, one holds the jewel as far as one is able, grasping bliss by way of supreme joy. Meditating on and abiding in the reality of the song, one should not let it shift. For it is said: if the awakening mind has fallen, from what would accomplishment come, from what miraculous powers, and from what meditation?

The Reality of Song, composed by the master, the learned and glorious Saroruha, is complete. It was translated by the Indian preceptor Ratnavajra and the monk Śākya Yeshé.

This is a word-by-word commentary on the Apabhraṃśa song of the Hevajra Tantra. The song's words, written in Tibetan letters, are romanized as spelled rather than translated. Several glosses are very terse.

In "na ro lā: battle," the gloss seems to explain rolā as "battle," with the negative giving "no battle."

The word glossed as "the lovely" could also mean "suffering," since the Tibetan has both senses.

Rasanā, lalanā and avadhūtī render the Tibetan terms for the right, left and central channels.

"Joy of cessation" renders the term for viramānanda, the third of the four joys.

The sentence about the avadhūtī and the emanation body is syntactically difficult, and its rendering is tentative.  The phrase rendered "by one who is not a mendicant" is obscure in the Tibetan and its sense is uncertain. The sentence about the yogin for whom reality is "this way" or "that way" is also compressed; it appears to criticize a yogin who clings to fixed formulations. The quoted song words are Apabhraṃśa terms from the Hevajra song: kakkolā refers to the lotus and bolā to the vajra. "Should not let it shift" refers to not releasing the bodhicitta, as the following sentence makes explicit.
