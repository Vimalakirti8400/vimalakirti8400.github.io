The Noble Dhāraṇī-Mantra That Dispels All Obstacles (Ārya-sarvāntarasaṃgrāsa-dhāraṇī-mantra)

The Noble Dhāraṇī-Mantra That Dispels All Obstacles, known in Sanskrit as the Ārya Sarvāntarasaṃgrāsa Dhāraṇī Mantra and in Tibetan as Pakpa Bardu Chöpa Tamché Selwé Zung Ngak, is a short dhāraṇī text in which the Blessed One addresses the bodhisattva Vajrapāṇi about the protective power of a sacred incantation. The Buddha explains that anyone who upholds, carries, and recites this dhāraṇī-mantra will not fall prey to the thirteen kinds of untimely death, among them poison, weapons, starvation, wild beasts, fire, drowning, epidemics, incurable disease, falls, sorcery, choking, and suicide, and goes on to describe further protections against hostile and dangerous beings. Its concern is thus the removal of obstacles and the safeguarding of life through devoted recitation. The work is Toh 1010 in the Kangyur, where it belongs to the collection of dhāraṇī texts gathered among the tantric scriptures.

In the language of India: ārya sarva antara saṃgrāsa dhāraṇi mantra

In the language of Tibet: The Noble Dhāraṇī-Mantra That Dispels All Obstacles.

Homage to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas.

The Blessed One spoke to the bodhisattva, the great being Vajrapāṇi:

"Vajrapāṇi, whoever upholds, carries and recites this dhāraṇī-mantra will not be overcome by the thirteen kinds of untimely death. These are death by poison and by weapons, starvation, being struck by weapons, being devoured by savage beasts, being burned by fire, dying in water, other dreadful contagious epidemics, leprosy, dreadful incurable diseases, falling from a precipice, the sorcery of others, choking on food, and killing oneself.

"Vajrapāṇi, whoever upholds, carries and recites this dhāraṇī-mantra, even the savage and arrogant obstructors that dwell on the earth and in the sky will find no opportunity to harm them.

"Vajrapāṇi, for whoever upholds, carries and recites this dhāraṇī-mantra, all the obscurations of karma, the obscurations of affliction and the obscurations to the knowable will be purified and cleansed, and they will be born in the world of Sukhāvatī.

oṃ vajra caṇḍa mahāroṣaṇa hūṃ phaṭ apratihatabala hūṃ phaṭ sarva vighanāṃ antarāya vināśakara māraya hūṃ phaṭ

"If one recites this a hundred and eight times, it will be accomplished. It will certainly be accomplished."

Thus he spoke.

This completes the Noble Dhāraṇī-Mantra That Dispels All Obstacles.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The text speaks of "thirteen" untimely deaths, but its list can be counted as fourteen items. The translation reads "death by poison and by weapons" as a single item so that the count matches, but the Tibetan itself does not mark how the items are grouped. "Obscurations to the knowable" renders shes bya'i sgrib pa (jñeyāvaraṇa), the cognitive obscurations. "Untimely death" renders gshis ngan du 'chi ba, literally "dying in an ill-fated way."
