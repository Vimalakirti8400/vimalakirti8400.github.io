Instructions on the Means of Accomplishing Glorious Hevajra (Śrīhevajranāmasādhanopika)

Instructions on the Means of Accomplishing Glorious Hevajra, whose Sanskrit title is given as Śrī Hevajranāmasādhanopika and whose Tibetan title is Pal Kyé Dorjé Drubpé Tab kyi Men-ngak, is a short tantric meditation manual, or sādhana, for practicing the deity Hevajra together with his consort Nairātmyā. Its colophon names it the Sādhana of the Innate Hevajra and attributes it to the great master Śrī Ānandavajra, who sets out the practice in three stages for practitioners of lesser, middling, and highest capacity. After praising the rare chance of a human life and a qualified guru, it describes the empowerments, the generation stage visualization and mantra recitation, the completion stage yoga of the channel wheels and drops, and the yoga of the innate, in which the four joys are linked with the four empowerments, citing tantras such as the Vajrapañjara and the Catuṣpīṭha along the way. It closes with a torma offering to the spirits, aspiration prayers, and advice on how the yogin should conduct himself. The work is Toh 1302 in the Tengyur, where it belongs to the tantra commentaries, among the texts connected with the Hevajra cycle.

In the language of India: śrī hevajranāmasādhanopika. In the language of Tibet: Instructions on the Means of Accomplishing Glorious Hevajra.

Homage to Mañjuśrī in youthful form.

Having paid homage to Hevajra and Nairātmyā,
Embodiments of wisdom and method,
Together with the guru,
I set down a sādhana in three stages.

Human existence is hard to find, and harder still
To find a guru and gladly serve him.
Rely on the way of mantra, from which one's own and others' aims arise,
The path that benefits beings.

To one endowed with good qualities and with diligence,
Devoted to the guru and possessed of faith,
Without doubt about the Dharma of the Victors,
Since by the definitions of empowerment and its divisions he is a vessel,
The kindness should be granted:
The vase empowerment with water and the rest,
Then in order the secret empowerment,
And the empowerment of wisdom and gnosis;
Thus far reach the guru's instructions.

In a pleasing place,
On mountains, in forests and in caves,
On riverbanks, at crossroads and at lakes,
At solitary trees, in secluded spots and charnel grounds,
Build a meditation hut,
And smear it with nectar and the like.
Arrange paintings and other offerings,
Rise early in the morning, with mind collected,
And when all the outer ablutions and the rest are done,
Sit down upon a comfortable seat,
And with the purifying formulas, "pure" and the rest,
Accomplish the deity of your choice.

Visualizing the guru as the vajra holder,
Send forth clouds of offerings
Arising from the jewel of the mind,
And then present them in order.
Then make offering with the eight goddesses:
Gaurī offers with the awakening mind,
Caurī with self-arisen flowers,
Vetālī holds Akṣobhya,
Ghasmarī offers with Vairocana,
Pukkasī offers with a hook,
Śavarī holds the taste,
Caṇḍālī holds a small drum,
And Ḍombī performs nondual union.

To the Buddha, the Dharma and the supreme assembly
I go for refuge until awakening.
To Hevajra, embodiment of wisdom,
And to the master I go for refuge.
Whatever wrongs I have done,
All of them without exception, for my own sake or for others,
I confess each one, and I take
The vow not to commit them again.
In the merit of all the Sugatas,
Of the bodhisattvas,
And of all beings,
I rejoice with all my heart.
To the buddhas dwelling in the ten directions
I make this appeal:
For sentient beings lost in the darkness of suffering,
I urge you to turn the wheel of the Dharma.
Until sentient beings attain buddhahood,
May the Victors remain
And never pass beyond sorrow:
With joined palms I make this supplication.
The awakening mind, which is emptiness and compassion inseparable,
Which teaches the path of awakening,
And is the great source of all merit,
I likewise generate.
Whatever merit of body, speech and mind
I have performed, have caused to be performed,
Or have rejoiced in, whatever it may be,
By it may I become omniscient for the sake of the world.

With a mind moistened by the moisture of compassion,
Meditate on the four abodes of Brahmā:
First, meditate on loving-kindness;
Second, meditate on compassion;
Third, meditate on joy;
And last of all, on equanimity.
All things are to be gathered into oneself,
And oneself, too, is the wisdom being.
The wisdom being, up to the nāda,
Is itself like a mirage, like an illusion.

Then, out of the nature of non-arising, one should resolve upon arising itself.

From kaṃ arises a sun maṇḍala, and upon it, from hūṃ, a crossed vajra bearing the seed syllable. Light rays streaming from it subdue the māras of the ten directions, and one should imagine that they become the lattice, the fence and the ground. In its center one imagines a white, triangular source of phenomena. Upon it, from paṃ arises a multicolored lotus, and from khaṃ a crossed vajra. Upon that, from laṃ arises an earth maṇḍala, square and green; on it, from vaṃ, a water maṇḍala, round and white; from that, from raṃ, a fire maṇḍala, triangular and red; and on it, from yaṃ, a wind maṇḍala shaped like a bow, black. Having imagined these marked with a drop, they merge into one, and so there is a celestial palace, square with four doors, with four porticoes and eight pillars, adorned with nets and half-nets, with jewels, with the objects of sensual delight and the rest. In its center, one should imagine an eight-petaled lotus, and on it a sun maṇḍala together with a corpse, all produced from the mind.

In its center, from āli arises a moon maṇḍala: this is the mirror-like wisdom. On it, from kāli: this is the wisdom of equality. In its center, from hūṃ and aṃ arise a vajra and a curved knife marked with the seed syllables: this is the discriminating wisdom. All of these becoming one is the all-accomplishing wisdom. The complete body is the wisdom of the dharmadhātu.

One should imagine him blue in color, with one face and two arms, holding a vajra and a skull cup and embracing the wisdom consort, endowed with the five mudrās, three-eyed, his head adorned with five skulls and marked with a crossed vajra, his tawny hair standing upright, wearing a necklace of fifty-four fresh skulls, naked, and standing with his right leg extended. One should imagine Nairātmyā holding a curved knife and a skull cup and embracing his body, with a necklace of men's skulls, standing with her left leg extended; in other respects she is like the method.

Within, too, one should imagine in the same way: on the eyes, Delusion Vajra; on the ears, Hatred Vajra; on the nose, Avarice Vajra; on the tongue, Desire Vajra; on the body, Jealousy Vajra; and on the mind, Nairātmyā.

Then, from the seed syllable in one's own heart, black light rays shaped like hooks are sent forth, inviting the wisdom being from its natural abode. Having worshiped it with water for the guests and other offerings, one causes it to enter the circle of commitment, and imagines the two as nondual. Again, with those light rays one invites all the Tathāgatas in the form of Heruka and makes offerings and so forth to them. Then one should imagine that they empower oneself with the awakening mind, and that one's head is adorned with Akṣobhya. One should practice this yoga for as long as one does not grow weary.

When weary, one should do the recitation. One imagines in the heart of the consort a sun maṇḍala, and in its center a curved knife together with its seed syllable. From it a garland of mantra, like jasmine and full of light, emerges and enters one's own mouth, comes out through the secret vajra, descends into the goddess's lotus, and from the goddess's mouth enters one's own mouth again; so one should imagine.

In the same way one should imagine it again and again. oṃ deva picu vajra hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ svāhā Or else, following the guru's instruction, one should do the recitation in the secret vajra and the lotus. In the Sixteenth Chapter it is said:

Unpierced, there is separation from union;
Pierced, it bestows joy.
Thus it is the very jewel, endowed with five qualities,
That is desired in saṃsāra.

Next I shall explain the yoga of completion. One imagines at the navel the wheel of emanation, white and with sixteen petals, and in its center, within a jeweled casket, abides the very nature of self-awareness. At the heart is the wheel of Dharma, with sixteen petals, endowed with a drop. At the throat is the wheel of enjoyment, white and with sixteen petals, endowed with the awakening mind. At the head is the wheel of great bliss, white and with sixteen petals, and in its center one imagines the extraordinary awakening mind, endowed with light. Then light rays radiate from the wheel of emanation and pass upward through the wheel of Dharma and the wheel of enjoyment. They circle the great bliss three times, come out through the hair-curl between the brows, purify all sentient beings, and enter the hearts of the Sugatas. Having drawn forth the nectar of wisdom, they enter the aperture of Brahmā, and, free of apprehended and apprehender, they descend into the casket; so one imagines, and one should rest one-pointedly on that. If it is not clear, one should do the same again.

Or else, one dissolves Nairātmyā into one's own heart, and one dissolves oneself into hūṃ. That too one should imagine as a drop endowed with light:

The sublime secret mantra in the form of a drop:
Meditate fully on one's own mind as endowed with light.
All things have as their nature the essence of one's own mind.

When arising, too, one should regard oneself and others as the wheel, or as Caṇḍālī, and one should not perform offerings and the like. As it is said in the Tent (Vajrapañjara):

Give up entirely the binding of mudrās;
Do not perform the work of stūpas;
Do not bow to the supreme Three Vajras.
Bodily acts such as the maṇḍala
Should not be done even in a dream.

And it is said in the Catuṣpīṭha:

Do not build stūpas;
Likewise, do not make offerings and the like.
Treatises are easy to obtain;
Their purpose is hard to find.

Next I shall explain the yoga of the innate:

When, by the wind of wisdom,
Self-awareness is stirred,
Through the burning of the buddhas
Joy and the rest arise in sequence.
If one asks what it is like when it arises:
Like the joy of a young maiden,
Like the joy of a mute;
Nondual wisdom will arise.
Without beginning, end, or middle,
It is not saṃsāra, it is not nirvāṇa,
Neither self nor other exists:
This is the supreme bliss.
It becomes the very wisdom of selflessness.
The awareness of self and other is wholly abandoned;
Equal to space, flawless, innate,
Its nature abides in the bodies of all.
The first joy is the form of saṃsāra;
Likewise supreme joy is saṃsāra itself;
The joy of cessation is the very nature of saṃsāra;
In the three there is no innate.
Joy and supreme joy,
The joy of cessation and the rest:
If the third were abandoned,
From what would the fourth arise?
Therefore, making the three the means,
One should cultivate the fourth.
Make the root cause threefold;
Its fruit is the fourth.
Or else, the fruit is abandoned by the three;
Or else, by itself it is the third.
Know them by the distinctions of joy:
This is the sequence of the wheels.

So it is said in the Later Tantra.

The first is the vase empowerment;
Supreme joy is the secret empowerment;
The third is the wisdom-gnosis empowerment;
The fourth is the Great Seal (mahāmudrā).
Even at times out of meditative equipoise,
Whatever activity one engages in,
Apart from the innate
One does not abide with any other mind.

This too is said in the Sixteenth Chapter:

Bliss is black, and bliss is yellow,
Bliss is red, bliss is white,
Bliss is green, bliss is blue,
Bliss is all that moves and does not move.
Bliss is method and wisdom,
And likewise meditative equipoise.
Bliss is existent things, bliss is the nonexistent;
Vajrasattva is declared to be bliss.

Next, while abiding in the generation stage, in order to pacify all obstacles completely, there is the torma for all the bhūtas. Having completed the protection wheel, one imagines in its center a source of dharmas. Upon it one imagines, arising from paṃ, an eight-petalled lotus together with a sun maṇḍala. Upon it, from oṃ, there is a wheel together with its seed syllable, from which light rays radiate. By those light rays all the gods of the formless realm, of the form realm, of the desire realm, and so forth are made into Body Vajra; they are gathered into it and become one, and from that one should imagine Body Vajra, with one face and two arms, white in color, embracing the wisdom consort with vajra and bell, adorned with the five mudrās, and seated with the right leg extended. The wisdom consort is likewise similar to the method figure. In between, from hūṃ comes a vajra; from it all the guardians of the directions are gathered into it and become one, and from that one should imagine Mind Vajra, blue in color. Below that, from a comes a lotus, by which all the asuras and the like are made into Speech Vajra; one should imagine it red in color. The rest is like Body Vajra.

Then one invites the wisdom beings, makes offerings and so forth, and imagines that they become one. Then again one invites the tathāgatas and the rest, and having conferred the empowerment of the awakening mind, one imagines that the head of Body Vajra is adorned by Vairocana, that of Mind Vajra by Akṣobhya, and that of Speech Vajra by Amitābha.

Then one should bless the torma. From yaṃ comes a wind maṇḍala; upon it, from raṃ, a fire maṇḍala; on that, from a, a skull cup, inside which are bhrūṃ āṃ jrāṃ khaṃ hūṃ. Then one imagines them as nectars, and one should imagine them marked by those very syllables. Above it, from a, comes a moon maṇḍala, and in its center one imagines, from hūṃ, a vajra marked with its seed syllable; by its power the contents are brought to a boil. Above it, aṃ draws in the nectar of wisdom, and one should imagine that they become one. Having blessed it with the three heart syllables of suchness, one should offer it with this:

When the yogin has grown weary,
Then he should make aspirations:
Born in the family, keeping the pledges,
Clear of mind, devoted to Hevajra,
Reverent to the guru and endowed with compassion,
So may I be born, birth after birth.
Holding the vajra in hand, ringing the bell,
Reciting the profound Dharma,
Taking the queen's fluid as food in equal share,
So may I be born, birth after birth.

With this one recollects oneself:
The eyes of compassion are red;
The black limbs are the mind of loving-kindness;
The two arms are method and wisdom;
The one face is nondual wisdom;
The two legs are recalled as the two truths;
The five mudrās are the five buddhas;
One is wrathful in order to tame the wicked.
Having thus resolved, the mantra practitioner
Should go about as a yogin of the deity.
Constantly and attentively, from mantra recitation
And from meditative concentration, one should not be parted.
One should always gather students,
And one should always teach.
One should always protect sentient beings,
And always make offerings to the buddhas.
One should give water to Jambhala,
And carry out all the activities thoroughly.
The work of stūpas, made from sand,
The wise should perform according to the rite.

Although I am not gifted in composing sādhanas,
Following the instructions of many gurus
I have made this sādhana; whatever in it is at fault,
May you who know reality forgive it.
For the sake of those of lesser, middling, and highest capacity
I have made it with three stages;
By the stainless virtue gained from this,
May that precious reality become fully clear.

The Sādhana of the Innate Hevajra, composed by the great master Śrī Ānandavajra, is complete.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

"When weary" reads skyo na for the written skyon ("fault"), following the preceding sentence. "Śavarī holds the taste" renders ro 'dzin, where ro may mean either taste or corpse. The text gives the necklace as fifty-four fresh skulls, and the deity's seat as a sun "together with a corpse" (srog song ba, "one whose life has departed"). The five vajras on the senses are rendered by meaning.  "The Sixteenth Chapter" renders brtag pa bcu drug pa; it may instead be a name for a text of sixteen chapters rather than a single chapter, and the source of the quoted verses is not certain. In the opening quotation the image is of a jewel such as a pearl, which is of use only once pierced. The "wheel" arising from oṃ in the torma rite is the emblem of Body Vajra, not a drawn diagram.  "Grown weary" follows the evident sense of a word written dwa ba, which as it stands is not a recognizable word; it is taken as a slip for ngal ba, "weary", which matches the earlier instruction to recite when weary. "Clear of mind" renders smyo med, literally "without madness". "Reciting" takes glog pa as a slip for klog pa, "to read aloud, recite".
