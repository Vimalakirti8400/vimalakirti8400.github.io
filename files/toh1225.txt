The Praise of the Bhaḍarāga of Glorious Hevajra (Śrīhevajrabhaḍarāgastotranāma)

This text is the Praise of Glorious Hevajra in Twenty Verses, whose Sanskrit title is given as Śrī Hevajra Bhaḍarāga Stotra and whose Tibetan title is Dpal kye rdo rje'i bha ḍa rā ga'i bstod pa; it is attributed to the great accomplished master Saroruhavajra and was translated into Tibetan by the Indian preceptor Gayādhara and the Tibetan translator Shākya Yeshé. A devotional hymn in verse, it praises Hevajra in union with his consort Nairātmyā, describing his eight faces, sixteen arms holding skull cups, four legs treading down the four māras, and his ornaments, and then salutes in turn the eight goddesses of his maṇḍala, Gaurī, Caurī, Vetālī, Ghasmarī, Pukkasī, Śabarī, Caṇḍālī and Ḍombī, each with her seed syllable, direction, emblems and the god she treads upon. It closes by honoring the deity as the pure nature of the aggregates and elements and the source of the three worlds, and dedicates the merit of the praise to the world. It is Toh 1225 in the Tengyur, where it is placed among the tantra commentaries as one of the praises belonging to the Hevajra cycle.

In the language of India: śrī hebajra bhaḍarāga stotra nāma. In the language of Tibet: The Praise of the bhaḍarā of Glorious Hevajra.

Homage to glorious Hevajra.

Supreme intrinsic nature of all things,
abiding from the very beginning as the nature of all,
the nonduality of method and wisdom:
to Hevajra I pay homage.

Embraced about the neck by Nairātmyā,
his yellow hair blazing upward,
with three eyes in each of his eight faces:
to that protector I pay homage.

The principal face and the one on top are a lovely black,
white and red are those on the right and the left,
and his remaining faces are black:
to that black one I pay homage.

Elephant, horse, donkey, ox, camel,
human, lion, and the cat:
you who hold these eight skull cups
resting in your eight right hands,

and earth, water, fire and wind,
moon, sun, Yama and the Guhyaka,
holding them in skull cups
resting in your eight left hands: to you I pay homage.

With your four feet you tread down the four māras
all alike, and you are naked;
upon the sun, your right leg outstretched, and blue,
you dance: to you I pay homage.

Terrifying, embodiment of the four wheels,
adorned with dripping heads,
adorned with the six mudrās
that are the pure tathāgatas,

marked at the crown with a crossed vajra,
with the lord of the family on your forehead,
you have truly attained the supreme joy:
to that great bliss I pay homage.

Born from gaṃ, Gaurī in the east,
her limbs black, in her left hand a fish,
and in her right she holds a curved knife;
she treads down Brahmā: to her I pay homage.

Born from ci, Caurī,
madder red in color, in the south,
holds in her hands a pig and a hand drum;
she treads down Indra: to her I pay homage.

Born from baṃ, the yellow one in the west,
Vetālī, treads down Upendra (Viṣṇu);
in her left hand a bowl, and with her right
she holds a tortoise: to her I pay homage.

Ghasmarī, at the northern gate,
green, arisen from ghaṃ,
her two hands busy with a snake and a skull cup;
she treads down Rudra: to her I pay homage.

Treading down Yama, arisen from paṃ,
in her left an axe, in her right a lion,
the one of terrifying form in the northeast:
homage to Pukkasī, the blue one.

Śabarī, white, arisen from śaṃ,
in the southeast stands upon the Lord of Wealth (Kubera);
in her left hand a rattle, and in her right
she holds a monk: to her I pay homage.

Caṇḍālī, pale blue as the sky,
in the rākṣasa quarter, arisen from laṃ,
in her left a skull cup and in her right a wheel:
homage to her there with the rākṣasa.

Ḍombī, born from ḍaṃ,
in the northwest stands upon Vemacitrin;
her left hand in the threatening gesture, a vajra in her right;
to the many-colored one I pay homage.

Abiding in the dance with legs drawn up,
adorned with the five mudrās,
naked and three-eyed: to all of them
I pay homage with reverence.

The aggregates and elements utterly pure,
arisen from the mind of awakening:
to all of these who grant supreme bliss
all things pay homage.

Held in your own right hands,
the hand emblems arisen from seed syllables:
to all of those that long for great bliss
I always truly pay homage.

You, the lord together with your consort, born from the two,
the seeds abiding in the union of sun and moon,
who by yourself, out of this maṇḍala of your own,
bring forth the three worlds as your emanation,
who from yourself grant peaceful bliss to all of these without exception,
together with your consort,
at peace in the state that is the very taste of the nectar
of passion for wisdom: to you I pay homage.

By praising Hevajra, lord of the ḍākinīs,
together with his circle of goddesses,
whatever merit I have gained,
by that may the world become like him.

The Praise of the bhaḍarā of the Venerable Glorious Hevajra in Twenty Verses, composed by the great master who attained accomplishment, Saroruhavajra, is complete. It was translated by the Indian preceptor Gayādhara and the Tibetan translator, the monk Shākya Yeshé.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The Guhyaka among the gods held in the left hands most likely stands for Vaiśravaṇa, lord of the guhyakas, who completes this set of world guardians in the Hevajra Tantra. The line on the faces names the principal face (literally "the crown") and the upper face as black; the reading follows the standard Hevajra iconography of a black main face, white right face and red left face. The emblem in Śabarī's left hand is literally "the jingler", most likely a rattle or a monk's jingling staff. The last line of the Caṇḍālī verse reads literally "homage to the rākṣasa itself"; it is taken as referring to her station in the rākṣasa (southwest) quarter, perhaps upon the rākṣasa, as the other goddesses tread down their gods.
