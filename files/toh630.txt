The Noble Dhāraṇī Mantra That Clears Away All Interruptions (Ārya-sarva-antarasaṃgrāsa-dhāraṇī-mantra)

Toh 630 in the Kangyur is a dhāraṇī text known in English as The Noble Dhāraṇī Mantra That Clears Away All Interruptions, in Sanskrit as the Ārya-sarva-antarasaṃgrāsa-dhāraṇī-mantra, and in Tibetan as the Phags pa bar du gcod pa thams cad sel ba'i gzungs sngags. It belongs to the dhāraṇī section of the Kangyur, a class of texts centered on the transmission and protective power of sacred mantra formulas. The text takes the form of a teaching delivered by the Buddha to the bodhisattva Vajrapāṇi, in which the Buddha enumerates thirteen specific causes of untimely death — including poison, weapons, wild beasts, fire, drowning, epidemic disease, and sorcery — and declares that whoever holds, bears, and recites this dhāraṇī will be shielded from all of them. Its primary concern is thus with protection from harm and the removal of obstacles that might cut short a practitioner's life and spiritual path.

In the language of India: Ārya-sarva-antarasaṃgrāsa-dhāraṇī-mantra. In the Tibetan language: The Noble Dhāraṇī Mantra That Clears Away All Interruptions.

Homage to all buddhas and bodhisattvas.

The Blessed One spoke to the bodhisattva, the great being Vajrapāṇi:

Vajrapāṇi, whoever holds, bears, and recites this dhāraṇī mantra — for that person, these thirteen conditions will not become the cause of death: death by poison; death by weapons; death by starvation; being struck by weapons; being devoured by ferocious wild beasts; being burned by fire; death by drowning; contagious epidemic diseases; leprosy; incurable and terrifying diseases; falling from a precipice; harmful sorcery performed by others; being poisoned through food; and being killed by one's own hand. In this way, death through these thirteen bad conditions will not occur.

Vajrapāṇi, whoever holds, bears, and recites this dhāraṇī mantra — even the arrogant and malevolent obstructing spirits who dwell on the earth and in the sky will find no opportunity to harm that person.

Vajrapāṇi, whoever holds, bears, and recites this dhāraṇī mantra — all their karmic obscurations, afflictive obscurations, and cognitive obscurations will be entirely purified and cleansed, and they will be born in the world of Sukhāvatī.

Oṃ vajra caṇḍa mahāroṣaṇa hūṃ phaṭ. Apratihata bala hūṃ phaṭ. Sarva vighnaṃ antarāya vināśakara māraya hūṃ phaṭ.

If this is recited one hundred and eight times, it will be accomplished — it will most certainly be accomplished. So it was declared.

The Noble Dhāraṇī Mantra That Clears Away All Interruptions is complete.

The enumeration of the thirteen bad deaths in the Tibetan is slightly irregular: the list as given contains items that, depending on how one counts "poisoning through food" separately from "death by poison" and "being killed by one's own hand," reaches thirteen only with careful parsing. The translation follows the text as given without harmonizing the count.

The mantra syllables are rendered as they appear in the Tibetan transliteration and are not normalized to reconstructed Sanskrit, as the Tibetan phonetic rendering is itself the liturgically authoritative form here.
