The Tantra-Ritual of the Noble Vajra Chain (Ārya-vajra-śṛṅkhalā-tantra-kalpa)

Toh 758 in the Kangyur is a tantric text known in Sanskrit as the Ārya-vajra-śṛṅkhalā-tantra-kalpa and in Tibetan as the Tantra-Ritual of the Noble Vajra Chain Tantra. It belongs to the class of tantric ritual literature and concerns the practice of the Noble Vajra Chain, a deity associated with liberation from bondage, the dispelling of suffering, and the cultivation of qualities such as faith, compassion, and disciplined wisdom. The text takes the form of instructional verse, guiding practitioners through ritual observance and the recitation of secret mantra, emphasizing the indispensability of the teacher and the proper orientation of mind. It was translated into Tibetan by the great translator Bandhe Yeshe De and his colleagues, and subsequently revised and finalized in the careful editorial tradition of the early translation period.

Translated by the great translator Bandhe Yeshe De and others, revised and finalized.

In the language of India: Ārya-vajra-śṛṅkhalā-tantra-kalpa. In the Tibetan language: The Tantra-Ritual of the Noble Vajra Chain Tantra.

Homage to the Glorious Lord of the World.

Now, what is to be correctly taught:
Listen well to the beautiful ritual
that generates splendor, dispels suffering,
and frees one from the bonds of hatred's assemblage.

Without bathing and purification,
having fully abandoned the practice of asceticism,
this secret mantra, recited
with a fierce and forceful mind, swiftly accomplishes.

Adorned with faith and joyous effort,
endowed with great heedfulness and compassion,
one who does not abandon the teacher
is greatly pleased by brave and disciplined wisdom.

One who has loving-kindness toward all beings —
through merely beginning mantra, meditative absorption, mudrā, and the rest,
accomplishment arises.

One who lacks faith, is lazy, and given to harm,
who is not devoted to the teacher,
whose wisdom is weak and whose strength is small,
who is heedless and bears ill-will toward others —
even if, upon receiving a lofty empowerment,
such a one were to strive for thousands of eons,
accomplishing it ten million times over,
there would be no fruit.

It is just as when some foolish children,
desiring fruit, beat the seedling stalk:
from sheer exhaustion alone,
fruit will never come.

This great king of secret mantras, supreme among mantra-kings,
is the source of many kinds of blessing.

Then the Blessed One of Great Bliss,
known as "Certain Accomplishment,"
entered into meditative absorption, and from his own unsurpassed vajra body, speech, and mind
this mantra-king was drawn forth.

The very moment this vidyā-mantra was proclaimed,
these vast world-systems
trembled in six ways,
and gods, asuras, rākṣasas,
yakṣas, gandharvas, and nāgas,
garuḍas, serpents, and decomposing corpses,
nāgas, kiṃnaras, and the rest —
all those hosts fell into a stupor
and collapsed face-downward on the ground,
their senses utterly lost.
The very instant the rays of great bliss touched them,
they regained consciousness and their splendour was reborn.
Then they praised her with the king of praises:

How wondrous! From the glorious supreme great bliss itself,
through the auspiciousness born of flourishing power,
by the mere utterance of Hūṃ by the goddess,
it resounds through the expanse of space.
Sovereign of primordial wisdom of great bliss,
the goddess who bears the name of Vajra,
who is like an unbroken rosary —
we bow to her with reverence.
By the rays of diverse colours from her body,
which dispel the afflictions of wandering beings,
bliss arose in me through that light.
O goddess of bliss, to you we bow.
Aspirant of emptiness, free from characteristics,
from the vidyā of all the Victors,
the wondrous goddess's nature of mind —
free from conceptual thought, her intrinsic nature — to that we bow.

Thereupon the vidyā-goddess who embodies all the Victors in form and the rest,
having made fine offerings at this maṇḍala
and gathering with one voice together,
praised her with vajra-words as proclaimed by the buddhas:

To the vajra born from space,
the mother who gives birth to all buddhas,
luminous in nature as space free from conceptual elaboration —
to the vajra mind we bow.
With the sound of the vajra form
she binds without exception all the hosts of gods.
The vajra sound that cuts down all māras —
to that voice in space we bow.
She who subdues the māras of the six classes of beings, the six-faced one,
who bows for the sake of pure outer and inner dependent origination,
graceful and yet inspiring slight awe —
to her vajra body we bow.

With those words all the gods and the rest
praised the vajra goddess
with that wondrous king of praises,
and the very instant she was praised,
the goddess arose with great resplendent majesty
and proclaimed her own heart-essence:

Oṃ Vajra-śṛṅkhalā Hūṃ Hūṃ Phaṭ Svāhā.

By the mere recitation of this garland of vidyā-dhāraṇī,
this very secret mantra,

epidemics and swellings, skin diseases,
leprosy, mottled flesh, tumors born of that affliction,
brain disease, eye disease, ear disease,
diseases of the internal organs, lung and liver complaints,
stabbing pains in the joints, heart disease, kidney disease,
disease of the limbs, and pervasive illness,
lymphatic disorders and diseases of the blood,
phlegm, wind, and bile,
and diseases arising from severe imbalance of humors —
all epidemic diseases without exception it pacifies.

Furthermore, the oblivion-casting spirits that cause forgetfulness,
the flesh-eating ḍākinīs and demonesses,
malefic planetary powers and wandering stars that lead astray,
fierce harmful sorcery and binding spells,
the fierce vidyā-mantras of outsiders, and all the rest —
every terrifying harm of every kind
it scatters like clouds before the wind.

Furthermore, tigers, leopards, bears, brown bears,
leaping creatures, great wolves,
hares, rats, snakes, mice,
insects, long-beaked creatures, scorpions,
crows, owls, horses and the like —
however many and various the terrors may be,
by the mere recitation and hearing of this secret mantra
they are driven far away.
All harms of every variety
are cleared by the application of this mantra.
Those who do harm are rendered harmless,
and the harmless are brought to bear harm as well;
a binding application, too, is itself bound by another's application,
and another's binding is itself fettered.

This concludes the first chapter of the Vajra Chain Tantra, the chapter teaching the secret mantra from the Meditations of that Tantra.

Now here is what is to be explained.
The beautiful vajra wrathful goddess declares:
Listen with the great bindu to the divine meditative absorption practices.

Oṃ Vajra-śṛṅkhale
Ātma-rakṣa rakṣa
Hūṃ Hūṃ Hūṃ Phaṭ Svāhā.

With this secret mantra one is protected,
and the body is purified of ordinary conceptual thinking.
From a Hūṃ syllable standing on a moon disc at the heart,
rays of light stream forth;
the sovereign lady of one's lineage
and the lama buddha are invited
and clearly visualized before one.
From the light-rays at the tip of the seed-syllable at the heart,
goddess-attendants holding flowers and the like make offerings;
the vajra goddesses of form and the other senses
bow down and each makes excellent offering in turn.

From throughout the beginningless stream of existence,
all the masses of evil deeds
performed by body, speech, and mind
without exception and in their entirety —
those one did oneself, those one caused others to do,
and those in which one rejoiced after they were done —
all of these, every last one, throughout all existences,
I confess individually.
And the extensive vow never to commit them again
I now take upon myself.

I rejoice in the deeds of the Victors together with their bodhisattva heirs,
and in the virtue of all migrating beings.
In the Victors, the bodhisattvas,
the noble vajra goddesses of the lineage,
and the vast wheel of the mantra tantras,
I take refuge with complete and wholehearted devotion.

I beseech you: in order to give rise to non-conceptual wisdom,
please turn the wheel of Dharma,
and do not pass into nirvāṇa
until all those to be tamed are fully satisfied.

The awakening mind supreme in all its aspects
I shall now arouse.
From the very beginning until the present moment,
whatever this body has failed to do —
now, understanding the wind of conditioned existence,
I offer up this very body of mine.
The vast ocean of the supreme and unsurpassable path of the Great Vehicle —
until the fruit of the Great Vehicle is attained,
I shall rely upon it without interruption.

In this way, having applied faith and aspiration
and having accumulated vast stores of merit,
all that virtue, unsurpassed,
I dedicate entirely to awakening.

First, with rays of loving-kindness,
establish all beings in happiness.
Second, with rays of compassion,
arouse the intention to free beings from suffering.
Third, with joy, may they obtain bliss.
Fourth, free from attachment, rest in equanimity.

After that, abandoning the power of coarseness
and clearing away the stains of habitual tendencies,
one then recollects purity
in the manner of practicing like space.

Oṃ Svabhāvaśuddhāḥ sarvadharmāḥ svabhāvaśuddho 'haṃ Hūṃ.

Having been purified by that,
emptiness is then to be meditated upon in sequence.

Oṃ Śūnyatājñānavajrasvabhāvātmako 'haṃ.

All phenomena without exception
are to be meditated upon as like space.

Within that, from the syllable Raṃ arises a solar maṇḍala,
red, blazing with clustered rays — meditate on this.
In its center, from the blue syllable Hūṃ,
light rays drive away the māras of beings,
and then the light is drawn back into the syllable:
the Vajra Wrathful One appears in full perfection —
three faces, six arms, abiding in a mass of fire.
At her heart, on a sun disc, visualize the blue Hūṃ;
by its light, create the protection wheel.

To the east, a vajra; to the south, a sword;
to the west, water; to the north, an arrow.
To the northeast, a three-pointed spear; to the southeast, fire;
to the southwest, a sword; to the northwest, wind.
Below, a crossed vajra of many kinds;
above, a pavilion capped with a five-pointed vajra.
Through the recitation of the secret mantra, this is to be meditated upon.

From Hūṃ, rays of light radiate forth,
and on the outer perimeter of the arranged wheel,
a three-pointed vajra fence
and a pavilion are to be perfectly meditated upon.

After that, the Vajra Wrathful One
dissolves and becomes devoid of inherent existence.
From that, resting upon a sun disc,
the four māras are suppressed, and the sun and moon
arise from the vowels and consonants (Ālikāli).
In the middle, from Hūṃ arises a vajra,
encircled by light in the nature of the Wrathful One.
The Wrathful One together with her retinue of light rays
abides within the vajra; the form arises —
six faces, six arms, and six feet,
or else four feet, suppressing the four māras.
All her faces are blue and frowning with wrathful brows;
her hair, reddish-gold, streams upward; she is adorned with a skull garland.
Her body is ornamented with vajras, the color of the sky.
She has twelve arms. In the right hands
she holds a vajra, a lasso, a nocked arrow,
a snake, a war-axe, a curved blade.
In the left hands: a bell, a threatening finger gesture, a bow,
a club, a three-pointed hammer.

Alternatively, with six arms: the first right hand holds a vajra,
the second, iron chains,
the third draws an arrow;
the first left hand makes the threatening finger gesture,
the second holds a snake lasso,
the third holds a bow in a drawing posture.

The Vajra Chain Tantra (vajraśṛṅkhala) teaches:
when one rests in meditative concentration and samādhi,
one becomes the very identity of all qualities —
the root of all mantras.

Fully arousing the identity of mantra
and accomplishing the inherent nature of the deity,
through the garland of the precious dhāraṇī
all phenomena will be fully obtained:
the sword, the eye-ointment, the pill,
the foot-anointing, the great treasure,
traveling under the earth, extraction of essence,
traveling through the sky, transmutation into gold,
likewise traveling to other lands,
pacification, enrichment, magnetizing, and subjugation,
stupefaction, paralysis, expulsion,
separation, summoning, binding of vidyā mantras,
protection and great reversal,
making mute, making dumb, causing unconsciousness,
causing blindness, and bringing about various diseases —
the accomplishment of these vast ritual activities,
the full arising of the six kinds of direct knowledge,
and the attainment of the great seal (mahāmudrā) of the vajra of body, speech, and mind:
of what need is it to speak of the minor siddhis,
such as merely seeing auspicious signs?

The deity produced by the practice of mantra,
who has become the very root of visualization —
the goddess born from Hūṃ:
blue, three-faced, six-armed;
the right face yellow, the left face red;
three eyes, gazing with wrathful, angry eyes,
frowning with wrath;
adorned with reddish-gold upswept hair and skulls;
ornamented and resplendent with bone ornaments;
garlanded with a skull necklace, adorned with snakes;
two feet, one extended and one drawn in, stamping upon a corpse.
In the six hands — first right holds a vajra,
second a curved blade,
third holds a snake lasso;
first left makes the threatening finger gesture,
second holds a skull,
third holds a white snake lasso.

Alternatively, all faces are blue.
By means of this deity, all activities of binding and of magnetizing
are brought to complete accomplishment.

In existence, the brahmin-born goddess —

Blue, one face, two hands.
The right hand holds a vajra, the left an iron chain.
Naked, adorned with bone ornaments.
All activities of slaying
are brought to complete accomplishment by this Vajrayoginī.

Then the Blessed One, the Vajra-Bearer,
entered into meditative absorption in the samādhi
called "Mudrā Arising, which causes all phenomena to arise
and swiftly generates siddhis,"
and declared the auxiliary mudrās.

Join the two hands in añjali, palms pressed together,
then fold them back into fists,
bring the two backs of the hands together, and bind
the index fingers and little fingers with an iron-chain grip.
Hold them level before the mouth,
and by reciting the vidyā rosary,
all desires are fulfilled.

With this mudrā sealed, merely by mentally recalling the secret mantra,
one travels the road in ease and is liberated.
It also severs the mantras of others.

This concludes the second chapter of the tantra of the Noble Vajra Chain Tantra — the chapter establishing the deities and mudrās from the vidyāmantra.

Then, what is to be correctly taught —
the supreme union of substances,
which accomplishes all that is comparable to it —
let the Vajra Ḍākinī hear:

Oṃ sarva tathāgata abhiṣiñcatu māṃ. Oṃ sarva tathāgata abhiṣekata samaya śriye hūṃ.

Empowerment is supreme beyond all others.
It purifies the mass of defilements,
and for as long as it is needed, until the accumulation of wisdom has grown vast,
one attains the king of vidyās.

Jaḥ hūṃ baṃ hoḥ. Oṃ vajra puṣpe āḥ hūṃ svāhā. Dhūpe dīpe gandhe. Naivedye āḥ hūṃ svāhā. Oṃ kṣī hrīḥ skala. Ghaṇa vina vare. Śyarya saparivrāra a a a. Abhitiranti. Abhitarante. Abhitarantu. Satha daśa dik. Lokapāla. Lokapāli. Emaṃ pāligṛhṇantu. Khādantu pibantu hūṃ phaṭ. Kha kha khāhi khāhi. Llai llai vajra dharaya. Ājñāvayati svāhā.

Through the union with the wisdom being (jñānasattva),
dualistic conceptuality is abandoned.
Through offerings and torma (ritual cake offerings),
all interruptions and obstacles are completely cleared away.

Oṃ kāya vajra svabhāva ātmako 'ham.

By the vajra of body, speech, and mind,
one ornamented with marks that are thoroughly adorned —
knowing that those without marks abide within those marks —
the characteristics are thoroughly purified.

Oṃ sarva tathāgata jñāna vajra svabhāva ātmako 'ham.

That which is indicated by the sense faculties as object,
the indicated, the characteristic, consciousness,
and gnosis itself directly —
concerning the arising of these, there is no doubt.

Then the chief of the great unions —
the ritual of the cakra — shall be explained.

On a piece of coarse cloth or on bark,
write with saffron, civet, and sandalwood:
a cakra of ten spokes upon a lotus, drawn in yellow pukka,
encircled at the periphery by a garland of fire.
On the ten petals of the lotus,
write the ten — vajra and so forth — that bind in all ten directions.
On the back of each of these, write in sequence the binding mantras for each of the ten directions.
On the ten spokes of the cakra,
write the name of the one to be accomplished, enclosed between two hūṃs,
together with the protective mantra.
In the four directions, write the mantras, mudrās, and the yantra for cutting, binding, and magnetizing.
Furthermore, at the six intermediate directions,
write the six bindings — for gods, nāgas, asuras,
yakṣas, piśācas, and so forth.
At the center, draw an image of the one to be accomplished,
seated in cross-legged posture, hands joined in añjali at the heart,
wearing beautiful garments.
Then recite the mantra rosary one thousand times.
After that, praise with hymns of praise.
Melt together gold and so forth, and apply it to the body or throat,
or wherever one pleases to place it.
By this, for the protection of the yogin,
all that one wishes is accomplished,
and the eight fears are eliminated.

Then furthermore, the king's cloth —

With gold, sandalwood, camphor,
and fragrant substances, prepare it,
then on the day of the lunar mansion Revatī,
draw a wheel with saffron.
At the center, on the pericarp of a lotus with eight petals
enclosed by three outer borders,
draw the form of the one to be accomplished,
marked with the vajra of the limbs.
Surround it with the mantra of the heart essence.
Then, on the border around the hub,
write the binding mantras of above and below.
The mantras of the directions and intermediate directions go on the petals.
On the first outer border,
arrange the long root mantra.
On the second, draw a fence of light.
Place fragrance upon the maṇḍala,
and complete the yogic union with the deity as oneself.
From the seed at the heart, with rays of light,
purify the wheel in the sky;
visualize it in the form of the syllable HŪṂ.
From its total transformation, meditate on the assembled bodies.
At the center, visualize wisdom:
above a moon disk, from HŪṂ,
meditate on a wheel with spokes.
There, the form of the syllable —
visualize it clearly, like a lamp flame —
becomes inseparable from the ḍākinī.
Recite the secret mantra one thousand times,
arising from the seed in one's own heart.
Then melt gold and so forth, and if it is fastened to the body or throat,
it protects from the eight fears
and is certain to guard in all circumstances.

With the five kinds of white mustard
together with stalks of dūrvā grass,
recite the secret mantra one thousand times,
and by applying them through the ritual of the nameless old woman,
making drops at the three places and protecting with them,
life will increase a thousandfold many times over.

With kuśa grass, clarified butter, white mustard, and sesame,
recite the secret mantra one thousand times,
and on an auspicious day
place it on the crown of a stūpa:
epidemic diseases that have taken hold will cease.

In blood and bile,
recite the secret mantra three thousand times,
at the time of a lunar eclipse,
completing the close approach practice.
Apply the substance very finely,
then press the mantra into the heart
of whatever assembly of gods one has in mind as the target of harm:
whatever obstacle one aims at will be utterly destroyed.

On fresh new woolen cloth,
having washed it with wisdom-marked water,
and placed it on an auspicious day,
tie knots in the number of the bindings,
then recite the mantra one thousand times
and fasten it to the body, throat, or crown of the head.
By this one can withstand a thousand years of life,
and epidemic illness will not harm even slightly.

Clothing, food, vessels and utensils,
medicine, and other substances —
if protected with the unshaken seal of the king,
nothing whatsoever can cause harm.

If one speaks three owl-cries
in the manner of a great lion's roar,
even a thousand vidyā-mantras of outsiders
will be utterly unable to prevail.

Remaining in the natural mode of the king of nāgas,
with one-pointed mind and the vajra mudrā,
then reciting the secret mantra three times,
illnesses, spirit afflictions, and the like are cleared away.

Making the boundary of the rabbit's nipple,
with the mantra sealed by HŪṂ,
with a mind settled in objectless meditative absorption —
by this too one will be protected from harm.

Abandoning all externals and the like,
train the mind on appearances,
and clearly focus on the non-apparent:
this is declared the supreme of all protections.

Then yet further practices are described.
In charnel grounds and other frightening places,
or beside a lake, a pond, a hermitage,
a sacred site, a great pleasure grove,
a flower garden, a country region,
the bank of a river, a single tree,
or indeed in one's own home —
on a surface well smeared with maṇḍala,
relying on that, accomplish the yogic union with the deity.
With cloth painting and the like set before one,
seated cross-legged on a comfortable seat,
enter into meditative equipoise.
With a body trained in the ordinary way —

one attains the great liberation — otherwise, it is not so.

On a mountain whose ground is exceedingly delightful and well adorned, in a grove of most beautiful forest, in a place where cool streams flow down, within a carefully arranged bower of flowers, in a dwelling whose door faces east, whose corners are bound with the four mantras according to the rite, that is built in a place equipped with windows — where sweet-voiced birds cry out, where fragrant incense drifts its scent, where bells ring with a lovely tinkling sound, and which is encircled with garlands of rustling silken banners — having abandoned all excessive social activity, together with companions whose conduct is harmonious, one should joyfully accomplish awakening.

Yet the place, companions, and so forth are taken up only to draw the yogi's mind in; beyond all these, when they are entirely abandoned, there is no higher awakening. All the varied experiences of pleasure and suffering arise from one's own mind — this I will not deny — yet from the complete abandonment of mind and its conceptual thoughts, I have taught no other Dharma whatsoever. Therefore, in order to abandon the various workings of mind and conceptual thought, various methods have been taught: mantras, mudrās, meditative absorptions, and the great range of practices.
