The Dhāraṇī of Non-forgetting

The Dhāraṇī of Non-forgetting is a very brief incantation text, known in Tibetan by a title meaning "the dhāraṇī for not forgetting," and preserved in the Kangyur as Toh 1048, where it belongs among the dhāraṇī texts. It opens with homage to the Three Jewels and to the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara, then presents a short Sanskrit mantra invoking memory, mind, and radiant knowledge. The text gives a single practical instruction: one recites the mantra over white sweet flag, a medicinal root long associated with sharpening the mind, and then eats it. Doing so, the text promises, will without doubt enable one to retain whatever one hears. It thus offers a compact ritual aid for memory, valued in a tradition where hearing, remembering, and reciting the teachings were central to study and practice.

Homage to the Three Jewels.
Homage to the bodhisattva, the great being, the noble Avalokiteśvara.

tadyathā smṛti mati mati tejo bidyā huru me hara huru kṛṣṇa ruru me svāhā

If one recites this mantra over white sweet flag and then eats it, one will without doubt come to retain whatever one hears.

The Dhāraṇī of Non-forgetting is complete.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The phrase rendered "without doubt" is the common Tibetan idiom meaning "undoubtedly." Here the word that elsewhere means a seizing spirit (graha) does not carry that sense. "White sweet flag" is calamus (Acorus calamus, Sanskrit vacā), a medicinal root traditionally credited with strengthening memory. The final syllable of the colophon appears to be a scribal variant of the closing particle and has been rendered simply as "is complete."
