The Dhāraṇī Called "Increasing Wisdom" (Prajñāvardhanīnāmadhāraṇī)

The Dhāraṇī Called "Increasing Wisdom," known in Sanskrit as the Prajñāvardhanī nāma dhāraṇī and in Tibetan as Sherab Kyepa Zhejawai Zung, is a brief incantatory text that pays homage to the Three Jewels and to the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara, the great compassionate one. It then gives a short mantra invoking the growth of wisdom, intelligence, and understanding. The text closes with a simple rite in which the practitioner recites the mantra twenty-one times each morning over a palmful of a root preparation and drinks it, so that after a month one will retain whatever one has heard. It was translated into Tibetan by the Indian preceptor Jñānagarbha and the monk Lui Wangpo, and later revised by Vidyākarasiṃha and Devacandra. The work is Toh 1039 in the Kangyur, where it belongs to the class of dhāraṇī texts, the collection of protective and empowering incantations within the tantric section of the canon.

In the language of India: prajñā vardhanī nāma dhāraṇī
In the language of Tibet: The Dhāraṇī Called "Increasing Wisdom."

namo ratna trayāya nama āryāvalokiteśvarāya bodhisatvāya mahāsatvāya mahākāruṇikāya tadyathā oṃ viva viva prajñā varttani jara jara medhā vardhani dhiri dhiri buddhi vardhani svāhā

This is the rite for this vidyā-mantra. Every morning, recite it twenty-one times over a palmful of the root preparation. If one drinks this for one month, one will retain whatever one has heard.

Here ends the dhāraṇī called "Increasing Wisdom."

It was translated by the Indian preceptor Jñānagarbha and the translator, the monk Lui Wangpo. It was edited and finalized by the Indian preceptor Vidyākarasiṃha and the chief editor-translator, the monk Devacandra.

The substance used in the rite, written in Tibetan as "rtsa ba mo," cannot be identified with certainty. It is taken here as a root or herbal preparation, since the text says it is drunk. It may instead refer to a particular medicinal plant, or to dew gathered from grass.
