Verses of Auspiciousness of the Seven Buddhas

This short work can be called in English the Verses of Auspiciousness of the Seven Buddhas. Its Sanskrit and Tibetan titles are not given in the opening lines supplied here. After a homage to the Three Jewels, it moves through a sequence of benedictory stanzas, each recalling one of the buddhas of the past, beginning with Vipaśyin, Śikhin, and Viśvabhū. For each, it notes his caste, the human lifespan in his age, the city where he lived, and the tree under which he attained full awakening. Each stanza closes with the refrain asking that the perfect auspiciousness of that Sugata be present today, so the text works as a recitation for invoking blessing and good fortune. It is Toh 1104 in the Kangyur, where it belongs among the brief prayers and auspicious verses collected alongside the dhāraṇī texts.

Homage to the Three Jewels.

The Blessed Vipaśyin, of royal caste and full of splendor,
in the age when the lifespan was eighty thousand years, in the city called Endowed with Beauty,
attained full awakening at the foot of a śirīṣa tree:
may the perfect auspiciousness of that Sugata be here today!

The Sugata Śikhin, of royal caste and possessed of miraculous powers,
in the age when the lifespan was seventy thousand years, in the city of Suka,
attained full awakening at the foot of the supreme tree, the sundha:
may the perfect auspiciousness of that Sugata be here today!

The Victor Viśvabhū, of royal caste and endowed with wisdom,
in the age when the lifespan was sixty thousand years, in the city of Anupama,
attained full awakening at the foot of the supreme tree, the sāla:
may the perfect auspiciousness of that Sugata be here today!

The Sage Krakucchanda, powerful, of brahmin caste,
in the age when the lifespan was forty thousand years, in the city called Endowed with Beauty,
attained full awakening at the foot of a śirīṣa tree:
may the perfect auspiciousness of that Sugata be here today!

The Guide Kanakamuni, endowed with supreme training, of brahmin caste,
in the age when the lifespan was thirty thousand years, in the city called Endowed with Bliss,
attained full awakening at the foot of an udumbara tree:
may the perfect auspiciousness of that Sugata be here today!

The Protector Kāśyapa, of royal caste and endowed with unsurpassed qualities,
in the age when the lifespan was twenty thousand years, in Vārāṇasī,
attained full awakening at the foot of a nyagrodha tree:
may the perfect auspiciousness of that Sugata be here today!

The Protector of Beings Śākyamuni, of royal caste and endowed with diligence,
in the age when the lifespan was a hundred years, in the city of Kapilavastu,
attained full awakening at the foot of an aśvattha tree:
may the perfect auspiciousness of that Sugata be here today!

Whatever perfect auspiciousness there is of the seven successive buddhas,
the wondrous blessing of the sugatas of all directions and times,
the pure dharmadhātu, the powerful and unchanging interdependence:
may the perfect auspiciousness of the Sugata be here today!

The Auspicious Verses of the Seven Successive Buddhas are complete.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The lifespan for Kanakamuni is written "yum khri," which makes no sense. "Sum khri," thirty thousand, plainly fits the descending series and is translated here.

The city names "Endowed with Beauty," used for both Vipaśyin and Krakucchanda, and "Endowed with Bliss," used for Kanakamuni, could not be securely matched to Sanskrit names, so they are translated literally. The text also gives the śirīṣa tree for both Vipaśyin and Krakucchanda. Both points are rendered as the text stands.

"Suka" and the "sundha" tree are kept as transliterated in the Tibetan.
