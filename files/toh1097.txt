The Aspiration of Supreme Conduct (Agracaryāpraṇidhāna)

The Aspiration of Supreme Conduct, known in Sanskrit as Agracaryāpraṇidhāna and in Tibetan as Chokgi Chöpé Mönlam, is a short verse prayer of aspiration in the Mahāyāna tradition. It opens with an homage to all buddhas and bodhisattvas and bows to the buddhas of the ten directions for the benefit and happiness of all beings. It invokes Maitreya, Mañjuśrī, and Dharmamati as models of how to pay homage, asks the compassionate Victors to keep the practitioner in mind, and moves on to the confession of past misdeeds. Like other prayers of its kind, it expresses the bodhisattva's wish that all beings attain supreme awakening. The text is Toh 1097 in the Kangyur, where it belongs to the class of aspiration prayers, or praṇidhāna, which are recited to direct one's merit and intentions toward enlightenment.

In the language of India: agracāryapraṇidhāna. In the language of Tibet: The Aspiration of Supreme Conduct.

Homage to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas.

For the benefit and happiness of all creatures,
I bow to the buddhas in the worlds of the ten directions.
To them I shall pay honor and make offerings.
May all beings attain supreme awakening.

Maitreya, and Mañjuśrī, the lord of insight,
the hero Dharmamati, whose speech is nectar:
just as those heroes know how to pay homage,
so too do I bow to the lords of men.

Compassionate, acting for the welfare of the world,
the Victors, for whom nothing is unknown, unseen, or lacking,
lords of men who dwell in tender loving-kindness:
may all those Victors keep me in mind.

Whatever evil deeds I myself have done
in my former existences, I confess.
Likewise, whatever was done by body, speech, and mind,
all of it I confess, each one.

Just as Śākyamuni, Akṣobhya, and Amitābha
know and understand the thorough purification of deeds,
and the way of remorse and confession,
so too shall I confess my wrongdoing.

To all holy beings who bring joy to all,
to all who have entered upon awakening, I make my appeal.
May the four lower realms swiftly be pacified.
May I wander about, destroying the unfree states.

Just as the buddhas Bhadracarya and the lord Vimala,
likewise Meruśrī and Sūryapradīpa,
know how to rejoice in merit,
so too do I rejoice in merit.

Whatever practice of generosity there is in the ten directions,
likewise all practice of moral discipline and concentration,
all cultivation of wisdom and all power of merit:
in these I rejoice for the sake of the qualities of awakening.

In the boundless qualities of all the Victors,
and in all who teach the Dharma and establish others in awakening,
I rejoice for the sake of the qualities of awakening.
May all beings attain supreme awakening.

I urge all the Victors who dwell in the world,
all who have attained awakening in the ten directions:
just as Brahmā once entreated them,
may the Victors remain and teach the supreme Dharma.
May all the Victors remain for many eons.
May all beings attain supreme awakening.
May all human beings swiftly become supreme and unsurpassed,
lamps of the world, Sugatas.

Whatever homage and offerings I have made to the buddhas,
whatever rejoicing there has been in virtuous deeds,
whatever confession there has been of evil deeds,
whatever beholding of the Victors, lords among two-legged beings,
whatever slight virtue I have done,
whether it arose from body, speech, or mind,
whatever I have done, am doing, and shall do:
all of it I give for the benefit of creatures.

Just as the buddhas of the ten directions, knowing,
have given well, given generously and abundantly,
so too do I give for the benefit of creatures.
May all beings attain supreme awakening.

Whatever unsurpassed fruit of merit there may be
from all that I have given to beings,
I dedicate it wholly to the Great Vehicle.
May the fruit of awakening be accomplished for me.

May the incomparable gateways in every respect
become directly present to me for the sake of beings.
Training in the footsteps of Mañjuśrī,
I shall make the aspiration for the conduct of awakening.

Even for the sake of a single person,
though I should have to undergo all the feelings of the Avīci hell,
I shall persevere with enthusiasm for many eons.
May all beings be free from sorrow.

The sufferings of the hells and of the world of Yama,
the sufferings of animals and the sufferings of humans,
the boundless masses of the sufferings of beings:
may they fall upon me, and may beings be happy.

The pure fields of the Victors,
pure beings, and pure wisdom,
and whatever boundless aspirations the Victors have made:
may all those aspirations be accomplished in me.

May my aspiration never decline.
May I never become weary.
Until I attain supreme awakening,
may I persevere with enthusiasm for many eons.

The Aspiration of Supreme Conduct is complete.

It was translated, edited, and finalized by the Indian preceptor Prajñāvarman and the chief editor-translator Bandé Yeshé Dé, among others.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The second verse is difficult. "Lord of insight" renders a phrase that may instead name a separate bodhisattva, and "Dharmamati" and "whose speech is nectar" might also be read as distinct figures. In the seventh verse, the names Bhadracarya, Vimala, Meruśrī, and Sūryapradīpa are reconstructed from the Tibetan, and the grouping of the names is uncertain. The word rendered "pay honor" in the first verse is irregular in the Tibetan and has been translated by its evident sense. In the verse on suffering, the Tibetan reads "mind-hells," which is taken as a copyist's slip for "hells." "Gateways" may refer to the gateways of dhāraṇī and concentration.
