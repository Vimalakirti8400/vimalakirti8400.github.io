Thus did I hear. At one time the Blessed One was dwelling in Śrāvastī, in the Jeta Grove, the garden of Anāthapiṇḍada, in the residence of Prince Jeta, together with a great Saṅgha of one thousand two hundred and fifty monks.

At that time the venerable Ānanda completed the rains retreat in the city where he was residing. When the three months of the rains season had passed, he performed the making of the rains-robes. Having received the rains-robes, he took up his bowl and robes and went to where the Blessed One was. He arrived, bowed his head at the feet of the Blessed One, and sat down to one side.

Seated to one side, the Blessed One declared these words to the venerable Ānanda: "Ānanda, for the sake of the welfare, benefit, happiness, strength, and splendor of all sentient beings, take up, bear, recite, retain, thoroughly master, and keep in mind these root mantras of the great dhāraṇī. And why is that? Because the root mantras of the great dhāraṇī are difficult to come by, and because the Tathāgatas, arhats, perfectly and completely awakened buddhas are themselves rare to encounter in the world — like the flower of the udumbara tree. Therefore take them up, bear them, recite them, retain them, keep them in mind, and thoroughly master them.

"Ānanda, whatever son or daughter of a faithful family holds, bears, recites, retains, or thoroughly masters these root mantras of the great dhāraṇī shall not meet an untimely death by weapons. Not by poison. Not by fire. Not by water. Not by royal punishment. They shall not meet an untimely death at all. Gods, asuras, nāgas, wind-gods, garuḍas, gandharvas, kinnaras, great serpents, yakṣas, rākṣasas, hungry ghosts, flesh-eaters, kumbhāṇḍas, piśācas, decaying spirits, elemental spirits, spirits that cause forgetfulness, spirits that cause madness, oppressing spirits, shadow-afflicting spirits, spirits that bring death, and spirits that bring harm shall have no power over such a person.
