The Praise by Śakra, Lord of the Gods

The Praise by Śakra, Lord of the Gods, known in Tibetan as Lha'i dbang po brgya byin gyis bstod pa, is a very brief verse hymn attributed to Śakra, the king of the gods also known as Indra. No Sanskrit title is preserved with it. In a few lines, it describes the suffering of saṃsāra across the three times, driven by attachment, aversion, and delusion, in which gods and asuras alike are shaped by the ripening of their deeds. It then addresses a fierce protective goddess who delights in flesh and blood, calling on her to keep her pledge of victory to the gods and destroy the armies of the asuras on the field of battle. The work is Toh 1089 in the Kangyur, where it belongs among the short praises and invocations connected with the tantric and dhāraṇī collections.

The Praise by Śakra (Indra), Lord of the Gods

Amid the great suffering of saṃsāra throughout the three times,
through the cause of the three, attachment, aversion, and delusion,
gods and asuras are formed by karma, the ripening of evil deeds.
Here in this realm where hosts of hells blaze like fire,
you, goddess, who revel in flesh and red blood,
having pledged victory to the gods on the field of battle,
destroy the armies of the asuras!

Thus he praised. It is complete.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

In line six, རྒྱལ་བ is taken in its ordinary sense of "victory" in battle, not as the epithet Jina. The goddess praised is not named in this passage. The closing word written རྫོགས་སྷོ is read as རྫོགས་སོ, "it is complete."
