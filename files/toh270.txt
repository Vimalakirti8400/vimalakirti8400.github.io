At one time, the Blessed One was dwelling on the summit of Mount Tise (Kailāsa), in a divine abode, not far at all from where the sages practiced — together with a great Saṅgha of monks numbering around five hundred, and five hundred bodhisattvas, namely: the bodhisattva, the great being Maitreya (Loving-Kindness); the bodhisattva, the great being Ākāśagarbha (Essence of Space); Samantabhadra; Anantapuṣpa (Boundless Flowers); Sarvatrapuṣpa (Flowers Everywhere); and so forth — all of them bodhisattvas who were bound by only one more birth, assembled together in that same place.

Then the Blessed One, there in the forest grove on that very mountain summit, observed a monk who had been seized by a spirit and was writhing in agony, stripped bare without clothes, his arms outstretched, weeping.

Thereupon the bodhisattva, the great being Ākāśagarbha, drew his upper robe over one shoulder, placed his right knee upon the ground, and bowing with palms joined in the direction of the Blessed One, addressed the Blessed One in these words: "Blessed One, who is this in the middle of the sky uttering the cry 'Kyi hud!' — and who is this monk, stripped bare, without garments, his one hand empty?"

Then the Blessed One declared to the bodhisattva, the great being Ākāśagarbha: "Ākāśagarbha, this one has been struck by illness. And that other one has also been struck by illness."

Thereupon the bodhisattva, the great being Ākāśagarbha, addressed the Blessed One in these words: "Blessed One, may the Blessed One please teach and proclaim the knowledge-mantra (vidyā-mantra) that utterly pacifies all illness and repels all spirits."

Then the Blessed One performed a buddha-emanation, and from the sky appeared six Tathāgatas. The seventh is the Blessed One himself, the Tathāgata Śākyamuni. Then the Tathāgata Vipaśyin declared to the bodhisattva, the great being Ākāśagarbha: "Ākāśagarbha, whoever carries this knowledge-mantra, or recites it, or causes it to be perfected — no weapon can harm that person's body; they will not meet their end from water, or from the suffering of sickness; they will not die an untimely death, nor will they meet a timely end by premature dying. Even if they consume poison, it will become like food. If this mantra is recited seven times over food and drink and then consumed, harm from enemies will not arise. They will not be struck by any illness. They will live long. They will retain what they hear. They will behold the forms of the seven Tathāgatas. If the mantra is recited into an ear pierced by a weapon and sesame-flower butter is applied therein — and for those struck by leprosy and dropsy, if butter blended with blue lotus, cardamom, and knotgrass plants is given — all illnesses will be healed. Ākāśagarbha, these foundations of secret mantra were taught by many hundreds of thousands of ten million buddhas for the benefit of all sentient beings, for the complete pacification of all illnesses, for the reversal of errors in meditation, for the reversal of all evil dreams, and for the prevention of untimely death.
