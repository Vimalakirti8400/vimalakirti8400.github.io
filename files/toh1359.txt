The Rite of the Maṇḍala of Glorious Kālacakra (Śrīkālacakramaṇḍalopāyikāvidhi)

The Rite of the Maṇḍala of Glorious Kālacakra, known in Sanskrit as Śrīkālacakramaṇḍalopāyikāvidhi and in Tibetan as Dpal dus kyi 'khor lo'i dkyil 'khor gyi cho ga, is a ritual manual devoted to the maṇḍala of Kālacakra, the "Wheel of Time" deity of highest yoga tantra. It opens with homage to Kālacakra as the all-pervading lord whose nature unites method and wisdom and who destroys the terrors of saṃsāra. The author then promises to explain the rite of his maṇḍala in keeping with what the tantra teaches, for the benefit of oneself and others. The work frames this undertaking with a reflection on how beings wander on false paths because beginningless ignorance obscures their understanding, which presents the maṇḍala rite as a means of liberation. It is Toh 1359 in the Tengyur, where it belongs among the tantric treatises and ritual texts associated with the Kālacakra cycle.

In the language of India: śrīkālacakramaṇḍalopayikābidhiḥ
In the language of Tibet: The Rite of the Maṇḍala of Glorious Kālacakra.

Homage to glorious Kālacakra.

He who, knowing things and non-things, utterly destroys the terrors of saṃsāra,
The all-pervading lord whose nature is method and wisdom, glorious Kālacakra, who acts wholly for the sake of others:
Having paid homage to him, I shall explain, for the sake of beings and in keeping with the rite taught in the tantra, the very rite of his maṇḍala,
Which serves one's own aim and also the aim of others, and accomplishes what the mind desires.

Here, sentient beings travel a false path because the eye of their intelligence is obscured by the mass of darkness of beginningless ignorance. Certain beings have truly seen that they are able to remove the suffering these beings undergo. They may be monks, novices or householders who possess supreme compassion and have attained the superknowledges, or they may be vajra masters who possess compassion and the rest but lack the superknowledges. First they establish the disciples in the training of the fasting vows (upavāsa) and so forth. Then, by the path that enters the city of nirvāṇa, namely empowerment in the maṇḍala and so forth, they take them into their care according to the degrees of their capacity. This is done as follows.

When excellent disciples have made their request, and when tormas, offerings and the like have first been made on an auspicious day or similar occasion, the ground for the maṇḍala should be thoroughly examined.

To the northeast and north of the town or other settlement, the site serves for pacifying and enriching. To the east and southeast, for causing division and expelling. To the southwest and south, for subjugating and summoning. To the west and northwest, for paralyzing and stupefying. In the center of the town or other settlement, the maṇḍala for all activities is to be drawn.

The ground to be chosen is slightly raised in the center and slopes slightly to the east and north. It is of four kinds, distinguished as the ground of brahmins, of kṣatriyas, of vaiśyas and of śūdras, and these are to be recognized by color, smell and taste. White is for pacifying and enriching. Red is for subjugating and summoning. Yellow is for paralyzing and stupefying. Black is for killing and expelling. Green, having all tastes and all smells, and the purity of the space element, is for all activities.

There dig a pit, and with that same earth
Quickly fill the pit back in.
If it then turns out full,
Know that very ground to be auspicious.
Again dig that pit about a span deep,
And fill it up with water;
Then walk a hundred paces.
Having come back, look at it:
If it is full, the ground is good;
If it is low, it is not held good; avoid it.
If the water merely makes a sound,
Understand there is danger from nāgas and the like.

This is the examination of the ground.

Once the master has thus learned the characteristics of the ground, he should act on the fifth or the tenth day of the middle spring month or the last spring month, or of other months, in the morning, at a time free from malign planets, viṣṭi and the like. An area of about four cubits is smeared with cow dung. In the center of the portion of ground examined for the maṇḍala, he sits facing east and abides in deity yoga. He worships the field protectors (kṣetrapālas) dwelling there with flowers and so on, and says three times:

Gods and nāgas who dwell here,
Yakṣas, rākṣasas, or any others:
From you I ask for this ground;
Grant it for the sake of the maṇḍala!

Then he performs purification and the rest by the rite that will be taught below, and gives torma with the name of the town and this mantra:

oṃ āḥ hūṃ amukasya saparivāraya idam balim gandhaṃ puṣpaṃ dhūgaṃ dīpaṃ akṣatam daṅāma hespa'i vārāḥ śīghraṃ āgacchatu jaḥ hūṃ bam hoḥ yidaṃ bali gṛhnatu khādaṃtu pivatus parivārākaḥ sarva satvānāṃ śāntiṃ puṣṭiṃ rakṣa avarṇa guptiṃ karotu idaṃ sthanaṃ tyakatva bāhye maṇḍala bhumyāṃ tiṣṭhatu hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ vajradhār ājñāpayati sāhā

This is the rite for the departure of the field protectors.

Then, by the procedure taught in the sādhana, he meditates on the protection wheel, burns, expels and nails down the obstructors, emanates the host of wrathful ones and guards the ten directions. After that the earth goddess Pṛthivī is to be invited. The sequence is this. At one's own heart, on a clear moon as a base, and upon that a lotus, on a moon, the syllable lāṃ transforms into a yellow wheel. That transforms into the earth goddess, three-faced and six-armed, holding a wheel, a staff and a vajra in her right hands, and a conch, an iron chain and a bell in her left, clad in yellow garments and adorned with jewels. So one imagines her. With the light rays from the seed syllable at her heart, she is invited in the form just described with the mantra jaḥ hūṃ baṃ hoḥ and made one with the commitment being (samayasattva). Then comes the verse for offering the arghya, water in its vessel together with flowers, scents and the rest:

When the Victor crushed the armies of Māra, with their lord of the gods, then, goddess, you became the witness.
Just as you accepted the worship and homage paid to you, supreme goddess, so likewise accept this arghya of mine.

With this he praises her, and then makes his request:

Just as the Victor, for the sake of awakening, crushed the armies of Māra endowed with supreme strength,
So likewise I too, for the sake of the disciples' empowerment, shall destroy the forces of Māra.

He then worships her with this mantra:

oṃ lāṃ āḥ hūṃ vasundhari sarva buddha jñānni sarva satvopakāriṇi vajra satva ājñapayati śīghraṃ āgccha āgaccha yidaṃ gandhaṃ puṣpaṃ dhūpaṃ dībaṃ naivedyaṃ phala akṣataṃ dadāmi tāva tvameva gṛhṇa grihṇa maṇḍalarthaṃ mama sthānāṃ dadā sarva satvānāṃ śāntiṃ puṣṭiṃ kṛtva subha citte na idaṃ sthānā gaccha svāhā

This is the rite of requesting the earth goddess to depart.

Then he regards the ten directions, the whole realm of space, as pervaded by buddhas and bodhisattvas, presents them with arghya and the rest, worships them with flowers and so forth, and earnestly entreats them with these words:

Buddhas, and bodhisattvas free of wrongdoing, who dwell in all the directions together with your consorts,
You who possess supreme compassion, I pray you, protect me for the sake of empowerment in the maṇḍala.

Then the obstructors, the misleaders (vināyakas) and all the others are to be summoned with the vajra hook.

They are to be bound with the vajra noose. Vajrakīla, king of the wrathful ones, whose body below the navel has the form of a spike, is imagined driving down upon the hostile ones. The lines of his mantra are these: oṃ ā hūṃ ho hom kṣaḥ hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ phaṭ phaṭ vajrakīlya vākacittajñān adhipate śaṭamukha śaṭabhuja ṣaṭa sarvacaraṇa sarvavighnāṃ vināyākādi duṣṭānāṃ kāyavākacittajñānā kīlaya hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ phaṭ.

Then the mantra of the vajra mallet is this: oṃ llī vajramuṅgara mārādīnāṃ śirasi kaṇṭha hridaye nabhyo guhya anti vajrakīlakana ākaṭaya kṣaṃ kṣīṃ kṣiṃ kṣri kṣṛṃ kṣaṃ kṣūṃ kṣḷṃ kṣḹṃ hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ phaṭ phaṭ.

While reciting this, the pegs are smeared with red sandalwood and wound with thread of the five colors. At the top they are tied with flowers and consecrated white mustard seed. With these pegs made of khadira wood, twelve finger-widths long and four finger-widths thick, one should strike into the ten directions of the ground. Upon them one emanates and sets in place the ten wrathful ones, each with his own weapon. This is the rite of nailing the obstructors with pegs in order to protect the ground with pegs.

Then, having thus regarded the ground as free of obstructors, one should examine the mahoraga, the great serpent of the earth. On the ground where the maṇḍala is to be made, one builds a square earthen platform, and having learned the characteristics, such as how the mahoraga lies in the center of that very ground, one should purify the ground. Moreover, according to the divisions of the sun's course, for each set of three months in turn, beginning with the first month of autumn, it lies with its head toward the east, the south, the west and the north, facing south and so on; for it is taught, "The mahoraga lies in the center of that ground."

Having understood this, together with the practitioner on the north side, he holds the cord with the vajra fist. Standing at the fire corner and the wind corner, they move clockwise toward the east, the south, the north and the west, and mark out the lines. Then, circling in the same way, one should dig the ground. One digs from the side of the mahoraga's navel, that is, leaving a cubit's distance from its belly, or leaving the spot directly over its navel.

If stones remain in that ground, it will be destroyed by wind. Bones will cause stabbing pains. Potsherds will cause muteness. Chaff and charcoal will cause fever or death. Hair, grass, ash, sticks and the like will cause minor obstacles. For that reason these should be removed. If jewels, conch shells or beads come up from it, that will be most auspicious.

If ground with the characteristics stated earlier is not found, one digs until water or rock is seen, removes those things, and fills the pit with other earth. For the sake of scent and taste, the kind of earth follows the kind of rite. For white earth, one fills it with earth mixed with sandalwood water, astringent and bitter substances. For red, with lotus water and the three pungents. For yellow, with the urine of donkeys, horses and humans, and with molasses. For black, with rotten flesh, salt and sour substances.

Then, for the white, the pacifying rite, crystal should be placed in the heart of the ground. For increasing, conch. For overcoming epidemics, pearl. For the red, magnetizing, gorocana. For summoning, vermilion. For paralyzing poison, realgar. For the yellow, nailing with the peg, a ram's horn. For immobilizing, orpiment. For bewildering, a snake. For the black, killing, human bone. For expelling, a crow's feather. For the green, the five precious substances are placed.

Then the ground of the maṇḍala is prepared a cubit and a half in height, level all around, slightly like the back of a tortoise, sloping toward the east and the north.

It is further stated:

If one is unable to remove
All the faults of taste without exception,
Press it down with the hand and recite the mantra;
That is declared the great purification.
A mansion, the top of a rock, a pasture and a road,
A temple and the like, a riverbank,
And places that were purified before:
These are pure even without cleansing.

Although this is taught, here the expelling of obstructors through the yoga of Vajravega is done by striking with white mustard seed, water and ash over which the seven-syllable mantra has been recited. Beginning from the east, the ground is smeared with the five substances and spread out, and it is purified again as follows. Reciting oṃ bhuḥ khaṃ, one makes it space. Then with hūṃ lāṃ hūṃ one imagines it as the vajra ground, and recites the mantras oṃ medinī vajrābhava vajrabandha hūṃ oṃ hana hana vajrakrodha hūṃ phaṭ om āḥ hūṃ śodhaya śodhaya rakṣa rakṣa hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ, places the palms on the ground, and blesses it.

Then one mentally invites Pṛthivī, the earth goddess, who earlier departed to another place, and worships her with torma, water for the feet and so forth. Reciting oṃ āḥ hūṃ ho haṃ kṣaḥ, one makes the lotus-turning gesture of the hands and imagines her dissolving into that very ground.

Then, reciting the sixteen-part mantra, one should resolve with intense conviction that the three realms are firmly established as empty and of one taste, in the very nature of the completely purified ground. This is the unsurpassed purification.

Then the entire ground of the maṇḍala should be beautifully adorned with various garlands of flowers hung with bells, with various cloths, and with canopies. This is the rite of the three kinds of purification of the ground.

Then, seated in the center of the maṇḍala ground adorned with flowers and holding the vajra and the vajra bell, one who possesses the yoga of the maṇḍala to be drawn destroys the obstructors by means of the protection wheel and the rest, as follows. Holding the incense vessel, and having generated the tongue as a vajra from hūṃ, one says:

Buddhas dwelling in every direction,
Please turn your minds to me.
Here I, Vajra So-and-so,
Wishing to draw the maṇḍala,
Request the buddhas and the rest to come.
Grant here the supreme accomplishments.

With these words one makes supplication to the perfect buddhas and the rest. Then four disciples, or three and so on, stationed in the four directions beginning with the east, or else the master himself, holding vajra and bell, go to each of those places in turn, according to the yoga of Amoghasiddhi and the others, and recite:

Great mind beyond all beings,
Stainless through natural purity,
Samantabhadra, supreme of mind,
Please explain the sublime maṇḍala.

Free of intrinsic nature, all the Tathāgatas,
Abode of all the Tathāgatas,
Supreme selflessness of all phenomena,
Please explain the sublime maṇḍala.

Supreme one arisen from the peace of phenomena,
Engaged in utterly pure wisdom,
Samantabhadra, supreme of speech,
Please explain the sublime maṇḍala.

Fully complete in all characteristics,
Free of characteristics, having wholly cast them off,
Samantabhadra, supreme of body,
Please explain the sublime maṇḍala.

In the order of these four verses one should have the supplication made, and so forth.

Then, preceded by the emanation of the maṇḍala wheel and so on, the maṇḍala one has meditated upon is placed at the crown of the head with this gesture: bind the two vajra fists, raise the index fingers with their tips joined and pointing downward, and, saying hūṃ vajra utiṣṭha, lift it into the sky.

Unfolding the cross-legged posture, one rises and stands evenly, facing east. Joining the palms at the crown of the head, one bows to the Tathāgatas. Seeing that sentient beings have fallen into the darkness of ignorance and are under the sway of Māra, in order to destroy those māras one instantly makes oneself into the body of Vajravega, with wrathful frown and bared fangs. In the right eye a sun arises from ma, in the left a moon from ṭa, and on the pupils, from hūṃ, a blaze like the fire at the end of time. With this vajra gaze one looks upon the beings in all directions. Generating divine pride:

I am glorious Vajradhara.
I myself am Vajravega.
I am the great king of buddhas.
I am the vajra of great power.
I am the king of the lords of yoga.
I am the one who displays the vajra mudrā.

Thus it is done. Then, visualizing a blazing vajra at the soles of the feet, one performs the turning of the lotus hands, together with the actions, in the sequence about to be described. Sounding the vajra and ringing the bell, beginning from Īśāna in the northeast and moving clockwise, one treads with the vajra gait. Together with the disciples, or alone, one dances upon the maṇḍala ground:

With the eyes, the hands, the hips,
The feet, the ribs and the sides, turn to the right;
Joining radiance and majesty,
Do likewise to the left.
Having danced thus without a break,
Draw in and circle round; and then again
With swift majesty dance in the same way,
And circle round in the form of the principal deity.
With body, speech and mind abiding firm:
Listen, all you hosts of obstructors!
If you do not act as commanded
By me, glorious Vajradhara,
With this blazing vajra
Sprung from the three families I shall destroy you.
If you transgress my command
You will be utterly destroyed, and nothing else.

Reciting this three times, one emanates countless wrathful ones and crushes the obstructors, and blesses the space beneath the maṇḍala ground as having the nature of a vajra arisen from hūṃ. Here, at the time of treading, each should place his own vajra inside the bell held by the one on his left; that is, the vajra of each goes into the bell on his left. The same applies to those in front, on the right and behind.

Then, visualizing beneath the feet a five-pronged vajra arisen from hūṃ hūṃ, one touches the soles of the feet with the five-pronged vajra mudrā, and with that vajra at the soles one treads the center of the ground, circling about in the right-extended, left-extended and even stances. Placing yaṃ yā on the soles and touching them with the sword mudrā, one treads in the east in the round posture. Placing raṃ rāḥ and touching with the jewel mudrā, in the south with the playful posture. Placing laṃ lāḥ and touching with the wheel mudrā, in the west with the vaiśākha posture. Placing vaṃ vāḥ and touching with the lotus mudrā, in the north with the lotus posture; having thus blessed it, one treads.

As for these: join the two hands palm to palm, join the thumbs, and likewise the little fingers. Join the middle fingers at the top, and behind them bend the two index fingers into the shape of a half moon. The two ring fingers reach into their own palms. This is the five-pronged vajra mudrā. Binding the vajra fist, joining the middle and index fingers and raising them, is the sword mudrā. Joining the five fingers, with the middle finger protruding slightly upward from the center, is the jewel mudrā. The fingers stretched out in the form of the spokes of a wheel is the wheel mudrā. Joining the thumb and little finger and stretching out the rest is the lotus mudrā.

Then, bending the left leg and extending the right leg five spans, standing straight in the playful manner, is the right-extended stance. The reverse of that is the left-extended stance. The toes of both feet resting evenly is the even posture. With twenty-six finger-widths between the two feet, one knee raised slightly upward, is the playful posture. With twenty-four finger-widths between the two feet, both knees bent slightly backward, is the vaiśākha. From the vaiśākha, making the legs like the wings of a goose with two cubits between them, is the round posture.

Then one anoints the maṇḍala ground with scent and worships it properly with mantra lines such as vajra gandha. Again, as taught in the earlier rite, the mantra for inviting the vajra earth goddess Pṛthivī is oṃ lāṃ hūṃ vajra bhūmi tiṣṭha ma cal hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ vajra dhā ra ā jñā pa ya ti svā hā. With this, one should then again offer water for welcome and so forth. This is the rite of consecrating the ground.

Some hold that before the preparatory rite one should take possession of the ground and stake the obstructors with ritual daggers. That is not correct. Why? Because taking possession has its purpose within the preparatory rite, and the rite of staking the obstructors belongs to the time when the ground is purified. It is right to purify the ground after one has burned and destroyed the obstructors by meditating on the protection wheel and then staked them with daggers. As the tantra says, "Having made supplication to all of them, one should strike the ground with hard pegs made of khadira wood."

Someone may ask: "You also accept staking the obstructors with daggers at the times of meditative equipoise in the three periods of the day. Why then do you not accept it when the rites are separated by the interval of a day and a night?" We do accept it at the time of meditation, but there we do not accept pegs of khadira wood and the like. By your view, it would follow that the rite of staking the obstructors would also be required when the colors are drawn, and it would follow here because the preparatory rite of the ground was performed earlier. If one asks why, the point is this: since that was already done when the ground was purified, the rite of staking the obstructors is not needed before the preparatory rite of the ground.

Then the disciples bathe and anoint their bodies with fragrances, and are examined by means of the tooth-stick, according to the rite to be explained below. In their six cakras, on the six seats of moon and the rest, those of the method families are to be protected with the six long vowels, according to the distinctions of body, speech, and mind, and those of the wisdom families with the six short ones. The same applies to objects to be consecrated, such as cast images. For books, the short vowels are used.

Then the supplication is to be made:
"As a cause of liberation for sentient beings, I shall draw the maṇḍala of glorious Kālacakra."
For this reason the buddhas and bodhisattvas bless the disciples and the others. This is the sequence of the rite of accepting the disciples.

Then, reciting the six-syllable mantra over the ground of the maṇḍala, one anoints it with fragrances and the five nectars and scatters masses of flowers over it. One bathes one's body, anoints it with fragrances, and adorns oneself with red garments and garlands of flowers. Earrings, necklace, bracelets, anklets, belt, crest jewel, ashes, half moon, sacred thread, and tiger skin: these ornaments, as taught in the sādhana, should be blessed with mantras and then worn.

Then, standing to the east of the maṇḍala and facing west, possessed of the yoga of the principal deity of the maṇḍala, one determines the seats of the deities and makes circles with sandalwood, saffron, and the like. In each of these one visualizes the samaya wheel, invites the wisdom beings dwelling in space and causes them to enter, and performs the rites of offering, praise, and tasting the nectar. Then six or ten vases are to be prepared in the eastern and other directions outside the maṇḍala that is meditated upon.

For pacifying and increasing, the vase is sixteen finger-widths in inner breadth and twenty finger-widths in height from the base to the lip; the lip hangs down two finger-widths; the neck is six finger-widths; the mouth measures eight finger-widths from lip to lip, of which two parts belong to the central opening and one part to each of the two lips. For killing, it is a human skull cup smeared with charcoal from the charnel ground and human fat. For dividing and expelling, the vase is twelve finger-widths in inner breadth and fourteen finger-widths in height; the neck is eight finger-widths and somewhat crooked; the mouth is six finger-widths and the lip one finger-width. For subjugating and summoning, it is a vase just as the craftsman made it. For paralyzing, it is squat, sixteen finger-widths in breadth and height; the neck is four finger-widths, the mouth six finger-widths, and the lip two finger-widths. For all activities, one should take the vase described for the activity of pacifying and the others.

Vases made of gold, silver, crystal, or copper are used as they are available. If there is no gold or the like, vases having the characteristics described are dissolved into emptiness and imagined as forms of jewels arising from vaṃ. For pacifying and increasing they are smeared with rice paste, vajra substance, or white sandalwood. The emblems of the deities are drawn on them. Inside are fragrant water together with the five nectars, the five jewels, the five medicines, and the five grains. Furnished with beautiful twigs and with their necks wrapped in a pair of cloths, they are placed in front, beneath the canopy, upon separate seats appropriately made on a fragrant circle in the form of the maṇḍala, following the sequence for setting out the emblems on the colored-powder maṇḍala that will be explained below.

Moreover:
"With white cotton thread
The wise one wraps them according to the rite."
According to the intent of this, they are to be wrapped with a thread over which hūṃ has been recited seven times, with the mantra oṃ vajra śikhare ruṭa maṭa dīśāṃ bandhā bandhā hūṃ.

Now the inner contents are shown. Sapphire, ruby, moonstone crystal, karketana, and kekeru gems; gold, silver, copper, iron, and magnetite, that is, the metals; and likewise pearl, coral, mumen, śūlamaṇi, and the six-spotted stone: these are of middling quality. Crystal, carnelian, zi stone, lapis, and blue-green gems are the lowest. If these are lacking, fragrant flowers of five colors are used.

Further, the medicines are brahmadaṇḍī, two parts; viṣṇukrānta, one part; rudrajaṭā, three parts; īśārī, four parts; devaṭālī, five parts; and so on. Further, the grains are sesame, barley, māṣa beans, millet, wheat, and so on. Further, the twigs are of the sour tree, keśara, aśoka, the bodhi tree, udumbara, and other trees. Further, the cloths are blue, black, red, white, and yellow.

For the victory vase: at the hub of a multicolored lotus, upon the discs of the moon, the sun, Rāhu, and the fire of time, which are white, red, black, and yellow, is a blue three-pointed vajra, the emblem of the Blessed One.

On the eastern vase draw a black sword; on the southern, a red jewel; on the western, a yellow wheel; on the northern, a white lotus; on that of the fire quarter, a black sword; on that of Nairṛti, a red lotus; on that of Śaṅkara, a white utpala; and on that of the wind quarter, a yellow wheel. On the western Jaya vase a curved knife should be drawn. On top of the Vijaya vase, place the conch of the Great Vijaya.

As for what goes into them: into the Great Vijaya, a green jewel. Into the Jaya and the Vijaya, blue. Into those of the east and the fire quarter, black. Into those of the south and Nairṛti, red. Into those of the north and Īśāna, white. Into those of the west and the wind quarter, yellow. The lodestone, steel, copper, silver and gold are distributed in the same way. Likewise, one portion of the medicines goes to the Vijaya. Likewise the second, third, fourth and fifth portions go, in their order, into the vases of the east and the fire quarter, the south and Nairṛti, the north and Īśāna, and the west and the wind quarter.

All the bundles are put into the Great Vijaya, and that is set on top of the Vijaya, the principal vase, which performs all the activities. Into it pour fragrant water together with the five nectars taught earlier, and adorn it with garlands of flowers. Recite oṃ vajra amṛta udaka ṭha hūṃ and the six-syllable mantra seven times each, and so turn it into nectar. Within it, with oṃ tāpte tāpte mahā tāpte svāhā, invite the deities in the nature of water. Visualize the wheel of deities together with their seats, and with the light rays of the seed syllable at your own heart invite the wisdom beings. After first offering water for the feet, water for sipping, welcoming water and so on, cause them to enter that very samaya wheel. Visualize that within it, through great passion, they melt and become of one taste in the form of bodhicitta. Then, through the emblems, regard the samaya deities and the wisdom deities as having become of one taste.

Then, holding a vajra hung with a garland of flowers, touch the Vijaya vase and the others, and recite the heart mantra of the lord of the wheel one hundred and eight times, together with the mantra oṃ haṃ hāṃ hiṃ hīṃ hī hīṃ huṃ hūṃ hliṃ hlīṃ vajra padma amṛta ghaṭa su bi śu ddha dharmā dhātu sva bhā ba sarva dharmā na su bi śuddha dharmā dhātu sva bhā ba sarva dharmā na su be śuddha dharmā dhātu sva bhā vāṃ kuru kuru svāhā, and place the Vijaya vase in the center of the maṇḍala. For the other vases, recite each deity's own mantra twenty-one times and this mantra twenty-one times, and place each in its own position.

Then, having duly worshiped the Vijaya and the other vases with flowers and so on, say, "Guard against the obstructors until the work of the maṇḍala is complete!" Give them torma and touch them. Having first praised them with the verse beginning "Bestower of the supreme, supreme vajra, to you I bow," ring the bell. The mantras for ringing the bell are these: oṃ hraṃ hīṃ hraṃ hṝṃ hrlaṃ hrāḥ oṃ vajratvāya svāhā oṃ amoghasiddhaya svāhā oṃ ratnasaṃbhavāya svāhā oṃ amitābhāya svāhā oṃ vairocanāya svāhā oṃ tāre tuttāre ture svāhā oṃ pāṇḍaravāsini vārde svāhā oṃ mamakī kiri kiri svāhā oṃ locana basude svāhā.

Then take thread spun by a maiden and bought at whatever price the hero names, twist it with five fine colors, and bless it with oṃ āṃ ī ṝ ūṃ ḹṃ vajra sūtra sarva dharmā eka ā kāra sva bhā vena kuru kuru svāhā. Worship it with scent and the rest, and request it to remain on the eastern side of the base.

Then take the colors, made from pearls and the like or from śāli rice and the like, and well dyed into pigments. Bless them with oṃ aṃ i ṛ uṃ liṃ su bi śuddha pañca skandha sva bha va pñac skandha na su bi śuddha dharma na kuru kuru svāhā, give them scent and the rest, and place the containers of color on the western side.

Then a vajra possessing the proper characteristics is placed on the southern side, together with oṃ hūṃ krodha vajra sarva satva kāruṇa ātmakā sarva dharmma eka sukha sva bhā va sarva dharmmā na vajra su kha sva bhā va na kuru kuru svāhā.

Then, having strewn fragrant flowers over the base of the maṇḍala, make a circle with fragrant water together with the five nectars at each deity's place. Upon that, with the light rays of the seed syllable dwelling at your own heart, invite the host of deities together with the lord of the wheel, and seat them in order on their seats. Worship them duly with water for the feet and the rest, praise them, and have them taste the nectar. Then, setting the right knee on the ground, generate from a hūṃ on your tongue a one-pronged vajra marked with hūṃ. In order to care for disciples

Having understood the meaning of this, make the supplication three times. Again make offerings with flowers and so on. This is the rite for consecrating the deities.

Then, beginning with meditation on the protection wheel, one should focus on the yoga of the Blessed One. One again requests the deities to depart, recites the sixteen-line mantra seven times, and should then lie down according to the procedure that will be explained. This continues until one sees an auspicious sign. If one sees an inauspicious one, one should give a torma according to the procedure that will be stated. So it is said.

Then, because it is said:

The disciple is one whom one has already empowered, who has the required qualifications, who keeps the observances that will be described, and who is free of the fourteen root downfalls. He is preceded by the vows that will be described, or else by the vows of a master. Seated at the eastern door, he has become one to be trained, and one should perform the preparatory consecration on him with the consecration rite. This may be done with four, or with one, or else with the action vajra as the fifth. If these are lacking, one does it oneself. For the sake of the preparatory consecration, one properly worships the deities of the maṇḍala, who are present. For the sake of the wisdom lines and the rest, one mentally focuses on the space above the maṇḍala ground and sees the signs. On the two eyes one places a yellow moon and sun arising from the syllable ja. One recites oṃ dīpta dṛṣṭāṃ kuśi jaḥ, rolls the two eyes, and summons with the eyelashes. That is, with a blazing gaze like a hook, one emanates Vairocana and the others, summons them again and again, and makes them enter the respective colors of the thread held in the hand. In this way these threads come to have the nature of the five wisdoms, mirror-like wisdom and the rest.

Then the disciples are visualized in the form of Vairocana or of Vajravega. "Buddhas" means the aggregates of form, feeling, perception, formations and consciousness. "Bodhisattvas" means the elements of the faculties of eye, ear, nose, tongue, body and mind. By the word "also," the elements of earth and the rest, and the elements of the objects, form and the rest, are to be worshipped: they are to be fully purified by the unchanging great bliss of the non-emitted drops of bodhicitta. This is the definitive meaning. Externally, again, they are to be worshipped, for the sake of external purification, with the experiences that come from the emission of the drop. Internally, for the purification of the mahāmudrā, worship is made with one's own substance, the drops that have entered the jewel. This is the definitive sense of the worship with the drop.

On the passage "seeing that" and so on, one should declare liberation from all wrongdoing. Here seeing is of two kinds, external and internal in nature, and likewise touching. Here external seeing should be understood as seeing the action seal (karmamudrā) and the wisdom seal (jñānamudrā). Touching is touching their mouth, breasts and womb; this is what "from both of these, seeing and touching" refers to. Internally, it is seeing the whole of the three realms as images of the Buddha, seeing the mahāmudrā, and touching their great bliss; this is what "from both of these, seeing and touching" means.

Likewise, on "And also by hearing and remembering": here this means by hearing the sublime Dharma, and by remembering the guru, the buddhas, the bodhisattvas, the heroes, and the sovereign ladies of the heroines. By that hearing and remembering also "one will be freed from all evils." That is, one will be freed from all of these: the ten non-virtues done by body, speech and mind, the five acts of immediate retribution, and the rest.

"Abiding in the vow of not committing evils" means abiding, from the time of the permission onward, in the vow of not committing evils. Then one will also be fully freed from things one has done that were not permitted, such as certain acts of desire.

"Thus, without any doubt": in the sequence taught in this way, the yogin who has given up the fourteen root downfalls unites bhaga and liṅga. By meditating on the great bliss in which the awakening mind does not slip away, he generates merit, and so this is the rite of liberation from evil.

Now, with the words beginning "yogin-hood," the method by which buddhahood is accomplished will be stated. Here yogin-hood is of two kinds, mundane and supramundane. Of these, the supramundane yogin is buddhahood itself. Its distinction is "supreme merit," because the accumulation of merit is completely perfected. Likewise, "the purifier, the destroyer of evil," because that same one has completely perfected the accumulation of wisdom. That, as buddhahood, "is accomplished also by mantra recitation, meditative concentration, and bliss." Here, by this method yogins accomplish the yogin-hood that has been described, and the words "will be accomplished" are a scriptural declaration.

The method for this is the six-limbed yoga. Here the limbs are taught in sequence, with one limb left out between each. What is not stated for an earlier limb should be understood by implication from what is stated for a later limb. Just as here, when "day-maker" is said, the other, "night-maker," is apprehended by implication alone. Likewise, things that depend on one another are apprehended by the implication of what is stated, even if they themselves are not stated. Therefore, since in this tantra each limb is shown with the other three included within it, the statement of the intermediate limbs is to be understood first, and here it is known from the guru's oral instructions. In the east, the Jaya vase as the sixth. On top of it, the conch of Mahāvijaya. This is the system of six vases. According to the system of ten vases, eight are set in the eight cardinal and intermediate directions. Outside the western vase, two finger-widths away so that it does not touch, is the Vijaya vase. Outside the eastern vase, two finger-widths away so that it does not touch, is the Jaya vase. On top of it is the conch of Mahāvijaya as the eleventh.

Further, for pacifying, increasing, and all rites, a conch is used. For all fierce rites, a skull. For subjugating and summoning, copper and gold. For immobilizing and stupefying, an earthenware bowl. Then one should wind cloth and thread matching the rite around the necks. This placing is the rite of setting out the vases.

Then, at the four intermediate directions, four texts, the Perfection of Wisdom and the others, should be read aloud. Various incenses such as camphor, frankincense (turuṣka) and aloeswood are set out in the eight directions and intermediate directions. Over the various cloths, victory banners, pennants and so forth, one recites the mantra of the principal deity and the mantra oṃ vajra sparṇa khaṃ, and lays them out in their places in due order. At the doors of the maṇḍala house, the gateways are adorned with various cloths, bells and yak-tail whisks. Plantain-tree pillars with their leaves, and full vases with mouth ornaments, are also set up.

Then the master, seated in the east, performs the confession of misdeeds and the other rites taught in the sādhana, together with the protection wheel, the Supreme King of Maṇḍalas, the Supreme King of Actions, the yoga of the drop, and the subtle yoga. Then, as is proclaimed:

A consort who has reached sixteen years of age,
exceedingly and supremely beautiful,
endowed with fragrance and with flowers,
and well trained in that very reality:
in her center one should practice desire.
The ocean of qualities, Viśvamātā,
emanating the hosts of buddhas,
is well adorned in the element of space.

Accordingly, one enters into union with an outer mudrā, or, if that is not the case, enters into union with the wisdom mudrā. With the mantra jaḥ hūṃ vaṃ hoḥ one performs the summoning, the absorbing, the binding and the bringing under control of the wheel of wisdom deities invited by the sound of union. Then, preceded by water for the feet, offering water and so forth, one causes them to enter oneself. Melted by great passion, they emerge by way of the vajra path, and one should emanate the wheel of wisdom deities into the lotus of the goddess. Visualizing the colored-powder maṇḍala, one should emanate them according to the order of their places.

Then, emanating the offering goddesses from the seed syllable at one's heart, one should make offering with the outer and secret offerings. The sequence of that offering is as follows. One makes the supplication oṃ āḥ hūṃ ho traidhātukeśvara kālacakra arghaṃ pratīccha saparivārā tvāṃ bhagavāna me varado bhava śiṣyañca. Then the maṇḍalas for the offering water are made outside the colored-powder maṇḍala, and the other maṇḍalas in front of the victory vase. Reciting the mantras with oṃ at the beginning and namaḥ at the end, one makes offering with flowers and so forth, thus:

buddhāya dharmmāya saṃghāya vajrasatvāya prajñāpāramitāyai svabhāvikakāyāya dharmmakāyāya saṃbhogakāyāya nirmāṇakāyāya jñānavajrāya cittavajrāya vākvajrāya kāyavajrāya caturavimokṣebhyo caturbrahmavihārebhyo saptatriṃśadbhyo dhipakṣikadharmmebhyo catursītisāhapradharmaskandhebhyo sarvadharmmādeśakabhyo ratnatrayāya śākyamunaye kālacakrāy dānapāramitāyai śīlapāramitāyai kṣāntipāramitāyai vīryāpāramitāyai dhyanapāramitāyai prajñāpārmitāyai upāyapāramitāyai praṇidhanapāramitāyai balapāramitāyai jñānparamitāyai jayaghaṭebhyo vijayaghaṭebhyo sarvacitnobhyo sarvamudrabhyo cintamaṇiyai dharmmaghaṇaṭyai dharmaśaṃkhāya kavavṛkṣāya akṣobhyāya amoghasiddhāyai ratnasaṃbhavāya amitābhāya vairocanāyai locanyai māmakyai paṇḍarāyai tārāyai vajradhātvīśvarye vajrapāṇayai khagarbhāya kṣitigarbhāya lokeśvarāya sarvanivaraṇibhaskambhini samantabhadrāya gandhavajrāyai rūpavajrāyai rāsavajrāyai sparśavajrāyai śaptavajrāyai dharmmmādhātuvajrāye uṣṇīṣāya vighanāntakāya prajñāntakāya padmanatakāya yamāntakāya staṃbhiṇye māniṇye staṃbhiṇyai ativīdyāyai atinīlayai sarvadhariṇībhyo ṣaṭvāṃgāya

This is the rite of offering to the maṇḍala of mind.

vajracarcikāyai vajravārahyai vajramaheśvaryai vajravārahyai vajramaheśvaryai vajraindyai vajrabrahmaṇyai vajramahālakyai vajrakaumāryai vajraviṣṇavydai aṣṭa aṣṭakena catuḥ ṣaṣṭhi vajrayoginibho ṣaḍtriṃśad īcchabhyo

This is the rite of offering to the maṇḍala of speech.

vajraviṣṇavai vajranairityāya vajraagnāye vajreddhaye vajrendrāya vajreśvarāya vajrabrahmaṇe vajravināyakāya vajrakīrtikeyāye vajravāyavai vajrayamāya vajrayakṣebhyo varuṇyai vāyavyai yāmiṇyai yākṣiṇyai mahābalāya acalāya ṭakkirājāya nīladaṇḍāya sumbharājāya rodākṣiye vajraśrīkhalāyai cundāyai bhṛkuṭyai māricyai pratyekamābhedena ṣṣṭi uttaratraṃśata vajratithidevībhya ṣḍatriṃśadpratīcchabhyo vajrajayāya vajrakarkoṭakāya vajravāsukaye vajraanantāya vajratakṣakāya vajramahāpadmāya vajrakulikāya vajraśikhapālāya vajrapadmāya vajravijayāya

This is the offering to the maṇḍala of body.

śvānāsyāyai sukārsyāyai vyāghrāsyāyai jaṃbukāsyāyai garuḍāsyayai ulūkāsyāyai gṛbhrasyāyai kākāsyayai sarvabhūtebhyo

This is the offering to the eight charnel grounds.

vajracndrāya vajrasāryāya vajramaṃgalāya vajrabuddhaya vajrabṛhaspataye vajraśukrāya vajrasanaiścarāya vajraketava vajrarāhve vajrakālaagnaye vajradhruve vajragastyaya sarvanakṣatrebhyo dvādaśarāsibhyo ṣoḍaśakālābhyo daśadikpālebhyo nandikeśvarāya vajramahākālāya vajraghaṇḍakarṇāya vajrabhriṃgini sarvakṣetrapālebhyo sarvadūtibhyo hārītyai sarvasiddhibhyo dharmmacakrāya bhadragaḍāya vajradundubhi bodhivṛkṣāya gurubuddhabodhisatvabhyo namo namaḥ

So it is said. Having made offering in this way, one should ring the bell and offer praise. One's own awakening mind, together with the flower of the wisdom consort, is poured into the lotus vessel, made into nectar, and tasted. Making the wheel into the nature of great bliss, one recites the mantra of the principal deity one hundred and eight times. One recites the mantras of the retinue twenty-one times each, and the hundred-syllable mantra.

The giving of torma to all the bhūtas is done by the rite that will be explained below: having had the practitioner's assistants, those making offerings who are stationed in the directions, guard the maṇḍala, one goes outside and gives torma to the wrathful deities and the others.

Then, in order to pacify faults in the rite such as excesses and omissions, a detailed and extensive fire offering is performed by a disciple, following the procedure that will be explained below. If there is no such disciple, one performs it oneself. Endowed with the samādhi of Vajravega, reciting the flower-garland mantra and the sixteen-line verse, brandishing the vajra and ringing the bell, one should circumambulate three times together with the master of the eastern gate. Facing the maṇḍala, one offers the flower garland to the principal deity and imagines that the four gates are opened by the mantra oṃ vajra udghaṭaya samaya praveśaya hūṃ. Then one says oṃ samaya praveśayami. Dancing once more, one takes back the flower garland and places it on one's own head. Bowing to the wheel, one makes the supplication, "Grant sentient beings the kindness of all the accomplishments!" This is the sequence of the rite of consecrating the maṇḍala.

Then, having made offerings to the maṇḍala, one should accept, outside it, disciples who have been well examined: those free of faults such as pride, who abide in the trainings as laymen and so on, who have gone to the far shore of learning and the like, and who are devoted to the vows of the Great Vehicle. Even those who lack good family and qualities, and those of ugly appearance, are to be accepted, provided they have supreme faith in that very master, are especially devoted to the vow of Vajrasattva, and in the Vajra Vehicle do not turn to another master. As it is said in the Three Hundred and Fifty:

The fourfold assembly, all without exception,
who abide in their respective trainings
and are especially devoted to the Great Vehicle,
though ugly, lacking qualities,
or lowly, are to be taken in by the maṇḍala's
rite and given the preparatory rite.

So it says. The disciples, then, whether one, two, or many, offer a maṇḍala, place both knees on the ground, and hold flowers in their cupped hands. One makes them understand the meaning of these words:

For the sake of happiness in this world,
for the sake of happiness in the world beyond,
and above all for the sake of attaining buddhahood,
the intelligent should enter.

Then one draws them in through one's own mouth, and as they rest in the lotus of the wisdom consort, having passed along the vajra path, one imagines that the tathāgatas together with their consorts, emanated from the seed syllable at one's own heart, enter through the mouth, melt into the awakening mind, and confer empowerment. The disciple, standing at the eastern gate of the maṇḍala, then makes the request for entry:

You who draw beings from the ocean of saṃsāra,
fearsome with birth, old age, and death,
with sea monsters and the like:
great protector, be my teacher!
Since I seek great bliss,
grant me the pledge
that shows the path of great awakening.
Give me also the awakening mind.
The Buddha, the Dharma, and the Saṅgha,
the three refuges, grant to me as well.
Into the supreme city of great liberation,
protector, I pray, lead me in.

Having said this, one takes one disciple as the principal and says:

Come here, child, you who have set out
upon the great path of the Great Vehicle.
The conduct of mantra, the path of the rite,
I shall rightly explain to you.
The buddhas abiding in the three times,
holders of the vajra of body, speech, and mind,
through this very power of mantra
attained incomparable wisdom.
Through the incomparable practice of mantra
Śākyasiṃha and the others
vanquished the hosts of Māra,
utterly unbearable and mighty though they were.
To ferry the beings of the world across,
they turned the wheel and passed into nirvāṇa.
To attain omniscience,
O wise one, do likewise.

One should make the disciples understand the meaning of this. Then:

I go for refuge to the Three Jewels.
I confess each and every misdeed.
I rejoice in the virtue of beings.
I give rise to the mind set on a buddha's awakening.

This is said three times. Then: "All buddhas and bodhisattvas, please attend to me. I, named so-and-so, from this time onward until I attain the heart of awakening,

just as the protectors of the three times
made certain of awakening,
so shall I give rise to the unsurpassed,
holy and supreme awakening mind.
The moral discipline of the training,
the gathering of virtuous dharmas,
and working for the benefit of beings: these three disciplines
I shall firmly uphold.
The Buddha, the Dharma, and the Saṅgha,
the unsurpassed Three Jewels,
which arise from the vow of the Buddha,
I shall uphold from this time on."

And:

"Vajra, bell, mudrā, and guru, these cherished vajras, I shall hold upon the crown of my head.
Here in the Jewel family I shall give gifts; in the Wheel family I shall keep the pledge of the supreme Victor.
In the Sword family I shall make offerings; clearly, in the Lotus family I shall rightly keep the vow.
As the cause of liberation for sentient beings, in the family that gives birth to the Victors, I shall give rise to the awakening mind."

And this too should be recited:

"Those not ferried across I shall ferry across;
those not freed I shall free;
those not yet relieved I shall relieve,
and I shall set beings in nirvāṇa."

Then one protects the disciple with the six syllables, and at his heart, throat, and forehead one should visualize a black hūṃ, a red āḥ, and a white oṃ, resting respectively on Rāhu, the sun, and the moon. Reciting these, one touches his heart, throat, and forehead with a vajra together with urine and flowers, and offers flowers, incense, scent, lamps, and food offerings.

Then one takes a tooth stick of udumbara or similar wood, twelve finger-widths long, free of insects, not crooked, with its bark not stripped and without blemish, washed with fragrant water, with flowers tied to its upper part, and gives it to the disciple, who faces east, with this mantra: oṃ āḥ huṃ ho haṃ kṣaḥ vajradantakāṣṭha caturvimokṣamukha viśuddhi svabhāva kāya vāka citta jñān mukha dantā ādi malaṃ viśodhaya viśodhaya svāhā. On a square maṇḍala four cubits in size, smeared with cow dung and the like, the disciple recites oṃ āḥ hūṃ seven times, rubs his teeth, and throws the stick. Where the tooth stick falls on the maṇḍala, one should examine, by the direction it points, the accomplishment that will come about. If it falls outside the maṇḍala, there is no accomplishment.

Then one has the disciple drink three palmfuls of fragrant water mixed with the five nectars, reciting oṃ hrīḥ viśuddha dharmmā sarva pāpāni cāsya śodhaya sarva vikalapāna apanaya hūṃ. Then, settling into meditative equipoise, one recites that same syllable and the six-syllable mantra over kuśa grass arisen from hūṃ, its upper ends made even. This is for the disciple's mat.

In the same way, for the pillow, one recites the essence mantra and the six-syllable mantra over the grass arisen from dhīḥ. It should be given while saying oṃ vajra tīkṣṇa.

Then one recites the essence mantra and the six-syllable mantra over a red thread of three twisted strands arisen from hūṃ, and ties three knots in it. It is bound on, for the reason stated in the tantras, while saying oṃ buddha maitri rakṣa rakṣa sarvva na svāhā.

Then one sets out the six families, oṃ āḥ hūṃ ho haṃ kṣaḥ, in order at the forehead, the throat, the heart, the navel, the secret place and the crown. With the following mantra one urges Vajrasattva: oṃ a ā aṃ aḥ vajra satvā mahā sukha vajra kāla cakra śiṣya sya abhi mukho bhava sana tuṣṭa bhava varado bhava kāya vāka citta adhiṣṭhānāṃ kuru kuru svāhā.

Then, with the thought that those who dwell in saṃsāra should aim to find the Dharma of the Buddha, and that by obtaining it they will be freed from every suffering, one encourages them against fear:

In the womb, dwelling in the womb, at the time of birth, and in the state of childhood, there is unbearable suffering.
In youth and the prime of life, through desire for women, wealth and good fortune, one is filled with afflictions and great suffering.
Then come the great sufferings of aging and death, and again the six destinies, as donkeys and the like, that bring terror.
All these beings without exception, deluded by illusion, pass from suffering into suffering.
Even if, within saṃsāra, one becomes a human being, a mind turned to the Dharma comes perhaps once in a hundred.
Of those, even if through the power of virtue one comes to love buddhahood, one enters the first vehicle.
Of those, a mind that enters the glorious Vajra Vehicle and meditates on the complete essence arises perhaps once in a hundred.
Of those, to enter buddhahood, the state of the bliss of passion: ah, how very difficult it is!

And:

Whatever evil one has done before
Through countless tens of millions of eons,
By seeing a maṇḍala such as this
All of it will be exhausted.
Those whose minds are utterly stainless
In this supreme conduct
Destroy rebirth in the lower realms,
Where sufferings abide.
You great beings
Have today obtained an incomparable gain.
This teaching of the Victor together with his heirs,
All of you
Should take up completely.
Through being generated as great beings,
You will travel well
In the Great Vehicle.
Therefore this is the supreme path,
The ground from which the Great Vehicle arises,
And those who go upon it
Will become tathāgatas.

Then one protects them with the essence mantra and the six syllables, gives them the essence mantra to recite, and commands them, "Tell me your dreams early in the morning!" They should then lie down in the lion posture outside the curtain or in some other place. This is the sequence of the rite of the disciples' preparatory consecration.

Then the master himself, on the south side of the maṇḍala, should lie down and perform his own preparatory consecration with oṃ ho vajra kamala dala garbhe śa sjavajra sukha mahā nitraṃ karomi yathā tāthāgate na kṛtā haḥ hā svāhā. The master lies with his head to the west and his feet to the east.

If, through the power of meditation, when the dreams are examined, one sees buddhas, bodhisattvas, and members of the host of goddesses holding vīṇās and the like in their hands, one becomes pure. Or else, if one sees vajrayoginīs holding skull cups and curved knives in their hands, and the wrathful kings destroying Māras, obstructors, misleaders and the like, and making a protection wheel, it is good. If one sees nothing at all, it is middling. But if the master or a disciple sees hosts of Māras, hungry, thirsty, emaciated and withered, holding skull cups and curved knives in their hands and wishing to do what is improper to holy persons, it is a bad sign.

If such things are seen, the master gives up his sleeping place, sits on the ground on the east side of the maṇḍala, and meditates on the protection wheel. Moreover, by a rite that will protect them, one should offer a great torma, as follows: oṃ phreṃ vajra ḍākinī vajra dhātvī śvari gagana svabhāva sarva dravyāni viśodhani sarva dravyā viśodhaya hūṃ phreṃ phaṭ. This is the mantra for purifying the torma that pacifies the obstructors.

Then, for inviting the buddhas, with this verse:

Buddhas joined as father and mother,
With their heirs and retinues, together with companions and associates,
Surrounded by the gathered hosts of gods and goddesses:
Delighted, supreme Vajrasattva, please come!

one should cause the tathāgatas and the rest to enter their respective elements.

Then, with the vajra mudrā, the mantra for inviting the vajraḍākinīs is this: oṃ ha hā hi hī vajra bhairava ākarṣaya ākarṣaya praveśaya praveśaya bandhaya bndhaya toṣaya toṣaya jaḥ hūṃ vaṃ hoḥ vajra ḍākinīnāṃ hṛdayaṃ hūṃ hūṃ phreṃ phreṃ phaṭ.

Then, with the vajra mudrā, one offers the vajraḍākinīs torma together with scent, flowers, incense, lamps, welcoming water and unspoiled fruits, by the rite that will be explained, and one imagines that they eat and drink at the site of the outer charnel grounds.

Then, in order to pacify Māras, obstructors and the like, one recites the sixteen-line mantra and the garland mantra. This rite should also be understood as applying at the time of laying out the lines. This is the sequence of the rite for pacifying obstructors.

Then, early in the morning, the vajra master should offer the torma, come out of the maṇḍala house, and ask the disciples about their dreams. If they are inauspicious, the disciples are fully protected with the garland mantra.

Then, at the time of entering the maṇḍala house, in order to pacify Māra and the rest, one first sets up the protection wheel by the rite taught in the sādhana. One then focuses on the yoga of the Blessed One (the Buddha), sets out the six limbs, and sets out the six mudrās and the four mudrās. Then the disciples make their request with this: oṃ praveśa bhagavāna mahāsukha mokṣa suraṃ sarva siddhi sukha pradaṃ parama sukha uttama siddhya jaḥ hūṃ baṃ hoḥ prasiddhya svā.

As for entering: having circumambulated and sat at the western door, one uses the mantra for opening the eastern door of the maṇḍala and so on, namely: oṃ vajra uddhaṭaya samaya prebeśaya hūṃ.

Then the mantra for offering the flower garland to the maṇḍala is this: oṃ parama mahāsukhāśaya sulalīta bilāsamita namite rṇa māmi bhagavāntaṃ jaḥ hūṃ baṃ hoḥ hi hi hi hi hīḥ pratīccha kusuma añjaliṃ nātha hoḥ. Taking it back again, one binds it on one's own crown.

Then, visualizing the principal deity together with the circle of deities as the guru, one who seeks empowerment makes the request with these mantra words: oṃ haṃ hāṃ hiṃ hīṃ hṝṃ hṝṃ huṃ hḷṃ hḹṃ ā ī ṝ ū ḹ vajra ḍakinyo vajrāmṛta ghaṭe abhiṣiñcatu māṃ svāhā oṃ aṃ iṃ ṛ uṃ ḷṃ sarva buddha vajra mukutam mama bañca buddha ātma makaṃ bandhayantu huṃ hūṃ phaṭ oṃ a ā aṃ aḥ h hā haṃ haḥ phreṃ hoḥ sarva pāramitā mama vajra paṭaṃ bandhayantu huṃ hūṃ phaṭ oṃ hūṃ hoḥ vidñāna svabhābai kāruṇā prajñaa ātmake vajra vajra ghaṇṭe sabya uttara karayo rma ma vajra satvāḥ saprajño dadātu hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ oṃ a ā e ī yar a ra o au al āl aṃ āṃ sarva bodhisatvaḥ bhāryāḥ sarvadā sarva kāma upabhogaṃ vajraṃ prataṃ mamā dādāntu svāhā oṃ ha hā ya yā ra rā va vā la lā sarva krodha rājāḥ sabhārya metri kāruṇā madita upekṣā sarva samatva svabhāvaṃ vajra pūrvaṃ gamanā mame dādāntu huṃ hūṃ phaṭ oṃ e padma vajra cihnau prajñau ba au maṇḍal adhipatī vajra sukha jñānāga mama dad tāṃ hi haḥ huṃ hūṃ phaṭ.

Then one should imagine oneself being empowered with the seven empowerments. Then, generating the pride of having obtained the seven empowerments, one says: oṃ sarva tāthāgata sapta abhiṣaka sapta bhūmu prvapto haṃḥ. This is the rite of conferring the seven empowerments.

Then again, the mantra practitioner uses these words to seek the higher empowerments, the vase empowerment and the rest: oṃ oṃ prajñā upayau kalaśa abhiṣekaṃ med dadātāṃ huṃ hūṃ phaṭ oṃ āḥ prajñā upāyo guhya abhiṣekaṃ med dadātāṃ huṃ hū phaṭ oṃ hūṃ pradñā upāyau vajra satva biśva mā taro prajñā jñāna abhiśekaṃ me daṅ dātvaṃ hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ. With this one makes the request.

Then one imagines oneself being empowered by the rite that will be explained. Having imagined this, one says: oṃ oṃ vajra satvena vajra kalaśa abhiṣekena abhiṣikto vajra batva calā bhūmi lam dho haṃ oṃ āḥ vajra tvena guhya abhiṣekena abhiśikto vajra satva yuvarājo nava bhūmi lab dho haṃ oṃ bhūṃ vajra tena prajñā jñānina abhiśekena abhiśikto dvitīya vajra dharo haṃ dharmmā megha bhūmi lab dho haṃ. This is the rite of taking on the pride of the higher empowerments.

Then, the mantra words for seeking the instruction of the fourth empowerment, which is higher even than the higher empowerments, are these: oṃ ho prajñā upāya ātmakaṃ vajra satva mahāmudrā jñaana abhiśekaṃ mi prayaccha oṃ āḥ hūṃ ho phaṭ. Having made the request with this, one imagines oneself receiving the stainless empowerment. Then one takes on the pride of Vajrasattva: oṃ ho dharmmā dhāya kṣara caturtha abhiśekena abhiśikto vajra satvena ātmanā hāṃ. By the second, the twelfth ground is attained: Vajrasattva, of great purpose, supreme and unchanging, fully victorious over the three worlds, the Blessed One Kālacakra, called the syllables evaṃ. This is the rite that is higher even than the higher empowerments.

Then, having received permission for all the activities of a master, one should confer it upon the disciples.

Preceded by the praise of suchness, whose very self is great bliss and whose nature is wisdom and method, the one who desires the accomplishment of the maṇḍala recites the verses that effect the blessing of truth, as follows:

Like that which arises from space,
Supreme, without beginning or end,
By the very nature of Vajrasattva,
Grant the most excellent of all accomplishments.
The accomplishment of the Akṣobhya Vajra:
Great Lord, surpassing god,
Victor who holds all vajras,
Grant me the supreme accomplishment.
Permanence itself, free of all faults,
Passionately attached through great passion,
Great passion, great joy,
Grant me that very accomplishment.
Supreme of utterly pure Dharma,
Tathāgata liberated from the very first,
Samantabhadra, the self of all,
Grant the accomplishment of the awakening mind.
Great Lord, great mudrā,
Great supreme accomplishment of the vajra,
The most excellent of all accomplishments:
May the father of Viśvamātā grant it.
Pervading the minds of all sentient beings,
Dwelling in the hearts of all sentient beings,
Father of all sentient beings,
Summit of desire, great pledge,
Supreme wisdom of truth:
By the truth of the maṇḍala whose self is wisdom and method,
O Protector, I pray you,
Grant me all that I desire.

This is the stage of the rite of the master's self-entry.

Then the disciples who have performed the preliminary observance bathe and are purified with fragrances and the like, and then take the vows, which are these:

"Killing, falsehood, and the wives of others are to be given up, and likewise others' wealth and the drinking of liquor:
these five evils are vajra nooses in saṃsāra that destroy one's own virtue.
By this command of the Universal Lord, bestowed in the name of that teacher of gods and humans who has appeared at whatever time,
which destroys the terrors of existence, you too must keep them.
Gambling, blameworthy food, reading evil words, and the practices of bhūtas and asuras;
cows, children, women and men, and the teachers of gods and humans: these five are not to be killed.
There is to be no resentment toward friends, lords, the teacher of gods and humans, the Saṅgha, and those who place their trust in you,
and no clinging to the senses. These are the twenty-five vows of the Lord of Existence."

Then, outside the curtain, the disciples focus on the principal deity of the maṇḍala and tie on the lower garment with oṃ sarba tathāgata anuttar bodhi alaṃkara basta pūja megha samudra sparaṇa samaye hūṃ. They put on the upper garment with oṃ bajra rakṣa haṃ, and the headband with oṃ bajra uṣṇīṣa hūṃ phaṭ. The blindfold is tied on with oṃ dvādaśa aṃgika nirodhariṇe hūṃ phaṭ. With ā khaṃ bīra hūṃ they are made to hold a flower garland.

Then the disciple offers the fee for entering the maṇḍala and is sprinkled with water from the victory conch. The body is made stainless by the light rays of the three syllables standing on seats at the heart, the throat, and the forehead. After saying hūṃ, the disciple stands at the eastern door of the maṇḍala and is held by the action vajra master, and is questioned about entering the maṇḍala. When the master asks, "Kye ho! Dear child, who are you?", the disciple is made to give the reply, "I am the fortunate one of great bliss."

With oṃ sarba yogcitta utpādayāmi the disciple generates the awakening mind. A vajra together with flowers is placed at the heart, and while oṃ surate samaya stvaṃ ho siṅṅhi bajra yathā sukhaṃ is recited, the disciple is made to hold the vajra. Then the master says: "Today, since you are blessed by all the Tathāgatas, you must not speak of this great maṇḍala, the supreme secret of all the Tathāgatas, to those who have not seen the maṇḍala." Having said this, the master should draw the disciple in with oṃ bighanāntakṛta hūṃ.

Then again, with ā khaṃ bīra hūṃ, the disciple is brought inside the curtain, and while oṃ mahārata sutoṣya susukho bajrasatva ārya siddhya maṃ is recited, the circumambulation is performed.

Then, with palms joined, the disciple speaks at the eastern door: oṃ sarba tathāgata pūjā upasthānaya ātmanaṃ nīryātayāmi sarba tathāgata bajra stva adhitiṣṭha māṃ oṃ sarba tathāgata pūjā karmaṇe ātmanaṃ niryātayāmi sarba tthāgata bajra karma kuru maṃ. Then at the south: oṃ sarba tathāgata pūja abhiṣekaya ātmanaṃ niryātayāmi sarba tathāgata bajra rtnābhiṣiñca maṃ. Then at the west: oṃ sarba buddha pūja upasthānāya ātmanaṃ niryātaya maṃ sarba tathāgata bajra bairocana adhitiṣṭha maṃ. Then at the north: oṃ sarba tathāgata pūja pravartanāya ātmanaṃ niryātayāmi sarba tathāgata bajra dharmma pravartayamaṃ. Then, coming again to the east: oṃ guru caraṇa upasthānāya ātmanaṇ niryātayāmi oṃ sarba satva paritrāṇāya ātmanaṃ niryātayāmī. The master explains the meaning of these and has the disciple recite them.

Then again the master says: "Today, since you have entered the family of all the Tathāgatas, vajra wisdom is born. If by that wisdom one obtains even the accomplishments of all the Tathāgatas, what need is there to mention other accomplishments? If you transgress your sacred commitment, it will bring great suffering. Therefore you must not speak of this in front of those who have not seen the maṇḍala." Having said this, the master places the vajra on the disciple's head and, ringing the bell, says:

"This is the vajra of sacred commitment.
If you speak of it, it will split your head."

Having made this understood, the master holds the vajra at the disciple's heart and gives the command:

"Oṃ, Kālacakra
dwells truly in your very heart.
If you speak of this to others,
he will recall it at that very moment and depart."

Then the master takes up the victory conch with thumb and ring finger, and gives the water into the disciple's mouth, saying:

"This is your water of hell.
If you transgress the sacred commitment, it will burn you;
if you guard the sacred commitment, you will gain accomplishment.
Drink this water, the vajra nectar!"

Saying this, the master pours in the water of the oath, and says: "From this day forward, since I am your Kālacakra, you must do whatever I command. Therefore be conscientious. Do not act contrary to it and so fall into hell." This is the rite of the water of sacred commitment.

Then, having first performed the practice of accomplishment so that accomplishment is assured, one should make the deity descend upon a disciple who has the right characteristics. This is done as follows. In a place free of bustle, abiding in the yoga of Kālacakra, one pleases the deity with mantra recitation and fire offering. One who has the power to make the deity descend places the disciple in front, and after dissolving him into emptiness in an instant, generates him from the syllable a as Amitābha, and recites oṃ sarba tathāgataśca adhitṛṣṭhana taṃ kāla cagro me āveśatu.

At his navel, from laṃ, arises an earth maṇḍala, square and yellow, marked with a wheel, and upon the fire of time stands a yellow ho. At his heart, from yaṃ, arises a wind maṇḍala, black and shaped like a bow, marked with two fluttering banners, and upon Rāhu stands a black hūṃ. At his throat, from the syllable raṃ, arises a fire maṇḍala, triangular and red, marked with a jewel, and upon the sun stands a red āḥ. At his forehead, from the syllable vaṃ, arises a water maṇḍala, round and white, marked with a vase, and upon the moon stands a white oṃ. One visualizes all these, and then, with light rays from the seed syllable at one's own heart, one invites the vajras of wisdom, mind, speech, and body, and should meditate that they dissolve, in order, into ho huṃ āḥ oṃ.

Then, beneath the feet, one should meditate as follows. From yaṃ arises a wind maṇḍala, which kindles a fire maṇḍala arising from raṃ. It is marked with raṃ, red and triangular, and above it is the syllable djhaiḥ. Their light rays are set in motion, and this in turn sets in motion the light rays of ho and the others, until they completely fill the whole body. Fumigating with incense mixed with the five nectars and ringing the bell, one should cause the descent with oṃ a ra ra ra ra la la la la vajra āveśaya hūṃ. The fruit of this is to be learned from the tantra.

Then, if a flower is placed on the head with the mantra oṃ āḥ huṃ ho, what has descended will depart. Further, at the head, the heart and the crown, oṃ hūṃ haṃ belong to method. At the navel, the throat and the secret place, hoḥ āḥ kṣaḥ belong to the wisdoms. This is the sequence of the rite of descent.

Then one has the disciple look at the maṇḍala and asks what appears before his eyes. From the differences of color one should understand the differences of activity.

Then, with the right hand, one takes hold of the disciple's two thumbs together with the vajra, performs the vajra dance, recites the root mantra or the six-syllable mantra, and circumambulates three times. The blessing of truth is then made:

"Just as, into this sublime maṇḍala,
I have brought the disciple in,
by whatever merit that may hold, act on his behalf.
Through the gods, in the order of the families,
accomplishment arises from this as is fitting.
Wherever it falls, that is his family.
By whatever power of merit he has,
so it arises from this maṇḍala."

This is the sequence of the rite of entering the maṇḍala wearing the blindfold.

Then one draws a single flower from the earlier garland of flowers and, in order to purify the disciple's cupped hands, places that flower in them. One imagines that the disciple's evil deeds are purified as his own syllables are set ablaze. The master, too, blesses it in this way.

Then, with faith and devotion going before, the disciple focuses on the maṇḍala present before him, thinks of entering that maṇḍala, and says oṃ sarva tathāgata kula viśodhani svāhā. He casts the flower onto the emblems of the five Tathāgatas placed on the victory vase, or onto the painted scroll, and at the same moment the blindfold should be removed. If the flower is cast first and the blindfold removed afterward, this mantra is recited: oṃ de bya indrayāni uṅghaṭaya svāhā, and with it the eyes should be opened.

Whichever deity the flower falls upon is the family deity. The accomplishment of the action seal of that very family deity will be attained. One takes up that same flower, ties it to a garland, and with oṃ pratigṛhṇa tvamimaṃ satva bala fastens it on the disciple's head.

Then one says, "O vajra, look!" Beginning with the names of the deities, one points out the principal deity of the maṇḍala and the others, and says:

"Look upon this sublime maṇḍala!
Now give rise to intense faith.
You are entering the buddha family.
Blessed by mantra and by mudrā,
you will attain perfect accomplishment
and the supreme sacred commitment.
Delight in the supreme vajra and lotus,
and be diligent in the mantras."

Having made him understand in this way, one says oṃ vajra maṇḍal mahā maṇḍal ma praviṣṭo haṃ yoga maṇḍalla mahā maṇḍal mā dariṣto haṃ guhya maṇḍalla mahā maṇḍal dkṣito haṃ, and pronounces the sacred commitment: hoḥ hoḥ. This is the sequence of the rite of casting the flower, and of showing the maṇḍala to the disciple and bringing him in.

Then, for the sake of conferring the water empowerment and the others, the disciple kneels before the guru with palms joined and makes supplication, saying:

"Just as Bodhivajra conferred
the supreme and sublime empowerment on the buddhas,
so for me too, for the sake of protection,
grant today Space Vajra and the rest."

Then, in the order in which the master will speak, the disciple should be stationed at the eastern gate. Then the master, moving him by the left hand and holding the crossed vajra, leads him in circumambulation to the northern gate and seats him there on a seat. With light rays from the seed syllable in his heart he invites the Tathāgatas and the knowledge goddesses dwelling in all directions, makes offerings to them, and, in order to empower the disciple, supplicates:

"Just as Vajradhara empowered the buddhas,
so, in order to liberate beings,
O source of all good qualities,
grant this to him likewise, I pray."

Having supplicated thus, the Tathāgatas together with their wisdom consorts melt through great passion. The melted substance enters by the path of Vairocana and issues forth by the vajra path, and it in turn enters the disciple's mouth. Thereby, dwelling in the goddess's lotus, in an instant the form with vajra body together with wisdom, inseparable from the wisdom being, is empowered. Further, as he emerges from the lotus in a guise endowed with faces, arms and the rest, Locanā and the other goddesses, abiding so as to fill the whole element of space, send down a rain of parasols, victory banners, flags, garments, music, dance, song, saffron, camphor, flowers and the like, and holding in their hands white vases filled with the mind of awakening, they empower that disciple as he emerges from the lotus.

The goddesses Rūpavajrā and the rest recite:

"That auspiciousness which dwells in the hearts of all beings,
the supreme omniscient one, the very nature of all beings without exception:
just as great bliss is brought forth by the one who brings it forth,
so by this supreme empowerment, bestow auspiciousness here.

Glorious one, like the hare-marked moon,
protector of the three worlds, free of the three stains,
the Buddha's eyes, like a lotus born from a lake:
bestow that auspiciousness of his which brings peace.

The most supreme, the unwavering, which he taught,
object of worship for humans and gods, renowned throughout the three worlds,
being the supreme Dharma, bring all beings to peace:
bestow that auspiciousness of it which brings peace.

Endowed with the holy Dharma and with the auspiciousness of learning,
the Saṅgha, which gives the fruit of offerings to humans, gods and asuras,
which is the supreme of assemblies where glory abides:
bestow that auspiciousness of it which brings peace."

With this song of auspiciousness one attends the disciple, and so do oneself and the others as well.

Then, to purify the body, water is drawn from all the vases with the mantras of Tārā and the others and poured into the victory conch cup. At the crown of the head, at the right shoulder joint and the left, and likewise at the hip joints, one recites:

"The empowerment is the great vajra,
venerated by all the three realms,
the source from which the four secrets arise:
may all the buddhas bestow it!"

and oṃ ā ī ṝ ū ḹ pañca dhātu viśodhani svāhā. Empowering with the water of the victory conch cup in this way is done by generating the five elements as the five goddesses.

Then, for the crown empowerment, one empowers likewise in the five places with this mantra, generating the five aggregates as the five tathāgatas: oṃ a ī ṛ u ḷ pañca tathāgata pariśuddha svāhā. Then, reciting this mantra over a crown made of jewels, of gold, or of cloth, one should tie it on the head. Thus, by the two empowerments, the elements and the aggregates are purified, and this is the complete purification of the body. This is the rite of the water and crown empowerments.

Then one circles to the right and leads the disciple to the southern gate. To purify speech, the disciple, who supplicates as before, and who is inseparable from the wisdom being in the form of Speech Vajra, is empowered: one recites "The empowerment is the great vajra" and the rest, and with the mantra oṃ a ā aṃ aḥ ha hā haṃ haḥ hoḥ dśa parimita paripūraṇi svāhā one empowers in the five places, generating the ten winds as the ten goddesses. Then a pendant ribbon of jewels or gold, or a garland of flowers, should be tied on the forehead. Then, after the disciple has supplicated in the same way, one generates the two channels as father and mother, places the vajra and bell on the head with the mantra oṃ huṃ ho sūrya candra viśodhani svāhā, and empowers in the five places. By these the winds and channels are purified, and speech is completely purified. This is the sequence of the rite of the ribbon and vajra-and-bell empowerments.

Then, circling to the right, at the eastern gate one gives a vajra ring, and at the eastern face, in order to purify the mind, the disciple supplicates as before. To the one inseparable from the wisdom being in the form of Mind Vajra, one makes known the Blessed One, the Primordial Person, with these words:

"The being without beginning or end,
Vajrasattva, the great bliss,
Samantabhadra, the nature of all,
supreme lord of the consort Viśvamātā."

Then with the mantra o a ā e e ar ār o au al āl aṃ aḥ viṣya indriy viśodhani svāhā one gives the vajra whose nature is suchness itself, and then empowers in the five places, generating the sense faculties and their objects as the male and female bodhisattvas.

Then flowers are given at the ears and elsewhere. Then the name empowerment: after the supplication, one generates the faculties of action and their objects as the wrathful gods and goddesses, and empowers in the five places with the mantra oṃ ha hā ya yā ra rā va vā la lā catur brahma vihāra viśuddha svāhā. Then bracelets or garlands of flowers are tied on the hands and feet, and one says:

"Having drawn you out of the lower realms of existence,
in order to bring existence to supreme peace,
Vajrasattva, Tathāgata,
I give you this prophecy."

sarva tathāgata siddhya samaya śvaṃ bhurbhūva svaḥ. Having recited this, one prophesies by the name of one's own family, according to where the flower falls. The names para ārya, anusma, surata, samarasa, vijaya, alala, satva, mahāsukha, anāṃga, jñāna, and sahāja vajra are the variant names of Vajrasattva; it should be understood that "vajra" comes at the end of all of them.

Those of Akṣobhya are hāsa, vilasa, lilā, lalita, svayaṃbhu, dveṣa, he, karuṇa, citta, hūṃkara, and akṣobhya vajra.

Those of Amoghasiddhi are amogha, varmāśva, samaya, pāramita, karma, anila, sārśa, and arṣa.

Those of Ratnasambhava are ratna, cintāmaṇi, kha, gagana, tejo, martaṇaḍa, bhaskara, and māma.

Those of Vairocana are vairocana, oṃkāra, śaśāta, tathāgata, māyā, buddha, rūpa, and kāya vajra.

Those of Amitābha are rāga, avaloki, dharmma, padma, vajra aṃkura, padma aṃkura, surata oṃkura, karuṇa oṃkura, padma ananta, surata annta, amita, and vāka vajra.

Those of Prajñāpāramitā are vigi, velasini, padā, kokana, ananta, padākā, mālā, and vajra udaya.

Those of Vajradhātvīśvarī are vajra hara, sāsā, jvāla, mālā, ākāsā, and vajra ākāsa rati.

Those of Māmakī are ātama, hasta, avalī, karā, vajra māmakī, and dveṣa ratī.

Those of Tārā are garbha, māyā, vaḍava, tārā, vajra nartyeśvarī, samaya vajra, and vajra rati.

Those of Locanā are ḍākinī, gaurī, lāsya, locana, and vajra mohara.

Those of Pāṇḍaravāsinī are rāga, gīta, ahosukha, vaitāli, matī, hasyā, pāṇdari, and vajrarāgati. Each of these is given with "vajra" placed at its beginning. With these comes the ritual sequence of the vajra-conduct empowerment and the name empowerment, which purify the mind.

Then, leading the disciple clockwise around in the same way, at the western face the disciple makes supplication for the purification of gnosis. The master generates him as the gnosis-vajra body, inseparable from the gnosis being, as the father-mother pair of gnosis and consciousness, and recites oṃ haṃ kṣaḥ jñān viśuddha dharmmā cakra prakā svāhā. In place of dharmmā he inserts in turn vajra, khaḍga, ratna, cakra, and padma, reciting it five times and conferring empowerment at five places. This is the purification of gnosis.

Then, while the disciple sits on his seat, the master recites vajra he ru māṃ and places at his feet the wheel of Dharma arisen from bhūṃ. Reciting oṃ vajra bhāṣa raṃ, he places in his right hand the conch arisen from the last vowel. Reciting a, he places in the left the book arisen from the first vowel. Again, reciting that same syllable, he places in the left hand the bell arisen from a, and says:

"All things have the characteristic of space;
space itself has no characteristic.
Being equal to space,
supreme of all, sameness shines clear.
From this day on, by mere thought,
making the unsurpassed conch of Dharma
resound and fill every place,
turn the wheel of Dharma."

And he says:

"In order to benefit all sentient beings,
throughout the entire world without exception,
turn the wheel of the Dharma
in accord with those to be trained.
You whose very nature is wisdom and means,
like a wish-fulfilling jewel,
tireless and free of all objectification,
accomplish from today the aims of beings."

The disciple, for his part, bows and says, "As the Lord commands, so I shall do." This is the ritual sequence of the permission.

Then the master recites oṃ hrāṃ hṝṃ hṝṃ hru hrḹṃ hraḥ svāhā and oṃ āḥ huṃ ho haṃ kṣaḥ, and in this way the garland mantras and the rest, three times each. The giving is done with the words:

"Blessed One, whom I have given,
abide close to this one."

The disciple says:

"Since I have received the Blessed One,
abide close to me."

With these words he receives them. This is the mantra permission.

Then, in a vessel made of gold or silver, eye ointment of butter and honey is taken up with a golden spoon. Imagining the syllable pra in the disciple's eyes, one should anoint them while reciting oṃ vajra netra apahara paṭalaṃ hriḥ, and say:

"Just as the king of physicians
removes the cataracts of the world,
so, child, the Victors remove
the cataract of your ignorance."

This is the ritual sequence of the eye ointment.

Then he takes up the mirror, recites aḥ over it, shows it to the disciple, and says:

"Phenomena are like reflections,
pure, clear, and without stain,
ungraspable and inexpressible,
arising from causes and from karma.
Like the image abiding in a mirror,
Kālacakra, pure, clear, and without stain,
the lord of all the buddhas,
truly abides in your heart, child.
Since you possess the eye of the Guide,
know phenomena in this way,
as without essence and without basis,
and accomplish the aims of beings beyond compare."

This is the ritual sequence of the mirror.

Then, as he gives the bow arisen from the syllable ho with the word ho, he also recites oṃsarva tathāgatānān anurāgāya svāhā. The disciple, as if drawing the arrow, recites oṃ sarva tathāgatānān anurāgayāmi, and imagines mentally that he pierces the obstructors in the cardinal and intermediate directions, above and below. This is the ritual sequence of the bow and arrow.

Then, saying to the disciple, "O Vajra So-and-so," the master gives him the vajra and bell and should teach the precepts purified by supreme compassion:

"In the vajra family, you must kill; in the sword, speak untruthful words;
in the jewel, steal the wealth of others; and in the supreme lotus family, take the women of others.
In the wheel, rely fully on liquor, the lamps, the buddhas, and all the sense objects;
in the curved knife, do not disparage the space-lotus of any woman, the caṇḍālī and the rest.
For the sake of beings, give your wealth together with your body; these you must not hold back.
Sons of good family, otherwise there is no buddhahood even in countless eons: so the Victor has surely declared.
The water purifies as Tārā and the other goddesses; the crown, as the Victors; the silk ribbon, as the śaktis;
the vajra and bell, as sun and moon; the conduct, as the sense objects and the rest; and the name, as Maitreya and the others.
The permission is complete awakening, glorious Kālacakra, destroyer of the fears of existence, all-pervading.
Thus, since the seven empowerments destroy the stains of evil, they are perfectly bestowed within the maṇḍala itself."

Then, in a place made secluded with a cloth curtain and the like, the disciple offers, for the sake of the vase empowerment and the others, a mudrā endowed with beauty and youth and possessing the commitments, between eleven and twenty years of age, or, if there is none such, another. Joining his palms and visualizing the guru as Kālacakra, he supplicates with these words:

"I bow to you, Kālacakra,
who have abandoned all obscurations.
I bow to and praise the gnosis body
filled with supreme immutable bliss.
The immutable awakening mind
in which emptiness and compassion are inseparable:
Protector, by that empowerment,
grant me your kindness right now.
Together with my children, wife, and the rest,
in every way I am your servant.
Until the heart of awakening,
I have no other refuge."

Then the guru, holding the pride of the principal deity of the maṇḍala, has the disciple, in the form of the body vajra, touch the breasts of the knowledge consort, whose ordinary nature has been purified and who appears in the form of a fully perfected goddess, with a playful and passionate gaze. This is the ritual sequence of the vase empowerment, which purifies the body.

Then, endowed in this way with divine pride, the master makes supplication to her, and the student offers her. Out of the state of emptiness he generates her in the form of a goddess. First blessing the vajra and the lotus, he should then enter into union, reciting oṃ sarva tathāgata anurāgaṇa vajra svabhāva ātmako'haṃ. By the light rays of the seed syllable, the tathāgatas Vairocana and the rest, in union with Tārā and the rest, enter into himself. Through great passion they melt, pass by way of the avadhūtī, and the coemergent, made stable in the vajra jewel, comes forth from the lotus. Taking it between thumb and little finger, he understands the semen of the tathāgatas and his own bodhicitta to be not different. He lets it fall into the student's mouth, the speech vajra, with these words:

As the vajra-holders conferred empowerment
On the heirs of the buddhas of old,
So, with the delightful bodhicitta,
Bathe these sons!

He also recites the mantra of pride. With the thought of the whole assembly of tathāgatas, Vairocana and the rest, the student says, "Oh, the great bliss," and drinks. The wisdom consort then rises, without clothing, and lets the tathāgata drop of her own lotus, with the flower, fall into his mouth. He too should drink it in the same way. This is the secret empowerment with the conventional bodhicitta of wisdom and means. This is the sequence of the rite of the secret empowerment, the purification of speech.

Then the student says, "O Blessed One (the Buddha), all the perfections have been obtained. What shall I do now?" As the master instructs, the student who has made this supplication kisses the tip of the nose of that wisdom consort, who is fully endowed with beauty of form and the other qualities. He enters into union with her, makes her passionate, and relies on her, while the master says:

She holds it and is beautiful.
The buddhas declared that she is to be relied upon.
Through union, bring it down to the cakra,
And taste its single savor with supreme bliss.
The bodhicitta resting in the vajra
Should be watched at the tip of the jewel.

And:

Through full union with the action mudrā
And passion for the wisdom mudrā,
By the discipline of steady bodhicitta,
It is guarded in great bliss.
With the organ placed firmly in the bhaga,
Because the bodhicitta does not leak,
The three existences without exception
Should be meditated on as the body of the Buddha.

Speaking these words, he entrusts her to him. The wisdom woman, for her part, anoints her body with fragrant saffron and other scents, shows her lotus to the student, and says:

Oh, this lotus of mine,
Complete with bliss in full!
Whoever relies on it according to the rite,
Since I stand before him,
Enjoy the lotus as you wish.
For the pleasing of the buddhas and other ends,
I am the sovereign of great bliss.
Since it abides continually,
Please me with the approach of liberation.

The student, endowed with the pride of the deity, places the syllables oṃ āḥ hūṃ svāhā at forehead, throat, heart, secret place and crown. Abiding in a form that embodies the assembly of the five families, he visualizes the lotus arising from āḥ hūṃ, marked with a vajra, and the vajra arising from hūṃ āḥ, marked with an eight-petaled lotus. He imagines a red hri at the tip of the finger of the left channel on the left side. The arousing is done by uttering śī with the tongue. Then, reciting oṃ śī śī śī ha ha hā svāhā, and oṃ sarva tathāgata anurāgaṇa vajra sabhāva ātmako'haṃ together with hūṃ phaṭ, he understands this through the divisions of joy taught in the guru's oral instructions. His body is blessed by the tathāgatas invited by the light rays of the seed syllable, and the guru too invites countless Vairocanas and others. As they become a single taste, they should be pleased. This is as follows:

Body, speech, mind and passion
Are to be placed at the forehead and the rest,
svāhā at the secret place and the crown.
Then the lotus should be purified.
From a, a lotus with eight petals;
From hūṃ, endowed with a vajra.
Thus, together with the vajra,
It is the very continuum of wisdom and means.
From hūṃ, one's own vajra
Should be imagined as five-pronged.
In its center an eight-petaled lotus
Should be visualized arising from the letter a.
Thus the vajra together with the lotus
Should be placed within the lotus.
That yogin recites hūṃ phaṭ.
The vajra-holder moves within the womb.
With the liṅga firmly placed in the bhaga,
The bodhicitta should not be emitted.
The three realms without exception
Should be meditated on as the form of the Buddha.

He should understand the meaning of these words and hold them as is fitting. What is released by the maṇḍala he takes from the lotus with his tongue, and with the awareness of suchness he should drink the bodhicitta, whose nature is the samaya. This is the sequence of the rite of the wisdom-knowledge empowerment, the purification of mind.

Then, by the meaning of the words "bestowing the precious word empowerment," there is the end of special joy, according to the statement: "That which arises fully at the end of the three drops through meditating on bodhicitta, the nature free of afflictions, suchness, all phenomena inconceivable, the nature of the great Vajradhara, is called the fourth empowerment."

Then the feast gathering is performed, the deities are requested to depart, and the vow is given to the student: "The conventional end of special joy that I have taught, the coemergent instant which is the end of the three drops, is not the ultimate. The ultimate is the non-leaking bliss of yogins. Therefore, guard it!" Likewise, the Blessed One said in the Ādibuddha:

Through full union with the action mudrā
And passion for the wisdom mudrā,
By the discipline of steady bodhicitta,
It is guarded in great bliss.
With the organ placed firmly in the bhaga,
Because the bodhicitta does not leak,
One turns the wheel of Dharma.
There is no wisdom higher than this.
Supreme lord of the three realms,
Emptiness and compassion indivisible,
It fully accomplishes one's own and others' aims.
This vow of all the protectors,
When my son keeps it,
Blessed by all the buddhas,
He will attain awakening.
If one overcome by passion
Does not keep the vow
Taken before the all-pervading lord,
He goes to the Raurava hell.
When, through a lesser mind,
The bodhicitta has leaked into the place of birth,
Know that it is to be taken up with one's own tongue
From outside the lotus of the mudrā.

This is the sequence of the rite of the fourth empowerment, pure gnosis.

Then one places the hand of the wisdom consort (prajñā) into the disciple's hand. Then, holding her with one's own left hand, one places the right hand, together with the vajra, on the disciple's head and entrusts her to him, saying, "The lord of this is the Tathāgata himself." Having done so, one says:

"By no other means is there buddhahood.
Since the three realms are purified by this,
therefore never
separate from union with her.
This is the unsurpassed
awareness observance of all the buddhas.
If, deluded, you transgress it,
there will be no supreme accomplishment."

Having said this, one should bestow the awareness observance (vidyāvrata). This is the rite of the awareness observance.

Then, having received all the empowerments, the disciple says: "Glorious guru, through your kindness I now understand the entire meaning of the tantra, which arises from hearing, reflection and meditation and is the path of all the Sugatas. Therefore, from this day on, I shall not forsake the buddhas, the bodhisattvas and you in order to show reverence to another guru." Having made this pledge, the disciple then supplicates the guru with the earlier verses.

Then, for the sake of the supreme lordship, the rite is as follows. Outside the maṇḍala house, on a level piece of ground, an area four cubits across is smeared with cow dung and the like, and an enclosure of five colors is made. In its center, on a seat two cubits wide, an eight-petaled lotus is drawn, and on it the master who is a monk is placed. Eight maidens of beautiful form, adorned with ornaments, who have not borne children, and two young women bearing the Jaya and Vijaya vases, having bound on him the crown and the silk ribbon together with the mudrā, should bathe him. It is said: "Seeing the threatening forefinger and the robes of the vajra-holding monk, the vicious ones go down below."

Likewise, in the center of an enclosure with five lines, on a seat without a lotus, the master who is a novice is placed; he is given the silk ribbon together with the mudrā, and four maidens and two young women should bathe him. Likewise, in the center of an enclosure with three lines, on an ordinary seat, the master who is a householder is placed; he is given the mudrā, and a single young woman should bathe him with the Vijaya vase alone. This is the rite of the master empowerment for the superior, middling and lesser masters.

Then, for the sake of the higher empowerments, the disciple entrusts ten mudrās to the master. This is stated in the root tantra:

"The granddaughter and the daughter,
the sister, likewise the mother,
the wife's mother, that is, the mother-in-law,
likewise the maternal uncle's wife,
likewise the wife of the father's brother,
the sister of the mother,
the sibling of one's own mother,
and one's own wife of excellent form:
from Tārā to Dharmadhātu at the end,
the ten awareness consorts born of one's own family.
The householder who desires liberation
should give them at the time of empowerment.
Whoever, guarding his family,
does not offer an awareness consort to the guru,
to him empowerment should not be given;
otherwise, not from his household.
For conferring empowerment in the maṇḍalas,
furthermore, the groups born of the śūdra caste
are given by the guru
to monks and novices as well, O lord of men.
In order from wind and so forth: the śūdra woman,
the kṣatriya and brāhmaṇa likewise,
the vaiśya, the outcaste woman, the fisherwoman,
the dancer and the dyer likewise,
the leatherworker and the caṇḍāla woman:
these ten, up to Dharmadhātu at the end,
are rightly declared the great awareness consorts.
They fully bestow the fruits of enjoyment and liberation."

According to this, all these, preceded as before, the ten mudrās beginning with the śūdra woman, are for a monk; six of them are for a novice; and one's own wife alone is for a householder. This is the conferral of the Dharma empowerment of wisdom-gnosis. The rest is as before.

Through the seven empowerments of water and so forth, the layman is prophesied to the seventh ground. Through the vase, the novice is prophesied to the eighth, the Immovable, as is indicated by the words "of the youth." Through the secret, the monk is prophesied to Good Intelligence; he is a son of the Buddha, a regent. Through wisdom-gnosis, the elder is prophesied to the Cloud of Dharma; he is a second Buddha.

Then, for the sake of making body, speech and mind into vajra, the offering articles made of gold to be carried, the mudrā, the silk ribbon and the crown correspond to the vase, the secret and the wisdom-gnosis. This is the sequence of the rite of the higher and still higher empowerments.

Then, at the eastern door, holding a parasol over the disciple and circling clockwise, one says:

"With palms joined, circling the maṇḍala,
making offering together with the parasol:
this disciple I entrust to you;
make him a holder of the tantra."

Having thus supplicated the principal deity of the maṇḍala, one sets the parasol aside and grants what the disciple's mind desires:

"Now you become a maṇḍala master,
you, a holder of mantra and tantra.
The buddhas and bodhisattvas
and all the gods are pleased.
With love for all sentient beings,
you yourself, according to the rite,
should draw the maṇḍala with great effort.
Entered into the lineage of practitioners,
by beholding supremely and entering
the maṇḍala of the supreme great secret,
today your sins
are freed without remainder, and you abide rightly.
Moreover, for you there is no emission.
By this very vehicle of great bliss,
having thoroughly purified saṃsāra,
you have turned back from the sufferings of saṃsāra.
Long-lived one, today, through this empowerment,
together with all the buddhas and the vajra,
through sovereignty over all the three realms,
you are certainly the lord.
There is no sin like that of being free of desire
anywhere in the three realms;
therefore you should never
be free of desire.
Fully enjoy all objects of attachment;
never fear bliss.
Enjoy the five meats together with the nectars;
the samaya is always taught thus.
Never take life.
Do not abandon the glorious jewel.
Do not abandon the vow,
and you shall not forsake the master.
As the Tathāgata has taught,
there is nothing whatever that may not be done.
Endowed with a mind of wisdom and means,
you have no sin; you need not fear."

Having conferred the empowerment according to the rite through this dharmatā, one regards him with the loving mind of the buddhas and bodhisattvas, like love for an only child, intent on his virtue. This is the sequence of the rite of encouragement.

When the disciple has thus received empowerment and been consoled, he should do as follows. He says:

"Today my birth has borne fruit;
my life has borne its fruit.
Today I am born into the family of the buddhas;
today I have become a child of the buddhas."

Then comes the offering. Elephants, horses, jewels, gold, clothing, ornaments, cattle, buffaloes, male servants, female servants, an attractive wife, sons, daughters and so forth, whatever one has, should be offered to the guru with reverence.

Then, in the daytime, a feast of sweet things such as milk should be given to ten young men and young women. When seven hours have passed, the Saṅgha of monks and nuns should be pleased with sweet foods free of fish, meat and the like. A fee is offered, and they are requested to proceed to the temple.

In a place without openings, without interruption, and free of bustle, at night, when the sun has long declined, the heroes and the heroines, the sovereign ladies of the thirty-six classes, enter unnoticed by others and take their places in the order of the circles of the maṇḍala, as follows.

In the center, on a seat of cowhide and tiger skin, is the principal together with his consort, that is, the master sits there, and his vessel is of human skin. To his right, on a human skin, is an outcaste woman (ḍombī). In the directions, on seats of dog, horse, cow and elephant skin, are a śūdra woman, a kṣatriya woman, a brahmin woman and a vaiśya woman. In the intermediate directions, beginning from the northeast, are the vessels of urine, excrement, blood and marrow.

Then, in the directions of the enclosure surrounding the mind maṇḍala, on skins of sheep, wild dog, camel and marsh lion, are a bell-metal worker woman, a dancer upon an umbi, a jewel-maker woman and a well-digger woman. In the intermediate directions, on seats of goat, deer, donkey and pig skin, are a weaver woman, a liquor-seller woman, a goldsmith woman and a garland-maker woman.

Then, at the place of the third, the speech circle, in the directions and intermediate directions, on seats of a kumbhīra crocodile, a heap of cowries, a heap of kakari, an otter, a makara, a frog, a tortoise shell and a conch, are a butcher woman, a potter woman, a porter woman, a prostitute, a palanquin-bearer woman, a fisherwoman, a dancing woman and a washerwoman.

Then, in the directions of the fourth enclosure, on skins of rhinoceros, tiger, brown bear and black bear, are a blacksmith woman, a lac-dyer woman, a silk-worker woman and an oil-presser woman. In the intermediate directions, beginning from the fire quarter, on skins of female yak, jackal, otter and cat, are a bamboo-worker woman, a carpenter woman, a leather-worker woman and a barber woman.

In the fifth enclosure, on godhā lizard, mouse, sālajitaka and monkey, are a barbarian woman (mleccha), a sweeper of filth, a woman who dwells in the charnel ground and a dyer woman. In the intermediate directions, beginning from the fire quarter, on hare, zungmo, iṣukapaśu and frog, are a rawhide-worker woman, a sorceress, a wild woman and a mountain woman (śabarī).

If these skins are not available, then in the east and the fire quarter, dog skin; in the south and the quarter of Nairṛti, horse skin; in the west and the wind quarter, elephant skin; in the north and the quarter of Īśāna, cowhide; in the center, human skin.

All these yoginīs without exception are naked, with hair unbound, and bear the five mudrās. On the right of each of them is a hero, equal to them, likewise bearing the five mudrās. Skin seats should be given to them.

Their drinking vessels are: in the east and the fire quarter, mother-of-pearl shells; in the south and the southwest, vessels of coconut; in the west and the wind quarter, earthen bowls; in the north and Īśāna's quarter, human skulls; in the center, wooden bowls.

Then the nature of the samaya substances. The strengths of the thirty-six creatures of the seats are the samaya of those that move in water. Further, the strengths of the cātaka, the parrot, the cuckoo, the sārikā, the bat, the pigeon, the so bird, the sparrow, the ruddy goose, the goose, the byalong, the cuckoo-eye, the jaka, the bhagavatī, the potakī, the partridge, the zhé, the heron, the water bird, the crow, the vulture, the owl, the horpa, the peacock, the rooster, the hawk, the wolwa, the jenlak, the woodpecker, the blue-eyed cakora, the gowo, the great leather-winged bird, the dhātāha, the pahorta, the svāyi, the fish-eater, the crane and the amparika: these are the thirty-six samaya substances of those that move in the sky.

Then again: teeth, hair, skin, flesh, sinew, bone, stomach, bhaga, lice, body hair, worms, lungs, intestines, the sexual organ, phlegm, bile, lymph, blood, sweat, fat, tears, mucus, urine, grease and pus, of various colors and various smells, and grime, tongue, eyes, ears, nose, semen and menstrual blood: these are the samaya of the bodies of sentient beings.

Semen, urine, feces, marrow, flesh, spleen, bile, blood, intestines and skins, together with water, offering water, incense, lamps, flowers, unspoiled fruits and food offerings, are placed in vessels and set out in the eight directions and intermediate directions.

Then the master, having first meditated on the protection wheel, after emptiness, invites the wisdom circle with the light rays of his own seed syllable, and, having first offered water, causes it to enter the mind maṇḍala and the others. These too are visualized in the form of deities. Then the master sets out before him the aforementioned samaya substances, the strength, the intoxicant, the barley meal and so forth, and purifies the torma.

Or, another method of purification: with oṃ vajra krodheśvari sarva drabyāni viśodhaya hūṃ phaṭ one should dissolve the five nectars in the intoxicant and perform a cleansing torma.

Then, taking some with the ring finger, one makes a triangle, in its center a round maṇḍala, and invites the wisdom circle into it. Then, taking some with the ring finger, one should fully satisfy them with the aforementioned mantra.

Then perform the confession of wrongdoing and the rest. After making requests for the aims one desires, request the deities to depart. Then give torma to the field protectors with the mantra already given. Next, with "oṃ hāriti piṇaḍikaṃ pratīccha svāhā" and "oṃ dūtike agragrāsama pratīccha svāhā", consecrate beer, food and a first portion with the three syllables for Hārītī and the messengers. Place them on the ground and give them before eating any food.

Then the patron makes a maṇḍala offering and says, "Please partake as you wish."

Then the karma vajra, respectful and conscientious toward the guru, should respectfully offer each of the samaya substances. The vessel of intoxicant should be served with the lotus hand, and the one receiving it also takes it in a cupped lotus hand. Everything should be served to the master in double measure. They offer the first portion of the intoxicant to the master, bow, and then drink. Until permission has been given, the intoxicant is not to be set on the ground. If it is to be set down, they should instead pass it to one another with the lotus hand and receive it that way.

In this way, when the strengthener (bala), the intoxicant and the rest are served, the things to lick, drink, eat and chew, they recite:

Phenomena are pure by their nature.
Without harboring doubt about this,
brahmin, dog and outcaste
eat as of a single nature.
The Sugata's dharmas are free of defilement,
like space, without a stain,
free of grasped and grasper.
Guard this without confusion.

And further:

Without permission from the lord of the circle
one does not hold the feast gathering.
One endowed with the yoga of the deity
should do every action slowly.

Otherwise one becomes distracted. If one wishes to leave, bow to the lord of the circle and the others and ask permission. When coming in, bow first. Thus one partakes in meditative equipoise.

When the mind is uplifted by the strengthener, the intoxicant and the rest, those who have received permission from the guru sing some pleasing vajra songs in a soft voice, with the mudrās and dances of the respective deities.

Then, having eaten and drunk the strengthener, the intoxicant and the rest, one should look at the yoginīs abiding in their respective places with sidelong glances, and perform the various kinds of amorous acts (karaṇa), such as touching the high, firm breasts and the lotus, kissing the lips, embracing, and so on. Here, abandoning such conceptions about the yoginīs as distinctions, shame, caste and outcaste, the yogins gathered there should not reject sisters and others abiding in their respective places, nor those whose nose, ears, lips or the like have been cut off, nor those of ugly form and the like. The same applies to the yogins as well.

Moreover, if any young woman with eyes like blue lotuses worships the vajra of the master together with the heroes, her merit is greater even than that of offering immeasurable lands, elephants, horses, maidens and the like.

Here the hand gestures, the signs, the classification by family and so forth of the yoginīs have not been written down, for fear of making the text too long.

Then, having enjoyed the strengthener, the intoxicant and the rest, do everything as described. When it is finished, the master has the leftovers of what was eaten and drunk gathered up and placed in front. Intoxicant and the like are poured onto it again, and a first portion of beer consecrated with the three mantras is sprinkled over it. With the mantra "oṃ kha kha khāhi khāhi ucchiṣṭa balī ucchiṣṭa bhakṣaka svāhā", it is offered as the leftover torma, and he has it carried out the back way together with a lamp.

Then pairs of garments, bracelets, earrings and the like, with fragrant tooth powder and so on, are offered to the principal, the heroes, the heroines and the rest.

Then, making a maṇḍala offering, the patron should make the aspiration, saying:
"By this merit, may the countless sentient beings dwelling in the three realms attain the unsurpassed."

Then the remaining leftovers should be buried in a pit. In this way the feast gathering is held by oneself, by a king, or by another.

Apart from this, according to the particular ritual practice: from among ordinary women or men, as they can be found, five, thirteen, twenty-one, twenty-nine, or the full complement are respectfully invited, and everything is done in the same way, with seats and so on as they are available.

Next, the heroes' feast. If there is no maṇḍala of colored powders, make a square in the center with cow dung. Then the master does the inviting of the wisdom wheel and the rest as before. The leader of the gathering faces east. On his left, the patron should present one mudrā, because otherwise drinking beer without a mudrā is prohibited. On his right are the masters who are monks, seated in a row facing east. From the southwest are the masters who are novices, facing north. From the northeast are the masters who are householders, facing south. Nuns, female novices and laywomen are seated likewise. On the left of the female leader of the gathering, the patron should present one partner of method, because otherwise eating the strengthener without method is prohibited. The rest is the same as before. This is the sequence of the rite of the feast gathering together with the heroes' feast.

Then comes the consecration of cast images and the like, preceded by worship and so forth of the maṇḍala as taught, or of a maṇḍala such as a painted image. From within emptiness, the image is fully generated from the seed syllable and so on, or else in an instant; one imagines it as the form of the samaya being, with the seed syllable at its heart. It is dressed in new red garments and the like. Then the patron, holding flowers, is visualized as that very image holding flowers. The patron, together with the cast image and so forth, enters the maṇḍala of colored powders just as a disciple does. The master also has them offer the garland of flowers to the emblem of the particular deity of the cast image, or to the emblem of its family. Alternatively, the casting of the flower is omitted, and the cast image and the rest are made to enter merely by seeing the maṇḍala.

In the east of the maṇḍala, or in its northeast corner, the cloth for the bathing seat should be made of a suitable length and breadth. Alternatively, on ground that has been smeared and so on in the proper way, one smears it with the five products of the cow mixed with the five nectars. One sets out eight vases in the directions and the intermediate directions, and makes everything as pleasing as one can with flowers and the like, parasols, victory banners, pennants, bells, dance, song, music and so on. On an auspicious lunar day or the like, the master puts on all the ornaments. With the pride of Vajrasattva together with his consort, he makes in the center of the platform, with colored powders, a square the size of the mind maṇḍala, with a western door that has no portico, with a multicolored eight-petaled lotus, and with the emblems of Amoghasiddhi and the others in the directions and intermediate directions. On top of this, on a cloth on which an eight-petaled lotus has been drawn, the images and other objects that can be moved are invited to be seated facing east.

For temples and stupas, for perfumed chambers (gandhakuṭī), and for large images, one generates the samaya being as before and imagines them seated in the maṇḍala. Since these need no bathing cloth, there are thus two kinds.

Then, at evening time, the images and the rest having first been seated, one faces them oneself, and, beginning with water for the feet and the other offerings, performs the nirmañchana rite of purifying waving, as follows. Holding in the right and left hands a fistful of white mustard seed empowered with the mantra oṃ āḥ hūṃ ho haṃ kṣaḥ, one circles each hand twice to the left, recites oṃ sarva pāpaṃ dahana vajrāya vajrasatvasu sarva pāpaṃ daha svāhā, and casts the mustard seed into the fire. One does the same by circling to the right as well. In the same way, one holds a fistful of cow dung together with a bowl filled with water and dūrvā grass, and likewise barley dough, and the cooling fans and cloths; but the bowls and such things should not be thrown into the fire.

Then one touches the images and the rest with the hand, and, together with the mantra of the principal deity, makes offerings to them as appropriate, either directly or on their reflection in a mirror. Perfume is smeared on the heart; flowers are placed on the head; offering water is set in front; the lamp is offered while circling it; and, reciting the six syllables seven times, one fumigates them with butter and white incense. This is the sequence of the nirmañchana rite.

Then, reciting the six syllables, one takes a brush of dūrvā grass and, with curd, milk, butter, honey and sugar kept in a copper vessel, which have the nature of the five nectars, and also with curd, milk, butter, cow dung and water, smears those images and other objects that can be bathed, with the mantra oṃ hūṃ trāṃ hrīḥ a. For painted images, books and the like, this is done on their reflection in a mirror. Then, reciting the six syllables, one bathes them with clean water and fragrant water. Then they are purified with the five cleansing powders poured into a bell-metal vessel, using oṃ sarva tathāgata kāya viśodhani svāhā. One smears them with scented oil ripened by fire and sun, and again with cleansing powder. One smears them with a sour substance and bathes them in the same way; one smears them with turmeric from the bell-metal vessel and bathes them again. Then one smears them evenly with sandalwood, saffron, camphor, musk and so on. Imagining that one bathes them with the vases invited by the light rays of the seed syllable at one's heart, one bathes them with the water of the vases, accompanied by auspicious songs, together with the verses stated earlier. The water clinging to them is wiped off with a soft cloth, and one offers them garments as far as one is able.

Then, by means of the seed syllable, one invites into one's presence the wisdom beings, the buddhas, the bodhisattvas and the deities. Beginning with water for the feet, water for the mouth and offering water, one kneels with the right knee planted on the ground, rings the bell with the left hand, and with the right hand fumigates with the censer. With a vajra tongue arisen from hūṃ, one praises them, changing the words "the Blessed One, Kālacakra himself" to "I myself am Vajra so-and-so." Having bowed, one requests them to depart with oṃ vajra punarāgamanaya muḥ.

One smears perfume on the five places of the bathed images and the rest, and makes offerings. Touching them three times with the vajra in the right hand while reciting the six syllables, one recites the mantra of each respective deity. Then one offers torma to the guardians of the directions. Thus it is done.

When the consecration is to be performed even without the preparatory rite (adhivāsana), in the case of one who is steadfast, the rites that will be described are done in a single day. If the patron has wealth, the preparatory rite should be performed for the sake of the accumulation of merit. This is the sequence of the rite of the preparation of the deity.

Then, on the ground to the east of the maṇḍala, one makes a four-doored maṇḍala with five lines, and in its center an eight-petaled lotus. In the directions and intermediate directions one arranges the emblems in their proper order. Then the images and the rest are brought into the maṇḍala facing toward the front. The master should understand the particular ways of generating the deity set out in the tantras, the sādhanas and so on. For stupas and the like, one meditates on the form of the samaya being from emptiness, either gradually or instantaneously. For books, from the state of emptiness arises āḥ, and from it Amitābha together with his consort upon a lotus and a sun. From their transformation, one meditates on the samaya being in the form of Amitābha, whose nature is the image of the letters. For temples, stupas, perfumed chambers and the like, Vairocana, arisen from oṃ, abides upon a lotus and a moon, embracing Locanā. From their transformation, one meditates on the samaya being in the form of Vairocana, whose nature is the image of the temple or other object.

Furthermore, with the words:
"Having made it of the nature of emptiness,
the vajras of body, speech and mind together with the supreme Victor,"
one meditates on the assembly itself as Kālacakra, as taught in the sādhana. The deities and the rest are likewise meditated upon as their own forms arisen from their own seed syllables. From the white moon and the rest up to the green space maṇḍala, they are arranged in the cakras from the forehead down to the secret place, and one should meditate on oṃ through kṣaḥ.

For the deities, too, it is done in this way. With the light rays of one's own seed syllable, invite the wheel of wisdom beings and worship them with water for the feet and so on. Saying ja hūṃ baṃ hoḥ, and with the hook mudrā and the others, have the four wrathful deities draw them in, make them enter, bind them, and bring them under control, each in turn.

Then, for the cast images and so on, place the seed syllable in the lotus at the heart, inside a sun and a moon joined face to face.

Then the empowerments of water and the rest are given just as in the empowerment of the disciple explained earlier. Or else all of them may be completed through visualization.

Alternatively, one may omit the empowerment, place the seed syllable at the heart, regard the image with certainty as the form of the deity, and worship it with its own mantra or with this mantra: oṃ āḥ vajra va ye hūṃ svāhā. In the same way, with mantras that begin with oṃ āḥ vajra and end with hūṃ svāhā, offer flowers, incense, lamps, perfume, food offerings, ornaments and so on. oṃ āḥ sarva śodhana hūṃ phaṭ is the mantra for purifying the fruits and other offerings. oṃ vajra svāhā is the mantra for presenting the fruits. Then, having given the samaya substances, show the mirror with the mantra oṃ vajra āḥ.

Next comes pleasing the deities with a fire offering. It varies with the particular forms of the cast images and so on, in keeping with the particular wealth of the patron. Its purpose is to accomplish the aims one desires, from pacification and the other rites up to buddhahood.

With a golden probe take some honey placed in a vessel of silver or the like, and, reciting oṃ cakṣu samanta cakṣura viśodhane svāhā, anoint the eyes of the cast image and open them. For books and the like, do this to their reflection in a mirror.

Then, on the fire of the hearth or on another fire, pour milk, unbroken rice, butter, honey and sugar, with the homa ladle or with a pipal leaf, into a vessel set on the hearthstones. Reciting the mantra of the principal deity, cook it until it is done. Then, reciting oṃ daṃ byānaṃ samādhi dhrana priṇane svāhā, serve that porridge, regarding the cast images and so on as the images of the deities. Offer water for rinsing the mouth, mixed with fragrant honey.

Then have them hear this pledge: oṃ hūṃ trāṃ hrīḥ a.

Into the books dissolve Amitābha together with his consort, and thereby meditate on the pages as vowels and consonants whose nature is the thirty-two major marks and the eighty minor marks. Into temples and the like Vairocana together with his consort dissolves, and thereby one should imagine them as a mansion of the various samādhis of all the perfectly complete buddhas, of their Dharma, and of the bodhisattvas, the śrāvakas and the rest. Into a stupa, likewise, Vairocana together with his consort dissolves; thereby, at its center, upon the hub of a lotus, on a moon, there is a white eight-spoked wheel. On the petals of the north and the other directions one should imagine a sword, a vajra, a jewel and a lotus.

Then, having worshipped with flowers and so on, join the palms and say:

Just as all the buddhas,
from their abode in Tuṣita,
were born from the womb of the lovely goddess Māyā,
so in this very image,
O Protector, remain firm and forever,
and, so that the mind of awakening may grow,
please accept these offerings and the rest
for the sake of so-and-so.

This is for the purpose of requesting them to remain for as long as existence lasts.

Reciting the six-syllable mantra, indicate the upper, middle and lower parts of the cast image and so on by circling the vajra in the right hand around them three times. Praise them with "Bestower of the supreme, supreme vajra, to you I bow," and the rest. Then, to remove the faults of excess and omission, recite the hundred-syllable mantra thus: oṃ vajra sa tva sa ma ya ma nu pā la ya vajra sa tva tve no pa tiṣṭha dṛḍho me bha va su to ṣyo me bha va su po ṣyo me bha va a nu rakto me bha va sarva siddhiṃ me pra ya ccha sarva karma su ci me cittaṃ śi yaḥ ku ru hūṃ ha ha ha ha hoḥ bha ga vā na sarva ta thā ga ta vajra ma me mu ñca vajrī bha va ma hā sa ma ya sa tva āḥ. Having recited it, ask for forbearance with these words:

Whatever I have left undone or overdone
through lack, through ignorance, or through disrespect,
for all of it without exception, Compassionate Ones,
I ask your forbearance.

Buddhas, together with the goddesses,
their heirs, Brahmā, the nāgas and the rest:
for every omission in the rite and the like,
it is right to ask their forbearance.
Protect these cast images
from fire, earth, water and wind.
Bestow on the patron, without ceasing,
peace, increase and good fortune.
Be pleased with his children and others, with the disciples,
and with me as well, in the same way.
And though you go to the realm of the buddhas,
please come here again.

Recite this three times. Give torma to the guardians of the directions and to all the spirits (bhūtas). The patron too should circumambulate the cast images and so on, make offerings and prostrations, and present the fee to the master, pleasing him fully.

Books are consecrated in the maṇḍala of Amitābha; temples and the like in that of Vairocana; bodies or images whose identity is not known, in a maṇḍala with Vajrasattva as its principal. If the cast image or image is known to belong to a particular family, it is consecrated in the maṇḍala of the lord of that family. Elsewhere, too, it is said:

To consecrate an image,
according to the tantra in which it is found,
one should make the maṇḍala of that very tantra.
Or else, according to the deity
whose identity the master himself has assumed,
one should know its distinctions.

Therefore it is said that, by the intent of this supreme and unexcelled tantra, it is fitting to consecrate by means of the tantra and also by the deity taught in the tantra.

The consecration by the brief rite is this. From the state of emptiness, in an instant, the cast image or other object becomes the samaya being arisen from its own seed syllable and so on; into it, with the light rays of the seed syllable at one's own heart, one should make the wisdom being enter. The tathāgatas and others invited by the light rays of one's own seed syllable, and oneself as well, confer empowerment with the water of the vase. Having worshipped fully, one should recite its mantra one hundred and eight times.

This is the sequence of the rite for consecrating cast images and so on.

The consecration of a protection wheel is as follows. The master, holding the pride of Vajradhara, should cause the wisdom being that matches the commitment being of whatever protections guard a place to enter it, according to the rite, and should meditate on it. This is done for each one according to the particular aim to be accomplished. One meditates that it enters the mantra of the principal deity of the protection wheel and the name of the practitioner. The protection wheel enters through the opening of the aperture of Brahmā, passes down the vajra path, and descends into the lotus of the goddess. The Tathāgatas, drawn by the light rays of the seed syllable at the heart, enter in this way and confer empowerment. Then one also empowers it oneself with the water of the vase. One then recites the heart mantra of the principal deity of the protection one hundred and eight times.

Next, the master, holding the pride of Vajradhara, instantly visualizes a five-pronged vajra on the palm of the right hand and blesses it there with the syllable aḥ. With the left hand, one instantly visualizes the form of a lotus petal and blesses it with cog. The central thread of the rosary placed between them has the nature of Vajrasattva. The threads of the directions and intermediate directions have the nature of Padmapāṇi, Maitreya, Gaganagañja, Samantabhadra, Vajrapāṇi, Mañjuśrī, Sarvanivaraṇaviṣkambhin, and Kṣitigarbha. The beads have the nature of Vairocana and the other buddhas. The guru bead is instantly visualized as having the nature of qualities such as the powers. Vajrasattva and the others, invited by the light rays of the seed syllable at the heart, should be made to enter in the proper way. One blesses it with the mantra oṃ pade pade mahājñānāṃ sarbabuddha mahāṃ bhaved hūṃ hūṃ hoḥ hoḥ hoḥ aḥ khaṃ svāhā, empowers it with the water of the vase, and makes offerings.

For a less elaborate practice, one imagines the thread of the rosary as being of one taste with Vajrasattva, and imagines it transforming into the form of the rosary. For the empowerment, one recites the heart mantra of the vajra of speech one hundred and eight times. Alternatively, from the state of emptiness of that very rosary, one who has taken on the pride of Vajradhara invites the syllables hūṃ with the light rays of the seed syllable at the heart, and causes the nature of the major and minor marks to enter the guru bead and the other beads.

In the same way, the bell is consecrated with the thirty-two vowels beginning with a, and the vajra with the eighty consonants beginning with ka. So too, for the sake of offering, are the skull cup and the sacred thread. This is the sequence of the rite for consecrating protection wheels and the like.

For the sake of a capable patron, one erects on the bank of a pond a maṇḍala with Vairocana as its principal deity. One who possesses the yoga of the principal deity of the wheel casts torma in the directions and intermediate directions of the pond. Then, from the state of the water's emptiness, from bhūṃ arises a wheel marked with bhūṃ, and from that, Vairocana together with Locanā. Next, one meditates on Vajra-Varuṇa, lord of the waters, white, with a human face and seven serpent hoods, embracing his consort with his left hand, which holds a serpent noose, and abiding in play; in his right hand he holds a blue lotus, and below the navel he has the form of a serpent. Having meditated on him in the form of the commitment being, one radiates light from the seed syllable at one's own heart and invites Vairocana. Preceded by water for the feet and the other offerings, one says oṃ samaya stvaṃ and causes him to enter. One confers empowerment and makes offerings with flowers and so forth.

From his transformation comes the pond, with its reflected images, or filled with water that has the nature of the nectar of wisdom and possesses the eight qualities: fragrant, tasty, light, soft, cool, and harming neither throat nor stomach, reaching down to the depths. It is endowed with the eight nāgas and moves in waves toward the four directions. In its eastern and other directions are Nanda, Padma, Takṣaka, and Varuṇa; in the intermediate directions are Śaṅkhapāla, Karkoṭa, Kulika, and Mahāpadma. In order, they are made of, or visualized as made of, gold, brass, copper, silver, lead, bell metal, iron, and tin. One instantly imagines them in the form of commitment beings, invites the wisdom beings with the light rays of the seed syllable at the heart, and makes them abide inseparable from them.

At that time one erects an offering pillar of twice its height. At its center is Varuṇa, lord of the waters. Into it one should pour honey mixed with water and the five nectars. After properly worshipping it with flowers and so forth, one should perform the fire offering. On the bank, one smears the ground with cow dung and makes a maṇḍala of cool fragrance. Inviting the deities with the light rays of the seed syllable at one's own heart, one should visualize Vajra-Varuṇa at the center of the maṇḍala's lotus, Nanda and the others in the eastern and other directions and in the intermediate directions, and nāgas in all the outer directions. With each one's own mantra containing its name, in this way, oṃ āḥ bajra baruṇāya hūṃ svāhā, one worships them with white flowers. Then, displaying the nāga mudrā, one gives a torma of milk with the mantra oṃ ananta bāsuki takṣaka karkoṭa kra padma mahā padma śaṃkhapāla kulika debati mahā debati soma śikhi daṇḍara abalāla hulu hulu nanda unanda sāgara mahā sāgar tāpa mahā tāpha śī kānti mahā kānti ratna kānta surūpa mahā surūpa bhadritīka mahphuhodar sili mahā sala oṃ bhakṣa bhakṣa āgccha mahā nāg adhipati sarba bhurbhuva phruṃ phru svāhā.

Then, so that they may remain for as long as it endures, one recites the hundred-syllable mantra and scatters what has been blessed in the eight directions, and also beyond them. One should also please young maidens. This is the rite for consecrating ponds, bathing places, and wells.

Next, the rite for consecrating trees, parks, and the like. Having given torma in the directions, the master, whose nature is that of Vairocana, visualizes the chief of the trees, arising from the state of emptiness, as Vairocana. Preceded by offering water, one causes the wisdom being to enter it, empowers it with the water of the vase, and from its transformation the tree appears. In its midst, one should likewise intently visualize the trees in the same way.

One should also worship with flowers and the like the deity dwelling in that principal tree, perform the other activities, request it to remain for as long as it lasts, and cast tormas to the directions. This is the sequence of the rite for consecrating pleasure groves and the like.

Now I shall set out here the conventional fire offering, which is said to come before all rites, such as empowerment and consecration. In the first watch of the day the rites of pacifying and so on are performed, and likewise at night. For those who perform them in the watches, the all-purpose rite, pacifying, increasing, killing, dividing, subjugating, summoning, stupefying and immobilizing are placed, in that order, in the center of the city, in the quarter of Yama, of Nairṛti, of Varuṇa, lord of the waters, of Vāyu, of the yakṣa, of Īśāna, in the east, and in the southeast corner of fire. At the time of empowerment they are set outside the ring of fire of the colored-powder maṇḍala, leaving a space of two cubits, in the yakṣa's quarter or in the fierce one's quarter. They are round, square, bow-shaped, and five-cornered, seven-cornered, three-cornered, six-cornered and eight-cornered. They measure one cubit, two cubits, half a cubit, the same as the round one, forty finger-widths, twenty, and thirty.

Half the width of the fire pits is above. The rim of the hearth is one sixth, set aside straight across. Twice that is the platform. Twice that is for the colored rim. Further, a rim level with the lip is made on the outside. Above, the hearths are made marked with these emblems: a moon, a sun, a moon, a blue disk, a red one, a smoke-colored disk, a yellow disk, and two Mount Merus. The platform is divided into five parts; the two parts at the two sides are left aside, and on the three parts it is encircled from below, in order, with the emblems described below. If it is made of earth, it is done with half a part. If it is made of colored powders, there is no fixed rule.

Outside that, they are encircled with garlands: for two, of lotus petals; for two, of palms with fingers; of crow tail feathers; for two, of red arka leaves; and for two, of yellow arka leaves. Outside that they are encircled: for two, with kuśa grass, with the hair of the dead, and with leaves of the preta tree; for two, with red flowers; and for two, with yellow flowers.

The extent of the hearth is the lotus petals. The hub of the principal one is a twelfth part. On these one should set a black vajra, a white lotus, a white wheel, a black curved knife, a black sword, a red arrow, a red vajra hook, a yellow snake noose, and a yellow vajra chain. In the fire pit of the all-purpose rite, on the lotus petals of the east and the other directions, there are a sword, a jewel, a wheel and a lotus; in the intermediate directions, a black curved knife, a red hook, a yellow vajra noose and a white trident. It is likewise for pacifying. For increasing, too, the emblems beginning with the vajra are in the directions, and those beginning with the black curved knife in the intermediate directions. Likewise, for killing, the maṇḍala faces east; for killing and dividing, the emblems beginning with the sword face north. For subjugating and summoning they face south, with the jewel and the rest in the directions and the red hook and the rest in the intermediate directions. For stupefying and immobilizing they face west, with the wheel, the yellow noose and the rest in the directions and intermediate directions.

Leaving two cubits around the fire pit, three enclosure walls are made with white, red and black colored powders, with doors facing north and so on, one eighth of the width inside the enclosure. For all the fire pits they are made with archways like the doors of the maṇḍala. In front of the fire pit, at a place two cubits away, one sets in its own direction a one-cubit figure laid out with the shape distinguishing pacifying and the rest, with hands as before, a lotus, and the particular emblems. For the all-purpose rite it is marked by a crossed vajra contained within a source of phenomena.

Likewise, the seat for the ladles of the fire offering and so on, and the seat for the offering water, are on the right and the left. On the seat one should sit in the postures in their proper order. Placing the right foot above the left knee is the lotus posture. Placing the right foot on the left thigh and the left on the right thigh is the vajra posture. The asura posture is to sit with the body drawn in like a tortoise. Putting the two ankles together, bringing them against the buttocks, and raising both knees is the squatting posture. Crossing the left leg is another; the reverse of that is the second. Putting the two ankles of the left foot together, bringing them against the buttocks, and raising both knees is the squatting posture. With the left leg remaining crossed and the right squatting, slightly extended, is the half cross-legged posture; its reverse is the second variety. With the heel of the left foot resting at the anus and the right in the squatting position is the posture with the foot at the anus; and there are their reverses.

Then, in order, there are rosaries of crystal, pearl, human teeth, bodhi seeds, lotus seeds, red sandalwood, lungtang, and rudrākṣa. Then there are the vessels for the offering water, lotus-shaped vessels twelve finger-widths across in their petals and hub, made of crystal, silver, skull, iron, gold, copper, wood and clay.

Then the filling ladle has a handle one cubit long marked with a vajra, its upper part shaped like joined palms. Its inner center is square, marked with a lotus, and four finger-widths in size. Its tip is a five-pronged vajra. From the middle prong it has a channel through which the liquid flows. The pouring ladle is somewhat thinner and smaller than that, equal in length, with its upper part a lotus. Thus there are two each for pacifying and the rest.

There are five hundred kindling sticks of udumbara, bodhi tree, nyagrodha, bakaḍi and grapevine; five hundred human bones the size of the little finger; and likewise crow tail feathers. Likewise there are five hundred sticks of khadira or palāśa, and likewise of bilva, twelve finger-widths long. Likewise the substances: for pacifying, cow's milk and dūrvā grass; for increasing, butter and dūrvā grass; for killing, meat and poison.

For expelling and for sowing discord, it is rājikā mustard mixed with human fat, and salt. For bringing under control, it is menstrual blood and red flowers such as karavīra. For summoning, it is urine and the leaves and fruit of the pola tree. For paralyzing, it is spittle and withered flowers. For bewildering, it is salt and the leaves of the thornapple.

Likewise, for each pair of rites the flowers are, in turn: fragrant and white; pungent, thorny and black; red; and yellow.

Likewise, the cooling incense is made with aloewood, storax, camphor, and honey with sugar. The strongly smoking incense is made with excrement, flesh and blood. The incense that becomes strongly fragrant is made with guggulu, lac, white incense, kunduru, śrīvāsa resin and molasses. The astringent incense is made with powdered myrobalan and molasses.

Further, in their proper order, the foods are: milk food, food with curd, tripe, food with butter, food with sesame, food with mung beans, and food with chickpeas.

Likewise, for each pair one anoints with white sandalwood and blood, charcoal and saffron, gīvaṃ and red sandalwood, and turmeric and orpiment.

Likewise, the lamps are fed with butter, human fat, sesame oil, jyotiṣmatī oil and bhallātaka oil.

Likewise, the unbroken grains are śāli rice, rājikā mustard, white mustard seed and lentils.

Likewise, white garments and the rest are to be understood as belonging to pacifying and the other rites.

Then the master, seated on the seat as described and in the posture as described, for the all-purpose rite takes on green. For pacifying and the others, by pairs: peaceful and white; for wrath, dark; with passion, red; and paralyzing and yellow. Visualizing the form of Kālacakra as explained before, and having made the protection wheel, according to the ritual sequence explained before, one plants in the ten directions pegs made of udumbara, paṭa, aśoka, bone, iron, khadira, sakhok and bilva. With the mantra oṃ āḥ hūṃ hraḥ phaṭ one takes up the water offering with kuśa grass and sprinkles it for purification.

Then, for the all-purpose rite and for pacifying and the others, one stacks up fragrant woods such as sandalwood, woods of milky trees, woods such as red sandalwood, and astringent woods. One takes fire from a temple, and from the houses of a brahmin, a śūdra, a caṇḍāla, a kṣatriya and a vaiśya, places it there, and kindles it with a fan.

Then the fire god is invited with this mantra:
oṃ ehyehi mahābhuta devaṛṣi dvijastama
gṛhitvāyudha mahāra asmin sannihito bhab vajradhara ājñāpayati svāhā

Having recited this, one visualizes in one's own heart and in the outer hearth, on a moon or a sun according to the kind of rite, the form of the fire god arisen from raṃ. Saying jaḥ hūṃ baṃ hoḥ, one summons him with the hook mudrā, makes him enter with the vajra mudrā, binds him with the noose mudrā, and brings him under control with the bell mudrā.

Then again, for the all-purpose rite, his body is green in color; his right hands hold a vajra and a rosary, his left hands a bell and a lotus. His garments, eyes and hair are reddish yellow, and he is to be sealed with Akṣobhya. For pacifying he is white; his right hands hold a lotus and a crystal rosary, his left hands a water pot and a conch. For increasing, his right hands hold a wheel and a pearl rosary, his left hands a lotus and a water pot; he is sealed with Amitābha. For the secret rite he is black; his right hands hold a curved knife and a trident, his left hands a skull cup and a khaṭvāṅga; he is sealed with Amoghasiddhi. For expelling and sowing discord, his right hands hold a sword and a trident, his left hands a skull cup and a khaṭvāṅga. For bringing under control he is the color of saffron; his right hands hold an arrow and a jewel, his left hands a bow and a mirror; he is sealed with Ratnasambhava. For summoning, his right hands hold a vajra hook and a jewel, his left hands a vajra noose and a mirror. For bewildering, his right hands hold a snake and a staff, his left hands a wheel and a hammer. For paralyzing and for nailing down with the ritual dagger he is yellow; his right hands hold an iron chain and a hammer, his left hands a wheel and a hammer; he is to be sealed with Vairocana. For pacifying and the rest, his garments match their respective colors.

Then, with oṃ pravarasatakarmahārasmin pratīccha svāhā, one gives the water offering.

Then, with oṃ vajra at the beginning, puṣpaṃ and the others in the middle, and svāhā joined at the end, one worships with puṣpi, dhūpaṃ, gandhaṃ, dīpaṃ, akṣataṃ, naividyaṃ, lāsyaṃ, hāsyaṃ, vādyaṃ, nṛdyaṃ, gītaṃ and kāmaṃ.

Then, into the open mouth of the fire god, with oṃ sarva pāpaṃ dahana vajra agṇi vajrasatvāsya sarva pāpaṃ daharaha svāhā, he is invited to receive the first burnt offerings in the all-purpose rite and the others. In the secret rite and the others, it is done with oṃ vajra anala sarvabhūtana jvālaya sarvana bhasmiṃ kuru sarvaduṣṭāna svāhā.

Then, with oṃ āḥ huṃ ho haṃ kṣaḥ svāhā, one should offer one hundred and eight fuel sticks. With oṃ hrḷṃ hruṃ hṛṃ hriṃ hraḥ svāhā, one hundred and eight. Then with oṃ a ā i ī ṛ ṝ u ū ḷ ḹ aṃ aḥ svāhā, likewise. Then with oṃ a ā e ai ar ār o au al ā la aṃ aḥ svāhā, likewise. Then with oṃ ha hā ya yā ra rā va vā la lā haṃ haḥ svāhā, likewise. Thus five hundred plus forty is spoken of as five hundred.

For pacifying and the others, there are the colors of crystal, topaz, bandhūka and molten gold; the scents of mālatī, camphor, aloewood and campaka; the sounds of lute and zither, flute, mṛdaṅga drum, and exceedingly fierce sounds; and the forms of the śrīvatsa, the parasol, the sword, the lotus, the vase and so on.

One, two, three, four and so on are auspicious. Flames that flicker violently, that bear black spots, that throw off sparks, that turn toward one's own direction, that blaze toward the left, that are of various colors, or that are very black, together with crackling sounds and thunderous sounds, are auspicious for the rite of killing.

Then the grains and the other substances are offered by the mantra procedure given before, with the filling ladle as well. If there is no ladle, turn the left hand palm upward, stir with the thumb of the right hand that makes the offering, and cast the substances described before into the fire. For pacifying, the mantra ends in namaḥ. For increasing, it ends in vauṣaṭa. For killing, expelling and dividing, it ends in hūṃ. For subjugating, it is called vaṣat. For nailing with the ritual dagger, for paralyzing and for stupefying, one should recite the mantra with phaṭ attached at its end, and cast the offerings in.

Then, with the mantra hkṣmlvryaṃ, the ladle is filled with ghee, and the master, in meditative equipoise, together with the ritual assistant (karmavajra), should offer the full oblation to the fire god.

Then flowers and the rest are given to the ten directions with their mantras, namely: oṃ bhur batu basvaḥ, namo debebhyaḥ, svasti prajābhyaḥ, svāhā rājabhyaḥ, svādhā pitṛbhyaḥ, alaṃ bhūtebhyaḥ, vaṣaḍendrūya.

Having offered flowers and the rest in this way, one offers water for rinsing the mouth with oṃ āḥ hūṃ kaya vāg citta svabhāva śuddha svāhā.

Then, with oṃ jaḥ gaccha mahra rasmiḥ svāsthānaṃ santripta ho punanāgamyiṣṭa saya devāhvanayami svāhā, the wisdom fire god is requested to depart into the lotus of the heart, and the fire god of the hearth is likewise requested to depart. This is the sequence of the fire offering rite.

Then the master, having made offerings as before, takes up the vajra and bell and sits in the vajra posture. He requests the wisdom beings to depart into his own lotus, in order, into the cakras of the crown protuberance, the forehead, the throat, the heart, the navel and the secret place. The letters a i ri u ḷ haṃ, the totalities beginning with emptiness, the guardians of the directions, the planets, the nāgas, the bhūtas and the hosts of wrathful deities are requested to depart.

Then the pairs of cloth from the vases are to be given to the maidens who guard them. With a leaf of the bodhi tree, or with one's own hand, or with the vajra, one should make them depart, from the paths of the Brahmā lines up to the wheel of great bliss. Then, placing a little of the colored powder on the crown of one's own head, one should gather up the lotus and the rest.

Then the colored powder is swept up and poured into a vase, which, according to one's means, is placed on an elephant or the like. It is carried along with parasols, victory banners and so on, and should be cast into a river that flows to the ocean. That same vase is filled with water and brought back to the maṇḍala house, where the water is sprinkled, and the floor is also smeared with cow dung and the like. Staying there, one pleases the maidens with white foods such as milk, with sweets, and with pairs of cloth, and requests them to depart. At night, by the rite given before, the feast of the heroes should be given.
