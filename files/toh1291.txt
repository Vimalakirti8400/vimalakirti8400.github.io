The Means of Accomplishment of the Blessed Hevajra: The Stages of Clear Realization Called Thoroughly Illuminating the Meaning (Bhagavaddhevajrasādhanābhisamayakramārthaprasannanāma)

The Means of Accomplishing the Blessed Hevajra, the Stages of Clear Realization Called Thoroughly Illuminating the Meaning, known in Sanskrit as the Bhagavad Hevajra Sādhana Abhisamaya Kramārtha Prasanna and in Tibetan as Chomden De Kyé Dorjé Drubpé Tab Ngönpar Tokpé Rimpa Dön Rabtu Salwa, is a short Indian ritual manual, or sādhana, for the meditative practice of Hevajra, a principal deity of the highest class of Buddhist tantra. Opening with homage to Hevajra as one whose wisdom burns away all conceptual thought, it guides the yogin step by step through visualizing himself as the deity, generating the mandala and its goddesses, the inner fire of the completion stage, and the symbolic meaning of the deity's faces, arms, ornaments and attributes, before closing with mantra recitation, a torma offering to the gods, guardians of the directions and nāgas, and verses of aspiration. It is Toh 1291 in the Tengyur, where it belongs among the commentaries and practice texts of the tantra section connected with the Hevajra cycle.

In the language of India: bhagavān hevajra sādhanaṃ abhisamaya kramārṭha prasana nāma. In the language of Tibet: The Means of Accomplishing the Blessed Hevajra, Called "The Stages of Realization That Make the Meaning Fully Clear."

Homage to the Blessed One, glorious Hevajra.

To Hevajra, who with wisdom like fire
burns away every conceptual thought without exception
and gathers beings in with the water of compassion,
I pay homage, and for the sake of those of lesser understanding than myself
I compose this sādhana that makes the meaning fully clear.

First, the yogin sits on a comfortable seat in a pleasing place such as a charnel ground. Clearly visualizing himself as Hevajra, he imagines at his heart a lotus arising from paṃ, upon it a sun disc arising from raṃ, and upon that a blue hūṃ. Light radiates from it and goes out through the right nostril, inviting Hevajra together with his retinue, who are seated in the sky before him. Having worshipped them with various outer offerings, he should worship them with Gaurī holding a vessel of bodhicitta, Caurī holding a vessel of self-arisen flowers, Vetālī holding a vessel of urine, Ghasmarī holding a vessel of excrement, Pukkasī holding a vessel of various kinds of meat, Śabarī holding a vessel of liquor, Caṇḍālī beating a hand drum, and Ḍombī offering an embrace.

Then he should perform the confession of wrongdoing and the rest:

Whatever I have done with body, speech, and mind
through wrong conceptual thought
since beginningless saṃsāra,
I confess each one, and from now on I will refrain.
The accumulations of merit and wisdom
that beings have gathered for the sake of liberation,
in these I sincerely rejoice,
and I dedicate them to supreme awakening.
In Hevajra, the nature of all,
in the supreme path that he taught,
and in the supreme assembly, his gathered retinue,
I go for refuge always.
I shall generate the supreme awakening mind,
which clears away all faults without exception,
which increases every good quality,
and is like a wish-fulfilling jewel and the like.
From today until I attain omniscience
I shall offer my body
to the Sugatas and their heirs.
I shall abide on the sublime path.

Then he should meditate on loving-kindness, compassion, joy, and equanimity. After that, having meditated on emptiness, he should say: oṃ śūnyatā jñāna vajra svabhāva ātmako 'haṃ.

From this meditation on emptiness, from paṃ comes a variegated lotus, upon it from raṃ a sun disc, and upon that from hūṃ a crossed vajra, at whose hub is hūṃ. Light radiates from it and becomes a vajra fence and canopy, which should be consecrated with oṃ rakṣa rakṣa hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ svāhā. Within it he should imagine a white source of phenomena (dharmodaya) standing with its mouth upward. Within that, from yaṃ comes a blue wind maṇḍala shaped like a bow and marked with banners; upon it from raṃ a red, triangular fire maṇḍala marked with a vajra; upon it from vaṃ a white, round water maṇḍala marked with a vase; upon it from laṃ a yellow, square earth maṇḍala marked with a vajra; and upon that from bhūṃ a wheel. He should think of its nature as the habitual tendencies of the intermediate state. These melt into light, and from it arises a palace with four sides, adorned with four doors and four porticos, adorned with nets and half-nets, and beautified by eight pillars. In its center, from paṃ comes a red eight-petaled lotus, upon it from raṃ a sun disc, and upon that he should meditate on the forms of the four māras lying crossed. In the four directions he should place the four: Brahmā, Indra, Upendra, and Rudra. In the four intermediate directions he should set out Yama, Kubera, Nairṛti, and Vemacitrin, and consecrate them with this: oṃ sarva tathāgata alaya vajra svabhāva ātmako 'haṃ.

Then at the heart of the four māras, from a ā i ī u ū ṛ ṝ ḷ ḹ e ai o au aṃ aḥ comes a moon disc: this is the mirror-like wisdom. Upon it, from ka kha ga gha ṅa ca cha ja jha ña ṭa ṭha ḍa ḍha ṇa ta tha da dha na pa pha ba bha ma ya ra la va śa ṣa sa ha kṣaḥ and dha ya ra comes a sun disc: this is the wisdom of equality. Between the moon and the sun, from hūṃ and a come a vajra and a curved knife: this is the discriminating wisdom. Their blending like water and milk is the all-accomplishing wisdom. From that come oṃ and hūṃ, and those two become the vajra and the curved knife.

Arising from them is the Blessed One, with eight faces and sixteen arms, possessing four legs, his head encircled by a garland of skulls, embraced around the neck by Nairātmyā, wearing the five mudrās. His body is white, his right face blue, his left face red, and his rear face black. In the eight skull cups of his right hands he holds in order an elephant, a horse, a donkey, an ox, a camel, a man, a lion, and a cat. In the eight skull cups of his left hands he holds the god of earth, the god of water, the god of fire, the god of wind, the moon, the sun, Yama, and Kubera. The goddess has one face and two arms; she is black, her yellow hair bristles upward, and she holds a curved knife in her right hand and a skull cup in her left. She is the wisdom of the utterly pure dharmadhātu.

Then he should consecrate the three places of the goddess: oṃ at the forehead, hūṃ at the heart, and āḥ at the secret place. The mantra for consecrating the lotus is: oṃ padma sukha dhara mahā rāga sukhaṃ data catur ānandha bhaga visva hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ karyaṃ kuruṣva me. The mantra for consecrating the vajra is: oṃ vajra mahā dveṣa catur ānanda daya kakhaga mukhe karāśo nātha hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ karyaṃ kuruṣva me.

Then, roused by the sound of the father and mother's play of great passion, all the tathāgatas of the ten directions, as the five syllables bhrūṃ oṃ jrīṃ khaṃ hūṃ in the form of bodhicitta, enter by the father's mouth and come out by the path of the vajra, becoming eight drops. At the moment they stream into the lotus, he should recite this mantra: oṃ sarva tathāgata anurāgaṇa vajra svabhāva ātmako 'haṃ. From the eight drops come the seeds gaṃ caṃ vaṃ ghaṃ paṃ śaṃ laṃ ḍaṃ, and from these seeds come the eight emblems of the right hands. These become the eight goddesses. Black Gaurī, holding a curved knife in her right hand and a fish in her left, should be placed in the east upon Brahmā. Likewise, in the south, upon Indra, is Caurī, the color of madder, holding a hand drum in her right hand and a boar in her left.

Vetālī, reddish yellow like the color of refined gold, holding a turtle in her right hand and a skull cup in her left, is in the west upon Upendra. Ghasmarī, green in color, holding a snake in her right hand and a skull cup in her left, is in the north upon Rudra. Pukkasī, blue in color, holding a lion in her right hand and an axe in her left, is in the northeastern quarter upon Yama. Śabarī, white in body, holding a monk in her right hand and a ringing staff in her left, is in the southeastern quarter upon Kubera. Caṇḍālī, the color of the sky, holding a wheel in her right hand and a skull cup in her left, is in the southwestern quarter upon Nairṛti. Ḍombī, of many colors, holding a vajra in her right hand and making the threatening gesture with her left, should be placed in the northwestern quarter upon Vemacitrin. These goddesses stand in the half-crossed dancing posture, have three eyes, and are adorned with the five mudrās.

Then, roused by the sound of the father and mother's play, the five syllables ho oṃ āḥ hūṃ hoḥ, through the habitual tendencies of the intermediate state, enter through the mouth. As soon as they reach the lotus, father and mother both become a ball of light, and the goddesses of the intermediate quarters rouse him with songs:

With a heart of compassion, Lord, arise!
Protect me, Pukkasī.
Abandon the nature of emptiness
and join me in the yoga of great bliss.
Parted from you, I shall die;
Hevajra, arise!
Give up the nature of emptiness
and accomplish the purpose of Śabarī.
Chief who longs to make the world his guest,
why do you remain in emptiness?
I, Caṇḍālī, make this request:
if you do not rise, I am lost.
I long for your heart;
you who are mindful of the senses, arise!
I, Ḍombī, have lost my senses;
do not cut off the stream of your compassion!

Roused in this way, he remembers his former aspirations and arises from the form of the seminal fluid as described above, except that his body is black, his right face white, and his left face red; he is seated in the half-crossed posture, wears a necklace of a garland of heads, and bears a crossed vajra on the crown of his head.

Then one should imagine at the heart a lotus from paṃ, upon it a sun disc from raṃ, and upon that a vajra from hūṃ. From that comes a wisdom being like oneself. At its heart too are a lotus, a sun, and a vajra from hūṃ, with hūṃ at its hub. Then, saying oṃ vajra gaurī ākarṣaya jaḥ, one invites the wisdom maṇḍala into the sky in front and worships it with outer, inner, and secret offerings. Saying oṃ vajra caurī praveśaya hūṃ, one should make it enter. Saying oṃ vajra vetālī sphoṭaya vaṃ, one should bind it. Saying oṃ vajra ghasmarī vaśaṃ kuru hoḥ, one should make it delighted.

When the wisdom beings have thus been absorbed, one should consecrate the sense bases. Mohavajrā, Rāgavajrā, Īrṣyāvajrā, and Nairātmyayoginī should be generated on the eyes, ears, nose, tongue, body, and mind from the first letters of their names, maṃ daṃ maṃ raṃ saṃ naṃ. Each has one face and two arms, is of many colors, holds a curved knife in the right hand and a skull cup in the left, and has a corpse as her seat. From the three syllables oṃ āḥ hūṃ come the three, Khecarī, Bhūcarī, and Pātālavāsinī, white, red, and black.

Goddess arisen from the body family,
yoginī Khecarī,
having blessed them,
I pray you, grant your blessing.

oṃ sarva tathāgata pūjari vajra svabhāva ātmako 'haṃ

Goddess arisen from the mind family,
yoginī Pātālavāsinī,
I pray you, abide now as my blessing.

oṃ sarva tathāgata vattālī vajra svabhāva ātmako 'haṃ

Then, with hook-like rays of light from the seed at one's own heart, one invites all the buddhas of the ten directions, worships them with the three offerings, outer, inner, and secret, and should request empowerment:

Blessed tathāgatas,
protectors, empower me!

Then those tathāgatas take the form of Heruka and confer empowerment with vases filled with the five nectars. One should imagine Akṣobhya as the crown ornament of Caurī, Gaurī, and Pukkasī; Vairocana for Caurī and Śabarī; Ratnasambhava for Vetālī and Caṇḍālī; and Amitābha for Ghasmarī and Ḍombī. oṃ sarva tatāgata abhiṣekata vajra svabhāva ātmako 'haṃ

Then, upon wind from yaṃ and fire from raṃ, there is a skull cup from a. Within it, at the center of a moon and a sun, is a vajra from hūṃ, with hūṃ at its hub. Within the moon one generates the five buddhas from bhrūṃ āṃ jrāṃ khaṃ hūṃ and the five beings from go ku da ha na. The fire blazes and these melt into light. Light radiates from the hūṃ at the vajra's hub, draws in the nectar of the ten directions, and it dissolves into them. One consecrates it with the three syllables oṃ āḥ hūṃ, imagines on the tongues of the deities a vajra the size of a barley grain, and should taste the nectar. oṃ sarva tathāgata amṛta svadana vajra svabhāva ātmako 'haṃ

Then the eight goddesses emanate, and, gathering bodhicitta and the rest as described above, one should worship the maṇḍala, beginning with the principal deity. oṃ sarvva tathāgata pūja vajra svabhāva ātmako 'haṃ

Then those goddesses offer praise:

You are encircled by the yoginīs;
by your own mind your intention is fulfilled.
You accomplish all saṃsāra, moving and unmoving;
with a heart of compassion you bring beings to awakening.
You accomplish the mind that unfolds as illusion,
Vajra Heruka, glorious one, mind of delight.

oṃ sarva tathāgata stuti vajra svabhāva ātmako 'haṃ

Meditating in this way, there first appears something like clouds, second something like smoke, third something like the flash of lightning, fourth something like a lamp, and fifth something like a cloudless sky in which all is clear. Afterward everything becomes like an illusion and a dream.

Then one should dwell on the four cakras:

By kindling the inner fire at the navel,
the five tathāgatas are burned,
Locanā and the others are burned too,
and through this burning the moon drips from haṃ.

This is to be learned from the guru's own mouth.

Then one should recollect the purity of the deity. The red eyes are compassion. The black limbs are loving-kindness. The four legs are the four means of attracting disciples. The eight faces are the eight liberations. The sixteen arms are the sixteen emptinesses.

The wheel, the earrings, the necklace, the bracelets and the girdle are Akṣobhya, Amitābha, Ratnasambhava, Vairocana and Amoghasiddhi, in that order. The wrathful aspect is the taming of the wicked. The three eyes are the three bodies of a buddha. The bones are the Four Truths. The aggregates of form, feeling, perception, formations and consciousness are Vajrā, Secret Gaurī, Vāriyoginī, Vajraḍākinī and Nairātmyā. Earth, water, fire and wind are Pukkasī, Śabarī, Caṇḍālī and Ḍombī. Forms, sounds, smells, tastes, tangibles and phenomena are Gaurī, Caurī, Ghasmarī, Bhūcarī and Khecarī. That the eight skull cups in the left hands hold the eight beginning with Earth signifies bringing about perfect abundance of requisites. That the skull cups in the right hands hold the elephant and the rest signifies pacifying lung disease, difficulty in breathing, madness, vomiting of blood, leprosy, pox, diseases of the spleen and diseases of the liver.

When one grows weary of meditative concentration, one should do mantra recitation:

oṃ aṣṭānanāya piṃgalordhvakeśavartamane caturbiṃśatinetrāya ṣoḍaśabhujāya kṛṣṇajīmūtavapuṣe kapālamālānekādhāriṇe adhamātakrūracittāya ardhendudaṃṣṭrīṇe māraya māraya kāraya kāraya garjaya garjaya tarjaya tarjaya śoṣaya śoṣaya sabhasāgarān bandha bandha nāgāṣṭakān gṛhna gṛhna śatrūṃnaṃ ha hā hi hī hu hū he hai ho lau haṃ haḥ phaṭ svāhā

Or else:

oṃ devapicuvajra hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ svāhā

One should recite this continuously.

Then, as before, bless the torma into nectar. In the sky in front, within a source of phenomena (dharmodaya), upon an eight-petaled lotus in three tiers, white, black and red, arrange the eight gods, the eight guardians of the directions and the eight nāgas, arising from oṃ, hūṃ and āḥ. Dissolve the wisdom deities into them and bless their body, speech and mind. Imagining a vajra upon the tongue, send the offering forth with this mantra:

oṃ inda jama jala jakkha bhūta pahni ba u rakṣa canda sujja māda pappa talapātāle aṭṭa sappa svāhā idaṃ baliṃ bhuñja jigha phulla dhūppa māṃsa piṃgha ambha sappa kajja sāda khanti khuṇi pheḍa gāda oṃ akāro mukhaṃ sarvva dharmāṇāṃ ādyanutpannatvāta oṃ āḥ hūṃ phaṭ svāhā

At the end of the meditation, the aspiration is this:

Born into a good family and free of madness,
keeping the sacred commitments, a holder of Hevajra,
serving and honoring the guru, endowed with compassion,
so may I be born, and born again.

My hands ringing the vajra and bell,
reciting this profound Dharma,
and rightly partaking of the consort's fluid,
so may I be born, and born again.

Though I am not endowed with wisdom,
though distracted by an ocean of formations and of little understanding,
I have composed this sādhana with the wish to benefit;
by this, may all beings swiftly attain Hevajra.

The means of accomplishment of the Blessed One Hevajra, the stages of clear realization called Thoroughly Illuminating the Meaning, is complete.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The verb rendered "should be placed" for Gaurī is gdon par bya, read as dgod par bya ("should be set out"). "Self-arisen flowers" is the usual tantric code for menstrual blood. "Five mudrās" are the five bone ornaments. The two mantras for consecrating lotus and vajra, and the letters "dha ya ra," are given exactly as the Tibetan spells them.  The list of goddesses for the sense bases names only four, though six organs and six seed syllables are given; the Tibetan is translated as it stands. Likewise only two of the three goddesses born from oṃ āḥ hūṃ receive verses. In the empowerment, Caurī is named twice, once in transliteration (tsau ri) with Akṣobhya and once in translation (chom rkun ma) with Vairocana; this may be a scribal confusion. "The moon" renders ri bong, "the hare," a standard figure for the moon (the drop of bodhicitta). "Go ku da ha na" are the seed syllables of the five kinds of flesh.  The text lists six sense objects (forms through phenomena) but only five goddesses; the list is translated as it stands. "Secret Gaurī" renders gsang ba'i gau rī, and "Vāriyoginī" renders the abbreviated chu ma, following the standard Hevajra correlation of the aggregates with Vajrā, Gaurī, Vāriyoginī, Vajraḍākinī and Nairātmyā.
