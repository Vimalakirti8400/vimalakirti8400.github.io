The Means of Accomplishing Kurukullā

The Means of Accomplishing Kurukullā, known in Sanskrit as a Kurukullā sādhana and in Tibetan as Kurukullé Druppé Tap, is a brief tantric practice text, written largely in verse, devoted to the red goddess Kurukullā, "she whose form is supreme joy," the deity of magnetizing. After homage and a promise to explain her practice according to the tantra, it leads the yogin through offerings, confession, refuge, rejoicing and dedication, the four immeasurables, and the meditation on emptiness, then into the visualization of the four-armed dancing goddess with arrow, hook, utpala and bow, the recitation of her mantra, and the numbers of recitations said to bring kings, gods and the three worlds under control. Its colophon attributes it to the great master Kṛṣṇavajra and names Yeshé Gyaltsen as its translator. It is Toh 1320 in the Tengyur, where it belongs among the tantra commentaries as one of the sādhanas, or deity-practice texts, of that section.

Homage to the Blessed Lady Kurukullā.

Great goddess Kurukullā,
You whose form is supreme joy,
For the accomplishment of every wished-for aim,
With true reverence I bow to you.

I, a teacher who longs for wisdom,
For the sake of my disciples' merit,
Shall explain her sādhana in brief,
Following the sequence of the tantra.

First of all, the yogin should take his seat in a shrine room, make a maṇḍala of fine fragrance over the whole ground, and visualize before him the guru, the buddhas and bodhisattvas, and the form of the goddess. He should worship them properly with red flowers, lamps and the like, and perform the confession of wrongdoing and the rest.

All the wrongs I have committed I confess.
One should go for refuge to the Three Jewels.
I rejoice in the merit of beings.
I dedicate it wholly to the awakening of a buddha.

First one should practice the meditative concentration of loving-kindness;
Likewise, second, one cultivates compassion;
Third, one should cultivate joy;
After all of these, equanimity.

After that one should recite this mantra:
oṃ śunyatā jñāna vajra svabhāva ādmako'haṃ
One meditates on intrinsic nature as empty.
One recalls one's former aspirations.

Wisdom, she whose form is the letter e,
Should be imagined in the element of space.
A lovely red lotus of eight petals
One visualizes upon it.

Complete with a sun maṇḍala;
And what one visualizes fully upon that
Is the supreme seed, the syllable hrīḥ.
She whose form is supreme joy,

Red in body color, blazing with light rays,
Her body adorned with four arms:
On the right she holds the arrow and the hook,
Likewise on the left the utpala and the bow.

The goddess wears a crown of skulls,
Her body adorned with every ornament.
As one whose very self is her form,
One meditates in true yoga.

She whose form is supreme joy,
One imagines in the mind as a dancer.
With the secret offerings, khāsu and the rest,
Encircling the one in the center,

Presenting arghaṃ and the rest,
One should make various offerings according to the rite.
With light rays from the seed at the heart,
One fills all directions completely.

One makes beings into one's own form,
And likewise draws them into oneself.
Then one should recite with the mantra,
Using a rosary of one's own color.

Outside, and likewise within oneself,
One meditates on emanating and gathering.
oṃ kurukulle sarba satāna me vasamānaya hrīḥ svāhā

By practicing this very meditation,
The three worlds come under control.
With a hundred thousand, a king comes under control;
With ten thousand, the world comes under control;

Birds and cattle with ten million;
With seven hundred thousand, the asuras;
With two hundred thousand, the gods;
With a hundred, a minister comes under control.

In this way the recitation of the great vidyā
Should be done day and night, in every activity.
With particular offerings of flowers and the like
One should properly worship oneself.

One should fully dedicate to awakening.
One should hold the pride of being her.
And then one may act at ease, as one pleases.

This means of accomplishing Kurukullā
Was composed by a learned one of vajra love,
To accomplish the benefit of beings,
And because one with a vajra name requested it.

By the merit of having done so,
May all worlds in the ocean of saṃsāra
Become she whose form is supreme joy,
And attain perfect bliss.

The Means of Accomplishing Kurukullā, composed by the great master Kṛṣṇavajra, is complete. It was translated by Yeshé Gyaltsen.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

"A learned one of vajra love" and "one with a vajra name" (རྡོ་རྗེ་བརྩེ་བ, རྡོ་རྗེ་མཚན) may be the author's own epithet and the name of the person who requested the work; since neither can be identified with confidence, they are translated literally. The term khāsu for the secret offerings is obscure and is given as written.
