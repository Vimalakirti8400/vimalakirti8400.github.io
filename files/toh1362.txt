The Coemergent Means of Accomplishment of Glorious Kālacakra, Generated in an Instant (Śrīkālacakrariṭitisahajasādhanaṃ-nāma)

The Coemergent Means of Accomplishment of Glorious Kālacakra, Generated in an Instant, known in Sanskrit as the Śrī Kālacakra Sahaja Sādhana and in Tibetan as Pal Dükyi Khorlo Kechik Gi Kyepé Lhenchik Kyepé Druptap, is a brief tantric meditation manual, or sādhana, for the practice of the deity Kālacakra. After paying homage to the body of great bliss and to the Ādibuddha, the first buddha, it guides the practitioner to arise instantly from emptiness as Kālacakra in union with his consort Viśvamātā, then to dissolve the three realms and oneself step by step into luminosity, and to emanate and withdraw the deity again as the path of union in meditative equipoise. Composed by the master Lalitavajra and translated into Tibetan by the paṇḍita Vāgīśvara and the translator Chörab, it is Toh 1362 in the Tengyur, where it belongs among the commentaries and practice texts on the tantras.

In the language of India: śrī kālacakra riṭiti sahaja sādhanāṃ nāma
In the language of Tibet: The Coemergent Means of Accomplishment of Glorious Kālacakra, Generated in an Instant.

Homage to the body of great bliss.

Fully embraced by the Blessed Lady Prajñā, who is with aspects and without aspects,
free of laughter and the rest, in changeless bliss that has utterly abandoned arising and perishing,
the one who brings forth the buddhas, possessing the three bodies, who truly knows the three times:
to that omniscient one, the supreme first buddha (Ādibuddha), the nondual, I pay homage.

First, in a single instant, abide in emptiness; then, likewise in an instant, generate
the glorious lord Kālacakra, standing upon the maṇḍalas of a multicolored lotus, moon, sun and Rāhu,
his right leg extended upon the hearts of Īśvara (Śiva) and the god of desire (Kāmadeva), deep blue in color,
his two hands holding vajra and bell, fully embracing the body of the consort Viśvamātā.
His mass of matted locks, like a dark cloud, like the tamāla tree, is adorned with Akṣobhya and the others;
he is richly adorned with the six mudrās as well, and bound with a garland of white skulls.
Nondual with the glorious Viśvamātā, who wears a necklace of human heads, has three eyes,
a body like gold, and two hands holding a curved knife and a lotus vessel,
he is to be meditated upon as having the nature of great bliss, and so forth.

Then the three realms, all of them endowed with the same form as oneself, dissolve without remainder into the body of the goddess.
She in turn dissolves into oneself; one's own body together with its seat into the sun; the sun into hūṃ.
The ū dissolves into the ha; that in turn into the head; that into the crescent moon.
The crescent moon into the drop; that into the nāda; and that likewise into space, like breath striking a mirror.
Having thus entered luminosity, one again recalls oneself in a form like the former one,
and in an instant one should send forth various emanations through the three realms, pervading space.
Then, in the same way, withdraw them again, and again generate and send them forth in just the same way.
By this path of union in meditative equipoise,
one will truly go to the feet of the Buddha.

By whatever merit I have gained
in composing this means of accomplishment of glorious Kālacakra,
may worldly beings too attain the coemergent,
the feet of nondual nature.

The Coemergent Means of Accomplishment of Glorious Kālacakra, composed by the master Lalitavajra, is complete. It was translated by the paṇḍita Vāgīśvara and the translator Chörab.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The consort's second implement is written "lotus vessel" (pad snod); the usual iconography of Viśvamātā has a skull cup here, which may be what is meant. In the closing lines, "feet" (zhabs) carries the sense of pada, the state or station of buddhahood. The "laughter and the rest" of the homage verse refers to the modes of desire (laughing, gazing, holding hands, embracing) that the Kālacakra tradition says are transcended in changeless bliss.
