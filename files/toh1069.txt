Homage to the Tathāgata Ratnaketurāja

Toh 1069 in the Kangyur is a very brief dhāraṇī text, a short incantation of homage addressed to the Blessed One Ratnaketurāja, the "Jewel-Banner King," a tathāgata and perfectly awakened buddha. The source carries no separate formal title, so its Sanskrit and Tibetan names are best taken simply from this invocation of Ratnaketurāja. The text consists of a single mantra that salutes this buddha and then repeatedly invokes the jewel, ratna, calling on the great jewel and the jewel of victory before closing with the customary svāhā. It ends with a one-line instruction that the practitioner should recite the formula eight times as an act of homage. Like many similar short works, it belongs to the class of dhāraṇī texts within the Tantra division of the Kangyur, where brief spells and recitations of this kind are gathered.

oṃ namo bhagavate ratnaketurājāya tathāgatāya arta te saṃmyaksaṃbuddhaya tadyathā oṃ ratne ratne mahārathe ratnavijaye svāhā

Reciting this eight times, one should pay homage.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The dhāraṇī is addressed to the tathāgata Ratnaketurāja. The closing instruction may mean either reciting the formula eight times while prostrating, or reciting it eight times and then prostrating.
