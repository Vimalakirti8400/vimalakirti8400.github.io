The Glorious Bindu of Mahāmudrā, Sovereign Lord of the King of Tantras of the Great Yoginī (Śrī Mahāmudrātilaka Nāma Mahāyoginī Tantra Rāja Adhipati)

Toh 420 in the Kangyur is a tantra known in Sanskrit as the Śrī Mahāmudrātilaka and in Tibetan as Dpal Phyag rgya chen po'i thig le, which may be rendered into English as the Glorious Bindu of Mahāmudrā. It belongs to the class of Yoginī tantras — among the highest yoga tantras — and takes the form of a dialogue in which a ḍākinī questions Hevajra on the ultimate nature of reality, focusing especially on the esoteric doctrine of the bindu, or sacred drop, and its relationship to mahāmudrā, the great seal of awakening. The text presents itself as a sovereign among tantric scriptures, situating its teachings on the six yogic unions and the nature of suchness within the framework of the Hevajra cycle. As Toh 420 in the Kangyur, it is preserved among the Tibetan Buddhist canonical scriptures transmitted from the Indian tantric tradition.

In the language of India: Śrī Mahāmudrātilaka Nāma Mahāyoginī Tantra Rāja Adhipati. In the language of Tibet: The Glorious Bindu of Mahāmudrā, sovereign lord of the king of tantras of the great yoginī. Homage to the Glorious Bindu of Mahāmudrā.

O Blessed One, the ḍākinī who is wisdom itself,
who opens the heart of the secret lotus,
who dwells within the navel of the secret lotus —
be present as I put my questions to you.

O Hevajra, ocean of wisdom,
whose nature streams through five hundred thousand
and who yourself have declared suchness —
the teaching of the yoginī of the six yogic unions,
the supreme sovereign of omniscience —
that hero I recognize.

Yet what is the ultimate truth?
On that point doubt has arisen in me.
The drop, the bindu, and their mode —
the supreme beyond the merely supreme mode,
discerned through the six yogic unions:
where does it dwell?
Whence does vitality arise?
Where does the vajra-holder dissolve?
What precisely is the vajra-night?
What is the dawn that has arisen?
Where are the time and channel of setting,
and how many are the supreme sovereign?
Tell me the defining marks of time.
What does time accomplish in its aim?
What is the practice of the vowels and consonants?
Whence does the sovereign lord arise?
Teach the distinctions of taste,
and likewise of the joys.
The joys that belong to their respective times —
declare them, O sovereign of the vajra.
How is the dharmadhātu explained?
How, again, is the vajra taught?
How is the sound of the self declared?
What is called the secret, and how so?
What is called the supreme, and how?
Make clear the meaning of delight.
Teach the distinctions of the families.
What are the aggregates and sense sources?
Brahmā, Viṣṇu, the Wrathful One,
the lord who is always peaceful —
whence do they arise and where do they abide?
How is the great of the great?
Teach some of the distinctions of the mudrā.
What is Mahāmudrā, and how?
What distinctions of offering are there for the protector?
What is the flower therein?
What kind of offering is sought
for the Great Protector?
What is the Blessed One like
in the maṇḍala of supreme things?
What are the goddesses therein?
Of what sort are the goddesses said to be?
What charnel ground does the Blessed One inhabit?
How are the trees therein described?
The charnel grounds and their trees —
let the sovereign of the Victors declare them.
The protector's inner fire offering,
the rite of the five nectars,
the assembly places of the yoginīs,
and what sound is uttered there —
the very self-nature of the self:
O Great Protector, declare these.

This has been the first chapter, entitled "The Cause of All Tantras," from the Glorious Bindu of Mahāmudrā.

The Blessed One then declared:

What you have asked is profound and difficult to fathom —
the supreme essence of the essence,
the great secret of all buddhas,
the supreme beyond the supreme, the truly supreme.
The Great God (Śiva) and the rest,
and O Bhaṅgamā — she is a beast of the field.
In the ultimate, you alone
are the vessel, O fair-faced one.
The secret of secrets, the greatly delightful —
listen: I shall explain it truly.

First, by conferring the empowerments upon the student once,
one becomes at that very moment
a certain vessel for the explanation of the great secret.
Without empowerment there is no spiritual attainment —
like pressing sand in hopes of butter.
Whoever, through pride in tantra and scripture,
gives elucidation to one who has not been empowered:
both master and student die forthwith,
and though attainment may have been reached, they go to hell.
Therefore with every effort
request from the guru the meaning of empowerment.
There being empowerment and knowing suchness itself,
wisdom and great compassion,
genuine greatness, heroism,
freedom from miserliness, and a good nature —

One who is fortunate possesses all the defining marks complete:
envy's domain utterly abandoned,
a mind ever possessed of joy,
a mind of singular great desire.

When such a one knows the vow,
then upon such a guru should one rely.
A guru of that kind
should examine the student with careful effort,

just as gold is tested by burning and the rest,
just as a jewel is tested by an expert,
just as a maiden is tested for good health —
in just that way should the student be examined.

Therefore, with every effort,
let each examine the other in turn.
If this is not done, fault arises,
and spiritual attainment will never be reached.

Just as poison, when thoroughly purified by mantra,
is transformed into nectar —
so the student, through empowerment and the rest,
comes to have a body free of stain.

One who confers empowerment
should be regarded as Vajrasattva himself.
The guru should be regarded as an emanation king.
The glorious guru should be regarded as fire.
The guru should be regarded as surpassing even the Teacher.
Regard him as equal to a wish-fulfilling jewel.

Just as, through the ritual union and mantra,
from the crown of the emanation king
one finds jewels beyond measure,
and all poverty is made to cease —
but through wrong ritual union and wrong mantra,
the consort (mudrā) is surely burned —
so too through such a ritual,
through conduct such as the vows and the rest,
the very face of glory, the rich lord of empowerment,
can be found in this very life.

But if it is not so — if there is no reverence —
the fire of speech surely burns one.
Just as fire reduces wood to ash
in a mere instant,
so likewise, if the glorious guru is pleased,
sins are purified in an instant.

In this tantra the supreme is this:
there is a Blessed One who confers empowerment.

The Blessed One declared:
In the sequence of the four empowerments,
three are taught by way of result.
First is the master empowerment — these are one.
Second is the secret empowerment — the supreme.
Third is the wisdom-awareness empowerment.
And in like manner so is the fourth.

The master empowerment is divided into eleven.
The secret empowerment: twelve in the supreme.
The wisdom-awareness: the thirteenth.
And in like manner the fourteenth.

Each empowerment corresponds to one ground,
and those are the lords and sovereigns of the grounds.
This is to be known through the three root causes,
and the result thereof is the fourth.

Or else the result is the separation into three,
or else it is the very three identities.
The distinctions of joy are to be known
in sequence through the cakras.

On the third day of the waxing moon,
one should confer empowerment upon the supreme student.
With a fair face, long of eye,
adorned with a beautiful form in full bloom —
twelve years of age, or sixteen,
or if unavailable, one who has reached twenty is also suitable.
One who is over twenty has a different mudrā,
and that makes spiritual attainment remote.

A sister, or a daughter,
or a wife should be offered to the guru.
Then with various substances,
with faith, one should make offering to the guru:
an excellent variety of hundreds and more,
various precious victory banners supreme,
pearl garlands of priceless worth —
with countless gold should offering be made.

"From this day forward I am your servant" —
with the fourfold great offering of a great lord,
with such a ritual,
with effort one should make offering to the guru.

The guru's form is one's own self.
Having made offering to oneself, one attains spiritual attainment.
Then in that instant, with the great mudrā,
having made offering, one should perform the approach practice.

Whatever arises from that approach practice
should be let down into the student's mouth.
With the thick tip applied at the edge,
it should be brought within and enjoyed.

At that time, authentic primordial wisdom is born.
Such as the bliss of the maiden —
equalizing breath and effort,
one should make offering through supreme delight.

Place the syllables HŪṂ and PHAṬ upon the jewel's mouth,
then set it upon the arrow and release.
At that moment, the instant of joy
arises just as it does by nature.

In this way the mudrā is to be explained. This is the defining characteristic of joy. Then, placing it into the student's hand, one should speak these words: "By my scriptural transmission that is free of conceptuality, act in accordance with the true meaning of the lotus. In the center of the heart of the lotus, the sovereign lady of the vajra sphere (Skt. vajradhātu) abides. Until she is found, there is no awakening through any other means. She is the form of natural joy, the subtlest of the subtle — truly, she is to be worshipped."

From the Glorious Drop of the Great Mudrā (Skt. Śrī Mahāmudrātilaka): this is the second chapter, entitled "Examining the Master and Student, Presenting the Offering Fee, and Conferring Empowerment."

Then the secret is to be explained. The supreme, the most excellent, the highest of the high — the body that is the essential nature of the five aggregates, the six wheels of the sense-faculties' fine delight, the half-moon beautiful form, even as it rains — abides within the wheel of wind. It is neither half nor full, but is seen like the moon reflected in water. The great mother of illusory beings, she is served innumerably even as she rains. She is the supreme and the subtly other-supreme, unborn, non-dual, inconceivable. The mother who generates the wheel of wind — even buddhas find her difficult to find.

Within the wheel of fire there is a single drop, without letters. Everything is contained within, and all letters are within it. It pervades all things and bliss arises from it. It is that which multiplies bliss. It is free of vowels and minor marks. It is the cause that generates the countless three realms. Its nature is the abode of the sudden. It is free of all aspects. Its essence is like a serpent's tongue. It is that which generates the higher realms and the earth. It is not a syllable, nor is there any element; it is free of the ornament of lunar days and the drop. Unborn, innumerable, and inexhaustible — this is the mode of the ultimate truth.

The abode of the wheel of emanation is known as the equalization of the navel. The mere mode of the wheel of Dharma is like an emanation-eye, stainless. It is the abode of the stainless and uncontrived, without location, without aspect. Without motion and beyond expression, without the movement of breath. Its mode possesses supreme primordial wisdom; the thirteen sovereigns do not know it. It abides in the wheel of supreme enjoyment, the supreme aspect, the most excellent ambrosia, located at the boundary of the supreme vehicle — just like the power of wine. All beings are free of their lovely forms; it abides by way of a formless self. In the wheel of other-supreme great bliss it abides at the outermost limit of all. There is no viewer and nothing to be viewed; all inauspicious signs are absent. Inexpressible, inexhaustible, tranquil and present — such is the aspect of one's own delight and experiential taste. Through the kindness of the lama, one will obtain the wheel of splendid delight. Such great primordial wisdom, having the nature of the six consciousnesses, is explained as the ultimate within the center of the six wheels.

Through the union of above and below, it exists by way of joined beautiful form. Twelve and a half, and half of six, sixty-four, likewise eight, sixteen and thirty-two — this is the lotus wheel of poison.

From the Glorious Drop of the Great Mudrā: this is the third chapter, entitled "The Defining Characteristics of the Six Unions and the Individual Nature of the Six Wheels."

Listen, O goddess of great fortune! The supreme arising of the wheel of channels — seventy-two thousand channels — their classes are hereby explained. In the great tantra of the great lineage I have caused this to be clearly elaborated. Here the count of the six wheels shall be taught in brief summary.

The six channels of the wheel of wind are: the agitated, the sky, fire and hollow, the great revolver, and the greatly fearsome — those channels are the abode of the wheel of wind. The ida (Skt. iḍā), the rasanā, and the conceptuality-free channel — these three abide in a triangular arrangement. Within the wheel of fire, the five channel faculties are to be known.

The swift one, the perfectly swift, the very excellent, the excellent-going, the greatly powerful and greatly fearsome, the greatly proud and the greatly terrifying, the power-swift one, the power-portion, the power-water, the power-fragrance, the power one, the power-flowing one, the friction one, the anointing one, the many-powered, the fierce one that feeds on pride, the sky-goer, the bird-limbed one, the bird-going one, the wealthy one, the fearsome one, the roaring one, the disciplined one, the offering one, the intoxicated one, the sound one, the sound-arising one, the sinful one, the lotus-stalk one, the blue-green one, the essence one, the essence-arising one, the hair one, the opposite-facing one, the staff one, the staff-pride one, the primordial one, the swift-bellied one, the swift-hexagonal one, the seven-swift one, the diligent one, the swift-household one, the swift-mouthed one, the swift-luminous one, the swift sheep-lineage, the exhaler, the self-one, the luminous one, the stainless one, the fragrance-burning one, the cord-bellied one, the cord-joyous one, the cord one, the various-time cord one, the hollow owl, the hollow-born one, the subtle one, the leaf one, the sharp leaf, the garment-wearing one, and the illuminating one — these eight sets of eight channels are to be recalled. The sixty-four water-born ones abide in the emanation wheel of the navel. The precious one, the great lotus one, the great-tongued one, the greatly powerful one, the great-headed one, the greatly intoxicated one, the greatly joyous one, and the greatly joyless one — these eight, along with the Dharma-wheel water-born ones, flow into the ocean of the beautiful-faced heart center. The desirous one, the enjoyment one, the non-enjoyment one, the sunlight one, the luminous one, the bhaga one, the lū one, the ash-faced one, the food-giving one, the self-eating one, the hungry one, the rasāyana one, the bitter-taste one, the dripping-taste one, the taste one, the bent one — these constitute the abode of the enjoyment wheel. The twice-eight water-born petals belong to the successive class of the throat. The desirous one, the desire-conducting one, the swift-bodied one, the moon-drinking one, the moon-bellied one, the treasury one, the pleasant-voiced one, the mute one, the smoky one, the cuckoo, the hundredfold one, the withered one, the fire one, the hunter one, the moon one, the full one, the sleep one, the sleep-born one, the beer-seller, the beer-giver, the nectar one, the conch one, the conch-eared one, the conch-eyed one, the conch-nosed one, the power-rich one, the power-yellow one, the power-bewildered one, the power-fierce one, the strutting one, and the mountain-based one — these thirty-two abide in the skull, the site of the great bliss wheel, king of all wheels. It pervades all the wheels. All are subtle, without tangible substance, utterly free from apprehended and apprehender. Possessing the character of great bliss and bliss, it bestows great bliss and the rest. The arising of the elements at its root is to be understood by one who truly desires liberation. If the channel-born goddesses were to become non-arising, how could the arising of the aggregates take place? If there are no aggregates, how would the great yogins attain the great gnosis? The five aggregates are the very body of the self. They are well renowned as the five ḍākinīs. The five ḍākinīs are the five gnoses, possessed of the intrinsic nature of the five elements. If the channels themselves were to become free of the elements, then the aggregates too would be free of the elements. If there are no aggregates, the siddhi (Skt. siddhi) would not be attained by the great yogi. Therefore, with every effort, one must seek the wheel of channels. Just as rain from a cloud causes it to grow ever greater, and grants fruits — many and boundless — without doubt, so likewise is it called the tree of the aggregates: by the water of the channels it grows great, and being grown great, grants the fruit of liberation without doubt. As for the material substance of the goddesses, the channel form is their bodily form. Through the practice of joining with the element of touch and the victory-banner of the vajra, the water-born ones within the fire-wheel are stirred. When the vajra sets them in motion, the fire is absorbed within. All the water-born ones of the wheel become clearer and clearer, and the mouth becomes sealed. Then all the channels flow, like drops of water dripping from a vessel, and then they gather together at the emanation wheel and become one. From the tips of the mouths of the two channels, the moon and sun drip. The channels abide within the womb —

they exist in the form of a rope. That is called the single flowing channel. It divides into two distinct results: from the left flows the seminal fluid, and from the right flows blood. When the sun is on the right side, the bliss becomes red. Because the moon arises from the left, that bliss becomes white. The fire dwelling within those two is like the two faces of a turtle-egg. The karma-wind and consciousness from a former time, when they have entered into cyclic existence, at that moment the mind is transformed within the bhaga through great attachment. When the mother's lunar sign appears, and fire and sun appear clearly, at that moment the mind of great attachment reaches the supreme seventh touch. Having entered into the bolla and kakola, just as water is placed upon water, at that moment the seed arises. From the seed arises a lump of flesh; from that goddess the channels arise, and the fruit arises with certainty.

This concludes the fourth chapter, entitled "The Arising of the Channels of the Six Wheels from the Drop of Glorious Mahāmudrā."

Now listen, O Goddess, as I explain the manner in which the vital breath dissolves into the elements. Even before birth, it dwells within the inner secret meeting-place. That is the wisdom body (jñānakāya) free from consciousness and free from self. From that abode, the vital breath arises — the supreme vital breath of all living beings — ever dwelling as the great self, the vital breath, abiding in the five abodes. It is known as the five spaces. It dissolves into the five spaces. The four great elements are to be understood as the support of the vital breath — or alternatively, the vital breath is their support. O Goddess of Good Delight, know this. From consciousness, wind arises; from that, fire arises; from that, water arises; from that, earth arises. From all of these the aggregates arise; from there the sense bases arise; from there the nature of wisdom arises, by the sequence of one hundred and eight. This was taught by me previously in the great tantra called the Vajra Garland.

Whatever is night and whatever is day — night I shall explain. Night is known as wisdom, and wisdom becomes night. The form of the fire of night becomes the moon. The moon is of the nature of nirvāṇa. By the two wisdoms — conventional and ultimate — wisdom is to be known. At the conventional level it is to be known as touch. That which is bliss without tangible touch is the defining characteristic that is difficult to behold. That which has the nature of being without inherent nature — that is what I have called night. Daytime is known as skillful means; method becomes the sun. The element of the bhaga is the sun; the sun becomes the lord of the bhaga. The sun is the form of existence; the moon is the form of nirvāṇa. That existence is shown as the family. The family of the moon arises from the sun. Because it is without stain, it is the sun — free from the net of conceptual thought. Supremely subtle without stain, utterly clear, it is the emptiness body (śūnyatākāya). Truly pure, stainless, at rest and at peace, it is present everywhere throughout all things. Such is what I have called the day — the day hidden throughout all the channels.

The junction is the meeting of the two: from vajra and lotus arise sun and moon. The meeting of the two navels is the defining characteristic of a subtle instant. The junction is liberation from affliction — the supreme of supremes, free from all aspects — present between the two of the union, with neither application nor craving. That junction purifies the path of cyclic existence for all sentient beings. The east is called supreme — free from joy and free from what goes with it — the supreme abode at the tip of the knot. The form of the mustard-seed bones: these have two mouths. The east is to be known as the lord of the moon. Because the moon travels upward, it is known as the drop of the moon. The teaching is that the drop divides into four. Abiding in the middle between the first and the last —

the fourth is nowhere to be found. Because it is the very nature of self-cognizing awareness, it is therefore beyond all expression. That is shown as the secret, and the meaning of arising is gathered there.

Listen, O Goddess who grants bliss! I myself now teach the truth of setting.

When the mind sets, it first transforms into the form of the moon. At that time it goes to the place of setting. What does not move comes to move. When what was first goes to become last, that is taught as the setting. The last thing taught as setting is this: in the place of support, abiding without support. When there is support, supreme bliss is present — yet where can there be bliss without support?

This concludes the fifth chapter from the glorious Mahāmudrā Tilaka, entitled "The Teaching on Life, Night and Day, the Junction, and Arising and Setting."

Now first the arising of the root elements is taught. Listen: I shall explain time. Time is taught as measure; divide time into two distinctions. There is time that is beyond conception, and the most auspicious time is time indeed. The very auspicious time and the inauspicious time: the two apertures in the flaring of the nose — the in-breath is taught as the auspicious time, and the out-breath is explained as the time that is not auspicious. The time that is not auspicious is called the cut. The auspicious one is supremely to be held in mind. Free from both: that is suchness, gathered here, unprecedented. It is free from views and free from anything to be viewed, parted from all ill-signs. That time, I shall now, with beautiful and truthful mouth, explain to another.

At that time, if the yogin does not make the mind directed elsewhere, how shall I, the sovereign of sacred commitment, become other than what I am — the vajra-holder?

From where do Āli and Kāli arise? Listen — I shall explain this. They arise from the stainless, genuine abode. They have form that manifests as the spreading of luminosity. Endowed with thirty-three excellent marks, the maṇḍala of Kāli is utterly vivid. From the abode where luminosity spreads, that very luminosity arises in clarity — bearing sixteen vowel-letters: this is the expanded maṇḍala of Āli.

Through the union of these two, great flavor arises gradually. The form of the supreme nectar is the abode of great-bliss bliss. It resides at the tip of the ḍākinī's nose and illuminates the six cakras of the lotus. Its tactile quality bestows bliss — the eight great flavors are to be known: trembling, a sensation of particles, the rising of body-hair, the falling silent of speech, radiance of complexion, the changing of color, and intoxicated tears. These are the eight flavors of the essence.

Being drawn in by flavor, the joys arise as most excellent. Joy, supreme joy, joy-of-cessation, and innate joy — likewise in the same way. What is called joy is luminosity. Supreme joy is taught as the spreading of luminosity. Joy-of-cessation is called the attainment of luminosity. Innate joy is genuine clear light.

At that very time, in a single instant, arise the defining characteristics of variety and so forth. Luminosity is taught as the moon. Spreading luminosity is the sun. The junction is known as the attainment of luminosity. Free from the three is genuine clear light.

Whenever the mind is within the inner knot, joy arises at that time. From the junction of the knot, at about three units further, supreme joy arises. Going three places from the three, joy-of-cessation arises.

Whatever abandons both sets of three — suchness, whose intrinsic nature has arisen: that is the supreme of the great secret, the genuine supreme of the secret of secrets. In the tantras such as the Saṃvara and the rest, it is to be kept concealed from the beloved goddess of good delight. In the Saṃvara or the Hevajra, it has also not been made clear. This supreme act performed for your sake — this great secret I myself have made clear.

Buddhas, bodhisattvas, and others who have not died — listen, all you afflicted ones: this supreme suchness of that kind, if it is not I myself, is not known. Further, what is renowned as the dharmadhātu — that very suchness, listen — I myself shall explain.

Space is the dharmadhātu, the basic space of phenomena,
and that basic space is understood as the seed.
It dwells within all phenomena
and is the cause and supreme abode.

Just as oil exists within sesame,
and just as fire exists within wood,
so too does the dharmadhātu pervade
all phenomena throughout —
yet if it is present, it cannot be seen from without.

The dharmadhātu is renowned as "bhaga."
Bhaga is the precious vessel.
Endowed with qualities such as sovereignty and the rest,
it is universally known as bhaga.
Dharma is taught under the name bhaga.
The basic space is understood as mind.
Throughout the boundless expanse of the three realms,
mind is the cause of that.

At the level of conventional truth it appears as form,
like the reflection of the moon in water.
At the ultimate level, it is not seen —
yet its not being seen does not mean it is nothing,
for it is not a non-entity.

Self-knowing awareness is supremely peaceful,
free from entity and non-entity alike.
That is the supreme suchness,
the self of the dharmadhātu, peaceful.

Thus ends the sixth chapter of the Glorious Great Seal Bindu (Mahāmudrātilaka), entitled "The Arising of the Vowels and Consonants from the Bindu, the Characteristics of Time, and the Presentation of the Dharmadhātu."

Furthermore, listen — I shall explain.
What truly bears the characteristic of the vajra
is taught as the indivisible vajra,
the supreme very self of the five wisdoms,
absorbed into the center of the five spaces,
the very self of the five spaces.

It abides within the center of the lotus,
a single bindu, without syllable,
free from all aspects and without aspect —
that is universally known as the vajra.

The definitive meaning of the word "self" —
listen, for I shall explain that very suchness.

The syllable A is the supreme among syllabic forms
and dwells within the body of all without exception.
All supreme aspects are perfectly complete in it.
It is present in all, unborn, knowing all,
ever empty, pervading all,
the cause that unifies everything into one.

Existence, supreme existence, the day —
it is the bliss of the emanation body.
The body grants the refuge of the Tathāgata
and is capable of accomplishing all aims.

The syllable Hūṃ is free from form,
dwelling in the body yet not born from the body.
It is liberated from all aspects and without aspect,
free from all major and minor marks,
liberated from entity and non-entity,
the agent of arising and dissolution,
the extinguisher of all entities —
it possesses the intrinsic nature of the dharmakāya.
Mind is the refuge-granting Vajradhāra.
Free from all syllables,
it is the empty aspect, nirvāṇa,
possessing the intrinsic nature of the wisdom body.
Stainless, inexpressible, and peaceful —
that is perfectly accomplished in Hūṃ.

"I" am within the interior of all,
all things are the very self of "I," ever present.
In the very self of all entities
that pervades and abides throughout.
Were "I" absent, all beings
would be like a tree with its root severed.

At the border of the syllable Hūṃ,
the syllable A rests at the crown.
The syllable A is the moon,
the syllable Hūṃ is the light of the sun.
The syllable A is the support,
the syllable Hūṃ is what is supported.
The syllable A is explained as life-breath,
the syllable Hūṃ is understood as exertion.
Whoever unites these two in that place —
for that one the very self will become luminously clear.
Difficult to find in all the tantras,
this is explained as the authentic word "self."

Thus ends the seventh chapter of the Glorious Great Seal Bindu, entitled "The Characteristics of the Vajra and the Definitive Meaning of the Word 'Self.'"

Now the meaning of the secret is to be explained.
What is called "the secret" — what does one say?
"The secret" is presented as joy.
What is called "supreme" is indeed the supreme.
What is called "delight" is presented as free from joy.
Recollect the innate very self of all.
The secret itself is the secret —
the secret is presented as the vital fluid.
The vital fluid is taught as the life-breath,
and the life-breath is understood as the support.
The support is to be known as the earth;
it is the arising of all three realms.
The supreme is presented as water —

Water is the life of the vital breath.
At the end of that, it is taught as light.
Light is recollected as utter purity.
Utter purity is the very substance of great bliss —
that is taught as intrinsic nature.

Utter purity neither arises nor abides.
What descends is said to be pure.
What descends is taught as not being a concrete thing.
What is not a concrete thing cannot be called utter purity.
Utter purity is the subtle of the subtle.
The purity of the subtle abides elsewhere —
it is taught as "the intrinsic nature of the subtle."
Apprehended and apprehender are entirely abandoned.
The great bliss of one's own nature
is supremely renowned as the wind of gnosis.
That is explained as space.
Space is taught as emptiness.
From emptiness all things arise.
Into emptiness all things dissolve.
Emptiness abides in perfect purity, stainless,
free from all aspects,
the supreme aspect, free from arising and pure —
the very substance of self-arising experiential joy.
That is explained as intrinsic nature.
The self of all abides always.

Now, the defining characteristics of the five families —
listen, for I shall explain them.

Family is called kapāla.
Kapāla is explained as ka.
Ka is known as "root."
The root is recollected as fivefold in its distinctions.
The bodies of the five Tathāgatas —
these are recollected as the five aggregates.
The five aggregates are the body of accomplishment,
having the intrinsic nature of the five elements.

Brahmā, Viṣṇu, the Fierce One,
Īśvara, and the Ever-Peaceful —
these five are pervaded by the single one.
The self is the ultimate father,
the great emptiness that pervades all.
The self neither transfers nor is born.

Ka is that which drinks compassion.
Pā is that which guards the commitments.
La has gone beyond the lower destinies.
That is explained as kapāla.

The sound ka is intrinsic nature.
Ka does not abide anywhere whatsoever.
It pervades all things.
That is what is taught as ka.
That self is the very substance of Heruka.
It abides in the place of the wheel of Dharma.
The Ever-Peaceful dwells in the enjoyment wheel.
Īśvara is the wheel of great bliss.
The Fierce One abides at the navel wheel.
Viṣṇu is the wheel of fire.
Brahmā is in the wind wheel.
When the wind is present within all,
the self is at the center of the six wheels,
and abides pervading all of them.

Emptiness, the other supreme — emptiness itself —
the supreme of glorious Heruka,
the chief of the five ḍākinīs,
the lord of the wisdom ḍākinīs am I,
having the intrinsic nature of the five elements,
encircled on all sides by ḍākinīs.

This concludes the eighth chapter of the Glorious Great Seal Drop, entitled "The Secret, the Four Joys, and the Defining Characteristics of the Five Families."

Then again, what remains to be explained:
The signs of the mudrās are supreme.
Earrings, anklets, and the wheel,
likewise the necklace and the ash —
the sacred thread of Brahmā — these are the six mudrās,
to be recollected as the six perfections.
Generosity, moral discipline, patience, diligence,
meditative concentration, and wisdom — these are the six.
Generosity is taught as Dharma.
Moral discipline is recollected as karma.
Patience is recollected as commitment.
Diligence is to be understood as meditative absorption.
Meditative concentration is recollected as gnosis.
What is called wisdom is the great mudrā —
this is to be understood as ultimate.
The six sense bases are to be purified.
They are supremely renowned as the six mudrās.
The six families are to be understood in sequence.
The six mudrās are the very identity of the goddesses:
yaṃ, raṃ, laṃ, baṃ, aṃ, haṃ —
these are in the sequence of the goddesses.
Therein, worship is supremely to be performed.
The worship proceeds in the sequence of equal taste.
By the power of dissolution and enjoyment,
it is likewise for all places.

Caṇḍālī, Cooked-Food, Ḍombī,
the Dancer, the Skull-Bearer, and the Brahmin Woman —
Caṇḍālī belongs to the lotus family.
Cooked-Food is recollected as belonging to the jewel family.
Ḍombī is called the vajra family.
The Dancer is recollected as the sword family.
The Skull-Bearer belongs to the wheel family.
These five are the pure mudrās.

The wisdom mudrā, the Brahmin Woman,
is recollected as belonging to the family of Vajrasattva.
Yet she is not truly a brahmin woman —
the Skull-Bearer is recollected as the sister.
The Dancer is to be understood as the daughter.
Ḍombī is well known as the wife.
The Cooked-Food Woman is the bride.
Caṇḍālī is recollected as the sister-in-law.

By the rite of wisdom and skillful means,
worship is to be performed without conceptual thought.
Without conceptual elaboration, accomplishment is achieved.
In the very instant of approach practice,
the Mother is taught as mind.
Speech is here recollected as the sister.
The Daughter is well known as body.
The Wife is recollected as life-force.
The Bride is recollected as effort.
The Sister-in-Law is recollected as equanimity.

The Mother is the root of power —
the vessel of the precious buddha.
The Sister is the root of semen,
dwelling in the place of the highest.
The Daughter is the root of blood,
present below the root of semen.
The Wife is the root of fragrant water,
dwelling in between the two.
The Bride is the root of tears,
dwelling in the place of the throat.
The Sister-in-Law is the root of mucus,
dwelling in the place of the forehead.

The Mother is to be understood as earth.
The Sister is recollected as water.
The Daughter is recollected as fire.
The Wife is well known as wind.
The Bride is space itself.
The Sister-in-Law is not space.

The Mother becomes the sweet taste.
The Sister is recollected as the sour.
The Daughter is to be understood as the pungent.
The Wife is here recollected as the salty.
The hot is the Bride.
The Sister-in-Law is recollected as the astringent.

All of these are to be worshipped with effort,
and accomplishment will swiftly be obtained.
What is difficult to find in the tantras of yoga,
I have explained in the context of the families of mudrā.

This concludes the ninth chapter of the glorious Mahāmudrātilaka, entitled "The Symbolic Language of Mudrā and the Rite of Worship."

Now the rite of worship — I have taught it as outer and inner.
Toward stone and similar objects, and toward stūpas,
one should never make prostration.
Consecration, maṇḍala, and fire-offering —
these too are never to be performed.
Great fragrance, vajra-water, seed-syllables, and the like —
these are always to be offered to oneself.

Whatever moves or does not move,
all of it is mind.
The form produced thereby is not mind;
mind is empty in its intrinsic nature.
Devoid of essential nature, it is empty,
and thus all that moves is established as mind.
Having purified in just this way,
one should also make offering to the channels and so forth.

You yourself are the lord Vajrasattva.
Mind is to be recollected as the self.
What is not mind is the substance of the deity —
there is no other external one.
Having understood it thus, you yourself
should first worship that very suchness.

Conduct yourself completely within the five families;
examine them as a single family.
The distinctions of attachment and aversion,
and the distinction of drinking and not drinking — abandon these.
There is no woman who is an unsuitable vessel.
Do not speak of virtue and non-virtue.
If you desire to obtain the great accomplishment
without accomplishment-yoga, without mantra, and without a mantrin,
do not cultivate such a mind.

A great being of vast waves and profound depth
should cultivate that mind.
The mind that is on a level with śrāvakas —
never cultivate it in that way.
The unstable, those not of the fortune of śrāvakas,
whose wisdom is veiled by the darkness of the wrong path —
to these ones, even a mere word
a great being does not give.
Nor should you go to be criticized by them,
nor ever pay homage to them.

The practice of outer and inner union —
its abiding here I have explained.

The vajra-places are Uḍḍiyāna,

and Kāmarūpa is a place.

Jālandhara is recollected as the fontanelle.

Mālava is the tip of the nose,
Sindhu is known as the two eyes.
Nagara is recollected as the two ears.
Siṃgha resides at the lotus of space.
Mumūni is the throat-hollow.

Karmāliṅka is at the tip of the spine.
Suvarṇadvīpa is the place of the anus.

Vindhya is the two knees.
The preta-gathering is the tibia,
the ocean shore is at the two ankles.
Kaumārī is the big toes.
Cāritra is at the finger joints.
The land of Kashmir encompasses all the limbs.
Kerata is the sole of the foot.
Nepāla is the heel.

These are the ultimate places.

The moon, flame-flowers,
and red flowers, equal to the sun —
at the time of worship one should make offering.
The offering-gift of life-sacrifice
one should present there with earnest endeavor.
At the great lotus lake of that place,
the life should be cut.
One should never make it cease —
great diligence should be applied there.
Through the merit of life-sacrifice,
the supreme of supremes is truly obtained.

At that place, pure and stainless, triangular in form,
there are the two satellites of the maṇḍala.
As the inner satellite: the glorious Blessed One,
endowed with bodhicitta itself.
The outer: the yoginī of the three families —
the holder-goddesses and the rest, greatly supreme.
Since they arise from within the body,
they are taught as goddesses.
The maṇḍala is taught as the body,
and the vulva is explained as the maṇḍala.
The maṇḍala is the awakening mind —
these three maṇḍalas are to be understood.
Dwelling in the midst of eight charnel grounds,
these three maṇḍalas are to be meditated upon.
The breathing-out charnel ground, as it is called,
is furnished with nine gates.
Listen well, Beautiful-Faced One —
I shall explain the arising of these.

The fierce and violent one, anus-shaped,
is the secret vaginal opening.
The charnel ground called "The Blazing"
dwells in the two nostrils.
Hell and such — likewise the unbearable —
these dwell in the two eyes.
The raucous ha-ha and the gaping
dwell in the two ears.
In each charnel ground there is one tree,
and in it there is one who holds power.
The fierce and violent one: the palmyra tree,
and the inner form is the śikara.
The vaginal opening is the śāli-tree.
"The Blazing" is the śorisa tree.
Hell and such is the udumbara,
the unbearable is the pāṭali tree.
In the raucous ha-ha is the aśvattha,
and in the gaping the kesara tree.
These sixty-four forms of trees
each have many branches.

The woman of anus-power, fire-powered tip,
the mountain-peak, the corpse-bodied woman,
the sky-woman, the rain-giving woman,
the decayed woman, and the hungry woman —
these are recollected as divided into eight varieties.
The word "tree" means root,
because a tree has flowers.
On the supreme maṇḍala above,
if there are no flowers, how can one make offering?

This concludes the tenth chapter, entitled "The Maṇḍala, the Goddesses, the Sacred Sites, the Charnel Grounds, the Trees, and the Offering," from the Glorious Great Seal Drop.

Now listen — I shall explain the fire-offering
that eliminates all afflictions.
There abides a manner at the place of the navel,
and by that manner, at the outer rim is the secret lotus.

There, the wind of self-ignition
is made to circulate, then comes to rest.
Through the joining of the two faculties like rubbing sticks of wood,
the wind kindles the fire.

The wheel above where butter is present
is recollected as the vessel of butter.
Ha is explained as the ladle for pouring.
The full ladle is here shown as the vajra.
The fire-offering pit is explained as the bhaga.
The grain is recollected as the five faculties.
The yamwood is shown as the aggregates.
The awakening mind is burned as the butter.

Below the face of the butter,
descend until reaching the fish, and offer.
The drop, filling the ladle only halfway —
do not let it pass outside the fire-offering pit.

The buddhas, the bodhisattvas,
the gods, the asuras, the rākṣasas,
and the eight world-guardians —
all of these are to be fully satisfied with the oblation.

Whatever offering is made to the vajra-mind,
it should be presented filled with the substances of burning.
By mantras of the Hūṃ Phaṭ variety,
the skilled one should offer the burning substances.

By this, the ḍākinīs
and likewise the yoginīs are pleased.
Whatever is conceived in the mind,
immeasurable great siddhis are bestowed.
All wishes are fulfilled.
Such is the fire-offering that I have explained.

This concludes the eleventh chapter, entitled "The Rite of the Ultimate Fire-Offering," from the Glorious Great Seal Drop.

Now, I shall explain in brief
the supreme rite of the five nectars.
Vairocana, Akṣobhya, Amoghasiddhi,
Amitābha, and Ratnasambhava — these are shown as five.
Ratnasambhava is indicated as blood.
Amitābha is spoken of as semen.
Amoghasiddhi is great flesh.
Akṣobhya is vajra-water.
Vairocana is explained as great fragrance.
These, then, are the five nectars.

In a place of solitude, empty of people, joyful,
free from all harm —
there, one should thoroughly prepare a house,
two-storied and provided with a door.
One story is to be below ground,
and the second is above it.
Worldly people know it as one.
In such a manner should the skilled one make the house.

First, for the practice, one who is of fortunate capacity,
greatly learned and without miserliness,
moist with great compassion,
free from aversion and envy,
greatly courageous and of great waves of energy —
so too the companion in practice:
free from attachment, non-dual,
without miserliness and endowed with diligence,
without anger and greatly brave,
devoted to the guru, greatly learned.
With the three fruits and so forth,
the body should first be properly purified by those.

Then, the mantrin should recite clearly
one hundred thousand times the Sumbha mantra:
Oṃ sumbha ni sumbha hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ. Oṃ gṛhṇa hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ. Oṃ gṛhṇāpaya gṛhṇāpaya hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ. Oṃ ānaya ho bhagavan vajravidyārāja hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ.

Then, the three-syllable mantra
is recited mentally by the master of practice.
In a dream, one sees the following:
the assembly-gathering of yoginīs.
The gift of their speech
is carried off by the daughters of the Enchantress.
For the benefit of sentient beings, boundlessly,
as the Vajradhāra himself acts —
a beautiful girl is found,
endowed with form, youth, and ornaments,
swollen with great desire,
swaying with graceful, pleasing movement,
firm of breast, blue-black and bold,
free of conceptual thought and greatly learned.

For such a great woman,
at the time when the lunar sign arises,
through the joining of union with her,
one should perform the rite of wisdom and skillful means.
At the time when taste arises,
one should proceed thus, step by step.
At that time, from the mouth-lotus,
it is to be taken for seven days.
In a very clear lotus vessel,
place the rakta, resembling a jewel.
For three days, with the mouth,
one should press clean the supreme lotus.
The rite of wisdom and skillful means
is then again to be undertaken by the yogin.
There, wine is to be drunk,
and powerful sālu rice is to be eaten;
or else the milk of a she-goat,
or else the power-food of space.

By practicing by night and by day,
one should examine the great method of extraction.
When the great vital essence arrives
and the young woman becomes intoxicated,
at that time it is to be taken with the mouth.
By means of the tube of the tongue and so forth,
it is to be placed in a faultless place,
in a lotus vessel.
On the eighth, or the fourteenth,
or on the tenth of the waning fortnight,
one slain by the tip of a spear or by a sword —
one of the five families, a single family.
Freedom from illness and the attainment of bliss,
beauty and the bloom of youth —
one born seven times or else three times —
may a companion of accomplishment bring these and come.
With varied offerings and oblations,
one should make worship with single-pointed focus.
The most excellent of its limbs are to be taken;
the lotus vessel is to be made for it.
With wisdom endowed with skillful means,
wine is to be drunk from that vessel.
Of great power, a small amount of oneself —
to accomplish attainment, it is to be eaten.
What has been extracted in the lotus vessel,
with some effort, is to be set aside.
The other woman is to be prepared with medicine,
and cooked by the power of the companion's sense faculty.
Goat's milk, sesame butter,
butter combined with sugar,
treacle and so forth in equal measure —
the power, it is recalled, becomes fivefold.
In the mouth, the three hot spices,
cardamom (śrī-cardamom), nāgakeśara,
nutmeg, cloves,
and camphor are to be made progressively larger.
In an exceedingly smooth and pure vessel,
fumigation is to be made with aloe wood and so forth.
With effort it is to be cooked in another vessel,
and set aside after fumigation has been done.
For seven days
powerful sālu rice is to be eaten.
By merely eating that,
one who has been unmoving will regain his former state.
That is to be taken in the lotus vessel;
the top and bottom are to be removed by the skilled one.
At dawn, a small amount is to be drunk,
and a small amount is to be placed in the vessel.
The arising of the two, wisdom and skillful means —
the luminous display that is gathered thereby —
the yogin is to make single-pointed.
This is the practice of the yogin's companion.

The fruits of emblic myrobalan (āmalaka) tree, belleric myrobalan (vibhītaka) tree,
tikarakuṭa, blue lotus,
milk with sour fermented whey —
cooking these with single-pointed focus,
one should cook in an iron vessel.
If one is not found, then in an earthen vessel.
With flowers of the pure family,
one should fumigate for seven days.
Then that essence medicine
is to be poured in gradually, increasing.
Clove, camphor, lāṭa,
musk, sandalwood, nutmeg,
nutmeg fruit, cardamom,
cloves, the three hot spices and their tastes,
combined with white sugar
and butter, are to be cooked.
With the fire brought to a gentle maturation,
it is to be cooked by the power of the companion's sense faculty.
Fumigation with aloe wood and so forth is to be done,
and it is to be placed in a stone vessel.
Each day, two measures of curd —
if melted, one should eat it;
if melted, four measures of curd,
then six measures of curd are always to be eaten.

The rite of the five nectars —
this great secret I have explained.
Furthermore, the medicine of the five limbs —
the skilled one should form it into pills.
Having made it into the size of an emblic fruit,
it is to be dried in the shade.
Having washed the cloth and the limbs,
the bindu is likewise to be made.
With effort it is to be placed in the mouth;
the maṇḍala of the feast is also to be made.
Pacifying, enriching, empowering,
wrathful, fierce and so forth —
by merely making the pills,
all will be accomplished without doubt.

This concludes the twelfth chapter, called "The Teaching of the Five Nectars," from the Glorious Great Seal Bindu.

Whatever woman is reddish and slightly tawny, who emits the fragrance of a lotus, whose gaze is naturally peaceful, who follows after one who looks at her with longing, whose nails are red like a lotus, whose eyes possess the radiance of the sun — in her home there is a lotus, and she always performs the rites of offering. Then the completion stage:
the supreme sequential arising from the channels.
Each goddess, one at each tip —
their bliss, yours and mine,

By the true faculty-wood,

The form of the aggregate of conceptual thought —

By the true blazing of that fire —

Through that melting, from all the channels,
the supreme nectar (amṛta) drips.
Then the body recovers,

This is to be cultivated with one-pointed focus
through the yoga of habituation.
Even without an external mudrā,
bliss can arise —
mere gazing is sufficient.
Yet not all things are like all things:

Just as vessels in darkness

the vessel is to be guarded with care,

The defining characteristic of the lamp is here

Skillful means is taught as bliss,

The nature of bliss-gnosis,
whatever is found from the mother of wisdom —
that is the sovereignty of wisdom-gnosis.
That is the completion stage.

The very gnosis of great bliss
properly abides within the maṇḍala.
Divide great bliss into two:
conventional and ultimate.
From joy and so forth,

The two true stages thereof —
listen with faith; I shall explain.

Appearance, touch, union,
and going — that is the fourth.
These are to be understood in sequence.

On the side of the generation stage,

At that time joy arises,

When, together with that,

Touching the breast, gazing —

Now again, when the uta

By the syllable "uta," bliss is indicated.
She of excellently pleased sovereignty — that is I.

Desire, trembling, going,

That is the first union.
That is the meaning of joy.

Expansion, sufficiency, longing, arising,

That is the second union.
That is the meaning of supreme joy.

Blazing, the drop's intoxication,

That is the third union.
That is the meaning of joy-apart-from-joy.

Uta, manner, and sleep:
co-emergent joy is the fourth.
That is the fourth union.
The distinctions of joy are sixteen.

These four are not other than this —
here the supreme meaning is as follows.
Desire is explained as longing,
stirred up by the glorious Wisdom-Mother.
That longing is set in motion
and performed by one's own mind.
The word "desire" means joy:
it is performed from seeing.

That very Blessed Wisdom-Mother,
when expanded by great passion,
is then expansion.

That is the supreme joy, the second.

At the time of expansion, outer and inner —
when they blaze exceedingly,

Now by the blazing drop

Or else, that very Queen herself,

From the crown of great bliss

The characteristic of the gnosis of empowerment —

Again, let the Blessed One speak on the conduct of the lord of yogins.

The Blessed One declared: Listen, you of good fortune, to the conduct of the lord of yogins.

In solitude, practicing the secret for a month — at the border of a territory or in a forest — all aims shall be accomplished. Recite this mantra constantly:

Hā svā ka ro ro ra ta taṃ va de ro dha na la tta ci ko ra dhā ta mre ga lā na jrā va oṃ.

Then the ḍākinīs (yoginīs) of the sky, having assembled, shall declare and bestow: "Son, take this girl. Perform the supreme conduct, and let it be done by you."

The girl is sixteen years old, she has sihla and camphor, her complexion resembles the blue utpala flower, she has the fragrance of sandalwood, she is adorned with graceful ornaments, and she is skilled in all activities. With effort, enter into union with her in the secret form, and go in bliss together with her.

Once the aim has been obtained, each in their own form — in various guises — some practice with desire, some with a mind free from desire; some with a peaceful form, some with a mind endowed with pride; some are consumed by illness, some have a supremely blissful body; some make supplication, some are victorious like a great king; some are naked and impure, some are clean and spotlessly pure; some are satisfied with a little, some satisfy themselves greatly; some act with a sense of wonder, some with a mind free of shame; some sit in silence, some cry out with a great sound; some eat great masses of pungent food, some drink milk; some act sinfully, some act with great merit; some weep, some delight only in laughter; some play with ritual instruments, some dance alone.

Some are intoxicated with liquor, some follow the conduct of brahmins, some follow the conduct of skull-bearers, some serve as attendants of the fierce ones, some teach the Dharma, some listen to the Dharma, some perform illusions, some are free of illusion, some speak falsehood, some display the form of a child, some are old and advanced in years, some display the form of a prostitute, some engage in the supreme of desires, some attract through miserliness, some have a mind free of miserliness, some are branches of the animal realm, some are without consciousness, some are ineffable, and some display a form that is genuine and real. In this way the sovereign of yogins moves everywhere according to his own will.

This concludes the twentieth chapter, entitled "The Conduct of the Great Sovereign of Yogins," from the Śrī Mahāmudrātilaka.

The Blessed One, an ocean of wisdom — a doubt has arisen in me. Concerning the six wheels spoken of before: what are their seeds? By the mantra of whom are the five nectars eaten and consumed? And how is the essence of you and me certainly present?

For the protection of sentient beings, in the center of the gathered assembly-place, what is the nature of the wheel of the host? The Blessed One declared:

Listen, goddess of beautiful eyes. The seeds of the six wheels are: Kṣlīṃ, Mlīṃ, Plīṃ, Jlīṃ, Rlīṃ, Hlīṃ — place one of these in each center. A, Ā; I, Ī; U, Ū; Ṛ, Ṝ; Ḷ, Ḹ; E, Ai; O, Au; Aṃ, Āḥ — this enjoyment wheel of eighty-two characters should be written in leftward rotation. These doubled are the seeds of the great-bliss wheel.

The flat portion of earth, equal and level, should be anointed with liquor and the like. The wise one should honor the ground with supreme sandalwood and saffron, with the finest and most varied flowers, and with various excellent offerings.

A girl of divine form, captivating the mind, twenty years of age, of steady composure — in the place where the king of mantras has been gathered, first introduce her there. In the same way, the bindu of the student is applied to the girl. Send the torma — filled with great meat and the like — in the four directions. This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The title's Sanskrit is given in the text in transliterated Tibetan and has been restored in the heading. The term "bindu" (Tibetan: thig le) is retained in the title rendering as it is a technical tantric term; in the body it is rendered contextually as "bindu" or "drop."

The figure "Bhaṅgamā" (Tibetan: bzang mo / byang mo) in the line "O Bhaṅgamā — she is a beast of the field" is a significant crux. The Tibetan reads "bzang mo de ru byol song yin," meaning "the fair one is there an animal (byol song = tiryak)." This appears to be a paradoxical or antinomian statement characteristic of yoginī tantra: what appears noble or fair is, at the ultimate level, identified with an animal-realm being, or conversely, the questioner "Bzangmo" is being addressed and told that relative appearances (such as gods like Śiva) are in fact no better than animals. The translation renders it as best it can given the compressed verse, but this passage may require traditional commentary for full resolution.

The phrase "five hundred thousand" (འབུམ་ལྔ་ཡི་ནི་བདག་ཉིད་རྒྱུད་) refers to the five hundred thousand verses that are said to constitute the full Hevajra tantra cycle in its complete form, a standard claim in Indian tantric literature.  The passage is from a Vajrayāna tantra describing empowerment theory and the guru-student relationship in the context of highest yoga tantra practice. Several points deserve comment.

The enumeration of empowerments into eleven, twelve, thirteen, and fourteen sub-divisions correlates with the bodhisattva grounds: each empowerment authorizes mastery over a particular ground, making the "fourteenth" the result-ground of buddhahood. This is a standard Anuttarayoga structural schema.

The phrase rendered "the emanation king" refers to the human guru understood as a royal emanation body, a nirmaṇakāya king, which is a standard tantric way of presenting the guru's nature.
