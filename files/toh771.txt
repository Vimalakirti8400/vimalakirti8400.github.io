The Hundred and Eight Names of Noble Jambhala (Āryajambhalanāmāṣṭaśataka)

This text is known in English as *The Hundred and Eight Names of the Noble Jambhala*, corresponding to the Sanskrit *Ārya-jambhala-nāma-aṣṭaśataka* and the Tibetan *’Phags pa gnod ’dzin gyi mtshan brgya rtsa brgyad pa*. It belongs to the genre of name-litanies (nāmāṣṭaśataka) dedicated to a specific deity, in this case Jambhala, the wealth-bestowing yakṣa lord associated with prosperity and protection, whose epithets are recited as a devotional and ritual practice. Such texts typically combine praise with an implicit invocation of the deity's qualities and powers, intended for recitation to secure blessings, particularly material abundance and protection from want. Positioned within the Kangyur as Toh 771, this work falls among the tantric or ritual literature associated with minor deities, reflecting the broader Buddhist tradition of dhāraṇī and nāma texts used for devotional and apotropaic purposes.
