The Glorious Tantra King Called the Red Yamāri (Śrī Raktayamāritantrarāja)

Toh 474 in the Kangyur is a tantric scripture known in Sanskrit as the Śrī Raktayamāritantrarāja and in Tibetan as the Dpal gshin rje'i gshed dmar po zhes bya ba'i rgyud kyi rgyal po, meaning in English "The Glorious Tantra King Called the Red Yamāri." It belongs to the class of highest yoga tantras and centers on Raktayamāri, the Red Yamāri, a wrathful deity understood as the slayer or destroyer of Yama, the lord of death, who serves as a central focus of esoteric practice and ritual. The text opens in the classic canonical style with the declaration "Thus did I hear," locating the teaching in a visionary setting where the Blessed One abides within the secret essence of all the Tathāgatas, and the great bodhisattva Vajrapāṇi poses questions about the nature and number of those who destroy Yama. As a tantra king, this scripture holds a position of particular authority within its cycle, presenting doctrine, meditation, and the qualities of Yamāri practice in a revealed, dialogic form characteristic of the Vajrayāna tantric literature preserved in the Kangyur.

In the language of India: Śrī Raktayamāritantrarāja nāma. In the language of Tibet: The Glorious Tantra King Called the Red Yamāri.

Homage to the Blessed One, the Slayer of Yama.

Thus did I hear. At one time, the Blessed One dwelt within the secret places of the vajra-lady, the essence of the body, speech, and mind of all the Tathāgatas. There, the essence of the body, speech, and mind of all the Tathāgatas of the Blessed One — most supremely secret, beyond even the venerable secret — arose, and the bodhisattva Vajrapāṇi, of great compassion and great being, said: "Excellent! Excellent!" The vajra of aversion, the essence of the great bodhisattva — hear now the nature of the Red Yamāri.

Vajrapāṇi then asked: "How many are the destroyers of Yama spoken of here?" The reply was: there is the Vajra-Deluded Yamāri, the Vajra-Slanderous Yamāri, the Vajra-Attached Yamāri, and the Vajra-Jealous Yamāri. There are also Vajra Carcikā, Vajravārāhī, Vajra Sarasvatī, and Vajra Gaurī. At the directions there are the vases. By such an assembly of destroyers of Yama and others, it is accomplished.

Then the Blessed One, from within the very body, speech, and mind of the meditative absorption called the Vajra that Destroys All Māras — the master of all Tathāgatas — caused to emerge:

Mantras with the great power to summon and to command,
and the many rites of might in like fashion —
accomplishment of the sword, and kingship,
and the complete defeat of enemy armies;
invisibility, eye-medicine, and the extraction of essence,
likewise the learning of shadow-pressing,
the raising of corpses, the accomplishment of yakṣas,
and protection from deadly serpents and the like;
the rites of fire offering and of the maṇḍala;
the discernment of the awakening mind,
the conduct comprising all methods,
and the entering of enemies' towns —
for all of these, meditate upon
the vajra-protection, with five rays of light assembled.
For the portions of the vajra ground,
the enclosure and the tent are likewise so.

Then the Blessed One, having entered into meditative absorption called the Vajra that Completely Destroys All Māras, which all the Tathāgatas have generated, drew out from the very body, speech, and mind of this seed — the seed of all, beginning with the Deluded Yamāri — with certainty.

Ya is in the center.
Kṣema — me — ya — tsa — ni — rā — ja — sa — do — ru — ṇa — yo — ni — ra.

From the syllable Ya first arises the Yamāri.
From the syllable Kṣe comes what is called delusion.
From the syllable Ma comes what is described as slander.
From the syllable Me comes desire itself.
From the syllable Da comes jealousy.
Thus the Yamāris are renowned as five.
The syllable Ja is explained as Carcikā.
The syllable Sa is Vārāhī.
The syllable Do is explained as Sarasvatī.
The syllable Ru is held to be Gaurī.
From Ṇa, Yo, Ni, Ra at the four directions —
four skulls of human beings.

Then the Blessed One, entering into the meditative absorption called the Vajra-Yamāri, the master of all Tathāgatas, taught the yoga of cultivating the family of great aversion:

Endowed with generosity, moral discipline, and patience,
of one taste — the wise one for the benefit of sentient beings;
perfectly complete in all qualities such as loving-kindness,
adorned with the stream of learning;
at the outset, thoroughly sealing the mouth and so forth —
from the earliest time, possessed of the nature of compassion —
begin in earnest in a secluded place,
a charnel ground or similar, with a comfortable seat.
Withdrawn in solitude, at his heart
is a lotus and sun, blazing with brilliant light.
By the light thereof one gathers in
the welfare of sentient beings in full abundance.
The objects of meditation — gurus and buddhas,
however many, without exception, to the ends of the sky —
are visualized; prostrating to them, their —

fully accomplishing the arrangement of the body.
One makes offerings with citraka and the like.
Before them, one thoroughly confesses sins —
whatever wrongdoing has been done
through body, speech, and mind —
and so I confess each and every one.
In the protectors of the three times I rejoice,
and in those who are equal to the perfect buddhas
I rejoice in the accumulation of merit.
I go for refuge to the perfect buddhas,
to the sacred Dharma, and to the supreme Saṅgha.
To you, who are oceans of excellent qualities,
I offer this very body of mine.
Showing the place and all the Dharma,
supremely beautiful to the utmost,
and in accordance with the perfect buddha's suchness —
generosity and so forth, embodied in form —
in order to attain the gnosis of the three bodies,
I rely upon this and place full confidence in it.
For the benefit of sentient beings,
I now arouse the awakening mind (bodhicitta).
Whatever merit I have closely gathered,
I dedicate to perfect awakening.
So that they may bring benefit to all these wandering beings,
may the glorious Lord of Death become the final destroyer.
Having made this special aspiration,
one beseeches them to come.
Regard them with affection as though they were awakening itself.
Contemplate the three realms as empty.
Since they are free from engagement through inherent nature,
and free from conceptual thought
regarding causes and their characteristics likewise,
and having abandoned deities and aspirations entirely:

Om śūnyatā-jñāna-vajra-svabhāva-ātmako'ham.

From the four syllables Yaṃ, Raṃ, Baṃ, Laṃ, the maṇḍala having been gathered in, one meditates upon the celestial palace arisen from the syllable Hrūṃ.

Four-cornered, with four gates,
adorned with four porticos,
draped with nets and half-nets,
exquisitely beautiful with gods and goddesses;
with pennants and bells,
stirred by the wind at their many-colored peaks —
from the syllable Baṃ dwelling at its center,
one visualizes a multicolored lotus.
In the center of its pericarp
stands a multicolored vajra club with all the marks of full transformation.
Meditate clearly on the nature of aversion.
On a lotus and sun arranged in due order,
upon a red buffalo,
through miraculous display, all the māras without exception
are pressed down beneath the left-extended foot.
The deity blazing with the light of ten million suns,
embraced about the neck by a walking corpse consort.
The hero has one face and two hands,
gloriously adorned with eight nāgas.
Three eyes, red and awe-inspiring,
a garland of a hundred red skulls,
in the right hand a vajra club,
in the left a skull-cup held.
Endowed with the likeness of a wrathful one,
the tongue moving and the fangs bared,
brow wrinkled, body taut and stiff at the waist,
ornamented with a tiger-skin.
Short, with belly pendulous, fierce and blazing,
utterly pure, free of stain.
The walking-corpse consort holds in her right hand a chopper,
in her left a human skull.
Three eyes, hair flowing loose,
gloriously adorned with the five mudrās,
and bearing the matching garland of skulls.
At the head — the principal one who holds the essence of corpses —

On the eastern petal: Delusion — white vajra; entering into union with Vajra Citrikā.
On the southern petal: Slander — the Executioner of Yama (Yamāntaka), yellow in color; entering into union with Vajravārāhī.
On the western petal: Attachment — the Executioner of Yama, red in color; entering into union with Vajrasarasvatī.
On the northern petal: Envy — the Executioner of Yama, green in color; entering into union with Vajragaurī.

Citrikā is black. Vārāhī is yellow. Sarasvatī is white. Gaurī is blue. In the left hand a skull-cup, in the right hand a chopper. The hands, faces, and body colors are present exactly as in the hero. At the edge of the fire are four vases of darkness filled equally. In the truthless expanse are golden vases filled by a hireling.

In the wind direction: a vase of crystal filled with asafoetida. In the direction of Īśvara: a silver vase filled with camphor. These are to be visualized as covered with skull-cups. The mantras of the principal hero, the Wrathful King and the others, are as follows.

The heart mantra: Oṃ hrīḥ śṭrīḥ vikṛtānana hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ phaṭ svāhā.

The near-heart mantra: Oṃ hauṃ kṣauṃ kṣayauṃ kārāya gṛhṇa gṛhṇa hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ phaṭ svāhā.

The garland mantra: Oṃ mahāyakṣasenādhipataye mahādurdāntadamakāya nāśaya nāśaya vidrāpaya vidrāpaya hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ phaṭ svāhā.

The mantra of the noble lady: Oṃ dveṣarati nuda nuda truṭa truṭa hūṃ jaḥ svāhā.

The mantras of the four heroes are as follows.

Oṃ māṃ hāntakṛt hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ. Oṃ viśunāntakṛt hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ. Oṃ rāgāntakṛta hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ. Oṃ īrṣyāntakṛt hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ.

The mantras of the four heroines, the female lords of the heroes, are as follows.

Oṃ mohārati hūṃ jaḥ. Oṃ piśunarati hūṃ jaḥ. Oṃ rāgarati hūṃ jaḥ. Oṃ īrṣyārati hūṃ jaḥ.

The mantras of the ornamental pendants are as follows.

Oṃ marme mārmā vighāte oṃ vajracarcike hara hara bukkaṃ rāya — the great lady — jaḥ. Oṃ ghoṇe sughoṇe vajramāmakī bharbhar sambhara sambhara hūṃ jaḥ. Oṃ marme mārmā kṣobhaṇī vajrasarasvatī dardara tharthara pharphar kampa kapa dhuna dhuna hūṃ — the great lady — jaḥ. Oṃ gambhire vajrakāratike ghagha raktaśoṇati priye gaurī — the great lady — jaḥ.

Now the Lord of the Pendant-Ornaments, Joyful One, is described in verse:

His hair bound in blue, he strikes terror.
Adorned at his ear with a red nāga, coiled at the tip.
His shoulders graced with a variegated, learned one.
At his wrists, bracelets; at his chest, pendant ornaments of conch-shell and gold.
His necklace of utterly white lotuses,
and boundless blue garlands held as offerings —
he is made exceedingly beautiful.

The pendant of nectar-hue
has as its crown ornament the jewel of increasing wealth.
White as the stalk of a lotus,
with great lotus ankle-rings,
everything proclaims the syllable Phu-tā.
He has two arms and gnashes his fangs.
Known as the Slayer of Yama — Subduer of both saṃsāra and nirvāṇa —
and as the Destroyer of all conceptual thought,
he is to be consecrated through mantra.

All the buddhas pervading the sky
are drawn in by the iron hook from the seed syllable at one's own heart-center, drawn into the mouth, enter into bliss at the heart-center and dissolve. From this, in a state of joy, the seeds arise; as they dissolve they travel by the vajra path and come to rest within the inner reaches of one's own lotus of self-luminosity. Beholding that, the seed projects itself onto its own form. From the seed born of that seed, the wheel of seeds is entirely consecrated. The multitude of fire-rays blazing forth is made vivid. The skilled practitioner meditates according to the ritual. Having beheld and caused it to melt at the heart, it is drawn out through the vajra path. The hosts of deities are beheld in the pistil of the lotus of self-awareness. Offerings of various flowers and the like are made. Those who hold leaves in their hands are to make excellent offerings. Offering songs of delight and various dance-forms by nature are likewise offered with great bliss.

Ema! O red Executioner of Yama,
whose nature is that of a lama-demon —
since I, upon beholding you, am filled with fear,
abandon the nature of the lama-demon!
Your dance causes the heavens above, the earth, and the realms below to tremble.
With eyes anointed in blood and a wrathful mind,
you dance — you who bear the form of a walking corpse.
Red and short in your proportions,
you take on emanation forms of many kinds.
Vajrabhāratī has requested:
your dance is great bliss.
The dance of your hrīḥ śṭrīḥ mantra
dispels the confusion of all three levels of existence.
O wrathful noble lord, loving and wrathful —

you look upon beings and dance.

Oṃ sarva-tathāgata-pūja-vajra-svabhāva-ātmako 'haṃ.

Regard all assemblies of sound
as being like an echo.
Praise the leader of the wheel
as that, in that, just so.
You whose intrinsic nature is the vajra of delusion,
you are the utterly fearsome executioner of Yama —
I bow and offer praise to the vajra body,
the intrinsic nature of all Teacher buddhas.
You whose intrinsic nature is the vajra of slander,
you are the utterly fearsome executioner of Yama —
I bow and offer praise to the Jewel Vajra,
perfectly matched to the vajra mind.
You whose intrinsic nature is the vajra of attachment,
you are the supreme source, the chief wellspring of the Dharma of desire —
the supreme of all supreme speech —
I bow and offer praise to the Speech Vajra.
You whose intrinsic nature is the vajra of envy,
you who accomplish all actions as the executioner of Yama,
perfectly matched to the vajra body —
I bow and offer praise to you, sword in hand.
You whose intrinsic nature is that of all buddhas,
gathering all buddhas into one,
the supreme chief of all buddhas —
I bow and offer praise to the Lord of the Maṇḍala.

Born from the syllable Āḥ, utterly pure,
dwelling above the three skulls,
is a lotus vessel filled to the brim,
and beneath it the fire maṇḍala —
a triangle arisen from the syllable Raṃ.
Beneath that is the wind maṇḍala,
dark and moving, in the shape of a bow,
born from the syllable Yaṃ and marked with a banner.
Upon that stand Vairocana and the others,
arisen from the syllable Da and the other syllables —
the five, including the elephant, and so forth.
This vajra covering, born from Hūṃ,
should be visualized as a cover by the wise.
Stirred by wind and melted by fire,
one should meditate upon its appearance.
It becomes one taste, equal to the vajra —
behold it as utterly stainless and pure.
By the light of the Hūṃ syllable, invoke the buddhas
in their wisdom forms.
Once they have fully entered, through the form of the mantra,
behold the five supreme nectars.
Consecrate with those very three,
and make offerings to the lords of the maṇḍala.
On the tongue, the nature of the vajra, utterly pure,
arisen from the Hūṃ syllable — behold it.
From rays of light emanating from it,
invoke equally, and by that
draw in all the perfect buddhas dwelling in the three realms
into this supreme maṇḍala.
Draw in and absorb the heart gnosis.
Having abandoned all conceptual thought,
through the division of beginning, end, and middle,
know that the infinite variety of form is such,
and yet in form that is confused, suchness is not present.
That from which confusion arises abides in duality.
Bestow upon the mind all that is without confusion.
Take hold of emanation and absorption.

This is the first chapter, which sets forth the sequence of direct realization, drawn from the Tantra of the Red Yamāntaka, the Body, Speech, and Mind of All Tathāgatas.

Then all the Blessed One Tathāgatas made supplication to the Blessed One, the great bodhisattva Vajrasattva, with this King of Praises. And so: "O great vajra-holder, hear how the vast oceans of gnosis arise from the detailed differentiation of karma, according to how sentient beings engage. Explain this!"

Then the King Vajradhara, who perfectly accomplishes all actions and whose very nature heals all faults, declared these very words.

Then the Blessed One Tathāgatas, having made the vajra-mind of all Tathāgatas perfectly manifest, made offerings and prostrations to the Blessed One, circumambulated him three times to the right, and again and again prostrated — and hearkened to the words of the Blessed One.

Then the King Vajradhara spoke of the mechanical device for bringing under control. The supreme mechanical device, the Yamāntaka, will not arise if it has not yet arisen. For the subjugation of women, for the purpose of protection, and likewise for the purpose of pacification, one should take musk.

Likewise on birchbark or bamboo peel, one should clearly draw the two wheels. The name should be established with the syllable NĀMAḤ. Free from black roots and the like, two bowls should be properly joined. Into the center, clarified butter and honey are poured. It should be bound with white thread. White flowers at the three junctures — the offering is made facing east. Visualizing oneself as Yamāntaka, clear as crystal water, one should perform the accomplishment. Before one, upon a lunar maṇḍala, one gazes and bestows manifest empowerment. A white vase, like the moon, is completely filled with the five nectars. The buddhas perform the ablution — one gazes properly and begins the recitation:

OṂ HRĪḤ ṢṬHĪ VIKṚTVAN HŪṂ HŪṂ PHAṬ PHAṬ — NAMAḤ DEVADATTA ŚĀNTIṂ KURU NAMAḤ.

This is the samaya of pacification. For the pacification of persons, one may also draw with saffron in expanded form, drawing the two wheels with a wide mouth, placing the name in the center between SVĀHĀ — one takes the name of the object of accomplishment and therein a son should scatter it as an offering. The two bowls are joined and the contents placed inside, then bound with yellow thread. The mouth faces north at the three junctures. Worship should be offered with yellow flowers. The color is yellow — Yamāntaka. One should certainly gaze upon oneself as such. The object of accomplishment abides upon a yellow moon. The yellow vase is washed with water. One should meditate, bestowing manifest empowerment. Recite accurately and completely for the purpose of increase:

OṂ HRĪḤ ṢṬHRĪ VIKṚTĀNA HŪṂ HŪṂ PHAṬ PHAṬ SVĀHĀ — DEVADATTA PUṢṬIṂ KURU SVĀHĀ — PHOṬA ṬA OṂ HRĪḤ ṢṬHRĪ VIKṚTĀNA HŪṂ HŪṂ PHAṬ PHAṬ — DEVADATTA PUṢṬIṂ KURU BAUṢAṬ.

This is declared by the Blessed One as the samaya of increase.

On cloth — whether birchbark or cotton — with the juice of lac, or else on red sandalwood mixed with the blood of the nameless ones, one should properly draw the two wheels. With the syllable HOḤ as the support, the mechanical device, free from black and the like, has its two bowls properly joined. Butter and honey having been placed inside, it should be bound with red thread. The mouth faces west. Worship is offered with red flowers. The color is a great blazing red. Oneself is Yamāntaka. The object of accomplishment rests upon a red moon — one should contemplate it likewise. From one's own body there emerges a red-rayed iron hook: one should meditate, hooking the object of accomplishment. The mantra should be dispersed and recited some ten thousand times. By this, the object of accomplishment becomes intoxicated — the feet fall prostrate just so — and imagining them bare of clothes and hair unbound, one begins the recitation with this visualization. If subjugation has not occurred, a practitioner of discipline should heat the wheel. Having removed it from butter and the rest, one casts it into a fire of countless acacia logs.

OṂ HRĪḤ ṢṬHĪ VIKṚTĀNA HŪṂ HŪṂ PHAṬ PHAṬ HOḤ — DEVADATTA'S OFFERING-OBLATION, BRING UNDER CONTROL! SVĀHĀ. OṂ HRĪḤ ṢṬHRĪ VIKṚTĀNA HŪṂ HŪṂ PHAṬ PHAṬ — BAUṢAṬ! DEVADATTA'S OFFERING-OBLATION, BRING UNDER CONTROL! BAUṢAṬ!

This is declared here by the Blessed One as the samaya of subjugation.

Or else, on charnel-ground cloth or rags smeared by oneself, mixing lac with blood, one should properly draw the two wheels. With the syllables JAḤ and HRĪḤ as support, placing the name of the object of accomplishment, one should place them joined and inside within the lotus-vessel of a woman, and make offerings. Bound with red thread, worship is offered with red flowers. The color is as red as the rising sun. Oneself is Yamāntaka. With a red-colored iron hook, one should meditate, hooking the object of accomplishment. With the hook drawing at the heart, and the throat bound with a noose, mounted upon a red lion, with the maṇḍala of wind applied, one should contemplate their coming —

One should wash with the water of the five nectars. If the object of accomplishment does not come, one who possesses the ascetic practice should fan the mechanical device over a fire of acacia wood. Then recite the mantra precisely:

Oṃ hrīḥ ṣṭrīḥ vikṛtānana hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ phaṭ — you wrathful one, be hooked! hrīḥ.

Oṃ hrīḥ ṣṭrīḥ vikṛtānana hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ phaṭ — jaḥ, divine gift, be hooked! jaḥ.

This, the Blessed One declared as the binding commitment for hooking.

The wise one should draw the two wheels with the extract of charnel-ground cloth and of turmeric, with the syllables LA as support, taking up the name of the object of accomplishment, and placing them joined face to face at the center. One should place a yellow bowl on top. Upon that, the skilled practitioner should draw lines — drawing lines in sets of seven and four, and so forth — and having drawn them perfectly, enter meditative absorption. It is renowned as the eight-peaked summit of Mount Meru. Above that one should draw precisely the syllables HŪṂ and VAṂ, with Mount Meru at the center. Above that, with the syllable LAṂ, one should establish the maṇḍala of the Great Sovereign. One should press it down with the vishvavajra and bind it with yellow thread. Oneself is to be visualized as Yamāntaka, yellow in color, with one's face turned southward. One should visualize the object of accomplishment as yellow, pressed beneath the maṇḍala of the Great Sovereign. Swiftly placing a mountain atop and pressing it down, having pressed with the vishvavajra, one should meditate: below the maṇḍala of the Great Sovereign, the terrifying mountains — Mandara and the like — and the immovable snow mountains press down upon the object of accomplishment. One should recite in this manner:

Oṃ hrīḥ ṣṭrīḥ vikṛtānana hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ phaṭ — laṃ, divine gift, stiffen in your own place! laṃ svāhā.

This, the Blessed One declared as the binding commitment of great stiffening.

For one who desires to stupefy speech, one should likewise draw the two wheels. Even with the name of the object of accomplishment written inside, one should eagerly recite the mantra upon it:

Oṃ hrīḥ ṣṭrīḥ vikṛtānana hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ phaṭ — laṃ, for the sake of divine gift's desire, obstruct and speak ill!

The words of the fire offering are: stambhanaṃ kuru laṃ. This the Blessed One here declared as the binding commitment for stupefying speech.

Conceal it exceedingly well, hidden in the ultimate. As the rite here described is performed, it will be accomplished in a single day — through the joining with the red form of Yamāntaka.

On charnel-ground cloth as before, one who possesses the ascetic practice should draw the two wheels. Thorn-apple, salt, poison, and neem — or neem alone — the three pungent substances and salt alone, likewise charnel-ground charcoal, water from datura leaves, seeds of caṇḍā — likewise blood taken from the ring finger, or the extract of chitraka, soil taken from a place of slaughter, the strong clay of an outcaste's pottery, and a pen made from crow feathers: with these the ascetic practitioner should draw on the fourteenth day, with a mind of midnight wrath, for the secret subjugation of harmful beings — the subduer of various sentient beings — supported at the edges by the syllable HŪṂ. The yogin's face is turned to the south. Oneself is Yamāntaka: of wrathful form, immensely fierce, adorned with fragments of skulls, seated upon a buffalo, with a moving tongue, a great belly, a terrifier. Fangs bared, hair blazing upward, eyebrows reddish yellow. In the right hand a great vajra club, likewise in the left a skull-cup. Ugly, with fangs completely bared, blazing like the fire at the end of the age — thus should one meditate on oneself. Swiftly fix the object of accomplishment before you: seized and crushed by foul odors and wounds and by a malignant disease, stripped of clothes, causing trembling —

exhausted, the body covered with wounds. Seized about the body by a garland of intestines, weapons cause the wounds to gape open. Tormented by cold and by wind, placed squarely in the midst of fire, devoured by buffaloes, tigers, dogs, and long-muzzled beasts. From within the belly of the great malignant ones — those who destroy the assemblies of buddhas — entrails pour forth, the body filled with thorns, the feet bound and fettered with shackles. Likewise, imagining the body to be cut into pieces and the skin definitively flayed, the neck smeared with salt, meditate on the body being driven out by cold. The armor and other protections of that one — cut away, with oneself seized before one. The armor of that one is to be severed. Meditate on the body as likewise empty.

Born from one's own karma, in the form of the wrathful Yamāntaka, meditate on utterly annihilating — drinking blood and fat — and before that one, meditate on Yamāntaka, bearing a great and powerful club. That malignant, harmful one is to be utterly vanquished in meditation. The entrails and sinews of that one are seized by vultures and carried up into the sky; nāgas drag them down below the earth; asuras and the like press them out. In such a way should one contemplate the object of accomplishment. Bound fast by the mind of compassion, that one is brought beyond saṃsāra and goes to the buddhafields.

Then the Blessed One, the Tathāgatas, being pleased, declared thus in verse:

Aho! The one to be slain is well dispatched.
The one to be killed has not been truly killed.
As long as the evils are liberated,
even death is not real death.
One who has committed thousands of evil acts
is not born in the Avīci hell and the like.
Aho! The great being oneself —
through slaying, buddha-awakening is attained.
Again, with love, sentient beings
are to be slain by slaying what must be kept secret.
Aho! By the power of compassion —
with lesser compassion, accomplishment will not arise.

Oṃ hrīḥ ṣṭrīḥ vikṛtānana hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ phaṭ hūṃ devadatta sod hūṃ.
Oṃ hrīḥ ṣṭrīḥ vikṛtānana hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ phaṭ hūṃ phaṭ devadatta māra ya hūṃ phaṭ.

Cultivate the bewilderment of all sentient beings.
At midnight in your own home,
seated on a tiger-skin mat,
with one face, deep crimson, utterly fierce,
wielding a noose and an iron hook,
dwelling in great terror upon a corpse,
with three eyes blazing with dread,
bewildering all the gods,
blazing and massed with fire-tigers —
meditate thus and recite one hundred thousand times.
Afterward, perform the ritual practice.

Take the root of white karavīra,
and likewise the tip of red dantī.
Mix them with frog's blood.
One who holds the vow should prepare a drop.
Whoever's shadow one treads underfoot
will fall into a swoon —
two-footed and four-footed alike,
all will be bewildered, without doubt.

In the bright fortnight of the planetary day of the sun,
seek out a worm that has come from a belly.
Having found it, with effort,
prepare a drop with śṛṅgī.
By placing that drop on the forehead,
one causes another's shadow to scatter.
If they attain intoxication and one wishes to release them,
sprinkling with milk will make them comfortable.

Take dantīrohā, the dark open plain —
these roots, combined as one,
mixed with crow's blood —
one who holds the vow should prepare a drop.
By the power of this drop,
even the gods will be thoroughly shaken.
One should not bow in salutation;
if one does, that one will fall into a swoon.

And further, another preparation:
rasāśṛṅgī, kuthārī,
and paṇīnāgapaṇī —
all of these together, prepared by moonlight:
one who holds the vow should make pills.
Taking a drop in water of sky-nature,
hold the drop on the crown of the head.
By looking into the face while bowing in salutation,
that person will vomit blood.

Kāmacā and white madhu,
the eyes of a white cuckoo —
perfectly combined with sky-water,
prepare a drop upon the crown of the head.
By the power of that drop,
enemies will come under one's power.
Whoever one bows to in salutation
will assuredly become bewildered.

And further, another preparation:
the tongue of a black night-wandering creature,
and likewise the tongue of a nakula,
properly blended with rat's bile —
make fifty thousand pills of this.
By the power of this drop,
the wrathful mantra enters into it.
A king will constantly be bewildered
and will keep company with evildoers.

And further, another preparation:
the root of amṛta, white arka,
and likewise the fruit of bakurī —
these in equal measure:
a skilled one should make pills.
If one constantly holds a drop of this,
the three worlds will be bewildered.
Take the amṛta of the mouth
and with the nameless finger of the left hand,
consecrate all the pills with this mantra:

Oṃ mahāyakṣasenādhipataye mahādurdamtadamakāya nāśaya nāśaya vināśaya vināśaya vitrāpaya vitrāpaya hūṃ hūṃ sarvaduṣṭrāṃ ḍāṃ phaṭ.

This is the eighth chapter, the sequential exposition of the achievement of suppressing shadows from the tantra of Yamāntaka Red, the body, speech, and mind of all the Tathāgatas, the Blessed One.

Here the Blessed One declared the method of accomplishment for raising a corpse and so forth:

Oṃ hūṃ jaḥ aparājitaḥ. Oṃ hūṃ jaḥ ajitaḥ. Oṃ hūṃ jaḥ pūraṇa. Oṃ hūṃ jaḥ abūraṇa. Oṃ śrāṃ jaḥ śmaśānādhipatiḥ. Oṃ ryuṃ ja kuleśvaraḥ. Oṃ hūṃ jaḥ bhūteśvaraḥ. Oṃ aṃ jaḥ.

The phrases "kiṃ karota tatamas" and so forth are the great lords of spirits. If one undertakes the accomplishment of that which cannot be achieved by others, one should first perform a preliminary recitation of one hundred thousand repetitions before the glorious Vajradaṇḍadhara — the one who wields the vajra staff — and upon completing that, one should perform extensive offerings, and then make offerings as prescribed, using white food, yogurt, molasses, and milk porridge. Having offered bdellium incense, one should recite throughout the entire night. Very early in the morning, the spirit will certainly come. If it does not come, it will die on the spot. When it has come, it will make request for permission, saying, "What shall I do?" The practitioner should then speak: "Do whatever I command." From that point on, it will perform whatever tasks are commanded. It will grant mastery of mantra-vidyā and sovereignty over kings. It will punish enemies with calamities. It will take the goddess of the moon and bestow her. Riding upon its back, one may travel to the divine worlds, and Śakra too will be granted. One will live for seven eons.

Then, if one undertakes the accomplishment of Ajita, one should recite eight thousand repetitions throughout the night before a stūpa, and on the seventh or eighth day perform extensive offerings, present a torma, offer bdellium incense, and recite. At the conclusion of the recitation, he will certainly come and say, "Ho, practitioner, what do you require of me?" The practitioner should say, "I require that you do whatever my purpose demands." From that point on, it will perform the tasks commanded. Again, it will bestow sovereignty over kings, and one will live for a thousand years.

Then, if one undertakes a further accomplishment, one should enter the inner chamber of Vajrapāṇi and recite eight thousand repetitions. On the seventh or eighth day, one should perform a torma and extensive offerings, and having presented white food, yogurt, and the offerings as described, one should recite until the middle of the night. At midnight, he will come of his own accord. Having offered flowers and ablution offerings, and having satisfied him, he will bestow sovereignty over kings. He will fulfill all desires completely. One will live for a thousand years. For as long as one lives, he will perform whatever tasks are assigned. He will also bring about the destruction of the great ones, or he will cause the great ones to live.

Then, if one undertakes the accomplishment of the Earth Mother, one should first perform ten thousand repetitions as preliminary recitation before a stūpa surrounded by māras, and thereafter, on the completion of a full month, having performed offerings with the substances as described, one should recite until the middle of the night. At midnight she will come swiftly, and having come, she will sit before one and say, "My child, what do you need?" The practitioner should say, "I require that you do whatever my purpose demands." From that point on, she will perform whatever tasks are entrusted. She will take the finest daughters of the yakṣas and bestow them. She will bestow the finest foods of desire. Each day, with reverence, she will fully bestow two garments and five coins. One will live for five hundred years.

Then, if one undertakes the accomplishment of the lord of the charnel ground, one should go to the charnel ground at night and recite one thousand repetitions. On the seventh and eighth days, one should offer a great abundance of fish, sesame, zibu-lika, white foods, yogurt, molasses, incense, fruits, and various enjoyables, and present a torma as prescribed, and offer bdellium incense, and recite until the middle of the night. At midnight, a sound of "hā hā" laughter will be heard. One should not be frightened by it. He will come surrounded by a great retinue. When he has come, having been satisfied by the torma offering, all the spirits will do whatever is commanded. Each day, eight coins will be received.

All enemies will be vanquished and one will live for a thousand years.

Then, if one undertakes the accomplishment of the Lord of Lineages, one should go to the dwelling place of the deity and offer red fragrant substances, red flowers, and bdellium incense, having first performed ten thousand repetitions of the approach practice. On the fourteenth night of the dark fortnight, one should offer a torma of red foods along with fish, sesame, and zibu-lika, accompanied by whatever offerings are appropriate, and offer sīhla incense, then recite until midnight. After that, a form of immense blazing fire, boundless and exceedingly terrifying, will appear. One should not be frightened by it. It will say, "Child, what do you want me to do?" The practitioner should say, "Do whatever I command." From then on, for as long as one lives, it will perform whatever tasks are assigned. Each day, five coins will be given at three intervals. It will bestow the finest desirable foods, and one will live for five hundred years.

Then, if one undertakes the accomplishment of the Lord of Spirits, one should go to a place where there is a single liṅga and, for three days and nights, offer a torma of red foods, fish, sesame, and zibu-lika as a child would offer it. On the third day, that being itself will come swiftly and stand before one, saying, "What do you want me to do?" The practitioner should say, "Do whatever I command." Having said this, it will follow one always, and taking a divine maiden, will present her. Having itself taken food, covering above, it will give various things in different forms. One will live for two thousand years.

Then, if one undertakes the supreme accomplishment of sending a messenger, one should enter the chamber of the Vajra-holder and on the fourteenth of the dark fortnight recite one hundred thousand repetitions, having first performed the approach practice. Starting on the seventh day, one then begins the accomplishment. One should anoint the maṇḍala with sandalwood, offer bdellium incense, and set out white foods, yogurt, butter, and milk, and kindle butter lamps on a bundle of kuśa grass, then recite throughout the entire night. At midnight, the supreme messenger itself will come. When it has come, one should satisfy it with an offering of sandalwood water. It will ask, "What do you want me to do?" The practitioner should say, "Do whatever I command." From that point on, it will perform whatever tasks are assigned. It will bestow every desired fruit and bring every desired aim to perfect fulfillment.

Then, if one undertakes the accomplishment of the Mistress of Desires, one should draw her image on a wheat leaf using musk, and remain alone on one's sleeping place reciting one hundred thousand repetitions. Then, at the end of the month, having made a great offering and kindled butter lamps, one should recite in silence. Certainly at midnight she will come. When she has come, having done as desired, she will become one's wife. Adorned with beautiful ornaments and accompanied by food, she will sleep, and by day she will be entirely absent and gone. One should refrain from consorting with other women; otherwise, she will cause total destruction.

Then, if one undertakes the accomplishment of Kanamāni, one should go to the foot of a nyagrodha tree and, in accordance with the rite, offer a fish torma. Using what remains of one's own drink as a water offering, one should recite one hundred thousand repetitions. In the same way, on the night of the seventh day, she herself will come, adorned with every ornament and together with a retinue of three hundred, and will certainly arrive at midnight. When she has come, having done as desired, she will become one's wife. She will give clothing, ornaments, and food of twelve lifetimes. Each day she will readily bestow eight coins.

Then, if one undertakes the accomplishment of the Beloved One, one should draw a picture on canvas,

golden in color, adorned with every ornament, and holding a blue lotus in hand. One should make offerings of jasmine flowers. Having offered bdellium incense, one should recite eight thousand repetitions. At the end of the month one should make offerings with whatever suitable substances are available, lighting butter lamps abundantly, and reciting continuously until midnight — whereupon she herself will come. When she has come, remaining silent, one should meditate and, having done as desired, she will become one's wife. She will protect the retinue of the practitioner. She will bestow the finest foods one could desire, along with mercury and essence extracts, and each day she will readily give twenty-five coins.

Then, if one undertakes the accomplishment of Padmā, one should anoint a maṇḍala with sandalwood at the crown-point of one's own home, offer bdellium incense, and over the course of one month recite one thousand repetitions. When the month is fully complete, one should make offerings with whatever suitable substances are available, and by reciting until midnight she will certainly come. When she has come, having done as desired, she will become one's wife. She will bestow the finest of what one desires, along with mercury, essence extracts, and the substances of attainment.

Then I shall fully and correctly explain the method for accomplishing the One-Tressed One. At the time of accomplishing the yakṣinīs (Skt. yakṣinī), one should visualize Ekajaṭī, the One-Tressed One: one face, two hands, holding a curved knife and a skull, dark blue in color, to be meditated upon in the center of the great and terrifying maṇḍala of the secret place. On the eastern petal, visualize Jambhala. In the south, the one who holds the jewel. In the west, the Lord of Waters. In the north, the one who coils like a snake. At the four intermediate directions — fire and so forth — visualize four yakṣinīs, and arrange them ceremonially: Kuṇḍalāṃ, Hāḍhinī, Rohaṃ, and the Beautiful One. Then the door-guardians are to be visualized, and on all the spokes and so forth one should properly apply colors just as was done previously with the wheel. All of them hold the characteristic signs of lords. From outside as well, one recites the mantra:

Oṃ ekajaṭi vasu sādhani svāhā.

Then I shall explain the method for accomplishing Pukkasī. Merely by visualizing her for a single instant, Pukkasī appears in all three worlds: red-golden in color, four-faced, riding a donkey upon four arms — so she is to be meditated upon. The Pukkasī of action, together with a crow, a mallet, and a bow and arrow, should be meditated upon by one of ascetic discipline. One should recite the mantra:

Oṃ pukkasi oṃ.

Then I shall fully explain the method for accomplishing Mañjuvajra. Through the attainment arising from Mañjuvajra, in a single instant one will become invisible. This is the ninth chapter, on the sequential unfolding of accomplishing the zombie yakṣinī from the Tantra of the Body, Speech, and Mind of All Tathāgatas: the Red Yamāntaka.

Then the king who holds the vajra shall explain the mantra for protection against zombies, ferocious and poisonous beasts, and the like.

By reciting four hundred thousand times,
and scattering the seed of accomplishment,
the corpse will certainly rise and serve one's purpose —
no doubt, it will come alive.

For the pacifying rite, know always
to visualize White Yamāntaka as the yoga.

Holding in one's hand the seed of accomplishment,
by reciting four hundred thousand times,
with the corpse oriented facing one,
if one then pierces it with white mustard seeds,
that very corpse will undoubtedly rise.

Should it be seized by a dark force,
visualize one like the White Hūṃ-Maker
at the heart-center of the one being accomplished.
Recite the mantras beginning with Hrīḥ Ṣṭrīḥ and so forth.
With rays of light, draw out the poisons.
Right hand and likewise left hand
hold an eight-petaled lotus.
At its center stands the White Hūṃ-Maker,
from whom rays of light rain down as nectar.
Above the crown of that one's head,
at the center of the cool-rayed maṇḍala,
the wise one should contemplate the syllable Haḥ.

Draw out the poisons of his stream through the mantras beginning with Hrīḥ Ṣṭrīḥ and so forth. With the syllables of the mantra Oṃ Hrīḥ Ṣṭrīḥ Haḥ Hrāṃḥ Jaḥ Phuḥ, perform the mantra practice from the crown of the head down to the very tips of the toes. Meditate that a stream of nectar rains down without ceasing. By this, there is no doubt he will be sustained.

The dhāraṇī, arising from the seed syllables, will become supreme in binding. At the site of a wound, meditate on this: from yellow light arises the syllable Oṃ and the rest, each capped with a strong tsheg mark, combined with the form of Haṭha. Through this joining, make the signal. Headaches will be thoroughly pacified. Endowed with supreme intellect, in the same way meditate upon others as well. Vikaṭa and ransom-rites and the like — many such conjunctions — must be firmly grasped with the hand to sustain life.

Then the King Vajradhara, the master application of the protective wheel — in order that one possessed of discipline may vanquish obstructors, misleading spirits, and malicious ones — let one fully apply the emanated wheel of the glorious club-holder, oneself. Light from the club pervades one's own body, born from the three bodies.

O Goddess, you have now attained power over the conduct, the special qualities, the grounds, and the perfections of all protecting buddhas. In the maṇḍala of the Executioner of Yama, I the master will draw the maṇḍala. Having bound the face with a cloth, at the doorway of the maṇḍala hall one calls out: "Who is it that calls? Speak saying: I, the fortunate one." Having made the interior with a curtain, the skilled one petitions the master and protector. With great reverence, for the sake of empowerment, the one who follows and protects recites three times: "Just as the Buddha, through great bliss, bestowed empowerment upon Vajrasattva, so too may the Protector bestow empowerment upon me, and grant me the supreme as well." Likewise make offering with song and music. The offering of water for the feet is similarly presented by the disciple with reverence. Praise too shall be given to that one.

Then the King Vajradhara taught emptiness thus:

Oṃ śūnyatā-jñāna-vajra-svabhāva-ātmako'ham. Oṃ pūjā-vajra-svabhāva-ātmako'ham. Oṃ dharmadhātu-svabhāva-ātmako'ham.

Thereafter I shall explain my division of the fire-offering rites beginning with pacification and the rest. By whichever fire offering one performs, all activities will be accomplished. For pacification, with a peaceful mind; for enrichment, with an enriching mind; for magnetization, with a mind of passionate longing; for the secret activity, with a wrathful mind.

For pacification the form is that of a maṇḍala; for enrichment, the form of a pond; for magnetization, a half-moon; for slaying, the form of the space element.

For pacification the measure is one cubit; for enrichment likewise one and a half cubits. For enrichment and equally for magnetization, for slaying, the measure is twenty finger-widths. For pacification the depth is half a cubit; for enrichment the full cubit measure; for slaying, five times two. For enrichment, draw in according to the same proportion.

The pacification fire offering is on the first of the month; enrichment at the full moon; wrathful activity on the fourteenth; magnetization activity is on the eighth.

For the fish-shaped vessel and crystal, for pacification the supreme substance is pearl fruit; the conch offspring sustains life; for freedom from illness, mumena. Red sandalwood, the special quality of red coral, ragana and dark lung-thang, human bone, and likewise those called nāgaphaṇī — for pacification, one hundred of these; for enrichment, eight hundred and more in the same way; for magnetization and drawing in, twenty-five; for wrathful activity, sixty-four.

Anoint the lumps with mantras; accomplish them with fifty mantras. At the end of every rosary bead make a spear-thrust. Likewise two Mount Merus: for pacification, from the tip of the index finger; for enrichment, at the middle finger; for magnetization and drawing in, from the ring finger; for cutting with severance, from the little finger.

For pacification, the color is white; for enrichment, yellow; for magnetization, always visualize red; for wrathful activity aimed at killing, black.

This concludes the fire-offering ritual (homa) from the Tantra of Yamāntaka, Body, Speech, and Mind of All the Tathāgatas — the Red Yamāntaka — arranged in sequential sections: the tenth chapter.

A yogin endowed with the four yogas should meditate with great festivity upon the five great sounds of music, filling the space with variegated food offerings and the like, along with things to eat, things to taste, things to drink, things to lick, and perfectly arrayed perfumes, garlands, unguents, powders, garments, parasols, victory banners, bells, pennants, and the like. In front, one sets out the four tormas — carried on the shoulder, held to the side, borne on the back — with just a little food and water around the edges, stirred three times, together with the substances of the sacred commitment as one has found them. Having arranged these and visualized them as empty, one commences.

In the realm of space, visualize a lotus with eight petals. In the pericarp together with its filaments, from the syllable YAṂ arises the maṇḍala of wind: black in color, shaped like a bow, adorned with eight white fluttering pennants at its two tips. Above that, from the syllable RĀṂ arises the fire maṇḍala: triangular, red, adorned at its three corners with the syllable RA. Above that, from three syllables RU fully transformed, atop three skulls of red heads, from the syllable ĀḤ arises a lotus vessel — white on the outside, red on the inside — which one contemplates in the form of a water-born lotus with eight petals.

On the petals of that lotus, from east and the remaining directions, from the syllables OṂ, ĀṂ, JRĪṂ, KHAṂ, and HŪṂ arise the self-forms of Vairocana and the remaining deities. Likewise, at the intermediate directions — fire's quarter and the rest — from the syllables LĀṂ, MĀṂ, PĀṂ, TĀṂ, and VĀṂ, visualize the self-forms of Locanā and the remaining goddesses.

These ten syllables, when fully transformed, are: GO, KU, DA, HA, NA; and BI, RA, ŚU, MĀ, MŪ. The ten sacred-commitment substances marked by these seed syllables are beheld in the lotus vessel. Above them, one visualizes the form covered by the syllable OṂ in white color.

From the syllable OṂ arises a moon maṇḍala; above that, from the white syllable HŪṂ arises a vajra club. That, marked by the seed syllable, is gazed upon. Below the lotus vessel, at the tripod, one visualizes and recites OṂ ĀḤ HŪṂ three times. Having previously meditated without interruption upon the great expansive wind maṇḍala and the blazing fire maṇḍala, one gazes upon the vast and blazing self-forms of all the intermediate space — from GO, KU, and the rest, the ten sacred-commitment substances dissolving — being stirred by the vajra club.

Then, through the vajra club, the nectar melts and one contemplates it in the form of the color of the newly risen sun, or in the form of quicksilver. Upon that as well, one gazes at the three syllables OṂ ĀḤ HŪṂ. From those three syllables, a great multitude of rays radiates outward, drawing in by those rays the very many nectars dwelling in the three worlds, and causing them to enter therein. As they fully enter, from the utterly supreme and fully transformed gathering of moonbeams — compressed into a form like a mass of honey piled up like snow — one presses down upon it.

From outside, bless with the three syllables. With the intention that this becomes accomplished as a torma perfected by the union of wisdom nectar — possessed of color, fragrance, taste, and essence, like the stainless light of the autumn moon — perform that aspiration.

With the mantra that assumes the form of Yamāntaka, upon the wrathful ones who hold lasso and iron hook in their hands and emanate outward, visualize that with the iron hook one pierces the hearts of the directional guardians and with the lasso binds their throats, drawing them in.

Then, with the mantra Oṃ Hrīḥ Ṣṭrīḥ Vikṛtānana Hūṃ Hūṃ Phaṭ Phaṭ Duṣṭāndra Sapārivāra Mākarṣaya Jaḥ — by means of this mantra, Indra (the god of a thousand eyes), tawny-white in color, mounted upon a white elephant, with his right and left hands bearing a vajra and the threatening gesture, is to be visualized and arrayed in the east.

Likewise, with his mantra, his attendant deity, dwelling upon a Garuḍa bird, wearing a crown of precious jewels, holding a staff and wheel in the right hand and a conch shell and jewel in the left, is stationed not far to his left.

Again likewise, Yama, the lord of the dead, black in color and mounted on a buffalo, holding a staff, is stationed in the south.

Again likewise, Varuṇa, the lord of waters, white in color and dwelling upon a serpent, holding a nāga lasso in his hand, is stationed in the west.

Again likewise, the Malevolent One (Kubera), yellow and ugly in form, dwelling upon a treasure, holding a citron (bījapūraka) and a mongoose (nakulī) in his hands, is stationed in the north.

Likewise, the one with a nose, white in color, dwelling upon a rat, holding a counting rosary and a skull-cup in the right hand, and in the left a bag and a cord.

Likewise, Īśvara (the Great God, Śiva), white in color, mounted upon a fine white bull, holding a trident and bearing a skull in his hands, with his two arms embracing Gaurī, is stationed in Īśvara's own direction.

Again likewise, Agni, the fire deity, red in color, mounted upon a goat, holding a counting rosary and a water-flask, is stationed in the direction of fire.

Again likewise, the Rākṣasa, mounted upon a corpse, holding a curved knife and skull-cup, dark blue in color, wrathful and fierce, is stationed in the direction of the south-west.

Again likewise, Vāyu, the wind deity, smoke-colored, mounted upon a tawny deer, holding a wind banner, is stationed in the direction of wind.

Again likewise, the Sun is red, holding a lotus in each of his two hands, stationed above upon his seven horses in the direction of the zenith.

Again likewise, the Moon is white, holding a white lotus in each of his two hands, mounted upon a chariot, stationed in the direction of the zenith.

Again likewise, Brahmā is tawny-white with four faces, holding a water-flask and the gesture of fearlessness, a staff and a counting rosary, mounted upon a goose, stationed in the direction of the zenith.

Again likewise, the Earth Goddess holds a vase in her left hand and the mudrā of oath and acceptance in her right, stationed in the direction of the nadir.

Again likewise, Dhṛtarāṣṭra, blue in color, dwelling upon a chariot, holds a sword and the threatening gesture in his right and left hands respectively, and is likewise stationed in his own direction.

All of these are drawn in by the wrathful deity in the form of Yamāntaka, who holds a phurba (ritual dagger) in his hand, through rays of light emanating from the seed syllables at his throat and heart. One then drives this phurba into their hearts, throats, crowns of the head, and the tips of their feet, reciting:

Oṃ Hrīḥ Ṣṭrīḥ Vikṛtānana Hūṃ Hūṃ Phaṭ Phaṭ Hūṃ Sarvān Tadīna Kīlaya Hūṃ Phaṭ.

This means that the phurba, accomplished in an unbroken continuity through the gathering of bodhicitta-fluid and so forth, establishes all of these in the form perfected as Yamāntaka. At the ultimate conclusion accomplished through the sequential signs of the seeds of the form of bodhicitta-fluid and the wind-seed, that which is accomplished from the seed syllable Hrīḥ is Indra. That accomplished from the seed syllable Ṣṭrīḥ is Yama. That accomplished from the seed syllable Vi is Varuṇa. That accomplished from the seed syllable Kṛ is the Malevolent One. That accomplished from the seed syllable Tā is Īśvara. That accomplished from the seed syllable A is Fire. That accomplished from the seed syllable Na is the south-west. That accomplished from the seed syllable Na is Wind. That accomplished from the seed syllable Hūṃ is the Moon. That accomplished from the seed syllable Hūṃ is the Sun. That accomplished from the seed syllable Phaṭ is Brahmā. That accomplished from the seed syllable Phaṭ is the Stream of Wealth.

That accomplished from the seed syllable Sa is Viṭhāgra. That accomplished from the seed syllable Hā is all spirits. And so in the same way one should conceive each as being fully accomplished.

Concerning how they abide with respect to their eyes and other sense faculties: likewise, with respect to the eyes and other faculties as they appear in the perfect bodies and so forth, just as one confers empowerment upon the vajra body and the rest — so here, each is crowned by Yamāntaka. One should then offer the incense-scattering together with the water-offering and the like, and also present the mouth-cooling water and so forth.

The presentation of the outer torma (Skt. bali) is as follows: preceded by the complete sequence of the nectar-accomplishment ritual, one praises each of them with the three — the suchness — and then meditates by imagining their tongues being well engaged. The nectar is offered with this mantra:
