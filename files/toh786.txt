The Dhāraṇī that Thoroughly Pacifies All Hostility (Ārya-sarva-dveṣa-praśamanī-nāma-dhāraṇī)

This text, known in English as *The Dhāraṇī That Thoroughly Pacifies All Hostility*, and referred to in Sanskrit and Tibetan traditions by titles corresponding to this meaning (Tibetan: འཕགས་པ་སྡང་བ་ཐམས་ཅད་རབ་ཏུ་ཞི་བར་བྱེད་པའི་གཟུངས), is a short dhāraṇī text belonging to the Kangyur's collection of protective and ritual incantations. It opens with homage to the Three Jewels and to the Tathāgata, followed by a sacred mantra invoking blessings—associated here with figures such as Mañjuśrī—for the pacification of enmity and the cultivation of love among all beings. As is typical of this genre, the text emphasizes the transformative power of ritual speech and devotion, promising that those who recite or offer prayers connected to it will inspire universal affection rather than hostility. This work is catalogued as Toh 786 in the Kangyur, situated among the dhāraṇī literature devoted to protection, pacification, and the generation of harmonious relations through sacred formulas.

If one offers prayers to Śrī (Glory), all people will become loving.

This completes the dhāraṇī that thoroughly pacifies all noble ones' hostility.

Homage to the Three Jewels.

Homage to the Tathāgata, the Arhat, the Perfectly Complete Buddha.

Oṃ kumāru rūpadhāraṇi viśvasaṃbhava āgaccha āgaccha laghu laghu bhrūṃ bhrūṃ hūṃ hūṃ jina jika mañjuśriye tāraya māṃ sarva duḥkhebhyaḥ.
