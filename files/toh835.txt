The Noble Lasso of Skillful Means, the Lotus Garland

The Noble Lasso of Skillful Means, the Lotus Garland, known in Tibetan as Phakpa Thabkyi Zhakpa Padmo Threng and in Sanskrit, by reconstruction, as the Ārya Upāyapāśa Padmamālā, is a tantra of the Ancient, or Nyingma, tradition, closely associated with the Guhyagarbha cycle of Mahāyoga. Framed as a dialogue in which glorious Vajrasattva, after making vast offerings, questions glorious Vairocana, it serves as a condensed explanation of the meaning of the tantra's practice: the peaceful and wrathful deities of the maṇḍala, the root and action mantras of the ten wrathful ones, their mudrās, and the four kinds of ritual activity, namely pacifying, enriching, magnetizing and fierce, each with its own maṇḍala, fire offering, ritual dagger and torma. It closes with an explanation of the symbolism of the maṇḍala, in which every deity and emblem is shown to express the inseparability of the expanse of reality and knowing wisdom, and with a hymn of praise to the wrathful deities. It is Toh 835 in the Kangyur, where it belongs to the section of Ancient Tantras.

The Noble Lasso of Skillful Means, the Lotus Garland.

Homage to glorious Vajrasattva.

The condensed meaning of the Noble Lasso of Skillful Means, the Lotus Garland.

Then the Blessed One, glorious Vajrasattva, made offerings to the Blessed One, glorious Sarvavid Vairocana, with an unbroken expanse of offerings arisen from the blessing of awareness: celestial palaces, canopies, victory banners, pennants and parasols, the four inner offerings of the graceful goddess and the rest, and the four outer offerings of incense and the rest. He circumambulated him three times, bowed with the homage of an awareness that is not separate, sat down before him, and made this request: "Blessed One, the domain of the buddhas, the blessed ones, as known by the buddhas, the blessed ones, who have come in the three times, is not shared with the śrāvakas. By what means is it realized? Please explain it, so that one may attain the state of omniscience in this very life."

When he had made this request, the Blessed One, without saying anything, smiled, and then declared: "Great being, listen! This meaning and all phenomena, outer and inner, are alike: they are gathered into the two truths. And the two truths should be understood in this way:

The two truths are in the manner of cause and result.
When one abides in suchness,
The outer and the inner, the vessel and its contents, are utterly pure,
And all one's activity becomes the two accumulations.
The ten perfections are completed,
And as one traverses the ten grounds in sequence,
By the vajra of the awareness of equality
The grounds are joined in equality.
Through the vajra-like wisdom,
Because all phenomena are known as illusion,
The habitual tendencies toward entity and non-entity are burned;
This is also called the fire of wisdom.
The ripening of cause and result
Is not nonexistent: it is accomplished through the concordance of cause and result.
Likewise, through the wisdom of equality,
Awareness becomes the accumulation.
Among paths, this is the short path, blissful and complete;
Among means, it is the unerring and sublime one.
If one practices it, in this very life even great buddhahood,
The vajra of the three kāyas free of accepting and rejecting, is attained."

"Great being, I explain these pledges (samayas) for the benefit of self and others.

To hold the vow in which there is nothing to guard,

One wields mastery just as one wishes."

I teach this

From the Noble Lasso of Skillful Means, the Lotus Garland, the fifth chapter, on the maṇḍala of meditative absorption.

"Then, great being, all domains of experience should be understood in this way:

The five great elements, too, are the tathāgatas;
Know even a single one as five times five.
They are the very nature of the three realms,
And that is also called the three kāyas.
The five wisdoms and the five classes of beings
Are, through equality, each indivisible.
When one wields mastery through mantra and mudrā,
Every domain of experience is a maṇḍala."

The yogin who has a knowledge-holder consort
Moves in dance and performance
And blesses the gathering and radiating of light;

From the Noble Lasso of Skillful Means, the Lotus Garland, the ninth chapter, on mantras.

Calling to Vajrasattva, "Great being, listen!", he explained:

"Within the very state of the gods and goddesses,
Through mastery of the five limbs,
Every movement is a mudrā.
So the hero declared.
Now, the characteristics of the mudrās:
Within the very state of skillful means and wisdom,
Having trained the movements of the hands,
The palms joined firmly, crossed at the roots and lying side by side,
Is declared the vajra joining of palms.
The fingers interlaced inward and pressed tight,
The middle fingers, index fingers, thumbs and little fingers
Touching tip to tip in pairs and spread outward,
The ring fingers side by side and raised: this is the pledge seal.
The right toes press upon the left,
The right palm presses upon the left,
The thumb tips touch, placed in the lap:
This is the great seal of Vairocana.
From the vajra fist,
The left hand grasps the right index finger,
Joined so that each holds the other:
This is the supreme seal of union.
The two thumbs and the two little fingers
Touch tip to tip in pairs, forming a circle;
The rest radiate like light, the heels of the hands apart:
This is the mudrā of Akṣobhya.
The heels of the hands touching, the tips of the middle fingers joined,

The ring fingers and thumbs touching at the tips, the little fingers hidden:
This is the mudrā of Ratnasambhava.

The thumb tips touching, forming a circle:
This is the mudrā of Amitābha.

This is the mudrā of Amoghasiddhi.
These kings of mudrās

Holding the vajra and the bell,
At the heart, and resting oneself at the hip,

The hands crossed and held in fists,

This is the great seal of Vajrarāja.

The heels of the hands joined, the middle fingers touching at the tips,
The thumbs, index and ring fingers bent at the joints,

This is the great seal of Vajrasūrya.
The left forearm raised,

This is the great seal of Vajradhvaja.
The right placed upon the left,

The fingertips of the left

The thumb and little finger touching at the tips,

The left fist resting at the hip:

The hands crossed, the tips interlaced,
The tips of the two thumbs touching,

This is the great seal of Vajracakra.
From the fingers interlaced inward,
The middle fingers touching at the tips, the index fingers joined,
The thumbs pressing upon the middle fingers:
This is the great seal of Vajrabhāṣa.
From the wrists crossed,
At the first joints of the two middle fingers
The fingers rest, as on the ground, on the four sides:
This is the mudrā of Vajrakarma.
The fingers of both hands
Woven together as on a loom,
The two thumbs should be raised:
This is the mudrā of Vajrarakṣa.
From the vajra fist,
The two index fingers raised,
One should hold them at the two cheeks:
This is the great seal of Vajrayakṣa.
From the vajra fists,
Crossed, their faces joined together,
One should hold them firmly at the heart:

Having meditated on the mudrās of the families,

One should give rise to conviction in gathering in and radiating out,

The thumbs and little fingers touching within,
The middle fingers joined at the tips, the four others raised,
The heels of the hands touching as though holding a vajra:
This is the mudrā of Vajradhara.

The sixteen sattvas, lords of the families,

oṃ Through your great former vow,

Homage to the Body of Dharma, beyond all reference!

Having praised him in this way, they entered into his very heart. Then, in play, he entered into equipoise in the meditative absorption of the wrathful ones and taught the ritual of the wrathful ones.

Then Vajrapāṇi said this to the Blessed One, glorious Vajrasattva: "Blessed One, in the present and in future times, naked ascetics, wandering ascetics and others, through the rites of their own spells, will incite worldly gods such as Mahādeva (Śiva), the mighty yakṣas and all the sorceresses, and will create obstacles to the lives of sentient beings, harm them, and cause them great suffering. By what means is this to be turned back? How are their workings to be cut off?"

He declared: "Vajrapāṇi, listen! This was taught in the past by the lord of the vajra body, speech and mind of all the tathāgatas. It turns back even all the secret mantras made by you yourself, Vajrapāṇi, and all the secret mantras that bring existence to an end; it suppresses them; it cuts off their workings. What need is there to speak of others, such as wandering ascetics?"

Then the Blessed One entered into equipoise in the meditative absorption of the vajra weapon and, from the vajra of body, speech and mind, spoke this, called "Turning Back and Suppressing Secret Mantras and Cutting Off Their Workings":

oṃ vajra jvala vajra hūṃ mantra suṃbhani oṃ vajra jvala hūṃ oṃ brūṃ hūṃ brūṃ trūṃ brūṃ hūṃ brūṃ hrāṃḥ brūṃ ā brūṃ

As soon as he had spoken this, a great rain of weapons fell from the ten directions, and Vajrapāṇi himself fell on his face and vomited blood. All the gods and yakṣas, too, fainted, and their bodies were cut into pieces. Then the Blessed One, knowing that their workings had been cut off, raised them up again:

oṃ vajra satva rāja jha

Then the Blessed One said to Vajrapāṇi: "Vajrapāṇi, you too must not transgress this. If all your retinue were to transgress it, they would all be destroyed. Likewise, Vajrapāṇi, you must command all the gods and yakṣas."

Then Vajrapāṇi said: "Blessed One, here it is I who tame all the three realms of existence, I who tame all the mighty, I who crush the pride of all the mighty. Just as the Blessed One Śākyamuni gave me the name Vajrapāṇi because I possess might and strength, so here, when this mantra was uttered through the blessing of the Blessed One Vajrasattva, even I vomited blood and fell; what need is there to speak of others? This is hard to transgress. I shall likewise command the gods and yakṣas."

Then the Blessed One explained the rite for accomplishing this secret mantra in future times.

In the east is the sorceress Gaurī, blue in color, with two arms. In the left she holds a skull bowl filled with red sandalwood; in the right she holds a staff made of a small corpse. If one recites oṃ vajra gaurī jaḥ jaḥ hūṃ one thousand and eight times and entrusts her with the task, she can summon even the buddhas; what need is there to speak of others? Having entrusted her with the words "Bring so-and-so!", one recites oṃ rulu rulu hūṃ bhyo over the torma and the argha of great red sandalwood, and raises the argha three times.

In the south is Caurī, yellow in color, drawing a bow and arrow in her hands. If one entrusts her with oṃ vajra caurī hūṃ jaḥ one thousand and eight times, she can steal even the might of Vajrapāṇi until not a speck of it remains; what need is there to speak of others?

In the west is Pramohā, red, holding a makara banner in her hand. If one recites oṃ vajra pramoha hūṃ jaḥ one thousand and eight times and entrusts her with the task, even Vajrapāṇi will lay aside his own might and come under control; what need is there to speak of others?

In the north is Vetālī, black in color, holding a small corpse in her left hand and eating it, and brandishing a vajra in her right. If one recites oṃ vajra vaittali hūṃ jaḥ one thousand and eight times and entrusts her with the task, even all the accomplishments, worldly and beyond the world, are accomplished; what need is there to speak of petty worldly practices?

In the southeast is Pukkasī, reddish yellow in color, with her two hands drawing out the entrails of a small corpse and eating them. If one recites oṃ vajra pukkasi hūṃ jaḥ one thousand and eight times and entrusts her with the task, she can bind and drag off even Vajrapāṇi; what need is there to speak of binding and devouring others?

In the southwest is Ghasmarī, green in color, holding in her left hand a skull bowl filled with red sandalwood, stirring it with a vajra and drinking. If one recites oṃ vajra ghasmari hūṃ jaḥ one thousand and eight times and entrusts her with the task, she can intoxicate even Vajrapāṇi; what need is there even to speak of others?

In the northwest is Vajrakarma, green in color, holding a small corpse with both hands and eating its heart. If one recites oṃ vajra karma hūṃ jaḥ one thousand and eight times and entrusts her with the task, every rite one has to perform will be accomplished.

In the northeast is Caṇḍālī, pale yellow in color, holding in her right hand a skull bowl made from a small corpse and in her left a corpse staff. If one recites oṃ vajra caṇḍali hūṃ jaḥ one thousand and eight times and entrusts her with the task, she makes the line of descent increase even for the sterile and for eunuchs; what need is there to speak of increasing the line of descent, the lifespan and the power of those whose faculties are complete and unimpaired?

When urging these sorceresses to their activity, the offering of argha and torma is done as stated above.

If they are wrathful, all of them hold blazing tridents in their hands and in the left carry a small drum. Their seat is dark purple-black fire. Their bodies are black in color, adorned with clusters of drops of blood, grease and ash, their waists bound with entrails, and they wear golden hides draped across them; their bodies are adorned with srodta and aja. The host of naked ones blazes dark purple-black, the left hand carrying a small drum and the right dark purple fire. All of them, black in color, burn up the world system, and having consumed it, they are dissolved back into the body of the Great Glorious One.

Next, I shall explain the outer stage.

In the east is Siṃhamukhā, yellow in color, holding a small corpse in her mouth, her right and left hands crossed, with the head of a lion. In the south is Vyāghrīmukhā, red in color, her two hands crossed, gazing at a corpse lying before her, with the head of a tiger. In the west is Śṛgālamukhā, black in color, holding a small corpse with her two hands and licking it with her tongue, with the head of a fox. In the north is Śvānamukhā, blue in color, tearing open a corpse with her two hands and gazing into it, with the head of a dog. In the southeast is Gṛdhramukhā, dark red in color, holding the entrails of a small corpse in her mouth, a skull bowl in her right hand and a knife in her left, with the head of a vulture. From the Noble Lasso of Skillful Means, the Lotus Garland, the fortieth chapter, on the benefits of accomplishing the rites.

The name of this maṇḍala is the maṇḍala of the Gathering of Great Power. Making the base of the maṇḍala round signifies the dharmadhātu (the expanse of phenomena), free of elaboration. Making the wheel with spokes and corners, set within the round base, signifies that, while the dharmadhātu remains free of elaboration, the deities of blessing, the goddesses, the wrathful gods, the wrathful goddesses, the messengers, and the rest radiate forth of themselves. In another respect, it signifies that, while the dharmadhātu is free of conceptual thought, omniscient wisdom, which knows whatever there is, is spontaneously present.

Making the outer border square signifies that great bliss expands through mastery over all the worlds, both the container and its contents, of the ten directions. The five goddesses at the center face outward: the goddesses are the blessing of the nearness of the expanse itself, and this signifies that the sattvas see the nearness of the expanse itself. The five sattvas facing inward signifies looking upon the nearness of the expanse itself. The other sattvas facing outward means that the expanse itself is at no time inert like a stone, but is endowed with knowing wisdom, and that the sattva itself is not other than the expanse. The other female sattvas facing inward means that knowing wisdom is not conceptual thought with regard to things and all the rest, but is endowed with the expanse itself, and that the expanse itself, too, is not other than knowing wisdom. In another respect, the sattvas facing outward signifies mastery over all the worlds, container and contents; the female sattvas facing inward signifies that all the worlds, container and contents, are gathered under one's power; and the sattvas and female sattvas, outer and inner, gazing at one another means bringing delight. All the wrathful gods and goddesses facing outward signifies crushing and taming outer things, adversaries, and the like.

Here the expanse is like the disk of the sun. Knowing wisdom is like the sun's light, shining and clear. Its blessing is like its being warm and gentle. Crushing and taming outer things and the like is like trees and plants being dried out and burnt. The goddesses and female sattvas are the meaning of the awareness of the dharmadhātu appearing to the mind. The gods and sattvas are the seeing of the meaning that appears in the expanse. That, in the state of blessing of appearing and seeing within the expanse itself, the nature of things and adversaries is spontaneously crushed, and they are tamed by being joined to the expanse, signifies that the wrathful gods and goddesses perform the four rites as their very nature and protect the world with compassion. The world protectors and the rest abide, through blessing, as messengers. In another respect, this signifies the teaching that the mighty worldly gods are gathered as the retinue.

The eight auspicious symbols are auspicious because, though one has mastery over all and engages in saṃsāra, one does not become bound by the afflictions, and though one engages in nirvāṇa, one does not fall into the one-sided peace of the śrāvakas, and so one is overpowered by nothing at all. Among them, the endless knot is the auspiciousness of possessing knowing wisdom. The wheel is the auspiciousness of mastery over all and of turning the wheel of Dharma. The jewel is the auspiciousness of possessing what is inexhaustible. The lotus is the auspiciousness of not being seized by saṃsāra. The parasol is the auspiciousness of not being tormented by the afflictions. The vase is the auspiciousness of possessing the supreme nectar. The conch is the auspiciousness of proclaiming a sound that overawes all the doors. The fish signifies that, though one acts for the benefit of the beings of the five paths, there is no fear.

The four swirls are the blessing of the five families; or they show that the beings of the five paths possess the nature of the Tathāgata; or they mean acting for their benefit through compassion. In another respect, they signify that everything is gathered under one's power.

Here the chief goddesses, each with a retinue of five, do nothing at all. The twenty sattvas and female sattvas are like a parent to an only child toward all. By the force of acting in this way, the wrathful gods and goddesses with their retinues are, toward all outer and inner things, like a hunter who has sighted game. In this way, by the force of aversion, everything is joined to the expanse itself. When there is abundance, though one engages in the things of saṃsāra, it is great enjoyment, and since one does not become bound by the afflictions, to practice in this way is desire. The dharmadhātu being by nature secret is jealousy. Dharmatā being free of elaboration is delusion. Such dharmatā being victorious over the three realms is pride.

Here, the blessing of the chief lord with his retinue of five pervades everything, yet its immediate seat is the head, the front from the chest upward, and the back from the base of the spine upward. The blessing of Akṣobhya with his retinue of five is the right hand and the right side of the ribs. The blessing of Ratnasambhava with his retinue of five is the left hand and the left side of the ribs. The blessing of Amitābha with his retinue of five is from the left foot up to the left waist. The blessing of Amoghasiddhi with his retinue of five is from the right foot up to the left waist. All the wrathful gods and goddesses follow the blessing of their respective families. In this way, each of the five retinues also becomes fivefold. Everything that performs the function of solidity is the vajra family. Everything that performs the function of warmth is the jewel family. Everything that performs the function of moisture is the lotus family. Everything that performs the function of movement is the karma family. What is accomplished in general from these in equal portions, actually present, is the tathāgata family.

The blessing of She Who Is the Self of All, with her retinue of five, is everything that has become central. The absence of an intrinsic nature of solidity is the blessing of Locanā with her retinue of five. The absence of an intrinsic nature of warmth is the blessing of Māmakī with her retinue of five. The absence of an intrinsic nature of moisture is the blessing of Pāṇḍaravāsinī with her retinue of five. The absence of an intrinsic nature of movement is the blessing of Tārā with her retinue of five. That which is able to perform a function as its purpose is the sattva. Likewise, the absence of intrinsic nature is the female sattva. Awareness of it in this way is the wrathful gods and goddesses. All gathering in and sending forth, too, should be done in this way. All outer and inner phenomena, too, should be viewed in this way.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

"Universal Light" is the eleventh ground (Samantaprabhā); "Lotus Eye" is rendered literally and appears to name a further, higher ground or state beyond it. In chapter seven the Tibetan reads "when Vajrasattva had heard," where the parallel formula in chapter eight reads "calling to Vajrasattva"; it is translated as written. The Tibetan also gives "the left foot" twice in chapter seven, where one would expect the right foot for the first; this is translated as written. "The five great" in chapter six is taken as the five great elements. Chapter eight contains the language of sexual yoga (the lotus and vajra, the descent of the awakening mind) as is usual in this class of tantra.  In the description of the Vajrakarma mudrā, the phrase rendered "as on the ground" (literally "the fingers of the earth") is obscure and may reflect a copyist's slip. In the Vajralāsyā mudrā, the word taken as "the body" is uncertain in the Tibetan. "Rites" in the line on the kings of mudrās renders a word that may instead refer to the action seal (karmamudrā).  The word rendered "the Stira" (a figure struck, bound and devoured, apparently the victim or effigy of the rite) is unidentified, and so is "srodta and aja" in the description of the wrathful sorceresses; both are kept as they stand.
