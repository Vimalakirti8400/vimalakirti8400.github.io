The Noble Terrifying All Māras (Āryasarvamārabhayaṃkara)

The Noble Text Called Terrifying All Māras, known in Tibetan as Phakpa Dü Tamché Trakpar Jepa, is a very brief dhāraṇī that the colophon also names The Dhāraṇī That Terrifies the Māras. After an opening homage to the Blessed One, Śākyamuni, it presents a single Sanskrit mantra invoking the jewel-like radiance of all the tathāgatas and the essence of the realm of phenomena. It then states the mantra's benefits: when it is recited, the gods of the white side rejoice and offer protection, while the black māras and malicious, haughty spirits are terrified and faint. The work is Toh 1053 in the Kangyur, where it belongs to the collection of dhāraṇī texts, short works centered on protective incantations meant for recitation.

The noble text called Terrifying All Māras.

Homage to the Blessed One, Śākyamuni.

oṃ sarva tathāgata maṇi śata dīpti jvala jvala dharma dhātu garbhe maṇi maṇi mahṣa maṇi hṛdaya maṇi svāhā

By reciting this mantra, the gods of the white side rejoice and give protection. All the black māras are frightened and faint. The malicious haughty spirits are terrified and faint.

The Dhāraṇī That Terrifies the Māras is complete.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

"Haughty spirits" renders dregs pa, a class of arrogant, violent beings in Tibetan usage. The final syllable is written sho; it is read as the ordinary closing particle so, meaning "is complete." In the opening title, "text" stands for the implied noun of zhes bya ba ("that which is called").
