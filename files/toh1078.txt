The Dhāraṇī Called Surūpa (Surūpanāmadhāraṇī)

The Dhāraṇī Called Surūpa, known in Sanskrit as the Surūpanāmadhāraṇī and in Tibetan as Su ru pa zhes bya ba'i gzungs, is a brief ritual text centered on a dhāraṇī, a sacred incantation addressed to the Tathāgata Surūpa, whose name means "Beautiful Form." After a homage to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas, it presents the incantation and gives simple instructions for an offering to hungry ghosts. The practitioner recites the dhāraṇī seven times over food mixed with water, snaps the fingers of the left hand three times, and leaves the food in a secluded place for the hungry ghosts dwelling in all world systems, ideally before eating oneself. The text promises that, in this way, the hungry ghosts will be satisfied. This practice is closely related to the widespread Buddhist custom of feeding hungry spirits. It is Toh 1078 in the Kangyur, where it belongs to the class of dhāraṇī texts gathered among the scriptures attributed to the Buddha.

In the language of India: surūpanāmadhāraṇī. In the language of Tibet: The Dhāraṇī Called Surūpa.

Homage to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas.

namaḥ surūpāya tathāgatāya arhate soṃmyaksaṃbuddhaya tadyathā oṃ suru suru brasuru prasuru tara tara bhara bhara saṃbhara saṃbhara smara smara santarpaya santarpaya sarvapretānāṃ svāhā

One should recite this dhāraṇī seven times over food mixed with water. Then, having snapped the fingers of the left hand three times, one should give it to all the hungry ghosts in a secluded place, and one should also say these words: "You who seek an opportunity and look for an opening, depart! I offer this food to the hungry ghosts dwelling in all world systems."

It should be given before one has eaten oneself. In this way, each and every hungry ghost will have been given food amounting to a great measure of rice.

If one does this, then life after life one will not become weak, and one will not become poor. One will have great strength, be beautiful and a joy to behold, and be wealthy with abundant possessions. One will be long-lived and free of illness, and will swiftly and fully awaken to unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening. And after dying and passing on, one will be born in the world system of Sukhāvatī.

The dhāraṇī called Surūpa is complete.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

"A great measure of rice" renders the Tibetan "bre bo che," a large unit of volume, probably standing for the Indian droṇa measure. The food "mixed with water" (Tibetan zan) is a moistened porridge or dough, typically of barley flour. In the closing line the Tibetan translates the name Surūpa as "Good Form"; it is given here in its Sanskrit form for consistency.
