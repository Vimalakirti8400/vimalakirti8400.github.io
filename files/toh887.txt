The Noble Dhāraṇī Called Great Mahīndra (Āryamahāmahīndranāmadhāraṇī)

The Noble Dhāraṇī Called Great Mahīndra, known in Sanskrit as the Ārya Mahāmahīndra Nāma Dhāraṇī and in Tibetan as Phakpa Sai Wangpo Chenpo Zhe Jawai Zung, is a short scripture centered on a dhāraṇī, a sacred formula of protective and spiritual power. It opens in the classic sūtra style, recounting that the buddha Samantaprabha was staying in the dwelling of the brahmin Mahāmahīndra, a place attended by gods, and that the brahmin approached him, bowed at his feet, and sat to one side to address him. From this setting the text leads into the teaching and recitation of the dhāraṇī associated with the brahmin's name. The work is Toh 887 in the Kangyur, where it belongs among the dhāraṇī texts gathered in the canon's collections of incantations and protective formulas.

In the language of India: ārya mahāmahīndra nāma dhāraṇī. In the language of Tibet: The Noble Dhāraṇī Called Great Mahīndra.

Homage to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas.

Thus did I hear at one time. The Blessed One, the perfectly and fully awakened buddha Samantaprabha, was staying in the dwelling of the brahmin Mahāmahīndra, who was like a great sāla tree, a place attended by gods. Then the brahmin Mahāmahīndra, who was like a great sāla tree, went to where the Blessed One, the perfectly and fully awakened buddha Samantaprabha, was. When he arrived, he bowed his head at the feet of the Blessed One, the perfectly and fully awakened buddha Samantaprabha, and sat down to one side. Seated to one side, the brahmin Mahāmahīndra, who was like a great sāla tree, said this to the Blessed One, the perfectly and fully awakened buddha Samantaprabha:

"Blessed One, I have made the aspiration for unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening. By what means will I remember my births? Blessed One, please explain well to me the method by which I will not forget the mind set on unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening."

When he had made this request, the Blessed One, the perfectly and fully awakened buddha Samantaprabha, said this to the brahmin Mahāmahīndra, who was like a great sāla tree:

"Therefore, son, if you aspire in this way, then take up this heart of the vidyā-mantra of Samantaprabha! Hold it! Read it! Master it completely! What is the heart of the vidyā-mantra of Samantaprabha?

Son, this is the heart of the vidyā-mantra of Samantaprabha. Therefore, son, keep this heart of the vidyā-mantra of Samantaprabha in mind whether you are walking, standing, sitting, or lying down.

Son, whoever bears in mind this heart of the vidyā-mantra of Samantaprabha, whatever their name, family, or lineage may be, whether a man, a woman, a boy, or a girl, a god or a goddess, a nāga or a nāginī, a gandharva or a gandharvī, an asura or an asurī, a kinnara or a kinnarī, a male or female mahoraga, a male or female preta, a male or female piśāca, a male or female kumbhāṇḍa, a male or female pūtana, if they hold, read, transmit, teach, or explain this heart of the vidyā-mantra of Samantaprabha, they will remember their past lives for up to seven lifetimes. For as long as the cycle of existence lasts, they will not forget the mind set on unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening. Whether they become gods or humans, they will not meet an untimely death by weapons, poison, water, or fire.

When the Blessed One had spoken thus, the brahmin Mahāmahīndra, who was like a great sāla tree, rejoiced and praised what the Blessed One had said.

This concludes the noble dhāraṇī called Great Mahīndra.

The phrase "like a great sāla tree" applied to the brahmin seems to render the Sanskrit idiom brāhmaṇa-mahāśāla, "a brahmin of great wealth or standing". The phrase "a place attended by gods" renders a Tibetan expression meaning "endowed with gods"; it could also be the name of the dwelling. In the list of seven buddhas, the first name reads only "Samanta, the Victor" (or "All-Victorious"); it may stand for Samantaprabha, but it is given as written.
