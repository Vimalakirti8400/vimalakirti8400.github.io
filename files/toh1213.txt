The Eight Charnel Grounds (Aṣṭaśmaśānanāma)

The Eight Charnel Grounds, known in Sanskrit as Aṣṭaśmaśāna and in Tibetan as Dur khrod brgyad, is a short versified work in the tantric tradition of the deity Heruka. After paying homage to the guru as the glorious hero Heruka, who bestows both enjoyment and liberation, the author explains that he is writing as an aid to his own memory. He records the names and directional placement of the eight charnel grounds that encircle the mandala, together with their trees, the guardians of the directions, the nāga lords, and the rain-pouring cloud beings, all arranged in sets of four within the protective vajra tent. Such charnel ground descriptions belong to the visualization of the deity's mandala in practices associated with Heruka and the Cakrasaṃvara cycle. The text is Toh 1213 in the Tengyur, where it is placed among the tantric treatises and commentaries.

In the language of India: aṣṭaṣmaśananāma. In the language of Tibet: The Eight Charnel Grounds.

Homage to the glorious Heruka.

The guru is Heruka, the glorious hero,
beautiful with innate bliss,
who bestows the supreme fruits of enjoyment and liberation.
Having bowed to the guru's lotus feet,
I shall write down, so that I myself may remember,
the names and the directional arrangement
of the charnel grounds, the trees, the guardians of the directions,
the nāga lords, and the pourers of rain.

In the directions beginning with the east, moving to the left,
and in the directions beginning with Īśāna, moving to the right,
within the vajra tent,
they are to be arranged four and four.
Caṇḍogra, Gahara,
Blazing Ring, and Skull-Bearer,
terrifying, are set in the directions
beginning with the east, moving to the left.
Aṭṭahāsa is in Īśāna's quarter;
in the quarter of Agni is the one with Lakṣmī;
Ghorāndhakāra is in Nairṛti's quarter;
in Vāyu's quarter is the one with the cry of "kili kila."
With hosts of vetālas and bhūtas,
and with the terror of the jackals' howling,
within the vajra tent
the charnel grounds have frightening forms.
In this very order
the eight charnel grounds are situated.

What are the trees, the guardians of the directions, the nāga lords, and the lords of the rain clouds?

The śirīṣa and the aśvattha,
the kaṅkelli tree, the cūta,
the Like-the-Former, the vaṭa, the karañja.
Indra and Kubera,
Varuṇa, then the lord Yama,
Īśāna, then the lord Agni,
the lord of the rākṣasas, and the lord Vāyu.
Vāsuki's son and Takṣaka,
Karkoṭa and Padma,
Mahāpadma, Huluhulu,
Kulika, and Śaṅkhapāla.
Garji, Ghurmito, the Terrifying One,
Avato and Ghana,
Varṣaṇa and Buraṇa,
and the Fierce One: these are the lords of the rain clouds.

Translated by the Indian preceptor Ratnaśrīmitra and the monk Shakya Yeshé.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

"Blazing Ring" and "Skull-Bearer" are literal renderings of two charnel-ground names. Other lists of the eight charnel grounds give names such as Jvālākula and Karaṅkin, but the Sanskrit behind these Tibetan forms is not certain. "Terrifying" (rnam 'jigs) is read here as a description of the four cardinal charnel grounds, not as a fifth name, since the four cardinal and four intermediate names already make eight.

The tree list as transmitted has only seven items, though the text speaks of eight charnel grounds. Six are identifiable: śirīṣa, aśvattha, kaṅkelli, cūta, vaṭa and karañja. "Like-the-Former" (gong mtshungs) literally renders an unidentified term. It may be the name of a tree, or a scribal "ditto." Standard lists also include trees such as latāparkaṭī and arjuna.

Klu dbang among the direction guardians is Varuṇa, lord of the waters.

The nāga list reads "Vāsuki's son" (nor rgyas bu). Standard lists simply have Vāsuki.

"The Terrifying One" and "the Fierce One" are literal renderings of two cloud-lord names whose Sanskrit forms are uncertain. The other cloud-lord names follow the text's own spellings.

"The one with Lakṣmī" refers to the charnel ground Lakṣmīvana.
