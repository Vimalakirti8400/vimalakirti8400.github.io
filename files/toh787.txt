The Dhāraṇī Thoroughly Pacifying All Misdeeds (Sarvapāpapraśamanī-nāma-dhāraṇī)

The Dhāraṇī of Thoroughly Pacifying All Misdeeds (Tibetan: Sdig pa thams cad rab tu zhi bar byed pa zhes bya ba'i gzungs) is a very short dhāraṇī text, and its Sanskrit title is not clearly attested in the material at hand. It presents a brief mantra for pacifying misdeeds, paired with a second mantra, offered after a homage to the Three Jewels, for calming the anger of others. The accompanying instruction is simple and practical: one sits facing east, recites the mantra, and rubs the face seven times with one's own hand, so that those who are angry in an assembly one then enters will become pleased. It is Toh 787 in the Kangyur, where it belongs to the Tantra section, among the many brief dhāraṇīs and ritual spells preserved there.

Phaṭ phaṭ. Samaya samaya. Amṛto tbhava, botbhava. Pāvaṃ me nāśaya svāhā. Here ends the dhāraṇī called "Thoroughly Pacifying All Misdeeds."

Homage to the Three Jewels.

Oṃ vakavace jambhani stambhani mohani sarva-roṣa-praśamani svāhā.

Sit facing the east. Recite this mantra, and seven times rub your face with your own hand. Then, if you go into an assembly, those who are angry will become pleased.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The opening mantra is transmitted in corrupt, phonetically transliterated form, and I have given a conservative reconstruction rather than a translation. Samaya means "pledge" or "commitment," and "nāśaya svāhā" probably means "destroy (my misdeeds)." "Pāvaṃ me nāśaya" most likely corresponds to "pāpaṃ me nāśaya," meaning "destroy my misdeed," which fits the dhāraṇī's title. The phrases "amṛto tbhava" and "botbhava" are uncertain and may reflect scribal corruption.

The second mantra seems to be a fuller Sanskrit form. Jambhani, stambhani, and mohani mean "paralyzing," "immobilizing," and "bewildering," followed by "pacifying all anger." The transliteration "vakavace" is uncertain.

The final instruction is ritual and is rendered closely, with "those who are angry" and "become pleased" following the Tibetan.
