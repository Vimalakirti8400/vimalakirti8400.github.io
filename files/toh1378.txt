The Heart Vajra Words (Hṛdayavajrapada)

The Heart Vajra Words, known in Sanskrit as the Hṛdayavajrapada and in Tibetan as Nyingpo Dorjé Tsik, is a very brief tantric instruction in verse, catalogued as Toh 1378 in the Tengyur, within its section of commentaries and treatises on the tantras. After paying homage to the glorious guru who reveals the all-pervading, undivided maṇḍala, it offers a few pith verses on completion-stage practice: the yoga of joining the tongue's tip to the palate, directing the mind into the jewel, radiating the wisdom maṇḍala through the three realms, and keeping the illusory body in mind even in union with one or several consorts. Its colophon records that the teaching was bestowed by the mahāsiddha Śabarapāda upon the Indian paṇḍita Vanaratna, who translated it into Tibetan together with the great translator Sönam Gyatsö Dé.

In the language of India: hṛdayavajrapadanāma. In the language of Tibet: The Heart Vajra Words.

In the yoga of joining the tip of the tongue to the palate,
The yogin who strives single-pointedly
Finds the aspect of the maṇḍala that is not in fragments,
By which all that is stable and moving is pervaded.
To the glorious guru
By whom that state was shown, I bow.

The mind, which is also the vajra sun,
Should be seen as reaching into the jewel.
By means of the aspect of the wisdom maṇḍala,
One should make the three realms shine forth.

Whether in union with a single mudrā,
Or else in joining with four mudrās,
Even when entering the town of queens,
One should keep in mind the illusory body.

The Heart Vajra Words were bestowed by Śabarapāda upon the paṇḍita Vanaratna. They were translated by that same paṇḍita and the great translator Sönam Gyatsö Dé.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The line "Finds the aspect of the maṇḍala" supplies a verb implied by the compressed Tibetan, which strings the yogin, the undivided maṇḍala and its all-pervading aspect into the object of the guru's teaching; literally the opening verse runs, "the yogin who strives single-pointedly in the yoga of joining tongue-tip and palate, the aspect of the undivided maṇḍala by which the stable and moving are pervaded, that state: to the glorious guru by whom it was shown, I bow." The "jewel" and the "mudrās" belong to the vocabulary of completion-stage yoga with a consort (karmamudrā), and the "town of queens" refers to a royal women's quarters; the closing line teaches that even amid such union one maintains awareness of the illusory body. According to tradition, Vanaratna received this teaching from the mahāsiddha Śabara in a vision.
