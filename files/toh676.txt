Homage to all buddhas and bodhisattvas.

Thus did I hear. At one time the Blessed One was dwelling beside a pool encircled by the Gaṅgā, together with a great Saṅgha of five hundred monks and a great Saṅgha of bodhisattvas. Then the Blessed One addressed the monks. The monks listened as the Blessed One directed, and the Blessed One spoke to them these words:

To the west of here
lies the world-realm of Bliss.
There dwells a perfectly awakened buddha,
the Tathāgata Amitāyus.

Whoever recites that one's name
shall be reborn in that very place.
At the time of death, the Teacher appears,
attended by the Saṅgha of monks.

There are no women there,
no beings dwelling in a womb;
from lotuses of precious jewels
great miraculous powers arise.

Food, clothing, medicines,
Dharma robes, bedding, and almsbowls —
the very instant the mind conceives of them,
they appear without delay.

The buddhas dwelling in the ten directions
sing the praises of that Bliss;
thus are the buddhas inconceivable,
and the Dharma of the buddhas inconceivable,
and the noble Saṅgha inconceivable.
To have faith in the inconceivable
brings inconceivable karmic maturation:
one is born in a pure field.

O monks, there exists a palace of the Tathāgata, arhat, perfectly and completely awakened Amitāyus, together with his retinue, measuring ten thousand leagues in breadth. He was born into the warrior caste (kṣatriya).

O monks, the father of the Tathāgata, arhat, perfectly and completely awakened Amitāyus was a wheel-turning king (cakravartin) named Supreme Goodness. His mother was named Resplendent. His queen was named Protectress of the Noble Warrior Clan.

O monks, the son of the Tathāgata Amitāyus was named Moonlight. His attendant was named Pure Fame.

O monks, the foremost among the wise of the Tathāgata Amitāyus was named Crest of Goodness. The foremost among those endowed with miraculous powers and the foremost among those endowed with great diligence was named Great Splendor.

O monks, the māra of the Tathāgata Amitāyus was named King of Offerings. The name of the god Śakra (Indra) was Pure.

O monks, the great assembly of hearers of the Tathāgata Amitāyus comprised sixty thousand great hearers.

O monks, let all sentient beings, with earnest reflection, properly hold the name of the Tathāgata Amitāyus, and for ten days cultivate mindful recollection of the buddha without distraction of mind, keeping always in mind the Tathāgata Amitāyus dwelling in the world-realm of Bliss.

Let them also constantly recite this dhāraṇī called "The Glorious King of the Drum of Deathlessness."

Three times by day and three times by night, prostrating with the five limbs, let all sentient beings meditate upon the Tathāgata Amitāyus; after ten days have passed, they shall behold the Tathāgata Amitāyus. They shall also behold all the blessed buddhas dwelling in the ten directions.

If all their roots of virtue are fully dedicated to the world-realm of Bliss, then at the time of death the Tathāgata Amitāyus shall be present before them, and they shall be born in that Tathāgata Amitāyus's buddha-field.

Monks, whatever son or daughter of a family of faithful lineage recites as taught this dhāraṇī called "The Glorious King of the Drum of Deathlessness" — let that person, dressed in clean robes, in a pure and spotless place, make offering of flowers and incense to the Tathāgata Amitāyus. Let them also bear in mind the lotus seat of the awakening ground and the magnificent awakening tree. Having aroused aspiration for these, and having brought forth aspiration and faith, they shall be born in that Tathāgata Amitāyus's buddha-field.

Monks, the king of trees of the Tathāgata Amitāyus is called "The Precious Lotus, Radiant and Resplendent" — a tree replete with flowers and fruits in abundance. Its lotus throne, known as "Pure, Clear, Beautiful, and Endowed with Auspicious Rays," is encircled by many precious gems, and there the Tathāgata Amitāyus is seated. To his right is the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara, lord of vision; to his left is the bodhisattva Mahāsthāmaprāpta, and they are surrounded by an immeasurable assembly of bodhisattvas.

Whatever son or daughter of a faithful lineage is endowed with aspiration, faith, and great devotion shall be born there. On a vast golden ground, they shall be born by miraculous transformation upon great lotuses made of the seven precious substances.

Monks, whatever monk, nun, layman, or laywoman correctly holds the name of the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Amitāyus — for them there shall arise no fear of fire, no fear of water, no fear of poison, no fear of weapons, no fear of yakṣas, and no fear of rākṣasas. The karmic maturation of past actions shall have no hold upon them whatsoever.

When the Blessed One had spoken these words, those monks and all those in that fully assembled retinue — gods, humans, asuras, and gandharvas together with the world — were filled with joy, and they rejoiced in and praised what the Blessed One had declared.
