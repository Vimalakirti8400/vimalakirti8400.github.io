The Names of Noble Mañjuśrī

The Names of Noble Mañjuśrī is a very brief dhāraṇī-style text devoted to the bodhisattva of wisdom, Mañjuśrī. Its Tibetan title is likely along the lines of Phakpa Jampal gyi Tsen, and a Sanskrit form is not clearly attested in the source lines. After an opening homage to noble Mañjuśrī, it gives a short series of mantric names, each introduced by the syllables oṃ bākye. It then declares that reciting these names twenty-one times purifies all the karmic obscurations of the five acts of immediate retribution, the gravest misdeeds in Buddhist ethics. The text is Toh 894 in the Kangyur, where it belongs among the collected dhāraṇīs and short recitation texts.

Homage to noble Mañjuśrī.

oṃ bākye tejāya oṃ bākye ṣaṣesva oṃ bākye khanajaye oṃ bākye niṣṭha a oṃ bākye ya namaḥ oṃ bākye daṃ namaḥ

By reciting these very names of noble Mañjuśrī twenty-one times, all the karmic obscurations of the five acts of immediate retribution (ānantarya) are purified.

This completes The Names of Noble Mañjuśrī.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

Here མཚན has its ordinary sense of "name," as the title shows, not the technical sense of a major mark of a great being. The "five acts of immediate retribution" are the five heinous deeds that lead straight to hell at death without any intervening rebirth.
