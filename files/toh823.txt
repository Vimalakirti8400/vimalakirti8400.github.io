Structured as a sequence of verses praising these enlightened figures and calling forth auspiciousness in the present moment, the work belongs to the genre of auspicious-verse (bkra shis tshigs bcad) liturgies recited for blessing and ceremonial purposes. Its Sanskrit title, if it existed, is not preserved in the available materials, though its Tibetan title can be reconstructed as bKra shis tshigs su bcad pa lnga pa or similar, reflecting its structure of five sets of auspicious verses. The auspicious verses of the five are complete.

Homage to the Three Jewels.
The auspicious verses of the three families.

The Blessed One, like a mountain king of golden hue,
Protector of the three worlds, the youthful form of Mañjuśrī—
Whatever auspiciousness belongs to the body of the Sugata (the "Well-Gone One," an epithet of a buddha),
By that auspiciousness may there be goodness and excellence here today.

The Blessed One, like a mountain king of conch-white hue,
Protector of the three worlds, Lord Avalokiteśvara—
Whatever auspiciousness belongs to the speech of the Sugata,
By that auspiciousness may there be goodness and excellence here today.

The Blessed One, like a mountain king of greenish-yellow hue,
Protector of the three worlds, Vajrapāṇi—
Whatever auspiciousness belongs to the mind of the Sugata,
By that auspiciousness too may there be goodness and excellence here today.
