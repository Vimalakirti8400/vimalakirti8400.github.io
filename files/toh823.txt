The Auspiciousness of the Three Families (Trikulamaṅgala)

The Auspiciousness of the Three Families (Tibetan: Rigs gsum gyi bkra shis) is a short devotional text in verse, composed as a prayer for well-being and good fortune. It invokes the three bodhisattvas who are traditionally regarded as the protectors of the three families, namely Mañjuśrī, Avalokiteśvara, and Vajrapāṇi, each of whom is praised as protector of the three worlds. Each stanza pairs one of these figures with the body, speech, or mind of the Sugata, and closes with the wish that, by the auspiciousness of that aspect of the Buddha, there may be well-being here today. The text opens and closes with a rubric that links it to a companion piece, the verses of auspiciousness of the five, and it begins with homage to the Three Jewels. It is Toh 823 in the Kangyur, where it belongs to the Tantra section as one of a group of short auspiciousness verses, and I have not been able to determine a Sanskrit title from the source lines provided.

The Auspiciousness of the Three Families.

The Blessed One, golden in hue, like the king of mountains,
The form of Mañjuśrī, the youthful one, protector of the three worlds:
Whatever is the auspiciousness of the Sugata's body,
By that auspiciousness, may there be well-being here today.

The Blessed One, conch-white in hue, like the king of mountains,
Avalokiteśvara, the sovereign, protector of the three worlds:
Whatever is the auspiciousness of the Sugata's speech,
By that auspiciousness, may there be well-being here today.

The Blessed One, greenish-yellow, like the king of mountains,
Vajrapāṇi, protector of the three worlds:
Whatever is the auspiciousness of the Sugata's mind,
By that auspiciousness too, may there be well-being here today.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The colophon-like opening line, "Here ends the verses of auspiciousness of the five," belongs to the preceding text and was joined to this one in the source. The color of the third figure, ལྗང་སེར, is rendered "greenish-yellow." It could also be read as a yellow-green or olive shade. The sugata is the Buddha (the "well-gone one"). In each verse the Blessed One is identified with the body, speech, or mind of the Sugata, and through that with the figure of Mañjuśrī, Avalokiteśvara, or Vajrapāṇi, the lords of the three families. The phrase "like the king of mountains" (Mount Meru) is a standard comparison of splendor and stability.
