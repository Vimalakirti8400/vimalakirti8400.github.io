Homage to the Guru.

The speech of glorious Zangpo, renowned as excellence in Dharma.
The son of the fully awakened Buddha, possessing the body of the Sugata.
The sole lamp of the three worlds, the eye of the three realms of existence.
To one accomplished in gnosis and striving therein, I bow.

The source of compassion, performing the deeds of a Tathāgata.
The perfect guide, abiding in the state of mindful knowing and bliss.
Arisen into existence solely and certainly for the benefit of others.
To the very one who liberates the difficult to liberate, I bow.

Recollecting former lives, adorned with a supreme assemblage of excellent qualities, his body is supremely beautiful. Endowed with the adornment of gnosis, resplendent and vast in loving affection, he recollects future lives together with their lineages. Watering all beings with the gnosis-waters of the welfare of sentient beings and the unity of the three vehicles, empowering them like sovereign trees. To that mass of gnosis — the son of the Śākyas, possessing the qualities of the Sugata's body — I bow.

Having fully developed through all ten grounds, perfections, and the ten supreme virtues, reaching the far shore of the accumulation of merit. Having attained the qualities of the ten powers, reaching the culmination of all accumulations. He who liberates the ocean of existence and beyond existence — to him I bow.

This is a praise to the one who has gone to the far shore of all accumulations — to the holy supreme guru's quintessential instructions in the great drum of the sacred Dharma, adorned with reasoning and beautifully arranged words, he who bears the name of a bodhisattva, the sprout of buddhahood, named Zangpo Drakpa, endowed with the cool nectar of compassion and gnosis, having reached the far shore of all assemblies. This was requested by the Śākya monk Gelek Palgön, who dwells at the extremity of the divine land, and was beautifully arranged by the Śākya monk of the land of Hariputra named Chökyi Nyima. It is called "Received on the Crown of the Head." May it be of benefit to myself and to all beings.

Through this merit of mine,
May I become a buddha in the future,
And may all sentient beings suffering in saṃsāra
Be established in nirvāṇa.
