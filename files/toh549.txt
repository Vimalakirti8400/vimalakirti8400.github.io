Homage to the Three Jewels.

Namo Mañjuśrīye kumārāya. Tadyathā: Oṃ araje viraje śuddhe viśuddhe śodhanī viśodhanī śodhaya viśodhaya amale vimale jayavatī ruru cale hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ phaṭ phaṭ svāhā.

Whoever holds this dhāraṇī will, within a single month, come to possess intelligence. Their voice will become melodious. Their form will become beautiful. Whoever recites it once will, for the span of a thousand eons, turn their back on cyclic existence. Whoever merely carries it will remember their past lives. Whoever recites it one hundred thousand times will become learned. Whoever recites it two hundred thousand times will become a holder of knowledge. Whoever recites it three hundred thousand times will behold the face of Mañjuśrī. If one has committed the five acts of immediate retribution (the five heinous deeds with no interval before their karmic consequence) and yet fails to attain accomplishment through this practice, then at that time I myself shall commit the five acts of immediate retribution, and I myself shall have deceived the blessed buddhas.

The phrase "the five acts of immediate retribution" renders མཚམས་མེད་པ་ལྔ, literally "the five without interval," the standard Tibetan for the Sanskrit ānantarya-karma: patricide, matricide, killing an arhat, wounding a buddha, and causing a schism in the Saṅgha. The passage contains an extraordinary sworn guarantee by Mañjuśrī himself: he vows that even one who has committed these most heinous acts will attain results through recitation, staking his own integrity and his relationship to the buddhas on this promise.
