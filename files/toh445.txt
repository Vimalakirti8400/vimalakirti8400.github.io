The Great Yoga Tantra, the Explanatory Tantra of the Glorious Vajra Garland, the Analysis of the Secret Essence of All Tantras (Śrīvajramālābhidhānamahāyogatantrasarvatantrahṛdayarahasyavibhaṅga)

The Explanatory Tantra of the Glorious Vajra Garland, the Analysis of the Secret Essence of All Tantras, known in Sanskrit as the Śrī Vajramālābhidhāna Mahāyoga Tantra Sarvatantrahṛdayarahasyavibhaṅga and in Tibetan as the Palden Dorjé Trengwa, or Vajra Garland, is a great yoga tantra in which the Blessed One, the essence of the body, speech, and mind of all the tathāgatas, answers the questions of an assembly of bodhisattvas. Revered as an explanatory tantra of the Guhyasamāja cycle, it unfolds over sixty-eight chapters the practice of the generation and completion stages, including vajra recitation, the subtle winds and channels, the three appearances, the stages of manifest awakening, and the union of the two truths, along with the qualities of master and disciple, the maṇḍalas of body, speech, and mind, and rites for pacifying, increasing, subjugating, and fierce activity. It is Toh 445 in the Kangyur, where it belongs to the Tantra section among the tantras of the highest yoga class. According to its colophon, it was translated into Tibetan at the temple of Tholing by the Indian preceptor Sujanaśrījñāna and the royal monk-translator Zhiwa Ö.

In the language of India: śrī vajra māla abhidhāna mahā yoga tantra sarva tantra hṛdaya rahasya vibhadakaṃ iti. In the language of Tibet: The Great Yoga Tantra, the Explanatory Tantra of the Glorious Vajra Garland, the Analysis of the Secret Essence of All Tantras.

Homage to the noble Mañjuśrī Kumārabhūta, the Ever-Youthful. Homage to the Blessed One, the great Vairocana. Homage to the glorious Vajraḍāka. Homage to the Three Jewels.

Thus did I hear at one time. The Blessed One, the essence of the body, speech and mind of all the tathāgatas, was dwelling in the bhagas of the vajra queens, through full realization by means of the immeasurable great seal. He was dwelling together with many hundreds of thousands of koṭis of niyutas of bodhisattvas connected with the immeasurable vajra family, engaged in making manifest the realization of the great seal of all. He was supremely delighted in the lotus, joyful through experiencing the great bliss that is nondual. Because he saw all phenomena as experienced just as they are, he was turned directly toward the reality of nonconceptual wisdom.

He was supremely skilled in what has come under the power of the unsurpassed samaya of the great Vajradhara. Namely, there were the bodhisattva great being Samayavajra, the bodhisattva great being Dveṣavajra, the bodhisattva great being Rāgavajra, the bodhisattva great being Vajrakarma, the bodhisattva great being Vajrajñāna, the bodhisattva great being Pṛthivīvajra, the bodhisattva great being Jalavajra, the bodhisattva great being Agnivajra, and the bodhisattva great being Vāyuvajra. With these as their leaders, there were seventy-two thousand bodhisattvas connected with the family of the great samaya.

There were also hundreds of thousands of koṭis of niyutas of bodhisattvas connected with the great family, namely: the bodhisattva great being Sky Goer, the bodhisattva great being Sky Expanse, the bodhisattva great being Sky Moon, the bodhisattva great being Sky Crown, the bodhisattva great being Wholly Liberated by the Boundaries, the bodhisattva great being Vajrasattva, the bodhisattva great being Vajragarbha, the bodhisattva great being Vajramati, the bodhisattva great being Vajrapāṇi, the bodhisattva great being Vajrarāja, the bodhisattva great being Vajrasamaya, the bodhisattva great being Vajradharma, the bodhisattva great being Vajrabhoga, the bodhisattva great being Vajra Crown, the bodhisattva great being Suvikrāntavikrāmin, the bodhisattva great being Nonconceptual Crown, the bodhisattva great being Nonconceptual Sound, the bodhisattva great being Nonconceptual Dharma, the bodhisattva great being Nonconceptual Samaya, the bodhisattva great being Ānandavajra, the bodhisattva great being Paramānandavajra, the bodhisattva great being Vicitrānandavajra, and the bodhisattva great being Sahajavajra. He was dwelling together with these and with other immeasurable bodhisattva great beings, who were especially liberated through the Vajra Vehicle.

That Blessed One had thoroughly turned the wheel of Dharma, had thoroughly brought the deeds of a buddha to completion, and had thoroughly perfected the accumulations of merit and wisdom. He was dwelling with his face smiling broadly at the assembly.

Then the Blessed One looked upon the vast assembly of bodhisattva great beings, and in order to make them into suitable vessels he entered into the samādhi called the Vajra Vehicle of the Full Awakening of the Vajra Garland. From all the pores of his body he spread forth a great net of light rays, and so he dwelt. When that great net of light rays touched those bodhisattva great beings, they attained supreme joy, just like the Blessed One, the great Vajradhara, who is endowed with nondual union.

Then joy, bliss and supreme satisfaction arose in those very bodhisattva great beings, and they fainted.

Then, at that time, the great net of light rays was gathered back into the ūrṇā of the Blessed One. Once gathered, a great light ray in the form of a hook was drawn forth from the vajra path. It touched those who had fainted, and like a vajra garland it entered the wheel of the crown by way of the vajra path and remained there. Once again those bodhisattva great beings came to experience great bliss. By uttering the vajra syllable hūṃ, which is itself the experience of that very great bliss, they sent forth a great light ray from the channel at the center of the heart, and each came to be seated on his own seat.

Then, like those emerging from the depths of the water, joy arose in their minds. Seeing the great and wondrous miracle of the Blessed One, their eyes opened wide, and with one voice they praised the Blessed One perfectly.

"Blessed One, you who have become one taste,
Flourishing with innate joy,
O Blessed One, supremely at rest,
Beyond the range of the path of speech!
Just like the Blessed One's own delight,
The three realms of beings are truly blissful.
How wonderful! Tasting the nectar of the innate,
Great compassion is born.
Blessed One, you make clear
All the aims of all sentient beings.
One taste of the three realities,
The great seal, fully discerned:
The Blessed One delights in the great yoga,
And cuts through every bond.
The Blessed One rejoices greatly,
And surely makes beings fortunate.
How wonderful! The blissful Vajra Vehicle
Grants great joy and satisfaction.
How wonderful! The mantra, fully unfolded,
Accomplishes the self-arisen state.
How wonderful! You teach the most profound Dharma
Free of any object of reference.
How wonderful! Great Vajradhara, the chief,
Lord of Secrets, utterly secret!
How wonderful, the supremely awakened one!
How wonderful! Free of all elaboration,
You make clear the meaning of truth,
And illuminate with the lamp of the vehicle.
Protector, through your kindness
We attain the state of Vajrasattva.
Protector, we had entered wrong paths;
You set us on the true path.
Protector, we were lost in darkness;
You let us gain the light of wisdom.
O Samayavajra, sun
Who teaches the meaning of the great seal,
Blessed One, I bow to you and praise you,
The very self of the great seal of compassion.
Homage to you, sky of nondual joy;
To you, moon of joy, I bow."

When they had praised the Blessed One with these and other verses, they gathered together once more. Wishing to ask about the nature, just as it is, of the very subtle ground called the Explanation of the Vajra Garland, they were of a single attention. Wishing to hear the speech that gives joy to the one-pointed mind, with their hair standing on end and their breath trembling, they bowed to the Blessed One again and again. Then they sat down on their own seats and remained there, their minds full of hope and apprehension.

The Blessed One knew in his heart these thoughts in their minds, and again he entered the samādhi called the Full Awakening of the Vajra Garland. From the secret vajra he brought forth his own consort. Endowed with the moods of grace and the rest, they themselves cast off forms free of passion and took on forms endowed with the moods of grace and the rest. Each embraced his own consort and entered into the yoga of the great seal. Then, seeing themselves once again in such forms, they were filled with wonder. Their eyes opened wide, and since they saw it as if it were the arrangement of a painting, they said nothing at all. So the assembly remained properly seated there.

Then the Blessed One urged on his own consort, and setting aside the yoga of the great seal, from his own vajra of body, speech and mind he spoke this heart mantra of the vajra queen, the vajra family:

oṃ āḥ hūṃ

As soon as he had said this, all the tathāgatas, the consorts, the bodhisattvas and the wrathful kings emerged from the pores of the Blessed One. Having emerged, they took their places on their own seats in the east and the other directions around the Blessed One: some in the form of Vairocana, some in the form of Ratnasambhava, some in the form of Amitābha, and so on up to the forms of King Sumbha and the king Uṣṇīṣacakravartin. Then the Blessed One remained for a little while without saying anything.

At that time all those bodhisattvas spoke out with one voice and said:

"How wonderful, great vajra family,
You teach the meaning of the vajra garland!
How wonderful, clear Vajra Vehicle,
You teach the meaning of the dharmadhātu!"

Having said this three times, they bowed to the great Vajradhara and sat down.

Then at that time, in that instant, that moment, that very second, Vajrapāṇi saw the vast assembly of bodhisattva great beings gathered there, bowed to the Blessed One, and addressed him with these words:

"Blessed One, lord of the wisdom vajra,
Dwelling within the secret lotus,
Lord of the yoga of the great pledge,
In your compassion, hear my words!
The buddhas of the Fortunate Eon,
Who long for the Vajra Vehicle,
And the bodhisattva great beings,
All these sit here, having bowed with their bodies.

"In the time of the five degenerations,
Sentient beings of the future
Will have little diligence and little merit,
Many wrong views,
And short lives.
I ask so that they may attain the supreme state.
These children of the victors
And the sentient beings of the future,
By what will they be swiftly liberated
To the far shore beyond saṃsāra?

"To the lord, the great Vajradhara,
I make this request:
Lord of beings, through your kindness,
I seek the supreme reality.
The secret of all the buddhas,
The condensed meaning of all the tantras,
The two unsurpassed stages,
The full realization of the Vajra Garland:
I wish to ask a little about these.
The yoga of the completion stage,
Please teach it to me.
What was given only as a hint in the Glorious Guhyasamāja,
The unsurpassed yoga tantra,
Among the Great Sage's eighty-four thousand
Heaps of Dharma."

Having said this, he sat before him,
And, having bowed with his body, he said:
"Great wisdom, Blessed One,
Vajrasattva, guru of beings,
You who teach the meaning of the single taste of the three vajras,
And the state of the three realities,
Please teach me
The hidden meaning of the yoga tantras.

"Why is it named the Vajra Garland?
What is union, and what is empty?
The divisions of the drop, and sound,
The secret, and the pledge,
The vow, and mere sound,
The reality of wind: what is great bliss?
The union of vajra and lotus,
The reality of mantra, the yogin,
The winds and the wheels:
How many are there, and where do they abide?
What channels are in the yogin's body?
The moments, such as variety,
The four joys, and the substances,
Mantra, wind, and drop,
What are the three syllables and the three realities,
And likewise the three tips of the nose?
The emptiness of the body, and wisdom,
And how do the deities abide?
What are their names?
What are rising and setting?
Protector, how the joys
Truly arise, please explain clearly.

"The channels explained before,
How do they always flow in the vajra body?
Those three channels of the junctures,
How are they said to be the principal ones?
How do the winds rise,
And, Lord, how do they dissolve?
Their gathering into the ḍākinī,
Lord, how is it truly attained?
Having cut off the winds of conceptual thought,
How does nonconceptuality dawn?
How do the channels set,
And how does one undertake the six yogas?
What are the characteristics of time?
And what is the fruit of the secret?

"The vajra of the dharmadhātu,
What are the divisions of the families?
How many are the divisions of the mudrās?
The aggregates and the rest, the sense sources,
And why the name Great Seal (mahāmudrā)?
The divisions of the tastes,
And in what does the word 'I' abide?
The condensed divisions of emptiness,
And where does emptiness arise?
What are day and night?
The code of the vowels and consonants,
And what is the dawning at the junctures?

"Approach and the rest, taught before the tantra,
The activities of the twenty rites:
What is their reason, and what their result?
How is the rite of offering taught?
Likewise the nature of the goddesses,
And how are the goddesses' names taught?
What is meant by 'Thus I' and the rest,
Spoken at the beginning of the tantra?
The opening words, 'Thus' and so on,
How is their meaning to be elaborated?
What is the inner fire offering of oneself?
Eating the five nectars,
The gathering of the assembly of yoginīs,
And likewise the secret signs:
The maṇḍalas of the vajra
Of body, speech, and mind, how are they taught?
The Buddha, the true Dharma, and the Saṅgha,
How are their names explained?
Guru, teacher, master,
And Vajradhara, what is their explanation?

"Possessing what qualities
Does a disciple become a vessel for the yoga tantra?
How many divisions are there of their conduct,
And what is its result?
Pacifying, enriching, subjugating,
And likewise killing, expelling,
And immobilizing:
How are they applied in accomplishment?
The fierce rite against the target,
And what are the words of the mantra?
Protector, what is the recitation of the wrathful one?
Attaining the eight accomplishments,
And how are the five superknowledges attained?
How do yogins see reality,
And attain full awakening in a single instant,
With a stable body free of aging?
The practice of essence extraction (rasāyana),
The practices of turning metals into gold and the rest,
And the great accomplishment of the vajra mudrā:
Guru of beings, how are they attained?
The body's nature as a celestial palace,
What is its nature?
How do the sacred sites and the rest abide in it?
Dissolution, and the lord of enjoyment,
What is wind, and what is its characteristic?
And how do these come to perish?"

When the questions had thus been completed,
Vajrapāṇi joined his palms,
Sat before the guru of beings,
And bowed with his body. Then
The Lord gave Vajrasattva
His approval, saying "Excellent!",
And with a deep and melodious voice like Brahmā's,
The protector of beings spoke.

This concludes the first chapter of the Great Yoga Tantra, the Full Realization of the Glorious Vajra Garland, the chapter on the setting of the tantra, comprising the request, the questions, the activity of the disciple, and so on.

Then the Blessed Vajra spoke.
With the speech of reality, the protector of beings
Called out to Vajrapāṇi:
"The definitive explanation of what you have rightly asked,
The meaning of the essence, the condensed meaning,
All the matters hard to distinguish
That you have asked about,
Vajra Lord, master of the great secret:
In the action and performance tantras,
And in the yoga tantras, nowhere
Has the supreme secret been taught.
Vajra Lord, the supreme reality,
The answers to what was asked,
I shall definitely explain, and clearly."
In an instant he opened his eyes,
And, displaying the supremely marvelous,
Pervaded by compassion, he said this:
"Therefore listen well."

Then, Vajrapāṇi having made his request to the Blessed One, whose heart was moistened with compassion and who cuts off all doubts, the Blessed One gave his explanation in words of the supreme secret:

"Empowerment is foremost;
In it all accomplishments always abide.
I shall explain its meaning just as it is,
So first listen properly.
When first the intelligent disciple
Has been properly empowered,
Then he becomes a vessel
For the yoga of the completion stage.
If one lacks proper empowerment,
Even though the practitioner knows the meaning of the tantra,
Master and disciple alike
Go to the unbearable great hell.
Therefore the true guru
Should be served with deep reverence.

"One who has received empowerment and knows reality,
Intelligent, compassionate by nature,
Profound, vast, and stainless,
Who has given up harm and violence,
Fortunate, with a body complete,
Adorned with the major and minor marks,
Delighting solely in great passion,
Free of jealousy over sense objects,
Conscientious, and always joyful,
Born from the womb of a yoginī,
Devoted solely to the Vajra Vehicle,
Turning his back on the lesser vehicle:
A guru endowed with these and other qualities
The disciple should serve.
Having found a true guru,
The stains of the disciple's nature
Are purified, and realization and ripening are brought about;
Then one possesses excellent wisdom, just as it is.

"Just as a jewel is tested by rubbing,
And gold by burning and cutting,
So too the disciple
Should be examined for as long as twelve years.
Therefore, each examining the other in this way,
Examine thus at all times.
Otherwise obstructors will arise,
Destroying accomplishments and bringing suffering.
Just as poison is overcome by mantra,
And, purified by mantra, becomes an elixir,
So, with every effort,
Having brought the mantra disciple to understanding
Through the guru's empowerment and the rest,
The meaning as it is serves accomplishment.
Thus through empowerment the disciple
Who is impure becomes pure."

Then Vajrapāṇi asked
Vajrasattva, the great bliss:
"How is it that empowerment
Is declared foremost in the tantra?"
Vajrasattva, the omniscient,
Who cuts off all doubts,
Knowing reality,
Said to Vajrapāṇi:
"That meaning, just as it is,
I shall properly explain. Listen!

"As for the divisions of empowerment,
The first empowerment is the principal one;
The second bears the name secret;
The third is complete union;
The meaning of the fourth is the sublime.
The principal one has eleven divisions;
The twelfth is the secret;
The thirteenth is perfect union;
The fourteenth is the ultimate:
One for each empowerment.
The three are the causal empowerments;
The fourth should be known as the result.
Through the sequence of joy and the rest,
I pervade them all.

"On the third day of the waning fortnight,
And likewise on the tenth and the fourteenth,
The guru empowers, according to the rite,
The disciple endowed with good qualities.
One slender of body, sixteen years of age,
Adorned with beauty and good fortune,
With wide, dark blue eyes,
Of compassionate intent and true devotion,
Sweet of speech, who has received empowerment,
And is especially devoted to the yoga tantras;
Or else one twenty years of age,
Delightful and bestowing bliss.
Older than that, she becomes unsuitable,
And makes accomplishment distant.
Above all a gentle maiden,
One's wife, or one's sister,
Or one's daughter, should with faith
Be offered to the guru.
Having made offerings with gold, jewels, and clothing,
And with various garlands of pearls,
And sitting before the guru,
The disciple should say this:
'From this day on I am your servant.
Lord, accept my offering.'
Having pleased him in this way,
One should honor the guru with one's whole being.
The guru's essence is Vajradhara;
Therefore honor the true guru.

"Then the blessed mother, the consort,
Should be properly honored and then united with.
Whatever arises from that union,
That which has fallen, into the mouth of the fortunate one,
Applying the thumb and ring finger,
Should be properly enjoyed.
The wisdom born from that
Is great bliss, self-aware.
Exhalation and inhalation are of a single taste;
Honor it with supreme joy.
With the mantra hūṃ phaṭ, at the door of the jewel,
Having placed it, one should stir the vajra.
Just as the joys are,
Arising in that moment,
The bliss, with a joyful mind,
Is spoken from the mouth of the vajra lady.
She too, being entrusted
Into the disciple's hand, clearly says this:
'Worship the lotus as it should be done:
Do it, at the supreme face of e vaṃ,
Because the Lord of the Vajradhātu
Has himself entered her heart.'
By any other means
The yogin will not attain buddhahood.
That most subtle thing
Is realized by the yogin of great bliss.
It should be relied upon with great effort.
As for the characteristic of innate joy,
Since empowerment is foremost,
Through it everything is attained just as it is."

This concludes the second chapter of the Great Yoga Tantra, the Full Realization of the Glorious Vajra Garland, the chapter on examining master and disciple and on empowerment.

"Next, listen to further reality,
The clarification of the meaning of the Vajra Garland.
What is hidden in all the tantras
Should not be told to others who ask.
The name called Vajra Garland:
It is the thirteenth ground,
The abode of the meaning of the four joys.
The all-empty is joy,
Beyond the range of speech,
Free of all duality,
The culmination of all the yogas of the fortunate,
The domain of the yoginī's speech,
Emptiness, wisdom, and vajra:
This is explained as the Vajra Garland."

"Emptiness is the vajra, the secret lotus;
The channel is the Lord of the Vajradhātu;
Wisdom is the supreme great bliss,
The marvelous innate joy.
That vajra, moreover,
Is renowned as what pierces conceptual thoughts.
Garland, in turn, is their continuity.
The vajras of the mind of awakening,
Being indivisible, are called vajra.
What is hidden in all the tantras
Should not be told to just anyone.
It is renowned as the breath.
Garland: its continuity unbroken,
Through the nature of descending joy
It pierces all the winds of conceptual thought.
Otherwise one cannot pierce the wind.

"Always, day and night,
Clearly one hundred and eight
Winds whose nature is conceptual thought
Arise and are destroyed.
Distinguishing them precisely,
Listen to the names of the winds!
Release-and-Hold and Hundred Million,
Intoxicated and Evil Mind,
Cooler and Itch,
Mucus and Axe,
Fragment, Makara, and Jaśa,
Bared Fangs and Time of Intoxication,
Lust-Drunk, Sinew, and Makara,
Producer of Variety and Bowing,
Haughty, Drum, and Proclaimer of Sound,
Vāyu and Piercer of Vital Points,
Fire, Time, and What Shall I Do,
Vajra, Supreme Giver, and Cuta,
Tent and Moonlight,
Kamboja and Matted-Haired,
Black Mustard, Spread Out, and Summoner of Time,
Scorpion and Left-Leaning,
Steadfast and Throat,
Winter-Dispeller, Supreme Bestower, and Maddener,
Arrogance, Lake, and Gaṇapati,
Lizard and Earth Mind,
Excellent Vehicle and Supremely Born,
Stainless, Child, and Sun,
Illuminator and Sun-Subduer,
Powerful, Roar, and Time,
Vow and Leaping Goer,
Bee, Dog, and Sign,
Skull, Hunchback, and Upright,
Earth and She Who Goes on Earth,
Brahmin and Dweller in the Turning Nose,
Mātṛkā Family and Tongue-Mover,
Silent and Drum-Sound Cloud,
Delighter and Wide-Eyed,
Stainless, Kāśyapa, and Parrot,
Variegated Beak and Even-Eyed,
Beautiful and Staff of Dharma,
All-Going and Steadfast Courage,
Bold Eater, Donkey, and Equalizer,
Open Mouth, Great Sound, and Playing with Strength,
Channel Servant, Controller, and Deceitful,
Great Lord and Ever Joyful,
Lightning Garland and Joy-Maker,
Exhaler, Raga, and Tongue,
Saṃvara, Udumbara, and Bewildered,
Lotus Net and Lotus-Encircled,
Enemy Axe, Peacock, and Wind,
And Vajra Lord: these are the names of the winds,
As I have taught them."

"Blessed One, of the winds I asked about,
Distinguishing their locations,
I ask from where they rise
And how they spread."

The Blessed Vajrin spoke:
"As for the characteristic of the wind element,
The wind that pervades the six wheels
Always abides in the wheel of Dharma.
As what is called the life force, it pervades beings.
At midnight, free of conceptual thought,
Rising from the supreme space,
It destroys the nature of wind.
That wind moves in nine modes,
Abiding in the region of the wheel of Dharma.
Beginning from the time of midnight,
Ninefold in the six wheels,
Until midday comes to its end,
The winds rise.
Beginning from midday,
Ninefold from the six wheels,
Until the end of the time of midnight,
When the winds move distinctly,
Through the divisions of their very locations
They turn into the various kinds of thought.

"Distinguishing the conceptual thoughts,
I shall explain their names:
Attachment, aversion, and delusion,
Vanity, pride, and clinging,
Jealousy, anger, and avarice,
Dishonesty, deceit, and bad demeanor,
Wild mind, craving, and sleep,
Doubt and ego-grasping,
Joy, detachment, and arrogance,
Concealment, thirst, and habitual tendencies,
Pretense, confusion, and great desire,
Quarreling, flirtation, and audacity,
Hypocrisy, haughtiness, and torment,
Agitated demeanor and lack of vigilance,
Dullness and wrong pride,
Regret and lack of conscience,
Irreverence and lack of trust,
Low mind, shamelessness, and bondage,
Sense of shame and embarrassment,
Ungenerosity and grief,
Indecision and lack of conceit,
Tension, doubt, and weariness,
Power and deluded courage,
Supposing and fainting,
Dissolving and unruliness,
Crookedness, levelling, and intoxication,
Non-cognition and conscientiousness,
Dysfunction and timidity,
Unhappiness and overthinking,
Fear, torpor, and sleep,
Craving, amusement, and the crane,
Harmfulness, laziness, and deviousness,
Thinking, conduct, and discouragement,
The reality of the conventional and meaningfulness,
Giving and the desire for joy,
Fear, trembling, and subtlety,
Clarity, distinct clarity, and adulteration,
Connection and the reality of permanence,
The tortoise, insatiability, and harm,
Unclarity and attractiveness,
Restlessness and talkativeness,
Selfhood and personhood,
Wonder, joy, and vile people,
The reality of the inferior, the three sufferings,
And attachment to saṃsāra:
Always one hundred and eight,
Always, by day and by night,
They move with the nature of wind."

This concludes the third chapter of the Great Yoga Tantra, the Full Realization of the Glorious Vajra Garland, the chapter on the analysis of the words "Vajra Garland."

Then the sun of the three vajras,
Who opens the secret lotus,
Abiding in the state of great bliss,
Proclaimed the supreme meaning of union:
"Union is explained as blending.
Blending I shall explain to you.
Inseparable from the four wheels,
With mantra, according to the rite,
The hero and the yoginī join in union.
When the two secret channels
Touch in perfect union,
Then space itself expands.
The wind born from space
Makes the fire blaze up.
The seventy-two thousand channels,
The channels fully awakened by its splendor,
The supreme state of all bliss
Pervades the wheel of great bliss.
The mind of awakening of great bliss
Melts and comes to abide in the channels.
Because the three vajras become one in nature,
The elements become of one taste,
The aggregates are utterly exhausted,
And the winds come to rest,
The joys truly arise.
Then the mind of awakening too
Melts from all its locations
And becomes one in essence,
And the fruit of the single channel
Becomes of one taste:
This is what is called blending.
What is concealed in the aggregates, the elements,
The faculties, and the objects:
From the complete exhaustion of conceptual thought
Great bliss truly arises.
The single taste is repose.
That is union as blending,
The supreme secret reality,
Declared to be the state of Vajrasattva."

This concludes the fourth chapter of the Great Yoga Tantra, the Full Realization of the Glorious Vajra Garland, the chapter on the further explanation of the word "union."

"Next, listen further to reality,
The fully elaborated meaning of emptiness.
Emptiness is all phenomena;
Pervading within, it is also the nature of reality.
To know that reality truly
Is suchness and nonconceptuality.
Self, person, and phenomena
Are one in nature, selflessness.
Emptiness and self-awareness,
Always abiding as the self of all,
Are realized by the yogin alone;
They are not the domain of logicians.
All phenomena, in brief,
Are described as the six wheels.
Then, what pervades within as well
Is the well-expanded wheel of channels.
It is pervasive like space and subtle,
The pervader, characterized by peace,
Without reference point and without support,
Formless and without afflictions,
Nonconceptual and supremely peaceful,
Without intrinsic nature and stainless,
Nondual and without activity,
Free of grasped and grasper,
Beyond time and example,
Beyond the range of the path of speech.
The nature of all phenomena
Is expressed just as it is.
Free of entity and non-entity,
Released from the existence of virtue and nonvirtue,
It is not obscured at all;
Like space, it is supremely pervasive.
Phenomena arise just as they are;
That is called suchness.
It has come from nowhere,
And it goes nowhere.
Solely from delusion and error
It appears to proliferate.
Free of pleasure and pain,
Unstained by attachment, aversion, and the rest,
Free of error and confusion,
Free of thought and stainless.
Self, life, one who is born,
Man, and likewise person,
Son of Manu and child of Manu,
Agent and feeler,
Knower and seer:
It is free of these as superimpositions.
By the aggregates, sense sources, elements, and the rest,
By consciousness and the grasped,
It is unobscured, supremely subtle,
Truly abiding in supreme space.

"Thus the whole meaning of emptiness
Is free of expression and the expressed.
Though it is only a mere name,
It is self-arisen and supremely marvelous.
In the conventional terms of expression
I have properly explained it.
Because self-awareness is extremely subtle,
Reality is not seen.
It is known by oneself as one's own essence,
And is not the domain of others.
That is explained as self-awareness.
The abiding of all phenomena is peace.
Those logicians deluded by conceptual thought,
The confused who do not know,
Do not know the ultimate,
And hold the treasury of the enemy, the conventional.
Not knowing supreme wisdom,
The logicians talk about emptiness.
The yogin of yoga and meditative concentration
Engages with it as supremely subtle.
True emptiness, the great space,
Free of knower and known,
Free of all the aspects of thought,
Is called the wheel of emptiness.

"The body, whose nature is the five aggregates:
Within the hollow of the six wheels,
Like a sliver of the half moon,
Emptiness abides in the wheel of wind.
Neither half nor full,
Like habitual tendencies, like the moon in water,
The wheel of wind is explained as the life force.
It dawns in the instant of midnight.
To meditate properly in this way
Is to meditate on emptiness.
Through the yoga of ultimate nonduality,
Or else in order to join cause and result,
Meditate on it as accomplished by nature.
Meditating on the ultimate as its own essence,
And likewise on the conventional,
One destroys saṃsāra.
Just as sesame oil within sesame,
Though present, is not seen,
So emptiness within the body,
Though present, is not seen, because it is obscured.
Knowing this from the guru's mouth,
Through true awareness the conventional is abandoned."

This concludes the fifth chapter of the Great Yoga Tantra, the Full Realization of the Glorious Vajra Garland, the chapter teaching the meaning of emptiness.

"Next I shall explain more. Listen!
The unsurpassed divisions of the drop:
The world, lacking the divisions of the drop,
Circles again and again in saṃsāra.
That which abides in the center of the ūrṇā
Is properly explained as the drop, as wind.
It is opened by the reality of mantra.
The yogin, having untied the knots,
Knows the reality of wind
Through the practice of supreme opening.
Not knowing that practice,
The yoga of wind and mantra,
Beyond expression, the expressed, and example,
Those with clear eyes who see wrongly
Find it hard to attain the buddhas,
Who are free of meditative concentration and of anything to meditate on.
Through the kindness of the yoginī,
It abides, transmitted from mouth to mouth."

Vajrasattva again asked
The Blessed One, the great bliss:
"Protector of beings, please teach:
The divisions of the drop, together with their knowledge,
By what does the supreme yogin know them?
How does he open the knots through practice,
So that these beings of the three realms
Swiftly go to the supreme accomplishment?"

When this had been asked, Vajrin
Said to the Vajra Lord:
"This supreme secret
Has nowhere been taught clearly.
Those divisions of the drop
I shall explain. Listen to reality!
In the space within the hollow of the heart lotus
The wisdom vajra always abides.
Resting on what is above and below
Abide the great winds, the life wind and the downward-clearing wind.
That which is hard to divide
Is bound in the middle by three knots.
Because by these the supreme one is overcome,
It cannot go upward.
Overcome by them, it moves back in five ways:
The life wind, the downward-clearing wind, the upward-moving wind,
The pervading wind, and the evenly abiding wind.
From the five tips, continuously,
They rest upon five channels.
Vigor and Expansion,
Intoxication, Drink, and Friend:
These should be known as the five channels.
They clearly pervade below the vajra.
In the center of the upper life wind, too,
It goes to the expanse of space at the vajra navel.
The great wind called the life wind,
Meeting the knot at the navel,
Is overcome and moves in five ways,
At the tips, into five channels.
Moon Portion and Nose,
Service, Dewlap, and Liver:
These are the names of the five channels.
The winds that move from the five channels
Are the nāga, the tortoise, the lizard,
Devadatta, and Dhanañjaya.

"Thus, together with those channels,
Since they go downward in the body,
The channels of the wheel of emanation
Are stimulated again and again,
And when they expand through that stimulation,
Great delusion holds sway.
Above that, in the wheel of Dharma,
The channels there are stimulated,
And together with those, those channels
Are ruled by aversion.
Being stimulated, above that,
In the wheel of enjoyment, supreme wonder arises;
Together with those channels,
Being ruled by great passion
And stimulated, above that,
In the great bliss of the crown wheel,
Together with those channels,
It is filled with the mind of awakening.
Conceptual thoughts move about,
And from all their various divisions
The upper ones too go downward.
Overpowered by the life wind,
All of them enter the tip of the vajra
And face downward.

"Yet it does not go downward.
The wheel of the knot of ignorance,
The wind that is the chariot of consciousness,
Is mixed with habitual tendencies.
Eye and ear, and likewise nose
And tongue, the four consciousnesses,
Emerging from the four tips,
Habitual tendencies mixing with consciousness,
Consciousness together with the faculties
Fully develops the objects.
Then again, by that same path,
It enters in the corresponding sequence.
By joining with its very substance,
In the moment that consciousness engages,
It increases together with its habitual tendencies.
From the deluded wheel of saṃsāra,
Consciousness, thus corrupted by habitual tendencies,
Becomes the very essence of karma.
That itself, joined with the life wind,
Is the delusion of the three realms.
The agent experiences the fruit of karma;
There is no saṃsāra other than this.
Therefore, in order to destroy saṃsāra,
The state of the drop is clearly distinguished.
One should strive in meditation.
Ignorance is nothing other than not knowing.
That knot in the center,
When it is opened, brings accomplishment.
The yogin is accomplished in yoga;
Having attained the five wisdoms,
And attained the five superknowledges,
Having attained the supreme secret,
The one of excellent mind abides just as things are.

"There is no need of a rosary, no need of meditation,
No need of activity, no need of prohibition:
Through the reality of mantra alone
Perfect and complete buddhahood is attained.
The divisions of the action, Dharma, and pledge seals
Are not taught here.
Not through the divisions of the wheels,
Nor likewise through the joys and the rest,
But through the reality of mantra
Is the clear state of the Great Seal attained.
Emptiness, wisdom, and vajra,
The reality of nondual bliss,
The divisions of the secret drop,
The great bliss of the accomplished yogin,
Breaks open the eggshell of ignorance
And destroys the mountain of the view of the perishable collection.
The accomplishment of the Great Seal
And its bringing about are explained as supreme.
Through definitely applying the reality of mantra,
Whatever is fitting, with its branches made clear,
Through the kindness of the yoginī
And from the guru's mouth, comes to be definitively realized.

"Recitation without pith instructions
Makes the king of mantras confuse the mind.
It brings nothing but hardship.
Just as churning water does not yield butter,
So with mantra lacking pith instructions:
Though a person recite it
For as long as he lives,
He will not gain even a fraction of its fruit.
Some ignorant person,
Wishing very much to go to the land
On the far shore of the four oceans,
Who does not ask which way is the road,
Sets off thus on his own.
Likewise the practitioner lacking pith instructions,
Who undertakes great hardship,

Is without fruit; and even one who is learned,
That practitioner, grows angry.
In the very instant anger arises,
Fruitless, he goes off weeping and wailing.
Therefore, with every effort,
And with faith, from the lineage of gurus
One should learn the pith instructions.
Because it is pervaded by the scripture of the lineage,
It endures through the succession of the lineage.
From the mouth of the yoginī
And from the mouth of the guru, with effort,
One should learn that supreme reality.

When he had heard this pith instruction of mantra, the scripture of the lineage,
Vajrapāṇi in turn
Bowed to Vajrasattva, the great bliss,
Who cuts through every doubt, and asked:
"This very teaching you have given is supreme.
Blessed One, the king,
The unsurpassed accomplishment of mantra,
By learning which accomplishment comes in an instant!"

Then the Blessed Vajra One spoke:
"The reality of mantra, perfectly clear,
That reality, supremely secret,
I have not explained anywhere.
Out of regard for those without the fortune,
I have kept it hidden throughout all the tantras.
Listen, and I shall explain it:
The unsurpassed king of mantras,
Through which, by engaging in the conduct of mantra,
Accomplishment swiftly comes.

First write the last of the ūṣman letters,
Adorned with the sixth vowel,
Joined with the end of the last class,
Together with the vowelless part.
To the end of the ūṣman letters, this vowel,
The ninth, is properly joined;
At the end, the visarga, the supreme sound.
Thus one should extract the chief of mantras.

Thus the name of two syllables,
The marvelous code of mantra,
In order to abandon all grasping,
Has been given the name of sound and drop.
Opened by the two winds,
It undoes the knot of ignorance.
The cause of the supreme Mahāmudrā
Are these two supremely peaceful syllables.
The aim desired by one who has received the pith instructions:
Whoever recites them for six months,
For him the state of the sound of the drop becomes clear,
And he accomplishes every aim he desires.

In a place of the gods, secluded from people,
Free from the thorns of noise,
Without obstructors, auspicious and delightful,
A pleasure garden of great comfort,
By enjoying the five sense pleasures,
The chief of mantras will be accomplished.

Listen further, and I shall explain this:
Remaining at home with the mudrā of ease,
By which accomplishment becomes supreme.
The practitioner, sitting on a comfortable seat,
Fixes both eyes on the tip of the nose,
Sets the nose in line with the tongue,
Keeps the shoulders level, joins the tongue to the palate,
And lets the teeth and lips rest at ease.
Relaxing the outflow and inflow of breath,
Little by little, free of effort,
Breathing out and in just as it naturally goes,
Well settled in the vajra posture,
Thus endowed with the mudrā,
One should constantly recite the two syllables.
Through the division of the drop comes great bliss;
In six months the supreme yoga
Will be attained; of this there is no doubt.

This is the first, the root tantra:
The stainless abode of the avadhūtī,
The reality of wind, the great wisdom,
The cause of Vajrasattva.
The pill, the sword, the vetāla,
Kingship, the eye ointment, the extraction of essences,
Paralyzing armies and piercing walls,
The supreme state of a vidyādhara,
Invisibility and seeing treasures,
Transforming at will and seeing forms,
Turning things to gold and victory over poison,
Entering into fire,
The state of the five superknowledges,
And the five wisdoms will be attained.
Thus, in a single instant,
Is the state that bears the characteristics of awakening.
Abandoning austere practices,
Fully endowed with wisdom and compassion,
It bestows the great bliss that has been explained.
No vows are needed, nor fire offerings;
No rosary is needed, no counting is needed;
Here the divisions of the generation stage are not needed,
Nor are the prescribed austerities and the rest.
By the yoga of mantra alone,
Transmitted from the guru's mouth,
And by applying oneself to it for six months,
The supreme state will be attained."

This concludes the sixth chapter, on the division of the drop and the attainment of the supreme yoga, from the Manifest Awakening of the Great Yoga Tantra, the Glorious Vajra Garland.

"Then, further, listen to that reality:
The unsurpassed meaning of nāda,
By merely knowing which
Accomplishment comes in this very life.
The sound too is declared indestructible,
By nature luminous and stainless,
Utterly free from all aspects.
It truly abides in the emanation wheel at the navel,
Subtle as a serpent's tongue,
The marvelous nondual wisdom.
Brahmā, Viṣṇu, Rudra,
Īśvara, and Sadāśiva:
Thus that precious one
Is the father and mother of these creators.
Without syllable and with nothing above it,
Wholly free of the great elements,
Released from time and from concepts,
Unborn, unending, marvelous;
Having abandoned clinging to all grasping and the rest,
Without remainder,
Supreme among the supreme, without even peace,
It abides at the center of all things,
Free of conceptual thought and without support.
What is called the foundation consciousness
Is awakened by the afflicted mentation
By way of inert things.
Through the supremely nondual yoga
The unsurpassed state is attained.
Nondual and supremely clear,
It rests truly on the reality of wind.
Roused by the syllable hoḥ,
It awakens the wheel of fire.
The meaning of such a state of nāda
Truly abides in the guru's mouth."

This concludes the seventh chapter, explaining the words on nāda, from the Manifest Awakening of the Great Yoga Tantra, the Glorious Vajra Garland.

"Then, further, listen, and I shall explain
The unsurpassed meaning of the secret.
The secret is said to be the bhaga.
The bhaga, likewise, is the source of phenomena,
The support of the state of great bliss,
The cause that gives rise to joy,
The abode of Vajrasattva,
Clearly the very nature of the five wisdoms,
The great wisdom free of elaboration,
Which has abandoned all conceptual thought.
It is the culmination of the joy of cessation, supremely subtle;
The coemergent joy abides as lord of all.
The joy of cessation and the others abide for an instant.
Subtle, it is the genuine bodhicitta,
Which sustains life, benefiting it,
And abides like the mechanism of a balance beam.
Free of time, without conceptual thought,
It is emptiness, unborn and deathless.
As it dissolves into the emanation wheel,
The wheel of great bliss arises.
Inconceivable and peaceful,
Free of attachment, for all beings it is unborn.
Through the union of vajra and lotus
It bestows every bliss.
By knowing the reality of wind,
One holds it in the middle of the channel.
The experience of its arising
Is known as the secret.
That very reality, supreme among secrets,
Abides above all in the guru's mouth.
Through the succession of transmission from one to another,
It is known from the mouth of an excellent guru."

This concludes the eighth chapter, called the Teaching of the Entire Collection of the Great Secret, from the Manifest Awakening of the Great Yoga Tantra, the Glorious Vajra Garland.

"Then, further, listen, and I shall explain
The reality that is the meaning of the vow.
That which guards the vow
Is said to be bodhicitta.
Holding the mind at the tip of the vajra
Is known as guarding.
While the senses engage their objects,
That which constantly abides
Is explained as guarding the vow
And guarding the samaya.

The hard to tame, the hypocritical and deceitful,
The deluded, the angry-minded and the fickle,
Together with the faithless and the sophists,
Those who live by outward emblems,
Śrāvakas, those greedy for wealth,
Those who disparage reality,
Who do not know the ultimate,
Who hold nihilist views and have broken their discipline:
With them do not converse, chat, question, or the like,
And not even for the space of a finger snap
Should one stay together with them.

A delightful action seal:
If she is with childish people,
With her one should not perform the samaya;
The yoga would be ruined.
Entering the midst of the samaya when aroused,
Relying on the lotus vessel, the mudrā, and the rest,
Or entering out of a bond of greed:
One should not enter into the samaya in this way.
The vessel, the small drum, the curved knife,
The book and the rosary
Should never be shown anywhere
To others who lack the samaya.
To a monk who abides in his vows
One should not teach yogic conduct and the like,
Nor the pith instructions,
Nor show him paintings and the like.
With one who shares the samaya,
One practices together, properly, as is fitting.
If one does not speak of it even to the guru,
What need is there to mention others?

The senses' engagement with objects:
Having restrained it from wherever it goes,
If one further engages the conventional
Through emptiness, nonduality, illusion and the like,
Then through the bliss experienced
In the wisdom-gnosis empowerment and the others,
And through mindfulness of great bliss,
The senses turn back from their objects.
Praising oneself and disparaging others,
Pride in one's knowledge and the like, being utterly abandoned:
This is explained as the true vow
Of the intelligent Vajra Lord.
Samayas such as these
The yogin must always guard;
Otherwise the yoga will be ruined.
The yogin, the Vajrin,
Should keep hidden, even from the guru,
The rosary and the crown ornament,
The ornaments made of bone,
The book, the painting for meditation,
The skull cup and the rest, and the yoginī,
In every circumstance, for the sake of accomplishment.
The mantra should not be spoken aloud in words;
Without even a visible movement of the lips,
Uttering it softly in the throat,
Thus one should recite the king of mantras.
No accomplishment equals that which comes from secrecy;
For this reason it is explained as the vow.

One who is not a fit vessel, even if possessed
Of great wealth, is declared in the tantras to be a fault.
For the sake of unsurpassed accomplishment
The guru should strive to avoid him.
Whoever, attached to food, drink, clothing and the like,
And to great wealth,
Tells the supreme and marvelous pith instructions
To a disciple who is not a fit vessel,
Though he knows yoga, in this life
Goes to hell, weeping and wailing."

This concludes the ninth chapter, determining the words on the vow, from the Manifest Awakening of the Great Yoga Tantra, the Glorious Vajra Garland.

"Then, further, listen to that reality,
The unsurpassed meaning of samaya.
Whatever is the samaya of eating,
Its meaning I shall explain.
What is explained as eating
Is declared to be the five nectars.
Through applying the reality of wind,
They melt into one taste, and once again
Rise upward by the vajra path;
Their becoming one is eating.
Samaya by body, speech and mind,
The three realities and whatever arises,
And in the four wheels as well:
Whatever bliss is experienced,
Being of one taste with it, the yogin
Constantly practices; that is eating.
The supreme reality of bodhicitta
Dissolves into the state of great bliss;
Melted through the reality of wind,
Eating is excellent meditative concentration.
From the skull at the crown of the head,
Just that, by the yogin, constantly;
The drink of compassion there
Is the bodhisattva, the great bliss.
It is poured into the supreme vessel, the secret lotus;
The support fully realizes the eating.
When the mind drinks the nectar,
It drinks the substance of the taste of the five nectars.
The one who eats it is Vajradhara.
Elephant, horse, dog, cow and human,
And blood together with bodhicitta,
Mixed and eaten in that instant:
These are declared to be the five nectars.

In the vessel clear as the emptiness
Of conceptual thought and imagination,
The Vajrin constantly eats,
And goes to the far shore of the wisdom of emptiness.
The bliss of the third joy,
In the vessel together with the coemergent,
The Vajrin constantly eats,
And abides in the state of great bliss.
All the winds of conceptual thought,
In the excellent vessel of the reality of peace,
The one who knows the reality of wind eats,
And abides in the state free of conceptual thought.
Properly united with the three mudrās,
In the vessel declared to be the Mahāmudrā,
The one who knows the reality of yoga eats,
And abides in the state of breath control (prāṇāyāma).
The delusions of meditation, nonmeditation and the like,
Produced by conceptual thought and imagination,
In the vessel of the great life force;
The seeds of the net of concepts and the rest,
The fuel of the aggregates, in the excellent vessel,
Cooked by the fire of great wisdom,
The one who realizes the reality of mantra eats.
By the knowledge of the power
Of the great wrathful one of the four māras, the practitioner

Having settled well in meditative equipoise,
Constantly eats in the vessel of yoga.
The three drops of wind, of mantra and of substances,
Unsurpassed:
When they are eaten in the vessel of the indestructible state,
The yogin goes to the far shore.
The incomparable wisdom of emptiness,
Supremely, by means of the vessel of compassion,
Settling in equipoise in the great wisdom,
One drinks, and constantly drinks; this is drinking.
It is explained as the samaya of the mudrā:
Vajra and lotus perfectly united,
Whatever supreme reality arises,
That, in the fortunate vessel,
Is drunk by the yogin, delightful.
The supreme reality to be accomplished,
Coemergent and free of afflictions:
The aim of the Mahāmudrā should be accomplished.
Samayas such as these, and others,
I have explained nowhere else.
The supreme state of Vajrasattva
Is clearly realized from the guru's mouth."

This concludes the tenth chapter, determining the meaning of samaya, from the Manifest Awakening of the Great Yoga Tantra, the Glorious Vajra Garland.

"Then, further, listen, and I shall explain;
I shall explain the meaning of mere sound,
By merely knowing which
One attains the unchanging state.
In the lotus hollow of the wheel of Dharma
Mere sound truly abides:
Without attachment and without appearance,
Nondual and stainless,
Beyond example and unmoving,
Without basis and without covering,
The lord of the gods, the supreme wisdom,
Free of outbreath and inbreath.
The lord of the gods of yogins,
Such as this, is hard to find:
Supremely peaceful, great bliss,
Supreme form, supreme among the deathless.
Through the nature of dependent origination,
As one is intoxicated by the taste of grape wine,
Having gone supremely within,
It is likewise the abode of all aspects,
The support of the knowledge of the reality of mantra,
The place where one's own experience is understood."

Then Vajrasattva asked
The unsurpassed Teacher:
"Blessed One, who knows the peaceful reality,
The full analysis of the reality of mantra,
By merely understanding which
Reality will be accomplished!"

Then the Vajrin spoke:
"The unsurpassed reality of mantra,
Supreme, most supreme, and subtle,
The great wisdom, the great supreme,
The supreme essence of essences,
I shall truly explain; listen.
That which is hidden throughout all the tantras
Is the secret state without change.
The wheel of the navel is that supreme reality;
It truly abides as mere sound.
It dissolves into the center of the great emptiness,
And the state of great bliss becomes clear:
Nondual, free of fear and peaceful,
Without appearance and without the conventional.
Through the practice of nondual samādhi,
The reality of mantra is fully clear:
Beginningless and peaceful,
Chief, the very nature of entity and nonentity,
Emptiness and compassion indivisible,
The intrinsic nature of bodhicitta.
At that time, first meditate on emptiness:
The peaceful state without appearance,
The delightful abode without intrinsic nature,
Relying supremely on the nonconceptual.
The incomparable wisdom of emptiness
And the continuum of bodhicitta
Truly arise from the syllable hūṃ.
The great and marvelous knowledge of yoga:
The three realities, whose meaning is one taste,
Abide in the state of the avadhūtī.
Again, through the practice of joy,
Entering and the rest are utterly abandoned.
The state of great bliss
Is bodhicitta, stainless,
The variegated instant, indivisible,
The very nature of expression, free of vowels.
Even through the stream that constantly abides,
The descent of bodhicitta
Is attained through the yoga of mantra.
It is merely the drop, unborn and peaceful.
When one practices through the conduct of mantra,
The supreme wisdom is self-awareness."

This concludes the eleventh chapter, determining the meaning of mere sound, from the Manifest Awakening of the Great Yoga Tantra, the Glorious Vajra Garland.

"Then listen, and I shall explain
The supreme reality of wind, the ultimate.
If the yogin meditates on wind,
He will swiftly gain accomplishment.
The drop of wind is the great wisdom,
Subtle, the state of that reality, clear,
Accomplishing all things, delightful,
Nondual yoga, inexhaustible,
Unborn, all-pervading and peaceful,
Pervading all bodies,
Unconquered, empty, inconceivable,
The nature of all, ever abiding.
Through meditation, concentration and yoga,
One knows the drop's own essence:
The abode where phenomena become one,
The cause of the reality of mantra,
Utterly free from all habitual tendencies,
Omniscient, blissful, illuminating all.
At the tip of the nose, that supreme reality,
One should imagine a drop of light rays.
Properly practicing breath and its control,
Which gives the count of thoughts,
The fortunate one, the nature of the five wisdoms,
The rite of the five buddhas,
The form of union of the five jewels,
Simply the nature of the five elements.
First, the actuality of the body's mudrā;
The defining mark of the counted recitation of speech;
Devotion to the great yoga of mind:
The inexhaustible state of meditation.
Counting the units of breath,
Examining the winds,
The division by location of the channels,
Understanding the division by time,
Reversing the winds,
And abandoning them together with thought:
Thus, in brief, these six aspects
Are what is called outbreath and inbreath.

First, the outbreaths and inbreaths
Are twenty thousand and one thousand
And likewise six hundred in number,
Fixed for a day and a night.
Until the point where the count is reached,
For twenty-seven days,
By following the count,
Examine the count again and again.
Through the full division of the junctures of time,
It will then be repeated.
In each watch, the breaths
And their control should be examined:
By the count of two thousand seven hundred,
Without fixing it rigidly.
Having clearly determined them,
For twenty-seven days,
Through the division of the four wheels,
Definitely by day and by night,
Then, through effort in the locations,
Examine them thoroughly as distinct.
Through the movement in the wheel of channels,
The outbreath and inbreath, definitely,
From the practice of fixing them definitely,
One should practice with the wheel of channels.
Through the division of the channels of its wheels,
Out of the twenty-seven days,
After that, the stage of alternating the locations
Should be examined through the division of locations.
By examining each location individually,
One hundred and eight move in excess.
The supreme practice free of conceptual thought,
Bearing the mark of having abandoned thought,
The self that is the intrinsic nature of all things:
One hundred and eight move in excess.

The unsurpassed order of reckoning.

The characteristics of the seasons
I shall explain in brief.
What is made clear by the glory of the clouds:

Are well known as the seasons.
Two junctures make one season,

What is fixed as day and night

The six periods of exhalation and inhalation
Are known as a caṣa.

That is as long as turning a white mustard seed.
Three breaths make a tatkṣaṇa;
That tatkṣaṇa is a fleeting moment.
Three tatkṣaṇas make a blink of the eye;

From consciousness wind is born;
From that, fire is born;
From fire, water arises;
From that, earth is truly born.
From these the aggregates arise;

In a sequence of a hundred plus eight,

Having passed beyond the afflicted mind,
Having obtained mantra, it expands

Pervasive and subtle, it obscures.

Becomes inert and falls into a swoon.
It obscures the portion of the sense sources.
Pure wisdom, supreme of the supreme:
The life force that has dissolved from that
Is concomitant with consciousness.
Thus the supreme reality has been explained.
The dissolving and arising of the life force,
Fully understood just as it is,
Comes through the succession of the guru lineage.

This is explained as Samantabhadra.
Thus the eight supreme groups of consciousness
Are the correct and unsurpassed reality.
The consciousnesses of the senses, eye and the rest,
Have the nature of seeing form and the rest.
The shins, the shoulders, the thighs, the kneecaps,
The two doors, and the whole:
The characteristics of the ten knowings
Are fully known in the pervading places.
Yamāntaka, Prajñāntaka,
Padmāntaka, Vighnāntaka,
Ṭakkirāja, Acala,
Nīladaṇḍa and Mahābala,
Sumbharāja and Uṣṇīṣacakravartin:
Thus, through the division of their places,
They are fully illuminated by the three wisdoms.
Having become a single essence,
They are the support of correct suchness.
One who follows that reality according to the rite
Should learn it from the lineage of gurus.
The reality of mantra is the supreme yoga,
Great wisdom well expanded,
Unborn, unending, unmanifest,
Unchanging and without obscuration,
Nondual, free of fear, peaceful,
The supreme state of great bliss.
Through the full division into the snake, the compiled,
The endless, and the headless:
That with oṃ and ending in hūṃ phaṭ svā
Is designated the snake.
Its own name, qualities, and form
Bear the name of the characteristic of the compiled.
That beginning with oṃ but without a foot
Bears the name called the endless.

That ending in svā but without oṃ
Is explained as what is called the headless.
That having everything but without oṃ
Is explained as what is called the headless.
Having understood the characteristics in this way,
The reality of mantra is unsurpassed.
Following the root tantra,
One should learn its vast meaning.
The reality of wisdom is the supreme yoga,
Having entirely abandoned all duality,
Joining all the senses in equality,
All things a single body,
Having abandoned all conceptuality of speech,
Having entirely abandoned all appearances,
Having the nature of self-blessing,
Pervading all, marvelous:
The supreme yoga of self-awareness.
The characteristic of the four instants
Is partless, all-going, and pervasive;
It is not known by childish yogins,
Who do not fully know the conventional.
The ultimate, one's own essential nature,
Through the divisions of the completion stage,
Fully illuminates the reality of mantra.
One should meditate on a itself
Dissolving completely into the final drop.
The drop dissolves into the syllable a;
a into the mere ha at the end;
ha in turn into the syllable hūṃ;
The syllable ū into haṃ;
The syllable haṃ in turn dissolves into oṃ.
The syllable a is the mere drop;
The drop in turn dissolves into sound;
Sound in turn into mere sound;
Supreme mere sound into space;
And that in turn dissolves into luminosity.
The clear state of luminosity
Is the utterly pure dharmadhātu,
The supreme nondual wisdom,
Fully awakening and fully dawning,
Free of all the activities of speech,
Having entirely abandoned all duality.
The five aggregates are a single ground;
The five elements are rightly relied upon;
The group of eight consciousnesses is isolated;
The six outer objects are gathered in;
By the ten winds the ten knowings are gathered in.
Thus all the characteristics are gathered.
By the yoga of emanating on every side,
That in which the three existences abide,
Accomplishing all siddhis, glorious,
Increasing all good fortune:
That vajra is Vajrasattva,
Vajrabhairava and Īśvara,
Heruka and Kālacakra,
The Ādibuddha, the great sage,
Samantabhadra and Mañjuśrī,
Having the nature of emanating variety,
The ground of the host of varied qualities.
The yoga of emanating on every side
Is the great stainless yoga.
The clarity of the reality of wisdom,
Without conceptuality and without abiding,
Is called the transference of the dharmadhātu.
The characteristics of generation and completion
Are like the tip of a hair split eight thousand times,
Utterly clear and luminous.
Through the divisions of scripture and reasoning,
They are learned from the lineage of gurus.
Thus are the three supreme realities.
The stages transmitted orally arise.
The supreme wisdom of the secret,
The supreme instant, the characteristic of the fourth.

This is the twenty-third chapter, establishing the commitment of the three realities, from the Manifest Realization of the Glorious Vajra Garland, the great yoga tantra.

"Then I shall rightly explain; listen.
The characteristic of the three nose-tips:
The secret, the heart, and the nose of the face are their names.
These three are made clear from the tantra.
Through the divisions of the three wheels
Is the stage of the yogin's meditation.
For one who has come under the power of intense passion,
It grants the bliss of mind.
Through the practice of the reality of wind,
The wheels of the channels are awakened,
And great bliss arises everywhere.
The three realms having become of one taste,
One should perform attraction and subjugation according to the rite.
The mind of awakening is great bliss.
The characteristic of the coemergent instant
Should be held at the tip of the vajra.
That itself is great bliss,
Having the nature of the five wisdoms,
Having entirely abandoned all conceptual thought.
At the secret nose-tip, the substance, the drop,
Has the nature of being rightly held.
By the single mind of great passion
It is to be known as the supreme yoga.
On the sixty-four lotus petals
The wheels of the channels dawn in order.
Its hub is explained as the nose;
The secret grants all bliss.
The heart lotus has eight petals;
Its hub too is declared to be the nose.
One should meditate on the mind of aversion itself,
The wisdom mind of blazing secret mantra,
The cause that rightly emanates,
Having abandoned all conceptuality of speech,
The supreme wisdom, greatly marvelous.
Mantra always arises as nāda,
The great bliss free of conceptuality,
Joined to meditative concentration, the supreme of bliss.
By obtaining the oral instruction, bliss is victorious.
Having swiftly brought aversion under control,
The single cause of the reality of peace,
Moon, sun, and fire, these three,
Pervades from the center of the three paths.
This is explained as what is called the nose of the face.
The characteristics of the abodes of the winds,
And the great wisdom that abides there:
The drop is suppressed by delusion,
And meditative concentration is victorious over supreme delusion.
Having entirely abandoned all things,
The nature of the five wisdoms is clear,
The supreme support of the five realities.
Its rays of light, the mere drop,
Are rightly supported at the nose-tip of the face.
One should meditate on this supreme yoga.
It is victorious over the ocean of delusion.
That itself is rightly explained
As bearing the name of the commitment being.
The commitment of all buddhas
Has the characteristic of the five wisdoms.
By yogins who are skilled,
The darkness of delusion is destroyed.
The characteristic of realizing omniscience:
When the families of the five buddhas abide there,
The five winds emerge from the openings of the nose-tip
And rightly move upward,
And come to operate constantly in the body.
Moving conventionally in the nose,
They emerge from that door,
Left, right, both,
And moving slowly: four modes.
The element emerging from the right
Is the maṇḍala of fire;
Its color is a fine red,
And it is the movement of the Lotus Protector.
The element emerging from the left,
Mixed with the maṇḍala of wind,
Appears green and yellow,
And it is the movement of the Karma Protector.
The element emerging from both
Appears golden in color;
It is the maṇḍala of Mahendra,
And it is the movement of the Jewel Protector.
The element moving slowly and without movement,
Appearing like pure crystal,
Is the maṇḍala of water,
And it is the movement of the Vajra Protector.

It arises in truth from the three realms.
What is held as support and supported
Has the nature of Vairocana,
And comes forth at the end of dying.
Those four maṇḍalas,
Through their arrangement, are constantly repeated.
By the count of mantra recitation,
They are repeated constantly, day and night.
Thus one should meditate on the wind at the nose-tip
As five-colored.
Through the supreme yoga of nonduality,
The host of light rays is stainless.
Through it, the activities of pacifying and the rest
Are accomplished by gradual practice,
And one comes to go to Vajrasattva.
Then Vajrapāṇi asked
Vajrasattva, protector of beings:
"Concerning the system of the three drops,
If I am to know it by serving at your feet,
I have some doubt about it.
You who end existence, please make it clear.
When the three drops, substance,
Mantra, and likewise wind, are meditated upon,
Are they meditated by a single yogin,
Or by separate yogins?
Chief who cuts through all doubt about this!"
The Blessed Vajrasattva spoke:
"For those long under the sway of great passion,
Who are to be protected in the three existences,
For the sake of liberating them,
The drop of substance is taught.
Whatever people have come under the sway of great aversion,
With bodies untamed,
For the sake of their swift liberation,
The drop of mantra is taught.
Whoever are utterly confused by great delusion,
To protect them from the ocean of existence,
So that they may attain the fruit of the eight siddhis,
The ocean of wisdom
Is taught as the drop of wind.
The elaboration of correct reality
Is thus the three correct drops,
According to one's disposition and according to one's practice.
For the sake of accomplishment in this very life,
For the purification of all obscurations,
With a one-pointed mind,
On a delightful site where the three wisdoms abide,
Adorned with flowers, fruits, and water,
On a mountain peak secluded from people,
The gathering of all siddhis
Should be accomplished with effort."

This is the twenty-fourth chapter, establishing the three nose-tips, from the Manifest Realization of the Glorious Vajra Garland, the great yoga tantra.

"Then I shall rightly explain; listen.
The characteristic of the wisdom of emptiness
Is that very supreme thing that abides in the body.
As the self of all, it always abides.
It makes all phenomena appear.
Its characteristic is held to be emptiness.
From it comes wisdom, great bliss,
The utterly pure luminosity.
Because it enters avadhūtī,
It is held to be true emptiness.
The conceptual appearances of phenomena spread forth;
It cuts off all conceptual thought.
The resting of the wind of conceptuality
Is explained as emptiness.
That itself is the world realm
Of the great chief, Akṣobhya.
The ground where he constantly dwells
Bears the name of the wisdom of life;
It has the characteristic of the peaceful nature
Of the great formless Rudra.
In it is wisdom, great bliss,
Filled with the mind of awakening.
That itself is the abode of the state of great bliss.
Wisdom, the true mind of awakening,
And so on: by these sounds of names
The single thing expressed is great bliss,
Possessing a single entity.
It is called the wisdom of emptiness.
It is explained as Sukhāvatī, the Blissful.
Bliss is the characteristic of attaining virtue;
That which abides in bliss
Is called Sukhāvatī.
In the buddha field of Amitāyus,
The supreme ground of all bliss,
The mind of awakening is Amitāyus.
The light that comes forth and arises from it:
Therefore it is known as Amitābha,
The supreme ground of all bliss.
Emptiness is the Lord of the Vajradhātu;
And the union in equality of that
With the lord of the vajra of wisdom
Is called the wisdom of emptiness.
Wisdom is the lotus, lalanā;
When the menses, the sun, flows,
That too is known as empty,
Having entirely abandoned all duality.
Method is the vajra, rasanā;
The moon, śukra, flows forth;
That is taught as supreme wisdom,
The supreme state of great bliss.
Know the union of those two as supreme:
The inexhaustible wisdom of emptiness.
It is the full moon and that very reality.
It is learned from the mouth of a yoginī.

This is the twenty-fifth chapter, explaining the words of emptiness, from the Manifest Realization of the Glorious Vajra Garland, the great yoga tantra.

"Then, further, I shall explain; listen.
The body is the abode of emptiness.
That by which the body is known
Is the supreme palace of all the deities,
The support of enjoyment, Dharma, and emanation,
And of great bliss.
The wheels too, as they have been explained,
In their order, without exception,
Thus always truly abide there.
The lord of dissolution and enjoyment
Is the deity itself, supremely nondual,
The support of the state of the true essence.
For the sake of the state of great wisdom,
It is the true support of great bliss.
Great aversion and great delusion,
Great jealousy and the reality of passion:
Through these, the great wrathful ones and the supreme of aspects
Definitively cut the knots of saṃsāra.
Through the divisions of earth, water, likewise fire,
Wind, and space;
Form, sound, likewise smell,
Taste and the tangible;
The dharmadhātu, the fine eye,
Ear, nose, and tongue,
The body, consciousness, and phenomena,
The ground of all and the afflicted mind;
Through the divisions of the faculties of faith, diligence,
Mindfulness, concentration, and wisdom;
The power of faith, the power of diligence,
The power of mindfulness, the power of concentration,
And the unsurpassed power of wisdom:
These are rightly known in their places as they are,
And made clear as the deities of the body.
Through the divisions of their names as before,
Through the divisions of mantra and their order,
By the yoga of setting out the mantras,
Through the divisions of their places in order,
Through the divisions of the true places
Ascertained by scripture,
And with the goddesses, by the yogin constantly,
The state of self-blessing is established.
Enjoying the five sense pleasures,
One should make offerings with great effort.
Do not bow down to outer deities
Whose nature is stone, wood, or earth.
That very stage of self-blessing
Does not exist elsewhere in the three existences.
Reality is the supreme yoga,
The self-blessing free of obscuration.
The stages of mantra and mudrā and the rest,
Outer things, are not to be counted.
The yogin who has attained that reality
Is held to bless, with effort,
Supremely through the yoginī.
This is the characteristic of the method of self-blessing."

This is the twenty-sixth chapter, on the characteristic of the wisdom of the body's deities, from the Manifest Realization of the Glorious Vajra Garland, the great yoga tantra.

"Then, further, I shall explain
The characteristics of arising and setting.
Through the channels comes the supreme yoga,
The abode of all accomplishments.
From the objects that stand before one,
Through mental attention one does not go there.
The former habitual tendencies dwell within;
At that time the senses are utterly clear.
By that, that is fully awakened,
And they abide in order in the channels.
Once it has been awakened by that full awakening,
The goddess herself, too, from the wheel,
Through that, is said to be the stage of arising.
Setting, too, I shall explain.
The objects, too, are absent, devoid of substance;
Mental attention dwells elsewhere;
The habitual tendencies do not awaken;
The channels do not come to arise;
And for the deities to be truly awakened,
For that reason, it does not come about.
From the center of the heart, the reality of bliss,
The clear light of the buddha sun,
Through the forms of grasped and grasper as well,
Is the twelve rays of light:
Increasing Light, Ray, and She of Splendor,
Clear One, Blazing One, and She of Blazing Light;
Relying on the eye and the rest,
These grasp outer form and so on.
Dispeller of Darkness, She of Light, She Who Illumines,
Clear Beauty, Clear Light, and Clear Sun;
These are the aspects of outer form and so on.
These six are held to be below.
Whatever goddesses arise,
That is the arising of the sun of wisdom.
And its true setting
Is also the supreme setting.
The cessation of arising and setting
Should be known as the supreme state.
The great yoga, well awakened,
Is the restraint of the buddha sun.
This is the supreme suchness of yoga,
The characteristic of arising and setting.
Following the vajra secret,
It should be known from the lineage of gurus."

This is the twenty-eighth chapter, on arising and setting, from the Manifest Realization of the Glorious Vajra Garland, the great yoga tantra.

"Then, further, listen as I explain
The essential nature of the junction channels,
The arising of the restraint of the channels.
Whoever knows it constantly
Experiences supreme joy,
Makes the wisdom of the deity clear,
Truly joins with the mantra of the deity,
And the yoga of the deity, of definitive meaning,
The stainless union of the three suchnesses.
These three at once restrain the self.
Pervading the three worlds, and subtle,
It truly rests in the entire body.
The wisdom being is Vajrasattva;
Because the commitment being is bliss,
It should be known as the supreme yoga.
The marvelous suchness of pervasion,
Supreme, most supreme, and subtle,
Self-arisen wisdom, inexhaustible:
Thirty-two channels of the mind of awakening flow.
All the channels supremely expand
And abide in the wheel of great bliss.
The root, the nature of all the parts:
In its center are three channels,
Which are also truly explained as the principal ones.
Moon, sun, and fire move,
Pervading all the elements.

When it stands firm in the ground,
Completely pervades with trunk, branches,

Is supremely and completely pervaded.

The reality of the three doors of liberation,
The restraint of the three suchnesses,
Attachment, aversion, and delusion,

The three kinds of characteristic of commitment;

Are the characteristic of that threefold suchness.
Cause, result, and the attendant
Are the characteristic of the three yogas.
Wind, phlegm, and bile,
Divided into three, are the elements in summary.
The desire realm, the form realm, and the formless realm
Are the characteristic of the three worlds.
Three roots and a single fruit:
Becoming so in sequence, the wheel of Dharma turns.
That is made clear as three emanations,
Through the nature of body, speech, and mind.
When that is joined to that,
That is the characteristic of an eclipse.
When it is joined only slightly,
Then it is the characteristic of a partial eclipse.
Because when the life-sustaining wind is destroyed,
Then it is the characteristic of death.
The principal ones of the channels themselves
Are called the three certainties.
In detail, from the yoginī tantra,
The yogin knows them clearly."

This is the thirty-first chapter, on the certainty of the three junction channels, from the Manifest Realization of the Glorious Vajra Garland, the great yoga tantra.

"Then, further, listen as I explain
The characteristic of the arising of the winds:
When the opportunity of time is sought,
How it is truly known in reality.
The mind with afflicted habitual tendencies
Sees its own nature as another form;
It does not know the meaning as it is to be known,
And is deluded by wind alone.
Having drawn in the foundation consciousness,
It moves about within the habitual tendencies.
By the habitual tendencies consciousness is seized;
From the stage of mixing with the habitual tendencies,
Imbued with the inner habitual tendencies,
At the time of their menstrual period,
When the force enters the womb,
The vajra semen and blood, the melted drop,
Become mingled,
And from the foundation, consciousness arises.
Just as one is intoxicated by the taste of beer,
From the habitual tendencies of the foundation consciousness
The stream of consciousness arises.
The afflicted one itself, the life-sustaining wind itself,
Truly moves in a single instant.
At the time of the first transition,
It becomes like the shape of a fish.
At the time of the second transition,
Mingling once again with blood,
Through the division into above and below,
The life-sustaining wind goes in two.
In the going and coming of the downward-voiding wind,
It comes to take the shape of a tortoise.
Then, in the third transition,
When the blood and semen are consumed,
The mass of the downward-voiding wind
Dries, and wind arises everywhere.
It abides facing upward,
And comes to bear a body like a boar.
Again, in the fourth transition,
The blood and semen are imbued;
Having drawn in the upward-moving wind,
It moves facing downward.
And with the shape of a lion,
It emanates the pervading wind.
Then, in the fifth transition,
The blood and semen increase;
The fire-accompanying wind trembles,
And the form of fire proceeds in sequence.
The fire-accompanying wind emanates the pervading wind,
By which movement and trembling come about.
When the five aggregates are complete,
As has thus been taught, then from that, again,
At the time of the sixth transition,
The earth element and the two eyes appear,
And by the pervading wind the nāga wind is emanated;
It increases the substance of wind.
Then, in the seventh transition,
The water element and the openings of the nose emerge;
By the nāga wind the Kūrma wind
Is emanated, and they unite without duality.
Then, in the eighth transition,
Fire and the openings of the nose arise;
By the Kūrma wind the Kṛkara wind is emanated,
And it comes to know its own nature.
Then, in the ninth transition,
The wind element appears and taste is felt;
By the great wind called Kṛkara
The Devadatta wind is emanated.
After that, in the tenth transition,
By the Devadatta wind Dhanañjaya is emanated,
Which has the characteristic of gathering all the winds.
In the center of the nine is the pervading element,
Subtle, with the characteristic of being unobstructed;
The hollows of the channels, intestines, and sinews
Develop the nine doors.
The supreme feeling of the body
Holds the characteristic of a single touch.
In space, the supreme full awakening,
The excellent clear light, truly abides.
The ten knowledges of the buddhas,
Just such as these, I shall explain.
The ten transitions are the ten grounds,
To be known in their sequence.
The mother is the perfection of wisdom;
The bhaga is called the great mudrā.
The child is a truly perfect buddha,
All-knowing and all-seeing,
The hair of his head shaven,
Wearing the red robe of the caul,
Glorious, with palms joined,
Bowing with the crown of his head touching the ground.
He bears the thirty-two major marks,
Adorned with the eighty excellent minor marks,
The great wisdom well awakened,
Having attained the formations, together with liberation.
The great hero, free of conceptual thought,
Destroys the flesh that generates him.
The five nectars are great wisdom;
He constantly experiences the five nectars.
Having abandoned all entities,
The yogin acts like a lion.
These two supreme ones are secret,
And have not been taught anywhere else.
The certainty of the arising of the winds
Should be known from the lineage of gurus."

This is the thirty-second chapter, on the entry into birth of the person generated by the winds, from the Manifest Realization of the Glorious Vajra Garland, the great yoga tantra.

"Then, further, I shall explain
The supreme setting of the winds,
Through the true knowledge of which
One knows the characteristic of the moment of death.
They are truly awakened one after another,
And they dissolve in the order of their arising.
The true arising of the winds
Is like the sun setting in turn.
First, when ten years have passed,
When the wind intoxicated with vigor arises,
It devours the Dhanañjaya wind,
Gradually, as Rāhu devours the moon.
After that, when ten years have passed,
It truly arises through the wind of the waters of vigor,
And devours the wind called Devadatta,
The sequence of the winds being as before.
Further, when ten years have passed,
The wind of the waters of vigor arises
And devours the wind called Kṛkara.
Then, further, ten years pass,
And through the sequence born of the years,
The great wind of release and holding
Devours the Kūrma wind.
The nāga wind and the wind of release and holding,
Because they are equal in strength and splendor,
When another period of ten years has passed,
Are unable to do harm,
Because their strength and capacity are equal;
Neither is able
To do harm to the other.
That ten passes on further.

Once that has elapsed, the wind of release and holding gains great strength,
And gradually devours the nāga wind.
Then, when another ten has passed,
Blood and flesh increase;
Through the stage of vigor and intoxication,
It devours the wind of release and holding.
After that, when ten years have elapsed,
Having drawn in the wind of the waters of intoxication,
Fat and saliva increase,
And its release and holding devours the nāga.
Then, in the year when another ten has passed,
The strength of the wind of release and holding arises,
And it devours the wind of the time of intoxication.
Fire and wind increase;
The wind of release and holding itself transforms,
And great enjoyment comes to increase.
Then, when another ten years have elapsed,
Through wind the body swells and grows heavy.
When water and wind mingle,
From the stage of pervading and obscuring,
From that, another ten years pass,
And like the fruit of the plantain tree it is gone;
Together with its root, it comes to be no more.
By the limit of the ripening of karma,
Thus, through the tenth stage,
A month or another day,
Life having been shortened,
The life force should be known to be exhausted.
From the Ḍākinī Compendium Tantra
It should be known in great detail.

This is the thirty-third chapter, the detailed explanation of the exhaustion of life through the sequence of the winds, from the Manifest Realization of the Glorious Vajra Garland, the great yoga tantra.

"Then, further, listen as I explain
The great characteristic, the supreme wisdom,
The gathering ḍākinī, the supreme yoga,
The essence in which the meaning of the essence is gathered.
Because it is gathered by the ḍākinī,
Therefore it is called the gathering ḍākinī.
Ever dwelling in the center of the navel,
With light equal to a thousand suns,
The fire of great wisdom arises
And destroys all conceptual thought.
By its blazing, the conceptions of the aggregates
Are burned, and the body is without interruption.
The conceptions of the vajra element
Are gathered, and again, by its nature,
The conceptions of grasped and grasper
Compassion gathers there once more.
Thus, like a moth in a flame,
It burns up all bonds.
Through the manifest application of the reality of wind,
Through the application of the body of mantra,
Even the slightest conceptual thought
Is burned away; of this there is no doubt.
From the Ḍākinī Compendium Tantra,
If one knows the stages of the supreme yoga,
The wisdom of the ḍākinī in its fullness
Should be understood by relying on yoga."

This is the thirty-fourth chapter, on the gathering of the ḍākinīs, from the Manifest Realization of the Glorious Vajra Garland, the great yoga tantra.

"Then, further, listen as I explain
The cutting off of the winds of conceptual thought.
Know that by day and by night
One hundred and eight move in excess.
Those winds are conceptual thoughts;
Because it cuts them off,
That yoga is supremely peaceful
And makes everything into the nature of emptiness.
Without doubt, first, the six unions
Are to be supremely accomplished through knowledge of yoga.
Then the vowelless letter ha
Should be recited very extensively,
Because it destroys the knots
And grants the arising of bliss.
By it all conceptual thoughts
Are cut off in an instant.
Yoga and subsequent yoga,
Ultimate yoga and great yoga:
Through their eighteen divisions,
First the first union should be meditated;
Seventeen for subsequent yoga and the rest.
From that, the supreme king of maṇḍalas:
In the supreme yoga there are five divisions.
From that, too, the supreme king of actions:
Twelve divisions of yoga.
Its divisions are forty-nine.
Through the condensed generation stage,
It is the yoga that brings direct perception.
Then the supreme stage of vajra recitation,
The limb of yoga of the completion stage,
Destroys conceptual thought;
It is the subtle suchness, unsurpassed.
The yogin, according to the rite,
Endowed with intelligence, should meditate as before;
It cuts the bonds of existence.
Great yoga, great wisdom,
Great suchness, great marvel:
Through meditating with certainty, truly
The wind of conceptual thought is destroyed.
Through recitation that follows the meaning,
The single nature of nonentity,
And relying on emptiness, being intent upon it,
Meditation is made firmly stable.
When the practitioner truly meditates on
The unsurpassed stage of vajra recitation,
In its sequence, the supreme wisdom
Will be attained; of this there is no doubt."

This is the thirty-fifth chapter, on the characteristic of cutting off the winds of conceptual thought, from the Manifest Realization of the Glorious Vajra Garland, the great yoga tantra.

"Then, further, listen as I explain.
When the nonconceptual wind has dawned,
Whoever knows it attains the excellent state
That is Vajrasattva's own reality.
The great nonconceptual wind
Has abandoned all duality;
It is the self that is the intrinsic nature of all entities,
Free of the state of meditation and nonmeditation.
The great wisdom of the great yoga,
Without abode and without stain,
Abides in the center of all the subtle parts,
Free of bindu and nāda,
All-pervading and peaceful,
The single characteristic of self-blessing.
It is liberated from the bases of characteristics and the characteristics,
Free of habitual tendencies and conceptual thought,
Of joints and the path of the knots,
And free of meditation and the object of meditation;
It is the characteristic of the wisdom of emptiness.
The portions that are aspects of mantra and mudrā,
And conduct with aspects, it has abandoned.
It is not to be read even in the tantras
Of action, conduct, and yoga.
The great tantra of the great yoga,
Which is to be explained, is the supreme commitment;
In accordance with the instructions and in accordance with the rite,
It is fitting to explain it at the very beginning.
To know how to follow its sequence
Is the supreme state of the lineage of gurus."

This is the thirty-sixth chapter, on the characteristic of the nonconceptual, from the Manifest Realization of the Glorious Vajra Garland, the great yoga tantra.

Then Vajrin spoke:
"The yoga of the setting of the channels,
Its suchness, listen as I explain.
Objects and senses are truly joined,
And the channels, too, come to arise.
Having dawned and gone on as before,
There is the unsurpassed generation stage.
Because conceptual thought arises,
There is the sequence that exceeds by a hundred and eight.
From that come birth, old age, and sickness,
Death, and turning back once more.
Because of the wisdom purified
By the objects' lack of entity,
Because one practices that suchness here,
Because objects in reality lack entity,
The senses do not arise.
From that, the pairs are truly joined:
Entity and nonentity, emptiness.
Then the channels come to set,
Relying, too, on the sequence of the wheels.
From their setting comes pacification.
The seventy-two thousand
All become the wheel of channels.
That suchness pervades within all phenomena.
From the yoga of meditating on emptiness,
The essential nature of objects
Is known with certainty; and thus
The vajra of awareness conquers."

This is the thirty-seventh chapter, on purification through the setting of the channels, from the Manifest Realization of the Glorious Vajra Garland, the great yoga tantra.

"Then, further, listen as I explain
The characteristic of the state of the six practices:
It is joined just exactly as it is,
Relying on the limbs as one wishes,
And is divided from those six aspects.
Likewise, eights upon eights exceed that:
Eight twice, and four sets of eight;
Thus the union is made clear by number.
Like the portion of a half moon,
Emptiness abides in the wheel of wind.
Neither half nor full,
It appears like the moon in water.
The great illusion, the mother of beings,
Has the characteristic of supporting all;
The wheel of wind is called the life force.
It is not the domain of the learned.
The wheel of fire is pervaded by emptiness;
In its center, like a bindu,
Unchanging and going everywhere,
Self-arisen and pervading all,
It truly increases bliss.
Free of vowels and consonants,
When the three worlds without exception
Are made one, it generates them.
It has the state of indestructible nāda,
Free of all aspects,
Moving like the tongue of a snake,
And Brahmā and the other forefathers,
Neither fearsome nor moving,
Neither letter nor word,
Free of the mass of the elements,
Beyond number, inexhaustible, unborn.
The navel abides in the wheel of emanation.
Dwelling in the center of the wheel of Dharma,
Mere sound, free of stain,
Free of attachment and without attachment,
Without activity or agent, and without abode,
Without example and without abiding,
Free of life force and of effort,
The lord of the gods, the supreme wisdom:
Such as this is hard to find.
It abides supremely in the wheel of enjoyment,
The supreme aspect without bondage.
Supremely realized,
It truly dwells in all aspects.
Of the nature of dependent origination,
Like intoxication from the taste of beer,
The supreme, the first, without exception,
Abides within all entities.
It abides beyond beginning and end,
Without characteristic and without appearance,
Free of all characteristics,
Peaceful, fearless, inexpressible,
The experience of its nature, its very essence,
It abides in the wheel of great bliss.
It is obtained from the lineage of gurus.
Thus is the great distinction of wisdom:
Its own essence, the six consciousnesses,
Truly joined above and below,
Is renowned as yoga."

This is the thirty-eighth chapter, on meditating on the six wheels, from the Manifest Realization of the Glorious Vajra Garland, the great yoga tantra.

Then the Blessed Vajra One spoke:
"The restraint that is the characteristic of time,
Which the yogin fully knows,
Is the unsurpassed yoga of number.
Time is said to be the measure of time.
The reality of the conceived and of what is free of conception
Truly abides in two aspects:
Good time and bad time.
Emerging from the openings of the nose,
It constantly arises from the outer door.
Gathering inward is called good time;
Emerging is held to be bad time.
What is other than bad time is supreme;
Good time is supreme, itself.
The Age of Perfection (Kṛtayuga), the Age of Three (Tretā),
And the Age of Two (Dvāpara) are good time;
The Age of Strife (Kali) is bad.
Such is the characteristic of time as taught.
Joy, supreme joy,
And the coemergent are good time;
To be free of joy is bad time.
Thus the characteristic of time is explained.
The time of entering and of abiding
Is well known as good time;
Rising is bad time.
Thus is the characteristic of time.
Time is all the elements:
Equality, the supreme state of single taste.
Free of wisdom and consciousness,
Time is declared to be one.
Free of entity, nonentity, and elaboration,
The characteristic of the fourth moment,
Liberated from what is expressed and what expresses it,
Is declared to be 'one time.'
Definitively liberated from attachment and nonattachment,
Great bliss, having relinquished any state,
The basis of the meaning of the great mudrā,
Is declared to be 'one time.'
Without characteristic and without appearance,
Free of number and of yoga,
That suchness is definitively explained as time;
It should be known from the lineage of gurus."

This is the thirty-ninth chapter, explaining time and the measure of time, from the Manifest Realization of the Glorious Vajra Garland, the great yoga tantra.

"Then, further, I shall explain;
Listen to the supreme secret fruit,
Through which one goes to the supreme accomplishment.
Truly relying on the great mudrā,
It is called the hidden secret.
Childish yogins do not know it.
It bears the name 'the fourth empowerment.'
The stages transmitted from mouth to ear,
The supremely peaceful great wisdom,
Free of all conceptual thought,
The great accomplishment of the great mudrā,
The condensed meaning of the great yoga,
Learned from the yoginī tantra,
The supremely marvelous essential nature,
Which brings down the taste of great wisdom,
Endowed with the qualities of the great suchness,
The ground abiding in supreme space,
Vajrasattva's own essence,
Makes the blisses increase.
It transcends the state of great bliss,
Is free of knower and known,
Free of meditation and object of meditation,
Empty of the activities of mantra and tantra;
It abandons all conceptualization
And makes clear the state of all entities.
The person in the aspect of the three worlds,
Free of attachment and nonattachment,
Abides in the great commitment:
The innumerable three worlds, one reality.
This is the tantra of the suchness of accomplishment.
Thus the secret fruit is made clear,
Which has not been taught anywhere else.
The supreme wisdom of the supreme yoga
Should be known from the yoginī tantra."

This is the fortieth chapter, called The Secret Fruit, from the Manifest Realization of the Glorious Vajra Garland, the great yoga tantra.

Then the Blessed Vajra One spoke:
"The supreme state of the dharmadhātu:
The element that pervades from the dharmadhātu
Is therein declared to be the dharmadhātu.
All-pervading, supremely subtle,
Going everywhere, ever arising,
The five aggregates, the five elements,
Objects, sense sources, and consciousnesses,
And all the phenomena truly endowed with them,
Are explained to be the lord of the secret.
The element abiding in their center
Pervades, and is not a domain of experience.
Just as sesame oil is in sesame,
Just as fire is in wood,
So it pervades all phenomena,
And yet is not seen.
Stainless like space,
The supreme basis of all phenomena,
Though it pervades, through the conceptions
Of grasped and grasper it is not seen.
Through the yoga of the wisdom of emptiness,
The yogin sees it clearly.
The dharmadhātu is declared to be the bhaga.
The bhaga is a jewel casket.
Because it is endowed with the qualities
Of lordship and the rest, it is called the bhaga.
The bhaga is also explained as phenomena;
The space is said to be the mind of awakening.
The cause of the beings of the three realms
Is explained as what is called the bhaga.
The space, through the reality of true union,
Truly abides like the moon in water.
One knows the suchness of the single ultimate
Like an emblic fruit placed in the palm of the hand;
It appears as the supreme suchness.
Self-awareness, utterly stainless,
When entity and nonentity are exhausted,
Constantly sees that suchness, and it appears.
The state of the dharmadhātu is clear.
Thus it has been explained in great detail:
Through freedom from grasped and grasper,
Knowledge free of union is marvelous."

This is the forty-first chapter, explaining the meaning of the dharmadhātu, from the Manifest Realization of the Glorious Vajra Garland, the great yoga tantra.

Then Vajrasattva said:
"The unsurpassed rite of worship,
Through the yoga of nondual equality,
Is the sequence of the rite of worship within the body.
To images made of stone, wood,
Clay, or gold, one does not bow.
Consecration, maṇḍala, and fire offering:
From then on, one never performs them.
Excrement, vajra water, the jewel, and the rest:
The five that are oneself are always to be worshiped;
Therefore one is directly engaged in that.
The vajra of mind, oneself, is the principal.
That mind, too, has no substantial existence,
Because its characteristic is emptiness,
And emptiness too is without form.
Therefore everything is like space.
Thus consciousness too, completely purified,
Should be worshiped in the three channels as well.
Because whatever is the nature of mind
Does not exist as something other than mind,
One abides as Vajrasattva oneself
And in the nature of mind.
The unsurpassed form of the deity
Is nothing other than mind.
Therefore, the discerning one, in this way,
Always worships his own body alone.
Always practicing in the five families,
One examines them as a single family.
The distinctions of wanted and unwanted,
And of drinkable and undrinkable, are abandoned.
'A yogin is fortunate' or 'unfortunate':
This one should never say.
The aspects of the mudrā, blue and so forth:
Likewise form has three divisions.
Having summoned Rūpavajrā,
One should offer to Kṣitigarbha.
True song and melody beyond words:
Likewise sound has three divisions.
Properly summon Śabdavajrā
And offer to Vajrapāṇi.
Camphor, aloeswood, jasmine, and the like:
Likewise smell has three divisions.
Properly summon Gandhavajrā
And offer to Ākāśagarbha.
Kissing and sucking
And the six tastes likewise have three divisions.
Properly summon Rasavajrā
And offer to Lokeśvara.
Properly joining vajra and lotus,
Garments and embracing: three divisions.
Properly summon Sparśavajrā
And one should offer to Sarvanivaraṇaviṣkambhin.
All these outer dharmas,
Both those imagined and those not imagined:
Properly summon Dharmadhātuvajrā
And offer to Mañjuvajra.
Likewise the outer and the inner,
And both together, unapprehended,
Having made them threefold in form,
One should offer to the deities.
The five objects definitely abide;
That which definitely arises in the five continua,
In dream, and joined with the wind,
Enjoys the five desirables.
Through whichever faculty and whichever path
The practitioner obtains an object,
All of these, in order to please
The one of great compassion, should be offered.
With flowers of true joy,
Vajra, sun, and lotus arise.
Worshiping oneself with flowers
Is the supreme samaya of the three vajras.
That which is not the domain of conceptual ways,
And which is thus definitely burned
By the fire of caṇḍālī, is incense:
Offer it to the vajra of mind.
Arising from the stages
Of the vajra truly entering the lotus and the rest,
The indestructible sound of the yogin
Should be offered to the vajra of mind.
Truly arisen from the syllable hūṃ,
The mantra's light rays, utterly clear,
The true light of a blazing lamp,
Should be offered to the vajra of mind.
Great bliss abiding in space,
And the mind of awakening awakened,
Anointing with that very thing
Should be offered to the vajra of mind.
Here, whatever is enjoyed,
Properly relied upon by all the faculties,
All of that is the vajra of mind:
It should be offered in the vessel of food.
Laughter, flirtation, and play:
Through their variety they delight the mind.
These and the doors of liberation
Offer to the vajra of mind."
This concludes the fifty-sixth chapter, on the classification of the rite of inner self-worship, from the Great Yoga Tantra, the Manifest Realization of the Glorious Vajra Garland.
Then the Blessed Vajra One said:
"The supreme characteristics of the goddess:
Those who contemplate the bliss of yoga,
Through the nature of the divisions of the channels,
Always engage in the play of the body.
These are explained as its deities.
Always delighted by every bliss,
A body graceful in every way,
Wholly pervaded by great bliss,
Is properly declared to be the deity itself.
Joy and supreme joy,
Cessation, and the coemergent moment;
Rain and play,
Flirtation surpassing all,
Fainting and becoming awakened:
These are explained as the goddesses.
When they arise outwardly,
Because they come into true union,
The channels of the body are properly awakened;
Therefore they are explained as yoginīs."
This concludes the fifty-seventh chapter, on the characteristics of the goddesses, from the Great Yoga Tantra, the Manifest Realization of the Glorious Vajra Garland.
Then the Blessed Vajra One said:
"The two syllables called e baṃ:
e is explained as emptiness,
And likewise baṃ is compassion;
The bindu arises from the union of the two.
That is the arising of the supremely wondrous yoga.
The two, e and baṃ, are the Buddha
And, in brief, the seal of the Dharma.
Great king, the two seals
Were taught first in the tantras.
e is the characteristic of the bhaga."

It should be known as the support, the lotus;
The supported is said to be the vajra.
The form of the bindu of steadfast Vajradhara
That abides within it
Displays manifold emanations.
The abode that is the ground of all bliss
Is Vajrasattva, the great bliss.
e is the secret expanse of space;
In the bhaga, the source of phenomena, the lotus,
He sits upon the lion throne of yoga
And displays the supremely wondrous.
Vajrasattva, baṃ, the vajra,
Vajrabhairava, Īśvara,
Heruka, Kālacakra,
The Primordial Buddha, and so on, are its names.
e baṃ, the seal of the tathāgatas,
The nondual Saṃvara,
Emptiness and compassion indivisible:
That in which reality is made manifest in words.
Therein the seal of the tathāgatas,
The sign of nondual wisdom,
Is taught as the opening of all the tantras.
The condensed meaning of all the tantras,
Emptiness and compassion undivided,
That in which reality is made manifest in words,
Is the two syllables called e baṃ.
Where this itself is absent,
There is no suchness.
One who fully knows it,
Clearly, like refined gold,
Knows the tantra, that very well-spoken word,
And abides in the conduct of mantra.
Thus this is the supreme reality.
The meaning of e paṃ is ascertained:
The two syllables, the seal of the Dharma,
Are rightly explained as the opening words.

This concludes the fifty-eighth chapter, explaining the opening words called e baṃ and teaching their rationale, from the Great Yoga Tantra, the Manifest Realization of the Glorious Vajra Garland.

Then:
"Further, I shall explain; listen!
The extensive meaning of e baṃ and the rest:
Whoever rightly and fully knows it
Truly realizes the reality of phenomena.

e:
e is sublime wisdom,
Whose self is the instants such as freedom from desire.
Fully known within the three existences,
It is taught to be the root.

baṃ:
Beautiful like a light not yet raised up,
Endowed with the arising of attachment and the rest,
It is the consciousness of clear appearance;
Its name is skillful means.

ma:
Great awareness itself is the root;
It is what reverses ignorance.
Since this arises from ignorance,
Therefore appearance arises.

yā:
The first of the three consciousnesses of beings,
Its name is clear appearance.
It becomes great emptiness,
And that in turn passes into luminosity.

śru:
This, bearing the characteristics of the three appearances,
Which has attained the three consciousnesses,
Is the intrinsic nature and root
Of the entire realm of sentient beings.

taṃ:
It is the appearance of each object,
Which, taken up by the wind, does the grasping.

laṃ:
Consciousness joined with wind
Comes to abide in going everywhere.

e:
This is the great wind element,
The mount of the three consciousnesses.
By it they come to move
Into the natures in whatever way they do.

ka:
The solid earth element, water,
And likewise fire and wind,
Being taken up by consciousness,
Are born in the abodes of the three existences.

smi:
This arises as the five aggregates,
Whose characteristic is to be conditioned:
Form, feeling, perception, formations,
And consciousness itself, the fifth.

sa:
Equality, discernment,
Accomplishment of activity,
The mirror, and the expanse of phenomena:
These are the five wisdoms.

ma:
The sense base of the mind,
The eye, and likewise the ear,
The nose, the tongue, and the body:
This is the arising of the sense bases.

ye:
All the sentient beings of the three realms,
Relying on life breath and its exertion,
Recite the king of secret mantras
Without knowing it, free of meditation and of reading.

bha:
From this arise the natures of attachment,
Non-attachment, and the rest.
From that comes virtuous and nonvirtuous karma,
And from that, birth arises.

ga:
The aggregates from which beings arise
Have the nature of the five buddhas;
By the name "the fivefold manifest awakening"
They are called forth.

vān:
Wind, water, fire, and earth
Are the four, Locanā and the rest.
Know them as threefold, having the nature of wisdom;
They bestow the awakening of a buddha.

sa:
All are sealed with the four seals
As the body of the tathāgatas,
And there, as the nature of the eye and the rest,
Are Kṣitigarbha and the other heirs of the victors.

ha:
The mighty kings of wrath,
Who delight in play,
As the nature of its directions and intermediate directions,
Should be visualized in the shoulders and the limbs.

ta:
The various gods and goddesses
Arising from each of their families
Do not exist; and yet they
Are displayed for the sake of beings.

thā:
Hence the yogin who sees nonduality,
In order to see the three wisdoms,
Should settle upon the lesser, the middling,
And the supreme objects.

ga:
Each sense faculty goes
Toward its own object;
Whatever is the sphere of the faculties,
Each and every one is mere appearance.

ta:
The objects that the practitioner obtains
By way of those sense faculties
Should likewise be offered to bring satisfaction,
With nothing other than that prescribed.

kā:
The three bodies were rightly taught
By the Protector as distinct;
Through the yoga of the completion stage
They also become one.

yā:
That buddha body with its characteristics
Which is taught as conventional truth,
Through the yoga of the completion stage
Is purified into luminosity.

vāk:
This very object of the path of speech,
The lord whose nature is the wisdom body,
For the benefit of all sentient beings
Appears like a rainbow.

cit:
Mind, mental factors, ignorance,
Wisdom, skillful means, and near-attainment
Are also proclaimed as empty,
Very empty, and great emptiness.

ta:
When one knows the divisions of the natures and the appearances,
One should then fully engage in the conduct.
Having completely abandoned the body of karma,
One attains the vajra body itself.

hṛ:
Having set in his heart the conduct
For the benefit of worldly beings, the Tathāgata
Abides conventionally in an emanation body
And acts however he wishes.

da:
Like a supreme wish-fulfilling jewel,
He grants every desire.
Even the riches of the buddhas
He takes by force and enjoys.

ya:
Whatever the lord of yogins desires,
He does without obstruction.
Through the yoga that is not set in equipoise
He constantly rests in equipoise.

va:
Through the union of vajra and lotus,
Knowing the portions of the three wisdoms,
Whether his mind is touched or untouched,
He abides there ever in bliss.

jra:
Through the illusion-like concentration
He sports among all things,
And while performing the deeds of a buddha
Abides on the ground that has been established.

yo:
He himself becomes yoga, the yoga that surpasses yoga,
And great yoga.
Vajrin and the ḍākinī,
Whoever they may be, are that yogin himself.

ṣī:
Since he does even what is forbidden,
He is free of what is to be done and not to be done;
Like a lotus by the mud,
By his very nature, it is said, he is not stained.

bha:
Being endowed with the eight qualities,
He becomes the family of all sentient beings,
And throughout every world realm without exception
He moves in his own wisdom body.

ge:
Wherever the lord acts,
His home is space itself;
Through the concentration of great bliss
He ever sports therein.

ṣā:
Here, in distinguishing the appearances,
The worldly conventions of twilight,
Of day and of night, are used
In order to see the three wisdoms.

vi:
The manifold conventions
Imagined by worldly people
Arise from the three wisdoms
Through the division of the three paths.

ja:
Arising, abiding, and perishing,
And abiding in the intermediate state:
All that the world imagines
Is the magical display of the wind of mind.

ha:
Laughing and playing,
Being endowed with the nine moods of dance,
And conceiving mantras and mudrās:
This is the conduct of Vajrasattva.

ra:
Apart from the great self-blessing,
There is no other jewel here.
Like a gem purified by fire,
It is the form of natural luminosity.

The opening words "Thus" and the rest,
The extensive meaning of what was supremely spoken,
The root sūtra of the Guhyasamāja:
This condensed explanation of its reality
Should be known in its proper sequence.
The knowledge of the practitioner's reality,
With the characteristics of supreme wisdom,
Has nowhere been clearly taught.
By this sequence the yogin
Has sealed the Glorious Samāja.
Knowing it thus through this sequence
Brings benefit to all sentient beings.

This concludes the fifty-ninth chapter, explaining the opening words called baṃ ma yā, from the Great Yoga Tantra, the Manifest Realization of the Glorious Vajra Garland.

Then:
"Further, I shall explain; listen!
The supreme fire offering of the inner self:
The elements that move within,
That by which they are dissolved,
The seeds of conceptual imprints,
And the firewood of the five aggregates:
With the fire of the great wisdom of yoga
One should always perform the wondrous fire offering.
The mere sound abiding at the center of the navel;
The mere sound outside, the secret lotus.
There, by the fire
Stirred by one's own wind,
The two organs are truly joined,
And it blazes brilliantly with splendor.
When the secret lotus is filled with butter,
This is called the vessel.
haṃ is said to be the pouring ladle;
The vajra is called the filling ladle;
The bhaga is said to be the hearth.
The five senses are the substances to be burned,
And the aggregates become the kindling sticks;
The butter is explained as the mind of awakening.
From the new moon until the full moon,
For as long as one performs it, one makes the invocation.
The fire hearth is said to be the mere bindu;
The ladle never lets anything pass.
The buddhas, Moharati and the others,
Rūpavajrā and the others, the heirs of the victors,
And the ten kings of wrath
Should always be satisfied with the fire offering.
Whatever is the offering to Vajrasattva,
That too is called the oblation from the ladle.
With the mantra endowed with huṃ phaṭ,
Offer the oblations according to the rite.
When the one who dwells in the body is pleased by this,
The deity of unsurpassed commitment
Grants accomplishments
Just as one wishes in one's mind.
This fire offering is taught to be supreme;
It grants the fruit of all accomplishments."

This concludes the sixtieth chapter, on the fire offering of the inner self, from the Great Yoga Tantra, the Manifest Realization of the Glorious Vajra Garland.

Then the Blessed Vajra One said:
"Eating the five nectars
Is the supreme reality that brings about all accomplishments.
In accordance with the sequence of the families,
Vairocana, Akṣobhya,
Amoghasiddhi, Amitābha,
And Ratnaketu, these five,
Are the characteristics of the five nectars.

Blood is Ratneśvara,
The mind of awakening is Amitābha,
Likewise flesh is Amoghasiddhi,
And likewise Akṣobhya is the vajra water;
Excrement is explained as Vairocana.
These are the supreme five nectars.
In a delightful outlying place, secluded from people,
Free of hair and thorns,
The house to be built there
Is a house of two stories with doors.
One room should be made underground,
And the other above it.
The other is to be made in secret,
So that worldly people know of only one.
The practitioner knows all of reality,
Knows mantra, and is vast in intention,
Steadfast, compassionate, and learned,
And free of envy regarding objects.
Whatever the qualities of the principal practitioner,
Such indeed is the supreme companion in practice:
Free of both attachment and detachment,
Diligent and without greed,
Without anger and courageous,
Very learned and holding the view that things exist.
First the bodies of both
Should be purified with fruits and the like.
Then the mantrin should recite
The urging mantra of Sumbharāja a hundred thousand times:
oṃ sumbha ni sumbha hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ
oṃ gṛhṇa gṛhṇa hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ
oṃ gṛhṇā va ya gṛhṇā pa ya hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ
oṃ an ya ho bha ga vā na vidyā rā ja hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ
Then for one month the practitioner
Should recite the three syllables in silence.
Samantabhadra, lord of beings,
Is then seen in a dream in an instant,
And he gives this command:
"Rely on the supreme maiden,
And accomplish the incomparable benefit of beings,
Just as Vajradhara himself does."
A fortunate girl with wide eyes,
Adorned with beauty and youth,
Without greed and with a passionate mind,
Intoxicated with charm and with a proud bearing,
With full breasts, dusky blue, and delightful,
Free of conceptual thought and very learned:
The practitioner should desire her
For as long as it takes for the fluid to come.
Then, the mouth and the lotus, these two:
Taking what issues from both,
One should place it in that lotus vessel,
Under good signs, for seven days.
Elephant, horse, dog, cow, and human:
In the last month of spring, when both have appeared,
Having taken them from the place of birth,
Likewise, there, in due order,
One should place them in that same lotus vessel
For up to seven days.
For twenty-one days and nights
One should recite the mantra of three syllables.
In a secluded place free of bustle,
Having anointed it with fine sandalwood,
One should place it in a lotus vessel
Inscribed with the mantra jaḥ hūṃ baṃ hoḥ.
Goat's milk, sesame oil,
Butter, and white sugar
Are boiled together in equal portions,
And mixed with what was placed in the vessel.
One endowed with intelligence should make pills
The size of a juniper berry.
Having dried them in the shade,
One should eat them, gradually increasing the amount.
One should eat them together with one's vajra brothers and sisters
And the yoginīs.
For one month, and then beyond,
One should share them properly with one's friends.
On the eighth or the tenth day of the waning fortnight,
Or else beginning from the fourteenth,
The torma that is offered then
Is unsurpassed for every rite.
From that too accomplishments arise:
The great seal, manifest awakening,
The accomplishments of increase, pacification, and the rest,
Are obtained by it without effort.
One rightly learns the reality of the winds
And attains the state of a sky-goer.

This concludes the sixty-first chapter, on the rite of eating the five nectars, from the Great Yoga Tantra, the Manifest Realization of the Glorious Vajra Garland.

Then:
"Further, I shall explain; listen!
The supreme rite of the feast gathering,
Arising from the worship
By which the supreme accomplishment of the heirs of the victors comes:
Together with great yogins
One rightly abides in order to accomplish it.
Fully knowing the signs of the mudrās,
One enters into the commitments with them.
Their assembly is supremely excellent
And is called the feast gathering.
All abide in the commitments,
Have abandoned all conceptual thought,
Have faith in the guru, abide in the commitments,
And are adorned with generosity and the other virtues.
If the one called the means is free of obstructors,
He enters the feast gathering.
Otherwise faults will arise,
And there will be no accomplishment in the circle.
The wisdom partner too is like the means:
A good friend, highly skilled,
Who has reached the age of twenty-five.
Having entirely rejected the rest,
The means and the wisdom are each paired;
One for all brings no benefit.
First, in a pleasing and delightful place,
One should make a maṇḍala of flowers.
Its various offerings
One should offer after settling well in equipoise.
The principal should be worshiped with all things,
Just as Vajrasattva is.
After the feast offering has been made,
Through the maṇḍala in forward and reverse order,
With faith and with all things,
Having meditated with a gaze of equality,
Whatever contradicts the treatises, experience,
Or the world should not be brought in.
Further, as is taught in the treatises,
At midnight one should invite the guests.
One should rightly worship the master, the protector,
And request him to be seated upon the seat.
The seat, endowed with the great seal
Of Vajrasattva:
The vajra master himself, the wise one,
Sits at the center of the circle.
The other members of the maṇḍala,
And likewise the others who dwell in the circle,
Should be known to be like the members of the maṇḍala.
In the feast gathering, idle talk,
Disputing and likewise arguing a case,
Spitting out phlegm and laughing,
Stretching out the legs and so on,
And rising again and again:
These should never be done there at any time.
Through the many signs of the mudrās
One should know what to do and what to do next.
With mindful awareness, dance and song
Should be performed in the maṇḍala of the gathering.
For the principal, the supreme vessel
Is one of a single piece with excellent marks;
Lacking that, one of three pieces,
Worshiped with various flowers.
Whatever divine liquor and the like there is
Should be offered after settling well in equipoise.
For the assembly too, each has a separate vessel,
Completely filled with masses of flowers.
First the great torma is given,
Furnished, as before, with foods and the rest.
Whatever impairs the joys,
Such a thing should not be drunk.

Having bowed again and again,
And having bound the mudrā, one should drink.
Then all the foods
Are rightly purified with the purifying syllables and the rest.
With oṃ, āḥ, and hūṃ
They should be purified, awakened, and realized.
Saffron, sandalwood, "hero,"
"Snow," and musk:
These are the five purifying nectars.
One should fully partake of these foods.
Through the union of the gross one and the nameless one,
The heroes and the yoginīs, in due order,
Should taste it with their own tongues.
At all times, in the midst of the heroes,
One should set the hero upon the mouth
And make the unsurpassed offering.
The center of the hub of the lotus
Is said to be the seat of the heroes.
If a hero does not know
The characteristics of the meaning of "hero,"
That circle which grants all accomplishments
Bears no fruit for him.
Whatever little fruit of that kind there is,
One who rightly practices outer and inner as oneself
Through that comes to pure accomplishment,
And it grants the fruit of the feast gathering.
Mingling with the yoginīs
I shall fully explain; listen to its reality!
Some ḍākinīs of discipline
Come, drawn by a mind free of conceptual thought.
At that time the practitioner
Should not speak to them,
Nor answer them with mere words.
"Have you come in happiness?" should be asked with a mudrā.
Whatever little is to be done or said to them,
The master himself should do.
Or else all that is to be done
Should be rightly carried out by the action vajra,
Because one is oneself Vajrasattva,
And the action vajra is no other.
Yoginīs of various forms
Should be summoned again and again.
Among them there is no wavering conduct;
One acts like the master, the hero.
Whoever behaves with faulty conduct,
His body will fall.
Great joy rightly arises,
And one performs the conduct of the commanded dance.
One should also perform the vajra binding,
So that the deities are pleased.
Mother, sister, or daughter,
If one wishes to worship her,
Through the rite of wisdom and means
She should be shown, without conceptual thought.
The most supreme accomplishment of that
Will be attained; of this there is no doubt.
Having taken the leftover torma,
One should go to a great wilderness and cast it away,
Reciting the mantra
kṣmīṃ ucchiṣṭa kṣetāpā lāya svāhā.
If the yogin who performs it well in this way
Sees them again,
Then, having fully understood them thus,
These are the supreme signs for the yogin.
She whose face has a blazing radiance,
Round, smooth, and beautiful,
With long eyebrows: know that she
Is born of the family of Amitābha.
To her one shows the lotus mudrā;
The tortoise mudrā is her reply.
With full lips and long eyes,
Yellow in complexion, she captivates the mind;
On her forehead are three lines
Reaching up to the hairline.
She listens intently
To tales of those who die in battle.
Having seen one who listens like this,
One should show the spear mudrā.
Bending her left leg,
She clearly shows that she is dancing;
Having turned her left hand around,
She shows the mudrā in reply.
She delights in multicolored clothes,
And is born of the family of Ratnasambhava.
Black in complexion and black in clothing,
Her hair coiled and bound with silk,
With the shape of a bow on her forehead:
When one sees such a sign,
Her mind ever restless,
She is renowned as a mātṛkā.
One should show the bell mudrā;
She shows the mudrā with her left hand.
Know her to be born of the family of Akṣobhya.
The supreme one who comes and goes by night,
Short, big-bellied, and white in complexion,
With thick thighs and very beautiful,
Should be known as an earth-goer;
The conch mudrā is her reply.
She is born of the family of Vairocana.
With wide eyes and thick lashes,
All her limbs covered with fine hair,
Dusky blue, with red eyes,
Slender limbs and long arms:
She is renowned as a ḍākinī,
Born of the family of Amoghasiddhi.
When one clearly sees such a one,
There is the banner mudrā,
And the left-hand mudrā is her reply.
Having come to know them thus with effort,
The wise one, going forth at night,
Should have no fear of them at all,
For they are to be worshiped.

Then the Blessed Vajra One said:
"The characteristics, all summarized,
Of the Buddha, the sacred Dharma, the Saṅgha,
The guru, the teacher, the master,
The vajra holder, and the disciple, are these.
The stainless mind becomes the Buddha.
The Dharma is truly spoken in speech.
The body is called the Saṅgha,
The abode of the host of bodhisattvas.
The Buddha is explained as the syllable hūṃ.
Speech is declared to be the syllable āḥ.
What is called the Saṅgha is the syllable oṃ,
Whose nature radiates in all directions.
The Buddha becomes the avadhūtī.
The Dharma is declared to be the lalanā.
The Saṅgha is declared to be the rasanā.
This is the characteristic of the three.
The formless realm becomes the Buddha,
The form realm the Dharma,
And likewise the desire realm the Saṅgha.
Thus is the supreme characteristic of the three realms.
Apart from one's own life force, which has become the essence,
There is nothing else in the three realms.
The three realms of illusion and wind
Are seen as if dreaming a dream.
Suchness, the nature of the three realms,
Will swiftly be seen as one alone.
Having been blessed, suchness
And accomplishment will swiftly be attained.
The Buddha is the enjoyment body,
The sacred Dharma is the Dharma body,
And the Saṅgha becomes the emanation body.
That is the nature of the three bodies.
Appearance is the form of the Buddha.
The Dharma is the attainment of appearance.
The Saṅgha becomes the increase of appearance.
That is the characteristic of the three appearances.
One who is trained in the rites of pacifying and the rest,
Who knows the mantras and the great yogas,
And has learned to perform the twenty ritual procedures,
Is designated a guru.
One who is pure in moral discipline and practices giving,
Supremely endowed with patience and diligence,
Devoted to meditation day and night,
Endowed with wisdom and skilled in the arts,
With the qualities a guru has in general,
Is called a glorious master.
One who inwardly delights in the Secret Assembly,
While outwardly conducting himself as a śrāvaka,
And by such a sequence of instructions
Trains the disciples,
Is declared to be the Teacher.
One who does just as he has been told,
Who learns, and also causes others to learn,
The ritual procedures, the host of maṇḍalas,
The activities of the ultimate meaning,
And again the three appearances,
And the conduct of supreme yoga,
Is called a master.

One who has fully understood the ten principles,
Who knows the activities of the maṇḍala and the rest,
Who is able to care for disciples,
And who confers the secret empowerment,
Truly endowed with wisdom and compassion,
Fearless in the instructions of the tantras,
Truly endowed with depth and vastness,
A supreme second Vajrasattva,
Skilled in subduing and in benefiting,
Is explained to be a Vajradhara.
One who has faith and is devoted to the guru,
Who always abides in virtuous action,
Without duplicity and without deceit,
Who has utterly abandoned wicked thoughts,
Who has heard much of the scriptures,
Who has abandoned killing and harming,
Who, with the resolve to deliver sentient beings,
Is truly endowed with diligence in every way,
Truly endowed with these and other qualities,
And of great faith, is a good disciple.
That in which the inseparability of emptiness and compassion,
That very reality, is to be known with certainty;
The unsurpassed, supreme and excellent conduct;
The stages of mantra and yoga and the rest;
The complete purity of the five wisdoms;
The very nature of the five buddhas;
The fivefold manifest awakening;
The characteristics of the three appearances;
The meditation on the three bodies,
The Dharma body, the enjoyment body, and the emanation body;
The characteristics of the qualities of great bliss;
The divisions of joy and the others;
Likewise the sentiments, the erotic and the rest;
Yoga, subsequent yoga,
Intense yoga, and great yoga;
The first union, in brief;
Likewise the supreme king of maṇḍalas;
The supreme king of actions, the yoga;
Entering, abiding, and arising;
The yoga of the completion stage;
The two blisses, the two emergences of breath, and the rest:
The tantra in which these are explained at length
Is declared to be a great yoga tantra.
What qualities befit the tantra?
One is said to be the support and the vessel
Of the supreme qualities, learning and the rest,
And to be adorned with all the marks.
Just as the milk of a lioness
Should not be placed in an earthen vessel,
So too the great yoga tantra
Should not be given to those who are not vessels.
The disciple dies in that very instant,
And is ruined in this life and the next.
If one teaches the instructions to one who is not a vessel,
The master's accomplishment will decline.

This concludes the sixty-fifth chapter, the extensive words on the characteristics of the Buddha, the sacred Dharma, the Saṅgha, and the rest, from the Manifest Realization of the Glorious Vajra Garland, a Great Yoga Tantra.

Wearing shoes, a parasol, or a turban,

Throwing away lumps of snot, spittle and the like,

With awe, joining the palms,
Receiving his commands as a seal upon one's crown,
Having pledged to offer all one possesses,
With modesty and with lowered face,

If they wish for the supreme accomplishment,
Disciples in the guru's presence
Are said to conduct themselves in this and similar ways.
The pledges and vows explained earlier:
These are the conduct of the disciple.
His activities of pacifying and the rest
Are fit to be taught by the guru;
I shall explain them here in brief.
The characteristics of the common rites are these.
Pacifying and increasing, both of these,
Should be performed in the waxing fortnight of the moon.
The actions of subjugation and of fierce rites
Should be undertaken in the waning fortnight of the moon.
Facing north for pacifying,
And likewise facing east for increasing;
For fierce actions, toward the south;
For the action of subjugation, face west.
For killing itself, the direction of fire;
For causing enmity, toward Nairṛti;
For driving out from one's place, the direction of the wind;
For paralyzing, the direction of Īśāna.
The color of pacifying is white;
For increasing, yellow should be used;
For subjugating and summoning, red;
The color black is for killing.
With a mind of loving-kindness for pacifying,
With a mind of joy for increasing,
With a mind of passion for subjugation,
With a mind of wrath, killing.
Pacifying is mostly performed at dawn;
Likewise increasing is performed in the morning;
The actions of subjugation and of fierce rites
Are at midday and at midnight.
Thus it has been correctly explained as is fitting.
By the characteristics taught in the mantra tantras,
The eight accomplishments, pacifying and the rest,
The wise should accomplish according to the rite."

This concludes the sixty-sixth chapter, showing the complete purity of the disciple's conduct and the results of the actions of pacifying and the rest, from the Manifest Realization of the Glorious Vajra Garland, a Great Yoga Tantra.

For the purpose of expelling harmful beings.

I shall now explain:
namaḥ smanta kāya vākcitta vajrāṇāṃ oṃ aparājītiriji hūṃ phaṭ

dhṝḥ

Then, further, I shall explain
The summary of all accomplishments,
The supreme wisdom of the buddhas,
The characteristics of accomplishment and the rest.
Kṣitigarbha, Vajrapāṇi,
Mañjuśrī, Sarvanīvaraṇaviṣkambhin,
And Maitreya: these five,
Interwoven with the three syllables,
Bless the eyes, the ears, the mind, and the body,
And the wheels of the channels.
The three of the night are just like the day.
One thereby attains the five superknowledges.
Through the fourth empowerment,
The wise one will see reality itself.
Appearance becomes the portion of the night;
The day is the increase of appearance;
Twilight is the attainment of appearance.

Such are the characteristics of appearance and the rest.
That which is not night, not twilight, and not day
Abandons the winds of conceptual thought.
That is the instantaneous manifest awakening;
Its characteristic is declared to be momentary.
Whether arising at the end or arising at the beginning,
Or, though not arising in the middle, arising in the middle,
Or, though not an end, arising at the end,
That is declared to be the single instant of the universally empty.
That in which there is no going and no coming,
No exhaustion and no increase, neither entity nor non-entity,
Which has a wondrous form and yet is not wondrous,
Which neither comes to abide nor swiftly goes,
Which is free from all the conventions of existence and non-existence,
Which is not the form of merit and not the form of evil,
Not the self of merit or of evil, but supreme:
That is declared to be the single instant of the universally empty.
Manifest awakening in a single instant:
This characteristic is the supreme instant.
By holding the awakening mind at the tip of the vajra,
There is stability of the body.
Through that, by thoroughly applying
The reality of wind to the reality of the awakening mind,
And skillfully drawing it upward,
One will be free from aging, without a doubt.
Knowing hūṃ hoḥ in their order,
One interweaves them while holding upward.
The five nectars explained earlier:
This is declared to be the elixir.
Through this very thing one accomplishes;
Having turned iron into gold,
One attains the great accomplishment of the vajra seal
Through union.
The practice of the generation stage,
And likewise the completion stage too:
That in which these two become one
Is explained to be union.
Saṃsāra and nirvāṇa:
Through abandoning these two conceptions,
That in which things become one
Is what is called union.
The conventional and the ultimate,
Free from the two conceptions:
That in which they are truly mingled
Is explained to be union.
Through the thorough application of vajra recitation,
Having understood the characteristics of the winds,
One cuts off the winds of conceptual thought
And attains the focus on the mind.
Through the stage of self-blessing as well,
One attains the eight accomplishments.
Knowing the divisions of appearance and the rest,
One attains the stage of manifest awakening.
For one who abides in the stage of union,
The summary of all accomplishments
Will be accomplished in this very life
By the yogin, without a doubt.
The great accomplishment of the great seal,
Through the concentration of union,
Having entered nonconceptuality,
Is attained; of this there is no doubt.
The body becomes the celestial palace,
Truly relied upon by all the buddhas.
By the division of front and back,
And right and left, of its four corners,
And by the division of the doors above and below,
It is explained as having four doors.
By the shape of the tip of the nose, fourfold,
The four portals are to be described.
The nets are truly explained as the tendons;
What have become the sinews are the half nets.
The portions are the half moons,
And the vase is truly known as the belly.
The shins, thighs, upper arms, and forearms
Are truly explained as the eight pillars.
The eye consciousness is the mirror and the rest;
The consciousness of sound is the music;
The consciousness of the nose is the garland of flowers;
The jeweled parapet is the consciousness of the tongue;
The consciousness of the body is the cloth banners;
And the consciousness of the mind is the deer.
The eye is the emblem of the wheel;
The ears, all around, are the vajras;
The nose is explained as the victory banner;
The tongue is declared to be the bell;
The yak-tail whisks and the rest, completely pure,
Are explained to be the collection of the body.
The mind is declared to be the central lotus,
Truly relied upon by the winds of conceptual thought.
The five wisdoms, completely pure,
Are truly declared to be the five lines.
The five aggregates are the five purities;
The five pure colors are thus distinguished.
One should meditate on these within the celestial palace;
This should be known as the first union.
The five manifest awakenings,
The divisions of the families, and their own essence:
These are declared to abide externally;
This is the complete purity of the maṇḍala.
The nature of the four elements:
To summarize the aggregate of form,
The aggregates of form,
And the eye consciousness, which is Kṣitigarbha,
Are explained externally as Rūpavajrā.
Maitreya abides in the midst of the sinews.
Likewise the two wrathful kings
Are in the corner between east and south.
This is explained as the mirror-like wisdom,
The very complete purity of Vairocana.
Pleasure, suffering, fear, and weariness
Are held to be the characteristics of feeling.
Feeling is the water element,
Externally it is Śabdavajrā,
And the ear is explained as Vajrapāṇi.
The family is the very mingling of the two.
Gandhavajrā becomes the external;
The consciousness of the nose is Ākāśagarbha;
The two wrathful ones are to be recognized;
This is equality and Ratnasambhava.
Perception, of entities, non-entities and the rest,
Has the characteristic of recollecting what came before.
The aggregate of perception is the fire element,
Externally Gandhavajrā,
And the consciousness of the tongue is Lokanātha.
Likewise the two wrathful kings.
That is the discriminating wisdom,
Arisen from the family of Amitābha.
Through virtuous and nonvirtuous actions,
Formations have the characteristic of habitual tendencies.
The aggregate of formations is the wind element,
Externally Rasavajrā,
And the consciousness of the body is Sarvanīvaraṇaviṣkambhin.
Samantabhadra abides in the midst of the joints.
Likewise the two wrathful kings.
This is the wisdom of accomplishing activities,
Arisen from the family of Amoghasiddhi.
The nature of all things,
Whose characteristic is wisdom, is declared to be consciousness.
Consciousness is the space element,
And the mental consciousness is Mañjuvajra.
Likewise the two wrathful kings.
This is the utterly pure expanse of phenomena,
Arisen from the family of Akṣobhya.
The five manifest awakenings:
These abide within the celestial palace.
Through the divisions of the elements
I shall explain them in their proper order.
Head hair, bones, feces, spittle, and heart:
Earth is the nature of Vairocana.
Body hair, pus, the parts near the heart,
And nails are known as the water element;
They should be known as the support of the family
Whose nature is Ratnasambhava.
Teeth, skin, flesh, and heart
Are declared to be the fire element;
They should be known as the nature of Amitābha
And the division of his family.
Sinews, flesh, the ribs and sides, and heart:
Wind is Amoghasiddhi.

Dirt, sinews, bile, and heart
Are known as the space element,
Which is the nature of Akṣobhya;
And the heart is called the five consciousnesses.
In each of these, individually,
Know the emanation of five aspects.
The five aggregates of the Tathāgata,
The inner nature, are divided into substances:
Flesh, channels, sinews, lumps of flesh,
The brain and the brain membrane,
Bones, marrow, and the sheaths,
Kidneys, heart, and belly,
Lungs, spleen, and urine,
Feces, stomach, and intestines,
Fat, lymph, and pus,
Blood, bile, and phlegm,
Saliva and mucus, head hair and beard,
Nails, body hair, and skin,
Hands, feet, eyes, the limbs and the rest,
And what are called the hips and upper arms:
These should be known in their order.
Since the celestial palace is completely pure,
The division of the sacred sites within it
I shall explain in brief.
Pullīra is known as the head;
The crown of the head is Jālandhara;
At the right ear is Oḍiyāna;
The back of the crown is Arbuda;
Godāvarī is the left ear;
Rāmeśvara is between the eyebrows;
The two eyes are Devīkoṭa;
Mālava is the roots of the shoulders;
Kāmarūpa is the two armpits;
Oḍra is known as the two breasts;
Kosala is known as the navel;
Triśakuni is the root of the nose;
The lotus of the mouth is Kaliṅga;
The throat is declared to be Lampāka;
The heart should be known as Kāñcī;
Himālaya is known as the genital region;
The sexual organ is Gṛhadevatā;
Pretapurī is the anus;
The two thighs are Suvarṇadvīpa;
Saurāṣṭra is the two calves;
The insteps are the land of Sindhu;
The toes are Nagara;
The two big toes are known as Maru;
Kulutā is the soles of the feet.
Thus the purity of the sacred sites,
And the very purity of the ten grounds,
Should be known as endowed with reality.
Through dissolution, lordship over enjoyment,
Entry, and the rest, in their particulars,
One gradually becomes Vajrasattva himself.
Then, further, I shall fully explain
The supreme characteristics of the winds.
The life wind abides in the heart;
It is born from the family of Akṣobhya's division.
The downward-clearing wind, which abides in the genital region,
Is born of Ratnasambhava's division.
The upward-moving wind abides in the throat;
It is the nature of Amitābha.
The fire-accompanying wind abides in the navel;
It is the nature of Amoghasiddhi.
The pervading wind abides in all the limbs;
It is blessed by Vairocana.
From the stream through the doors of the senses,
This stream of life and exertion,
Which moves at all times,
Is explained to be what is called life.
Breath, wind, feces,
And likewise semen and the rest:
This leading downward is the downward-clearing wind;
The yogin should always know it.
Laughing, eating, belching,
Drinking, and sucking:
That which always abides evenly
Is declared to be what is called the evenly abiding.
Going upward and gathering in,
Eating, tasting, and the rest,
Knowing, and coming together:
These should be known as the actions of the upward-moving wind.
Pervading and holding,
Likewise going and returning, and the rest:
Because it pervades all the joints,
It is explained to be called the pervading.
Having explained the characteristics of the winds,
Next their dangers are declared.
As before, releasing, holding, and the rest
Harm one another;
One harms another.
By their nature of gaining strength,
One comes to dissolve into another.
Just as the moon is seized by Rāhu,
The winds gradually decline,
And, as before, higher and higher,
Dissolve into their own nature;
Then releasing and holding proceed again.
Just as what is burned by fire
Comes to dissolve into non-entity and causelessness,
So again the life wind arises.
The karmic winds are manifold;
Together with consciousness
They again abide in the three worlds.
From that comes action, and from that, birth;
From that, attachment and the rest, and habitual tendencies;
From that, again, death and birth:
Thus it is like turning a wheel.
As before, through vajra recitation and the rest,
It will gradually be separated.
Then all the great beings,
With the eye-salve stick of reality,
Removed the blindness of all delusion,
And saw true reality itself.
Then, prostrating again and again,
With tearful eyes, to the Blessed One,
They circumambulated him three times
And truly praised Vajradhara:
"How wonderful! All-pervading one, Lord of all,
You have attained equality with space.
How wonderful! You teach the three pledges, reality itself,
Supreme and marvelous.
Fully liberated from the three realms,
Very essence of the expanse of phenomena,
Unstained by desires,
Objectless one, homage to you!
Not relying on the aggregates,
The elements, and the sense sources,
Liberated from existence and from nirvāṇa,
Objectless one, we bow to you and praise you!
Thus, like space, without location,
Without elaboration and without stain,
Possessing a mind equal to space,
Objectless one, we bow to you and praise you!"
Then the body of the Blessed One
Became Trailokyavijaya, Victor over the Three Worlds.
He shook these three worlds,
And, having vanquished the māras,
Was victorious over the gods of the three worlds.
Then the ten wrathful ones, all around,
Will be given this command
By the chief, Trailokyavijaya:
"In whatever region of the earth
This great yoga pledge tantra,
Declared in the Vajra Garland,
Is practiced at all times,
There, continually, by their own power,
The ten wrathful ones shall always,
With ritual daggers against all obstructors,
Protect that land and its surroundings;
And with each one's own garland mantra,
Torma should be offered to them."
In order to care for sentient beings,
Displaying manifold emanations,
Vajrasattva, at all times,
Will abide by day and by night.

This concludes the sixty-eighth chapter, the summary of all accomplishments, from the Manifest Realization of the Glorious Vajra Garland, a Great Yoga Tantra.

This completes the Secret Analysis of the Essence of All Tantras, from the Manifest Realization of the Glorious Vajra Garland, a Great Yoga Tantra.

It was translated, edited, and finalized at the temple of Tholing, the Heart Commitment, Essence of the Earth, Glorious Incomparable and Spontaneously Accomplished, by the Indian preceptor Sujanaśrījñāna and the great translator-editor, the divine ruler of Tibet, the lama and monk Zhiwa Ö.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

Several bodhisattva names could not be identified with confidence in Sanskrit and are given in English: Sky Goer, Sky Expanse, Sky Moon, Sky Crown, Wholly Liberated by the Boundaries, Vajra Crown, Nonconceptual Crown, Nonconceptual Sound, Nonconceptual Dharma and Nonconceptual Samaya. The "vajra path" is the tantric term for the channel through which the light (and in yogic practice the vital essence) travels.  In the passage on the consort, the subject of "they themselves cast off forms free of passion" is not named; it is taken to be the assembled bodhisattvas, each embracing his own consort, which fits "each" and the following "seeing themselves once again in such forms." The moods of grace and the rest are the aesthetic sentiments (rasas), beginning with the erotic.  In the closing verses Vajrapāṇi, the questioner, is addressed as Vajrasattva, as is common in this literature; the Lord's earlier epithet "Vajrasattva, guru of beings" is likewise addressed to the Blessed One. "Full Realization" renders mngon par rtogs pa (abhisambodhi) as it stands in the chapter colophon and verse, though the opening title has mngon par brjod pa (abhidhāna). Several terse question lines (for instance "the three tips of the nose," "the lord of enjoyment") are rendered literally; their technical sense is given in the answers later in the tantra.  In the verses on the consort, ltung ba is taken in its tantric sense of "that which has fallen," the descended fluid of union, rather than "transgression." The pairing sbom po tha ltag is understood as thumb and ring finger, the fingers used for tasting the substance in parallel tantric passages; this identification is not fully certain. The lines "having brought the mantra disciple to understanding ... the meaning as it is serves accomplishment" are elliptical in the Tibetan and are rendered closely. "The all-empty" renders thams cad stong pa (sarvaśūnya), a technical term of the completion stage.  Most of the hundred and eight wind names are Tibetan renderings of Sanskrit names that cannot be securely identified; they are given in literal English, with a few kept as transcribed (Jaśa, Cuta, Kamboja, Raga, Udumbara). Some are especially uncertain: sbra ma ("Tent"), sdig pa ("Scorpion", possibly "Sin"), g.yon can sten ("Left-Leaning"), gun sel ("Winter-Dispeller", reading dgun sel), sna 'gyur gnas ("Dweller in the Turning Nose"), and khrung khrung ("the crane") in the list of thoughts. The listed items are not arranged here into an exact count of one hundred and eight; the Tibetan list is translated as it stands.  In the line "Free of error and confusion," the Tibetan reads 'brel ("connected with"), which contradicts the surrounding list of what emptiness is free of; it is taken as a slip for bral ("free of"). "Son of Manu and child of Manu" renders shed las skyes and shed bu (Skt. manuja, mānava), standard terms in lists of views of a self.  The names of the five channels (Vigor, Expansion, Intoxication, Drink, Friend; and Moon Portion, Nose, Service, Dewlap, Liver) are given literally, as their Sanskrit forms cannot be securely identified. The lines on the three knots ("Because by these the supreme one is overcome") are terse; the subject is taken to be the wisdom wind in the heart. The piece ends mid-sentence; the simile continues in the next piece.  The coded instructions for "extracting" the mantra describe two syllables: ha with the sixth vowel ū plus the final nasal (hūṃ), and ha with the ninth vowel o plus the visarga (hoḥ), the latter named explicitly in chapter seven. Vajrapāṇi's request ("Blessed One, the king...") is elliptical in the Tibetan; it is rendered as an exclamatory appeal rather than supplied with an added verb. "Sets the nose in line with the tongue" renders the literal "places the nose at the measure of the tongue"; the intended posture point is uncertain. In chapter seven, "father and mother of these creators" and "without even peace" render obscure lines literally. The chapter colophons call the work the Manifest Awakening (mngon par rtogs pa), whereas the opening title reads Abhidhāna (mngon par brjod pa); translated as written.  Viramānanda (joy of cessation) renders both bral dga' and dga' bral in chapter eight. In chapter nine, the four lines on entering the samaya ("Entering the midst of the samaya when aroused...") are obscure and rendered literally; skyed na may stand for skyes na. Also in chapter nine, "the painting for meditation" takes bsam gtan ras ris as a single item. In chapter ten, the line ending ye shes chen po'i me yis min makes little sense with min ("is not"); it is read as smin ("cooked, ripened"). The two lines beginning "From the skull at the crown of the head" are elliptical and rendered literally.  The figures for the breath are as the text gives them: twenty-one thousand six hundred breaths in a day and night, and two thousand seven hundred for each of eight watches. The phrase rendered "the stage of alternating the locations" reads sbol pa'i sa, which is obscure; it is taken as a slip for spel ba ("alternating"). In chapter eleven the Tibetan has Vajrasattva questioning the Teacher, and the reply is attributed to the Vajrin; this is rendered as written.  The opening lines of this piece continue a sentence begun in the previous piece and are elliptical; they are rendered closely. The four "protectors" (Lotus, Karma, Jewel, Vajra) are the lords of the buddha families, linked here with the four elemental maṇḍalas; Mahendra is the standard name of the earth maṇḍala. The lines on "holding in the vajrī" and being "filled by the parts of the five spaces" give a gloss on the two halves of the name Vajrasattva.  The lines on the wisdom of the śrāvakas and the "rhinoceros" (the solitary buddha) are elliptical and rendered literally. In "nor by stopping the winds in meditative concentration," bgegs pa (obstructor) is read as 'gegs pa ("to stop, block"). The six long lines near the opening are in a longer meter and are kept as single lines.  "Through the distinctions of the snake and the rest" renders the Tibetan literally; the reference is unclear and may concern the classification of breath or mantra practice. The three seed syllables are given by the letter-extraction code: the first letter with the fifth vowel and the end of the last class yields oṃ (body); the first letter doubled with the sibilant part yields āḥ (speech); the ūṣma letter with the sixth vowel and the final nasal yields hūṃ (mind). The colophon reads "Clear Realization" (mngon par rtogs pa) where the title elsewhere reads "Discourse" (mngon par brjod pa); translated as written. "The glory of the clouds" may be the name of a source text, but this could not be identified with confidence. The time units in the final section (nāḍikā, daṇḍa, caṣa, tatkṣaṇa and the rest) are given as the Tibetan states their relations, which are not wholly consistent with standard Indian reckoning.  The opening lines ("abides in bliss in the three", "the dreadful collapse") are rendered literally; their reference is unclear. "The third letter of the second class" yields the syllable ja when joined with its vowel. "Yoṣirak" transcribes an obscure term in the Tibetan (yo ṣi rag), perhaps from Sanskrit yoṣit, "woman", plus a further element, possibly rakta, "blood"; its meaning is uncertain. "Nyiga" (nyi dga') is an unidentified ingredient and is given in phonetics. "Leprosy breaking out in marks" could also be read as leprosy breaking out on the private parts. As in the previous chapter, the colophon reads "Clear Realization" (mngon par rtogs pa).  The names of the individual channels are Tibetan renderings of Sanskrit names that could not be identified with confidence; they are translated by meaning, and Kuśa, Śabi and Kisring are given as they appear. The word rendered "pride" in many channel names (rgyags pa) can also mean intoxication or nourishment. The Tibetan gives the total as a hundred and thirty-one, although the figures listed for the six wheels add up to a hundred and twenty-nine, and the list for the wheel of enjoyment yields fifteen names where sixteen are stated; both are translated as they stand. "The collection of a hundred thousand" ('bum sde) presumably refers to an extensive root tantra.  The syllables listed in the nineteenth chapter are Sanskrit terms for the acts of love-play (embracing, kissing, sucking, grasping the breasts, biting and so on), given in transliteration as the Tibetan spells them; only the first two are glossed. na li renders Sanskrit nāḍī, channel, and śu kra śukra, semen. In that chapter the third and fourth moments are called "without aspect" and "free of aspect", whereas the list just before names them crushing and free of characteristics; translated as written. In the last line of the passage the Tibetan reads "the moment of conceptuality" (rnam rtog); since the preceding lines say conceptuality is destroyed and the scheme requires the moment of crushing (rnam 'joms), the latter reading has been translated.  la la nā, ra sa nā and a ba dhū tī are the names of the left, right and central channels (lalanā, rasanā, avadhūtī). In the twenty-second chapter the Tibetan writes āḥ where the analysis of oṃ into a, u and ma would lead one to expect the syllable a; it is translated as written. "Not through cessation" may refer to stopping the breath. The correlation of the syllables with the wheels and realms is given as the Tibetan states it.  "The crane of the channel through which taste descends" renders ཁྲུང་ཁྲུང ("crane") as written; its sense in this yogic context is obscure, and the line's connection to "the sour" is uncertain. In "trembles, sweats," the text reads རྡུལ ("dust"); the evident reading in a list of bodily signs with trembling and horripilation is རྔུལ ("sweat"). In "are severed and depart," the text reads བཅས ("with"); it is taken as བཅད ("severed"), matching the following lines on cutting the winds. The colophon calls the text "Manifest Realization" (mngon par rtogs pa) where the title has "Discourse" (mngon par brjod pa); translated as written.  In the list of body sites for the ten wrathful deities, the first item (rje ngar) is obscure and is rendered "the shins," and thal gong is taken as "the kneecaps"; both are uncertain. "Melting, tears" renders ཞུ་བ་ཅི་མཆི, read as including mchi ma (tears); uncertain. "The ten knowings" renders ཤེས་པ་བཅུ as written, referring to the ten wrathful deities listed next. The four classes of mantra (snake, compiled, endless, headless) are translated literally, as their Sanskrit technical names are not certain.  "The drop is suppressed by delusion" renders ཐིག་ལེ་གཏི་མུག་གིས་གནོན་ཡིས as written; the grammar of this line is unclear. "Dalwar gyu" (moving slowly) is the fourth mode of breath; the text then calls the water element "moving slowly and without movement," translated as written.  In "for those long under the sway of great passion, who are to be protected in the three existences," the verb skyob ("protect") is translated as written, though the sense may be "wandering in" the three existences. "The fine eye" renders མིག་བཟངས literally within the list of sense bases.  Most of the goddesses' names in this chapter are Tibetan translations whose Sanskrit forms cannot be identified with confidence; they are therefore translated by meaning. Where one Tibetan line might hold one name or two (for example སྙིང་སྟོབས་ཡང་དག་སངས་རྒྱས་མ), the division is a judgment call. The line རལ་ག་རྣམས་ནི་ཡང་དག་བཞུགས is obscure (ral ga is not a clear word) and is rendered loosely as "these truly abide." "The Constant One" renders རྟག་བམ་མ, whose second element is unclear. "Narajana" transcribes ན་ར་ཛ་ན as written. The last lines on the guru, "should be pleased with effort, through the siddhis," follow the Tibetan grammar as written; the sense may be that the master should be pleased so that one gains the siddhis.  The twelve light goddesses are given with literal English renderings of their Tibetan names, since their Sanskrit forms cannot be identified with confidence. "Great Ocean Tantra" renders rgya mtsho chen po'i rgyud literally; it may refer to a specific yoginī tantra (possibly the Ḍākārṇava), but this is uncertain. Saṃvara (sdom pa), given in the house rendering "restraint," here probably also carries the sense of binding or union. The closing line "skyes bu mgor ldan pas" (literally "by the person endowed with a head") is obscure; it is taken here to refer to the root or source at the crown, matching the tree-root simile.  The ten "transitions" (pho ba) are the stages, evidently the months, of gestation, here correlated with the ten grounds. The shapes of the first stages (fish, tortoise, boar, lion) echo the sequence of Viṣṇu's descents; the text is translated as it stands. The seventh and eighth transitions both mention the openings of the nose; the seventh may originally have read "ear openings" (rna bug for sna bug), but the text is translated as written. The "wind of release and holding" (btang bzung) and the "wind intoxicated with vigor" are rendered literally; their identification is uncertain.  The opening word lit. "blind" (long ba) makes no sense here; it is read as lon pa, "having elapsed," which the following lines repeatedly use. "Ḍākinī Compendium Tantra" renders mkha' 'gro sdud pa'i rgyud literally; it may designate a specific scripture, but this is uncertain. The comparison "phye leb" is taken as phye ma leb, a moth drawn to a flame.  "One time" (dus gcig) echoes the opening formula of the scriptures, "at one time," here given an inner yogic meaning. The line rendered "Great bliss, having relinquished any state" (bde ba chen po go 'phang spangs) is terse and its construal is uncertain.  The line rendered "The person in the aspect of the three worlds" ('jig rten gsum gyi rnam pa'i mi) is obscure; mi may instead be a negation, giving "not in the aspect of the three worlds." The fourth wisdom is written "so sor rtog dad bya nan tan," where dad is read as dang, "and." The qualities "of lordship and the rest" refer to the traditional sixfold sense of bhaga (lordship, form, glory, fame, wisdom, and effort).  The term rendered "ḍombī" is literally "the woman with moles or marks"; it is taken as the Tibetan rendering of ḍombī, since the list and its family assignments (ḍombī to the Vajra family) parallel the Hevajra's six women. "Glorious Samāja" most likely refers to the Guhyasamāja. In the kinship correlations, the mind is said to become the mother and is then linked with "the sisters"; the text gives no separate item for the sister in this sequence, and it is translated as it stands. The names of the five aggregate deities (Victor of Victors, Jewel Holder, One Who Has Crossed Beyond Saṃsāra, Wisdom Holder) are rendered by meaning, since their Sanskrit forms cannot be identified with certainty.  The eight states listed in chapter forty-seven correspond to the eight sāttvika states of Indian aesthetic and tantric literature. The second item is written "dust"; it is read as "sweat," a similar-looking word, which the following explanation of the channels confirms. The channel name Nasara is a transliteration whose meaning is uncertain; the other channel names (Great Tortoise, Great Head, Great Humming, Great Joy, Great Intoxication) are rendered by meaning. In chapter forty-eight the Sanskrit "ahaṃ" ("I") is analyzed into its two syllables. Chapter forty-nine sets out the standard list of emptinesses; the line here rendered "are subsequently held to be emptiness" appears to describe inner emptiness.  Chapter forty-nine continues the classical list of emptinesses. The word rendered "bodily form" is written with an unusual syllable and is read as the similar-looking word for "body" (form body or image). In chapter fifty, "a million times" renders "ten hundred-thousands." In chapter fifty-three, the line rendered "merely the size of a chickpea" reads the transliterated word canaka as Sanskrit caṇaka, "chickpea"; this is somewhat uncertain. "The lord of the night" is the moon. "The cessation of joy" refers to one of the four joys of the completion stage.  The "three syllables" and the "hundred syllables" refer to the three-syllable mantra and the hundred-syllable mantra. The line rendered "adorned with a garland of ka letters" is literally "adorned with the garland of ka"; its exact sense is uncertain. A few lines of the maṇḍala instructions are terse, notably the description of the master's form while purifying the ground and the line "seven seats; for the others, two suns," and are translated as they stand.  "Jasmine" renders a flower name in the list of vase substances and is somewhat uncertain. Locanā, Māmakī, Pāṇḍaravāsinī and Tārā are the four goddesses placed in the vases; Pāṇḍaravāsinī is given in the Tibetan as "the White-Clad One."  The deity invited as "Mañjuvajra" is rendered from the Tibetan "gentle vajra," taken as the name of the principal deity of the Mañjuvajra Guhyasamāja maṇḍala. The lines on the harm-doers "rendered blameless" are terse and their construction is uncertain. "Many tens of millions" renders the number word koṭi as written; it may be intended loosely as "great quantities."  The object fashioned at twelve fingers' length and cast by the disciple is called a peg (kīla) in the Tibetan; in parallel rites this divination is performed with a tooth-stick, and the same object is evidently meant. "Great Joy" in the disciple's supplication renders an epithet of the master and may equally be read as "great bliss."  The opening line ("since recollecting the secret would become a fault") is terse, and its connection to the rite of descent that follows is uncertain. "From the depths of the water" renders the Tibetan literally; it seems to mean following on from the water empowerment, but this is not certain. The "fire of a preta" likely means fire taken from a cremation ground.  "Great Lord (Maheśvara)" is here an epithet addressed to the Blessed One as the teacher of the tantra, not a reference to the Hindu god. "The Liberator from Saṃsāra" translates a name in the list of tathāgatas whose Sanskrit form I cannot confirm. "Corpse ground" renders a phrase that could also be read as "the ground of taste"; the charnel setting seems most likely.  "In the five continua" renders a phrase that could also be read "in the five tantras"; the sense in this context of the sense objects seems to be the five streams of experience. "Vajra, sun, and lotus arise" is rendered literally; the line is terse. "Cessation" renders the Tibetan for "separation," evidently the joy of cessation (viramānanda) among the four joys.  The single syllables heading each verse after the chapter colophon spell out, one by one, the opening words of a tantra (evaṃ mayā śrutam ekasmin samaye bhagavān tathāga...), each explained in turn; they are romanized as the Tibetan writes them. The glossary rendering Saṃvara is kept for བདེ་མཆོག in "the nondual Saṃvara", though "supreme bliss" is also a possible reading here. The line འོད་མ་བསྒྲེང་བ་ལྟར་མཛེས is obscure (འོད་མ may also mean "bamboo"); "like a light not yet raised up" is tentative. In this passage དབང་པོ means the sense faculties and ཆགས་པ means attachment, not the names Indra and Rāgarati.  The syllables heading each verse continue the word-by-word commentary on the opening words of the Guhyasamāja, as the closing verses of the chapter state. སྦུལ་སྐུ is read as སྤྲུལ་སྐུ, "emanation body". In the fire-offering chapter, དབང་པོ refers to the sense faculties and to the two sexual organs, not to Indra.  "The supreme maiden" renders ཀ་ནྱ (Skt. kanyā). The line དཔྱིད་ཟླ་ཐ་ཆུངས་གཉིས་ཀ་ཤར is obscure; "when both have appeared" is a literal rendering, and what "both" refers to is unclear. The single- and three-piece vessels are presumably skull cups.  "Hero" and "snow" in the list of the five purifying nectars are rendered literally; "snow" most likely stands for camphor, and "hero" is probably a code word. "The gross one and the nameless one" is likewise a literal rendering of what seem to be code terms. In "the action vajra is no other," གཞན་དགའ is read as གཞན་དག. Near the end, ཤིས་ནས is read as ཤེས་ནས, "having come to know."  Chapter sixty-three is a list of code words, written in Tibetan letters as pseudo-Sanskrit, each with its meaning; they are given as the Tibetan spells them. Where the boundary between a code word and the Tibetan gloss is unclear, as in the lines glossed "fortunate," "what exists," "far from the mother," "homeland," and "having no guide," the division and the meaning are tentative. ཅི་འདྲ་བ ("What is it like?") is Tibetan, not a code word, and the sense of that line is uncertain. In the wind-element line, མི་བསྐྱོད ("unmoving") is read as བསྐྱོད ("movement"), which fits the wind element; it is not the name Akṣobhya here.  The ten wrathful deities placed on the body follow the familiar Guhyasamāja set; འདོད་པའི་རྒྱལ is Ṭakkirāja and འཁོར་ལོས་སྒྱུར is Uṣṇīṣacakravartin, given here as "Cakravartin" as the Tibetan has it.
