The Noble Dhāraṇī Called the Mother of All Phenomena (Āryā Sarvadharmamātṛkā Nāma Dhāraṇī)

Toh 573 in the Kangyur is a dhāraṇī text known in Sanskrit as the Āryā Sarvadharmamātṛkā Nāma Dhāraṇī and in Tibetan as the Pakma Chö Tamché Kyi Yum Shyé Jawé Zung, meaning in English the Noble Dhāraṇī Called the Mother of All Phenomena. As a dhāraṇī scripture, it belongs to the broader category of tantra-adjacent ritual texts and takes the form of a discourse delivered by the Buddha to his attendant Ānanda at Śrāvastī, in which the Buddha transmits a sacred spell said to pacify all illness and to have been proclaimed by all the Tathāgatas. The text includes the dhāraṇī mantra itself along with testimony from the gods of the Pure Abodes, situating it within a celestial and devotional frame characteristic of such works. It appears in the Kangyur as a self-contained dhāraṇī sūtra and is immediately preceded in the same manuscript by a distinct text called the Queen of Knowledge-Spells Known as She of the Finger-bones, indicating it was transmitted as part of a collection of related dhāraṇī works.

Thus is complete the queen of knowledge-spells called "She of the Finger-bones."

In the language of India: Āryā sarvadharmamātṛkā nāma dhāraṇī.
In the language of Tibet: The Noble Dhāraṇī Called the Mother of All Phenomena.

Homage to all buddhas and bodhisattvas.

Thus did I hear. At one time, the Blessed One was dwelling at Śrāvastī. Then the Blessed One spoke to the venerable Ānanda: "Ānanda, you should receive what I shall now explain — the dhāraṇī of the mudrā called 'the Mother of All Phenomena,' which pacifies all illness, and which has been proclaimed by all the Tathāgatas.

Tadyathā: Avarte avarte, vivarte vivarte, bhagama śuddhe, prabhāsvare tāraṇe, ukkhale, mugkhale, sarvadharma pāśivatani svāhā."

Then the gods of the Pure Abodes addressed the Blessed One, saying: "Blessed One, let us recite the essence of this dhāraṇī.

Tadyathā: Acche, anale, elule, eluphes, mūrta anugate sarvadharmādhipataye, prāpte jyoṣṇakara, adhigata-jñāne viśuddhe pati svāhā."

When the Blessed One had thus declared this teaching, the venerable Ānanda and the entire retinue rejoiced and offered heartfelt praise at what the Blessed One had proclaimed.
