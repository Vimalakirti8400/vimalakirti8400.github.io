The Torma Rite for All Bhūtas (Sarvabhūtabalividhi)

The Torma Rite for All Bhūtas, known in Sanskrit as Sarvabhūtabalividhi and in Tibetan as Jungpo Tamché kyi Tormé Choga, is a brief ritual manual for offering torma, ritual cakes, to the whole host of spirits so that beings may be protected from harmful forces and obstructing vināyakas. Written from the standpoint of the Hevajra practitioner, it guides the yogin to visualize three stacked eight-petaled lotuses bearing the eight great gods, the eight direction guardians and the eight great nāga kings, into whom all the beings of the three realms are gathered, and then to invite, empower and honor them before blessing and offering the torma with its mantras and dismissing the guests with the hundred-syllable mantra of Vajrasattva. The text closes by describing the benefits of the offering, from peace and freedom from disturbance to success in the various ritual activities, and its colophon attributes it to the master Kṛṣṇapāda, recording its translation by the Śākya monk Nyima Gyaltsen Palsangpo. It is Toh 1300 in the Tengyur, where it is found among the tantra commentaries as a ritual text associated with the Hevajra cycle of Highest Yoga Tantra.

In the language of India: sarba bhū ta ba liṃ bi dhi nā ma. In the language of Tibet: The Torma Rite for All Bhūtas.

Homage to glorious Hevajra.

Bowing to Vajrasattva,
Who abides with certainty in e vaṃ,
I set forth the torma, to protect beings
From harmers, vināyakas and the like.

The yogin endowed with Hevajra
Should meditate as follows.
Within the source of phenomena,
On three stacked lotuses of eight petals,
White, black and red, are oṃ hūṃ āḥ;
From these, in order, the eight great gods,
The eight direction guardians and the eight great nāgas
Are to be generated, like the petals of the lotus.
In front generate Maheśvara (Śiva);
On the right imagine the lord of the gods;
Behind generate Brahmā,
And on the left side, Viṣṇu.
In the southeast generate Kāmeśvara,
In the southwest Gaṇapati,
Bhṛṅgiriṭi in the northwest,
And in the northeast Kārttika.
On the front petal imagine Śakra (Indra);
On the right Antaka should be placed;
Varuṇa is on the rear petal;
Likewise on the left place Kubera.
In the southeast corner, the great Agni;
In the southwest, the rākṣasa;
In the northwest corner, Vāyu;
In the northeast corner, the lord of the bhūtas.
Karkoṭaka, Śaṅkhapāla,
Ananta and Vāsuki likewise,
Takṣaka and Kulika,
Padma and Mahāpadma.
All have one face and two arms,
And hold vajra and bell in their hands,
Fully embraced by consorts of their own light,
And sit upon their seats with the right leg extended.

From the seed syllable at each one's heart
Light rays stream out like hooks,
And into the eight great gods are gathered the gods
Who dwell in the form realm and the formless.
The gods and humans of the desire realm
Are fully gathered into the eight direction guardians.
The three lower destinies and the asuras
Are to be gathered into the eight great nāgas.
To invite the deities of the maṇḍala
Who dwell in their natural abode,
Gaurī draws them in,
Caurī binds them with the noose,
Vetālī fastens them with the chain,
And Ghasmarī delights them with the bell.
From the yogin's heart
Light rays stream out like hooks
And draw in the Victors of the ten directions together with their heirs.
Make outer, inner and secret offerings to them,
And when they have conferred empowerment,
Each is adorned on the head by its own lord:
The eight great gods by Śāśvata,
The eight direction guardians by Vajra,
And the eight great nāgas by Narteśvara.

For them prepare the torma according to the rite.
Above the maṇḍalas of wind and fire,
Imagine a lotus vessel arisen from āḥ.
In it are bhrūṃ āṃ jṝṃ khaṃ hūṃ,
And from go ku da ha na
The five buddhas and the five beings;
Imagine them between a moon and a sun.
At the center of the sun is a vajra arisen from hūṃ;
Through its blazing and radiating,
The deathless nectar is fully accomplished.
Having meditated on this inner nature,
Bless it with oṃ āḥ hūṃ and offer it.

Then the mantra for giving the torma:
oṃ inda ja ma ja la jakṣa bhū ta bahni ba u rakṣa canda sru jja mā ma pappa ta la pā tā li āḥ gha srā hā
These are the guests of the torma.
i daṃ ba liṃ bhu ñja ji gha phulla dhū ppa māṃ sa baṃ gha
That is the request to partake.
amba sarba kājja sā dha khanti khu ṇṇi pheṭ gaṅ
That is the request for the purpose.
oṃ a kā ro mu khaṃ sarba dha rmā ṇāṃ ādya nu tpanna tā ta oṃ āḥ hūṃ phaṭ svā hā
That is the mantra of blessing.

Then, having pleased them, request them to depart. One should recite the hundred-syllable mantra:
oṃ bajra satva sa ma ya ma nu pā la ya bajra sa tra tve no pa tiṣṭha dṛ ḍho me bha ba su to ṣya me bha ba a nu rakto me bha ba su poṣya me bha ba sarba siddhi mme pra yaccha sarba karmasu ccha me cittaṃ śre yaḥ ku ru hūṃ ha ha ha ha hoḥ bha ga bān sarba ta thā ga ta bajra mā me muñca bajrī bha ba ma hā sa ma ya satva āḥ

If one gives this torma and makes offerings fully, all the bhūtas will act kindly toward the yogin. Then he will have happiness free of turbulence, the deities will be pleased, and his circle will be virtuous. If torma is given to that host of bhūtas, they will bring about magnetizing, fierce rites, the destruction of hostile forces, expulsion, summoning, pacification, happiness and enrichment.

The Torma Rite for All Bhūtas, composed by the master Kṛṣṇapāda, is complete.

It was well translated and finalized by the translator, the Śākya monk Nyima Gyaltsen Palsangpo, at the great temple of Glorious Tharpa Ling.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The Tibetan names the last two nāga kings "great Padma" and "very great Padma"; these are taken as the standard pair Padma and Mahāpadma, which completes the eight. The three lords who crown the deities, Śāśvata ("the Eternal"), Vajra and Narteśvara ("Lord of Dance"), are the Hevajra Tantra's names for the family lords Vairocana, Akṣobhya and Amitābha. "The lord of the gods" on the right in the first ring is left unnamed, since Śakra appears separately in the second ring.
