The Sādhana of the Maṇḍala Rite Called Thoroughly Well Grasped (Suparigraha nāma maṇḍalanopikavidhi)

The Sādhana of the Maṇḍala Rite Called Thoroughly Well Grasped, known in Sanskrit as the Suparigraha maṇḍala rite and in Tibetan as Dkyil 'khor gyi cho ga'i sgrub thabs bzang po yongs su gzung ba, is a tantric ritual manual composed by the Indian scholar Durjayacandra. Opening with homage to the glorious Heruka and to the compassionate Buddha, it sets out in verse the procedures for a maṇḍala rite, from its preparations and mantras to the closing fire offering, with its reading of omens in the flames and its dismissal of the fire god. Its author presents it modestly, as a practical guide that follows established convention rather than an exhaustive account of every tantra's rites. The work is Toh 1240 in the Tengyur, where it is preserved among the commentaries on the tantras.

In the language of India: suparigraha nāma maṇḍalanopikavidhi. In the language of Tibet: The Sādhana of the Maṇḍala Rite Called Thoroughly Well Grasped.

Homage to the Blessed One, glorious Heruka.

Beings have fallen into the spreading light of saṃsāra's destroying fire, its garland of sickness and sorrow,
and are blistered by a disease that turns and is hard to turn back; to bring peace to these afflicted beings,
a river of loving-kindness for beings, filled with the water of a rain of the nectar of compassion,
flows down everywhere: to that Buddha, the Blessed One, I bow.

No one knows in full
the rites for the meaning of all the tantras;
no one is able to accomplish
the rites for the meaning of all the tantras.
Therefore, following mere convention,
I do not elaborate;
I compose this,
a good compendium of the maṇḍala rite.

A master not carried off by gain and honor,
skilled in the activities, who has seen the maṇḍala,
a learned one well praised
for accomplishment and emanation, should perform it.
For peace, increase, consecration and the rest,
able to bring about the aims desired,
with up to two or three disciples,
one lays things out clearly in order to draw the maṇḍala.

To increase the accumulations and pacify obstructors,
one who knows the path of omniscience
should first perform the approach;
without the approach there is no accomplishment.
One who knows the distinctions among places
should at once remain in solitude
and meditate on the circle of deities.
When weary, he recites the secret mantra:
he recites the mantra of the Lord of the Circle a hundred thousand times,
a hundred thousand for his own special deity,
and ten thousand for each of the others.
Having obtained the supreme signs of accomplishment,
he gathers the maṇḍala into his own body.
This is called the preliminary approach.
That was the preliminary approach.

Having destroyed the hosts of obstructors through the approach,
one should take possession of the ground:
a mountain, a village or a city,
a region where the mind delights.
Where there are bones, ashes, thorns, stones,
or uncleanness from excrement, go elsewhere.
Ground with udumbara, nyagrodha, aśvattha,
palāśa, bakula or arjuna trees,
or shaped like the back of a turtle:
these six kinds of ground should be avoided.
Ground where water turns to the right,
sloping to the east and the north,
four-cornered and whitish:
dig there, refill the hole with that earth, and some is left over.

The mantrin holding vajra and bell,
with a mind full of compassion,
standing in the posture with the left leg extended,
should clear away all obstructors.
Dig half a cubit deep and fill it with water;
walk a hundred paces,
come back quickly and look:
if it stays just so, that is especially praised.
There, give torma to the lords of the earth,
the guardians of the field, and the rest.
Then, with mantra
and mudrā, one should request the ground.
Bind the two vajra fists,
and hold both at the edge of the boundary;
request it with the words, "Grant me this good ground!"
oṃ hūṃ jraṃ jraṃ svāhā.
That is the mantra and mudrā for requesting the ground.

Signs of good and ill appear;
if the next day they are favorable,
one should perform a pacifying fire offering,
or else subdue the obstructors.
With a mind steady as a mountain,
guard the ground well with the circle of protection.
One should dig to the depth of the knee, the navel or the waist,
according to the accomplishment sought.
Beginning from the winter months and so on,
for three months at a time,
the mahoraga has its head
to the south, the west, the north and the east,
and lies on its left side.
Knowing where its belly is, dig.
The faults of digging at its head, its back and so on
are to be learned from the extensive works.
That was the rite of digging the ground for the maṇḍala and so on.

Throw out husks, stones, gravel,
charcoal and the like;
place jewels, grains and medicines within,
and pound it until very firm.
If one is not able to remove
all the flaws of thorns,
press it with the hand and recite the secret mantra;
it then becomes powerful and pure.
oṃ hana hana krota hūṃ phaṭ.
That is the mantra for purifying the ground.

Sprinkling it with urine,
take possession of this pure ground.
For five nights without interruption
the vajra master should do this.
Binding the two vajra fists
and placing them before the two breasts,
drive everything away,
and afterwards enter the ground.
oṃ apasarantu bhavanto yi ge ci ta sthana svamina deva nāga yakṣadi gandharva bhūta bhūtani ḍākini satva ath kṛya aparaṃ vagacha traṃ sigraṃ me vantu oṃ hūṃ phaṭ.
That is the mantra and mudrā of expulsion.

The vajra ground and fence,
the nets and the canopy,
a net of arrows outside,
and the mountain of fire as well, are to be blessed.
oṃ medīnī vajri bhava vajra bandha hūṃ oṃ vajra prākara hūṃ vaṃ hūṃ oṃ vajra pañcara hūṃ yaṃ hūṃ oṃ vajra vidhana hūṃ khaṃ hūṃ oṃ vajra sara jvala tra saṃ traṃ oṃ vajra jvala analārka hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ oṃ suṃbhani suṃbha hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ oṃ gṛhṇa gṛhṇa hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ oṃ gṛhṇapaya gṛhṇapaya hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ oṃ ānaya ho bhagavān vajra rājā hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ oṃ gha gha ghātaya ghātaya sarva duṣṭaṃ vighā hūṃ phaṭ oṃ kīlaya kīlaya sarva pāpaṃ hūṃ phaṭ oṃ hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ vajra kīlaya vajradhara ajñāpayati sarva vighnāṃ kāya vākcitta vajra kīlaya kīlaya hūṃ phaṭ oṃ vajra hūṃ muṅgara vajra kīlaya ākoṭaya hūṃ phaṭ.

With these accomplished mantra words,
outside the blazing fire,
the ritual daggers in wrathful form,
in forward and in reverse order,
are to be driven into the crowns of the obstructors.
That was the rite of the ritual daggers and so on.

The suchness of the vajra, the bell and the rest,
the welcoming water and the rest,
and the commitments of the extensive rite:
the door guardians are set in place with the action vajra.

Beginning from eight finger-widths
and going up to twenty, make them in even numbers.
Take vajras of three kinds,
made of gold, red bronze and the like:
three-pronged, five-pronged
and nine-pronged, these are the three kinds.
By distinguishing peaceful and not peaceful,
they are again said to be of two kinds.
The fourth is the crossed vajra itself.
The five-pronged has four faces.
The middle is the place where it is held;
the outer prongs are of that same measure.
The central prong is shaped like a barley grain.
Not going beyond their portions of the directions,
the outer prongs are tips of makara fangs,
emerging from the mouths of makaras:
thick at the root, growing thinner toward the tip,
sharp, their heads gradually curving.
On the inner side of the prongs,
leave a gap from the central prong.
In keeping with the size of the vajra,
increase or reduce by portions of two finger-widths.
Make the tips equal with one another,
and a little shorter than the central one.
At the roots of the eight prongs,
picture lotuses, lotuses in bloom.
Make the waistband somewhat
larger than the middle portion.
The remainder, equal on either side,
rises progressively twice as high.
With beautiful eight-petaled lotuses,
there are two bead strings at the head and the foot.
This is the description of the vajra.

In the center is Vairocana; Akṣobhya
is the east; likewise Ratnasambhava is the south;
on the western and northern prongs
are Amitābha and Amoghasiddhi.
The eight limbs of the noble path, made pure,
are on the eight petals of the lotus:
they are the eight bodhisattvas,
whose marks should be clearly seen:
Padmapāṇi, Maitreya,
Gaganagañja, Samantabhadra,
the Lord of Yakṣas (Vajrapāṇi), Mañjuśrī,
Sarvanivaraṇaviṣkambhin, and Kṣitigarbha.
The central one abides as the ground of all:
the essence of the buddhas, the casket.
Vajrāṅkuśī, Pāśī,
Vajrasphoṭā, and Tārā.
In the intermediate directions, the Urger, Jewel Lightning,
the Chain, and the Invincible
are declared to be the lotus ḍākinīs.
Their proper places on the prongs are declared:
in the east, Vajraḍākinī;
in the north, the Terrifying Ḍākinī;
in the west, know her as Vetālī;
in the south, call Caṇḍālī to mind;
in the center, Jñānaḍākinī.
This is held to be the suchness of the vajra.

Of the bell, in six parts overall,
four parts of it are to be made
of bell metal unmixed;
the rest should be examined from other sources.
The space within that bell
is equal in width and in height.
A garland, an utpala, eight marks,
above and in the center, in their order;
by the rite it is marked with eight petals.
On one portion above the utpala
rest three garlands, and above them
a vase made of gold or copper,
silver or brass.
Above that, of the same measure,
make the face of Prajñā, looking to the east.
Above the face is a half vajra,
like half of the vajra described before.
Thus the bell of eight finger-widths and so on
should be made to match the vajra.
Its mouth is like a lotus slightly opened,
or else like the lip of an ox.
This is the description of the bell.

Facing the eight directions and intermediate directions,
meditate on the seeds of the deities.
In the east is the goddess Tārā;
Pāṇḍaravāsinī is in the north;
in the west know her as Māmakī;
in the south is Buddhalocanā.
The Fair-Colored, the Red, the Radiant One,
and Vajra Garland are in the intermediate directions.
The space residing within
is the formless realm, made pure.
Of this form realm, completely pure,
the utpala at the base, in bloom,
abides as the nature of the nectar of wisdom,
the ground of the perfection of wisdom.
The lotus and vajra above
are to be imagined as a vajra.
The handle in the center is wisdom,
gathering in and sending out all things.
With five kinds of lotus-turning gestures,
it should be rung at the eight places.
This is the suchness of the bell.

From gold and other substances, from the wood of the sacrificial tree,
or else from nimba wood,
the filling ladle is two cubits long.
Its divisions shall now be explained.
On a width of eight finger-widths,
carve a hollow equal to half of that.
Carve a vajra equal to the hollow.
The mouth is like a lotus petal.
The rim is square,
four finger-widths all around.
In that two-finger carving, of that measure,
is a three-pronged vajra inside.
The hole from which its essence drips
is the size of the little finger.
Below the waistband, of that same measure,
is the place of beautiful petals,
like a datura flower;
make this at the top of the handle.
Below the handle, a jewel of two finger-widths,
and at the end, a vajra of six finger-widths.
This is the description of the filling ladle.

The pouring ladle is a cubit and a half.
Its mouth is six finger-widths,
shaped like the petal of a lotus.
In its center, in width and depth,
carve three finger-widths, of that measure,
adorned with a five-pronged vajra.
Two finger-widths are the place of adornment;
know that the jewel is one finger-width.
The handle of the filling ladle
is just long enough to grip in the fist and pour.
The pouring ladle is somewhat thinner,
and its vajra is three finger-widths.
This is the description of the pouring ladle.

Vessels of gold, silver, mother-of-pearl, conch,
stone, wood, and copper:
these are the vessels for the welcoming water;
at the least, use a vessel of leaves.
That teaches the vessels for the welcoming water.

Scented water, sweet milk,
pure water, and white flowers,
as they are found, are placed in the vessel;
then fumigate with incense and recite the secret mantra.
This is the rite of preparing the welcoming water.

White, yellow, red and green:
in accomplishing peace, increase and the rest,
prepare the scents, the water and the rest
in keeping with the action.
This is the rite of the varieties of welcoming water.

Flowers, incense, lamps,
the conch of scented water and the rest are set on the right;
on the left, the mantra's welcoming water first;
and a vessel is also placed in front.
This is the arrangement of the vessels for the welcoming water and so on.

The three-syllable or five-syllable
mantra, as taught in the tantra,
in keeping with the action,
is made the mantra for the welcoming water and the rest.
For peace it ends with vaśaṃ;
for increase the ending is vauṣaṭ;
for killing, hūṃ phaṭ comes at the end;
for summoning and subjugating, jaḥ and hriḥ.
These are the mantras for the welcoming water and the rest.

First, scented water should be offered;
second, sweet milk;
third, pure water;
fourth, flowers are added.
The scented water from the mouth of the conch
should be offered at the feet.
oṃ niriti hūṃ khaṃ: this is the mantra of the water for the feet.

For the water for rinsing, it is milk
and sweetness. The purifying sprinkling
is pure water alone; with a bundle
of kuśa grass, recite the mantra of the welcoming water.
oṃ sarbba saṃśodhani hūṃ phaṭ: this is the mantra of the water for the mouth and of the purifying sprinkling.

Having taken the flowers with the white wisdom,
one distinguishes the borders and the rest in sequence.
For the water for the feet, the water for the mouth and the basin:
copper bowls, or vessels of iron,
or vessels made of other substances
such as gold and silver, are set on stands.
This is the classification of the water for the feet and the rest.

The water for the feet, the water for the mouth, the purifying sprinkling and the rest
should be offered in their proper order.
Because all of these are limbs of the welcoming water,
they are spoken of by the word "welcoming water."

The sequence of the rite of offering the welcoming water:
binding the two fists evenly together,
extend the two middle fingers and join their tips;
this is shown as the great argham.
oṃ pratīccha arghaṃ hūṃ svāhā: this is the mantra and the mudrā for offering the welcoming water.

Recite the mantra of Amṛtakuṇḍalin over white mustard seed.
With the two fists, around the chosen deity,
circling with one's own circling,
one should purify, two by two, in the spaces between.
With both fists together at once,
one also circles to the right.
As before, the white mustard seed
should be put into the fire vessel in order to purify.
oṃ sarbba pāpaṃ dahana bajrāya bajra satvasya sarbba pāpaṃ daha svāhā: this is the rite of purification.

Having drawn the wisdom consciousness inward,
take water in the cupped hands,
circle it in the same way, and to purify,
put it into the fire, just as with the white mustard seed.
oṃ hūṃ taṭi phaṭ: this is the rite of ritual touching.

Moonstone, indranīla sapphire,
puṣparāga topaz, the red gem,
and the supreme emerald: the powder of these
is the finest jewel dust for coloring.
Gold and silver and pearl,
lapis lazuli and coral:
these are declared to be the five jewels.
I shall explain the five twigs:
they come from the jambu and the kadamba,
the bilva and the mango,
and the bījapūra citron.
Sesame and wheat, pulses, rice
and barley are to be known as the five grains.
hasadeba kapira kretaṃ jali bṛhapala:
these are to be known as four herbs;
the fifth is karṇika.
kaṇḍakari bṛhati hasadeba hasa sete aparaji:
these are the other group of herbs.
The two sandalwoods, and saffron,
and lakudranthika, making five:
these are taught as the five scents,
and are prescribed for bathing the deity.
udumbāra śiriṣa aśvattha and nyagrodha
and parkati: with the barks of these five
the oil from the deity's bath is cleared away.
The cow's ghee, its dung and urine,
curd and milk, properly mixed:
these are to be known as the five products of the cow;
they accomplish the bathing of the deities.

To delight fully
the buddhas and bodhisattvas,
one who always knows the pledges
should worship with the pledge offerings.
Song is offered to the Vajra Holder;
dance is offered to the buddhas;
likewise scent to supreme Hari;
food and drink to the Lord of the World (Lokeśvara);
the offering of garments to Paramāśva.
These are the supreme pledges.

hūṃ The scent arisen from the vajra:
black aloeswood for Heruka;
white sandalwood for the Permanent One;
red is offered to the Lord of the World;
saffron to Vajra Light;
for the King of the Seven, all of them.
Bitter and pungent are offered to Vajra;
sweet to Vajra Permanent;
sour to Vajra Sun;
likewise salty to the Lord of the World;
for the King of Horses, all of them,
or else astringent, should be carefully considered.
Beautiful black flowers that grow on trees
are for the Vajradhara of Hevajra.
Mallikā and mālatī
and kunda are always offered to the Buddha.
The supreme lord of trees and the stainless one
are to be offered to the Illuminator (Vairocana).
Red utpala, the water-born lotus,
red bandhu and pāṭala,
and lotuses are offered to Padmanarteśvara, Lord of Dance.
All of them are for Vajra Paramāśva.

Having performed the yoga of one's own deity,
with certainty driving away the adverse forces,
sitting in the center of the ground,
one should petition the buddhas:
"Until the ritual wheel is fully completed,
in order to protect us,
it is fitting that the Sugatas
bless this ground this very day."

The wise one, wrathful, sets upon
the host of hostile beings who would interfere.
On the right and left eyes,
from ma the sun, and from ṭa the moon.
With the rays of light streaming from the vajra beneath the feet,
one burns the nāgas and the rest.
Adorned with every ornament,
playfully twirling the vajra,
ringing the bell at each place,
and continuously proclaiming the dense sound of hūṃ,
the wise one, circling to the right,
goes all around the four directions.
With modulation and supreme grace,
first moving along playfully,
by the rite of the eight stances
one should dance with vajra steps.

Make the gesture of the flower's heart;
for incense, the gesture of the twig;
for the lamp, the hand manner of the wick;
and for anointing, anoint in the manner of anointing;
with the hands, the eyes and likewise the ears;
with both feet, circling to the right
with stamping steps,
and likewise circling to the left.
Then perform the continuous dance,
and after that the moving dance;
dance likewise with rapid stamping,
and whirl around like a wheel.

The right-extended stance, the left-extended stance, the even stance, the vajra stance, the round stance, the various-cutting stance, the tortoise stance, and the one called "like a flying bee": these are the eight stances. Stirring the ocean, lifting the mountain, the graceful step and the wrathful step: this is the arrangement of the stances.

The vajra mode, the wheel mode, the jewel mode, the lotus mode, the sword mode, the spear mode, the bow mode, the staff mode, the staff mode, the hammer mode, the pestle mode, the single-pointed vajra mode, the ten-pointed mode, the small sword mode, the variegated club mode, the short spear mode, and the single-pointed weapon mode: these are the sixteen modes.

The double banner, the triple banner, the flower mode, the incense mode, the lamp mode, the scent mode, the hand of Lāsyā, the hand of the garland, the hand of song, the hand of dance, the hand of the flute, the hand of the vīṇā, the hand of the clay drum, the hand of the mukuṇḍa drum, and the hand of infinite activity: this is the arrangement of the hands.

The vajra cross-legged posture, the sattva cross-legged posture, the lotus cross-legged posture, the excellent seated posture, the half cross-legged posture, and the auspicious seated posture: this is the arrangement of the seated postures.

The erotic sentiment, the heroic sentiment, the sentiment of disgust, the fierce sentiment, the comic sentiment, the terrifying sentiment, the peaceful sentiment, the majestic sentiment, and the sentiment of compassion: these are called the nine sentiments of dance.

The hand drum, the cymbals and the long drum,
and likewise the half clay drum;
the flute and the pipe, likewise;
the vīṇā, the many-stringed lute and the single-stringed lute:
these are to be played in three distinct ways,
slow, forceful and fast.
In the maṇḍala and the other places
this offering is unsurpassed.
These are the arrangement of the music.

The song following ṣaḍja, the song following ṛṣabha, the song following gāndhāra, the song following madhyama, the song following pañcama, the song following dhaivata, and the song following niṣāda; the slow division, the forceful division, the complete division, the deep division, halving, intermingling, taking two phrases, taking three phrases, sounding in the throat, sounding at the palate, sounding with the tongue, and sounding at the teeth: these are the arrangement of the song.

When taking possession of the ground,
these pledges are earnestly urged.
In order to accomplish all the actions,
the master, the vajra holder, should perform them.

After the vajra ground and the rest are accomplished,
one should meditate on the source of phenomena
arising from Vajrasattva. Within it
is a lotus arisen from hrī,
and on the lotus, arisen from a,
is the vajra facing in all directions.
On the vajra are wind and the other elements;
on these, from bhrūṃ, the wheel itself,
situated in the center of the expanse of space:
the maṇḍala that is the essence of the four elements.
On it, having fully meditated on the buddhas,
emanate them and set up the blazing celestial mansion.
One should meditate on the delightful palace
beautified with victory banners, pennants and the rest.

With white, gold and other powders
one should draw an eight-petaled lotus.
The vase called "Accomplishing All Actions"
is placed in the middle of the hub.
The thread of the five colors
is laid out on the eastern petal.
In the south the vajra should be placed,
and likewise in the north the bell.
Conch cups filled with the five nectars
are set on the petals other than the east.
The colored powders, the portions of the five buddhas,
having been placed each in its own
separate shining vessel,
are to be set at their respective corners.
In the vase, with bodies and seats just as they are,
one visualizes Vajradhara.
One worships properly with the offerings,
and urges with mantra and mudrā.

Binding both fists together,
extend the rocana and the white finger,
and joining tip to tip,
show the heart mudrā.
oṃ āḥ svāhā: this is the heart mudrā and mantra.

Touching the vase with the tip of the vajra,
recite the secret mantra a hundred and eight times.
oṃ hūṃ svāhā: this is the mantra.
Sprinkling that water on the colored powders and the marking cord,
make offerings and give the torma.
Recite the secret mantra of one's own lord,
and meditate on his body as well.
Emanating, one should then gather in the wheel.
At night one should remain there for a moment.
These are the taking possession of the ground.

The yogin rises at dawn,
and having done all the tasks of the body,
gathers the vase and the rest and places them
in the southern part of the maṇḍala ground.
Again, having anointed it with scent,
imagine the goddess arisen from baṃ,
her two hands holding an excellent vase,
her body yellow in color and radiant.
Worship properly the one perfect in wisdom,
and once she is firmly established, request her to depart:
"In the ways of conduct and the special qualities
of all the buddhas, the protectors,
and in the grounds and the perfections,
goddess, you have mastery.
In order to attain all the accomplishments,
I shall draw the maṇḍala."
Saying this three times, one requests Pṛthivī, the earth goddess, to depart.

Bathe the body and anoint it with scent,
adorn it with as many shining ornaments as there are,
and wearing yellow garments,
enter in the afternoon.
Seated in the center of the maṇḍala,
strewn with scent and flowers,
meditating on oneself as the chosen deity,
and joyfully holding incense,
bowing deeply with all the limbs,
one should petition the Victors:
"Buddhas dwelling in all directions
without exception, please consider me.
Great Vajradhara, I, so-and-so,
wish to draw the maṇḍala.
Buddhas and all the rest, please come,
and grant this accomplishment."
This is the petition to the buddhas.

To the master, who is like Vajrasattva,
and is equal to the Permanent One and the other Victors,
the disciples who hold vajra and bell
should make petition:
"Peace of all the Tathāgatas,
abode of all the Tathāgatas,
supreme selflessness of all phenomena,
please explain the sacred maṇḍala.
All the marks perfectly complete,
all that are not marks entirely abandoned,
Samantabhadra, supreme body,
please explain the sacred maṇḍala.
Arisen from the supreme Dharma of peace,
purifying the conduct of wisdom,
Samantabhadra, supreme speech,
please explain the sacred maṇḍala.
Great mind intent on the welfare of beings,
pure by nature and without stain,
Samantabhadra, supreme mind, the guide,
please explain the sacred maṇḍala."
Or else this single verse
should be recited by the four with one voice:
"Peace of all the Tathāgatas,
abode of all the Tathāgatas,
guide, please explain the maṇḍala
that arises from Vajrasattva."

Then, rising, and bowing
to the buddhas who dwell in the sky,
one should generate the pride of being
oneself the union of all the buddhas:
"I am the Buddha Vajrasattva;
I am Vajra Sun;
I am Amoghavajra, the Dharma;
I am all the buddhas."
With the mantra hūṃ bajrotiṣṭha,
one's own manifested wheel
is raised into the sky,
and with the vajra dance one circles all around it.
With the mantra explained earlier,
make the base into the nature of vajra.
The fence, the net, the canopy
and the rest are done as before.

For the pegs in particular:
a peg eighteen finger-widths long,
or else twelve finger-widths,
six or four finger-widths in thickness,
made of khadira wood, of bone or of iron.
It should be anointed with red sandalwood,
and beautified with garlands and a red crest.
It is to be held in the left fist.
The hammer, placed on the head of the peg
with the other hand,
strikes it with the earlier mantra,
and the pegs are driven in at the east and the other places.
Or else, with mantra and mudrā,
make the pegs, the fence and the net.
Showing the palm of the hand upward,
turn it over and strike the ground.
oṃ bajra hūṃ phaṭ: this is the mantra and mudrā of the vajra peg.
The two hands, with fingers interlinked like a chain,
are stretched upward and turned outward.
oṃ hūṃ phaṭ: this is the mantra and mudrā of the net.
Making both hands into fists,
extend the wisdom and the lotus.
Join the earth parasol and the eight consciousnesses;
join wisdom and consciousness;
joining the earth parasol and the lotus,
circle through the eight directions and intermediate directions.
oṃ jaṃbhani hūṃ phaṭ: this is the mantra and mudrā of the fence.

The one endowed with yoga
should consecrate the vase.
Vases free from black bases and other faults
are to be set out in front:
long-necked and big-bellied,
their mouths beautified with fine branches,
filled with milk, urine and the rest,
and their necks wrapped with cloth.
Having made the matching ones firm,
they are to be placed in the maṇḍala house.
In order to fulfill the aims of the eight accomplishments,
the recitation is done up to seven times.
oṃ tapte tapte maha tapte svāhā: this is the mantra for summoning the water of the Gaṅgā.
Joining the earth parasol and consciousness,
and extending all the rest,
touching the vase, with this secret mantra
one recites up to seven times:
oṃ suṃ kṣuṃ yuṃ hūṃ phaṭ svāhā. This is the consecration of the door vases.

With the stainless lines laid down,
at the four doors of the ritual wheel,
beginning from the eastern door,
the vases are to be set out.
Those called Victory and Auspiciousness
are on the two sides of the eastern door;
in the south are declared Accomplishment and Protection;
in the west, Victorious and Peace;
in the north, Accomplishment and Miraculous Power.
This is the offering of the precious accomplishments.

Whatever here is the abode of the deities,
wherever the maṇḍala is fixed,
that maṇḍala should be anointed
with the five scents, or else with others.
By emanating the syllables with their own mantras,
one should set out the wheel of the deities.
Then, by the practice of summoning,
the wheel of wisdom deities is summoned there.
Then with the five kinds of offerings
one worships the Victors properly, with faith,
and petitions them for blessing
until the maṇḍala is complete:
"Buddhas, whose intention is loving and beneficial,
please consider me.
Having come together with the mantras,
you take possession of the ground.
Great Vajradhara, I, of this name,
in order to create the maṇḍala of so-and-so,
with whatever offerings I am able,
shall draw the maṇḍala at dawn tomorrow.
In order to care for the disciples
and also to make offerings to you,
it is fitting that you, moved by loving compassion,
draw near and remain."
Saying this three times is the consecration of the deities of the maṇḍala.

Then the disciple, having bathed
and put on clean garments,
holding flowers in cupped hands and prostrating,
should petition the guru:
"You alone draw us out
from the ocean of existence in which we sink,
fearful with its sea monsters
of birth, aging, sickness and death.
Great Bliss, since you are my Teacher,
master, please consider me.
I seek you, great protector,
who show the way of great awakening.
Grant me that very pledge;
grant me also the mind of awakening;
grant me also the three refuges,
the Buddha, the Dharma and the Saṅgha.
Protector, please lead me
into the supreme city of great liberation."
This is the disciple's petition for the pledge.

"Since you are a vessel of the great way,
come here, my son! The rite
and the manner of conduct of the secret mantra
of the Great Vehicle I shall rightly explain to you.
The perfect buddhas who have passed,
likewise those who have not yet come,
and the protectors who have now appeared
and remain for the benefit of beings:
having known this excellent rite
of the secret mantra of them all,
the Hero, at the foot of the tree of awakening,
attained omniscience, free of signs.
Through the unequalled application of secret mantra,
the protector Śākyasiṃha
destroyed the most terrible hosts of Māra,
and their great armies as well.
Therefore, in order to attain omniscience,
my son, make this your resolve!"

Having thus advised the disciple,
one performs confession, rejoicing and the rest.
First one has him go for refuge to the three,
and then he should generate the mind of awakening:
"Just as the protectors of the three times
made certain of awakening,
so I shall generate the unsurpassed,
sacred mind of awakening.
The training of moral discipline,
the gathering of virtuous qualities,
and the working for the welfare of beings: these three moral disciplines
I shall firmly uphold.
The Buddha, the Dharma and the Saṅgha,
the unsurpassed Three Jewels:
the vow that arises from the yoga of the buddhas
I take first, beginning from now.
For the great supreme vajra family,
the vajra, the bell and the mudrā,
these very things I shall rightly uphold,
and I shall uphold the masters as well.
For the delightful pledge
connected with the jewel family,
every day at the six times
I shall always give the four kinds of giving.
For the great pure lotus family,
arisen from great awakening,
the outer, the inner and the secret, the three vehicles,
the sacred Dharma, I shall uphold each one.
For the great supreme action family,
I shall rightly uphold
being endowed with all the vows,
and I shall perform the acts of offering as best I can.
Having generated the unsurpassed,
sacred mind of awakening,
for the welfare of all beings
I take the vows without exception.
Those who have not crossed over I shall carry across;
those not liberated I shall liberate;
those not relieved I shall relieve;
and I shall establish beings in nirvāṇa."
Saying this three times, the vows are taken.

Then the disciple, set before one,
is sprinkled with scent, and at his heart
one holds a five-pronged vajra.
On the crown of his head one imagines
a vajra marked with various colors,
and at the place of the throat a lotus.
Then, having made offerings with flowers,
incense and the rest, set him in front.
A tooth stick twelve finger-widths long
is washed with scented water and milk,
its tip bound with flowers;
and scented water recited over seven times
is also given to purify the mouth.
oṃ ajitva saṃbartani hūṃ phaṭ: this is the mantra recited over the tooth stick.
oṃ hrīḥ biśuddha dharmmā sarbbā pāpaṃ niśca saṃśodhaya sarbba bikalpa apanaya hūṃ: this is the mantra for giving the water.

The disciple, facing north,
holding the tooth stick without splitting it,
throws it onto the ground,
onto an area the size of a cowhide.
If it falls with its upper end facing toward him,
he will be most fortunate.
If it falls with its outer end facing away,
he will be without good fortune.

For protection, over a thread
recite the mantra of Amṛtakuṇḍalin;
then make three knots in it
and tie it on the disciple's right upper arm.

Then, so that they will not turn back,
one should arouse enthusiasm in the disciples,
and so that they turn away from other vehicles,
proclaim the greatness of the Great Vehicle:
"You great beings
today have gained an unequalled gain,
because all the great Victors
together with their heirs
have taken all of you
into this teaching, and you are being born in it.
Therefore tomorrow you
will be truly born into the Great Vehicle.
This supreme path, from which the Great Vehicle
greatly arises, is glorious.
Since you are going there,
you self-arisen ones of great fortune,
through knowing all the world
you will become Tathāgatas.
Beyond existence and nonexistence,
stainless like space,
profound, wholly beyond the conceptions
of all reasoners, without any abode,
free of all elaboration,
yet elaborated through elaborations,
free of response, of action and the rest,
abiding in the two truths:
this supreme among excellent vehicles
is attained if one abides in its way.
My son, tomorrow the maṇḍala
will be drawn. Now sleep!"

Having said this, one enters, makes offerings properly,
and requests the maṇḍala deities to depart.
Having bestowed the yoga of the chosen deity,
one then brings the disciple
into the celestial mansion and protects him well.
One should carefully examine the lion's sleeping posture.
Shoots of kuśa grass with unbroken tips
should be imagined as arisen from dhi.
The first is placed as a pillow,
and the others are spread as a mat.
"With a virtuous mind, sleep,
and tell me the dream you see, just as it is."
Saying this, one has the disciple lie down.
One should ask what dream he saw.
Having heard it, one should explain without doubt
whether it is good or not good.
Through a virtuous dream the three kinds of accomplishment are achieved;
an unvirtuous one is to be pacified by fire offering and so forth.
This is the consecration of the disciple.

On a good planetary day, lunar mansion and the like,
and also on the fourth day,
the guru, having performed the fire offering,
together with the disciple begins the maṇḍala.
The six months, the four planets,
whichever are the fourteen lunar mansions,
and the number of seven lunar dates
are praised by the Victors for consecration and the rest.
The middle and last months of spring, the last of summer,
the autumn month, the middle and last of winter;
the planetary days of Mercury, Jupiter, Venus,
and the day of the moon: these are good.
The second day and the third day,
the fifth, the seventh, the tenth,
the thirteenth, and the full moon:
these seven days are good.
Puṣya, Hasta, Anurādhā, Uttaraphalgunī,
Rohiṇī, Abhijit, Mṛgaśiras and Ārdrā,
Jyeṣṭhā, Mūla, Pūrvāṣāḍhā and Uttarāṣāḍhā,
Pūrvabhādrapadā and Uttarabhādrapadā: these are the lunar mansions.
Under Uttaraphalgunī one should perform consecration;
through it wisdom will increase.
Whatever accomplishment abides in the mind
will be accomplished under Hasta.
Under Mūla and Abhijit
the teachings will be fully understood.
Ārdrā brings good fortune.
Under Rohiṇī wealth will arise.
Mṛgaśiras is declared to be for peace.
Pūrvāṣāḍhā and Uttarāṣāḍhā bring increase of sons.
Pūrvabhādrapadā and Uttarabhādrapadā are known for increase.
Under Puṣya one is victorious in everything.
Jyeṣṭhā rightly bestows happiness.

Anurādhā bestows the supreme.
Relying on the right time and good planets,
this activity is the consecration.

The marking cord, new and clean,
beautiful, of nine or five threads,
twisted into one, five in number,
should be twisted while reciting the secret mantra:
oṃ anyonya ānugata sarbba dharmāḥ para anuprabaṣṭi sarbba dharma atyanta ānuprabaṣṭi sarbba dharmāḥ. This is the mantra.

With a blazing gaze, from the heart
of the buddhas draw forth a cord of light,
and, holding it in the middle of the joined hands,
one then consecrates that marking cord.
oṃ a ā a ā āṃ svāhā: this is the mantra for consecrating the marking cord.

The guru, placing his left fist
at his navel, holds the marking cord;
immediately the disciple
should take hold of it with this mantra:
oṃ jja jja jja, which is said up to three times.

With the mantra, the pure water of all the vases
is sprinkled inside the house.
In the same order as laying down the marking cord,
the wisdom cord is stretched out.
oṃ sarbba saṃśodhani hūṃ phaṭ: this is the mantra for sprinkling the maṇḍala and the rest.
oṃ bajra sūtra samaya matikrama hūṃ: this is the mantra of the wisdom cord.

Without the signs, nothing is truly accomplished.
If the marking cord breaks, the master dies.
If it is drawn too long or too short, illness strikes.
Through confusion of directions, harm will be suffered.
Circling to the right,
laying down the lines as taught,
upon the lines of the emanated maṇḍala as well
the wisdom cord is laid down.
Accompanied by the cry hi hi,
the doors should be opened with the mind.
With oṃ hūṃ trāṃ aṃ khaṃ
the colored powders are applied as the forms of the deities.
They should be consecrated with a blazing gaze,
reciting the secret mantra a hundred and eight times.
Incense, garments and the rest
should be offered, as much as one has.
Twirling the vajra and ringing the bell,
urging with vajra songs and the rest,
wearing red garments, give the torma.
One should also do it with special devotion, in this way:
"This is the pure dharmadhātu,
which liberates the realm of sentient beings,
the abode of all the Tathāgatas."
Having made oneself the Victor Vairocana,
one should begin to lay out the colors,
free from every fault.
The master lays them first,
and afterwards the disciples all around.
Taking the powder with the left vajra fist,
beginning from Īśāna, the northeast, and circling to the right,
in the order that accords with the families of the wheel,
draw the colored powders with the mantra:
oṃ bajra citra samaya hūṃ. This is the secret mantra.

Afterwards the skilled disciple
should lay them out as is comfortable.
Crooked, impure, broken, thick,
thin and ill-formed lines are to be avoided.
Thick ones give rise to illnesses;
thin ones destroy wealth;
crooked ones cause discord;
broken ones bring death to master and disciple.
Freedom from these and other faults
will be known from the drawing of the colored powders.

Of the nature of indranīla sapphire,
and likewise another like emerald,
of the nature of padmarāga ruby,
and one known as blazing yellow,
and likewise another of white color:
thus one should know the colored powders in order.
In the east is great white;
in the south is blazing yellow;
in the west, intense red;
likewise in the north, green;
and in the central part of the ground
blue color should be carefully applied.

The master stays at the east of the wheel,
possessed of the concentration of Vajrasattva.
Four masters, embodying the yoga
of Hūṃkāra and the others, are at the four doors.
Those to be placed at the four corners
are four proclaimers of the Dharma.
For fear that the pledges may be broken,
one who is not a disciple should not be brought in.
If there are none, take two or three;
at the very least, even one is acceptable.

Twirling the vajra with the right hand,
ringing the bell with the left,
one dances, changing the mudrās, or lets them go.
With gaze and mudrā one conquers the enemies.
With the stamping stance and the mantra
one should press down the eastern place.
In this same sequence the others,
those stationed at the doors, also press it down.
With the right and left hands,
playfully one emanates out to the borders.
With angry eyes slightly widened,
one moves the eyebrows.
This, the cause of bringing beings under control,
is to be known as the mudrā of the eyes.
Right extended, left extended,
right extended and drawn in, left extended and drawn in,
together with the mantra hūṃ and the mudrā:
this is the yogin of Vajradhara.
That is for the principal master; now for the others.
The kneeling stance, the squatting stance,
the circling stance and the reversing stance.
oṃ hūṃ phaṭ: this is for the eastern door.
The knee-touching stance, the left-lifting stance,
the circling stance and the lion's offering stance.
oṃ hūṃ āḥ phaṭ: this is for the southern door.
Right extended, left extended,
and the round stance.
oṃ hūṃ āṃ āṃ āṃ phaṭ: this is for the western door.
The concordant stance, the discordant stance,
and the stance with one leg drawn in and the left extended.
oṃ hana māra duṣṭāna ghātaya ghātaya a a hūṃ hrīḥ phaṭ: this is the stance and mantra of the master of the northern door.

In order to accomplish the desired aims
and to pacify the hindrances of obstructors,

give the torma to the deities and the rest.
Binding the two vajra fists,
join them back to back.

extend the rocana and the white finger,

With this mantra, from the mudrā of invitation,
bind the index fingers, snap the fingers three times, and release.
This is the mantra and mudrā for pleasing them.

On a square maṇḍala of scented water
scatter white flowers.
With abundant food of śāli rice,
molasses, curd, cooked rice and butter,

draw their emblems correctly.

those that make the wheel complete,
as the Sugata (the Buddha) has taught,

Kacora and corika,

Kunduru and polaka,

The maṇḍala is said to be the lotus.
The sublime maṇḍala of the mind itself
is explained as the immeasurable mansion;
the mind is the tiered immeasurable mansion.
Its outer square
is equal in its measures.
The mind of loving-kindness and the rest
is spoken of as the four marking cords.
What is properly explained as the line
is recollection of the Dharma through union.
Gnosis fully freed from all views
is spoken of as the line.
What is properly explained as the ornaments
is the supreme vow of moral discipline and the rest.
The mind of the two faculties and the rest
arises as the five walls.
What are explained as the doors
are the four kinds of liberation.
The four pillars of the archways
are called the four right abandonments.
The four applications of mindfulness
are spoken of as the four porticoes.
The four doors, whatever they are,
are the four bases of miraculous power.
Likewise the adornment of flower garlands
is the seven limbs of awakening.
The eight limbs of the noble path
are called the eight pillars.
The special gnosis of the path
is known as the yak-tail fly whisks.
What are explained as the full necklaces
are the cutting of all afflictions;
by its nature of cutting completely,
the snatching away of afflictions is the netting.
Through the union of the two accumulations
the wheel is turned, and because
it cuts half of the afflictions,
it is called the half necklace.
This is the suchness of the maṇḍala.

The five aggregates, in brief,
are known as the five buddhas.
The vajra sense sources themselves
are the supreme maṇḍala of the bodhisattvas.
Earth is explained as Locanā,
the water element as Māmakī;
Pāṇḍaravāsinī and Tārā
are known as fire and wind.
By enjoying delusion with delusion,
the exhaustion of delusion is Yamāntaka.
Thereby it is the ender of the aggregates;
thereby it is the ender of the knowable.
By ending aversion to its exhaustion,
through the exhaustion of faults, it is Prajñāntaka.
Therefore it is the ender of mind;
thereby it becomes the ender of afflictions.
By enjoying desire with desire,
the exhaustion of desire is Padmāntaka.
Therefore it becomes the ender of speech,
and likewise the ender of meditative absorption.
The exhaustion of all afflictions,
and likewise the exhaustion of all karma,
the gnosis exhausting all obscurations,
is explained as Vighnāntaka, the ender of obstructors.
This is the suchness of the deities.
This is the irreversible master empowerment.

in disparagement of your vajra brothers and sisters.
Do not be lacking in love for sentient beings;
do not speak of suchness to those without the fortune;
do not abandon fortunate beings;
never disparage your own body.
Do not set limits on the sublime Dharma,
nor scorn the teachings of others.
Until you reach the heart of awakening,
do not abandon the Three Jewels.
Always guard the sacred commitments;
hold the vajra and the bell;
always keep the crown knot;
always perform the fire offering;
always erect maṇḍalas;
always consecrate the Sugatas;
give torma to the great bhūtas."
With these words and the like, the consolation should be given at length.

Having conferred the wisdom empowerment,
one should then speak of the fourth:
"Vajra and bell: by the sleep of that melody
the eyes are sealed;
realizing, and wholly free of concepts,
peace comes clearly forth.
This gnosis is exceedingly subtle,
the vajra essence, equal to space,
stainless, bestowing liberation, peace itself;
and you yourself are its father."
From the lips of the holy guru
the fourth is to be known.

After the disciple's rites are complete,
having come out of the maṇḍala,
one should perform the fire offering to complete the rite.
Then, having entered, ask the Victors' forbearance;
having made offerings and praises,
ring the bell with the left hand,
move the vajra with the right and offer incense,
and recite the verse requesting them to depart:
"oṃ You have accomplished every aim of beings;
grant the accomplishments that accord with them.
Though you depart to the buddha realm,
please come again."
This is the mantra requesting departure.

When the maṇḍala of deities has departed,
one should gather the colored powders with the mantra,
place them in a clean vessel,
and then cast them into a river.
oṃ gili gili hūṃ ja svāhā
This is the mantra for gathering and casting away the colored powders.

Then, anointing with nectar
the ground within the maṇḍala,
one should offer the torma of the guardians of the directions
or the torma for all bhūtas.
The torma for the guardians of the directions
was taught before the accomplishment of the maṇḍala.
Now, not unlike the former,
the torma for all bhūtas is explained.
In the center of the element of space
one should visualize an eight-petaled lotus.
On the eastern petal imagine Indra;
in the south place Antaka (Yama);
on the western petal the lord of waters, Varuṇa;
in the north, likewise, the yakṣa lord.
In the fire quarter, the southeast, the great Agni;
in the southwest, Nairṛti, lord of the rākṣasas;
in the wind quarter imagine the Homeless One, the wind god;
in the northeast, Īśāna, lord of the bhūtas.
The sun, the moon, Brahmā and the rest,
the holy Brahmakāyika gods,
the Brahmapurohita gods,
those dwelling in the Joyless and the Boundless,
the Truly Arisen, the Great Kings,
the Paranirmitavaśavartin gods,
the gods of Nirmāṇarati,
those dwelling in the Pure Abodes,
those dwelling in Sukhāvatī,
those dwelling in Akaniṣṭha,
the Radiant world realm,
and those dwelling in the Heaven of the Thirty-Three:
all of these are to be placed above.
Those of the lower realms, in the source of phenomena:
Karkoṭa and Śaṅkhapāla,
likewise Ananta and Vāsuki,
Takṣaka and Kulika,
Mahāpadma and the excellent Padma,
the asuras, and the beings of the hells:
imagine them below the source of phenomena.
From oṃ come Brahmā and the other gods,
from hūṃ come Śakra and the rest,
from āḥ come Karkoṭa and the rest,
each with two arms and one face.
The gods above are brilliant white,
Indra and the rest are black,
Karkoṭa and the rest are deep red.
Each is embraced by a consort like himself,
holding vajra and bell in the hands,
and stands on a seat with the right leg extended.
Brahmā and the rest are sealed by the Permanent One,
Śakra and the rest by Vajra,
Karkoṭa and the rest by Narteśvara;
all are born from the three families.
Gaurī having summoned them,
Caurī binds them with the noose,
Vetālī fetters them with chains,
and Ghasmarī delights them with the bell.
Then give water for the face and water for the feet,
and prepare the torma according to the rite.
Upon the maṇḍalas of wind and fire
imagine a lotus vessel arisen from a;
by blazing and radiating
one should prepare the five nectars.
Having meditated on this transformation of the parts,
bless it with oṃ and offer it.
oṃ akāro mukhaṃ sarbba dharmmāḥṇāṃ ādyanutpannatvāt oṃ ā hūṃ phaṭ svāhā
Then, having pleased them, one should request them to depart:
oṃ bajra satva samaya manupālaya bajra satva tvenopatiṣṭha dṛḍho me bhaba supoṣyo bhaba śaśvato me bhaba anurakto me bhaba sarba siddhimme prayaccha sarba karma suca me cittaṃ śriyaṃḥ kuru hūṃ ha ha ha ha ho bhagavān sarba tathāgata bajra māme muñca bajrī bhaba mahā samaya satva āmuḥ
These are the rites for accomplishing the maṇḍala.

Since consecration and the fire offering
are two limbs of the maṇḍala,
after the maṇḍala I shall teach
both the consecration and the fire offering.

A master of good birth and intelligence,
who performs all his actions without conflict with the world,
and who knows the ten principles,
is fit to perform the consecration.
At a captivating place of consecration,
anointed with the five well-thickened substances,
adorned with flowers,
beautified with a canopy, white parasols, pennants
and much else besides,
and fragrant with burning incense,
on a day of good planets and constellations, or on another,
the master, properly dressed, proceeds.
He should wear a necklace, anklets, armlets,
earrings and bracelets;
if he has none, at least a finger ring,
of no less than a māṣa in weight.
The images, painted scrolls and books there
are adorned with as many ornaments as there are.
Joining himself with the special deity,
he places them before him and performs the preliminary consecration.

First of all, the vidyādhara
diligently rings the bell,
brandishes the vajra in his right hand,
and gives the torma according to the rite.
With the five products of the cow, the five barks,
and in particular the five fragrances,
he washes what bears the form of the deity,
and offers water for the face and the rest as before.
Again, through that same yoga,
he washes it and blesses it.
Knowing the place and the time,
he performs the preliminary consecration in the evening.
He sprinkles cleansing water from the conch.
In the chamber that has been sprinkled,
upon the images and the rest set there,
the form accomplished through the six limbs,
he visualizes it as the very deity;
with verses he invites the forms of the deities
into the sky and properly worships them.
Thus he performs this preliminary consecration:
"Just as the perfect buddhas
abide in Tuṣita,
and as they entered the womb of Queen Māyā,
so likewise please abide in this form.
Protectors who always dwell here,
for the full increase of the awakening mind,
please accept with loving compassion
these flowers, incense and the rest."
He properly worships, and then praises;
after that he purifies,
and then fully protects.
This is the preliminary consecration.

"Akṣobhyavajra, great gnosis,
vajra sphere, great space,
supreme three vajras, three maṇḍalas:
to the vajra melody I bow and offer praise.
Vairocana, great purity,
vajra peace, great bliss,
luminous by nature, supreme of the supreme:
to the vajra Teacher I bow and offer praise.
Jewel King, Ratnasambhava, most excellent,
vajra space, stainless sky,
pure by nature, untainted:
to the vajra body I bow and offer praise.
Deathless Vajra, great king,
nonconceptual, holder of the vajra of space,
who has reached the far shore of desire:
to the vajra speech I bow and offer praise.
Amoghavajra, perfect buddha,
who fulfills all wishes,
arisen from the pure essential nature:
to Vajrasattva I bow and offer praise."
Brandishing the vajra in the right hand
and holding the bell in the left,
fully knowing the meaning as it is,
he praises with the song of these five verses.
These are the preliminary consecration of the deity.

A vase free of black marks, cracks at the base and the like,
long-necked and large-bellied,
smeared with water mixed with white stone powder,
its mouth adorned with fine leafy branches,
with jewels, grains and medicines placed inside,
marked with the emblem of the family,
its four sides of the belly furnished
with the emblems of the four Victors,
its neck wrapped in beautiful cloth:
this is held to be the victory vase.
The others are like it,
but smaller, each with a single family emblem.
A white cord twisted from seven strands,
over which hūṃ has been recited seven times,
and a garland of flowers are tied at the neck.
Placing in it water scented with white sandalwood,
one should firmly perform its preliminary consecration.
Holding the end of the cord in the hand,
one ties it to the middle of the vajra and sets it on top.
Meditating on its lord,
one recites his secret mantra a hundred and eight times.
The other eight vases
are arranged all around as in the maṇḍala,
and over each, with the secret mantra of the lord it bears as its mark,
one recites a hundred and eight times.
With the secret mantra of Amṛtakuṇḍalin
one recites over the tenth vase alone.
Because the lord is not fixed,
the secret mantras of the others are given:
oṃ dhara dhara dhāraya dhāraya mardha mardha bajra dhṛka dhitiṣṭha
oṃ śaśvat parama śaśvata ehyehi jin jṛk adhitiṣṭha
oṃ bajra sūrya tamobhidhamana samaya stvāṃ haṃ ratna dhṛka dhitiṣṭha
oṃ bajra dharmā samayastvāṃ hulu hulu āroḷka adhitiṣṭha
oṃ phu phu phu hi hi hi phrajñā dhṛka adhitiṣṭha
oṃ moha rati adhitiṣṭha
oṃ dveṣa rati adhitiṣṭha
oṃ rāga rati adhitiṣṭha
oṃ bajra rati adhitiṣṭha
At the place of the buddhas' preliminary consecration,
having worshipped these vases,
the wise one holding vajra and bell
should give the torma and draw the rite to a close.
This is the preliminary consecration of the vases.

Then, not very far
from the chamber of the preliminary consecration,
a square of about four cubits
and two cubits in height,
built of earth and stone or of brick,
beautified with auspicious features
and pounded very firm:
such a fine platform the wise one should make.
Here the deity is set facing east.
Sitting facing it,
holding vajra and bell,
one should give the torma. After that,
meditating on the seed syllable in one's own heart,
one draws in the buddhas abiding in space,
and through the yoga of the nectar of gnosis
emanates the offering goddesses in order.
From these, jaḥ hūṃ baṃ hoḥdi,
one should emanate the four gatekeepers;
by them the four actions
of summoning, drawing in and the rest are performed.

Covered with yellow cloth and turned to face east,
with oneself facing them, after that
the images seated
on the seat of the platform are bathed in order.
Painted images, books, clay figures and the like:
the one who knows the rite
washes the reflection of these deities in a mirror;
for the others, the form itself.
For purification, first,
with the five products of the cow placed in a bronze vessel,
reciting the secret mantra aloud,
one bathes the deity:
oṃ sarba tathāgata kāya biśodhani svāhā
With a brush made from the tips of bound dūrva grass,
one anoints with fragrant oil
placed in a bronze vessel,
and the mantrin recites its secret mantra:
oṃ hūṃ trāṃ hrī khāṃ
Then, to remove the oil,
one rubs it well with earth ground with the five barks,
and, as the very self of the deity,
bathes the deity once again.
Anointing it with the five fragrances taught before,
kept in a bronze vessel,
and reciting the secret mantra given above,
one bathes the image.
Auspicious songs, music,
dance and many other beautiful things are performed.
As at the perfect empowerment of the Victors,
all the splendors
are performed by Locanā and the others,
and with vases held by all the tathāgatas,
filled with the water of nectar,
the wise one washes it through supreme meditative concentration:
"Just as all the tathāgatas,
the moment they were born, were bathed
with the pure water of the gods,
so likewise do I bathe you."
oṃ sarba tathāgata abhiṣekata samaya śrīye āḥ hūṃ
This is the mantra for bathing the image.

With a golden stick
of no less than a māṣaka in weight,
one takes a little eye salve from the colored powders,
and with these one opens the eyes:
oṃ cakṣu samanta cakṣu biśodhani svāhā
Wiping off the water with a cloth,
one seats it firmly in whatever its place may be,
and to each deity of the circle
one offers a pair of garments:
oṃ bajra vastraye svāhā
This is the mantra for offering garments.
Having offered water for the feet, water for the face, water for rinsing the mouth,
and water for welcome, after that,
at the end of the purification,
one performs vast offerings:
ema puṣpa śubadrabya śucayaṃ śucayonāma yamanibitobaghya pratigṛhṇa prasiddhima banaspati rasahṛdo gandha dhūpa mudhama yamanibiditobakṣu pratigṛhna prasadme rākṣoghaśca papitraśca tamobhidhamanasubha mayanibidhitobhakṣa pratigṛhṇa prasidame sahajasaṃśogakiśuddho gandhoyaṃ ghaṇaḍarpaṇa yaṃmayanipidotyobāghya pratigṛhna prasadame naibidye sarba saṃyughaṃ khadabhojasamananitaṃ varṇa gandharāsobitaṃ dabhami pratighṛhṇa taṃ oṃ jaṃ saṃ svāhā
This is the mantra for offering all the fruits.
oṃ pratīccha arghaṃ hūṃ svāhā
This is the mantra for offering the water of welcome.

Brandishing vajra and bell,
like Vajrapāṇi himself,
singing the verses of auspiciousness,
one offers the torma to the buddhas:
ṇama hūṃ kāṃrana buru seji bisaddha uttaru tena hūṃ tena hūṃ tena tete hūṃ dhaṃbala su saṃkhaṃte hadharu saraya susobha candhamu hūṃ tena hūṃ tena hūṃ tena tete hūṃ dana bhila kanayo udharu gaṇḍa bajra mutta dharu tena hūṃ tena hūṃ tena tete hūṃ māyabīdhe hina jagu so i akarana rūpi tena hūṃ tena hūṃ tena tete hūṃ
It should be sung blended with the fifth melody.
These are the rites of consecration.

After the consecration and the rest,
whatever was forgotten, could not be done,
or left the rite incomplete
should be completed by the fire offering.
Through the variety of aims to be accomplished
the fire offering becomes of many kinds;
but since a lengthy text is daunting,
I shall teach here only a brief summary.
First, with mantra and mudrā,
one should purify the ground:
extending the right hand,
visualizing it as a vajra, one strikes the ground.
oṃ hana hana krodha hūṃ phaṭ
This is the mudrā and mantra for purifying the ground.
The hearth is a cubit across
and about half that in depth.
Then come the base and the rim,
and outside, a vajra garland.
The base has anthers,
the rim has lotus flowers;
each is four finger-widths,
equal throughout in breadth and height.
Wheel, jewel, lotus or sword,
and in the center of the hearth a vajra:
in the central field, one finger-width,
and at the lip, eight finger-widths, are to be drawn.
For pacifying, the colored powder is white;
for increasing, it is made yellow;
for subjugating, red; for the others,
black: these are the five kinds.
The directions differ according to the kind of rite,
and the times too are to be determined;
the wise examine the direction and the time
for each such rite.
Facing north, the mantrin
properly undertakes the rite of pacifying;
increasing is always facing east;
for fierce rites it is facing south;
for subjugating, west is taught,
and likewise for summoning and for expelling.
Pacifying is performed in the evening,
increasing in the morning,
fierce rites at midday,
and subjugating and the like at midnight.
Twigs of the sacrificial tree,
their tips unbroken,
fine, and bearing two leaves,
are to be distinguished according to the kind of rite:
for increasing, fourteen finger-widths;
for pacifying, twelve;
for subjugating, ten; for hostility
and for expelling, eight finger-widths.
The grain, the kindling sticks and the rest,
having been cleansed, are placed accordingly
in the right-hand corner,
and one recites the mantras over them all.
Joining the two hands as fists,
one fully extends the two middle fingers.
This is the mudrā for purifying all substances.
oṃ śriṃ svāhā
This is the mantra for purifying the butter.
oṃ svāhā
This is the mantra for washing the substances.
oṃ jṛṃ svāhā
This is the mantra for purifying the grain.

oṃ kuru kuru svāhā
This is the mantra for purifying the other substances.
oṃ ā svāhā
This is the mantra for purifying the kindling sticks.

The wood that comes from the limbs of the fire offering,
shorter than the hearth, is placed in the hearth.
Kindling the fire through meditative absorption,
one mentally gives the oblation of butter.
He appears distinctly,
possessing a body arisen from meditative concentration;
to him one offers in order water for the feet, water for the face,
water for rinsing the mouth, and the water of welcome.
Kuśa grass, not too long, its tips unbroken,
is spread facing east, turning to the right,
with the tips all pointing one way,
and is laid upon the rim.
With sandalwood or aloeswood,
or else with other fragrant woods,
in order to perform the fire offering,
one fans the fire into flame with a fan,
makes it blaze brightly, and examines the signs.
In the middle of the fire one meditates
on a lotus and a sun disc,
and then once more one invites him:
the god with three eyes, four faces and four arms,
large-bellied, with a topknot of matted hair,
in his right hands the gesture of granting boons and a rosary,
in his left a staff and a water pot,
his color according with the rite;
one visualizes him arisen from the source of the syllable ra.
oṃ ehyehi mahābhūta deba ṛṣi dveja satvama ahāṃti mahārasmin sannihito bhaba
This is the verse of invitation.
Having offered the water of welcome and water for the feet,
and having taken full possession of the enclosure,
in order to realize the accomplishment of the aims one desires,
one should properly recite the sacred commitment:
oṃ bajra anala mahābhūta jvalaya sarba bhasmin kuru sarba duṣṭa hūṃ phaṭ dṛśya jaḥ hūṃ baṃ hoḥ smayastvaṃ samaya ho
This is the verse for giving the sacred commitment.

On the fine filling ladle and pouring ladle
one visualizes the seed syllable marked with a vajra.
Holding them with the right hand
and keeping them between the knees,
having blessed body and speech,
one offers the oblations to the god of fire,
who is crowned with the lord of his own family
and whose tongue bears a three-pointed vajra.
oṃ agnaye svāhā
This is for the butter.
oṃ sarba pāpaṃ dahana bajrayā bajra satvasya sarba pāpaṃ dahana svāhā
This is for the black sesame.
oṃ bajra puṣṭaye svāhā
This is for the unbroken rice.
oṃ sarba saṃpade svāhā
This is for the curd and the finest food.
oṃ bajra bijaye svāhā
This is for the husked rice.
oṃ mahā begāya svāhā
This is for the barley.
oṃ mahā balāya svāhā
This is for the peas.
oṃ bajra ghasmari svāhā
This is for the wheat.
oṃ bajra āyuṣe svāhā
This is for the young dūrva grass.
oṃ apratihata bajraye svāhā
This is for the kuśa grass.
oṃ bajra yajñā svāhā
This is for the udumbara wood.
oṃ bodhi bṛkṣāya svāhā
This is for the aśvattha wood.
oṃ bajra lathāya svāhā
This is for the plakṣa wood.
oṃ bajra kuberāya svāhā
This is for the nyagrodha wood.
oṃ satakaraya svāhā
This is for the mango wood.

Holding the pouring ladle firmly
by its head with the right hand,
one fills the filling ladle to the brim of its mouth
with a stream of butter from the pouring ladle.
Then with filling ladle and pouring ladle together,
again three times each,
one should give full oblations,
in every way as much as one is able.
This is common to pacifying,
and the same is taught for increasing.
For other rites such as subjugating
other oblations are to be given.
Whatever substances, one, two or three,
bring about pacifying and increasing,
since a lengthy text is daunting,
I shall state them briefly.
oṃ padma rāga agne ma pṛṇaya amukasya śyantiṃ kuru svāhā
This is for lotuses together with butter.
oṃ gha gha ghātaya tejorā pṛṇaya śāntiṃ kuru svāhā
This is for blue lotuses together with milk.
oṃ supuṣpa kule bale jalane śāntiṃ kuru svāhā
This is for white water lilies together with saffron.
oṃ dharme dharmārāje daha daha sarba bighnāṃ śāntiṃ kuru svāhā
This is for storax together with sandalwood.
oṃ kili sukili kālareti sarba pāpaṃ daha amukasya śīntaṃ kuru svāhā
This is for oleander together with fragrant sesame oil.
For increasing, the mantrin should perform the fire offering
with the five substances,
mango, arka, kaṇḍapala,
bilva and udumbara,
using this mantra:
oṃ gagani gagana bilokite hūṃ phaṭ tejorāja amukasya puṣṭiṃ kuru svāhā

By the color, the smell, the tongues of fire,
the shape, the sound and the like,
one should examine the fire omen, as the oblations burn,
for the patron's good or ill fortune.
For pacifying, a white blaze;
for increasing, a yellow appearance;
for subjugating and for fierce rites
red and black fire are praised.
A fire like Indra's bow, the rainbow, is good,
and one that swirls to the right is best.

A sound deep like that of a clay drum
and flames that are glossy and single-pointed are praised.
Scattering sparks, much smoke,
rising by stages and blazing sluggishly,
at the end blazing of itself and splendid,
hot and radiating light,
three-pointed and thin,
and likewise like a cow's head:
these and other bad marks,
the inauspicious ones, should also be examined.

To bring the fire offering to its close,
offer a host of offerings, flowers, incense and the like,
praise the blazing fire,
and request it to depart with this verse:
"Eater of oblations, you have accomplished
my own aim and the aim of others, so depart.
Likewise come again at the proper time,
and grant me all accomplishments."
By this the wisdom fire is pleased,
Vajradhara is greatly pleased,
the Victor, the Permanent One, is pleased,
the Lord of Gold is gratified,
Padmanarteśvara, Lord of the Dance, is pleased,
and King Paramāśva becomes pleased.

Viṣṇu, the mighty Vāyu,
Kāmadeva, the god of desire, and the Plough-Bearer (Balarāma),
the Moon, the Sun and the other grahas,
the lords of the earth, the Noose-Holder (Varuṇa),
Kubera and Nairṛti,
the King of Dharma (Yama), and the nāgas,
gandharvas, asuras, kiṃnaras,
the gods dwelling in the Heaven of the Thirty-Three,
those dwelling in Akaniṣṭha,
those where the Great Kings dwell,
the gods dwelling in Yāma and in Tuṣita,
the gods of Nirmāṇarati,
likewise those of Paranirmitavaśavartin,
those who dwell in the Pure Abodes,
the Radiant world realm,
those dwelling in the Sahā world,
the sentient beings dwelling on the four continents,
whoever dwells in the three realms,
the world as vessel and the world of sentient beings,
grass, branches, herbs, trees and so on;
those who dwell on the Joyful Ground,
those who have reached the Stainless,
those dwelling on the Radiant,
those abiding on the Illuminating,
those who have reached the Difficult to Conquer,
those who have attained the Manifest,
those who have attained the Far-Gone,
those who have gained the Immovable,
those who dwell on Good Intelligence,
and those who have reached the Cloud of Dharma:
the lords of the ten grounds, the protectors,
all of them become pleased.
The eight noble persons,
the perfect buddhas and the śrāvakas,
the rhinoceros-like and those who live in groups,
hell-beings, hungry ghosts and animals,
gods, asuras and humans:
thus the whole circle of the assembly's maṇḍala,
all of them, by this rite
of the fire offering, become satisfied.
For pacifying, make offering with milk;
for increasing, accomplish it with yellow water;
for subjugating and for fierce rites
pour red and black water.
This is the quenching of the fire.

Flowers grown from the many trees of the tantras,
the stainless mantras, mudrās and the rest:
by this which Durjayacandra has composed,
may it be like a radiant garland of flowers for beings.

This completes the maṇḍala sādhana called Suparigraha, composed by the great scholar, the glorious Durjayacandra.

Though my mind is little practiced in the ocean of Sanskrit,
