The Aspiration Found in the Ritual Manual of Noble Avalokiteśvara, the Wish-Fulfilling Jewel (Āryāvalokiteśvaracintāmaṇikalpāt praṇidhānodbhava)

The Aspiration Found in the Ritual Manual of Noble Avalokiteśvara, the Wish-Fulfilling Jewel, known in Tibetan as Phakpa Chenrezik Wangchuk Yishin gyi Norbü Tokpa le Mönlam Jungwa, is a short aspiration prayer said to be drawn from a larger ritual manual devoted to Avalokiteśvara in his wish-fulfilling jewel form; no Sanskrit title is clearly attested for it. It opens with homage to the buddha Amitābha, to the Three Jewels, to Avalokiteśvara, and to the bodhisattva Mahāsthāmaprāpta, and then repeatedly calls on Avalokiteśvara, the Lord of Compassion. Its petitions ask that, through remembering him and reciting his name, the unbearable sufferings, fears, harms, and hostilities of the reciter and of all sentient beings may be pacified and cut off. The work is Toh 1094 in the Kangyur, where it belongs among the shorter devotional and ritual texts associated with the tantric literature centered on Avalokiteśvara.

Homage to the Tathāgata, the arhat, the perfectly and completely awakened buddha Amitābha. Homage to the Three Jewels. Homage to noble Avalokiteśvara. Homage to the bodhisattva, the great being Mahāsthāmaprāpta, who is endowed with great compassion.

Venerable Avalokiteśvara, endowed with great compassion, please heed me. Noble Lord of Compassion, by my recollecting you and reciting your name, may all the unbearable sufferings of myself and all sentient beings be pacified and cut off.

Homage to venerable Avalokiteśvara, endowed with great compassion. Noble Lord of Compassion, by my recollecting you and reciting your name, may all the fears, harms and hostilities that befall myself and all sentient beings be cut off.

Homage to venerable Avalokiteśvara, endowed with great compassion. Noble Lord of Compassion, by my recollecting you and reciting your name, may all the karma and afflictions of myself and all sentient beings, all unwholesome phenomena, and all that has arisen from the deeds of Māra be purified and released.

Homage to venerable Avalokiteśvara, endowed with great compassion. Noble Lord of Compassion, by my recollecting you and reciting your name, may I and all sentient beings always behold the face of venerable Avalokiteśvara, and may all the aspirations we have made swiftly be attained.

Homage to venerable Avalokiteśvara, endowed with great compassion. Noble Avalokiteśvara, Lord of Compassion, by my recollecting you and reciting your name, may I and all sentient beings behold the faces of the noble ones directly, and may all the supreme and sacred requests we make likewise be fulfilled.

Homage to venerable Avalokiteśvara, endowed with great compassion. Noble Lord of Compassion, by my recollecting you and reciting your name, may I and all sentient beings gain great power to benefit and bring happiness to all who are poor and destitute in the worlds.

Homage to venerable Avalokiteśvara, endowed with great compassion. Noble Lord of Compassion, by my recollecting you and reciting your name, may the great wilderness of famine, and whatever harms of fire, water, wind and the like arise in the worlds and afflict many people, be pacified and brought to an end.

Homage to venerable Avalokiteśvara, endowed with great compassion. Noble Lord of Compassion, by my recollecting you and reciting your name, when I and whatever sentient beings draw near to the great passage of dying and departing from this world to the beyond, may we then too see you, Noble Lord of Compassion, face to face, and may you grant us great consolation. May I and other beings who are dying, undisturbed by the afflictions, endowed with mindfulness of the selflessness of phenomena, firm in a boundless awakening mind, with minds undaunted and possessed of unimpeded confidence, with all painful feelings completely stilled, die with mindfulness fully established. And in all our lifetimes, may we be born at the feet of noble Avalokiteśvara and remember our former lives; and endowed with a perfect awakening mind, may we please the Noble Lord of Compassion and the others, and have the power to accomplish everything just as it has been taught.

Just as that Lord of Compassion has spontaneously accomplished the gateway to liberation of great compassion, may we too become of one taste with the compassion of all the buddhas. Just as the Lord of Compassion brings sentient beings to full maturity, purifies buddha fields, and brings all the qualities of a buddha to perfect completion, so may I and all sentient beings likewise bring all sentient beings to full maturity, purify all buddha fields, and bring all the qualities of buddha fields to perfect completion.

Since noble Avalokiteśvara is the embodiment of the compassion of all the buddhas and bodhisattvas of the three times, may our recollecting him and reciting his name become the reciting of the names of all the victors of the three times together with their heirs, and the honoring of them all. Since noble Avalokiteśvara is the very nature of the compassion of all the victors of the three times together with their heirs, by recollecting him, reciting his name and making offerings to him, may the application of the skillful means of great compassion arise without interruption toward all sentient beings, who are the objects of great compassion.

For as long as I remain in saṃsāra,
In however many bodies I take birth,
For that long may I make offerings to the Protector,
And gather that many devotions of body.

For as long as I remain in saṃsāra,
Whatever speech may arise in me,
For that long may I recite the Protector's name,
And gather devotions with my speech.

For as long as I am in saṃsāra,
However many thoughts arise in my mind,
For that many may I recollect the Protector,
And gather devotions of the mind.

Thus, by relying with body, speech and mind
Upon the Lord of Compassion,
May my body, speech and mind be free of faults
And become perfect in good qualities.

Thus, since with body, speech and mind
I constantly unite myself
With the body, speech and mind of the Lord of Compassion,
May I too become like the Lord of Compassion, Avalokiteśvara,
The sole refuge in whom the three realms of existence place their hope,
Whose qualities are beyond conception,
And may I fulfill the hopes of the three realms.

"Ritual manual" renders the Tibetan rtog pa (Skt. kalpa). In the sentence about the poor and destitute, the Tibetan genitive "of myself and all beings" is read as the subject ("may I and all beings gain great power"), which the sense requires. In the passage on dying, "undisturbed by the afflictions" interprets "not proliferated by the afflictions."  The Tibetan "sngags snying bzhi" is rendered "the four heart mantras." It refers to the four mantras given in the text: the root mantra, the heart mantra, the near-heart mantra and the secret heart mantra. It could also be read as "the four mantra-essences."
