The Noble Great Vehicle Sūtra Called Immeasurable Life and Wisdom (Ārya-aparimita-āyurjñāna-nāma-mahāyāna-sūtra)

Toh 674 in the Kangyur is a Mahāyāna sūtra known in Sanskrit as the Ārya-aparimita-āyurjñāna-nāma-mahāyāna-sūtra and in Tibetan as the Phags pa tshe dang ye shes dpag tu med pa zhes bya ba theg pa chen po'i mdo, rendered in English as "The Noble Great Vehicle Sūtra Called Immeasurable Life and Wisdom." The text belongs to the sūtra class of the Kangyur and concerns the immeasurable life and wisdom of a transcendent buddha named King of Resplendent Glory, who dwells in a distant world-realm called Immeasurable Qualities, as revealed by Śākyamuni Buddha in discourse to the bodhisattva Mañjuśrī at Śrāvastī. Its teachings are closely associated with the practice and veneration of Amitāyus, the buddha of boundless life, and the text has long been important in traditions focused on longevity and the cultivation of merit. The opening mantra and homage formula situate it within a devotional and ritual context characteristic of Mahāyāna sūtras dedicated to overcoming the obstacles of untimely death and ignorance.

Jñānasambharopacite svāhā.

In the language of India: Ārya-aparimita-āyurjñāna-nāma-mahāyāna-sūtra.
In the Tibetan language: The Noble Great Vehicle Sūtra Called Immeasurable Life and Wisdom.

Homage to all buddhas and bodhisattvas.

Thus did I hear. At one time the Blessed One was dwelling in Śrāvastī, in the Jeta Grove, the garden of Anāthapiṇḍada, together with a great Saṅgha of one thousand three hundred monks and with a great many bodhisattvas, great beings.

Then the Blessed One declared to Mañjuśrī, the youthful: "Mañjuśrī, in the direction above there exists a world-realm called Immeasurable Qualities and so forth. There dwells a Tathāgata, an arhat, a perfectly and fully awakened buddha named King of Resplendent Glory, Most Perfectly Determined in Immeasurable Life and Wisdom. He abides holding life, having brought it to perfection, and he also teaches the Dharma to sentient beings.

Mañjuśrī, the youthful, listen. The human beings of this Jambudvīpa have short lives — they can sustain life for at most one hundred years. And even of those, the greater part are shown to die before their time.

Mañjuśrī, whatever sentient beings shall copy out this Dharma teaching called the Comprehensive Recitation of the Qualities and Praises of the Tathāgata Amitāyus, or shall have it copied, or shall merely hear or read even its name, or having written it in a book shall keep it in their home, or shall make offerings to it with flowers, incense, perfumes, and garlands — for those whose life is running out, it shall become possible for them to sustain life for one hundred years.

Mañjuśrī, for those sentient beings who come to hear the one hundred and eight names of King of Resplendent Glory, Most Perfectly Determined in Immeasurable Life and Wisdom, their life too shall increase. For those sentient beings who, at the point of death, bear in mind these names, their life too shall greatly increase.

Mañjuśrī, therefore those sons of good family and daughters of good family who desire longevity should listen to or copy out, or have copied, these one hundred and eight names of the Tathāgata Amitāyus. The qualities and benefits they shall thereby gain are these:

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The opening dhāraṇī syllables "Jñānasambharopacite svāhā" appear to be the tail end of a dhāraṇī from a preceding section of the text; they are carried over as-is, since they form part of the transmitted recitation unit.

The title of the world-realm, rendered "Immeasurable Qualities and so forth," follows the Tibetan convention of abbreviating a long proper name with "and so forth" (la sogs pa); the full Sanskrit would be something like Aparimita-guṇa, but the exact full form is not given in this passage.

The phrase rendered "Most Perfectly Determined" (shin tu rnam par nges pa) could also be translated "most perfectly ascertained" or "most supremely definite." It is an unusual epithet; the rendering chosen reflects the established meaning of nges pa as certainty or determination in a strong doctrinal sense.
