The Noble Sūtra of the Great Vehicle Called Dependent Origination (Āryapratītyasamutpāda nāma mahāyānasūtra)

Homage to all buddhas and bodhisattvas.

Thus did I hear. At one time, the Blessed One was dwelling in the realm of the Thirty-Three Gods, upon a flat stone resembling a harmonium, together with the venerable Aśvajit and the other great śrāvakas; with the noble Maitreya (Lovingkindness Personified); with the noble Avalokiteśvara; with Vajrapāṇi; and with the other great bodhisattvas, great beings, adorned with immeasurable and precious qualities; together with Brahmā the Great, lord of the world Akanniṣṭha; with the son of Śiva; with the Great Lord (Maheśvara) and the other gods; with Śakra, lord of the gods; with Pañcaśikha, king of the gandharvas; and with many others, all assembled there together.

Then the great bodhisattva, the great being Avalokiteśvara rose from his seat, arranged his upper robe over one shoulder, placed his right knee on the ground, bowed with palms joined in the direction of the Blessed One, and spoke these words to the Blessed One:

"Blessed One, these gods here wish nothing more than to build a stūpa. They are gathered in this assembly, and so I request that the Blessed One teach the Dharma to them in whatever way will cause the merit of Brahmā to increase, and cause monks, nuns, laymen, and laywomen among the people of the world — including its gods, māras, Brahmās, its ascetics and brahmins — to greatly increase their merit."

Then the Blessed One, in response to this request, declared the verse of dependent origination:

Ye dharmā hetuprabhavā hetun teṣāṃ tathāgato hy avadat,
teṣāṃ ca yo nirodha evaṃvādī mahāśramaṇaḥ.

Whatever phenomena arise from causes —
their causes and their cessation too —
this the Tathāgata has declared;
thus spoke the great ascetic.

"Avalokiteśvara, this dependent origination is the dharmakāya, the truth body, of the Tathāgatas. Whoever sees dependent origination sees the Tathāgata.

Avalokiteśvara, if any son or daughter of a noble family who has faith should build a stūpa no larger than a myrobalan seed, with a central pole no larger than a sewing needle, and with an umbrella no larger than a bakula flower, in some uninhabited place, and should place inside it this verse of dependent origination from the dharmadhātu, the basic space of phenomena, that person will generate the merit of Brahmā, and passing on from here at the time of death will be reborn in the world of Brahmā."

When the Blessed One had spoken these words, those śrāvakas, those bodhisattvas, the whole of that assembly, together with the gods, humans, asuras, and gandharvas of the world, rejoiced.
