Verses for Well-Being (Svastyayanagāthā)

The Verses for Well-Being, known in Sanskrit as the Svastyayanagāthā and in Tibetan as Bde legs su 'gyur ba'i tshigs su bcad pa, is a brief scripture in verse that presents a dialogue between a god and the Buddha. The god approaches the Guide of the World and asks how one may always come to well-being, and the Buddha answers with a series of stanzas, each ending with the refrain that such people "come to well-being in every way." The qualities he lists include unwavering faith in the Tathāgata, virtue and moral discipline, respectful care for parents, children, and servants, and devoted offerings to renunciants. In this way the text closely recalls the well-known discourse on blessings found elsewhere in Buddhist literature. It is Toh 1102 in the Kangyur, where it belongs among the short recitation texts valued for invoking auspiciousness and protection.

In the language of India: svastyayanagāthā
In the language of Tibet: Verses for Well-Being

Homage to the Omniscient One.

Into the presence of the Guide of the World
A certain god came near, and said:
"By what does one always come to well-being?
This I ask; Blessed One, please speak."

The Holy One heard these words,
And the Guide of the World gave answer:
"That by which one comes to well-being in every way,
O god, listen to it well.

"Those whose faith in the Tathāgata
Is unwavering, excellent, and utterly firm,
Those who are virtuous and possess moral discipline:
They come to well-being in every way.

"Those who always properly
Serve and honor father and mother,
Sons and daughters, and servants:
They come to well-being in every way.

"Those who with deep faith make offerings
To the renowned arhats,
And act according to the word of the gods:
They come to well-being in every way.

"Those who commit no misdeeds
Of body, of speech,
Or of mind, and are well restrained in all three ways:
They come to well-being in every way.

"Those who toward all beings
Cultivate a mind of loving-kindness,
Immeasurable and wholly undisturbed:
They come to well-being in every way.

"Those who, to abandon all suffering,
Cultivate well the eight limbs of the noble path
That leads to happiness:
They come to well-being in every way.

"Those who always clearly see
The emptiness of all conditioned things,
And in whose minds this abides:
They come to well-being in every way.

"Those who go about having realized directly,
With the body, the stainless Dharma,
Arhats whose minds are liberated:
They come to well-being in every way.

"Those who are free of attachment
To desire, aversion, and delusion,
In whom undisturbed wisdom has arisen:
They come to well-being in every way.

"Those who have come to understand the Buddha,
Protector of the world with its gods,
Who has abandoned further existence:
They come to well-being in every way."

The Verses for Well-Being are complete.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The phrase rendered "having realized directly, with the body, the stainless Dharma" reflects the canonical idiom of touching or realizing with the body (Skt. kāyena sākṣātkṛtvā), meaning direct, embodied realization. The phrase "the word of the gods" is literal; the Tibetan may also be read as "the word of the god," possibly referring to the Buddha as god among gods.
