This text, Toh 1222 in the Tengyur, is a short verse hymn of praise to Hevajra, the wrathful tantric deity also invoked here as Heruka. Its Tibetan title, which survives only in fragmentary form in the source and opens with the word thams, meaning "all," cannot be fully reconstructed, so no reliable English or Sanskrit title can be given. The hymn honors Hevajra as the supreme intrinsic nature of all things and the embodiment of nondual meaning. Verse by verse, it reads the deity's appearance symbolically: his blue color signifies unchanging reality and red signifies unceasing compassion, his three eyes are the three doors of liberation, and the vajra, skull bowl, and khatvanga staff in his hands stand for crushing the vicious, sustaining bliss, and wisdom. Each verse closes with a refrain of homage, so the poem works as both devotional praise and a contemplative guide to the meaning of the deity's form. It sits among the tantric works of the Tengyur, the canonical collection of Indian treatises and commentaries, as part of the literature connected with the Hevajra cycle of the Highest Yoga Tantra tradition.

Supreme intrinsic nature of all things,
From the very beginning, all are thus-gone,
Embodiment of the nondual meaning:
To Hevajra I bow and offer praise.

Blue, the unchanging nature of reality;
Red, compassion that never ceases;
Hair bristling upward, guide of beings:
To Heruka I bow and offer praise.

Three eyes, the three doors of liberation;
Reddened, the meaning of passion for the nature of reality;
Proclaiming hūṃ, which is non-arising:
To the lord of all I bow and offer praise.

In the right hand, the vajra that crushes the vicious;
In the left, the skull bowl that sustains bliss;
The khaṭvāṅga staff that signifies wisdom:
To the enjoyment body (saṃbhogakāya) I bow and offer praise.

The skulls, the very substance of the five wisdoms;
The five mudrās, the five families;
Necklace and garland, the formations abandoned:
To you, emanation body (nirmāṇakāya), I bow and offer praise.

Lotus seat unstained by afflictions;
Corpse of Brahmā, free of conceptual thought;
Sun that burns away ignorance:
To the naked body I bow and offer praise.

It is complete.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

"Corpse of Brahmā" refers to the seat on which the deity stands. Here it symbolizes freedom from conceptual thought. "The skulls" (thöpa) in the fifth stanza most likely means the crown of five dry skulls, matching the five wisdoms, rather than the skull bowl held in the left hand. The skull bowl in the fourth stanza is a separate term, kapāla.
