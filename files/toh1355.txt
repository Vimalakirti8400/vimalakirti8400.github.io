A Commentary on the Empowerment Section of the King of Tantras, the Glorious Kālacakra, called Unraveling the Vajra Words (Śrīkālacakratantrarājasya Sekaprakriyāvṛtti Vajrapadodghaṭī nāma)

Unraveling the Vajra Words, whose fuller title is A Commentary on the Empowerment Section of the King of Tantras, the Glorious Kālacakra, is known in Sanskrit as Śrī Kālacakratantrarājasya Sekaprakriyāvṛtti Vajrapadodghaṭī and in Tibetan as rgyud kyi rgyal po dpal dus kyi 'khor lo'i dbang gi rab tu byed pa'i 'grel pa rdo rje'i tshig 'byed pa. It is a commentary on the section of the Kālacakra Tantra that sets out the empowerments, the initiation rites through which a disciple is admitted to the practice of this highest yoga tantra system. The work opens with a homage to the glorious Kālacakra and to the First Buddha, Ādibuddha, portrayed as drying up the ocean of defilement with the fire of wisdom and sending down a rain of great bliss. The author then prays that the vajra holders, the sky-goers, and the vajra-bearing gurus will allow the treatise to be completed without obstacle. It is Toh 1355 in the Tengyur, where it belongs among the tantra commentaries devoted to the Kālacakra cycle.

In the language of India: śrī kālacakra tantra rājasya śeka prakriyṛ bratti vajra bādoṅghati nāma
In the language of Tibet: A Commentary on the Empowerment Section of the King of Tantras, the Glorious Kālacakra, called Unraveling the Vajra Words.

Homage to the glorious Kālacakra.

He who, with the fire of wisdom, dries up the poisoned waters of the seeping ocean,
And from the expanse of the sky of great bliss, roaring forth thunder, sends down what is like a stream of rain;
He who for countless eons displays to beings many dancelike means,
To that First Buddha (Ādibuddha), supreme among all dancelike means, I bow.

Those who bear the vajra name,
The two kinds of sky-goers,
And the gurus who hold the vajra:
By them may this treatise be accomplished without obstacle.

The one and only Buddha, who brought light to the three realms of existence sunk in pitch darkness, has passed into nirvāṇa.
After him, the noble Nāgārjuna and the others who served as arbiters no longer dwell here now.
This wrong path, which teaches devotion to the bliss of couple upon couple and brings ruin to beings:
To make those self-willed sophists understand, and to remind myself, I shall write this commentary.

Moreover, it is said:

Having entered this supreme great secret,
They say, "We practice yogic conduct,"
And engage in all that should not be done.
Thieves who corrupt the world arise.

Saying, "This brings the world relief,"
They pour streams of water over people's heads.
Crowns, vajras, bells and the like
They hold firmly on their heads and in their hands,
And by that vajra they make their living.

Some gather crowds of disciples,
Saying, "My words alone are pure,"
And so they deceive the childish,
Teaching the secret in a wrong way.

Some settle in remote borderlands,
And without abiding even for an instant
In the selfless perfection of wisdom,
They practice worldly mantras and the like.

Some wander about begging,
Go everywhere and speak recklessly,
Are deeply attached to women,
And utterly destroy the Buddha's pledges.

Some settle in villages,
Deeply attached to food and drink,
And with many boys and girls
They laugh and frolic.

Some put on music and dance,
Songs, and games of chance,
Look on at gathered crowds,
And study the arts and crafts.

Some take up trade and farming,
Kill elephants and cattle,
Drive chariots and the like,
And commit many wrongs.

Moreover, those of yogic conduct,
Nuns and female renunciants,
Laywomen and laymen,
Strike at one another with weapons.

They do not abide by the well-spoken Vinaya,
Nor do they keep it;
They disgrace the Buddha's banner
And commit the defeats and other offenses.

Those who do not know the supreme drink
Drink intoxicating liquor
Brewed from rice and the like,
And through it bring about ruin.

So spoke King Sucandra.

Therefore, in order to turn back from the wrong path those who, not knowing the vajra words, have entered upon a wrong path, I shall unravel, exactly as it is, the provisional and definitive meaning of this section on the two kinds of empowerment, which sets out the meaning of the five-chapter Condensed Tantra. I do so on the basis of the definitive and provisional meanings of each tantra, which I heard from the mouth of the holy guru and reflected on well, and moreover on the basis of the scripture and reasoning that show how bodhisattvas who practice their conduct through the gateway of secret mantra are to conduct themselves and train.

Although the lord of men, Puṇḍarīka, who holds a lotus in his hand,
Has condensed it well and explained it thoroughly,
Yet just as apprentices, carrying chisel and axe, follow closely
In the footsteps of a skilled carpenter,
So I too, an apprentice following the lord of men, the carpenter,
Firmly grasp the chisel and axe of wisdom,
Lay down the craftsman's chalk-line of scripture, reasoning and the guru's instructions,
Build the city of liberation, and compose a commentary on the words.

Apart from this commentary,
The reality of the precious twofold empowerment,
Clear and free of doubt,
Will not be found even by the learned.

On the passage beginning "Because descent is abandoned": "Descent" means this. When a person wishes to receive the worldly and transworldly empowerments, then for the sake of the common accomplishments, in order to draw in childish beings, if the descent has not properly taken place in one maṇḍala, another maṇḍala is to be emanated. But this is not so for the sake of the supreme accomplishment, because that would be contradictory. For if, in the case of the supreme accomplishment, a descent that had not properly taken place in one maṇḍala became supreme by descending elsewhere, then the sugatas too would have a grasped object and a grasping subject, and moreover would have greater and lesser powers. Therefore it should be understood that causing the descent is merely conventional, for the sake of drawing in childish ordinary beings, and not ultimate.

Now, the common accomplishment that the childish are to understand merely conventionally is as follows. The descent is brought about by three things: meditative absorption, mantra, and substances. Masters supremely devoted to meditative absorption, who have gained the power of meditative absorption, bring about the descent by meditative absorption alone. It is also brought about by mantra recitation performed according to the rite. It is also brought about by one who possesses both. There are also those who bring it about by substances alone, but that is not supreme.

When a descent of any of these kinds takes place in those who are entering the maṇḍala, one should ask about all that is virtuous and nonvirtuous. If it proves virtuous, that is good, and one should proceed with the rite. If it proves nonvirtuous, then in order to pacify it, one should perform pacifying and enriching fire offerings and the like until the bad signs have been pacified, and afterward proceed with the rite.

Here, the descent to be brought upon a disciple entering this glorious Kālacakra is as follows. First, incense of the five ambrosias and the five lamps is prepared according to the rite, and the disciple should be fumigated with it. Then, together with oṃ āḥ ra ra ra ra la la la la vajra ā be śa ya hūṃ, one should urge on the king of wrathful deities. Moreover, only after one has first performed the approach for these mantra lines by reciting them ten million times and performing a million fire offerings will the descent occur by mere thought, and not otherwise.

Moreover, bringing about the descent by substances alone is as follows:

Burn guggulu in the mouth of a preta
On the preta day of the waning fortnight;
By fumigating with what has been burned in a preta's fire,
One brings the descent upon all beings without exception.

And further:

That skull, the fruit of the golden tree,
Citron, and alacikraśu,
And a hen's egg: if one fumigates with these, the descent comes.

So it is taught, but that is not the supreme method.

Now, if the king of wrathful deities has descended, these are the signs:

When the king of wrathful deities descends,
With hands holding weapons
He shows a wrathful smile toward the hosts of māras,
And crushes the stable and the moving that harm the Dharma.
With his feet, in the right-extended stance and the like,
He performs various vajra dances.
With the sound of the syllable hūṃ
He sends forth a sound that burns Māra's armies.
For the yogin who is a beginner,
Through what is taught in countless tantras,
He displays songs and dances to people.
By the power of the wrathful one's qualities
He is free of shame and without doubt.
Whatever signs he then displays,
Know them to be causes of accomplishment.

By the division into body and speech:
When the body descends, he travels through the sky,
Draws maṇḍalas he has never seen,
And lifts mountains and the like.
By the mere descent of speech
He is victorious in debate
Over the gods and nāgas dwelling in the three realms of existence,
And becomes equal to Mañjughoṣa.
When the mind descends, he knows the minds of others,
And knows all hidden things of the three times,
Past, future and present,
Completely, leaving nothing out.
For the yogin who is a beginner,
Through the descent of earth
Blessed by Buddhalocanā he becomes like a mountain,
Not to be moved by countless men.
Through the descent of water
Blessed by Māmakī he becomes cool,
And those seized by fevers and the like
He cools merely by embracing them.
Through the descent of fire
Blessed by Pāṇḍaravāsinī he burns;
Whatever he thinks of, that he burns,
And even mountains he turns to something like ash.
Through the descent of wind
Blessed by Tārā, by mere thought
He carries men across
And drives them ten million leagues.
Through the descent of space he becomes invisible,
And becomes one who moves through the sky.
Through the descent of the eye he sees beautiful forms,
And even what was unseen becomes visible.
If the divine ear descends,
He hears the sounds of beings.
If the divine mind descends,
The wisdom that knows the minds of others arises.
If the divine faculty of body descends,
He apprehends supreme objects of touch.
Through the descent of the divine tongue
He tastes excellent, supreme flavors.
Through the descent of the divine nose
He perceives fragrant scents with his nose.

This is certain: it is the Buddha's own word. This has been a detailed teaching of the signs of descent.

"Having dismissed" is to be connected with "the descent." Here, once the king of wrathful deities and the deities have descended through blessing, one should ask them what is to be done and what is not to be done, and then dismiss them. This is done by consecrating a flower, or else the hem of one's own garment, with oṃ āḥ hūṃ, and placing it on the crown of the head or brushing with it; they will then depart to their respective abodes. If even by that they are not dismissed, then by pressing down on his crown with the earth maṇḍala and Mount Meru they will be dismissed, and not before.

Next, in the passage beginning "From the head, the heart, the crown, the navel, the throat and the secret place," the syllable oṃ is at the head, the syllable hūṃ is at the heart, and haṃ is at the crown; these are the aspect of skillful means. "Abiding each in its own awareness" means that the domain of skillful means is the head, the crown and the heart, while the domain of wisdom is the navel, the throat and the secret place. "The three vajras" means the nature of the three syllables of body, speech and mind. If one asks what syllables are to be placed on the aspect of wisdom: at the navel ho, at the throat ā, and at the secret place kṣa. "Protect" means to protect those six places. Otherwise, harm from Māra would befall the childish who do not know the vajra of the wisdom of suchness.

"Clothing the body in a red garment and covering the eyes" is so that wisdom is not yet revealed. "The disciple is to be brought in here" means here into this wheel of the Kālacakra maṇḍala. "The conventional observance" is to be connected with "observance": it is something that beginners, childish people who have not seen this very reality, must guard with effort for the sake of the accumulation of merit, until they have attained wisdom. "Having gone the supreme way" means that all, from Vipaśyin up to Śākyamuni, relying on this very observance, attained the supreme state. "Way" means that by that accumulation of merit and this accumulation of wisdom one aims at the supreme state. "Give" means that teachers should give it with effort to their beginner disciples; this is the injunction of the Tathāgata.

And what is that observance? It is taught in the passage beginning, "Abandoning malice, falsehood, the women of others, the wealth of others, and likewise liquor." By the word "malice," it should be understood that, let alone killing human beings and the like, even harboring malice for a single instant toward any living being whatsoever is a falling away from the conduct of awakening. By "falsehood": if, having fully taken up means of deceiving sentient beings, with one's mind fixed on one's own desires one tells lies, this should be understood as a transgression for beginners among those who practice the conduct of the gateway of secret mantra. Then "abandoning the women of others": for those who practice the conduct of secret mantra, in order to realize the culmination of the great seal (mahāmudrā), a woman possessing the perfect marks described in the various tantras, who is not taken by another, is indeed permitted. Accordingly, for those who have obtained the irreversible empowerment, it is not permitted for the sake of the bliss of emission, but for the sake of the unchanging she is indeed to be relied upon. For beginners, even though there is emission, there is no prohibition of their own. Thus neither of these two kinds of yogin should rely on a woman taken by another. If one does rely on her, it becomes a sin through worldly reproach, and by its force one's sacred commitment is broken. "The wealth of others" means that what is owned by any being, down to animals, should not be taken as one's own with the intent to steal, because the Sugata has prohibited it.

Likewise, "liquor" is to be construed with "is not to be drunk." Because it is the root of all faults, one should not drink it to the point of intoxication. Where there is no intoxication, there is no prohibition. So the meaning is that one should not drink until one is intoxicated.

If one asks why these five must also be given up, the text says, "These five, which destroy one's virtue, are the adamantine nooses of sin that bind one to saṃsāra." One who does not fully understand their intrinsic nature and indulges in them brings about ruin, and so they are to be given up. That is why this applies to beginners. For others, who know reality, they are not declared to be faults.

In the passage beginning "Who, at whose time," "who" is the Sugata. "At whose time" means in the teaching of discipline given by that very one. "Who became the teacher of gods and humans" refers to Śākyamuni and the others. "Giving in his name" means giving by naming the word of Śākyamuni and the others. One may wonder how one gives by naming them. "The chief of the manifold" means the chief of all beings, Śākyamuni and the others. "By this command" is to be construed as "by this command of Śākyamuni." "Since it protects from the terror of existence" means that by keeping this command of Śākyamuni in this way, one is protected from the suffering of saṃsāra. So the meaning is: "You too, sons and daughters of good family who practice the conduct of secret mantra, must guard and protect it!" The master gives those pledges, together with their meaning, to the beginner disciples.

After that, if they are eager, he also gives the vow not to engage in what the verse names: "gambling, blameworthy food, evil talk, the religion of spirits (bhūtas) and the religion of the lords of the asuras." These are the things taught by the Vedic teachers of the non-Buddhists (tīrthikas): the practice of scattering offerings to the forefathers and the like, together with regarding this as liberation; likewise gambling; eating the flesh of slaughtered animals; telling stories of men, women, kings, thieves, armies and so on; and sacrifices that involve taking life, and the like.

Likewise, if they are eager, he gives the vow not to commit the five killings named in "killing cattle, children, women, men, and the teachers of gods and humans."

Likewise, if they are eager, he gives the vow not to do the things named in "anger toward friends, the sovereign, the teachers of gods and humans, the Saṅgha, and those who place their trust in one."

Further, he should also give what is stated in "Do not cling to the objects of the senses: these are the twenty-five vows of the Lord of Existence." The sense objects are said to be the cause that brings about ruin.

But has it not been said,
"Union of the two, the taking of life,
taking what is not given, and speaking falsehood:
by indulging in these without giving them up,
one will attain nondual wisdom"?
If one asks, then why must harmful intent, falsehood, another's wife and the rest be given up: although that is true, the statement that one attains nondual wisdom by indulging in the objects of desire is meant for yogins who fully understand intrinsic nature. It is not spoken for yogins who belong among beginners and have a childish nature. For childish yogins, that very thing binds them completely, just as poison, or gold and silver taken as food and drink, when not first subdued by mantra and the like, produce a great mass of suffering. Therefore it is forbidden to those of childish nature, and permitted only to yogins who have attained the immutable and who see all phenomena as like the expanse of the sky.

Thus, for the beginners to whom the twenty-five vows have been given, in order to examine thoroughly the family of those who have received them, the text says, "Placing a single flower, consecrated by the glorious mantra, within the lotus of the hand." The master places in the disciple's hand a flower consecrated with body, speech and mind. That is for the purpose of entering the maṇḍala, and for nothing else. "First circling three times" means that the disciple does so accompanied by the karmavajra. "From the lotus of the hand, the flower should be cast onto the maṇḍala" means that after circling the maṇḍala three times, it should be cast onto the heaps of flowers of the families, or onto the vases and so on, but not onto the colored powders. "The place where the flower falls is surely his family, O lord of men" means that afterward, if one takes a karmamudrā corresponding to the deity of the family where the flower fell, one attains the accomplishment of the karmamudrā. After that, the family deity and so on should also be pointed out.

"Afterward, the seven empowerments and the threefold great supreme are to be given here." This is the sequence of the master's actions. The master, at the eastern gate of the maṇḍala, holding the pride of the principal deity of the maṇḍala, takes up postures matching those of the deities, the right leg extended, the left leg drawn in, seated, and standing, and should empower the disciple with water and the rest. "Seven kinds" are the seven beginning with water, by which children are to be brought in. "Three kinds" are the vase, the secret, and the wisdom-gnosis empowerments, the three worldly, conventional empowerments. "The great supreme" is the transmundane empowerment, which is given immediately after the worldly ones, and not before them.

Now, in order to explain the rite of the seven empowerments, regarding the passage beginning "medicines and fragrant": as for the medicines, one should first take the ingredients designated by "eye" and the rest from the perfume grid of compartments taught in the root tantra. One should take the collection of five medicines as they are found from the middle of the twenty-five compartments, as taught in the tantra.

As it says in the First Buddha (Ādibuddha):

"O king, the fragrances, the armpit substance, and the herb portions:
The medicines that are to be set out in order,
In portions of eye, moon, fire and so on,
In groups of five, beginning with Brahmā:
Brahmā, Viṣṇu, Rudra, and
Īśvarī and the Supreme Mistress,
Śakrāṇī (the consort of Indra), glorious Vārāhī, and
Kumārī, and likewise Carcikā;
Earth, water, and light,
Wind, and likewise Khecarī;
Mother, sister, daughter,
Granddaughter, and likewise mother-in-law;
Brahmin, king, the merchant caste,
The common caste, and likewise the outcaste.
These medicines at all times
Bestow great accomplishment, nourishment, and miraculous powers."

Thus spoke the Tathāgata. Therefore those medicines and fragrances are to be poured into the vases.

"Jaya and Vijaya" refers to the principal vase and the vase for all activities.

"Or earthenware vases by their nature" means this: in general all the vases should be made of the jewel and vajra types. If these are not available, they may also be made of earthen material. However, jewels, grains, and medicines should still be put inside them.

Among the jewels, the four that belong to the castes, beginning with the brahmin, are the principal ones. Likewise, of the vajra type, sapphire, ruby, water crystal, karketana, and emerald are the principal ones. Likewise there are the three metals, namely gold, silver, and copper, and fine sharp iron is another. Likewise the middling jewels:

Pearl, coral, rājāvarta,
Śūla, gems, and tilaka:
These are declared to be of six kinds.

The inferior kinds of jewel:

The kinds called crystal and "life,"
Onghari and blue stone,
And the kinds of green gem:
Whatever of these is found should be taken.

If these are not available, one may instead put in fragrant flowers of five colors.

Further, a green jewel is to be put into the conch that sits on top of Vijaya. A blue jewel is put into the Jaya and Vijaya vases of the east and west. Into the vases of the east and of Agni goes a black one. Into the vases of the south and of Nairṛti goes a red one. Into the vases of the north and of Īśāna goes a white one. Into the vases of the west and of Vāyu goes a yellow one.

Likewise, of the medicines, the first portion goes into the Jaya and Vijaya vases. The second portion goes into the east and Agni. The third portion goes into Yama and the asura direction. The fourth portion goes into the north and Īśāna. The fifth portion goes into the west and Vāyu. In this way those twenty-five are tied into packets and placed in the vases.

"Four, eight" gives the number of vases according to the kind of recipient. For one who is to be made a monk vajra holder there are ten vases; for a novice, six; for a householder, one. Of these, four belong to the novice, and with the two of Jaya and Vijaya they make six. Eight belong to the monk, and with Jaya and Vijaya they make ten. For the householder's empowerment there is one, either Jaya or Vijaya.

The five places at which one empowers with these are: the crown of the head, the right and left shoulders, the right and left elbows, the buttocks, and the thighs.

Then, to purify the family deity, the vajra master consecrates a flower with oṃ sarva tathāgata kula viśodhani svāhā and the student casts it for that purpose. After that, the eyes are opened with oṃ davyi na sa muṅgha ṭa yā mi, and, with the lord of the maṇḍala going in front, the entire maṇḍala and the family deity are to be shown. This is now from the eastern gate.

Then the master urges the student on with his left hand and leads him around the maṇḍala together with the action vajra. He sends him to the northern gate, and, in order to purify the stains of the body merely on the conventional level, the goddesses such as Tārā take water from all the mantra vases and pour it into the conch of Vijaya. Again, the water empowerment is to be conferred on the five places of birth, with the mantra oṃ a oṃ ṛ u li pañca dhātu viśodhani svāhā.

Then one should also empower the five places of birth with the crown, conferring the empowerment with oṃ ā a ī ṝ ū ldṛ pañca tathāgata pariśuddhe svāhā. Then a crown made of jewels, gold, or cloth is to be tied on the head. Since the elements and aggregates are thus thoroughly purified by the two empowerments, the body becomes pure merely on the conventional level. Then the body is to be purified at the eastern station, the face of the Body.

Then, again circling clockwise, one goes to the southern gate in order to purify speech, and confers the empowerment of the silk streamer on the five places of birth. With this mantra, oṃ a ā aṃ aḥ h hā haṃ haḥ ho phreṃ daśa pāramitā paripūraṇe svāhā, a streamer of jewels or gold, or, if these are not available, of flower garlands and the like, is to be tied on.

Then again one empowers the five places of birth with this mantra of the vajra and bell: with oṃ hūṃ hoḥ srurya candra viśodhaka svāhā one places the vajra and bell on the crown of the head and empowers with water from the conch. Having thus purified the speech vajra by the two empowerments, one gives the vajra ring. Circling clockwise, at the face of the eastern gate, in order to purify the mind vajra, one should confer the empowerment of conduct on the five places of birth with this mantra: oṃ a ā e ai ar ā ra o au a la ā la viṣa enti ya viśodhani svāhā.

Then flower garlands are hung on the ears and so forth, and again the five places of birth are empowered with this mantra of the name empowerment: oṃ ha hā ya yā ra rā la va la lā catur brahma vihāra viśodha svāhā. If there are no bracelets, anklets, and the like, the hands and feet should be bound with flower garlands.

Then the empowerment of prediction is: "You are Vajra so-and-so." Having thus purified the mind vajra by the two empowerments of vajra and bell, then, in order to purify wisdom, the student is again brought round from the right to the face of the western gate, and the permission empowerment is conferred on the five places of birth with this mantra: oṃ haṃ kṣadha rmma cakra pra varta ka svāhā. Having conferred the empowerment with this, the vajras of body, speech, mind, and wisdom are to be purified.

Then the text says, "Then the vajra and bell are bestowed, and with supreme compassion the pure Dharma is to be taught." The master calls the disciple, "Vajra So-and-so," and should place the vajra and bell in his hands. Next, "the pure Dharma" is this: it should be taught with the one and a half verses that begin "Of the vajra family, one must surely take life" and end "gives rightly in the maṇḍala." That completes the rite of the seven empowerments.

Then, so that the order of seniority among the vajra brothers and sisters may be known, the vajra master should think of the disciple as blessed by the buddhas and should pronounce the words of the year and the rest: "Here, in the year so-and-so, while the Kalkin king So-and-so is teaching the Dharma, in the month so-and-so, on the day so-and-so, under the planet so-and-so, on the lunar day so-and-so, under the constellation so-and-so, in the yoga so-and-so and the karaṇa so-and-so, in the maṇḍala of the supreme First Buddha (Ādibuddha), or in another maṇḍala, I, the vajra master So-and-so, have given prophecy and authorization to the disciple So-and-so, reaching as far as the abode of Akaniṣṭha. I have done so in order that he himself, as the principal, may bring all sentient beings to the fruit of merit and wisdom, and in order that he may accomplish the worldly siddhis." This is the definitive account of the seven empowerments.

Furthermore, the empowerment taught earlier in other tantras in terms of the purity of the five Tathāgatas serves to introduce childish people. If the water empowerment belongs to Vairocana and the crown to Ratnasambhava, then since all tantras teach that the family deity is the one on whom the flower falls in the maṇḍala, how could that be correct? Therefore, whichever deity the flower falls upon, through the mudrā of that family accomplishment comes to the disciple. This is the definitive rule of the Tathāgatas. The Chanting of the Names also says, "Buddha, sovereign, wearer of the crest." Therefore the one on whom the flower falls becomes the lord of the crown. It is not Ratnasambhava in every case.

Now another purity is to be explained. With the words beginning "One must take the life of the vajra family," the commitments of killing and the rest are given. These are of two kinds, by provisional and by definitive meaning, namely outer and inner. The outer is this: those whom the Blessed One said are to be killed are, definitively, those who commit the five deeds of immediate retribution, those who harm the Buddha's teaching, and those who have broken their samaya. Therefore, with one who commits the five deeds of immediate retribution, one must know what came before and what came after. If someone first commits the deeds of immediate retribution and the like but later no longer does so, he is to be slain for only an instant, as in the case of the Dharma king Aśoka. Apart from this, if those who do not know the defining marks of virtue and non-virtue, or the vision of the superknowledges, carry out the act of killing, they will surely go to hell. Therefore, as long as superknowledge has not been attained in yoga, the mantra practitioner should not perform fierce rites. He may perform only pacifying, enriching, subjugating, summoning, and the like.

In the same way, speaking falsehood, taking what is not given, carrying off another's woman, relying on the samaya substances, consorting with women of caste and outside caste, and giving gifts to the point of giving one's own body: all these are done for the sake of others, not for one's own sake. This is definitive. If someone commits the deeds of the five immediate retributions for as long as he lives, then one should accomplish the wrathful vidyā-mantra taught earlier and slay him through this samādhi of the Blessed One Akṣobhya. This is definitive.

Moreover, one speaks falsehood in order to lead those who have fallen onto wrong paths, not for one's own sake. This is definitive. Likewise, taking what is not given is done to turn beings back from going to the realm of the pretas, not for one's own sake. Likewise, going to another's woman is done to turn beings back from the realm of animals, not for one's own desire. Relying on the samaya of the five nectars and the like serves to destroy clinging to caste. Further, in order to accomplish the action seal, one should not despise women of low caste and the like. Further, for the accumulation of merit one should also give vast gifts. In this way, having accomplished the mantras through the families of the sword, the jewel, the lotus, the wheel, and the curved knife, the capable yogin should do all of these things, and he will not become an object of worldly ridicule. This is the provisional meaning.

Now the definitive meaning will be explained. When embodied beings make the life-breath of their own bodies fall, that is taking life. "The vajra family" means it is to be stopped at the crown of the head. By that taking of life, the yogin's awakening mind is sent upward. This is definitive.

"With the sword, falsehood": in the provisional sense, falsehood is speech that does not abide in truth. In the definitive sense, it is teaching the Dharma to all sentient beings at once, in the sounds and languages of sentient beings. That same speech is the unstruck sound at the heart. This is definitive.

"With the jewel, steal another's wealth": here "another" is Vajrasattva, and his jewel is the wish-fulfilling jewel. Stealing it means the jewel family, that is, the throat, in the eighth position. This is definitive.

"With the supreme lotus family, too, carry off another's woman": here "another's woman" is the Mahāmudrā. Stealing her is carrying off another's woman. Stealing her means being parted by the awakening mind and arriving at the tenth position of the lotus family. This is definitive.

On "Liquor, lamps, buddhas, and all fine objects are to be shown upon the wheel": here liquor is innate joy. "Lamps" means drawing inward the five incompatible substances, those of the ox, the dog, and the rest. The buddhas are the five nectars, and the wheel is the lotus of bliss.

"Should be relied upon" means eating. Through the power of meditative absorption, causing feces, urine and semen not to leak outward: that is the definitive meaning. "Low-caste women and the like" refers to women of the four castes, and "the curved knife" is the secret lotus. "Lotus of space" means: through the meditative absorption free of attachment. "Do not despise" means that at the time of churning in the place of birth, by force of celibate conduct one does not release the bodhicitta outward: that is the definitive meaning.

"Give your own body along with your wealth for the sake of beings;
here you must not hold it back."
This means that, for the sake of the accumulation of merit, disciples offer to the vajra master their own bodies together with their children and wives, and their excellent possessions. Moreover, it is in this way: through these two accumulations, of merit and of moral discipline, the accumulation of wisdom is brought to completion, and through the three accumulations one becomes a perfectly complete buddha. "O sons of noble family," it says. Other than as taught in this way, "even through immeasurable eons there is no buddhahood, so the Victor has declared." That is the meaning of "sons of noble family." It is the teaching of the pure Dharma, the definitive meaning of all the tantras.

Then, in order to state the purification of the empowerments, there is the passage beginning "Water is Tārā." Whatever is here is the water empowerment; through it the five elements, wind and the rest, are purified: this is the unobscured body. Likewise, through the crown empowerment the five aggregates, consciousness and the rest, are purified. Through the empowerment of the hero's silk streamer, for the purpose of fully purifying the ten perfections, there are the ten śaktis, generosity and the rest. Through the vajra and bell empowerment, lalanā and rasanā are purified: they are the unobscured sun and moon. The observance empowerment is the purification of the objects, form and the rest, and of the five sense faculties, the eye and the rest: it is the entry of the divine eye and the rest. The name empowerment is the yoga of entering loving-kindness and the rest; abiding in the four divine abodes, one is at all times purified, free of obscurations such as attachment and aversion. The permission empowerment is the glory of perfect, complete awakening: turning the wheel of Dharma and teaching the Dharma. It is also called "destroying the fear of existence," because it benefits others. "The family of Kālacakra" means the teaching of emptiness that experiences immutable bliss: that is what is certain.

"These seven empowerments that destroy the stains of evil
are rightly bestowed in the maṇḍala."
This means: by the vajra masters to the disciples. That the vajra masters bestow the seven empowerments is the certainty of the tathāgatas.

Now the fruit of the empowerments is to be stated. Regarding "by the force of the virtue of conferring the seven empowerments": here, if one is empowered in this great maṇḍala and the commitments are not impaired, one becomes lord of the seventh. Further, if one actualizes the wheel of the maṇḍala, one attains the seven grounds in this very body. Otherwise, having given up the ten nonvirtues, by the force of virtue one is certain to go to lordship of the seven grounds.

"Conferral of the vase and secret empowerments also
certainly leads into the Immovable and so forth."
Here the conferral of the vase and secret empowerments is the power of moral discipline. "Irreversible" means immovable: the word "immovable" means that, from guarding the lotus, the bodhicitta does not move. "Excellent Intelligence" means that by that cause there is the mind of great bliss, which is without emission.

"Through the higher conferral of the wisdom-gnosis empowerment":
this is the power of the accumulation of moral discipline. "As Mañjughoṣa himself" means the destruction of existence. "Becomes" means going to the Cloud of Dharma; and here the Cloud of Dharma is the descent of great bliss that is able to accomplish the aims of oneself and others. This is the certainty of the tathāgatas.

Therefore, in this way, what is preceded by the two accumulations of merit and moral discipline is the accumulation of wisdom, and that through its power yogis come to the twelve grounds is the certainty of the tathāgatas.

Furthermore, "if through deceit a hundred root downfalls occur" means: through entering the path of the ten nonvirtuous actions after having abided in the empowerment. "This" means this very empowerment; that through practicing it perversely one comes to the suffering of hell is certain.

"If one abides in the seven empowerments and is endowed with good qualities,
the root downfalls will be purified."
This shows the cause by which one is freed from root downfalls. It is like this: if one who abides in the seven empowerments wishes to rectify a root downfall that has occurred, then first the purifying recitation is done as many times as there are deities in the maṇḍala, and it amounts to thirty-six thousand. "Endowed with good qualities" means: if one abides in the vow not to do it again.

"By the strength of a firm observance, for the vase and secret, if one has not prevailed":
this means that if a root downfall occurs for one who abides in the vase and secret empowerments, then one is purified through merit and moral discipline, and not otherwise. "By the strength of a firm observance" means that the vajra master gives an act of penalty to whoever wishes to be purified again, and that person too should accept the penalty and carry it out.

"For the higher ones there is no purification" means this. If those who have received the wisdom-gnosis empowerment incur a root downfall, it is not purified when others act as the condition, that is, when the vajra master or someone else imposes a penalty. Here it is purified only by the complete purification of one's own mind. That is done in this way: through the power of merit and moral discipline one takes oneself in hand, and confessing misdeeds and the like are carried out. Going to a place blessed by the buddhas and bodhisattvas and purifying it oneself is the tradition of the Buddhists.

Furthermore, one may ask: "Why are the earlier ones purified through the power of another as condition, while some with the wisdom-gnosis are not purified through the power of another but through their own purification?" It is like this. In the world too, a wrong a child does while small is done out of ignorance, and so in the world it is a small fault. What is done by those who have grown into adults is done knowingly, and so it is a great fault. In the same way, the seven empowerments, the vase, the secret, and the wisdom-gnosis are distinguished in turn as child, youth and elder. Therefore each earlier one is childlike, unknowing, unclear and unskilled, and each later one is knowing, mature, clear and of the nature of awareness.

"Whoever has incurred a root downfall should be made to enter this maṇḍala again in order to purify it." This teaches the method for purifying root downfalls. First the penalty taught earlier is carried out, and afterward the person is made to enter the maṇḍala. Purification by any other means than this has not been taught. This applies to those who vow not to do it again, and not to those with no intention of restraint afterward. Otherwise it would be like a person seized by poison to whom a physician gives a medicinal drink to counteract it, and who then goes on eating poison as before.

"Having obtained authorization once more, his name as eldest of the assembly becomes that of a junior." This means that an elder who has incurred a root downfall and is made to enter the maṇḍala again is then reckoned anew in the maṇḍala as a junior. This is the ruling of the Tathāgata.

Now, to state the root downfalls, the text says "disturbing the mind of the glorious guru" and so on. In all the tantras the root downfalls are fixed at fourteen, and they should be stated one by one, just as they are. The words "Sons, the root downfalls, the mountain-holder" are a general statement. The meaning is this: if the fourteen are not guarded as taught in the tantra, then just as a palm tree whose crown has been cut off has no chance to grow again and falls, so one falls from the state of Vajradhara.

On "disturbing the mind of the glorious guru": gurus are of two kinds, genuine and not genuine. The genuine one's mind is disturbed for the sake of all sentient beings. The one who is not genuine has his mind disturbed for the sake of his own service and honor. Therefore disturbing the guru's mind, that is, committing the ten non-virtues and the like, or doing any kind of non-virtue toward the guru, becomes a root downfall. It is certain, however, that it is not one with regard to a guru who incurs downfalls for his own ends and himself engages in the ten non-virtues. One incurs this downfall through six factors: knowing that he is a genuine guru; knowing that he will be displeased; an act of body or speech motivated by affliction; its not being for the sake of others; the intention to incur the downfall for one's own ends; and having no intention of purifying it afterward.

"Transgressing his command" is to be joined with "that guru's." Transgressing the command means not heeding what a genuine guru has instructed. This too comes about with the six factors. "Becomes another" means it becomes the second.

"Anger toward brothers is surely the third." Brothers are those cared for by one and the same guru. Anger toward the brothers of others is said to be a small fault. If one does not respectfully accept what such a brother has instructed and taught, and grows angry, that is the third, and it becomes a root downfall. It has the seven factors: the six above, specified by being one's own vajra brother.

On "abandoning loving-kindness": here there are four kinds of abandoning loving-kindness, lesser, middling, great, and greatest. The lesser is like a line drawn in water with a stick: though one is parted from loving-kindness for a single instant, one enters it again. The middling is like a line drawn in sand, quickly destroyed by the wind: one enters loving-kindness again right away. The great is like cracks in the earth being worn away by a stream of rain: one remembers loving-kindness after a little while. The greatest is like hard things such as rock, which, once cracks and the like have appeared, never have the chance to become even again; or like fruit that has ripened and fallen to the ground, which has no chance to cling to the trees again and does not stay there. Abandoning loving-kindness in this way becomes a root downfall, within a span of sixty nāḍikās. It is certain to become the fourth root downfall.

On "abandoning the awakening mind": here it is the seed of the awakening mind. If it is lost, the bliss becomes dim. Therefore, for those who have not attained that very state and wish to become buddhas through the bliss of the two organs, it is the fifth root downfall, called "arrow." Here, with the great wisdom consort, there is the intrinsic nature of the unchanging; there is the intention to take it as merely changing bliss; holding that to be good; secrecy; its not being for the sake of others' bliss; and its not being for the purpose of pills and the like. Possessing the four factors, it becomes the fifth, called "arrow."

"Reviling the philosophical tenets" refers to the Perfection of Wisdom vehicle. To hold that it and the way of secret mantra are different is a root downfall; reviling vehicles other than that is not a root downfall. This too robs one of the common accomplishments.

Furthermore, there are five factors: the Mantra vehicle and the Perfection of Wisdom vehicle being indivisible, speaking against them while abiding in aversion, doing so not for the sake of others, holding this to be right, and having no regret. When these five factors are present, it becomes the sixth root downfall.

On the words "Giving secrets to sentient beings who are unripened": an unripened person is one overwhelmed by the path of the afflictions. If the secrets of Secret Mantra are proclaimed to such a person, he becomes frightened and reviles them, so telling him is a downfall. It is taught that there is no fault in telling those who will come to have devotion, and those who have faith and the fortune for liberation. There are three factors: a person in whom proclaiming the secrets will produce a lack of devotion, the act not being for the sake of taming him, and the secret of great bliss being what is taught. When these three factors are present, it becomes the seventh. The downfall of teaching secrets other than the secret of great bliss destroys mundane accomplishments.

As for "tormenting the body": the body, that is, the five aggregates and so on, has the nature of the Victors of the five families. The elements and sense sources are the goddesses and the bodhisattvas. Tormenting is of two kinds: tormenting the body through heat and cold and through food and drink, and producing torment by striving to kill oneself. If one asks why this is a root downfall: when the aggregates are tormented, bliss does not arise, and because bliss does not arise, one falls away from nondual wisdom. As for striving to kill, it is taught to become a downfall once the intention has been formed. That is why it is a root downfall. There are three factors: knowing that the aggregates and so on belong to the Victors, the goddesses and the bodhisattvas; knowing that by tormenting them one falls away from bliss; and the act not being for the sake of taming others. When these three factors are present, it becomes the eighth downfall.

As for "And lack of faith in the pure Dharma": the pure Dharma is the profound emptiness that utterly abandons all grasping. Not having faith in it and refuting it is a root downfall. Through two factors, the act not being for the sake of taming, and abandoning emptiness while embracing what is accompanied by conceptual elaboration, it becomes the ninth downfall.

As for "deceitful love": deceitful love is hypocrisy. It means not keeping the samayas and so on out of faith, but keeping them in a manner bent on gain and verses of praise, while secretly doing what is unwholesome. Such a person is ruled by deceit itself, and through that first factor alone it becomes the tenth downfall.

As for "Giving the bliss free from names and so on": the nature of the suchness of the body, speech and mind of the tathāgatas, in the way of Secret Mantra, is the unchanging bliss of the moon of bodhicitta, which is beyond all worldly examples. When it is given, if one does not accept it because of doubt and hesitation, and instead conceptualizes it, that is a downfall. There are three factors: its not being changing bliss, its being unchanging bliss beyond anything used as an example, and the act not being for the sake of taming. Through these three factors it is a grave downfall, the eleventh.

"Pure beings" means yogins who possess the yogic disposition. "Speaking of their faults" means doing so out of jealousy, and by that one incurs a downfall. There are three factors: the person possessing the yogic disposition, oneself speaking out of jealousy, and the act not being for the sake of taming others. If one speaks with these three factors present, it becomes the twelfth downfall.

As for "the samaya that has been obtained": there are two aspects, the guarding of samaya and the particulars of what is to be consumed. Guarding here means guarding the four drops, distinguished as the body, speech, mind and wisdom, as explained earlier. Here, however, it refers to the gathering of food and the like, and to what is called "obtained" at the time of the heroes' feast, that is, food and drink that have been found and whose time has come, and the fire practice with women. If one does not accept these, it is a downfall. There are three factors: the time for samaya having arrived, the refusal not being out of deference to others, and the refusal not being for the sake of taming others. When these three factors are present, it becomes the thirteenth downfall.

"Disparaging women, reviling them with mind and speech," is a root downfall. That is, if a yogin abiding in the Vajra Vehicle disparages them while holding that perception, and if he reviles them in speech not for the sake of taming others, it becomes the fourteenth.

Having thus taught the root downfalls to the disciples, the vajra master should confer empowerment through the various kinds of empowerment.

Moreover, in order to make a monk a vajra-holder, outside the maṇḍala, on the ground, one makes five lines four cubits in extent. In the center, on a wooden seat, one makes an eight-petaled lotus two cubits across, in five colors, leaving out the emblem mudrās of the tathāgatas. The monk is seated there, and after that he is surrounded by eight young women untouched by the vajra weapon, beautiful in form, dressed in white and well adorned. Two of the young women act as though handing over the water, the diadem, and the silk streamers and other things to be tied on. Its benefit is taught thus:

When a monk is made a vajra-holder,
he becomes supreme;
by the mere sight of his Dharma robes,
asuras and the like flee beneath the earth.

Further, the empowerment of a novice is said to be done with only two or three of the five lines, placing him on a wooden seat, with four young women acting in the manner of the water and so on. It is said:

A novice who is a vajra-holder,
possessed of the ten trainings,
was declared by Vajradhara
to be a middling master.

For householders, one makes a single line one cubit in size and places them on a wooden seat. This is the master empowerment. It is said:

A householder clad in white
may be made a vajra-holder,
for monks who are vajra-holders
are to be served and honored as befits them.

This is the lowest master. Teaching all the tantras after granting permission is the fixed rule of the tathāgatas.

Furthermore, the pure way is this: the seven empowerments, the vase, and the secret, which are the seventh and eighth grounds and Excellent Intelligence (Sādhumatī), are to be applied to laymen, novices and monks respectively. Through the wisdom-gnosis empowerment one is a senior elder, and because one is thereby prophesied to the Cloud of Dharma (Dharmameghā), this is the prophecy given to the disciples.

Likewise, it is said that this meaning is taught in the Chanting of the Names (Nāmasaṃgīti) by the passage running from "In the three worlds, the single youthful body" up to "liberated, peaceful, peace itself."

Furthermore, the vase, the secret, and the wisdom-gnosis empowerments purify body, speech and mind. They are also given the names of Brahmā, Maheśvara (Śiva) and Viṣṇu, in order to bring in the non-Buddhists (tīrthikas). Thus it is said:

Brahmā is the holder of the vajra of body;
the vajra of speech is the Great Lord, Maheśvara;
the Victor who holds the vajra of mind,
he is Viṣṇu of great miraculous power.

As for the vajra master empowerment, it is likewise said to be taught in the Chanting of the Names by the passage running from "crest" up to "with five locks of hair, a crown of flowers." Now the Blessed One, the Buddha, spoke this as the definitive meaning of all the tantras.

Accordingly, here, monks, novices and householders who have received these empowerments must not depend on others, and must not engage in farming, the sale of weapons, the sale of ink, or any other trade. If they do so, then because they have betrayed the Buddha's word, they will go to the Avīci hell.

Now, in order to teach in detail the rite of the three higher empowerments, beginning with the vase empowerment, the text says "from one up to the years of the directions," that is, ten. Beginning with "one," the king of the path, up until she has completed ten years, she is only to be looked at and touched; she does not give rise to the capacity for any other act, because she has no breasts. Moreover, the completion of ten years is simply the purity of the ten perfections, beginning with generosity. That is to say: as long as the falling of the teeth has not occurred, the element of gnosis is inexhaustible. Once the falling of the teeth has occurred through the vajra, from then on it is the element of space. Therefore, because of the two elements, the young girl's womb does not become impaired. For this reason she is only to be looked at, touched, and worshipped.

"Then one should embrace" means that, beginning with the eleventh year and going up to the twentieth, the practice of embracing is to be done. "The ocean together with the tastes" means four together with six, that is, ten. "Motion and the rest should be shown" means: in the eleventh year, wind; in the twelfth, fire; in the thirteenth, water; in the fourteenth, earth; these are the four. Likewise, in the fifteenth, sound; in the sixteenth, touch; in the seventeenth, taste; in the eighteenth, form; in the nineteenth, smell; in the twentieth, the sphere of phenomena (dharmadhātu).

"Past twenty years, the mudrās of the wrathful, bhūta and asura families are supremely fearsome" means this. Here, beginning from the twenty-first year, for each year in its order, there are Nīlā, Atibalā, Anantavīryā, Jambhī, Mānī, Stambhī, Marīcī, the Impeller, Bhṛkuṭī, Vajraśṛṅkhalā, and the Fierce One: these are the ten goddesses of the wrathful family. Then, from the thirty-first year, there are the eight of the bhūta family: Carcikā, Vārāhī, Raudrī, Aindrī, Brahmāṇī, Mahālakṣmī, Kaumārī and Vaiṣṇavī. Likewise, from the thirty-ninth year, the eight of the asura family are: the Dog-Faced, the Sow-Faced, the Tiger-Faced, the Jackal-Faced, the Garuḍa-Faced, the Owl-Faced, the Vulture-Faced, and the Raven-Faced. Since these are goddesses who produce supreme fear, they are to be avoided on the occasion of empowerment.

"Six and four for the purpose of empowerment" refers to the ten, from the element of fire up to the element of the sphere of phenomena. The element of wind is not to be taken, because the menstrual blood does not yet occur; this is definitive. "Another purpose, for meditation" refers merely to the purpose of showing bliss, from the twenty-first year up to the forty-sixth.

Here the three higher empowerments have two purposes. The first is to bring sentient beings in so that they fully understand the path, and to empower them to hear the tantra. The second is to make them expounders for others and to confer on them the rank of great master.

To begin with, then, regarding the empowerments: having abandoned the fault of confusion, one should confer empowerment on the disciple. On the line "Whoever first touches the breast of the glorious Prajñā, that here is the vase empowerment": first, whoever has fully matured a young woman of twelve years offers her to the guru, and then makes supplication to the guru. After that, the guru, pleased, has him touch, in accordance with worldly convention, the breast of his own mudrā. That is the vase empowerment.

On the line "Tasting the hare-bearer and the gazing: from the secret, this becomes the secret empowerment": next, worship is offered to the secret consort; the disciple is given the nectar and is shown the bhaga of the mudrā. That is the secret empowerment.

On the line "In the wisdom-gnosis empowerment, the limbs and faces are purified by the families of the Victors": on the faces of the action mudrā (karmamudrā), one makes them endowed with the five families by means of the seed syllables beginning with oṃ. Then one requests the Victors for empowerment, as it is said: "The guru gives the mudrā to the disciple." This concerns holding hands, embracing in pairs, and so forth. The guru brings the Victors, Vajrasattva and the rest, under control by making this request: "Just as for these sentient beings who are difficult to tame, this is for the sake of meditation, of hearing all the tantras, of teaching all the tantras to my own disciples, of drawing maṇḍalas and so forth, and not otherwise for the sake of merely experiencing worldly bliss." So he makes the request, and brings them under control with the mind. This is the definitive account of the empowerment by which the disciple is first brought in.

Likewise, the bliss of the seed whose nature is two qualities is the master empowerment, through the purity of the gods' laughter. The secret empowerment has the nature of three qualities, because it is the purity of gazing. Holding hands is through the purity of the nature of four qualities. Embracing in pairs is the bliss of the seed through the nature of five qualities, through the complete purity of the gods. Therefore, for the sake of bringing sentient beings in by way of worldly convention, the empowerments are definitely fourfold.

Then, having come to understand the provisional and definitive meaning of the tantras, and in order to become a great lord, the student should offer the ten vidyās to the guru in their proper order. As for these vidyās, the Blessed One said in the root tantra:

A niece, a daughter,
a sister, likewise a mother,
whoever is one's wife's mother,
likewise the wife of a maternal uncle,
the wife of a paternal uncle,
likewise the father's sister,
one's own mother's younger sister,
and one's own wife of supreme form:
Tārā first and Dharmadhātu last,
the ten vidyās born of one's own family.
The householder who desires liberation
offers them to the guru at the time of empowerment.
If, to protect the family,
he does not offer the vidyā to the guru,
then empowerment is not to be given.
For monks and novices,
and for others such as śūdras,
so that they may be empowered in the maṇḍala,
the guru gives them, O lord of men.
In order beginning with wind: a śūdra woman,
a kṣatriya woman, likewise a brahmin woman,
a vaiśya woman, a low-caste woman, a fisherwoman,
a dancer, likewise a dyer woman,
a leatherworker woman and a caṇḍāla woman:
the ten sky-born of the dharmadhātu,
bestowers of the fruits of enjoyment and liberation.
The great vidyā has proclaimed:
these bestow the fruits of enjoyment and liberation.

In the order thus spoken, they are the ten perfections, the ten masteries, the ten grounds and the ten strengths; that is, they are the purification of these. Likewise, the Litany of the Names of Mañjuśrī also teaches this, from the line "Buddha, great Vairocana" up to "the tenfold lord, the great one."

Therefore, for one who especially wishes to become a master of the great maṇḍala, offering the ten mudrās is the established rule of the Tathāgata. Having thus offered the ten mudrās to the guru, the student should make the supplication, "Then please grant permission," saying, "O Blessed One, since you have attained all the perfections, what shall I now do?" And the master in turn should instruct him: "With your own wife, or with any one of these ten, first make the supplication together with her." For a novice or a monk with much conceptual thought, it is explained that this should be done with a caṇḍāla woman.

Then the guru, having accepted them, has them make a maṇḍala offering of gold and the like, and the two should make this supplication:

Homage to you, Kālacakra,
who have wholly abandoned all obscurations.
Homage to you, the body of wisdom
filled with the supreme unchanging.
Emptiness and compassion inseparable,
the mind of awakening that never changes:
Protector, it is fitting that today
you kindly grant me that empowerment.
Together with children, wife and the rest,
I am wholly your servant.
Until I reach the essence of awakening,
I shall be the disciple of no other.

On seeing the student who has made this supplication, Śabdavajrā is adorned with every ornament, made naked and made to embrace; for a householder, this is done by his own mother-in-law. Then the nine yoginīs are made naked, with hair unbound, holding curved knives and skull cups: in the east Tārā, in the south Pāṇḍarā, in the west Locanā, in the north Māmakī, in the southeast Sparśavajrā, in the southwest Vajrā, in the northwest Gandhavajrā, in the northeast Rūpavajrā, and in the center the fire session is to be performed.

Thus the earlier empowerments are the seven beginning with water. The empowerment to be received after these is performed with any one ordinary action seal. The empowerment of the guru above gurus is performed with the ten mudrās mentioned above, the śūdra woman and the rest. There too it is said:

For monks, novices
and householders, these three,
in order: ten mudrās, six,
and one, which is for householders.

Then, for the vase empowerment, a mudrā should be offered. Concerning this, the line "Like refined gold in color, adorned with ornaments, a fine girl twelve years of age" shows that the action seal ranges from twelve years of age up to a limit of twenty. "Having the nature of wisdom and method" means the deity yoga about to be explained. "With one's own vajra jewel" means with the secret vajra. "Desire with passion" means: since the mudrā has first been with menstrual flow, on the night her menses has come, begin with that young woman. "Knowing the student to be pure" refers to the empowerment of becoming the guru above gurus. "With one's own seed vajra, cast it into the mouth" is for the sake of tasting the bodhicitta. "Afterwards, give one's own mudrā again" means that the guru should hand over either the one he has made his own or the one who is the student's own.

Further, here at the time of the vase empowerment, the guru should touch the breast of whoever is the student's wisdom consort. In the secret empowerment, the student should place the master's vajra in the mouth of the wisdom consort. The faces of both the student and the secret consort should be covered, and the guru should kiss the nose of the wisdom consort. After that, for the wisdom-gnosis empowerment, the guru entrusts to the student the student's own mudrā so that he may attain the state of a great master. That is, he gives whichever of the ten mudrās the student desires, and from two water-clock periods past midnight until sunrise it should be experienced.

Then, having performed the activities of the feast gathering, the guru should give the vow to the student: "What I have said here, that the moment of the innate arises at the end of the special joy, is conventional. Ultimately, the ultimate that is unchanging is precisely that unchanging bliss." Thus it is taught to yogins.

Therefore you must guard this great bliss. Likewise, the First Buddha (Ādibuddha) also says:

In union with the action seal,
And clinging to the wisdom seal,
With an observance that is utterly firm,
Guard the great bliss, the awakening mind.
Placing the male organ well within the bhaga,
Without emitting the awakening mind,
Meditate on the entire world-system
As the forms of buddhas.
By guarding this very thing,
One becomes a buddha in this very life.
Endowed with merit and wisdom,
The collection of moral discipline is perfectly complete;
Attaining the ten perfections,
The perfect buddhas of the three times have gone this way.
This itself is the perfect buddha;
It turns the wheel of Dharma.
Even the lord of the three worlds
Is found nowhere apart from this wisdom.
Emptiness and compassion inseparable:
In order fully to accomplish your own and others' aims,
If, O son, you keep it,
You, the very self of the vow of all protectors,
Will be blessed by all the buddhas,
And then attain perfect awakening.
But if you exhaust desire
And do not keep the vow
You took before the Lord,
Then you will go to the Wailing Hell.
If, through a lesser mind,
The awakening mind leaks into the womb,
Then, thinking of the seal, with your own tongue
Take it from outside her lotus.

With these words spoken by the Sugata, the extensive vow of the master is given to the disciple. The disciple, for his part, offers a gift to the guru, makes a maṇḍala offering to the guru, and makes this request: "O Blessed One, please grant me the kindness of the detailed classification of the empowerments."

Then the guru calls the disciple and says: "Man, listen! I shall teach you the characteristics of the sixteen joys, the bliss shared by all sentient beings. This, moreover, is realized through a seal endowed with good qualities, not through any other."

As for the seals of bad character that are to be avoided: "a frightened and deluded mind" means that she is frightened and afraid of the guru. Grasping the meaning wrongly is "deluded." "Crooked" means acting deceitfully. "Empowered by another" means attached to another man. "Having an inner ailment" means having a chronic illness. "With a child" means that, having given birth, her womb is wide, so that bliss does not arise and the bliss is diminished. "Angry" means short-tempered. "Arrogant" means proud on account of her caste and so forth. Desiring payment out of attachment to goods means a prostitute. "Untruthful" means speaking lies. "Quarrelsome" means always fighting. "Impure" means lacking the defining marks. "Deficient in limbs" means lacking feet, hands, eyes, and so on. A seal who has these faults is to be avoided in the context of the wisdom-gnosis empowerment, but not avoided in other circumstances, for that is declared to be a root downfall. Therefore it says: "This one is to be avoided by the holy guru, O lord of men."

"Respectful toward the buddhas and the guru, holding the commitments, he should worship as was stated before." This means: as for the characteristics of the seal, she is under twenty years of age, of noble family, and free of anger, and she should be worshiped with many kinds of offerings; that is the intent. From this the characteristics of the sixteen joys arise just as they are.

On the line, "Beings stir up desire in my mind and reach completion": here, the stirring of desire when beings look with their eyes is the first joy, and "completion" is the fifth lunar day. Then the stage of melting through laughter is the stage at which the awakening mind residing at the forehead melts. It includes everything up to embracing the wisdom woman; the lunar days are those up to the tenth, and the joy is the second; it is the second Full day.

"Melting from the hare-bearer, from the supreme body the excellent drop flows, full and blazing." The hare-bearer (the moon) is the awakening mind, and "melting" means moving. From where? "The supreme body" is stated: its flowing from the crown of the head at the stage of union, at the time called "blazing," is the third joy; it is the fifteenth lunar day.

"Invited by oṃ ḍa, the three drops that have flowed grant supreme bliss at the end." Thus oṃ is the stage at which the drop flows. Moreover, at the end of the flowing of the drops of body, speech, and mind, the sole innate bliss is the fourth joy; it should be understood as having the nature of the sixteen digits.

Accordingly, from the first lunar day onward, in each set of five days, the natures of space, wind, fire, water, and earth, under the names Joyful, Good, Victorious, Empty, and Full, are characterized by the vowels and letters called a i ṛ u ḷ, a e, a ra o a la, and ha ya ra va la. Thus the Full of the fifth day is joy. The Full of the tenth day is supreme joy. The Full of the full moon is the joy free of joy. The sixteenth digit is innate joy; it is the perfect gathering, the perfect assembly, the equalizing, the union, and the combining of the elements of all the Full days. For the awakening mind in its attachment is the bright fortnight, and its freedom from attachment is the dark fortnight. Likewise, the Litany of Names says:

Free of attachment, primal, great in attachment,
Light blazing in many colors,
Perfect buddha seated in vajra posture,
Holder of the Dharma that the buddhas proclaim.

And further:

Possessing all aspects, yet without aspect,
Bearing the drop of the sixteen halves of halves,
Without parts, beyond all reckoning,
Holding the summit of the fourth meditative concentration.

This is the Blessed One's definitive teaching.

Now I shall explain the fourfold unions within each of the sixteen joys. On the passage beginning "Desire and": here, for all sentient beings, divided into waking, dream, deep sleep, and the fourth state, there are the unions of body, speech, mind, and gnosis. These are also the sixteen divisions of joy and the rest. Among them, "desire" is the joy of body, "joy" is the joy of speech, "trembling" is the joy of mind, and "immutable" is the joy of gnosis. Therefore these four unions are called "one," the single joy, the first. Likewise, "complete" is the supreme joy of body, "supreme joy" is the supreme joy of speech, "birth" is the supreme joy of mind, and "joyful power" is the supreme joy of gnosis. "Of the second" is said because it is the second, supreme joy. "Blazing and" is the joy free of joy of body, "joy free of joy" is the joy free of joy of speech, and "victory" is the joy free of joy of mind.

"Drop" is the joy of cessation of wisdom; being the third joy, it is the third combination. "oṃ ḍa" is the innate of body. "Innate joy" is the innate joy of speech. "Two" is the innate joy of mind. "nāda" is the innate joy of wisdom. Thus, by the division into the emanation body, the enjoyment body, the Dharma body and the essential-nature body, each of the four, body, speech, mind and wisdom, should be understood as fourfold, and so there come to be sixteen joys. It is certain that at the end of the sixteen joys the innate joy arises.

Now, in order to state the purity of the action seal (karmamudrā), "mother" and so on is mentioned. In all the tantras, the seals such as "mother" are taught in common so that yogis may be attached to them. That, however, is for those who have made the awakening mind stable, not for others. In other texts, the women of worldly convention are to be regarded as mother and so on according to the different occasions. Here, if the yogi thinks of her with the first mind, she becomes the mother. At the time of touching she is imagined as the sister, because she gives a share. At the time the vajra has entered she is imagined as the daughter, because she yields qualities. Joy accompanied by compassion: since bliss trickles, she is imagined as the niece, because great compassion stirs. When the drop falls she is imagined as the wife, because bliss arises from oneself.

"Free of passion for the yoginī, another caste": when the awakening mind trickles, the awakening mind is then free of passion, and so one looks elsewhere and does not touch her. "Other" means she is imagined as of the caṇḍāla caste.

As for "earth, water, fire, wind and space: the state of cessation," here, when the awakening mind stirs in the continuum of any embodied being, first the earth element is disturbed, then water is disturbed, then fire is disturbed, then wind is disturbed, then the space element is disturbed, and then emission occurs. "These six are the yoga seals": thus the right and left maṇḍalas are made five each, and at the time of emission the mother and the rest should be known as purified. These very things, the wheel, the earrings, the jewel, the bracelets, the girdle and the ash, through the purity of the six families, are made to adorn the yoginīs as signs. Likewise the six perfections, generosity and the rest, are also ascertained as purified.

Now, in order to state the threefold seal of body and so on, "in the lotus" and so on is mentioned. In all the tantras, in order to lead others, the forehead is the seal of body, the throat is the seal of speech, and the heart is the seal of mind. In the definitive meaning, "the vajra entered into the lotus" means being always fit for vajra activity; that is the seal of body. "Wind into fire" is the life wind entering the central channel; that is the seal of speech. The drop not falling is the seal of mind. "The seals of body, speech and mind" are those just taught. "These three yogas" means that what has been taught as the three seals is gathered, just as it is, into the central channel. "Taught" means taught to the disciples.

After that comes "passion and passionlessness, beginning and end." Passion is the white fortnight; this refers to its end. Passionlessness is the dark fortnight; this refers to its beginning. What lies at the end of these is to be known as the place of the sixteenth digit, and it is spoken of as such for as long as there is no trickling. There, "bestowing supreme joy" means bestowing every excellence. "The fourth, traversed by yogis" means that this fourth is to be traversed by the six-limbed yoga of completion of the yogis, and not otherwise. "Seal" means that in all the earlier and later tantras the supramundane seal is to be realized through the six limbs. "Supreme mother" is so called because she brings all embodied beings to accomplish nondual wisdom. "Becomes tenfold" refers to the ten perfections, generosity and the rest, through the division into smoke and so on. "Of the glorious guru" means that this fourth, the source of all qualities, should be realized from the mouth of the guru Vajrasattva. Furthermore, whatever is the fourth is called the mother whose property is essential nature; this is the definitive meaning.

Having thus properly taught the disciple the characteristics of the empowerment and of the seal, one gives the injunction, "Perform a feast gathering," and invites the thirty-six family goddesses. On that very day, ten young girls not wounded by the arrow of the vajra are satisfied with milk porridge, sweet foods and the like, offered gifts, and sent away. Then, when the seventh period of the water clock arrives, the twofold Saṅgha of monks headed by the Buddha is invited, pleased with blameless sweet food, offered gifts, and the noble Saṅgha is requested to return to the monastery.

Then, for the rite of the feast gathering, in a house with no people and no openings, one should arrange the places of the heroes and of the sovereign heroines. Here, in accordance with one's means, the chief guru, embodiment of wisdom and skillful means, is requested to sit in the center. At the four quarters are the four yoginīs, and in the center a caṇḍāla woman. In the east is a śūdra woman, in the south a kṣatriya woman, in the west a vaiśya woman, and in the north a brahmin woman. They embody the five lords of the gathering. Their seats are human skin, dog skin, horse skin, cow skin and elephant skin.

Then, in the intermediate directions, the two: in Īśāna, the northeast, the vessel of urine; in Vāyu, the northwest, the vessel of excrement; in Nairṛti, the southwest, the vessel of blood; and in Agni, the southeast, the vessel of brains. Then the second, the place of the mind wheel, encircles this. To its east is the staff-maker woman, to the south the tree-climber woman, to the north the jewel-maker woman, to the west the fierce-eyed woman, in the southeast the garland-maker woman, in the southwest the liquor-seller woman, in the northeast the goldsmith woman, and in the northwest the garland-stringer woman. These are the eight yoginīs.

Their seats, in the same order, are: a sheepskin, the skin of a herder's dog, a camel skin, a lion skin, a goatskin, a deerskin, a donkey skin, and a pigskin.

Then, for the third, which surrounds the speech wheel on all sides: in the east is the butcher woman, in the southeast the potter woman, in the south the cook woman, in the southwest the prostitute, in the west the churning woman, in the northwest the fisherwoman, in the north the astrologer woman, and in the northeast the dyer woman. These are the eight yoginīs. Their seats, in the same order, are: the skin of a makara, the tail of a cowrie creature, the tail of a frog, a fish skin, the skin of the child-killing makara (śiśumāra), a frog skin, the shell of a tortoise, and a conch.

Then, for the fourth, of the body maṇḍala: in the east is the blacksmith woman, in the south the lac-worker woman, in the west the cotton-worker woman, in the north the sesame-oil woman, in the southeast the bamboo-worker woman, in the southwest the woodworker woman, in the northwest the leatherworker woman, and in the northeast the barber woman. These are the eight yoginīs. Their seats are: a rhinoceros skin, a tiger skin, a bear skin, a mongoose skin, a yak skin, a jackal skin, an otter skin, and a cat skin.

Then, for the fifth, which is surrounded on all sides by charnel grounds: in the east is the barbarian woman (mlecchī), in the south the god-devouring woman, in the west the caṇḍāla woman, in the north the Persian woman, in the southeast the woman of mad ways, in the southwest the corpse-burner woman, in the northwest the cowherd woman, and in the northeast the mountain-dweller woman (śabarī). These are the eight. Their seats are: a snakeskin, a cat skin, the skin of a white-head, a monkey skin, a hare skin, the skin of a hunting dog, the skin of an eśuka, and a lizard skin.

If these skins are not available, then from the east to the southeast use dog skins, from the south to the southwest horse skins, from the west to the northwest elephant skins, in the north and the northeast cow skins, and in the center a human skin.

Having offered the seats in this way, one requests the yoginīs to be seated, and these skins too are to be offered. Likewise the plates: in the east and the southeast, mother-of-pearl shells; in the south and the southwest, coconut shells; in the west and the northwest, earthenware bowls; in the north and the northeast, human skulls; and in the center, a wooden vessel. Then one offers the ten kinds, liquor and so on, food and drink, lamps and so on, as is being explained. Then, having made a maṇḍala offering, one gives water for the guest offering and so forth. The disciple, together with the action vajra, thus gathers the offering substances. This is the determination of the place and the family of the feast gathering. Then performing the practice of union (maithuna) is the determination of the Tathāgata.

"And the seventh, the worldly conventional empowerment" means from the water empowerment through the authorization. "The third" means the vase, the secret, and the wisdom-gnosis empowerments. Hence there are ten. As for "the fourth: the fourth is again likewise," since there are the vase, the secret, the wisdom-gnosis, and that which is to be given after it, it is the fourth; therefore it is called the fourth, the supramundane empowerment.

As for the statement "The fourth does not become the third, the wisdom-gnosis empowerment," if one asks why, it is because it is spoken of as "the fourth," distinct from the third. "The fourth is wisdom-gnosis" means that it is the great wisdom-gnosis. As for "because it is stated 'again likewise,'" just as the third relies on one endowed with breasts and hair, this fourth also again relies on the Great Mudrā. Or else, when one wishes to complete the fourth after the third has been completed, the earlier third and the rest are repeated once more, and after that the authorization is to be given; this is the meaning of "because it is stated." Therefore the third itself is not the fourth.

This is also stated by the Blessed One in this very Abridged Tantra:

"Furthermore, the tasting of nectar and the gazing,
and from union with the consort, in the fourth moment the nectar moves and the bodhicitta melts."

Here, concerning "the consort" and so on, there are the gazing, the laughing, the holding of hands, and what is called embracing two by two; and there are the four kinds of empowerment, namely the master, the secret, the wisdom-gnosis, and "the fourth is again likewise." Likewise in the Guhyasamāja and other tantras, the empowerments of the vase and so on are spoken of as the master empowerment; that is conventional, not reality. Moreover, because it is said,

"Having conferred empowerment, explain reality,"

the fourfold laughing, gazing, holding hands, and union two by two does not become nondual. Here reality is nondual, and what arises by way of the action mudrā does not become nondual gnosis. Therefore touching the breasts of the consort is the vase empowerment. Further, experiencing the nectar and gazing is the secret empowerment. What is spoken of as wisdom-gnosis, the nectar moving and the bodhicitta melting in the fourth moment, serves to introduce the childish. Therefore it says:

"All these are certainly worldly, taught as the cause of the path of supreme compassion."

Further, concerning the supramundane empowerment, it says:

"Union with the excellent mudrā of the supreme Victor is the supramundane empowerment."

The supreme Victors are Akṣobhya and the others, and their lord is Vajrasattva. His excellent mudrā is the Perfection of Wisdom endowed with the supreme of all aspects, who gives birth to all the buddhas of the three times. Absorption in union with her is called the supramundane empowerment. Of that supramundane empowerment it is said that the action mudrā and the imagined gnosis mudrā are abandoned, because the mind, being stained, does not attain stability through them.

"As the cause of perfect awakening" means for the sake of the vajra-like body adorned with the great bliss of nonduality, not for the awakening of śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas. "Supreme Victor" means that which is other than the śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas, and that is the very nature of Akṣobhya and the others. "Mother" means she who gives birth to Akṣobhya and the others.

That very one is the excellent mudrā; the meaning is that she should be meditated upon as the cause of perfect awakening. She uproots every affliction entirely, makes one unmoving, makes one unchanging, and destroys all the pitch-black darkness of ignorance. Furthermore, she pervades everything like space; this is called "abiding freely within beings." Again, because she abides in all beings without being stained, like space, she is called "equal to space." Because she alone is what those who desire liberation should seek, it says "traversed by yogis."

Furthermore, "abiding in the body" means that she abides in the bodies of all embodied beings as the four drops and so on. As for "the splendor of gnosis": when the four drops and so on that abide in this way are melted and burned by the fire described as "arisen from space, self-arisen," it is called "stealing away the sins of existence." The sin of existence is the outward emission of the four drops, and cutting off further emission by the fire arisen from space, self-arisen, is called "stealing away sin."

"Known by Kālacakra" means that it is because Kālacakra understands the yoga.

"Merely an appearance of mind" means that whenever things are seen like smoke and so on, they should be understood as appearances of the transformed mind. Therefore it is also said, "born from one's own mind." Furthermore, all things should be realized and known as like the eight or the twelve examples of illusion; this applies at the times of the modes of conduct and so on. At other times, the meaning is, they should be understood otherwise.

As for "On whom the Victor's heirs and the buddhas rely, the lord of yogis should rely": the Perfection of Wisdom, which transcends worldly examples, is what the Victor's heirs, that is, the bodhisattvas of the three times, and the buddhas of the three times rely upon and honor. The instruction is: "You too, lord of yogis, rely on it with every effort at all times."

"Through the full spread of the light of gnosis" means when the fire of gnosis has fully blazed up. "The hosts of māras with their domains, without remainder" are the four, the divine-son māra and the rest. In the definitive meaning, the outward ejection of the four drops is to be understood as the māras, and those māras are burned by the fire of Brahmā, arisen from space, self-arisen. It is not taught that a fabricated, imagined fire is able to burn them.

"That which is equal to desire and the rest" is the bliss of the unchanging drop, beside which the bliss of the action seal and the wisdom seal is inferior. "To the yogi" means to the yogi who has entered union with the great wisdom consort. "Grants" means it comes to be attained. "By the yogi in years" means that in three years and one and a half months the movement of the winds is cut off and the unchanging is granted.

To the thought, "What is that wisdom-gnosis like?" the text states: "That wisdom-gnosis is the mind that becomes tenfold; that is appearance." Here wisdom-gnosis is the apprehending mind, and "becoming tenfold" refers to what is apprehended: smoke and the rest. "Appearance" is the appearing to the apprehending mind as smoke and the rest.

"Like stainless moonlight" means this: when the mind has been freed of the stain of conceptual thought and abides like space, there are the signs, the appearance of space and so on, inseparable like the moon and its light; or else it is the bliss that is indivisible like the moon and its light. "Whoever has entered this empowerment" means entering this empowerment through which such things are realized and come to arise. That is, it is entering the six-branch yoga that turns back from apprehended objects, described as:

Withdrawal and meditative concentration,
breath control, and likewise retention,
recollection, and samādhi.

By its power there then arises the non-emitting coemergent called "the unchanging," the bliss of inseparable emptiness and compassion, the coemergent free of all emission. That very thing which abides neither in emptiness nor in compassion is also called "the fourth." Furthermore, childhood, youth, and old age serve as examples of a bliss that changes and is interrupted, but the fourth transcends the examples of the three worlds. It is free from the elaborations of the three realms.

As for "He in whose mouth and heart this face of the Buddha abides": this is the speech of the Buddha, that is, this gnosis of speech. "Abiding in the heart" means meditated upon and experienced. "Abiding in speech" means that the master teaches it well to the disciples. Whoever experiences and teaches it at all times is called "that glorious guru," meaning that he is equal to Vajrasattva.

Now, in order to describe entry into the appearance of one's own mind, the passage beginning "space" is given. In general, the cultivation of practice is of two kinds: that of the Perfection Vehicle and that of the Mantra Vehicle. The cultivation of practice in the Perfection Vehicle is without a covering. The Mantra Vehicle is that which performs the yoga of space; this is taught by "all of space" and so on, and one gazes at the signs, smoke and the rest, in space.

"With unblinking eyes he fully enters the vajra path": the unblinking eye is the sign of the divine eye, and the vajra path is the entry of the life-wind into the avadhūtī, the central channel. Therefore it says, "All of space: he fully enters the vajra path of mind."

"In emptiness, smoke, mirage, clear and stainless fireflies, the lamp itself": these the yogi comes to see. Then he sees something like a cloudless sky. From that space comes what is described as "Arisen from space, self-arisen, the great fire of wisdom-gnosis": in that very cloudless sky one sees something like a blaze. Likewise, "Great light that illuminates, the light of gnosis, radiant" is the appearance of the moon; "lamp of beings" is the appearance of the sun; "lamp of gnosis" is the appearance of Rāhu; "great splendor, clear light" is the appearance of lightning; and "supreme mantra, lord of mantras, king of vidyā-mantras" is the appearance as a drop. These are the ten signs. They are also taught in the Guhyasamāja and in the Chanting of the Names.

After that comes "The king of secret mantras accomplishes the great aim." This resembles the supreme of all aspects, such as a vase, a cloth, and so on. "In its center" means in the center of that drop. "The image of the Buddha, utterly free of objects": this is because no substantial nature is imagined in it. That very image is the enjoyment body, which does not long for the five qualities of sense pleasure. By that yoga of form there is also the unstruck sound. Seeing those forms is the emanation body, because it appears as form. What appears as the unstruck sound is the enjoyment body. That is the meaning.

Further: "With one-pointed yoga, one should gaze at the cloudless sky with unblinking eyes." Those who wish to see the signs should turn their backs to the sun in the morning or the afternoon, and should not do it in any way that would harm the eyes. Of those who turn inward it says: "until one definitely sees a black line, stainless and clear, in the channel of time." The channel of time is the avadhūtī. In its center one sees a black line about the width of a single hair, unmixed with any other stain, endowed with five rays of light, because it is the nature of the five Sugatas.

There, "the form of the Omniscient One" means that the three realms without exception are the form of the Buddha, seen as the form of great bliss. "Like the sun in water, free of turbidity": this is because, being the body of great bliss, it has abandoned all afflictions without exception. "Of various colors" means that by its power one sees various colors, such as that of smoke and so on.

"Endowed with all aspects, free of objects, it is one's own mind and not the mind of another." This means that although one thus sees all aspects, such as vases and chariots, all endowed as what is called the body of the Buddha, it is whoever practices yoga who sees them, through being endowed with that yoga; others who lack yoga do not see them. Or else, as for seeing forms and so on while abiding in the samādhi of emptiness: "other" would be seeing entities such as a self, which is an extreme, whereas this is called seeing the appearance of oneself. Or else, nothing other is seen, which means that one realizes one's own mind by oneself.

Here, first, with the fleshly eye one sees the appearance of one's own mind, something like a Tathāgata. Then, with the divine eye and the others, it is called knowing or seeing one's own mind. Therefore in the Dharmasaṃgīti too the Blessed One (the Buddha) taught five eyes. In order, they are the fleshly eye, the divine eye, the buddha eye, the wisdom eye, and the gnosis eye. These arise by the power of samādhi. After that, through the nature of omniscience, there is not even an atom of anything that is not known and not seen.

To increase great bliss, breath control (prāṇāyāma) is taught by the line "Having seen the form, each day, at the proper time, one should stop the life wind." Breath control is to be learned from the guru's oral instructions. This line teaches the stopping of the wind. Its limit is stated: "until the mind definitely sees one's own body surrounded by a wheel of light." This means that when one sees in one's own awareness something like a whirling firebrand wheel, one should give it up. Otherwise one will die an untimely death.

To increase bliss at the time of breath control, the text says, up to the word jāgāna:

"Having set the victory banner firmly in place,
with a lovely and radiant goddess
one should enter fully into union with her;
great bliss will surely increase.
If one is practiced in withdrawal,
that is not to be done;
it is said that it becomes a cause of downfall."

That very instruction is to be received from the holy guru. First a heroes' feast or a great gaṇacakra offering is to be held, and a twenty-year-old secret consort is first to be ripened. Having offered her to the master, one should come to realize it. This is the Tathāgata's rule. Any disciple who, apart from this, seeks to realize it by mere words falls into downfall, and accomplishment will not arise. Māras and others will also create obstacles.

"For those who strive, after six months, freedom from touch." This means that if one continues without interruption for half a year, one is free of touch, that is, free of what arises from the joining of the two organs; it is no longer needed. "Equal bliss" means that one also remains unchanging. "By that it becomes the path" means that through the samādhi so described it becomes the path of nondual gnosis. "For those who strive" refers to beginners who practice celibacy.

After that, the characteristic of complete awakening in a single instant is stated in the line "The end of passion and dispassion, and so on." There, passion is the bright fortnight, and its end is the sixteenth digit. Dispassion is the dark fortnight. Abiding at its beginning, the very entry into the dark fortnight, is the characteristic of the time of complete awakening. "Instant" here is the breath, and "multiplying the count" means the count multiplied when, in the instants of bliss, the breath is held still three thousand six hundred times. From the tip of the vajra jewel up to the secret place there are three thousand six hundred. Then, at the emanation, the Dharma, the enjoyment, the great bliss, and the uṣṇīṣa, one comes to four grounds, six, eight, and ten grounds, and at the uṣṇīṣa twelve grounds, because the life wind is exhausted without remainder.

Therefore "in the middle of the blazing Oḍḍa" means abiding at the secret place. "Joy of cessation, the coemergent" refers to the navel. "In the middle of sleep and the Victor" refers to the heart. "In the middle of knowing and consciousness" refers to the throat. Therefore "the coming of the forms of the drops of body, speech, mind, and gnosis" means this: having separated it from the union of the two and so on, the bliss of the drop of the awakening mind that has come into the jewel is to be increased; that is, it is to be sent upward. "Through the guru's definitive power" means that it increases through the stopping of the life wind described before; that is, through the guru's power. The increase goes up to the forehead.

"Having conquered the afflictions and māras, one abides in the state of the Victor." The movement of the right and the left is the māra, and the stopping of that movement is called "conquering." "Abides in the state of the Victor" means when one abides in emptiness. "In years" refers to the yoga of three years and one and a half months. Two million, increased by four ten-millions and three hundred thousand, are to be known as the breaths in the central channel of a person with a lifespan of a hundred years; that is why it says "years." The definitive point, therefore, is that through the practice of the vase breath (kumbhaka) the moment of great bliss is to be generated and increased.

Now, in order to state the characteristics of form, the text says "earth" and so on. Here things appear as emptiness because they do not exist as substances. "Though it appears in the form of earth" refers to the ten forms and so on, or else to whatever appears at all; the intent is that the entities earth, water, fire and wind do not exist as separate things. Therefore the form of moist water is a mirage, not water. "Though it appears in the form of fire" refers to sunlight and the like; it is not fire and so on. "Though it is the body of moving wind" means that although things are seen to move, the moving is not wind. Why? Because it is not established as a substance.

Then, if appearing things are empty, how are they seen? "Though empty, they are seen": appearing things are seen like an illusion, a dream, or a city of the gandharvas, but things are never seen as having any core. Therefore, though they appear white, blue and various, they are not colors, because they do not exist as substances. Thus, although things are from the very beginning possessed of the essence that is free of any essence, empty by their essential nature, endowed with the supreme of all aspects, childish beings do not see this at all. Why? "One's own mind, its stains, the afflictions, the power of māra." The definitive point is that they do not see because of the power of that māra, the activity of the life-wind in the right and left channels that has not been stopped.

Now the locations of the channels through which the life-wind and so on move are to be stated, and so the text says "sound" and so on. Here "sound" is the nature of the drop of mind at the heart, which produces deep sleep. "Drop" is the drop of body at the forehead, which produces the waking state. "Part" is the drop of speech at the throat, which produces dreams. "Gnosis" is the drop of gnosis at the navel, which produces the fourth state.

"Abiding in the place of nectar, the channels bound like a chain": the place of nectar is the forehead, and "abiding" refers to lalanā, rasanā and avadhūtī. "Bound like a chain" means that those three channels make three loops at the center of the navel and of the heart. Again, the avadhūtī goes on, piercing the pod-like places at the heart and so on, while lalanā and rasanā go by the force of the activity of the lotuses on the right and left. Then again, from the center of the heart and the throat, they go by three paths. In the same way, having made three paths at the forehead, the center of the throat, the crown protuberance, and the center of the forehead, lalanā goes out from the left nostril and rasanā from the right, while avadhūtī makes the breath emerge from both nostrils at once. "The downward-clearing wind" means that it likewise goes below the navel. "Life-wind and downward-clearing wind, three paths" means that above there are three, right, left and center, and below there are the three paths of excrement, urine and semen. "Together with vajra and lotus" is said because it is the nature of women's place of birth and of what is concordant with its cause. "The lotus itself possesses the vajra" is said because of its nature. "In the middle where the two winds meet" means at the navel, in the middle where the life-wind and the downward-clearing wind meet. "Objects and subjects" refers to the emergence and entry of the channels of the eye and so on; this too is just what was stated earlier. "Also grasping the signs such as smoke" means by way of the avadhūtī. The word "also" means that same thing at the navel. "All is the great secret" means that it is definitely secret from childish beings.

Then, in order to state the yogin's summation, the text says "the life-wind entering the center" and so on. Here, first the yogin makes the life-wind enter the center, that is, the avadhūtī, and then certain signs will be seen. "Glorious guru" means the single vajra of the body of the Buddha's nature. "Abiding with the sun" means together with the movement on the right. "Abiding in the hare" means the right wind in the left channel; this is the definitive practice of binding that life-wind, and therefore "life-wind entered" states the second, the speech vajra. "The mind fully joined with the mudrā" is the third, the mind vajra, the awakening mind melted through passion for form. "In order to join the vajra in the abode of supreme bliss" means this: if joy then arises through passion for form, the "sound of the vajra in the lotus" is to be done for only an instant, not continually. If no wisdom consort is found, then "for bliss, nourishing the lotus with one's own hand" refers not to an actual hand but to the jāgāna described earlier. That too is for the sake of the bliss without emission; it was not taught for the sake of emission. Therefore, "By that bliss of not releasing the seed, the fear of death is destroyed: the face of the glorious guru" is the fourth, the gnosis vajra itself; this is the definitive understanding of those practiced in yoga.

Now, in order to state the ruling and dissolution of the maṇḍalas, the text says "earth" and so on. When the yogin, with the wrathful gaze, has placed the mind on emptiness at the time the breath flows on the right or the left: "earth" was taught for those who place the mind on emptiness at the time the right flows, and was not taught for the time the left channel flows. Why is that? Because from the left the maṇḍalas of space and so on flow, it is not that "earth enters water." Rather, that statement applies when the life-wind of the right flows: it flows first from earth, and so it was taught, "Earth enters water, water fire, and fire also enters the winds," and so on. In this way, each enters into the next, up to the element of gnosis.

Furthermore, the left channel is not a place where the winds move out from space and the other elements. Rather, from the right they finally enter and move into the empty one. This is what is meant by "In the empty, the ten kinds of signs." Through its power, forms of every aspect arise, such as the smoke-like sign and the rest. From form, immutable bliss arises, and the gnosis of the Perfection of Wisdom enters. "From that gnosis, miraculous power" means that miraculous powers such as travelling through the sky come from that gnosis, and lordship over the three realms is also accomplished in this very life. "O lord of men" is the call to the one who is to enter the path.

Now, in order to state the sixteen divisions of the four bodies, the text says "From desire" and so on. Here, by virtue of the truth of the origin, each single body becomes fourfold, as body, speech, mind and gnosis. Therefore "From desire" means that when the joy of body ceases, it becomes the emanation body of the buddhas. "Then the perfect speech" means that from the cessation of the supreme joy of body it becomes the speech of the emanation body. "The blazing stainless mind" means that from the cessation of the joy of cessation of body it becomes the mind of the emanation body. "And from the gnosis of supreme bliss, oṃ ḍa" means that when the innate joy of body has ceased, it becomes the gnosis of the emanation body. Thus the emanation body has four aspects.

In the same way, the four enjoyment bodies are as follows. "From joy, the enjoyment body" means that through the cessation of the joy of speech it becomes the enjoyment body of the buddhas. "Supreme joy, together with the joy of cessation, these in order" means that through the cessation of the supreme joy of speech it becomes the speech of the enjoyment body. "Joy of cessation" means that through the cessation of the joy of cessation of speech it becomes the mind of the enjoyment body. "Innate" means that through the cessation of the innate joy of speech it becomes the gnosis of the enjoyment body.

"Then the basis of Dharma is trembling, and" means that through the cessation of the joy of mind it becomes the Dharma body of the buddhas. "What arises from that" means that from the cessation of the supreme joy of mind it becomes the speech of the Dharma body. "From swooning, certainly the mind of Dharma" means that from the cessation of the joy of cessation of mind it becomes the mind of the Dharma body. "This gnosis in the two" means that from the cessation of the innate joy of mind it becomes the gnosis of the Dharma body.

"The body certainly pure of color" is the innate body. "Immutable" means that through the cessation of the joy of gnosis it becomes the innate body. That itself is free of the expanse; being inexpressible, it is also called "free of the expanse." "Part, and" is like the digit of the moon: through the cessation of the supreme joy of gnosis it becomes the speech of the innate body. That same thing is also called "potency"; this is the term used by others. "Drop" means that through the cessation of the joy of cessation of gnosis it becomes the gnosis of the innate body. "Nāda" means sound: through the cessation of the innate joy of gnosis it becomes the gnosis of the innate body.

Therefore "abiding in the three existences, in speech, mind, gnosis, vajra, the source of bliss, the pure body" is the division of the sixteen joys of Vajrasattva, because it is said, "Knowing suchness in its sixteen aspects." This suchness in sixteen aspects is, ultimately, great immutable bliss itself; conventionally, by virtue of the twelve aspects of the meaning of truth that arise in the forward order, that same suchness in sixteen aspects has the nature of flowing down.

For the buddhas, because the life-wind is exhausted by reversing from the forward order the winds of the twelve transitions, it is said, "Vajra sun, great illumination, possessing the twelve aspects of the meaning of truth." This is the sun, which has the property of the twelve transitions, that is, the menstrual element or blood, which has ceased. And because the awakening mind, which has the property of the moon with its sixteen digits, has also ceased, it is said, "Vajra moon, stainless light, knowing suchness in its sixteen aspects." Thus it is established as Kālacakra.

Now the cessation of the four states, waking and the rest, will be stated. As for "Waking, and": here, what produces the waking state of beings in saṃsāra is "abiding in the body," meaning the awakening mind abiding at the crown, which produces waking; this is the drop of body. "Dream": what produces the dream state of saṃsāra is "breath dissolved," meaning the drop of awakening mind abiding at the throat, which produces dream; this is the drop of speech. "And these very ones, other natures": what produces the sleep state of saṃsāra is "the unmoving mind dissolved." Since it does not engage the objects of either of the two states, and the mind is dissolved, that which produces the deep sleep of embodied beings, abiding at the heart, is called the drop of mind. "Abiding in gnosis": here, what produces the bliss arising from the conduct of beings in saṃsāra is to be understood from union with women. The drop of awakening mind that produces the bliss of the seed of saṃsāric beings, abiding in the emanation wheel, is called the drop of gnosis.

"Even for an instant the awakening mind melts" means that in this way, even from a mere instant of union with women, the four drops are called forth and the four states of saṃsāric beings are produced. When these cease, there are the emanation body and the rest; this is what is meant by "Certainly arising in sequence, the mind vajra of four aspects," for the buddhas.

"Thus abiding in the three existences" means that the four minds which produce the four states of waking and the rest rest in the center of the drops of embodied beings. "As the cause of liberation, giving the fruit thought of as bliss, all-pervading, the lord of yogins should guard it" means that whoever guards these four drops of awakening mind, those very drops grant the fruit of liberation. "The state of supreme bliss" is the abode of Vajradhara, and what brings about its attainment is holding to the peak of the four meditative concentrations.

"The seed from which yoga arises": this is because it causes embodied beings to be born into nondual awakening.

"Having let the drop fall, what liberation is there?" means: if the seed, the drop of the awakening mind (bodhicitta), leaks away, where could nondual wisdom be found? Why is that? It is said so because the drop is the support of bliss. Therefore it says, "The momentary bliss of saṃsāra is to be entirely abandoned here by yogins." That is, whatever has the nature described as "changing and changing, and then concordant" is to be abandoned. With the nature called "concordant with the cause," the awakening mind is sent upward, and yogins are to send it until it is stainless. This is the definitive way of the Tathāgatas. Therefore the etymology of the word Tathāgata is this: because they have gone thus, just as they came, they are called Tathāgatas.

In order to teach the yoga of conduct relevant to this occasion, and the yoga of drinking, eating, and so forth, it says: oṃ āḥ hūṃ ho. "In due order, first, here one should always purify and realize" means that this is to be done for all food, things eaten and things chewed, and so forth.

Next, the drinking of liquor. At a great gaṇa offering, a feast of heroes, and the like, when a yogin partakes of drink: "Having made the liquor blaze, it should be passed around in order, like the melted moon." When yogins enjoy liquor, they should first take as the drinking vessel a special skull cup. If there is none, they should take one of bell metal. If even that is lacking, they should visualize any vessel at all as a skull cup and put the liquor in it. Kindling it with wind and fire, they make it of the nature of nectar. They draw the seat of emptiness and compassion on the ground or in the air, invite the wisdom being, and satisfy it. Only after also satisfying whoever is the wisdom consort (prajñā) should they drink; otherwise drinking is not taught.

This is explained in detail: "Moreover, with drops from the vessel, using the ring finger of the moon hand, on the ground..." The left hand is the moon because it is the portion of the awakening mind. The outer triangle, which represents emptiness, is the letter e. "Like the maker of day, in its center make a circle" means that in the center of that emptiness one makes a round drop, which represents compassion. This is the reality of e vaṃ. It is what is taught at the opening of all the tantras by the words "Thus have I heard" and so on.

"In its center, the wheel of wisdom abiding in the three existences" means: the wheel of wisdom, which abides within whatever is e vaṃ and whose nature is the complete transformation of the three existences, is invited there.

"With thumb and ring finger, satisfy it at the proper times each day" means that the wheel of wisdom is to be satisfied with the thumb and the ring finger, which have the nature of method and wisdom.

The passage from "lord of country and city" up to the mantras of the six families is easy to understand, so it is not explained here.

Now, "to the ultimate truth" means this: the yogas of food and conduct taught above, in the sequence just described, are simply a teaching of conventional conduct. What is contrary to that and does not accord with it is the ultimate, and that is the fourth. Therefore the fourth is the essence of all the tantras that Vajradhara taught, and it is also what is taught as their result. The other words and letters are taught as the cause or means of realizing it. However many worldly accomplishments and elaborate activities there may be, they too are taught only so that the fourth itself may be properly realized indirectly. Nothing at all is taught for any other purpose. Therefore I too shall teach the fourth itself as best I can.

On the words "as the Blessed One (the Buddha) said in each and every tantra, 'The fourth is again likewise'": vajra words are of two kinds, mundane and supramundane. "The fourth again" is to be understood as a supramundane vajra word. With the word "fourth" it is said, "The fourth is the bliss concordant with the cause." By mentioning "concordant with the cause," it is set apart from what is described as "interrupted by changing and changing." Here "changing" is leaking: this is the vase. The second "changing" is also leaking: this is the secret. "Interrupted" is also leaking: this is the wisdom-knowledge. The phrase "concordant with the cause" means non-leaking.

There is, on the one hand, the bliss that is imperishable, beyond any worldly example, and without fault, which takes as its object the Mother, the Perfection of Wisdom, endowed with every supreme aspect, beside whom the action mudrā and the wisdom mudrā are inferior, and which is the perfection of both mundane and supramundane goods. On the other hand, there is what is called worldly wisdom-knowledge: the downward leaking of the fluid of the awakening mind, which harms the aggregates, the elements, and the rest. It is common to all worldly people and is faulty. It takes as its object an action mudrā with breasts and hair, filled with every affliction, and it destroys all mundane and supramundane goods. Their distinctions, divisions, and natures are taught to be utterly different. Yet the childish, the deluded, the unlearned, and the unclear, attached to the view of a self and covered by the cataract of ignorance, explain the meaning of the tantras wrongly and hold that the fourth arises from the faulty third.

Some, who do not even know or have heard the mere name "the fourth," practice only with an action mudrā, and when they die they go to Avīci hell. Some reject the fourth and hold that merely repeating the series again, beginning from the vase and so on, is the fourth. Some, when asked what the wisdom (prajñā) is, say it is a woman, and hold the bliss arising from her to be the fourth. Some hold that "the fourth" is the result, the state of Vajradhara. All these are nothing but positions forced into place by the childish and deluded, who lack both scripture and reasoning.

Therefore, because they are hard to understand and extremely hard to come upon, they are called vajra words. What is spoken through a combination of seemingly contradictory words is the statement "It is again the same." The word "again" is said as though it reaches back to the third empowerment. In truth, however, "again the same" refers only to the arising of the bliss of the awakening mind, not to the third. Therefore, because it says "It is again the same," the childish are confused and fail to understand the meaning.

"It is that very wisdom-gnosis" is to be connected with "that great wisdom." How is this evident? Because further on it also says, "It is free of the action seal and the gnosis seal." Therefore one should understand that between the changing bliss of the third and the unchanging bliss of the fourth there is not even so much connection as between a pillar and a vase.

How is this evident? It states, "because its nature is the causally concordant bliss." For the bliss whose nature is causally concordant is without emission, whereas the gnosis of the action seal is by nature emission.

"Because whatever is wisdom-gnosis arises from the gnosis of the Great Seal" means that the bliss which arises from that wholly nonconceptual meditative absorption is without emission. Therefore, as for the bliss without emission: "the gnosis of the action seal" refers to the bliss of emission that arises from union with a human woman, and "the gnosis of the gnosis seal" refers to bliss produced by conceptual thought, which is likewise of the nature of emission. The meaning is that these are not the gnosis of the unemitting bliss of the Great Seal.

How is it evident that it is without emission? "This is stated in the Recitation of the Names:

Endowed with all aspects, yet aspectless,
holding the drop of sixteen halved and halved,
beyond reckoning, without parts,
holding the peak of the fourth meditative concentration."

This means the following. Having gone beyond the reckoning of "childhood" and the rest, which serve as worldly examples, great wisdom is to be generated through the six limbs. The first of the six limbs, withdrawal, is the realization that all the aspects of outer objects such as vases are of one taste with emptiness endowed with all supreme aspects. At that time the aspects of the signs, such as smoke, also arise; and these signs too are aspectless, because they have no separate substance. "The drop of sixteen halved and halved" is the sixteen joys, divided into sixteen by the distinction of body, speech, mind and gnosis; holding these with the tight noose of emptiness is what is meant. "Beyond reckoning, without parts" means not abiding in either the waxing or the waning fortnight. "Holding the peak of the fourth meditative concentration": holding at the culmination of the fourth drop, which comprises the sixteen joys, is called holding the peak. Therefore the Great Seal is to be understood, without error, as the very unchanging bliss born from emptiness.

"Just as other ordinary beings emit the drop": non-emission is declared supreme and is the fourth. Ordinary beings are those who are discordant with and contrary to it; worldly empowerment involves emission. "Likewise, this Blessed One (the Buddha)" means that one who has obtained the transcendent empowerment does not emit the drop. Why? Because he is firmly bound to great passion. Joy, supreme joy, special joy and the innate: he holds the peak of the fourth meditative concentration, because he is never separated from that undefiled great passion.

The scripture on the unemitting great bliss, distinct from worldly emission, which bestows the state of the great Vajradhara, is this: "Likewise also:

With the vajra posture, the mind
is to be seen abiding within the jewel,
filled with the bliss of the causally concordant and the rest.
It, stainless, at the navel
is declared the causally concordant itself.
Ripening is to be recalled at the heart,
personal effort at the throat,
the chief, the stainless, at the crown.

Thus spoke the Blessed One." Here "vajra" is the action vajra, and "posture" is its place. "Mind" is the awakening mind. One is to see that mind contained within the jewel of the action vajra, not by its departing from it. That very thing is "filled with the bliss of the causally concordant and the rest": what is called causally concordant, from the vajra jewel to below the navel, is to be made principal as the unemitting, and not anything else. Proceeding from that, it says, "the chief, the stainless, at the crown." The meaning is: so long as what abides in the vajra jewel has not reached the crown, the uprooting of the afflictions is not taught; so what need is there to say that a person subject to emission has no state of liberation? "Thus spoke the Blessed One" means "because he spoke thus."

Therefore, "The emission of the outer joy of cessation does not bestow buddhahood" is in keeping with the manner explained above: it is because the great outer joy of cessation throws the three worlds into turmoil. Why does it cause turmoil? It says, "because it abides in existence and nirvāṇa." By these two, existence and nirvāṇa, it causes turmoil and makes beings circle, and "because it causes great ruin," it is said. This Blessed One is called "not abiding in existence or nirvāṇa"; it is that which brings about cyclic existence that abides in existence and nirvāṇa. What is he, then? It says, "the gnosis without emission," which means that he has gained mastery over the gnosis of unemitted bliss, the reverse of emission.

What, then, is meant by the conventional terms "existence" and "nirvāṇa"? It says, "Existence is the forehead, the place of birth." That lack of any experience of gnosis due to the lunar stream which, in ordinary persons, abides from the forehead, the place of great bliss, down to what is named the emanation wheel, is called existence, or method, or compassion. Nirvāṇa is so called because it resembles nirvāṇa; this is "from the neck of the vajra jewel," that is, from the neck of the secret vajra. "The four drops are emitted"

...means that it abides at the place where the stream of the moon arises, and is made to flow.

"Not abiding at all" means not abiding in the two things just explained, saṃsāra and nirvāṇa. Not abiding at the forehead is for the sake of fully understanding the faults of saṃsāra. Then the awakening mind that has reached the end of the vajra jewel does not pass back out, for the sake of fully understanding a nirvāṇa that has faults. Therefore "the Blessed One" is the heart of awakening, where the precious buddha principally abides. Such a Blessed One destroys all the faults of saṃsāra and gives rise to great bliss, and that itself is the transmundane empowerment.

That being free of emerging and entering likewise destroys the faults of saṃsāra and bestows the fruit of great bliss is also taught in another tantra. "Likewise also" are the words that point to that other tantra.

"Beginning and end pacified": the beginning is the forehead and so on; the end is the tip and so on. "Things and non-things exhausted" refers either to what was stated above, or else to their being realized as beginning and end, that is, exhausted. The one by whom they are exhausted is the Lord, the sovereign of the three realms. That itself is also called the inseparability of emptiness and compassion, which applies to both of the ways stated above. "Is called the awakening mind" means that one should understand that the conventional awakening mind and the ultimate awakening mind, the two being inseparable, are called the awakening mind. "Thus spoke the Blessed One" means that it was spoken by the Blessed One in the glorious Guhyasamāja; that is why it says "therefore."

To unravel the meaning of that tantra: "beginning and end" was explained above. In "free of arising and destruction," arising is the beginning and destruction is the end, and the meaning is that this Blessed One is free of them and does not possess them. "Peaceful" means that whatever bears the name of beginning and end is peaceful. If one asks what that is, the explanation is "free of the change of the faculties": by the power of that pacification of beginning and end, the faculties such as the eye do not run off from their objects. Or else, if the change of the faculties is taken to refer to the wind or to the awakening mind, then, because of that, their not moving and not dripping is what is called "free."

"Things and non-things exhausted": being free of conceiving things and conceiving non-things is called "exhausted." "Which is entirely free of permanence and annihilation": here the thing is the view of permanence, and the non-thing is the view of annihilation; it is free of these.

By stating it this way, the first of the six limbs, called withdrawal (pratyāhāra), is taught, and that withdrawal is held to be precisely what is isolated from the net of concepts. For one who has no net of concepts, "the Lord" is "the sovereign of the three realms," meaning one who has transcended the conduct of the three realms.

"Emptiness and compassion inseparable, whether they depart": this teaches the result of the withdrawal above. From being gathered in by the great withdrawal, the awakening mind arises and abides in the throat of the vajra, that is, of the jewel, and does not flow out; this is emptiness, and that is what is intended.

"The forehead, the place of existence, is compassion" means that the forehead is the abode of the moon with sixteen digits, the awakening mind of compassion. "In the middle of this" means between the forehead and the jewel. "Whatever abides" means whatever awakening mind abides that has been generated by emptiness.

"Innate": the suchness of phenomena abides from the very beginning; whether the tathāgatas of the three times come or whether they depart, it abides unchanging. That is the innate of worldly convention, and it is realized and understood by this very great wisdom of non-dripping bliss and not otherwise; that is the meaning. The name of the result is applied to the cause. The innate great bliss taught above is to be accomplished through the non-dripping of the worldly conventional awakening mind, and that non-dripping is the fourth, which is also to be called the transmundane empowerment.

That is conferred at the end of the vase, the secret, and the wisdom-gnosis empowerments. The third, wisdom-gnosis and the rest, is not called the fourth; but in order to explain that the fourth is merely a number, it says, "That is the ḍākinī vajra tent." "That" means "that fourth." "Ḍākinī" teaches the space-like samādhi. "Vajra tent" refers to the indivisible state, the vajra state, in which every net of concepts has been cleared away. This is taught in the explanatory tantra of Hevajra:

The first is the vase empowerment;
The second is called the secret;
Wisdom-gnosis is the third.

This teaches the mundane empowerments, and up to the third they are definitely mundane. Of the transmundane empowerment, which is distinct from them, it says, "It is not the thus-gone, just as it is." The definition of "thus-gone" is also given:

Going just as one came,
Or doing just as one said;
Speaking truth, with words unchanging,
Doing just as one has said.

In this context "tathāgata" refers to the awakening mind. It is also said, "As outside, so inside." That is not present in the third, and therefore the third is not said to be the Tathāgata. Moreover, in the fourth the awakening mind goes just as it came, and so it is called the Tathāgata. It is also called "doing just as one has said," as when, at the time the guru gave the injunction, he said:

You must never at any time
Become free of attachment to desire.

"In the three realms there is no other evil
like being free of attachment."
This injunction is implicitly accepted, and so it is "what was said." The disciple repeats it, and sends the awakening mind upward in the same way that it would descend; that is "doing just so."

"It is said that, in the order stated, there is certainly no bliss without emission in the third": this is because the same is said in other tantras of the Tathāgata as well. "Likewise also" introduces a further scriptural source from the tantras. If one asks which it is, the text says, "from the root tantra." "Root tantra" means the root tantra of the Paramādibuddha. If one asks what it says:

"Abandoning the action seals
and the imagined wisdom seal,
through union with the supreme immutable
the Great Seal is to be definitively shown."

As for this passage: the action seal is abandoned because it leads to emission. The wisdom seal is by nature imagined, and since it is imagined it does not touch suchness. That is why both are said to be abandoned. Therefore the Great Seal, which is distinct from the action seal and the wisdom seal, is described as "through union with the supreme immutable." The supreme immutable is the awakening mind. What makes it immutable is shown to be firmly reining in the horse of wind that carries consciousness, which runs off toward sense objects. For holding that wind, nothing is said to be taken up other than the meditative absorption on things as they are. Therefore the meditative absorption on things as they are is shown to be union with the supreme immutable, because it makes the awakening mind immutable.

Here, the Great Seal is the bliss endowed with the supreme of all aspects, beyond any worldly comparison, free of fault, a wondrous bliss that is the cause of benefit for oneself and others. The words "the Great Seal is to be definitively shown" refer to this, and the line "said the Tathāgata" is to be joined with them.

In the order thus stated:

"This word of the buddhas,
virtuous in the beginning, the middle and the end,
was spoken by the buddhas of the past;
those yet to come will speak it too;
and the perfect buddhas of the present
speak it again and again."

So the Tathāgata said. What shows this to be far superior to what Paśupati, Brahmā, Śiva and others taught is the statement: "One should know that whatever is virtuous in the beginning, the middle and the end has been spoken by the buddhas, by the buddhas of the past, of the future and of the present." Because this is said, the teaching of Īśvara and the rest that liberation comes through killing is not supreme at all. Then there are other yogins who go under the name of Buddhists. They are not themselves Vajrasattva, yet they teach that by taking on his pride one comes to have his nature. They give a human woman the name "wisdom" and teach that merely by entering into union with her one attains liberation. These should be known as not teaching the true path. The Great Seal cannot be attained by worldly beings who still have defilements. Therefore it must be meditated on with every conceptual thought abandoned and the mind gathered inward.

"The eleventh, the transcendent empowerment": it is transcendent because it teaches a bliss and a conduct at odds with those of the world. It is the eleventh because it is taught after the ten worldly empowerments.

One should imagine that they bless the food and other things, remove the stain of the fault of impurity, and turn them into the nectar of the awakening mind.

"One should increase with the syllable āḥ": the right hand belongs to the sun. One should imagine that they bless the food and other things so that they become abundant, and cause them to be realized and understood as nectar with the taste of passion.

"One should kindle with the syllable hūṃ": the two palms joined and turned face down belong to Rāhu. "One requests departure with the syllable kṣa": kṣa has the sense of "exhausted, brought to its end," and so this means the gesture of requesting departure with the back of the left hand. Therefore this is the meaning of the passage from "One should purify with the left" up to "with the left hand one requests departure."

"Having visualized the liquor in the form of a melted moon": liquor is given only as an example. The meaning is that whatever one eats and drinks, one should imagine it as the nectar of the awakening mind.

"In its center one should make the round form of a drop" is symbolic. The meaning is that these are the mantra words for offering to what bears the qualities gathered by wind, fire, water and earth, namely what bears the qualities of the five aggregates, as in "consciousness belongs to the family of the space element," and to the five elements beginning with the space element.

They have the nature of Dveṣavajra and the others and have become the action faculties and the activities of the action faculties.

This means that it is the rite of mantra words for offering to the deity families of all that belongs to the yoga and yoginī tantras.

A syllable joined with a single consonant symbolizes method. One that is further joined above with a consonant symbolizes wisdom. Likewise, all short vowels are method and all long vowels are wisdom. Likewise, the āli is method and the kāli is wisdom, the anusvāra dot is method and the visarga is wisdom. This is what the great root tantra teaches, and it should be applied elsewhere as well, as appropriate.
