The Sādhana of the Glorious Constellation Maṇḍala in Eleven Limbs (Śrīnakṣatramaṇḍalasādhanaikādaśāṅga)

The Sādhana of the Glorious Constellation Maṇḍala in Eleven Limbs, known in Sanskrit as the Śrī Nakṣatra Maṇḍala Sādhana Ekādaśāṅga and in Tibetan as Palden Gyukar gyi Kyilkhor gyi Druptap Yenlak Chuchikpa, is a ritual manual for meditating on the maṇḍala of the Kālacakra, or Wheel of Time, tantra, whose cosmic and astral symbolism gives the constellation maṇḍala its name. After paying homage to the Buddha and the spiritual friend, it lays out the practice in eleven limbs, beginning with protection, purification and the gathering of the two accumulations, and moving through the generation of the deities, empowerment, offering and praise, the meditations on the subtle body and the drop, and mantra recitation, which includes long garland mantras for the many deities of the maṇḍala, before closing with dedication and the dissolution of the deities into the body. Its colophon attributes it to the master Kālacakrapāda and records that it was translated by the Kashmiri paṇḍita Somanātha together with the Tibetan translator Sherab Drak. It is Toh 1357 in the Tengyur, where it belongs among the tantra commentaries as one of the sādhanas of the Kālacakra cycle.

In the language of India: śrī nakṣatra maṇḍal sādhanamekadasaṃga nāma. In the language of Tibet: The Sādhana of the Glorious Constellation Maṇḍala in Eleven Limbs.

Homage to the glorious spiritual friend.

Wisdom body of omniscience,
Like the light of the sun, the maker of day,
With eyes like a blossoming lotus,
Buddha seated on the lion throne:
Having paid homage to the supreme deity,
I shall write the sādhana of the constellation maṇḍala

A person who has the right characteristics,
In the places that are explained in the tantras,

Protection, purification of the colors,

Having made various offerings, I confess the many kinds of misdeeds.
I, the practitioner, with body, speech and mind, and with faith, go respectfully to the Three.
All the varied virtue done by the perfect buddhas, the bodhisattvas and the Saṅgha,

To the Buddha, the Dharma and the Saṅgha, who destroy the fears of existence, I go for refuge until awakening.
I make the aspiration that, for the sake of sentient beings, I may attain complete awakening.
