The Benefits of the Hand Emblems

The Benefits of the Hand Emblems, in Tibetan Chaktsen gyi Penyön, is a short verse work on the symbolism of the attributes held in the hands of the tantric deity Hevajra, who is joined with his consort Nairātmyā and surrounded by eight ḍākinīs; no Sanskrit title is given. After paying homage, the author explains one emblem after another, such as the curved knife, the skull cup, the hand drum, the wheel, and animal figures like the elephant, fish, turtle, snake, and lion, showing how each one heals disease, brings wealth, or cuts through pride, delusion, desire, aversion, and wrong views for the sake of beings. It closes with a dedication that all beings may cross the ocean of saṃsāra on the great ship of method and wisdom. It is Toh 1289 in the Tengyur, where it is found among the commentaries on the tantras, in the material connected with the Hevajra cycle.

Here are The Benefits of the Hand Emblems.

Homage to the Blessed One Hevajra.

To Hevajra, joined with Nairātmyā,
encircled by the eight ḍākinīs,
I pay homage, and then I shall tell in order
the benefits of holding the hand emblems.

Holding the elephant and the rest
brings diseases to peace:
cough, breathing troubles, and madness too,
vomiting blood, leprosy, and pustules,
and the disorders of spleen and liver.
These are known as the elephant and the rest, in order.
The eight, earth and the others,
bring all wealth to accomplishment.

To cut the six faults beginning with pride,
the curved knife is in the right hand;
against the concepts of existence and nonexistence,
in the left is the lotus vessel.

To draw out beings who are like fish
from the poisoned waters of the ocean of saṃsāra,
the auspicious rohita fish is held;
for the net of the afflictions, the curved knife in the right.

Beings like pigs,
utterly stupefied by delusion:
to rouse them from the sleep of delusion,
in the right hand the hand drum resounds.

The turtle tormented by desire:
to clear away the diseases of beings
and to satisfy them with the nectar of great passion,
the turtle and the lotus vessel are held.

Beings like snakes,
overcome by the enemy, aversion:
to satisfy them with great aversion,
in the hands are a snake and a skull cup.

To terrify the deer
of evil thoughts, the lion;
or to crush pride, which is like a lion,
the lion and the axe are held.

For those who hold views such as the view of a self,
this is the second.
To loosen the knot of the view of a self
and of avarice,
the monk and the ringing staff are held.

As with the skull cup, for example,
by turning the wheel of the Dharma
the hardened afflictions are destroyed;
for this, the wheel and the skull cup are held.

To level the mountain of conceptual thought
and to subdue the vicious,
the right hand shows the threatening forefinger.

Of the Blessed One, the great glorious Heruka,
encircled by the host of mātṛkās,
I have told as best I can
the virtues of holding the hand emblems.
Through the virtues thus gained,
relying on the great ship of method and wisdom,
may limitless sentient beings
cross the ocean of saṃsāra
and dwell on the dry land of liberation.

The Benefits of the Hand Emblems is complete.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

Glang pa is read as glang po ("elephant"). The line "this is the second" (lta ba po yi gnyis pa'o) is obscure as it stands; it may refer to a second hand or a second emblem, and a line may be missing. "The monk and the ringing staff" renders dge slong dang ni 'khor gsil literally; the sense is probably the monk's ringing staff (khakkhara). "Hardened afflictions" takes tha ba as "hard, stubborn"; the reading is uncertain.
