The Blessed One spoke: "Noble son, I do not cast aside the realm of sentient beings. Noble son, nevertheless the teaching of the bodhisattvas is a teaching that is measureless, and a teaching that is inconceivable."

Then Mañjuśrī, the youthful one, said this to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, please explain the dhāraṇī called 'The Jewel Palm.'"

The Blessed One spoke: "Noble son, ask this of the bodhisattva Samantabhadra, and he will teach it to you. Why is this? Noble son, this sublime being abides in the accumulation of wisdom."

Mañjuśrī said: "If the Tathāgata so intends regarding me, I shall ask this sublime being."

The Blessed One spoke: "Mañjuśrī, since you have attained as many absorptions (samādhis) as there are atoms of the finest particles, what need have you to petition the Tathāgata? Noble son, you are endowed with the fortune for all the qualities of a buddha."

Mañjuśrī said: "Blessed One, let it be understood that all those qualities of a buddha which I have relied upon, cultivated in meditation, and practiced extensively, are all through the power of the Tathāgata's person."

The Blessed One spoke: "Noble son, excellent, excellent! You have spoken these words well. Nevertheless, Mañjuśrī, I request of you: ask the bodhisattva Samantabhadra."

Then Mañjuśrī, the youthful one, said this to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, this bodhisattva Samantabhadra teaches what is extremely profound; he has realized and fully comprehended the Great Vehicle."

The Blessed One spoke: "Noble son, you too are the sovereign king of all phenomena, so do not say this to me. Moreover, noble son, you possess an inconceivable absorption that abides in emptiness."

Then Mañjuśrī, the youthful one, joined his ten fingers in añjali and, having paid homage to the bodhisattva Samantabhadra, spoke these words to him: "O child of the Victor, if you grant me the occasion, I will ask you a word of a few syllables."

The bodhisattva Samantabhadra said: "Noble son, if you know this to be the proper time, then ask."

Then Mañjuśrī, the youthful one, spoke these words to the bodhisattva mahāsattva Samantabhadra: "O child of the Victor, what is the meaning of the ground of what is called 'buddha, buddha'?"

The bodhisattva Samantabhadra said: "O child of the Victor, the meaning of the ground of buddhahood is the meaning of groundlessness. The meaning of the ground of buddhahood is the meaning of the absence of any entity. The meaning of the ground of buddhahood is the meaning of the inconceivable. The meaning of the ground of buddhahood is the meaning of the unequaled that is equal. The meaning of the ground of buddhahood is the meaning of freedom from conceptual elaboration. The meaning of the ground of buddhahood is the meaning of non-elaboration. The meaning of the ground of buddhahood is the meaning of nothing to be taken up. The meaning of the ground of buddhahood is the meaning of nothing to be abandoned. The meaning of the ground of buddhahood is the meaning of being equal to space. The meaning of the ground of buddhahood is the meaning of being beyond expression. Noble son, such is the intrinsic nature of the qualities of a buddha."

Mañjuśrī said: "O child of the Victor, if all phenomena are free of conceptual elaboration, then how is it taught that the qualities of a buddha are known?"

The bodhisattva Samantabhadra said: "O child of the Victor, this teaching is itself something that cannot be taught."

Mañjuśrī said:

"O child of the Victor, if it cannot be taught, then what is being taught?"

The bodhisattva Samantabhadra said: "What cannot be taught is taught by means of designation."

Mañjuśrī said: "O child of the Victor, by means of designation, what is being designated?"

The bodhisattva Samantabhadra said: "Mañjuśrī, by means of designation, neither an entity nor the absence of an entity is designated."

Mañjuśrī said: "O child of the Victor, if by means of designation neither an entity nor the absence of an entity is designated, then how are the three vehicles taught?"

The bodhisattva Samantabhadra said: "Mañjuśrī, do not become attached to the dharmadhātu, which is free of attachment. Why is this? Mañjuśrī, it is because the Tathāgata has taught that all phenomena are without entity. And as for why they are without entity, it is because the five aggregates are without any basis for apprehension."

Mañjuśrī said: "Is awakening accompanied by conceptual elaboration, or is it free of conceptual elaboration?"

The bodhisattva Samantabhadra said: "Noble son, awakening is neither accompanied by conceptual elaboration nor free of conceptual elaboration. For awakening, that which is accompanied by conceptual elaboration, and that which is free of conceptual elaboration — these are not two; they are indivisible as two."

Then the Blessed One gave his approval to the bodhisattva mahāsattva Samantabhadra, saying: "Noble son, excellent, excellent is this inconceivable teaching you have given." When these words were spoken, this world together with its gods became bewildered.

Mañjuśrī said: "Blessed One, a teaching of the Dharma such as this is not one that ought to be sought by ordinary means."

The Blessed One declared: "Noble son, it is just so; it is as you have said."

Then Mañjuśrī, the youthful, said this to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, is it by this very designation that all phenomena are designated?"

The Blessed One declared: "Noble son, it is just so; it is as you have said."

Then the bodhisattva Samantabhadra said this to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, this door of complete purity is difficult to have faith in."

The Blessed One declared: "Noble son, it is just so; it is as you have said."

Then the bodhisattva Sāgaramati said this to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, this door of complete purity taught by the bodhisattva Samantabhadra has been well spoken."

The Blessed One declared: "Noble son, it is just so; it is as you have said. Noble son, furthermore, this is concordant with all phenomena. This is the great rain of the Dharma."

Then the brahmin who was like a great sāla tree, abiding in equality, said this to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, awakening is the inconceivable equality, in which no syllable is apprehended and no word is apprehended."

The Blessed One declared: "Noble son, it is just so. In the dharmadhātu no word appears, and no meaning appears either."

Then Mañjuśrī, the youthful, said this to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, what is concordant with emptiness? What is its aspect? What is its sign? What is its characteristic?"

The Blessed One declared: "Mañjuśrī, emptiness is inexpressible and indescribable. Whatever faith there is in this inexpressible nature of reality — that is called emptiness. This emptiness is without letters; therefore it is called emptiness. This emptiness is indescribable; therefore it is called emptiness."





























Then the bodhisattva Samantabhadra said this to the bodhisattva Dharmamati: "Child of noble family, you have spoken well in explaining the dhāraṇī called the Rare and Supreme Jewel. Child of noble family, the qualities of a buddha are inconceivable. If even those sentient beings who merely hear the name of this category of teaching thereby draw close to omniscience itself, then what need is there to mention those who hold this category of teaching, recite it, master it completely, and accomplish it in accordance with suchness? Such beings will certainly become buddhas."

Dharmamati said: "Kye, child of the Victor, it is indeed so. It is just as you have said. One should understand that those endowed with profound wisdom have been empowered by the Tathāgata."

The bodhisattva Samantabhadra said: "If even those excellent persons into whose ears such teachings as these have merely resounded are to be understood as having received prophecy regarding the qualities of a buddha, then what need is there to mention those who have gained this category of teaching in hand?"

Then the venerable Śāradvatīputra bowed his head at the feet of the Blessed One and said this: "Blessed One, please observe — because I have not known such teachings as these, I am like one born blind. Blessed One, it is not only I alone who have become like one born blind; Blessed One, all those sentient beings who have not heard this category of teaching have likewise become like ones born blind."

The Blessed One declared: "Elder, it is just so. It is just as you have said."

Śāradvatīputra said: "Blessed One, please explain that very inconceivability."

The Blessed One declared: "Śāradvatīputra, go and urge Śakra (Indra), Brahmā, and all the guardians of the world. Proclaim also the command of the Tathāgata. Proclaim to that assembly this dhāraṇī as well, called the Supreme Dharma Seal, the Rare and Supreme Jewel."

Then the venerable Śāradvatīputra, having heard the command of the Tathāgata, for the sake of the meaning of this dhāraṇī of the Rare and Supreme Jewel, with his throat utterly parched, spoke of this matter to Śakra, Brahmā, and those guardians of the world, saying: "Gather together, all you who listen to the Dharma. Kye, friends, it is extremely difficult to find such a precious sūtra as this arising in the world."

Then the venerable Śāradvatīputra said this to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, please explain the dhāraṇī called the Rare and Supreme Jewel. Sugata, please explain it."

Samantabhadra, a son or daughter of noble family who proclaims the Dharma will, after passing away and transmigrating from this life, be born in a buddha-field, a world-system adorned by the Tathāgata of inconceivable confidence, utterly magnificent — in that world-system there are none but bodhisattvas, entirely beautiful. At the time of death, that son of noble family will behold one hundred twenty million buddhas face to face. The Blessed One declared: "Samantabhadra, the beings who will abandon such teachings as these will for the most part be those who have gone forth, saying: 'These are mere poetic compositions; these were not spoken by the Tathāgata; these were fabricated by their own authors; these sūtras were never heard of before' — grumbling thus, they will abandon this discourse on Dharma. Those sentient beings will become like a stūpa worthy of worship for the world together with its gods, māras, and Brahmās. They cannot be harmed by māras, nor by the gods of the class of māras, nor by renunciants, nor by those who dwell in the household life, nor likewise by kings, nor by great ministers, nor by householders — so saying, they raised a great clamor of acclaim.

Then the venerable Subhūti said this to the Blessed One: Blessed One, it is difficult to find such a precious sūtra arising in the world. Yet through the power of the Tathāgata such a Dharma has arisen in the world, and through that very power of the Tathāgata these sentient beings as well have heard it.

Then the Blessed One, extending the organ of his tongue, said to the venerable Ānanda: Ānanda, hold this discourse of Dharma! Retain it! Recite it! Master it completely!

Then the venerable Ānanda circumambulated the Blessed One three times and said this: Blessed One, I have held this discourse of Dharma, and henceforth I shall not let it slip from memory.

When the Blessed One had spoken thus, that entire assembly of bodhisattvas, together with the noble bodhisattva Samantabhadra, Mañjuśrī the Youthful, the venerable Śāradvatīputra, the other great śrāvakas, and the world together with its gods, humans, asuras, and gandharvas, all rejoiced and highly praised what the Blessed One had spoken.

The noble discourse of Dharma called the Dhāraṇī of the Precious Tālala is complete.
