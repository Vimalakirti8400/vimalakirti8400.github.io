The Dhāraṇī for Giving Away the Bodily Secretions as an Offering

This short text, titled in English *The Dhāraṇī for Giving Away the Bodily Secretions as an Offering*, is catalogued as Toh 804 in the Kangyur; no Sanskrit title is given in the opening, and the Tibetan title is not fully determinable from the source lines alone. It is a brief ritual formula of the kind classed among the tantras, or among the dhāraṇī texts that sit alongside them, and it prescribes mantras for offering the body's secretions, namely saliva, phlegm, nasal mucus, bodily dirt, excrement, and urine, to beings who might feed on them. Each substance has its own short mantra, spoken with the wish that those who consume it may obtain it, and followed by a snap of the fingers. The practitioner then dedicates all the merit of this giving to awakening for the sake of all sentient beings. According to its colophon, the material was compiled from sūtras and tantras by the lama Vajrāsanapa and translated by Bari Lotsāwa, which places it among the later Indian-derived ritual writings translated into Tibetan.

Homage to the Three Jewels.

The dhāraṇī for giving away the bodily secretions as an offering.

For saliva: Oṃ āḥ śleṣmā hare bhyaḥ svāhā.

For phlegm: Oṃ āḥ khreḍa āhare bhyaḥ svāhā.

For nasal mucus: Oṃ āḥ siṅghāṇa āhare bhyaḥ svāhā.

For bodily dirt: Oṃ āḥ mala āhare bhyaḥ svāhā.

For excrement: Oṃ āḥ viṭ āhare bhyaḥ svāhā.

For urine: Oṃ āḥ mūtra āhare bhyaḥ svāhā.

For each of these, one should say, "May those who consume these obtain them." Then one snaps the fingers.

Whatever merit is gathered by giving these in this way, all of it should be dedicated to awakening for the sake of all sentient beings.

These teachings were drawn from the sūtras and tantras by the lama Vajrāsanapa, and were translated by Bari Lotsāwa.
