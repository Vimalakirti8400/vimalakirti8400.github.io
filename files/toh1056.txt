The Mantra That Dispels Bile Disorders

The Mantra That Dispels Bile Disorders, known in Tibetan as Mkhris pa'i nad sel ba'i sngags, is a very brief healing text consisting of a homage to the Three Jewels, a short mantra, and a single line of ritual instruction. It states that fanning the body with a fan while employing the mantra will relieve bile disorders, jaundice, and itching ailments, and it closes by calling itself a dhāraṇī for dispelling bile disorders. The work reflects the practical, protective side of Buddhist ritual literature, in which short spells are used to address specific illnesses, here those that traditional medicine associated with an imbalance of bile. It is Toh 1056 in the Kangyur, where it belongs to the collection of dhāraṇīs and mantras gathered among the tantric texts.

The Mantra That Dispels Bile Disorders

Homage to the Three Jewels.

namo hitu hege svāhā

If one fans the body with a fan, this dispels bile disorders, jaundice, and itching ailments.

The dhāraṇī that dispels bile disorders is complete.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

"Bile" renders the Tibetan humoral term for the bile humor (Skt. pitta), one of the three humors in Indo-Tibetan medicine. "Jaundice" translates a Tibetan term that literally means "yellow eyes."
