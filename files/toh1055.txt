The Dhāraṇī That Fully Pacifies the Pain of Burns

The Dhāraṇī That Fully Pacifies the Pain of Burns, known in Tibetan as Me'i zug rngu rab tu zhi bar byed pa'i gzungs, is a very brief ritual incantation for relieving the pain of burns caused by fire. It opens with homage to the cooling god of water and then gives a short mantra, instructing that it be recited eleven times before spittle is applied to the burn so that the pain will subside. Texts like this reflect the practical, healing side of Buddhist dhāraṇī literature, in which sacred formulas are used for protection and relief from everyday afflictions. It is catalogued as Toh 1055 in the Kangyur, where it belongs to the collection of dhāraṇīs.

The Dhāraṇī That Fully Pacifies the Pain of Burns

Homage to the cooling god of water!

oṃ bhramaṇi bhramaṇi jhori bhramaṇi svāhā

If one recites this eleven times and then applies spittle, the pain of a burn from fire will be pacified.

The Dhāraṇī That Fully Pacifies the Pain of Burns is complete.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

"The cooling god of water" renders the Tibetan descriptive epithet. It may stand for Varuṇa, the Indian god of the waters, but the text does not name him, so the epithet is translated literally.
