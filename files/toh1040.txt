The Dhāraṇī for Retaining What Is Heard (Śrutadhāraṇī)

The Dhāraṇī for Retaining What Is Heard is a very brief incantation text in which the speaker pays homage to the Three Jewels and to the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara, the great being associated with compassion. It then gives a short mantra invoking memory and intelligence. Its title is known here in English from its closing line, and its Sanskrit and Tibetan names are not preserved in the opening lines provided. The text directs that the mantra be recited one hundred and eight times over black sweet flag, a medicinal root, which is then eaten. It promises that the practitioner will come to remember whatever they hear, a benefit valued by students and those who memorize teachings. It is Toh 1040 in the Kangyur, where it belongs among the dhāraṇī texts, the collection of mantras and spells for protection and for particular practical aims.

Homage to the Three Jewels!
Homage to the bodhisattva, the great being, the noble Avalokiteśvara!

tadyathā smṛti mati mati tejo bidyā huru me huru huru me kṛṣṇa huru me svāhā

If one recites this mantra one hundred and eight times over black sweet flag and then eats it, one will without doubt come to retain whatever one hears.

The Dhāraṇī for Retaining What Is Heard is complete.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

Black sweet flag renders ཤུ་དག་ནག་པོ. ཤུ་དག is the medicinal calamus root, Sanskrit vacā, which is traditionally used to strengthen memory. The phrase གདོན་མི་ཟ་བར here is the idiom "without doubt." It has nothing to do with gdon in the sense of a graha or seizing spirit. The closing syllable སྷོ after the title appears to be a scribal flourish and has not been translated.
