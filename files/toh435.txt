Tantra of the Vajra Nectar (Vajrāmṛtatantra)

Toh 435 in the Kangyur is the Vajra Amṛta Tantra, known in Tibetan as the Rdo rje bdud rtsi'i rgyud, or the Tantra of the Vajra Nectar. It is a Buddhist tantric text belonging to the class of esoteric scriptures, and its opening situates it within a visionary setting in which the Blessed One dwells in the secret lotus of the Vajra Nectar and sports together with the goddess Māmakī in a stacked palace of immeasurable dimensions. The text takes the form of a dialogue in which the goddess petitions the deity to reveal methods of accomplishment associated with the nectar of the vajra, a subject connected to purification, transformation, and the esoteric powers of enlightened body, speech, and mind. Notably, the translation opens with what is presented as the fourth and final chapter of a separate but related work, the Great Secret of the Immovable, before the Vajra Amṛta Tantra itself begins, suggesting a close editorial relationship between these two tantric cycles within the Kangyur collection.

This is the fourth chapter, the chapter on secret mantra, from the noble tantra of the Great Secret of the Immovable. The noble tantra of the Great Secret of the Immovable is complete.

In the Indian language: Vajra Amṛta Tantra. In the Tibetan language: the Tantra of the Vajra Nectar. Homage to the glorious Vajrasattva.

Thus did I hear at one time. The Blessed One, the Tathāgata — the essence of the body, speech, and mind of all tathāgatas — dwelt in the secret lotus of the Vajra Nectar. In the stacked palace of immeasurable dimensions, together with Māmakī, the Blessed Vajra One sported in delight. Then the goddess made this request in verse:

O Great Secret, supremely abiding,
supreme tantra of the Vajra Nectar —
what you, a god, previously proclaimed,
the method for accomplishing the nectar —
please now declare it again in great bliss.

Then the Blessed Vajra One spoke:

The Vajra Nectar, great bliss,
beyond thought, inexhaustible, subtle,
purposeful, transcending the sense faculties,
excellent, peaceful, utterly pure —
this is what is called the Vajra Nectar.

Then the Blessed Vajra One, with loving desire for Māmakī, through the meditative absorption of the Vajra Nectar, made the Great Secret manifest directly. The supreme secret gnosis — the Vajra Nectar — in all its tantras I shall now explain.

Through firm embrace and milking,
through playful union in joy,
through melody, sweet music, and dance,
the supreme tantra of the Vajra Nectar
will thus be swiftly accomplished.

What is called the nectar fluid
assuredly generates the three classes of beings.
For you, the joyful one, the fair-faced,
I shall explain the method of its accomplishment.
Listen with full attention to these words.
Setting it forth in brief, I shall explain:

For the benefit and welfare of sentient beings,
the supreme Vajra Nectar of union —
whatever yogin comes to know it
will be swiftly liberated from the bonds of saṃsāra.
It is beyond all teaching and vast in gnosis;
its accomplishment (siddhi, Skt. siddhi) fulfills the three worlds.

For the accomplishment of body, speech, and mind,
the practitioner engages in bliss.
Through the union of vajra and lotus,
the Vajra Nectar is drawn forth.
Milk is poured into the bhaga's lotus;
the Vajra Māmakī pours milk.
The Vajra Nectar, great bliss —
so the Blessed Vajra One declared.

By pouring milk into the bhaga's lotus,
at that moment the joyful goddess
requested the secret maṇḍala of delightful experience
that arises from the Vajra Nectar:

By what ritual sequence
should the offering maṇḍala be established?
Here, how exactly is the offering performed,
and likewise, what is the maṇḍala like?
Great bliss is difficult to attain —
please speak in full delight.

Then the Blessed Vajra One, greatly delighted by the milking of the Vajra Nectar and great bliss, laughed and spoke thus:

For the accomplishment of body, speech, and mind,
and in order to liberate beings from the ocean of saṃsāra,
through the practice of great bliss union,
Māmakī here drew this design.
Having drawn a triangular maṇḍala,
place within it the secret lotus of Māmakī.
Inside, bearing the heart-essence,
make an eight-petaled lotus.
At the center, the great secret vidyā
with the chief figure — three faces, six hands:
the first face is black,
the right face is desired to be white,
the left is declared to be red.
Adorned with divine ornaments,
wearing divine garments and a flower garland,
anointed with divine fragrances,
performing the pacifying divine accomplishment,
bearing the vajra of body, speech, and mind.
The outer maṇḍala also
is to be thoroughly arrayed with four gates.
At the east, place the Frowning One;
at the south, the Terrifier of the Terrifying;
at the west, the Mare-Formed One;
at the north, the Leader of the Host.
The wise one shall draw the eight vidyās
equally on the eight lotus petals.

The Beautiful One and the Peaceful Face,
the Moon Woman and the Conch-Bearing One,
the Hare-Holder and the Maṇḍala Woman,
the Hare-Holder's Image, the Delightful,
and those who bring ease to the mind —
the eight vidyās and the eight virtuous ones,
all with three faces and six arms,
of varied forms, joy-inspiring —
shall be drawn steadily
in the cardinal directions and the intermediate directions.

As offerings: the vīṇā and the drum likewise,
and incense and flowers and so forth;
song, dance, and worship,
with joyful festivity and union —
the great bliss of the vajra nectar
of the secret wheel is to be accomplished.

From mother, maidservant, daughter,
sister, and kinswoman alike,
take from them the vajra nectar
and offer to the secret maṇḍala.
By taking the nectar from one's mother,
it becomes equal to the vajra nectar.
The sister, free of illness, with white healthy teeth;
the daughter grants all that is desired;
the brahmin woman accomplishes what is meaningful.
Having been desired for the secret maṇḍala,
let the tongue be rolled,
and with the mouth receive it from the lotus.
The great bliss of the vajra nectar —
from that, accomplishment swiftly arises.

After the manner of a bee enjoying itself,
enjoy every woman according to desire.
Drawing together, pressing close, and stretching out,
lifting up, called the full extension —
by performing these binding unions,
accomplish the three levels.

With great flesh and with great blood,
and likewise with excrement, urine, and semen —
these are the five vajra nectars,
to be accomplished day by day.
The great bliss of the vajra nectar:
if one desires accomplishment for oneself,
then the practitioner shall supremely accomplish
all purposes through these means.

Magnetization, ensnaring, conflict,
killing, expelling, and the rest —
with the great bliss of the vajra nectar,
let every activity be performed throughout.

The great bliss of the vajra nectar —
this was proclaimed by the Blessed Vajra One.

Thus ends the first chapter, which presents the mode of performing the secret maṇḍala, from the King of Tantras called the Vajra Nectar of Great Bliss, the Vajra Nectar Tantra of the vajra of body, speech, and mind of all Tathāgatas.

Then the goddess (lha mo) who had been gladdened,
Māmakī of the fully bloomed lotus,
having embraced him firmly,
poured milk upon all his senses.
Holding her lips with her mouth, pouring milk,
the siddha mantra is to be recited.
Holding his body also with the vajra bond,
the Vajra-Bearing One performed the secret act,
having inserted the vajra into the lotus.
In passion and delight, let desire be indulged.

With the pleasant sounds of the siddha tone,
the cuckoo bird (kaukkila) and flute and such —
sport with the goddess Māmakī.
With the great bliss of the vajra nectar,
grasp the breast and press it,
and trace upon it with the nails.
And removing the garment,
look upon her above and below.
Having poured milk into the fully bloomed lotus,
words of joy are also to be spoken:
"O Māmakī of the blooming lotus,
O great goddess — excellent, excellent!
With eternal attachment and desire,
I shall delight in you.
Whatever desire you have requested,
I bestow upon you supremely."

Then the goddess Māmakī
addressed these words in turn:
"The Blessed One himself, in suchness,
manifested by the awakening mind —
just as inexhaustible wisdom plays
through the utterly subtle,
so does a god act in this way.
Through the union of the two senses,
doubt arises in me concerning these things,
and I become supremely amazed."

Then the Blessed Vajra One
explained the great bliss of the vajra nectar:
"Listen to this, O Māmakī —
the penetrating union of wisdom,
exceedingly subtle, the great secret,
the essential nature of the object of wisdom.
This I shall expound:
that which supremely accomplishes buddha awakening,
fearless, inexhaustible, and permanent,
without beginning, beyond the senses.
Both being and non-being,
with sense faculties and without sense faculties,
not seen in any element of arising —"

"It is seen through the path of union.
The union is taught as being of two kinds:
outer and inner.
The outer is the aggregate of form and the rest,
the arising of all kinds at the conventional level —
diverse, moving and unmoving.
Throughout all three realms without remainder,
that single one pervades everything:
all is consciousness alone.
Why is it that sentient beings, who are without self,
have a stable aggregate of consciousness?
Even those dull-minded people of a degenerate age
do not understand this at all.
That which has not arisen from mind, yet is the face of mind —
the essential nature of wisdom and its object —
all is that single consciousness.
When it becomes the condition for the body,
it becomes one with phenomena and a single phenomenon,
and at that time all things are transformed.
Why is it that among the many phenomena,
though phenomena are themselves many,
liberation — like rivers flowing to the ocean —
is one alone
and cannot be found as many?
How it dwells in great abundance within:
this is obtained only from the lips of the guru.

The lotus of the bhaga (the sacred feminine) is filled with space,
adorned by the five spaces.
Endowed with heart and pith,
it is enveloped by eight petals.
At its center there dwells the Vajra One,
possessed of the nectar of the awakening mind.
The bindu, freed from partial, non-dharmic aspects,
is fully relinquished.
Perpetually flowing there is the nectar —
the form of the nectar of the awakening mind (bodhicitta),
the foundation of all the elements,
pervading above and below throughout.
By it, seeing and hearing,
smelling and tasting,
touching and the enjoyment of pleasure,
food, garlands of flowers,
ornaments and robes as adornments,
various sounds celebrated by voice,
things to be taken up and held,
sport and movement,
sitting and likewise lying down,
waking and turning,
all that is seen in dreams,
laughter and likewise noise,
exceedingly mind-capturing dance,
and the beating of drums —
all these various things being spoken of here,
the forms of madness and intoxication,
delusion and desire in their very suchness,
the formless one possessed of many forms,
male and female and the neither —
the self of all those possessed of many forms —
like a wish-fulfilling jewel of varied appearance:
having pervaded by the nature of pervasion,
that very suchness abides in supreme greatness.

This, O Māmakī, is suchness:
there is no recitation and no grasping,
there is no act of counting on a rosary at all,
nor should food be abandoned.
Having made offering to the lotus of the bhaga,
the nectar is to be consumed by the wise.
The great bliss of the vajra nectar —
by that, suchness itself is accomplished.
That which is the channel through which the nectar flows —
the lotus at the center of the bhaga —
among the thirty-two other channels,
that one is central, that is the chief.
Far superior to all the others,
nine channels are present.
The vidyās (awareness-holders) that reside at the nine gates
are to be venerated by the practitioner.
The five goddesses of sensual qualities
always gaze together with the Vajra One.
When one practices them supremely in this way,
the Omniscient One declares this 'freedom from attachment.'
These supreme and excellent great secrets
are taught, O beautiful Māmakī, as union.
The various Tathāgatas such as Vairocana and the rest —
this is not known even by the Tathāgatas.
Eternal joy of mind's happiness:
this supreme one is excellently revealed through union."

Upon seeing the secret lotus,
and the greatly expansive Māmakī,
the great bliss of the vajra nectar —
this suchness I shall explain.

This concludes the second chapter, called "The Display of the Wisdom of Suchness and Union," from the Great Tantra of the Vajra Nectar, the Great Bliss of the Vajra — the vajra of body, speech, and mind of all the Tathāgatas.

Then that goddess of joy,
adorned with anklets of hara bells,
taking up this melody,
delights the great bodhisattva.
From the Vajra of Great Bliss, the Evoker —

"I seek the supreme syllable.
Great bliss in the lotus of the bhaga —
this is the vajra's meaning, great bliss.

She who holds the earth, the garland of sounds,
and the six-limbed one, and others — the manner of gathering the peripheral retinues:
the melodious sound of the various, the common, and the pure
is to be taken up.

Having taken up those pure melodic tones,
through the display of joy and mental ease,
with the vajra nectar of great bliss,
accomplish the awakening of the buddhas.

Having taught through the secret maṇḍala,
suchness has resolved my doubts.
By your grace, Vajrin,
I shall ever be engaged in primordial wisdom."

The Blessed One free from attachment,
this arising of the vajra nectar —
the union that is the arising of the Vajra Vehicle,
I shall now set forth.

"Yet I still have doubt:
the display of the two mudrās —
how does the perfect mudrā arise,
and what are their mantras?

From what do the mantras arise?
And in particular, also the gods —
the god is uncertain about this very point.
Please tell me in brief."

Then the Blessed One, the Vajrin, spoke the Great Bliss, the Vajra Nectar: "The arising of the mudrās and mantras — hear it, O Māmakī!

Three kinds of mudrā are taught, arising from body, speech, and mind.
Body becomes the supreme mudrā.
Speech is the mudrā, the activity of mantra.
The mudrā of the sign of mind
abides in meditation, in the image, in the non-dual.

From the Great Tantra of the Vajra Nectar,
the action mudrā (karma-mudrā) is to be known.
The sign mudrā (samaya-mudrā) is not drawn.
The mudrā directly perceived is form.
The god is the goddess, the woman.
Through the bliss of all the senses,
make offering at the lotus of the bhaga.
Through union of the vajra and the lotus,
accomplish all activities.

Just as the great wish-fulfilling tree,
with its thousands of hundreds of branches,
and its roots and what is held by it,
all become a condition for birth —
so too the channel through which nectar flows
is the mother of mantras and mudrās,
and the arising of these mantras
dwells at the center of the bhaga.

Then the Blessed One, the Vajrin, with the Great Bliss of the Vajra Nectar, having inserted the vajra into Māmakī, declared: 'Hear, O Māmakī — the characteristic of the mantras.

First is the union with Vairocana:
Oṃ vajra amṛta mahāsukha —
combined with the four seeds,
joined also with the syllable Svāhā.

And again, the offering to Vairocana:
two syllables, then the fourth.
That which is endowed with the fourth —
the supreme essence of Māmakī —
is solely the eight, and the one at the center.
Adorned with the eight ornaments,
applied to the eight petals,
the mantras of the goddesses are extracted through mantra.

Now hear the mantras of the gatekeepers,
O fortunate Māmakī.
Extracted from the last of those mantras —
by the knower of mantra using the eight seeds,
pressed by the deity of emptiness,
endowed with Vairocana,
combined equally with the syllable Svāhā —
are the bestowers of all accomplishments.

Four are of the gatekeepers.
Four are incense, flowers, and so forth.
With the hermaphrodite syllable at the four corners,
Svāhā and Vairocana also —

The goddess, the secret Māmakī,
with drum and all offerings, is to be worshipped."

The Great Bliss, the Vajra Nectar — thus spoke the Blessed One, the Vajrin.

Then the rites of fire offering shall be explained in sequence.
Through the ritual of the union tantra,
body, speech, and mind are accomplished.

Pacifying and enriching activities,
likewise subjugating and forceful,
and equally battle and dispersal —
all these accomplishments of mantra and mudrā
I shall explain in their entirety: the arising of the Vajra Nectar.

The bodhisattva Vajrasattva, the dancer —

Standing with a belt and support, adorned with all ornaments, the master shall enter the maṇḍala. Having conquered the three realms, the right foot shall be extended and then the left foot extended as well. With both feet level, one immediately purifies the ground by means of the earth-goddess. When the earth is touched, softness arises.

Then, after driving out the obstructors, and likewise the śruta and others — the guru is saluted above. Next, from outside, the goddess of the earth is invited:

Oṃ, ehyehi — O great goddess, steady earth-goddess, mother of the world, who perfectly fulfils all treasures, adorned with divine ornaments, who brings forth the sounds hara nupura — offer worship to Vajrasattva. Having accepted these gifts, may you accomplish the fire-offering rite. Hi hi hi hā svāhā.

Urine, excrement, blood, and semen shall be scattered upon the ground of the maṇḍala. For the purpose of consecration, great flesh shall be offered as incense. Then the vidyā (Skt. vidyā, the wisdom-consort) is introduced: a caṇḍāla woman, a smeared woman, a dancer, a brahmin woman, likewise a queen, a woman of the common class, and a woman of the royal class — these are to be worshipped with fragrance and incense and adorned with divine ornaments. On the five limbs, the syllable Hrī; in the center of the bhaga the syllable Hūṃ. Locanā, Tārā, Pāṇḍaravāsinī, and Māmakī — one shall visualize these. Into the center of the bhaga the master shall cast flowers alone.

Then the mantra-practitioner shall everywhere measure and mark the fire-offering pit. For pacifying rites it shall be round, measured and drawn with a cord one cubit across. Having dug half a cubit into the earth, it is painted with white chalk. Everywhere it is drawn evenly after the manner of a wheel, with equal sides. For enriching rites, it is one and a half cubits, and one cubit shall be dug. It is square with equal sides, drawn with black soot and an iron stylus. For wrathful rites it is triangular, twenty finger-breadths wide; when dug to ten finger-breadths, in its center a blazing vajra-wheel is drawn, agitated by a garland of flames. Likewise for subjugation and summoning, a perfect crescent moon is drawn.

Having properly laid out the fire-offering pit, the directional orientation shall also be indicated. Facing north, the mantra-practitioner shall undertake pacifying rites. For enriching rites, east is specified; for wrathful rites, south; for subjugation, west alone is taught; for summoning and dispersing it is likewise. For wrathful rites it is always black; for subjugation and the rest, the five colors are used.

With rice, sesame, milk, butter, and honey combined, together with the five nectars, one performs the fire offering for pacification and enrichment. All the wood for burning shall be fragrant, milk-sap-bearing wood. Various foods or torma offerings shall be given to all elemental spirits. Whatever torma the practitioner offers is given with the great mudrā of the blood-drinker. Whatever is desired in one's mind is always accomplished by the yogin.

During summer the season is for pacifying; for enriching it is likewise winter. Spring is for wrathful activities — the practitioner shall perform all of them accordingly. Dawn is declared supremely suited for pacifying; for enriching it is the early evening; at midday or at midnight, wrathful rites are to be performed always.

In the center of the fire-offering pit, the yidam deity is generated by the syllable Hūṃ, and one visualizes the face gazing upward — the fire offering is then to be presented to that deity. If a hundred thousand fire offerings are presented, direct, manifest results will arise before one.

First, the fire is to be summoned — the site of offering for all gods, the fierce deity with a blazing garland of light-rays, through whom the awakening of a buddha is accomplished:

Oṃ, ehyehi — come here, you, without any doubt. O fire-god, grant manifest accomplishment. Fierce one, worshipped by the three realms, saluted by Viṣṇu and Brahmā —

Accept these offerings, O holder of the vajra, even though you shift your left foot. Oṃ Hūṃ Hūṃ Hūṃ Phaṭ Phaṭ Svāhā.

First, the offering of fee is presented; then, afterward, the god's torma and flowers are offered.

Oṃ — divine flowers, deeply red, fragrant and exceedingly beautiful: accept these flowers today and bestow your blessing upon them. Hūṃ — this is the mantra of the flower offering.

Oṃ — a lamp with blazing rays of light, dispelling the terrors of the lower realms, the auspicious eye of the gods: O lamp, we pray you to accept this. Hūṃ — this is the mantra of the lamp offering.

Oṃ — the wholesome incense fire of sandalwood and camphor, fragrant and sweet: accept this offering, which is to be endowed with power, and bestow your blessing. Hūṃ — this is the mantra of the incense offering.

Oṃ — the various divine fragrances, adorned with many kinds of camphor, arising from the various syllables and from Hūṃ: we offer this perfume and pray you to accept it. Hūṃ — this is the mantra of the perfume offering.

First, the ritual of worship is to be performed for the fire without any doubt. Afterward, the vidyā-mantra of one's own yidam deity is to be recited and recognized. With the many forms of song, dance, and food, the vidyā-holder and the practitioner are to please the gaṇa assembly through offering.

If accomplishments are desired, one performs the accomplishment of body, speech, and mind. The fire-god, the lama, the vajra-holder — worship them and bring it to completion.

Oṃ — accomplished through the fire-god, leading all sentient beings to happiness: for the sake of the accomplishment of body, speech, and mind, protect us in accordance with the gods. O fire-god, please depart in peace and pacify the ground of the earth. Through moving and unmoving things, grant to the practitioner and to all — grant the ripening fruit of my merit and bestow the accomplishments of mantra. Vajra-nectar, great bliss — so declared the Blessed One with his vajra. This is the fourth chapter, entitled "The Teaching of the Fire-Offering Ritual," from the Great Tantra of the Vajra-Nectar of Great Bliss of the Vajra of Body, Speech, and Mind of All the Tathāgatas: the Vajra-Nectar.

Now the explanation of the eye-ointment is to be given to you.

Take the smoke of the essence of great fat, then burn an owl's head and mix it with abundant blood. Apply it by night, grind it into fine powder alone, and fill the gall of a vulture. With many applicators of human bone, anoint the eyes — this the yogin is always to do.

The preparation of the accomplishment of the eye-ointment arising from the vajra-nectar is to be accomplished on the eighth and fourteenth days of the dark fortnight, within the bhaga. It performs the activities of pacification, enrichment, as well as magnetization and subjugation. It eliminates all manner of fierce possessing spirits, and the four-day fever and poison entirely — O goddess, the Joyful One, we speak truly to you. Worshipped greatly in Rājagṛha, it destroys enemies as well, and likewise blocks obstructors. It is to be recited while bearing the eye-ointment.

Oṃ — wide-eyed one whose form fulfills desire, delighting in excrement, urine, and blood, goddess with three faces and six hands, Māmakī of fierce form — Hrīṃ Hrīṃ Hūṃ Hūṃ A Aṃḥ A Hā Hāṃ Svāhā.

The first face is black; know that the right is white and the left is red. On the right side she holds a sword and a wheel; on the left, a lasso and an iron hook, and likewise a vajra and a bell. Visualize her within the bhaga, and in that place the eye-ointment is to be accomplished.

Fill the bhaga with saffron, then afterward insert a liṅga into it. The mantra to be recited over it is: Oṃ — the sound of the syllable, the opening of the syllable — Vajra Amṛta — is proclaimed. The sound resounds with Phaṭ. Along with the nectar that is its equal, it is to be drawn forth from within the bhaga.

Having recited a hundred thousand mantras over it, one should then use it exclusively for the bindu. The woman will enslave her husband, and even a king with a retinue assembled before him will be brought under sway. Whatever man performs this rite will gain the good fortune of any woman he desires. In the great capital city he will dominate, and his enemies will be utterly destroyed.

Take the fruit of the arka plant, mix it with nectar, and then recite the mantra of the wrathful three-faced form and of the fierce executioner of Yama, Lord of Death. Having prepared it with great flesh and mixed it with beer, give it to drink. The person will be perpetually intoxicated, wander in confusion, become emaciated, and die.

Oṃ Hrīḥ Ṣṭhri Vikṛtānana Sarva Śatrūn Vināśaya Stambhaya Māraya Hūṃ Hūṃ Phaṭ.

The mantrin takes musk, writes the seed syllable upon the bhaga, anoints it evenly with sandalwood, and causes her to dwell together with him. The impure dwelling place becomes great bliss, and retinue and servants will become his in actuality.

Having made an image from urine and excrement, offer incense of great flesh. Bind it with the enemy's hair, smear it with powdered bone, bury it hidden at the enemy's door, and within seven days he will die.

Make a burnt offering with great flesh mixed with urine, excrement, and blood, using the cremation ground's firewood. At a crossroads with a single tree, draw a fire-pit and perform the burnt offering at midday and at midnight. Smearing oneself with ash in the form of the great wrathful executioner of Yama, and meditating while pressing forcefully upon the enemy, he will without fail die.

The Great Vajra Nectar of Great Bliss — thus it was taught by the Blessed Vajra One.

This concludes the fifth chapter, called "Teaching the Great Activity," from the Great Tantra of Vajra Nectar of Great Bliss, the Vajra Nectar Tantra of the body, speech, and mind vajra of all Tathāgatas.

From the Great Vajra Nectar Tantra, the practice of the Glorious Vajra Hūṃkāra will now be briefly explained, along with the sequence of the maṇḍala ritual.

The maṇḍala ritual is drawn from the Trailokavijaya. One draws lines in four cubits or in one and a half cubits. In a maṇḍala resembling the disk of the moon, blazing and swirling with rays of light, one places the Glorious Vajra Hūṃkāra — three-faced, likewise six-armed, complete with all ornaments, hands adorned with mudrās, accompanied by four knowledge consorts, seated at the firm breast of Umā, slightly baring his fangs, laughing wrathfully. On the right is drawn an arrow, and likewise on the left a bow. In the fourth pair of hands is drawn: the second pair holds the breast. All the knowledge consorts have three faces. Likewise the gatekeepers with iron hooks, and likewise flowers, incense, lamps, fine perfumes — all are to be drawn truly. The one who enchants with a garland, likewise the one who enchants with melody, the dancing woman too has six arms.
