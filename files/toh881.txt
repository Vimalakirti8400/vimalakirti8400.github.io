The Noble One Hundred and Eight Names of Kṣitigarbha, Together with the Dhāraṇī-Mantra (Ārya Kṣitigarbhāṣṭottaraśatakanāma Dhāraṇīmantrasahita)

The Noble One Hundred and Eight Names of Kṣitigarbha, Together with the Dhāraṇī-Mantra, known in Tibetan as Phagpa Sai Nyingpoi Tsen Gya Tsa Gyepa Zung Ngak Dang Chepa, is a short devotional and incantatory work centered on the bodhisattva Kṣitigarbha, whose name in Sanskrit means "Earth Womb" or "Earth Matrix." It opens with homage to Kṣitigarbha as great king of the maṇḍala and gives his dhāraṇī. In verse, the recitation causes the vajra maṇḍala and Mount Kailāsa to shake, and Vajrapāṇi then praises Kṣitigarbha for a mantra that averts the suffering and faults of beings, so that those who hear it remain unstained by saṃsāra, like lotuses rising from the mud. The text belongs to the genre of litanies of one hundred and eight names joined with protective and meritorious mantras. It is Toh 881 in the Kangyur, where it is found among the dhāraṇī texts.

Then, as soon as this dhāraṇī
That bestows good qualities had been spoken,
The sublime vajra maṇḍala
Shook, together with snowy Mount Kailāsa.
To Kṣitigarbha, lord of the assembly,
The Blessed One, the Vajra Holder (Vajrapāṇi), spoke:
"Excellent, excellent, greatly fortunate one!
You have made the face of the earth vast.
Your dhāraṇī is widely renowned,
And brings forth immeasurable supreme qualities.
Those who hear what averts
The suffering and faults of all beings,
Even as they wander through saṃsāra,
Are not stained by any fault,
Just as lotuses in the mud
Are not stained by the faults of the mud.
Whoever among embodied beings
Holds your secret mantra,
Not even a single one of them
Will turn back from awakening.
Whatever wise person, having heard
The supreme dhāraṇī-mantra, takes it to heart with faith
Will certainly attain
All good qualities; of this there is no doubt."

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

Within the list of epithets, the word rendered "Subhūti" (following the house glossary) may here simply be an epithet meaning "of excellent prosperity" rather than an allusion to the disciple Subhūti. "The Vajra Holder" (rdo rje can) is taken as Vajrapāṇi, who is named later in the list. Several epithets (for example "having the way of the wind, possessed of a foundation") are terse and their exact sense is uncertain. "Maheśvara" in the list is taken as one of the epithets applied to Kṣitigarbha, like Indra, Yama, Viṣṇu and Brahmā later in the list; it could also be read generically as "great lord".  The opening line, literally "possessing a maṇḍala with yellow veins/lines," is obscure. It may describe a yellow-hued disc or aura.

The speaker who says "I have seen nothing on earth" is "the vajra, supreme lord of the three worlds." This may refer to Vajradhara answering Vajrasattva's praise, but the Tibetan does not name him explicitly.

The lines on Meru, Kailāsa, and Malaya read the particle "yas" as comparative ("more than") with "non pa," "to overpower or surpass." An alternative reading is "overpowering the profound domain, like Meru, Kailāsa, and Malaya."

"Three worlds" renders sa gsum, the three levels of the world (below, upon, and above the earth). It is distinct from the three realms (khams gsum).

The Tibetan has "mi brgya rtsa brgyad" in one line, evidently a scribal slip for "ming brgya rtsa brgyad," "hundred and eight names."
