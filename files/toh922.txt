The Noble Dhāraṇī Called "The Crest Jewel" (Āryacūḍamaṇināmadhāraṇī)

The Noble Dhāraṇī Called "The Crest Jewel," known in Sanskrit as the Ārya Cūḍamaṇi Nāma Dhāraṇī and in Tibetan as Pakpa Tsuk gi Norbu Shejawai Zung, is a short devotional and protective text centered on the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara. It opens by paying homage to all the buddhas, bodhisattvas, śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas, to the Three Jewels, and to the fully awakened buddhas of the ten directions, whose pure minds pervade all that can be known. It then praises Avalokiteśvara as the great compassionate one who grants fearlessness, protects from want, liberates from fear, ends epidemic disease and harm, pacifies discord and conflict, and overcomes malevolent spirits, setting the stage for the recitation of the dhāraṇī itself. The work is Toh 922 in the Kangyur, where it belongs to the class of dhāraṇī texts, the incantations valued in the Tibetan tradition for their protective and beneficial power.

In the language of Tibet: The Noble Dhāraṇī Called "The Crest Jewel."

Homage to all the buddhas, bodhisattvas, noble śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas. Homage to the Three Jewels. Homage to the tathāgatas, arhats, perfectly complete buddhas of the ten directions, whose minds, supremely wondrous and utterly pure, pervade without exception all that is knowable, which is without end.

Homage to the bodhisattva, the great being, the noble Avalokiteśvara, who is endowed with great compassion; who gives fearlessness; who fully protects from all want; who liberates from all fears; who destroys epidemic disease and all harm; who thoroughly pacifies all discord, disputes, quarrels and battles; who conquers all evil; who destroys all bhūtas, vetālas, untimely death, bad dreams, and the vināyakas among the obstructors; who turns back hostile armies, famine, and all fears of grief; who draws to himself all the gods, nāgas, yakṣas, rākṣasas, gandharvas, asuras, garuḍas, kinnaras and mahoragas; and who destroys all fears of poison, smallpox, pustules, killers, sorcery, vetālas and ill omen.

He clears away all killers
And carries off all evil,
Increasing wealth and grain.
That my sons and grandsons,
My servants and retinue may always flourish,
This Averter should be recited.

When the gods and asuras joined battle,
Śakra (Indra) was defeated by the asuras.
Then, in the greatest haste,
He came before Lokeśvara,
And, his heart stricken with terror,
He spoke these words:
"Lokeśvara, I am defeated by the asuras.
How am I to strive?
Lord of the World, upon me
Look always with your divine gaze."
Having heard the words of Śakra,
The Lord spoke thus:
"Lord of the gods, listen to me.
I shall give you protection."

"Lord of the gods, long ago, in times past, in the world system called Beheld by the World, I received from the Tathāgata, the arhat, the perfectly complete buddha Lokeśvaraprabha the Averter called the Crest Jewel, which was spoken by ten million tathāgatas.

Whatever wise person writes down this vidyā
And binds it upon the crown of the head,
Or recites it, or reads it,
Will be free of every fear.
It always guards and shelters,
And brings victory in battle;
Evil and bad dreams are pacified,
And merit and good fortune greatly increase.
Therefore, lord of the gods,
One who wishes to be victorious
Should write it and bind it upon the crown of the head,
And always keep it in mind.
It is not for battle alone:
This always carries off evil,
Causes women to give birth to sons,
Fully protects the one dwelling in the womb,
Brings about every excellence,
And utterly destroys all evil.
It is called the peerless Averter;
It turns back what befalls the limbs.

Lord of the gods, take up this great queen of vidyās called the Averter, which benefits all sentient beings, defeats the asuras, and thoroughly pacifies all hostile armies."

Homage to the Buddha. Homage to the Dharma. Homage to the Saṅgha. Homage to the seven perfectly complete buddhas, together with the Saṅgha of śrāvakas and together with the bodhisattvas.

The Averter called the Crest Jewel purifies all karmic obscurations, thoroughly pacifies all diseases, brings pacification in every respect, and gathers all merit; the buddhas and

bodhisattvas it perfectly reveals, every one; and it brings glory in every way. By writing it down, teaching it, reading it, and holding it in mind, one protects oneself and others. Likewise, through ritual bathing and the fire offering, it thoroughly pacifies all epidemic diseases and every harm.

The name of the world system, rendered literally as "Beheld by the World," could not be identified with confidence as a Sanskrit name. The lines "It is called the peerless Averter; it turns back what befalls the limbs" are obscure in the Tibetan. "Peerless" interprets the word "without," and the sense of the final line is uncertain. "Untimely death" renders a phrase meaning literally "dying in an evil manner." In the verses, Lokeśvara ("Lord of the World") refers to Avalokiteśvara.  The opening clause continues the previous piece, which ended "the buddhas and."
