The Great Perfection of All Phenomena, the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind (Sarvadharmamahāsantibodhicittakulayarājā)

The Great Perfection of All Phenomena, the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, known in Tibetan as Kunjé Gyalpo and given a Sanskrit title rendered in the text as Sarva Dharma Mahā Santi Bodhi Citta Kulaya Rājā, is Toh 828 in the Kangyur, where it stands among the Ancient, or Nyingma, tantras. It is the principal scripture of the Mind Class of the Great Perfection, a tantra set in the realm of Akaniṣṭha, in which the primordial buddha, the All-Creating King, who is the awakening mind itself, teaches his own retinue and the interlocutor Sattvavajra. In eighty-four chapters it presents all phenomena of saṃsāra and nirvāṇa as the display of self-arisen wisdom, unborn and uncontrived, and teaches that realization is reached not through effort, striving or accomplishment but by resting in that natural state just as it is. The closing chapters set out who is a fit recipient for the teaching, list the names of the tantra, and entrust it to Sattvavajra, and the colophon credits its translation to the Indian preceptor Śrīsiṃha and the Tibetan monk Vairocana.

In the language of India: sarva dharma mahā santi bodhi citta kulaya rājā

In the language of Tibet: The Great Perfection of All Phenomena, the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind.

Homage to the Blessed One, the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind.

At the time when this was spoken, in the abode of Akaniṣṭha, in the sphere of the expanse of space of the nature of reality itself, the abode of mind itself, in the celestial palace of wisdom free of obscuration, there was this retinue, manifested from his own nature, his own essence and his own compassionate wisdom. That is: the retinue of his own nature, the retinue called the dharmakāya; the retinue of his own essence, the retinue of the saṃbhogakāya called earth; the retinue of his own essence, the retinue of the saṃbhogakāya called water; the retinue of his own essence, the retinue of the saṃbhogakāya called fire; the retinue of his own essence, the retinue of the saṃbhogakāya called wind; and the retinue of his own essence, the retinue of the saṃbhogakāya called space. Further, there was this retinue of emanations manifested from compassionate wisdom: the retinue called the sentient beings of the desire realm, the retinue called the sentient beings of the form realm, and the retinue called the sentient beings of the formless realm. Further, there were these four kinds of retinue that look upon his own nature: the retinue called Utmost Yoga (atiyoga), the retinue called Thorough Yoga (anuyoga), the retinue called Great Yoga (mahāyoga), and the retinue called Sattva Yoga. Since his own nature, his own essence and his own compassion admit no division in their nature, they are together as one. Further, there was this retinue that realizes his own nature: the retinue that abides in the buddhas who have passed before, the retinue for whose sake the buddhas who dwell at present act, and the retinue arising from the buddhas who will come hereafter. By their nature, which admits no division, they dwelt together as one.

Then, in order to bless the whole retinue as his own nature, the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, caused the whole retinue to dissolve into the heart of his own mind itself. He made self-arisen wisdom clear. Then, so that all might be endowed with the nature of reality, he gathered them all together and abided as one great sphere (bindu).

Then Sattvavajra came forth from that very nature which abode as one great sphere, and sat before the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, radiant with clear faith from a joyful mind. The All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, said to Sattvavajra:

"Sattvavajra, how wonderful!
A joyful mind has arisen, how wonderful!
Radiant with clear faith, how wonderful!
Arisen from me, how wonderful!"

When he had said this, Sattvavajra then asked:

"O All-Creating King, Teacher of teachers,
Is the Teacher himself the sphere free of elaboration?
Are all the retinues too the sphere free of elaboration?
Are all the teachings too the sphere free of elaboration?
Are time and place too the sphere free of elaboration?
If everything, all of it, has the nature of the sphere,
What does the Teacher of teachers teach?
As for the retinue, around what does the retinue gather?
What teaching does he teach to the retinue?
And how are time and place one?"

So he asked. Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke thus to Sattvavajra:

"O great being, set your mind on the sound! I shall explain the meaning in sound. O great being:

I, mind itself, the All-Creating King,
Am the heart of all phenomena whatsoever.
The heart, unelaborated, is the sphere from the very beginning;
The sphere is the meaning free of elaboration from the very beginning.
Teacher and teaching, retinue, time and place,
Since they arise from me, are the sphere from the very beginning;
My nature, the sphere, is the teaching."

So he spoke.

From the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, this is the first chapter, the chapter on the setting.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, abided in the meditative absorption called All Phenomena Arising from Oneself. Then Sattvavajra rose from the nature of the retinue, sat radiant before the face of the Teacher, the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, and asked:

"O All-Creating King, Teacher of teachers,
I have gathered and sit in your retinue.
What is the aim that I seek?
Please teach the aim that I seek."

So he asked. Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, arrayed all phenomena from his own nature, his own essence and his own compassion. That is, from his one great self-arisen wisdom arose five great self-arisen wisdoms, namely: the great self-arisen wisdom of aversion, the great self-arisen wisdom of attachment, the great self-arisen wisdom of delusion, the great self-arisen wisdom of jealousy, and the great self-arisen wisdom of pride. Those five self-arisen wisdoms brought forth the five great causes of ornament and arrayed the three great realms on which the world rests. The five bodies of the causes of ornament were taken up as one, namely: the body of the cause of ornament called earth, the body of the cause of ornament called water, the body of the cause of ornament called fire, the body of the cause of ornament called wind, and the body of the cause of ornament called space; in these all bodies were taken up as one. Dividing the families of the five wisdoms one by one, namely the family of self-arisen wisdom as aversion, the family of self-arisen wisdom as attachment, the family of self-arisen wisdom as delusion, the family of self-arisen wisdom as jealousy, and the family of self-arisen wisdom as pride, he arrayed the families of the five self-arisen wisdoms as the nature of reality of embodied beings with bodies of ornament. And the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, himself abides just as the nature of reality of his own nature has been arrayed.

Then Sattvavajra sat once again before the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, and asked:

"O Teacher, All-Creating King,
From the self-arisen wisdom that is your single mind
Five kinds of self-arisen wisdom arose,
And from them the five causes of ornament arose;
In taking up these five bodies of the five wisdoms,
What great purpose does the All-Creating King have?"

So he asked. Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke again:

"O great being, the purpose is this:
Apart from me, the maker, the All-Creating King,
There is no other maker at all.
The maker of the nature of reality too is none other than me.
The one who sets forth the three teachers too is none other than me.
The one who sets forth the gathering of the retinue too is none other than me.
Likewise, the one who sets forth the nature of reality is none other than me.
Sattvavajra, to you
I shall teach my nature.
I have three kinds of nature,
And my nature is the awakening mind.
To teach the nature of purity:
The threefold perfection, the nature of reality, is pure.
To teach the nature of completion:
Through the gathering of the three kinds of purpose,
It abides pervading all, like space.
To teach the nature of what is called mind:
It is the All-Creating King, unceasing, without remainder;
Having made all, it has made it, without remainder, into the awakening mind."

So he spoke.

From the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, this is the second chapter, the chapter teaching the nature of reality by making it manifest.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke this account of how he, the maker, arose before all phenomena:

"O great being, let your mind enter the faculty of hearing! Let the faculty of hearing understand the sound!

O, I am the All-Creating King;
I am the all-creating awakening mind.
Before I existed,
There was no heart from which phenomena arise.
Before I existed,
There was no king who creates all phenomena.
Before I existed,
There was from the very beginning nothing called a teacher.
Before I existed,
There was from the very beginning nothing called a teaching.
Before I existed,
There was from the very beginning nothing called a retinue.
Sattvavajra, let no doubt arise:
Great being, you yourself
Are an emanation of my essence."

So he spoke. Then Sattvavajra asked the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind:

"O All-Creating King, Awakening Mind,
You are great from the very beginning.
Since teacher and teaching,
Retinue, and all phenomena,
Have been made by you, the maker,
All phenomena, however they appear,
Appear as made by you, the maker.
Is there one teacher, or how many kinds?
Is there one teaching, or how many kinds?
Is the retinue one, or how many kinds?"

So he asked. Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke again:

"O, self-arisen wisdom is one, the teacher of all.
As my nature appears in three ways,
The teachers arose in number as three kinds.
Since I am one, as suchness,
The nature of reality of all phenomena is suchness;
There is nothing other than me, the awakening mind.
The teachers who arose from me, the three bodies,
Taught the teaching to the retinue in three kinds.
In order to bring forth my essence as one
And to teach my nature as one,
In the retinue of me, the All-Creating, all too are one.
Because of the teachers who arose from me, the three bodies,
There are three kinds of distinction in the retinue."

So he spoke. Sattvavajra asked:

"O All-Creating King, Teacher of teachers,
For the teachers of the three bodies who arose from you,
Is there or is there not a path that goes to your ground?
If there is not, do they reach your ground or not?
If there is, do they reach it by traveling or without traveling?"

"O great being, listen!
For the teachers of the three bodies who arose from me
There is no path to be traveled to my ground;
It is the teaching of my self-arisen wisdom.
To the retinues of those teachers of the three bodies,
The paths of liberation are taught as five kinds,
As the path of all the buddhas of the three times.
The five paths of the five self-arisen wisdoms
Are the five kinds: attachment, aversion, delusion,
Pride and jealousy;
These five kinds of self-arisen wisdom are the path of all."

So he spoke.

"O All-Creating King, Teacher of teachers,
As for the teaching of the five paths of self-arisen wisdom,
How has what is called self-arisen arisen?
To what is the term wisdom applied?
To what does the meaning of the word path refer?
Please explain the meaning of how these five kinds,
Attachment, aversion, delusion, pride,
And jealousy and the rest, are the five paths."

So he asked.

"O great being,
The path of the five kinds of self-arisen wisdom:
Self-arisen means arisen without causes and conditions.
Wisdom means clear without ceasing.
The path, being without causes and conditions, need not be traveled;
The five untraveled self-arisen ones are explained as the five paths.
In attachment, the desire is to desire phenomena as a self;
The attachment is attachment to that desire for a self.
In aversion, the heart is the nature that is unborn;
The aversion is aversion toward the varied miraculous displays.
Delusion is the meditative absorption without concepts,
In which there is no distinguishing or excluding within the equality of the nature of reality.
The I of pride is the unerring awakening mind;
It is victorious as suchness from the very beginning.
Within the oneness of all as suchness,
Jealousy is jealousy toward superimposing and denying what is and what is not.
This is the teaching of the five paths that cannot be traveled.

O great being, listen!
All phenomena whatsoever, however they appear,
Arose from these five paths of self-arisen wisdom.
The desire realm too is self-arisen wisdom;
The form realm too is self-arisen wisdom;
The formless realm too is self-arisen wisdom.
Because I, the All-Creating King, made them,
I explain all that I have made as the essence of wisdom.
All the sentient beings of the three realms in the six lineages of beings
I have not explained as anything other than self-arisen wisdom.
Other than that, I have not explained the awakening mind,
The forefather of all the buddhas.
In the nature of me, the All-Creating King,
The nature of reality is taught as three kinds.
The unborn nature of reality does not appear as a thing:
That is taught as the formless realm.
The miraculous display of birth appearing as form:
That is taught as the form realm.
Bringing forth the essence of compassionate wisdom,
So that compassion may exhaust the karma of pleasure and pain,
The desire realm is brought forth by compassion."

So he spoke.

From the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, this is the third chapter, the chapter teaching the source of how all phenomena arose from being made by the All-Creating King.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke this teaching on the names of his own nature:

"O great being, Vajra, listen!
I am self-arisen wisdom from the very beginning;
I am the heart of all phenomena from the very beginning;
I am the All-Creating King, the awakening mind.
Being, understand these names of mine!
If you, being, understand these names of mine,
You will understand all phenomena without exception.

What is called I is the heart;
It is the heart of all phenomena.
What is called self-arisen,
Being the heart without causes and conditions,
Is beyond all effort and striving.
What is called wisdom,
Being unceasing and unobscured,
Reveals all phenomena without exception.
I am called the awakening mind.
The meaning of from the very beginning
Is that it abides from the very first.
What is called all phenomena:
All teachers too are the nature of reality;
All teachings too are the nature of reality;
Retinue, place and time too are the nature of reality;
There is not a single thing that is not the nature of reality.
The meaning of the word heart
Is the heart from which all arises:
From the nature of the awakening mind
The three teachers too arose;
The three teachings too arose from it;
Retinue, place and time too arose from it.
It is explained as the heart from which all arises.
I, the heart, mind itself,
Create all phenomena without exception.
All, the word all, is all phenomena.
If one asks which phenomena these are:
Teacher and teaching,
Retinue, place and time, all of them, everything.
What is called creating is the maker;
Since it creates all, teacher and teaching,
Retinue, place and time,
Self-arisen wisdom is the maker.
What is called king
Is that heart, self-arisen wisdom,
Sovereign over every maker,
Sovereign over all who make phenomena.
The meaning of awakening's purity is this:
That heart, the awakening mind,
Being self-arisen and utterly pure from the very beginning,
All that the All-Creating King has made
Being utterly pure as Samantabhadra himself,
Is explained as what is called pure.
The meaning of awakening's completion is this:
That heart, self-arisen wisdom,
Is complete in all, pervading all:
Appearance and existence,
All that is gathered as world and beings,
All the buddhas of the three times,
The sentient beings of the three realms in the six lineages,
And suchness, in all of them.
Therefore it is explained as what is called complete.
The meaning of mind is this:
That heart, self-arisen wisdom,
Enters into all appearance and existence, all worlds and beings,
Masters them, and clearly discerns them.
Therefore it is explained as what is called mind.
That heart without causes and conditions
Masters all and makes everything.

O great being,
If you understand my nature,
You will understand all teachers too,
And you will understand all teachings too;
You will know the thoughts of the retinue,
And you will know that all times and places too are one.
Since all phenomena are me,
If you know this nature of mine,
You will know all phenomena,
And so, beyond all acting and doing, effort and striving,
You will be spontaneously accomplished without effort."

So he spoke.

From the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, this is the fourth chapter, the chapter explaining the names of the I itself.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, taught Sattvavajra this meaning, gathered into a sūtra, teaching his own nature:

"O great being, listen!
My nature is this:
As for existing, it is nothing but one;
As for teaching, it is taught in two ways;
As for arising, it arises as nine vehicles;
As for gathering, it gathers into the Great Perfection.
As for being, it is the awakening mind;
As for abiding, it abides in the expanse of the nature of reality;
As for clarity, awareness is clear like the sky;
As for pervading, it pervades all worlds and beings;
As for arising, it arises as all appearance and existence.
To be shown, it has no thing that is a sign;
To be seen, it is free of any object of focus;
To be known, it cannot be expressed in words.
This heart, which did not arise from causes,
Is free of all that words impute.
If you wish to understand this meaning with certainty,
Consider the example: it is like space.
The meaning: the nature of reality is unborn.
The sign: mind itself is unceasing.
The nature of reality, which is like space,
Is indicated by the example of space.
The nature of reality, which cannot be an object of focus,
Is indicated and taught as beyond focus.
For what cannot be expressed in words,
Words of the inexpressible are spoken,
Teaching it as the essence of the meaning beyond focus.
This meaning, taught in brief,
Is a commentary on the essence of the meaning:
Through this, understand my meaning!
Through this, my meaning is understood.
If my meaning is not understood through this,
Then whatever sounds and words of the meaning are taught,
One will not meet me,
And, straying from me and obscuring me,
One will not see the heart of phenomena."

So he spoke.

From the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, this is the fifth chapter, the chapter of commentary on the meaning.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke these words teaching the Great Perfection in the nature of all phenomena:

"O Sattvavajra, listen to these words!
I, the All-Creating King,
First arrayed my phenomena;
I brought my phenomena into manifestation;
I taught you my essence.
Having taught my nature,
And having indicated the meaning with words and sounds as well,
Since the essence has been taught, you have seen it;
Since the nature has been taught, you know it;
Since it has been put into words, you have heard the meaning as sound."

Having spoken thus, he remained without saying anything at all, abiding as the essence of all phenomena. Sattvavajra rose from within the retinue and sat radiant before the face of the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind. The All-Creating King said, "O great being, you who sit radiant before me, ask whatever meaning you wish!"

Then Sattvavajra asked:

"O All-Creating King, Teacher of teachers,
If all phenomena are your nature,
Why have you arrayed teacher, teaching and retinue?
What is the purpose of the perfections?"

So he asked. Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke thus:

"O great being, listen!
The arraying of phenomena as perfections
Is the nature of my own perfection.
I shall show my nature to you.
Because my nature is free of elaboration and indivisible,
This place, the dharmadhātu, was made by me;
There, nothing other than awakening itself dwells.
Because my nature is unobscured and all-pervading,
The clear sky is the celestial palace of wisdom;
Nothing other than self-arisen wisdom dwells there.
Because I am the essence from which all things arise,
The five great elements, the three realms, and the six classes of beings
Are nothing other than my body, speech and mind;
So my nature has been arrayed by me.
The buddhas of the three times and the sentient beings of the three realms as well
Are my nature, shown to you.
Because my nature is unborn and free of elaboration,
It does not abide; it transcends every sphere of experience.
It does not appear; it transcends the domain of meditative absorption.
Although my nature does not appear to you,
I have made my essence appear to you.
Thus the three realms, the five great elements and the six classes of beings:
From the five awakening minds whose nature is appearance
Arose the five self-arisen essential wisdoms;
As the five objects of the five wisdoms clearly arose,
The five desires arose, the five attachments arose,
And the fruit of the five attachments, ripening each on its own,
Appears as the separate features of the six kinds of beings.
This appearing in such a way is what I have shown to you.
The three bodies and the six vehicles that are taught:
When I taught them, you did not see them;
Each of them is one aspect of my nature,
Each of them is one aspect of my essence:
A body, a speech and a mind for each,
Each an essence that all can see.
That too is the showing of my nature.
I, awakening, the essence of all that is genuine,
I am the essence of unelaborated space,
And I am the great abode of all the buddhas;
So my display, the three realms and six classes of beings, dawns from me.
Happiness and suffering are distinctions made by karma;
Genuinely, unerringly, I have shown my compassion.
To those of the vehicles of cause and result
This scripture of mine, the All-Creating, should not be taught.
If this scripture of mine should be taught to them,
They would say that virtuous and evil actions have cause and result,
And, superimposing on and denying me, the genuine,
They would not meet me, the genuine, for a long time.
I am the Teacher, the All-Creating Awakening Mind.
The awakening mind is the All-Creating King.
The buddhas of the three times were made by the awakening mind.
The sentient beings of the three realms were made by the awakening mind.
The world of appearance and existence, the vessel and its contents, were made by the awakening mind.
Cause and result, example, meaning and reasoning were made to accord.
At the time of the cause, it made the five great elements;
At the time of the result, it made the sentient beings of the three realms.
At the time of the example, it makes space the example for all;
At the time of the meaning, it makes the unborn the meaning of all.
At the time of the sign, it is the all-creating awakening mind;
At the time of the reasoning, it shows self-arisen wisdom.
Cause and result, example, meaning and reasoning were made to accord.
The awakening mind made everything, yet did not make the one.
Making everything, it made the nature of awakening itself;
As for the one that is not made, it need not be made.
Not examining my nature, the All-Creating,
And fixing on the phenomena made by me as real,
Through the power of craving and attachment appearances become solid.
Impermanent, illusory by nature, they will perish.
Theirs is the nature of the blind who know nothing.
Not knowing that it was made by the All-Creating King,
When the six objects appear to the six sense faculties,
They see suffering and so suppress the faculties.
Because they do not know what the All-Creating has made,
They do not have the fruit free of arising and ceasing.
Within the one certainty two truths arise:
In the nature of reality made by me, the All-Creating,
The two truths, ultimate and relative, have arisen;
So those of the cause and result of the two truths have nothing at all.
They do not have the fruit beyond cause and result.
Seeking what is not there, with pure meditative absorption,
And meditating on the limbs of miraculous display that do not arise,
They do not attain the fruit free of accepting and rejecting.
Meditating on the three absorptions of the single meaning,
Meditating step by step on the four phases of approach and accomplishment,
They do not have the fruit beyond striving and accomplishing.
Being spontaneously present, it is free of all doing.
Expressing it as unborn, beyond indication and beyond imputation,
One knows that inexpressible, free of superimposition and denial.
All phenomena are the awakening mind; their example
Is in nature like space, the example for all;
The meaning of the awakening mind is likewise.
Space, wind, water, earth and fire, the five appearances,
Arisen wondrously from the awakening mind, are manifestly awakened.
The appearances of the three worlds, the five paths and the six kinds of beings too,
Unmoved by ripening karma, are manifestly awakened.
The three realms are from the beginning awakened body, speech and mind.
Therefore all appearance and existence, the vessel and its contents,
Just as there is nothing that does not abide within space,
Since the great expanse of the awakening mind is a vast domain,
Buddhas, sentient beings, the vessel and its contents, all abide in it.
In the completely pure nature of reality of all things
There is no duality; it is free of all superimposition and denial.
Whoever has not seen mind, the essence that is nothing at all,
Will not meet the meaning for many eons.
The root of all phenomena is one in the awakening mind.
Buddhas, sentient beings, all appearance and existence, the vessel and its contents,
In the essence of awakening from which all things arise,
Are not even one, yet if counted, they could never be told.
The body and speech of the buddhas, and the body and speech of sentient beings too,
Are the awakening mind, primordially free of apprehended and apprehender.
Whoever has established that freedom from apprehended and apprehender
Has thereby established everything, the root of all phenomena.
The single root sūtra of the awakening mind
Those who delight in thinking and conceptualizing have enumerated in many ways.
For those who delight in duality and in numbers, I have taught it.
In the one mind, the nature of reality, just as it naturally is,
Three aspects of its nature appear within mind;
These three natures are the three supreme arrays,
And that is the nature of reality of the perfections."

"Because all is connected in the nature of the nondual one,
Within the one natural state there are four kinds of joining;
It is shown as the domain of the four kinds of yoga.
The one mind itself is shown as five greatnesses:
Because the nature of mind is endowed with five qualities,
These five natures are given the name of the five greatnesses.
Because talk of 'it is' and 'it is not' arose within the one certainty,
The inconceivable deviations, obscurations, doubts and concepts
Are cleared away by the All-Creating, beyond striving and conception.
In order to clear away doubts of existence and nonexistence, of superimposition and denial,
Through the nonconceptual awakening of suchness
That unerring, definitive scripture settles it.
In order to show clearly the confidence in that very thing,
Through the ten scriptures of decisive confidence,
The meaning of no striving, accomplishing or doing is shown, and that is trusted.
As for the nine true statements spoken about what does not exist:
Within the one essence, nine domains of seeing arose,
And each spoke nine words of truth according to what it sees.
Because everything is complete in me, the All-Creating,
Hold this meaning of the awakening mind as great!
The root of all phenomena is the all-creating awakening mind.
Whatever appears is my essence.
Whatever arises is my miraculous display.
All sounds and words, however they arise,
Are my very meaning brought forth as words and sounds.
The bodies, wisdoms and qualities of the buddhas,
The bodies and habitual tendencies of sentient beings, and so on,
All that is gathered in appearance and existence, the vessel and its contents,
Are primordially the essence of the awakening mind.
As for phenomena that are other than mind,
To the buddhas who came from me and passed away before,
I, the All-Creating King, did not teach them;
To those present now and to those who will come later,
The All-Creating King, awakening, does not teach them."

Thus he spoke. This is the sixth chapter, the chapter on the single root, from The All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke this classification of the phenomena that arise from what he himself made:

"O great being!
Mind is the essence of things just as they are.
In the great bliss, nondual, just as it is,
It is endowed with all aspects, yet has no aspects at all.
Free of elaboration, it abides primordially like space;
Beyond the domain of reference, it cannot be counted even as 'one.'
Since the awakening mind cannot be counted or indicated,
Enumerations arose among the phenomena made by mind.
If one asks what it is that mind has made:
Appearance and existence, the vessel and its contents, buddhas and sentient beings,
Were made from that essence, the nature of mind.
Having been made, what is brought forth and shown,
The appearances of the five great elements and the six classes of beings,
And the two form bodies that act for their benefit,
Are brought forth from the nature of mind and then enumerated."

Thus he declared. Then Sattvavajra asked:

"O All-Creating King, how wonderful!
Samantabhadra, how wonderful!
You, the awakening mind, how wonderful!
When first the awakening mind arose,
Not knowing ourselves or phenomena,
We gathered as your retinue, O Omniscient One.
Since we do not understand the focus of our request,
You, the Omniscient One, have declared
That phenomena are gathered into one.
If you have declared the sūtra of the awakening mind,
What is the meaning of these enumerations
That teach two within the one?"

So he asked. Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, declared:

"Listen! I am primordially, spontaneously present.
I am the spontaneously present essence
Of all the buddhas.
I am the essence of compassion;
Whenever someone delights in something,
In order to act for the sake of whatever they delight in,
I show the meaning of what I have made.
Sattvavajra, listen!
For one who delights in the one,
Since phenomena are gathered into one,
The essence of the awakening mind,
The essence of mind, the All-Creating, is taught.
For those who delight in signs and numbers,
The variety made by the All-Creating King
Is taught as beyond all telling.
That too teaches my nature.
The one is I myself, the essence;
The arising of two is what I have made;
It is the enumeration of made phenomena.
The meaning of number is one and two;
The limit of numbers cannot be expressed.
The inexpressible is the essence of phenomena;
The inexpressible essence is taught as one.
The one is the all-creating awakening mind.
Made phenomena are of two kinds:
From the two, cause and result,
Through the two, ultimate and relative,
As example, meaning, sign and reasoning,
They are explained as two, definitive scripture and provisional scripture.
The cause is the five great elements;
The result is the sentient beings of the three realms.
Taking the sentient beings of the three realms as cause,
The result is given the name of buddha;
Cause and result, example and meaning, are taught in this way.
Teaching the sentient beings of the three realms as the cause,
And foretelling buddhahood as the result:
That is explained as provisional scripture.
These should be divided into two.
The cause is the five great elements;
All made phenomena, every one,
Are mind itself brought forth into manifestation.
Apart from suchness,
Through ignorance, wrong conception and the duality of apprehended and apprehender,
Phenomena of the six objects are fixed upon as real;
So the six perishings and the six supports
Are explained as the world.
Relying on the six objects and the six attachments,
The six attachments, suffering and misery
Are explained as sorrow, sorrow.
What is called passing beyond sorrow, nirvāṇa:
By not conceptualizing phenomena of the six objects,
One is not born into the six places of birth;
Because it passes beyond the six sufferings,
That is called passing beyond sorrow.
From the essence, awakening just as it is,
The five great elements too, dawning as a nature not sought,
Each hold their own characteristic;
And the essence of all, awakening too,
Holds the essence that has no reference.
So these are explained as characteristic upon characteristic.
The five great elements, dawning as nature,
Are the essence that gives rise to all phenomena.
The sentient beings they produce,
Not realizing that they are self-arisen,
Regard the path of conduct as a cause,
And since they hold that results arise from causes,
It is given the name of cause.
Whatever result is attained is explained as a vehicle,
And is taught as abandoning, blocking and purifying.
What is given the name of result
Is the essence, explained as a result arising from a cause.
The nature of that essence,
A nature without arising or perishing,
Is called the vajra.
The two outer classes that accomplish that vajra
Hold that body, speech, mind and wisdom
Are accomplished like the moon in water,
By way of accepting and rejecting.
The inner ones, without accepting and rejecting,
Through the three stages and the essence,
View themselves as basic space and wisdom.
This non-doing that relies on nothing at all,
Having become the essence of all,
Is beyond the activity of doing.
For those who reject the bliss of non-doing,
Predictions of the bliss beyond effort have been set out:
Three eons, and seven lifetimes,
Six months, one year, sixteen months.
By teaching this essence of non-doing
To those of the vehicles that make effort,
Effortless, spontaneously present bliss is in their hands;
Within it there is nothing that is not complete.

"Since the one is complete, the two complete, and all complete,
Activity is blissful as perfection.
The one complete is complete as the awakening mind.
The two complete is complete as what mind has made.
All complete is complete as perfection.
Through this scripture of completeness in the one,
The realization of the buddhas abides in this.
Through this meaning of all complete,
Everything is made perfect.
Whoever abides in this non-doing,
Though in this body a person, a god or a human,
Their realization is the nature of reality, buddha.
If through this they act for the benefit of sentient beings,
Without striving or accomplishing they will come to bliss."

Thus he spoke. This is the seventh chapter, the chapter summarizing the enumerations, from The All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke of this: how, since all phenomena he has made are one in suchness, three natures come to appear within suchness, appearing each on its own.

"O great being!
My single suchness
Appears as the three perfections.
The nature unborn, just as it is,
That suchness, appears as the place.
That place is the suchness of all;
All, having examined it,
Look upon suchness:
Unobscured, self-arisen wisdom
Appears as the teachers of cause and result, the three bodies.
To some it appears as the teacher of the cause;
To some it appears as the teacher of the connection;
To some it appears as the teacher of the result.
From the five self-arisen wisdoms
That bring compassion forth into manifestation,
The buddhas and sentient beings that arose
Appear as the place of the teaching and the retinue.
To such an assembly of the retinue,
The teachers of cause and result, the three bodies,
Within the single domain of suchness,
Because the views of the retinue differ,
To some teach it as an object of abandoning and blocking;
To some teach it as purifying and traversing;
To some teach it as blessing and purity;
To some teach it as accomplished through accepting and rejecting;
To some teach it as self-existing purity,
Something there to be sought and accomplished.
Thus the teachers, the three bodies,
Taught with their intent on cause and result.
Yet cause and result, the two,
Have never moved from suchness.
I, the All-Creating, have declared it.
Suchness is like this:
I, the All-Creating, am suchness;
All that I have made is suchness.
The six objects were made by me;
The six faculties are my awareness;
The groups of consciousness
Are my self-arisen wisdom.
The five great elements are the five causes;
The five causes of all are suchness.
Compassion, the five self-arisen wisdoms,
And the three realms and six classes of beings brought forth
Are explained as the essence of suchness.
The nature of suchness
Is an essence without duality;
Within what abides as a single nature,
The four settlings of yoga that were brought forth
Arose from the four kinds of yoga.
O great being, listen!
This awakening mind, the essence of all,
Is the essence of the natural state of all phenomena;
Just as it is, it abides primordially as suchness.
Utmost Yoga, Thorough Yoga,
Great Yoga and Sattva Yoga, these four,
Looking upon the non-doing natural state of the essential meaning,
See four kinds of domain.
Sattva Yoga sees it as objects and faculties.
Blessing through the five awakenings and the four kinds of miraculous display,
It views the two deities of the sattva.
It does not see the natural state of the essential meaning.
Sattva Yoga, settle in unaltered suchness!
What the Great Yogas see
In the non-doing natural state of the essential meaning
Is, in the maṇḍala of primordial self-existing purity,
The maṇḍala of deities, a result arising from a cause;
Completing the limbs of the four phases of approach and accomplishment,
They view emanation and absorption as the spontaneously present self.
They do not see the natural state of the essential meaning.
Great Yoga, settle in unaltered suchness!
What the Thorough Yogas see
In the non-doing natural state of the essential meaning
Is basic space, seen as the cause of the essential natural state,
And great wisdom, seen as the result of the natural state.
Because they see two, cause and result, within the one essence,
They do not see the natural state beyond cause and result.
Thorough Yoga, settle in unaltered suchness!
In the non-doing natural state of the essential meaning,
Utmost Yoga, the Great Perfection, views and sees
The awakening mind as the essence of all,
And sees that very thing as the unaltered natural state of the meaning.
It settles the view as primordially without anything to meditate on;
It settles the commitments as primordially without anything to guard;
It settles enlightened activity as primordially without anything to seek.
By so settling, one is joined with the natural state in suchness.
As for the distinctions of view among the four kinds of yoga,
Each single domain is again divided into four.
Sattva Yoga is divided into four distinctions:
Mind that does not conceptualize objects and faculties
Is therefore explained as the Sattva Yoga itself.
Bringing forth the awakenings, the blessings and the miraculous displays
Is called Great Yoga.
Being endowed with the four great mudrās
Is called Thorough Yoga.
The view that wisdom and the commitments are without intrinsic nature
The sattvas hold to be the realization of Utmost Yoga.
They do not see the yoga without accepting and rejecting.
Great Yoga is divided into four distinctions:
First, contemplating the three meditative absorptions
Is called Sattva Yoga.
Viewing wisdom through self-existing purity
Is called Great Yoga.
Fully completing the four phases of approach and accomplishment
Is called Thorough Yoga.
Viewing everything as the great self
Is called Utmost Yoga.
They do not see the natural state beyond striving and accomplishing.
Thorough Yoga is viewed in four distinctions:
Not generating oneself as the deity's body through the stages of absorption,
Because it contemplates the nature of reality as self-existing purity,
Is called Sattva Yoga.
Not depending on father and mother, skillful means and wisdom,
Because the maṇḍala is completed by the essence alone,
Is explained as the realization of Great Yoga.
Conduct within the nature of reality, where appearances have no intrinsic nature,
Is explained as Thorough Yoga itself.
Because all phenomena, however they appear,
Are viewed as basic space, the cause, and wisdom, the result,
This is called Utmost Yoga.
They do not see the natural state beyond cause and result.
The four distinctions of Utmost Yoga are these:
The awakening mind without accepting and rejecting
Is called Sattva Yoga.
The awakening mind beyond striving and accomplishing
Is called Great Yoga.
The awakening mind beyond cause and result
Is called Thorough Yoga.
Because it is beyond 'it is' and 'it is not,' beyond superimposition and denial,
I have explained it as Utmost Yoga.
For the three, Thorough Yoga and the others,

Since they do not possess the greatness of self-arisen wisdom,
They traverse paths and train on the grounds;
Since they guard the commitments properly and meditate on a view,
They do not have the view and conduct of the swift and slow stride.
Therefore the view and conduct of Utmost Yoga,
Because it is primordially made by the All-Creating itself,
Does not traverse a path and does not practice on the grounds,
Does not guard commitments and does not meditate on a view.
Because everything arises from the path of great awakening,
Awakening itself does not traverse awakening itself.
Because there is no ground to traverse other than awakening itself,
Awakening itself does not practice in awakening itself.
Because the nature of the commitments is awakening itself,
Awakening itself does not guard awakening itself.
Because the nature of meditation is awakening itself,
Awakening itself has nothing to meditate on in itself.
Because the object of the view is awakening itself,
And awakening itself has nothing to look at in itself,
This non-doing view and conduct of Utmost Yoga,
Self-arisen wisdom with its swift and slow stride,
Is the stride of me, the All-Creating King;
It is not the sphere of those of the vehicles of cause and result."

Thus he spoke. This is the eighth chapter, the chapter on arraying the nature of reality in every domain of the nature of reality, out of the nature of reality itself, from The All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke of this: how, by mistaking the path traversed in the vehicles of cause and result, one strays from the Great Perfection and becomes obscured.

"O great being!
This very nature of the awakening mind
Is the essence of all phenomena without exception.
Being unborn and completely pure, it has no obscuration;
Being free of a path to traverse, it has no deviation;
Being primordially, spontaneously present, there is nothing to seek.
All phenomena are one in the essence, the awakening mind.
By enumerating the one in many ways, deviation and obscuration arose.
By traversing where there is nothing to traverse, deviation arose.
By looking at what has no reference and not seeing it, obscuration arose.
Where all things are one in suchness,
To hold that the cause is practiced and the result comes later,
Wishing to attain the ten perfections and the ten grounds,
Is to stray and be obscured for three eons.
Even if, through the three purities of outer, inner and thought,
The commitments and vows of the five awakenings and the limbs of miraculous display
Are kept pure as prescribed,
One strays and is obscured for seven lifetimes.
Practicing the cause and looking toward the result,
Looking upon view and conduct as two separate things,
One is obscured and strays from the nondual for three lifetimes.
Accepting and rejecting within the one essence,
Since one does not see it as one but sees it as two,
One strays and is obscured until one is free of accepting and rejecting.
By meditating that the one self-arisen is three,
One strays from the scripture of effortlessness; so declare it!
Regarding phenomena of appearance and existence,
Not realizing the essence as suchness,
Because one regards basic space and wisdom as cause and result,
The cause is superimposition and the result is denial;
So one is obscured until confident in freedom from both superimposition and denial.
The six kinds of wishing for a result that does not exist are:
Attaining through the two truths applied to the cause;
Attaining the result by practicing the three kinds of speech;
Wishing to attain through view and conduct held separately;
Wishing to attain by practicing accepting and rejecting;
Wishing to attain by meditating on the three awarenesses;
And wishing to attain by regarding cause and result as basic space itself and wisdom.
These six kinds of certain attainment one wishes to attain
Are six deviations and six obscurations each.
Because these are faults of straying and obscuration from the awakening mind,
The faults and merits of the six certain attainments are clearly spoken.
Conception and analysis, the two, are deviation and obscuration;
Apprehended and apprehender, the two, are explained as deviation and obscuration;
View and conduct, the two, are explained as deviation and obscuration.
Beyond view and conduct, accepting and rejecting, striving and accomplishing, cause and result,
In great effortless bliss, all these stray and are obscured.
The awakening mind is like space;
In mind itself, the nature of reality, which is like space,
There is no meditating on a view, no commitment to guard,
No enlightened activity to seek, no obscuration of wisdom,
No ground on which to train, and no path to traverse,
No subtle phenomena, no duality, no connection;
There is no definitive scripture that settles anything apart from mind;
Being beyond superimposition and denial, there is no pith instruction to recite.
This is the view of the awakening mind, the Great Perfection.

Because six kinds of meditating arose within the one where there is nothing to look at,
Some strayed and some were obscured: deviation and obscuration regarding the view arose.
Because six vows were guarded within the one where there is nothing to guard,
Some strayed and some were obscured: deviation and obscuration regarding the commitments arose.
Because six activities were striven for within the one where there is nothing to seek,
Some strayed and some were obscured: deviation and obscuration regarding enlightened activity arose.
Because six wisdoms arose within the one where there is no obscuration,
Some strayed and some were obscured: deviation and obscuration regarding wisdom arose.
Because six kinds of training and attainment arose within the one where there is nothing to train,
Some strayed and some were obscured: deviation and obscuration regarding the grounds arose.
Because six kinds of traversing a path arose within the one where there is nothing to traverse,
Some strayed and some were obscured: deviation and obscuration regarding the path arose.
Because six kinds of apprehending arose within the one where there is nothing apprehended,
Some strayed and some were obscured: deviation and obscuration regarding phenomena arose.
Because six connections arose within the one where there is no duality,
Some strayed and some were obscured: deviation and obscuration regarding connection arose.
Because six certainties arose within the one truth,
Some strayed and some were obscured: deviation and obscuration regarding scripture arose.
Because six kinds of expression arose within the one that is inexpressible,
Some strayed and some were obscured: deviation and obscuration regarding pith instruction arose.
This awakening mind, the essence of all,
Being primordially, spontaneously present in its own nature,
Need not be sought or accomplished through the ten natures.
My nature is like space, the example for all.
In pure space, no one makes any effort.
Even if all were to make effort in pure space,
Space, which makes all, is beyond all striving and accomplishing.
Likewise, the all-creating essence, the awakening mind,
Since I myself am beyond every sphere of experience,
In me there is primordially no view to look at and nothing to meditate on.
Likewise, even by those ten natures,
There is nothing to be done to me, who am beyond them.
Those of the vehicles of cause and result,
Seeking me by means of the ten natures,
Wishing to see me and my nature,
Are like someone who strides out into the sky and falls to the ground:
By traversing with the ten natures, they will fall.
Here my nature is definitively shown at its source:
Because I am beyond every sphere of experience,
Just as there is no view to meditate on,
So too the ten natures are without meaning;
Do not conceive that the ten natures have meaning!
Because I am not an object to be seen by looking,
Do not make a view; rest in suchness!
Because there is no cause to be separated from, free of elaboration and unborn,
There is no need to guard vows and moral discipline.
Because I am primordially, spontaneously present as the essence,
There is no need to strive or to accomplish.
Because self-arisen wisdom has no obscuration,
There is no wisdom of awareness to be clearly generated.
Because all things abide on my ground,
There is no ground to be trained in and traversed.
Because I abide pervading all,
There is no path by which to travel to me.
Because I am primordially free of apprehended and apprehender,
The name "subtle" cannot be applied.
Because my form pervades all,
There has never been anything known as two.
Because I am primordially self-arisen wisdom,
I cannot be settled by anything else.
Because I am the awakening essence of all,
There is no secret pith instruction anywhere else.
Because I am beyond all superimposition and denial,
I have crossed the summit of all phenomena.
Because there is no domain other than me,
I have crossed the summit too into a view with nothing to meditate on.
Because there is nothing to guard other than me,
I have crossed the summit too into commitments with nothing to guard.
Because there is nothing to seek other than me,
I have crossed the summit too into activity with nothing to seek.
Because nothing abides other than me,
I have crossed the summit too into grounds with nothing to train in.
Because in me there is primordially no obscuration,
I have crossed the summit too into being self-arisen wisdom.
Because I am the unborn nature of reality,
I have crossed the summit too into the subtle being the nature of reality.
Because there is nothing to traverse other than me,
I have crossed the summit too into a path with nothing to traverse.
Because buddhas, sentient beings, all appearance and existence, the vessel and its contents,
Arise from me, the essence, awakening,
I have crossed the summit too into primordial nonduality.
Because self-arisen wisdom is what settles everything,
I have crossed the summit too into being the great scripture that decides at a stroke.
Because all phenomena are nothing apart from me,
I, the All-Creating, am the summit crossed by all.
Not knowing me is explained as the essence of obscuration;
Seeking something other than me is how deviation arose.
These are taught as two kinds, of karma and of the knowable:
Not seeing is the obscuration of karma;
Not knowing is the obscuration of the knowable.
Since all phenomena, however they appear,
Are the essence of the awakening mind, the essence,
Ignorance and not knowing are explained as the essence of the two obscurations,
And seeking and accomplishing I have explained as deviation.
O great being, listen!
All phenomena, however they appear,
Are oneself and not other; if one conceives of oneself in them,
That is oneself obscuring oneself,
And one does not thereby know one's own nature.
All phenomena, however they appear,
Are the sattva itself and nothing other than itself;
So enjoy them blissfully in nonconceptual sameness.
The meaning of bliss in this is as follows:
There is no need to strive at the ten great natures.
By not seeking through the ten great natures,
The six deviations and six obscurations of each of the ten,
The three hundred and sixty deviations and obscurations, the endless ones, are not there.
O great being!
All phenomena, however they appear,
Arise manifestly as one's own essence, awakening;
No phenomenon other than this has been taught.
I declare: do not conceptualize about this!
Sattva, to the assemblies of the retinue,
Just as I have taught, teach it to the assembly!

Thus he spoke. This is the ninth chapter, the chapter on clearing away the faults of deviation and obscuration, from The All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke this, on the perfection of his own nature:

O great being, listen!
Because in me, the all-creator, everything is gathered together,
It is therefore called "perfection."
Because from the threefold nature of me, the all-creator,
The teachers, the teachings, and the retinues arose,
It is therefore designated "all-creating."
First, the perfection of the teacher:
From the self-arisen primordial wisdom of me, the all-creator,
What arose as a threefold nature
Is shown as the three kāyas, the three teachers.
They are shown in three aspects:
The dharmakāya, the sambhogakāya (the body of perfect enjoyment),
And the nirmāṇakāya (the emanation body).
To show the nature of those three kāyas:
As for showing the nature of the dharmakāya,
It abides as a nature unborn, free of apprehended and apprehender.
As for the nature of the sambhogakāya,
It abides in the perfect enjoyment of whatever is desired.
As for showing the nature of the nirmāṇakāya,
It abides emanating as bodies suited to whoever is to be tamed.
The teachings of those teachers, the three kāyas,
Are shown in three aspects: outer, inner, and secret.
The teaching of the dharmakāya teacher is this:
It teaches the nature of the three secrets.
From the nature of the ground, the pure nature of reality,
Come secret generation, secret completion,
And secret great perfection: these three natures
Are not the domain of everyone, and so are called secret.
Teaching the secret of generating the three stages that have no existence:
That is called secret generation.
What is taught as secret completion
Does not conceive the three samādhis of inner wisdom.
Upon all phenomena, however they appear,
It pronounces the essence of inner samādhi and wisdom.
Having made one's own continuum, pure from the very beginning, into the deity,
It holds the vajra sense sources of one's own body,
And view and conduct, adopting and rejecting, to be of nondual nature.
That is called the inner secret completion.
As for teaching the secret great perfection:
All phenomena, however they appear,
Are not generated as the awakening mind through the three samādhis,
Nor are they completed by reciting essence syllables.
I, the all-creator, am the great perfection;
In me there is nothing whatsoever that is not perfected.
Teaching my threefold nature
Is teaching the threefold awakening mind of the great perfection.
That is called the secret great perfection.
That is called the teaching of the dharmakāya teacher.
The teaching of the sambhogakāya teacher
Is the three outer classes that engage in activity.
The class of the conceptual ones who engage in activity
First, through nonconceptual samādhi,
Meditates on the three purities of outer and inner conception,
And enters by way of planets, stars, and times, conceptually reckoned.
Through awakening and various miraculous displays,
Self and wisdom deity are held as lord and servant.
Having made offerings of the three purities,
It wishes for accomplishments of body, speech, and mind that do not exist.
It explains both the ultimate and the relative as illusion.
Dividing view and conduct into two,
Relying on the two, it seeks to accomplish a single aim.
Seeking to accomplish what does not exist through conduct that exists
Is like a she-wolf pawing at the groundless sky.
As for the secret of the outer ones who adopt and reject:
Through nonconceptual samādhi, blessing, and miraculous display,
They bless everything and make it into wisdom beings.
Having offered the three offerings, outer, inner, and secret,
They hope to attain whatever accomplishments they wish,
Endowed with body, speech, and mind and the four mudrās.
This samādhi that sets its hope on non-attachment,
Because it adopts and rejects in view and conduct, has no accomplishment.
This is called the teaching of the sambhogakāya.
The teaching of the nirmāṇakāya teacher
Is the three classes of sūtra: śrāvaka, pratyekabuddha, and Great Vehicle,
Called outer, and called the outer vehicle of characteristics.
The five sense pleasures, which are the essence of me, the all-creator,
Came forth as the essence of the five wisdoms;
From them arose the three: attachment, aversion, and delusion.
What was taught as the antidote to tame them
Is the eighty-four thousand doors of Dharma.
The five kinds of sense pleasure arose:
From form, sound, smell, taste, and touch
Arose the three, attachment, aversion, and delusion.
As the antidote to tame them, there are the three baskets.
What was taught as the antidote to tame attachment
Is the twenty-one thousand of the Vinaya.
What was taught as the antidote to tame delusion
Is the twenty-one thousand of the Sūtra Basket.
What was taught as the antidote to tame aversion
Is the twenty-one thousand of the Abhidharma Basket.
As the antidote to tame the three poisons in equal measure,
Twenty-one thousand were taught from the three baskets together.
That eighty-four thousand were taught in all
Was so that the three poisons might be tamed by their antidotes.
The causal vehicles of characteristics are three,
And their doors are the three: Sūtra, Vinaya, and Abhidharma.
That is taught as the condensed meaning.
Within the sūtras, too, there are twelve modes of exposition,
Taught as the meaning divided in detail.
From the cause, the three poisons, comes the result, the three lower realms.
For sentient beings, the cause is the conduct of karma, the two, virtue and evil;
The result is the two, the higher realms and the lower realms.
This is what is proclaimed by the causal vehicle of characteristics.
This was taught before, is taught now, and will be taught hereafter.
But this meaning, the meaning that cannot be expressed,
Was not taught before, is not taught now,
And will not be taught hereafter by the nirmāṇakāya.
Therefore this is called the teaching of the nirmāṇakāya.
O great being!
In the threefold nature of me, the all-creator,
There first dawned the teachers of the retinue, the three kāyas.
Now the retinues of the three teachers, the three kāyas, are shown.
O great being, listen!
The retinue of the dharmakāya teacher is shown thus:
All the retinue that I have made, each and every one,
Is the retinue of the dharmakāya, my own nature.
Buddhas, sentient beings, all appearance and existence, the whole world and its beings:
There is none that is not included in the retinue of the dharmakāya.

Thus he spoke.

O great being, listen!
The retinue of the sambhogakāya teacher is shown thus:
Those who have passed beyond the stage of the four levels of aspirational conduct,
Have set foot on the first ground, Perfect Joy,
Up to the tenth ground, Cloud of Dharma:
These are shown as the retinue of the sambhogakāya.
The retinue of the nirmāṇakāya teacher is shown thus:
The four, the non-Buddhists (tīrthikas), the nihilists, the materialists (Lokāyatas), and the heedless,
Have utterly strayed from the path of unsurpassed awakening;
So, having turned their faculties and minds and blessed them with compassion,
The four, monks and nuns, laymen and laywomen,
Are shown as the retinue of the nirmāṇakāya teacher.

Thus he spoke.

O great being, listen!
Of the teachers, the three kāyas, that arose from me,
The abode of each of the three kāyas is shown.
The abode of the dharmakāya teacher is this:
Akaniṣṭha, the palace of the dharmadhātu (the expanse of reality);
That is called the Akaniṣṭha of genuine meaning.
Showing the abode of the sambhogakāya teacher:
Above all the signs of the world,
The celestial mansion, the many-storied palace of the Akaniṣṭha abode:
That is called the Akaniṣṭha of place.
Showing the abode of the nirmāṇakāya teacher:
The nirmāṇakāya is Śākyamuni of the seven generations;
His abode is shown as Vulture Peak;
Yet in truth the emanations of compassion abide in no fixed place.

Thus he spoke.

O great being, listen!
Of the teachers, the three kāyas, that arose from me,
The particular realms to be tamed are shown thus:
The realm to be tamed by the dharmakāya teacher
Reaches to the limits of the dharmadhātu,
And as the element of space pervades, so does it:
That is the realm to be tamed by the dharmakāya.
The realm to be tamed by the sambhogakāya
Is form, sound, smell, taste, and touch, which arose from me;
It tames through the perfect enjoyment of whatever is desired.
As for the realm to be tamed by the nirmāṇakāya,
Across a billion Jambudvīpas,
Self-arisen primordial wisdom tames the six classes of beings through compassion.
That shows how they differ while according.
The teachers, the three kāyas, that arose from me
Accord in cause, accord in result, and are one in nature.
As the cause, all accord in being the awakening mind;
As the result, all likewise accord in being buddha;
In nature they are shown to be one essence, the nature of reality.
In the time of taming there is no division of earlier and later.

Thus he spoke.

O great being, listen once more!
The manner of explaining of the teachers, the three kāyas,
That arose from me, the All-Creating King, is this:
The dharmakāya teacher explains by blessing;
The sambhogakāya explains by its own essence;
The nirmāṇakāya joins word and meaning,
And shows word and meaning to the ear faculty of persons.
As for the dharmakāya explaining by blessing:
It shows the nature of me, the all-creator;
As the miraculous display of birth from the unborn,
All appearance and existence, the whole world and its beings, just as they are,
Are brought forth as the miraculous display of essence and compassion:
This is called explaining by the blessing of the nature.
The sambhogakāya shows its own essence:
All phenomena, however they appear,
It shows as the perfection of all desired enjoyments,
Of form, sound, smell, taste, touch, and the rest.
This I have shown as explaining by its own essence.
As for the nirmāṇakāya explaining by joining words and letters:
All phenomena, however they appear,
When shown as the teacher's own essence, are not understood;
So the emanation, holding to the distinction called "teaching,"
Voices the meanings as sound, in words and letters.
Having made the meaning understood by the sound of the words,
That meaning is explained by that sound of words.
O great being, listen!
I, the all-creator, am the essence of the three kāyas.
That which abides as nonconceptual equality
Is the essence of the dharmakāya, free of elaboration.
The miraculous display of birth that arises as nature
Is the sambhogakāya, from which what is desired arises.
Acting with compassion for beings in accord with how they are to be tamed:
That is the essence of the nirmāṇakāya.
Through the threefold nature of the All-Creating King,
It shows itself as the body that tames each being in whatever way suits.
The meaning, the inexpressible nature of the teaching,
Acts for beings through whatever befits each one:
That is called the perfection of the teaching.
As for the nature of the retinue, whoever gathers to whomever,
Having gathered there, holds the lineage of that one:
That is called the perfect retinue.
I am the teacher, the all-creator, the awakening mind.
The single all-creator is counted in three aspects:
I, the all-creator, my nature,
And the phenomena I have made: these three aspects.
I am the teacher, the all-creator, the forefather of all.
My nature is shown in ten aspects;
That is called the heart of all the teachings.
As for the phenomena made by me, the all-creator,
The buddhas who abide as one throughout the three times,
And all the beings who dwell in the three realms,
Without distinction, I have made into the holy retinue.
Because the single all-creator brought about perfection,
All the mind's needs were met, and it is perfect.
That whatever each needs is gathered together,
I, the all-creator, call perfection.
Showing the three aspects of the teacher's nature:
At the head of the retinue, the teachers dawned as the three kāyas.
The nature that is unborn and free of apprehended and apprehender
Dawned at the head of the retinue as the teacher, the dharmakāya.
The miraculous display of birth, the play of Samantabhadra,
Dawned as the sambhogakāya, enjoying what is desired.
Having arisen in variety, just as is desired,
It satisfies all in accordance with that:
That is called the varied nirmāṇakāya.
Those who see the renowned teachers, the three kāyas,
Are three: buddhas, bodhisattvas, and those who have attained acceptance.
Gathering to whichever teacher's body is fitting,
They remain in a corresponding abode where the meaning is shown.
The nature of the teaching is likewise:
Whichever of the three kāyas teaches whatever to the retinue,
Through the aims of taming each being by whatever means,
It tames buddhas, gods, humans, and the world of karma by teaching;
Therefore it is called perfection.
The dharmakāya, which does not abide, dwelling in the expanse,
Teaches, to the buddhas of the three times and the sentient beings of the three realms,
The awakening mind beyond elaboration.
The sambhogakāya of primordial wisdom, dwelling in Akaniṣṭha,
Explains to the bodhisattvas of the tenth ground
That awakening mind as body and primordial wisdom.
The emanations of varied compassion, with no fixed form,
Teach the retinue gathered wherever there are those who have attained acceptance
The meaning of taming by whatever means, unfixed.
Through aspiration and good karma, uninterrupted by adverse conditions,
My compassion has come to the time of the sattva.
You, who compile from among the retinue I have taught,
And the nondual meaning itself, and my teaching: these three
Being gathered, the time, the place, and the nature are complete in it.
Those three perfections
Are shown as the Three Jewels, rare from the very beginning,
And are explained as the body, speech, and mind of me, the all-creator.
The teacher, the rare Buddha Jewel,
Is the dharmakāya, the sambhogakāya, and the nirmāṇakāya.
The teaching, the holy Dharma, the rare supreme speech,
Is the three supreme speeches of the inexpressible all-creator.
To the retinue, the Saṅgha, the supreme mind,
It teaches whatever each needs and whatever befits each.
I am the teacher, the all-creator, the awakening mind.
Having made the teachers that the three kāyas comprise,
The teaching, the holy Dharma, set forth in three, is

Outer, inner, and secret.
Within these, three each, they are renowned as nine:
The nirmāṇakāya, through compassion, proclaims three, in accord with how beings are tamed;
The sambhogakāya proclaims the three that engage in activity, outer and inner;
The dharmakāya proclaims the three secret abhidharmas.
Those three, outer, inner, and secret,
Are body, speech, and mind, and enlightened body, speech, and mind;
Radiating out from these, they came to be ninefold.
By traversing nine passes, nine valleys, and nine paths,
One proceeds by the single vehicle to the ground of all-creating awakening.
The all-creator, without act or agent, is beyond effort and striving;
Without traversing, one has arrived at the ground of awakening.

Thus he spoke. This is the tenth chapter, the chapter on the perfections, exceedingly rare, from The All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke this summary of the root of all phenomena:

O great being, listen! The nature and the root of all phenomena is as follows:
I am the nature of all phenomena;
Apart from my nature there is no other phenomenon.
The teachers, the three kāyas, are my nature.
The buddhas of the three times are my nature.
The bodhisattvas are my nature.
The four kinds of yoga are my nature.
The three realms too, the desire realm, the form realm, and the formless realm,
Are shown to be the nature of me, the all-creator.
The five great elements too are my nature.
The sentient beings of the six classes are my nature.
All appearance is my nature.
All existence is my nature.
The world and its beings, gathered together, are my nature.
Since there is nothing other than my nature,
The root of all phenomena is gathered in me;
There is not a single one not gathered in me.
The unborn, the miraculous display of birth,
And the nature of compassion: these three that arose
Are the nature of the three teachers.
The three times too, past, present, and future,
From the nature of my own abiding as one:
Because all the buddhas abide as one,
That too is shown to be my nature.
Because I am purified, being free of apprehended and apprehender,
Because I pervade all, like space,
And because I am the heart of all phenomena,
The awakening mind too is my nature.
Since I, the all-creator, abide as one,
And my nature is likewise the natural state,
The four kinds of union are united as one in the natural state;
Therefore they are called my nature.
From the body, speech, and mind of me, the all-creator,
Whatever arises arises from my nature;
Whatever gathers gathers into my nature;
Whatever is, is my essence;
Therefore the three realms are called my nature.
The fivefold nature of me, the all-creator,
Is my essence brought forth in five aspects:
The five appearances, space, wind, water, earth, and fire,
Are shown to be the nature of me, the all-creator.
Manifesting through the compassion of me, the all-creator,
The five kinds of self-arisen primordial wisdom are shown;
And the appearances of beings, the sentient beings of the six classes,
Are shown to be the nature of me, the all-creator.
From the nature of me, the All-Creating King,
All appearance and existence, the world and its beings gathered together,
Since all were made by me and arose from me,
There is not a single phenomenon not gathered in me.
The teachers, the three kāyas, who arose from me,
Show the threefold nature of me, the all-creator.
The buddhas who abide as one in the three aspects
Of the three times, past, present, and future,
Abide without before or after as the nature of the all-creator.
The bodhisattvas, free of apprehended and apprehender,
Make the nature of me, the all-creator, their very being.
Those who settle the four kinds of yoga in the natural state
Settle naturally in me, the all-creator.
Just as the All-Creating King has made everything,
Everything gathers into the All-Creating King himself.

Thus he spoke. This is the eleventh chapter, the chapter on the root of all phenomena being gathered solely into the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind himself, from The All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind.

Then I, the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke this sūtra on the source of all phenomena:

O great being!
I am the scriptural source of all.
All phenomena, however they appear,
Know them all to have arisen from me.
Listen to this great general source of the Dharma,
And declare all phenomena in this way!
I, the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind,
Since I am the king of all the great scriptures,
Even the three teachers first arose from me.
What is called "scripture" is a name for the word of the Teacher.
Since I am the root of all pith instructions,
A nature of reality other than mind
Has never truly been taught by me, the all-creator,
To the buddhas of the three times or the sentient beings of the three realms.
I am the general commentary on all the tantras and sūtras.
The teacher who teaches the tantras and sūtras
Is also brought forth from the nature of me, the all-creator;
I comment on both the teacher and the teaching.
I, the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind,
Am called the summit of all the teachings.
Through the teachers, the three kāyas, who arose from me,
The teachings, Vinaya, Sūtra, and Abhidharma,
The classes of the individual tantras, the collections of a hundred thousand verses,
And generation, completion, and the secret, and the rest, all of which involve effort and striving,
All of them journey toward me, who am beyond effort and striving.
Yet through effort and striving itself, they do not see me, who am beyond it.
Therefore I am called the summit of all the teachings.
I, the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind,
Am called the quintessence of all the vehicles.
There are three vehicles, in which the three teachers abide,
But in definitive truth there is nothing but a single vehicle.
The one is the vehicle of all, through the awakening mind;
That is called the quintessence of all the vehicles.
O! I dispel the darkness of ignorance
And spread the light of great primordial wisdom.
If it is asked how this is so:
All phenomena, however they appear,
Are not recognized as awakening, as suchness;
For those enveloped in the darkness of ignorant conceptuality,
Since all phenomena, however they appear,
Are made by the all-creating awakening mind,
I make them recognize them as the all-creating awakening mind;
And having dispelled the darkness of discriminating thought
About all phenomena, however they appear,
The light of great self-arisen primordial wisdom spreads.
Therefore it is called dispelling the darkness of ignorance and spreading the light.
O great being!
Having torn apart the net of conceptual thought,
I cut the string of the rosary of the afflictions.
Regarding all these phenomena, however they appear,
Among those of the vehicles of cause and of result,
Some see poison and form the concept of abandoning it;
Some see objects to which the mind attaches,
And form the concept of non-attachment through the two truths;
Some see objects to be made utterly pure,
And through the three purities, blessing, and miraculous display,
Form concepts in the manner of lord and servant;
Some, regarding the nature that is pure in itself,
Form the concept of wishing to accomplish it through the four kinds of approach and accomplishment.
For all of these, I, the all-creator, the awakening mind,
Tear apart the concept that anything is other than mind,
And cut the string of the rosary of afflictions of discriminating thought.

Thus he spoke.

O great being!
I am the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind.
Realize this nature of mine,
And declare all phenomena, however they appear,
To be nothing other than me!
If you teach this scripture of mine,
All the assemblies of the retinue gathered around you
Will realize the nature of me, the all-creator,
And all of them will become my very nature.
When all have become my nature,
Regarding all phenomena, however they appear,
None will abandon or obstruct, nor train in the two truths;
None will consecrate cleanliness and the three purities;
None will strive for and accomplish samādhi and the generation of the mind.
Since everything has already been made into me, the all-creator,
In me, the all-creator, everything is equality itself.
Equality itself: I need not make equality.
That equality need not be made, I declared before.

Thus he spoke. This is the twelfth chapter, the chapter showing definitively the source of scripture, from The All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind.

Then, since that All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, is the king of all scriptures, he spoke this mode of explanation that explains the general scripture of all phenomena:

O great being, listen!
Because I am the general scripture of the Dharma,
The five modes of explaining the meaning by reasoning
Are explained through five general meanings:
The mode of explaining the meaning of the history is shown;
The mode of explaining the meaning of the root is shown;
The mode of explaining the meaning of yoga is shown;
The mode of explaining the meaning of the purpose is shown;
And the mode of explaining the meaning of the words is shown.
First, to show the source for confidence,
I shall show the mode of explaining the meaning of the history.
Because the root of all phenomena is gathered into mind,
I shall therefore show that meaning of the root.
To distinguish separately the particular features of the vehicles,
I shall show the root of the meaning of yoga.
To show that effort and striving need not be made,
The purpose of the meaning of the purpose is shown there.
To make understood the meaning that is not realized,
I shall voice the meaning of the words as sound.
O great being!
Showing the purpose of the meaning of the history:
First, to show the source for confidence,
The modes of explaining the meaning of the history
Are the mode of explaining by the blessing of the nature,
The mode of explaining by showing its own essence,
And the mode of explaining by joining sound and words to the meaning.

From the threefold nature of me, the all-creator,
Through the teachers, the three kāyas, who arose from me,
By nature, by essence, and by sound and words:
Through that source, showing how it arose, confidence is gained.
O great being, listen!
Showing the purpose of the meaning of the root:
All appearance and existence, the world and its beings gathered together,
And all buddhas and sentient beings gathered together,
Because they were made by me, all-creating awakening,
Because from the very beginning there is nothing called "other"
Apart from the awakening mind,
And because all are shown to be the awakening mind,
This shows the purpose of the meaning of the root.
O great being, listen!
Showing the purpose of the meaning of yoga:
Through the three kāyas, who arose from me, the all-creator,
Among the classes of yoga, the tantras and the rest,
Of the teachings of the sambhogakāya and the dharmakāya,
Although there are particular distinctions in each,
My teaching, which is beyond cause and result,
This meaning of the Great Perfection, Atiyoga,
Is called the scripture of perfection that sets the vehicles apart.
Therefore it is called showing the purpose of the meaning of yoga.
O great being, listen!
Showing the purpose of the meaning of the purpose:
From before countless aeons,
For those of Utmost Yoga, with the karma and the good fortune
Of faith in me, all-creating awakening,
Who see that in the view there is nothing to meditate, in the commitments nothing to keep,
In enlightened activity nothing to seek, on the path nothing to traverse,
In the grounds nothing to train, no cause and no result,
No two, ultimate and relative,
Nothing to meditate on or accomplish,
And who see no generating of the mind and no antidote,
Who see the nature of the all-creating mind:
For such as these the purpose is shown.

Thus he spoke.

O great being, listen!
Showing the purpose of the meaning of the words:
I, the all-creator, the awakening mind, am suchness.
The phenomena, however they appear, that are taught
Are the nature of all-creating suchness.
If words and letters were not proclaimed as sound,
For persons who know through the faculty of the mind,
The nature of suchness would remain hidden and would not appear.
Therefore words and letters are proclaimed as sound;
Therefore it is called the purpose of the meaning of the words.
O great being!
As for explaining the history of what and of whom:
It is the history of the teacher, the teaching, and the retinue.
From the threefold nature of the teacher, the all-creator,
The teachers, the three kāyas, arose, the nature of the retinue.
The three modes of explaining of the teachers, the three kāyas,
Are the history of the three teachers and their teachings.
As for explaining the root of what and of whom:
Because all the phenomena, as they have been taught
By the teachers, the three kāyas, who arose from me,
Are shown to have the awakening mind as their root,
This shows the reasoning of the meaning of the root
For the teacher, the teaching, and the retinues.
Within my nature, the single suchness,
The distinctions of the four kinds of yoga,
In view, conduct, commitment, and practice,
Each with its own discordant particular features,
Are set apart by Utmost Yoga, which is beyond effort and striving.
Therefore the reasoning of the meaning of yoga is shown.
Showing the definition of the meaning of the purpose:
The purpose is this: if all the sentient beings of the three realms
Were to see their own mind as suchness,
They would not remain on the ground of a buddha in words,
But from this very moment would attain Utmost Yoga.
For those without the karma, of little fortune,
Even when it is brought out, unhidden and manifest, it is not realized;
Just as one who wants a precious jewel
Will not get one by polishing a piece of wood.
For those of Utmost Yoga, of good karma and fortune,
There is no view, commitment, enlightened activity, grounds and paths,
Generation of the mind, cause and result, meditation, accomplishment, or antidote;
They see that there are not two, ultimate and relative.
Therefore, because they see the suchness of mind,
That is the purpose of the purpose for such as these.

Thus he spoke.

The definition of the purpose of the meaning of the words:
The ten meanings of nonexistence, the sound and words of nonexistence,
By being expressed in words, the meaning in which there is nothing to do is seen,
And the all-creator, who need not be made, is attained;
Thus the purpose of the meaning of the words is shown.

Thus he spoke. This is the thirteenth chapter, the chapter on the modes of explaining the mind, from The All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke this, his own sacred secret instruction:

O great being!
I, the all-creator, am secret in every way.
To the teachers, the three kāyas, who arose from me,
My threefold nature is not shown; it is secret.
To the buddhas of the three times who abide in me,
My nature is not to be shown; it is to be kept secret.
To all the assemblies of the retinue gathered in me,
My nature is not to be shown; it is to be kept secret.
To the sentient beings of the three realms, made by me,
My nature is not to be shown; it is to be kept secret.
Had I shown my nature without keeping it secret,
The teachers, the three kāyas, would not have arisen from me.
Had the teachers, the three kāyas, not arisen from me,
The three teachings, the three vehicles, and the three retinues would not have gathered.
Had the three teachings, the three vehicles, and the three retinues not gathered,
There would be no Three Jewels, Buddha, Dharma, and Saṅgha,
And no knowledge of unsurpassed awakening for anyone.
If to the buddhas of the three times, who arose from me,
My nature were shown without being kept secret,
There would be the fault that there were no three kāyas and no three teachers.
If to the assemblies of the retinue gathered in me
My nature were shown without being kept secret,
The three teachers and the distinctions of the vehicles would not be set apart.
If to the sentient beings of the three realms, made by me,
My nature were shown out of compassion,
There would be no occasion for the teachings of the three teachers.
If there were no occasion for the teachings of the three teachers,
Who would name as "perfection"
The phenomena made by me, the all-creator?
Therefore I, the All-Creating King,
Having brought forth my nature myself,
Have shown my nature to myself.
To the teachers and retinues who arose from me,
I, the all-creator, have not imparted the scripture of this teaching.
Since Utmost Yoga is I, the all-creator,
To it the nature of the all-creator is to be shown.

Thus he spoke. This is the fourteenth chapter, the chapter on secrecy toward those without good fortune, from The All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, taught this scripture that brings forth his own great scripture:

O great sattva, listen!
I, the All-Creating King,
Have taught three things brought forth;
I have taught my nature as threefold:
The teachers who teach the meanings,
The retinues who wish to know the meaning,
And the unmistaken definitive meaning: these three
I, the all-creator, have brought forth.

The bringing forth of the teachers of the meaning
Is the dharmakāya, the sambhogakāya,
And the nirmāṇakāya: three kinds.
First of the circle, the teachers are the three kāyas.
That teacher who teaches the meaning
Is the nature free of apprehended and apprehender,
The one who shows my very essence,
And the one who acts for others' benefit with the nature of compassion: three.
They are brought forth from the three natures;
The three teachers too are brought forth.

The bringing forth of those who wish to know the meaning
Is the three retinues of the three teachers.
The particulars of what I teach are these:
The buddhas who abide in the three times
And all sentient beings who dwell in the three realms,
Abiding in nonconceptual sameness:
That is the retinue I, the all-creator, have brought forth.
Toward the forms of the sense faculties' objects
No afflictions arise in them; they do not conceptualize.
Yet toward their own awakening mind itself
They conceptualize phenomena and the nature of reality,
And so are obscured from the first ground to the tenth.
The bodhisattvas of the ten grounds,
And the sambhogakāya seen in the form of the five sense pleasures:
These two, teacher and retinue,
Are also brought forth as my very essence.
Toward the nature of suchness,
With that mental faculty of awareness,
The cognition that sees by examining:
Some see it as permanence;
Some see emptiness as annihilation.
Blessing them in the form of the four kinds of tīrthikas
Who see cause and result wrongly,
And the four kinds of virtuous minds who abandon evil,
To whom cause and result were taught,
The retinue and the teacher who remain
For as long as three great eons:
These too are brought forth by me.
For those whose domain is the awareness of primordial wisdom,
I have brought forth the genuine and definitive.

O great sattva,
As for what is called bringing forth,
Who brought forth what?
By whom? It was brought forth by me.
What was brought forth?
It was the mind itself that was brought forth.
For whose sake was it brought forth?
It was brought forth for the sake of the retinues
Who view objects as separate.
For some it was brought forth for the sake of abandoning;
For some it was brought forth for the sake of suppressing;
For some it was brought forth for the sake of conduct;
For some it was brought forth for the sake of purifying;
For some it was brought forth for the sake of accepting and rejecting;
For some it was brought forth for the sake of making it their own.
Some see it as an object to be abandoned;
Some see it as an object to be suppressed;
Some see it as an object of conduct;
Some see it as an object to be purified;
Some see it as an object of accepting and rejecting;
Some see it as an object to be brought under control.
Thus do the assembled retinues
Of the teachers of cause and result see it.

Great sattva,
This bringing forth of my nature:
You must explain it definitively!
I am your very essence;
That bringing forth of my nature
Is the bringing forth of your nature.
Through that bringing forth of your nature,
Come to know your own nature!
Come to know your own essence!
Explain it to the retinues of cause and result!

Thus he spoke. Then Sattvavajra spoke thus to the retinues of cause and result:

O teachers and retinues of cause and result,
The teacher of teachers, the all-creator,
Has made all things perfectly complete.
Since the bringing forth of that perfection
Is the bringing forth of your own nature,
Realize that it is your own nature!
Realize that it is your own essence!
Not taking it to be something other,
Let all realize that it is themselves.

Not realizing that it is themselves,
Not realizing the object as themselves,
Imagining the object to be an enemy and a thorn,
They abandon their own nature,
And for hundreds of eons, for a long time,
They do not see their own mind, the nature of reality.
If they do not see their own mind, the nature of reality,
For hundreds of eons, for a long time,
They will abandon their own awakening
And will not attain that great bliss beyond action.

Not realizing the object as themselves,
Imagining the object, through conditions, to be an enemy,
If they suppress their own nature,
For hundreds of eons, for a long time,
They do not see their own mind, the nature of reality.
If they do not see their own mind, the nature of reality,
For hundreds of eons, for a long time,
Because they suppress their own awakening,
They will not attain that great bliss beyond action.

Not realizing the object as themselves,
Seeing the object as an object to be purified,
If they purify their own nature,
For as long as three great eons
They do not see their own mind, the nature of reality.
Not seeing their own mind, the nature of reality,
They are parted from the confidence of their own mind, which needs no purifying.

Seeing in the object, in awakening, which is naturally pure,
The stain of apprehended and apprehender,
With conceptions of outer and inner and the three purities,
They purify themselves with bathing and cleanliness.
Not seeing their own pure mind,
They are parted from that purity of their own mind.
For seven human lifetimes
They do not see their own mind, the nature of reality.

Toward awakening, in which there is no accepting and rejecting,
Seeing objects to be accepted and rejected,
They accept and reject the self-arisen itself;
They themselves accept and reject themselves.
Without interruption, for three lifetimes,
They do not see their own nondual mind.

Toward awakening, which is spontaneously present,
Seeing an object to be sought and accomplished,
Generating the mind through the three kinds of samādhi,
They bring all under control and make it their own.
Because they wish to accomplish by seeking
Their own awakening, which needs no seeking,
They practice on the appointed days for sixteen months,
And for one thousand one hundred and six years
They take on the guise of the sambhogakāya,
And then conceive an acceptance of their own mind's spontaneous presence.
This bringing forth of one's own mind, the nature of reality,
Though it brings no benefit to those of effort and accomplishment
Who conceptualize cause and result,
Was brought forth for the purpose and benefit
Of those endowed with karma and good fortune.

Thus spoke Sattvavajra.

Then the All-Creating King himself gave this explanation:

O great sattva,
The bringing forth of the nature of reality is your own mind.
Those of the vehicles of cause and result
Do not realize this bringing forth of their own mind;
Abandoning and suppressing, they remain for long eons.
Purifying and traversing the path, they remain for three eons.
With purifying baths and cleanliness, it takes seven human lifetimes.
Those of blessing, accepting and rejecting take three human lifetimes.
Those who wish for control and mastery, for one thousand one hundred and six years,
In the guise of the sambhogakāya, come to realize their own mind.
When at last they realize it, they attain effortless great bliss.

O great sattva, listen!
Not in the manner of the great vehicle of cause and result,
Hold definitively to this scripture of mine, the all-creator.
Since all phenomena, however they appear,
And one's own mind, the nature of reality, are primordially one,
Do not conceptualize this in the manner of cause and result.
Do not conceptualize your own mind, suchness.
Where one would examine one's own mind, suchness,
It is the essence of one's own awakening that appears.
Not realizing this, each assigns names:
They designate the relative as appearing and the ultimate as not appearing.
The ultimate and the relative are nondual, a single truth.
Yet truth does not definitively exist as something genuine;
To designate it as genuine would be delusion, even for me.
Since wanting bliss is the disease of attachment,
It is by having no desire that one takes up bliss.
Buddhahood is not achieved by practicing for it;
Abiding in the unsought nature, it is spontaneously present.
Abiding in the unsought nature, leave it without conceptualizing.
In this name, a mere label for the meaning of buddha,
I do not teach that a buddha truly exists.
Whoever sees a buddha as existing
Will not find a buddha in the expanse of the nature of reality.
Do not look for a buddha; realize your own mind directly.
Examined, it is not there; your own mind is primordially clear.
Not appearing as a thing, your own mind pervades all.
This appearing is the essence of what is.
Yet the great vehicle of cause and result, thinking it is not this,
With abandoning, suppressing, purifying, and various blessings,
Strays from its own mind, the nature of reality, beyond action.
Leaving what is at hand, they seek something else far away.
Casting aside the bliss beyond action, they embrace effort.
There is no disease more fatal than that.
Undistracted samādhi is a tethering stake for grasping.
Since it is so primordially, there is no losing it through distraction.
That deceiver, the hope for undistracted samādhi,
Is the provisional teaching of the great vehicle of cause and result.
It is so primordially, with no distraction and no loss;
This destroys the antidotes of all effort and accomplishment.
If this scripture of mine, the all-creator, were taught
To the teachers and retinues who teach cause and result,
They would superimpose and deny, saying that the result arises from a cause.
Yogins of samādhi who want buddhahood,
Wanting samādhi, abandon uncontrived yoga.
The uncontrived natural state is the nature of reality of all.
Apart from the nature of reality there is no buddha anywhere separate.
A buddha designated by a name is a mere label.
What is called the nature of reality is nothing other than your own mind;
Your own mind, uncontrived, is explained to be the dharmakāya.
Since the uncontrived is primordially unborn,
In the unborn meaning there is nothing to seek or accomplish.
By seeking and accomplishing, what is beyond action will not be achieved.

O great sattva, listen!
The dharmakāya, the sambhogakāya,
And the nirmāṇakāya: these three kinds of teachers,
The teachers of the one vehicle, the two vehicles, and the three vehicles,
I, the All-Creating King, have brought forth.
I am the source of all teachers;
They are brought forth from my threefold nature.
For the retinues of joy, free of elaboration and unborn,
From my nature, free of apprehended and apprehender,
Arose the dharmakāya of nonconceptual sameness.
The retinue of the dharmakāya is undivided;
The meaning of unborn sameness that it teaches
Is explained as the vehicle that leaves nothing remaining.
From my nature that appears everywhere,
For the assemblies of retinues whose enjoyment of the senses is pure,
My essence is brought forth as the sambhogakāya.
In that essence it teaches the completion of view and conduct.
That is the uncommon vehicle;
It is the bringing forth of the essence of my display.
From my nature arising as compassion,
For the nirmāṇakāya, which acts for others' benefit, emanating to tame each as befits them,
And for the four kinds of assembled retinues,
Compassion suited to cause and result is brought forth.

O great sattva,
In brief, what is called the scripture of bringing forth
Is this: the teaching of my nature in this way
Is taught as the scripture of bringing forth.
In my single nature, the all-creator,
The objects of appearance appear in three kinds,
And the phenomena that arise from it are these:
Buddhas, sentient beings, all appearance and existence, the vessel and its contents,
Are the bringing forth of my nature, the all-creator.
Those who conceive this to be something other
Lack the fortune to meet me, the all-creator,
And so are the retinues of the vehicles of cause and result,
Trained in stages by the first of the circle, the teachers who are the three kāyas.

O great sattva,
This bringing forth by me
Is not the bringing forth of something other.
It is the bringing forth of myself;
It is the bringing forth of the nature of reality;
It is the bringing forth of one's own mind;
It is the unmistaken bringing forth;
It is the definitive bringing forth;
It is the bringing forth of suchness.

O great sattva,
By this bringing forth of my nature,
The buddha of the five greatnesses is brought forth.
The buddha of the five greatnesses is as follows.
The bringing forth of my essence:
This nature of reality of mine, appearance and existence, the vessel and its contents,
Is manifestly brought forth because it appears to all.
Since nothing needs to be done, it is primordially buddha;
Since there is no effort or accomplishment, it is explained as primordially great.
It is explained as the manifestly awakened.
This bringing forth of my own mind,
Since it has become the heart essence, is the very self of all.
Since nothing needs to be done, it is primordially buddha;
Since there is no effort or accomplishment, it is explained as primordially great.
It is explained as the greatness of the buddha who is the great self.
This bringing forth of the unborn, free of elaboration,
Being primordially free of apprehended and apprehender, is the dharmadhātu.
Since nothing needs to be done, it is primordially buddha;
Since no effort or accomplishment is needed, it is explained as primordially great.
It is explained as the greatness of the dharmadhātu buddha.
This definitive bringing forth by me
Is taught in three ways: example, meaning, and sign.
For the meaning, the nature of reality, the example of space is taught;
Through the sign of the awakening mind, definitively,
And for those who doubt, manifestly,
Through example, meaning, and sign, it is shown that this is buddha.
This very unmistakenness of my nature,
Though brought forth to anyone, does not appear as something other.
Its "such" refers to the unmistaken nature;
Its "thus" is the nature without contrivance;
Its "ness" is a designation for the essential nature.
In that very nature of suchness,
One does not superimpose that the buddhas of the three times exist.

Nor denies, saying the sentient beings of the three realms do not exist.
Since it has no intention toward any conception, analysis, or thought,
I explain it as the greatness in which there is no buddha.

Thus he spoke. This is the fifteenth chapter, the chapter on bringing forth the three natures, from The All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke this personal instruction, conferring it on Sattvavajra:

O great Sattvavajra, listen to this!
I am primordially the All-Creating King.
Having made the teacher, the teaching, the retinue, and the time,
I also act as the teacher of teachers.
The teaching shows that nature of mine,
And the retinue too is brought forth from my essence;
The time and place are that nature of mine.
Apart from teaching my nature, the all-creator,
Not a single phenomenon other than me is taught.
Great sattva Vajra, you yourself too,
In order to teach my nature, the all-creator,
Were brought forth by me from me.
Set me, the all-creator, as the heart of the Dharma.
Since I, the all-creator, have primordially already done it,
Do not teach anyone that something must now be done.
If you teach the retinue that something must now be done,
The retinues will be seized by the disease of effort.
It will become the fault of there being no self-arisen wisdom;
It will become the fault of contriving the awakening mind;
It will become the fault of contriving suchness.
Through that fault, the qualities will be belittled.
Since truth is deceived and corrupted by falsehood,
Do not, through misguided effort, try to accomplish the actionless that already is.
Sattva, since you know me, the all-creator,
All the phenomena I have made
Are primordially self-arisen wisdom, suchness.
The three teachers are my self-arisen wisdom;
The three teachings too are my suchness;
All the retinues too are in the manner of Sattvavajra;
All the perfect times and places
Are I, the All-Creating King, the awakening mind.

Thus he spoke. This is the sixteenth chapter, the chapter on conferring personal instruction on Sattvavajra, from The All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, said, "Hold these bodily remains of mine!"

O great sattva, hold this!
If you always keep these bodily remains and relics,
You will be equal to me, the all-creator, the great-grandfather of the victors.

Thus he spoke. Then Sattvavajra asked:

O great-grandfather of all the buddhas of the three times,
Teacher of teachers, All-Creating King,
You say, "Always hold the bodily remains and relics."
The body is the body of which victor?
The remains are the remains of which buddha?
And what is it that is called relics?

Thus he asked.

O great sattva, listen!
The body is the body of the three victors, my children.
The remains are my mind, the victor of the three times.
If, sattva, you hold this timelessly and always,
It is the support for the offerings of the buddhas of the three times.
That is what is called bodily remains and relics.

O teacher of teachers, All-Creating King,
Even if the bodily remains and relics are thus,
How do the buddhas of the three times make offerings to them?
And what qualities are there in making offerings?

Thus he asked.

O great sattva, listen!
To those bodily remains and relics of mine
The buddhas of the three times always make offerings by gazing at mind.
Having gained their inseparable qualities,
They become able to be the All-Creating King of phenomena.

Thus he spoke. This is the seventeenth chapter, the chapter on entrusting the bodily remains, from The All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke this scripture showing that he himself is the nature of reality of all phenomena:

O great sattva, listen!
All appearance and existence, the vessel and its contents:
As appearance, they appear as my essence;
As purity, they are pure in the expanse of the nature of reality.
They show various kinds of discipline.
Having arisen as the various three kāyas,
They also show the three vehicles.
Those of cause and result are satisfied by that.
Not depending on objects that are past,
One does not cultivate causes or desire results.
With an intention free of desire,
The nature becomes spontaneously present.
It is so primordially, and nothing needs to be done.
Since things as they are are unmoved,
There is no need to accomplish what is as it is.
Because all phenomena are by nature spontaneously present,
Even the buddhas of the three times, being already accomplished,
Do not impart a scripture of effort and accomplishment.
If one seeks, whatever one seeks by that samādhi
Is not accomplished; samādhi becomes a great disease.

O great sattva,
Whatever all the teachers other than me focus on,
The various enlightened activities to be done,
The stages of samādhi through thought,
The various rays of light emitted,
And the objects to be looked at,
There is no movement in what is as it is.
Nothing is accomplished by the action to be done;
The samādhi of thought dissolves away;
When the emitted rays of light subside,
No object to be looked at is seen at all.
Therefore do not engage in the action of seeking.

O great sattva,
That scripture of accomplishment by seeking among objects
Is what those of the vehicles of cause and result teach.
As for the nature of reality of those of the vehicles of cause and result,
Having divided phenomena and the nature of reality into two,
There is no scripture of finding the nature of reality by seeking it through phenomena.

Thus he spoke. This is the eighteenth chapter, the chapter on not being accomplished by seeking, from The All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke this chapter on accomplishment without seeking:

O great sattva, listen!
As for the three vehicles taught
By the teachers of the three kāyas who arose from me,
The three teachers did not teach the scripture of non-effort.
The single vehicle that I, the all-creator, teach,
I did not teach as a scripture of accomplishment by seeking.
O great sattva, listen!
From my nature, the all-creator, awakening,
What is unsought and spontaneously present by nature
Is the three kāyas, the heart essence of all victors:
My nature, uncontrived, is accomplished as the dharmakāya;
My essence, uncontrived, is the sambhogakāya;
My compassion, brought forth, is the nirmāṇakāya: these three.
No result accomplished by seeking is taught.

Thus he spoke.

O great sattva, listen!
The three kāyas are gathered in me, the all-creator.
All phenomena, however they appear,
Are nature, essence, and compassion, the three uncontrived;
The three kāyas are taught as my suchness.
Within me and my suchness
There is not even a trace of the qualities called buddha,
Nor any form of the faults called sentient being;
It abides just so in nonconceptual sameness.
Since there is nothing other than this,
The buddhas have received from me, the all-creator,
No definitive scripture surpassing this.
Even I, the maker, the All-Creating King,
Apart from this nonconceptual sameness, this suchness,
Have not an atom to teach myself by myself.

Thus he spoke. This is the nineteenth chapter, the chapter on what is unsought and spontaneously present, from The All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke this, which arises from the very source of all phenomena:

O Vajra who gathers all phenomena, listen!
I, the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind,
Am the heart essence of all phenomena;
I am the seed of all phenomena;
I am also the cause of all phenomena;
I am the trunk of all phenomena;
I am the foundation of phenomena;
I am the root of phenomena.
Because all phenomena, however they appear,
Are gathered in me, I am explained as the heart essence.
Because all phenomena, however they appear,
Are born from me, I am the seed.
Because all phenomena, however they appear,
Arise from me, I am also the cause.
Because the branches of all phenomena, however they appear,
Grow from me, I am the trunk.
Because all phenomena, however they appear,
Abide in me, I am the foundation.
Because all phenomena, however they appear,
Are me, I am explained as the root.

O great sattva Vajra, listen!
I, the all-creator, the awakening mind,
Teach how, from my threefold uncontrived nature,
The three kāyas arise as mere names.
Definitively, the three kāya teachers too
Have never moved from suchness.
I, the all-creator, the awakening mind,
From what is unborn and free of apprehended and apprehender,
Bring forth the dharmakāya, unborn, as a mere name.
Definitively, the dharmakāya is a mere name;
It has never moved from suchness.
The bringing forth of the essence of me,
The all-creator, the awakening mind, as a mere name,
Is the sambhogakāya in the manner of a mere name.
Definitively, that sambhogakāya
Has never moved from suchness.
I, the all-creator, the awakening mind,
Bring forth the nirmāṇakāya of compassion:
From the uncontrived nature of compassion
The nirmāṇakāya arises in the manner of a mere name.
Definitively, that nirmāṇakāya too
Has never moved from suchness.
Just as the three kāyas have never moved
From the nature of suchness,
So the phenomena taught by the three kāyas,
The retinues gathered around the three kāyas,
And all phenomena too have never moved.
Those who designate as cause and result
The nature of reality that has never moved,
And, making effort and practice,
Wish a result to arise from a cause,
Have strayed from what I, the all-creator, have made,
Which is beyond cause and result.

Thus he spoke.

O great sattva, listen!
The three kāya teachers who arose from me,
The buddhas of the three times who arose from me,
The retinues, places, times, and so forth that arose from me,
Since all of them are my awakening mind,
Sattvavajra, do you conceptualize them or not?
Sattvavajra, do not conceptualize them!
I am primordially the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind;
All that I have made is also the awakening mind.
My nature is primordially utterly pure;
My pervading of all is primordially perfected;
My nature, being clear, is primordially mind.
I, by a nature like space,
Through the awakening mind that primordially is,
Just as phenomena appear in the world like illusions,
The three kāya teachers and the buddhas of the three times
Arise as children and grandchildren in the form of a retinue,
And just as they impart the unmistaken, true personal instruction,
Having taught the awakening mind as like space,
I teach the way of arising from the all-creator as like space.

Thus he spoke. This is the twentieth chapter, the chapter on the source, showing that all phenomena arise from the all-creator, the awakening mind, from the Sūtra of the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, because he is the king who is the source of all phenomena, spoke these words that arise from himself:

O great sattva Vajra, listen!
I am the All-Creating King of phenomena.
I am the father and mother of the three kāya teachers,
And the grandfather of all the buddhas of the three times;
I act as the lamp of the teachers of the retinues, places, and times.
Since the three kāya teachers were brought forth from me,
From my threefold nature, the all-creator,
Through the three ways of explaining of those three kāyas,
The three ways of explaining the Dharma are also taught.
And those too are taught by me, the all-creator.
Because this nature of mine, the All-Creating King,
Is superior to all teachers,
Through the five ways of explaining the meaning of the proofs,
I teach the source of the unmistaken, definitive proofs.

O great sattva,
The All-Creating King is the awakening mind.
The awakening mind is primordially the Great Perfection.
The Great Perfection is beyond cause and result.
There is the teaching of the way of explaining what is beyond cause and result,
And the way of explaining how the three kāya teachers arose
From the nature of the all-creator, the awakening mind:
These two ways of explaining are taught.

As for the way of explaining the all-creator, the awakening mind:
Because the awakening mind is beyond cause and result,
It is explained through the five ways of explaining the meaning of the proofs.
As for the ways of explaining of the three kāya teachers:
Within the five perfections,
The dharmakāya explains through blessing;
The sambhogakāya shows its own essence;
The nirmāṇakāya composes the meanings into words.
These are the three ways of explaining of the three kāya teachers;
They are the ways of explaining my threefold nature.
I, the all-creator, am beyond cause and result;
Being beyond cause and result, I am beyond every sphere of activity.
The nature of reality beyond all is the awakening mind.
The awakening mind is the heart essence of all;
Being the heart essence, it also shows the source of conviction.
The awakening mind is the root of all phenomena;
Being the root, the meaning of everything is gathered in it.
The awakening mind is superior to all;
Being superior, it also tells the vehicles apart.
When the awakening mind is realized, all, the nature of reality, are buddha;
Therefore it is taught for the sake of the fortunate.
The nature of mind cannot be expressed in words;
Yet if it is not expressed, the nature of mind does not appear.
If one did not say that it does not appear and cannot be indicated,
None would see their own mind, and all would make effort and practice.
Therefore the meaning of words for the inexpressible is also explained.
Therefore, through the five ways of explaining the meaning of the proofs,
The meaning of the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind,
Is established unmistakenly and definitively.
It is established as without cause and result.
One is convinced that everything is the awakening mind.
Being convinced that it is primordially the awakening mind,
One is also convinced that there is no seeking in mind.
Being convinced that there is primordially nothing to seek,
One abides in the state of effortless great bliss.
My nature, the all-creator, abides like this;
There is no buddha other than me, the all-creator.

Thus he spoke. This is the twenty-first chapter, the chapter on the way of explaining the proofs, from The All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind.

Then, in order to settle the mind in its natural state, the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke this great scriptural source of the perfection beyond action:

O great sattva Vajra, listen!
O, the domain beyond action is nonabiding, like space:
Nonabiding, without an object of focus, nonconceptual, the path of the Dharma.
It arises in the domain of the subtle portions of dedication.
Since a dharmakāya of conceptual meditation has no special point,
Self-arisen wisdom, nonconceptual, abides everywhere just as it is.
Free of action, not abiding in objects, it cannot be altered by antidotes.
Through the power of the ancillary teachings one seeks the domain of the heart essence,
And, conceiving of no aspect at all, plays in it through skillful means.
Since the heart essence arises as nature, there is no dharmakāya elsewhere.
Held within a single atom, it is yet free of all the ten directions.
The nonabiding meaning itself is the excellent self-arisen wisdom.
In the vast heart essence, nonconceptual toward all that is directly perceived,
Entering that utterly pure path, one attains the king of sameness.
Unchanging and without change, it has no place for attachment;
Likewise there is no object grasped, and no place for the mind either.
Wishing to attain it directly, they always meditate on the cause;
In that bliss of attachment to meditation, the meaning of sameness does not arise.
Since a single kāya pervades all, there is no phenomenon to add;
Since it never reaches an extreme, nothing is taken from the expanse.
Through the play of the Dharma there is no place of loss or surplus;
In the great domain of the self-arisen it abides everywhere just as it is.
Wondrous! There is no object to see, and for the eye that would see it
There is nothing that may rightly be heard; it is free of all designations.
Dharma and non-dharma are always mingled and equal.
Nor is there any teaching of a higher state called knowing the ultimate Dharma.
Nor is that path of delusion and awakening attained through conception.
Self-arisen wisdom itself is free of the limits of words.
Toward the self that is primordially vast and abides directly,
Conception arises about it, as in the example of a shadow and a form.
It is not, yet it is not nonexistent; the heart essence of nonexistence arises.
It is empty, yet it is not emptiness; it abides in the domain of emptiness.
In the nature of space, the mindfulness that arises there is born.
The bliss of being already beyond action is taken up without desire.
There is no object to consume; in it the arising of wisdom begins.
Set aside, too, the mind attached to following the sages of old.
Alas, they are wholly seized by the torment of laborious striving!
Entering that path of nature, they think of the "just as it is" of omniscience,
And for that reason meditate with conceptions.
Desiring great bliss is the disease of attachment;
If one does not take the great medicine of abiding unmoving in sameness,
That cause for traveling to the higher realms will be seized by afflictions.
Great is the disease of those who enter a path where there is no path;
Wanting to arrive, they are like a deer chasing a mirage.
There is no object to find; it does not occur in the three worlds;
Even a state that depends on the ten grounds is an obscuration to awakening.
Exceedingly swift wisdom is free of all thought.
Like a precious jewel that comes forth from all spiritual friends,
Without focus and not depending on any place of change,
Through the excellence of its own nature it fulfills every hope.
Examined, it is not there; yet left as it is, great excellence arises.
Not appearing as a thing, it shows itself to all in the guises of attachment.
The teacher free of self and other is a treasury of jewels.
Selflessness and compassion teach it as "the domain where all is accomplished."
Not moving from within, there is no place to seek within;
Objects take form outside, yet there is no focus of dedicating to objects.
Through that selflessness and compassion, neither emerging nor entering,
It is no one else's wealth; it does not come; it abides primordially.
If you want bliss, you turn your back on bliss;
Seized by bliss, by bliss you seek bliss.
Deluded about awakening, ever hungry, they feed on dharmas.
One with an object such as this sees no buddha.
Since there is no buddha, there is not even the name buddha.
To teach a buddha and designate it by a name is a mistake;
To attain buddhahood from somewhere else is a mistaken path.
Of all formless phenomena there is not an atom to explain.
Free of grasping and attachment, and at peace,
Without entities, and wholly relinquishing,
In the nature of the great nectar
There is no dependence on any means of eating.
Vast! Great! The great Dharma
Is the antidote to the lesser ones.
Having leveled the aspect of focusing on the great,
Small and great alike are free of focus.
Speech, expansion, focus, and appearance
Are like the drawings of a magician.
Through dullness toward wisdom, with emerging and entering,
Beings are born under its sway.
This is the king of all vehicles.
Let go! Leave it be! It is the nature.
Without desire and without taking up,
It does not give rise to even an atom of relish.

Like the great garuḍa soaring through the sky,
There is no projecting and no gathering in;
There is no fear of loss, and nothing to consume.
Abiding primordially like the ocean,
It gives rise to the manifold phenomena.
Its qualities are equal to the limits of space;
There is no fixed place in which to gather them.
At the seat of awakening, at that very instant,
The great king of meditative absorptions arises.
Appearance is like a great ocean;
Nonconceptuality is as vast as the limits of space.
In the domain of Samantabhadra's experience
There is no phenomenon of birth or transmigration.
The twelve links of causes and conditions
Are spoken of as imputed by way of deprecation.
As merely the doorway of delusions:
So should the wise understand them.
The appearances of the six classes of beings, too,
Should be understood as the primordial path.
Since sensual enjoyment is moistened by compassion,
Wherever one takes delight, one practices awakening.
Butchers, prostitutes, the five acts of immediate retribution:
These the world shuns as blameworthy,
Yet in the reckoning of the completely perfect Dharma
They are nothing other than great bliss.
Moreover, all phenomena, therefore,
Since they are that very nature of phenomena,
To seek the nature of reality by means of phenomena,
Is like space seeking space;
A nature of reality that depends on something else
Is like fire killing fire:
It is to do a task most difficult.
This essence of nonconceptuality
Is concealed in no one's mind-stream.
For those whose conduct of awakening takes nothing up,
It abides as the nature in every way.

Thus it was spoken. This is the twenty-second chapter, on non-abiding and the absence of an object, from the Ten Scriptures of Effortless Perfection.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke this scripture of the view without an object: "O great being, listen!

All phenomena, however they appear,
Since they are one's own awakening mind,
Have no object there to be looked at.
Being nonconceptual, they are equality itself.
Likewise this is the realization that is like space,
And that is what is called yoga.
The appearances to the five senses,
Being self-luminous, are not conceptualized there;
That too abides as the space-like realization,
And the yogi abides in suchness.
Though words, letters and meaning are examined,
Since there is nothing to examine in words, letters and meaning,
Nonconceptual equality is suchness;
That too abides as the space-like realization.
This inconceivable meaning
Abides primordially, like space."

Thus it was spoken. This is the twenty-third chapter, teaching the space-like realization of the view without an object, from Effortless Perfection.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, taught this chapter of the scripture of effortless perfection on the way the seal abides: "O great being, listen!

This is the teaching on how mind itself,
Samantabhadra, abides:
When it abides as the awakening mind,
It abides as the essence of all phenomena.
To realize this is the All-Creating King.
The All-Creating King is without change,
And therefore it is taught as changeless.
When it abides as the dharmakāya,
It abides free of the elaborations of apprehended and apprehender;
Free of apprehended and apprehender, it is without change.
When it abides as the sambhogakāya,
Since it possesses the five qualities of sense pleasure,
It abides in the perfection of what is desired.
When it abides as the nirmāṇakāya,
At the very time that beings are to be trained,
It emanates whatever body each one needs,
And abides in the perfection of what is needed.
Throughout the three times it abides without change.
The seal of all phenomena is the characteristic of space;
The characteristic of space is suchness;
The characteristic of the three bodies abides as suchness;
Everything abides as suchness.
All phenomena, however they appear,
Being just as they are, are not to be contrived.
Through all seeking and accomplishing,
Not a single one has arrived beforehand by traveling;
Not a single one has finished beforehand by doing;
None has been accomplished beforehand by accomplishing and seeking.
All, being just as it is, is without change."

Thus it was spoken. This is the twenty-fourth chapter, the scripture of the changeless seal of effortless perfection, from the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke this teaching that for the sentient beings of the three realms their own mind is the Teacher.

"O all you sentient beings of the three realms!
Your own awakening mind
Is revealed to be the Teacher.
Because for hundreds of thousands of eons
You did not see your own mind as the Teacher,
I, the All-Creating King,
Have brought forth your own mind as the Teacher.
Listen to the Dharma of your own mind as the Teacher!
I, the All-Creating King,
From the awakening mind, the cause of all,
Brought forth the five great elements, the essence of mind,
The five teachers, the awakening mind.
The body is the sambhogakāya;
The Dharma is expounded through its own essence;
The teaching teaches its own nature.
That Teacher, the sambhogakāya,
Does not conceptualize its realization itself,
Nor does it conceptualize other objects or phenomena.
Through the five teachers of the awakening mind,
All teach the nature of reality just as it is.
Through that wisdom of the awakening mind,
The Teacher, having arisen as the body of earth,
Does not teach through words and letters,
But teaches its own nature;
Not conceiving of self, not conceiving of other,
It teaches the realization of nonconceptual equality.
All sentient beings of the three realms,
Realizing this, are equal to all the buddhas;
The reality one strives for is accomplished without seeking.
Through that wisdom of the awakening mind,
The Teacher, having arisen as the body of water,
Does not teach through words and letters,
But teaches through its own essence;
Not conceiving of self, not conceiving of other,
It teaches the realization of nonconceptual equality.
All sentient beings of the three realms,
Realizing this, are equal to all the buddhas;
The reality one strives for is accomplished without seeking.
Through that wisdom of the awakening mind,
The Teacher, having arisen as the body of wind,
Without teaching through words and letters,
Teaches the Dharma through its own essence;
Without conceiving of self and other as two,
It teaches the realization of nonconceptual equality.
All sentient beings of the three realms,
Realizing this, are equal to all the buddhas;
What one strives for is accomplished without seeking.
Through that wisdom of the awakening mind,
The Teacher, having arisen as the body of space,
Without teaching through words and letters,
Teaches the Dharma through its own essence;
Without conceiving of self and other as two,
It teaches the realization of nonconceptual equality.
All sentient beings of the three realms,
Realizing this, are equal to all the buddhas;
What one strives for is accomplished without seeking.
Through that wisdom of the awakening mind,
The Teacher, having arisen as the body of space,
Does not teach through words and letters,
But teaches the Dharma through its own essence;
Without conceiving of self and other as two,
It teaches the realization of nondiscrimination.
All you sentient beings of the three realms:
These teachers that have been brought forth
Expound the Dharma through their own essence;
Through this, all of you, understand!
O all you sentient beings of the three realms!
I, the All-Creating King, say:
Because you were made by me,
You are my children, equal to me.
Because you are not two with me, you are me.
What I have brought forth for you
Through the five teachers, my own essence,
Shows the five natures to be one.
The one is I, the all-creator;
So you too, have faith in this!
O all sentient beings of the three realms without exception!
When I do not exist, you too do not exist.
At the very time when you do not exist,
The five teachers do not arise either,
And the nonconceptual Dharma is not taught either."

Thus it was spoken. This is the twenty-fifth chapter, on bringing forth one's own mind as the Teacher, from the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, as a sign that the five great phenomena arising from mind had come forth as teachers, spoke this, accepting the unborn awakening mind as the Teacher: "O great being, listen!

The awakening mind, beyond thought and inexpressible,
Having become the lamp of the Teacher, has its qualities highly praised;
Having become the essence of the Dharma, it is youthful Mañjuśrī himself;
Free of activity, spontaneously perfect, it abides in spontaneous bliss.
It is the ground of the ocean of conduct, moral discipline and the rest,
Teaching all the paths of liberation, however many there may be.
That path shared by all who become mothers of the sugatas:
Without it, nothing arises; therefore it is the supreme path of liberation.
Subtle and hard to know, it is the path of all, nonconceptual, beyond concepts;
Nonabiding, without reference, free of elaboration, free of all thought;
Words do not reach it; it is no domain of shape and color;
It is hard to show and hard to examine, and there is not an atom to express.
Whoever among the sages of old entered upon the path
Was surely seized by the disease of a path attached to meditation.
If, following the Teacher's scriptures, one sees conduct at the limit of words as the path,
That is following the stream of thought, like chasing a mirage.
The true path cannot be indicated by words;
Even my teaching it as the true itself is a mistake.
Pure and impure are nondual, blended and equal.
Not distinguishing in any way between wisdom and delusion,
An unobstructed, luminous lamp, free of all thought,
Naturally unmoving and still, it abides as the king of meditative absorptions.
Since in seeing directly there is no seeing, it is the eye that sees directly;
Therefore it is called the eye of omniscience.
In the vast essence that has no edge or center,
Neither taking up nor discarding, it abides as the king of equality.
Mind and habitual tendencies are nondual, blended and equal.
The phenomena that grasping imputes and that appear,
Since they appear as its own ornament, are neither discarded nor rejected.
Thinking of nothing at all, it plays with them through skillful means.
The phenomena at odds with all, which everyone rejects,
The five afflictions and the five acts of immediate retribution:
Entering the path of their complete purity, one attains the king of equality.
Women and all the rest, too, are not rejected.
To set both the account and its meaning in a mind of valid cognition,
To stabilize the three meditative absorptions and practice the extremes of tenet systems,
Is called straying from the scripture beyond dispute: it is a mistake.
Free of activity, spontaneously perfect, it abides in spontaneous bliss,
Self-arisen wisdom, the very essence of the great,
Unmoving, unchanging, free of all designations.
With the nectar of completion, it destroys the suffering of striving;
Free of activity, it abides just as it is in the domain of completion.
O great being, listen!
Since all phenomena have the nature of the great sphere,
There is no projecting and no gathering,
No arising and no ceasing;
Without cessation, it abides just so.
This essence of nonconceptuality,
Since it abides primordially like space,
Transcends the domain of conceptual speech and thought."

Thus it was spoken. This is the twenty-sixth chapter, on effortless perfection that is not to be meditated upon, from the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, taught in stages the great scripture in accord with the conferral of his own instructions: "O great being, listen!

Since all phenomena arise from me,
All phenomena, however they appear,
Are taught as the scripture of the primordially pure expanse.
Outer and inner are all primordially the dharmadhātu.
In the domain of experience that is primordially pure,
Buddhas and sentient beings are not two.
How could there be contriving by path and antidote?
Since there is nothing to accomplish, no exertion of skill, and no aspiration,
Effortless spontaneous perfection, primordial, is the very meaning.
In the dharmadhātu, where conceptual conduct and complete purity are not two,
How those of wrong thinking are deceived by childish conduct!
In great bliss beyond duality, the conduct of all sentient beings,
Even what delusion imagines to be a mistaken path,
Is not two with the great path taught above;
Knowing it as equal, one is the lord of all buddhas.
To conceive of "I" and "self" is the deluded path of the tīrthikas.
Those deceived by the childish, who thrust Dharma practice into the path of conceptual thought,
Have no time of arrival and no time of realization;
How could reality, searching for reality, ever find it?
The scriptures of monkey-like masters who have not become valid authorities:
Their teaching and their path are mistaken, surely seized by conceptual thought.
Therefore the precious treasury of the master who teaches what is true,
For which one would hand over gold by the fistful,
Is worthy to be bought at a price beyond measure."

Thus it was spoken. This is the twenty-seventh chapter, teaching the scripture of the pure expanse of effortless perfection, from the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke this on his own unchanging nature: "O great being, listen!

My nature is unchanging in the expanse.
My wisdom, space, is unchanging.
My reality, the intrinsic nature, is unchanging.
My mind, the essence of phenomena, is unchanging.
Though in the same way everything is unchanging,
The teachers of the three bodies who arose from me
Gave names to phenomena as cause and result,
And the emanations declared that the senses and their objects are to be abandoned.
To sentient beings who hold the five phenomena of their own conditions,
They did not declare that these are self-arisen reality.
Declaring that the lower realms and the obscurations of evil are great,
They had beings abandon the evils of body, speech and mind and practice the ten virtues.
Because one practices accepting and rejecting toward self-arisen phenomena,
One will not meet self-arisen wisdom for many eons.
O great being, listen!
To the retinue that gathered from the emanation itself,
Having rejected the Dharma of the five self-arisen wisdoms,
They declared: abandon the five wisdoms and conquer the five enemies.
Not realizing the five enemies as self-arisen wisdom,
They would abandon their own mind; this I have not declared.
O great being, listen!
Regarding the reality that arises from one's own mind,
Saying that through training by means of the two truths, ultimate and conventional,
The result arises from the cause,
Some train for as long as three eons;
Yet through the vows and the ten perfections, it does not change itself.
I have not declared that mind itself changes.
O great being, listen!
Having blessed one's own mind, reality, as a meditative absorption,
And made it clear like the moon in water through purity,
Regarding it as a lord and presenting it with pleasing offerings,
Some hope thereby to attain the fulfillment of their wishes.
There is no moon in the water; because they have not realized their own mind,
For as long as seven lifetimes there is no self-arisen wisdom.
This is not what I have declared.
O great being, listen!
Through the meditative absorption in which one meditates on the blessing and miraculous display of one's own mind,
Some hope that the wisdom being of absorption, an equal companion,
Will grant them the accomplishments they desire.
In self-arisen reality there is no desiring and no taking up.
One's own effortless mind will not be met in three human lifetimes.
This is not what I, the all-creator, have declared.
O great being, listen!
Making a deity of the reality that arises from the natural state,
Through the four mudrās and the radiating and gathering of the heart mantra,
Some practice to accomplish unchanging reality as a cause,
Thinking to attain it through the power of the mind's absorption.
I have not declared that it is attained even through that.
O great being, listen!
Because the ground, the expanse, is unchanging,
I have not declared that this expanse changes.
The three bodies, which hold mastery over the expanse,
Taught their own retinues a meditative absorption that seeks calm abiding;
Therefore it is taught as a scripture that leads onward.
Likewise space, which is unchanging:
Space itself will not accomplish space.
Likewise reality, which is unchanging:
Phenomena themselves will not accomplish reality.
Likewise mind itself, which is unchanging:
Mind itself will not by itself accomplish mind itself.
The meditative absorption that would alter the unchanging,
Setting times, aspirations and hopes,
Is bound to time, and so the absorption has a limited life.
I, the all-creator, have given no definitive teaching on it."

Thus it was spoken. This is the twenty-eighth chapter, on the unchanging effortless perfection that is not found by searching, from the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke this on resting the mind in its natural state.

"O great being, listen!
I am the all-creator, the awakening mind.
All phenomena are me,
And my nature, all of it,
Is primordially at rest in its natural state.
The teaching of my nature
Should be understood as fivefold:
Teacher, teaching, retinue, place and time;
These five by nature rest in their natural state.
The nature of the teacher is set out like this:
The dharmakāya, uncontrived, rests in its natural state;
That is the resting of my very own nature.
The sambhogakāya, uncontrived, rests as essence;
That is the resting of my nature.
The nirmāṇakāya, uncontrived, rests as compassion;
That is the resting of my nature,
The resting of self-arisen wisdom.
The place is the place where one abides;
That too rests in its natural state.
The time is whatever time, earlier or later, comes together;
It comes together in the time of natural rest.
O great being, now listen!
Apart from the five perfections
Resting uncontrived in their natural state,
There is no other thing called Dharma.
O great being!
Not a single phenomenon not contained in the perfections
Has been said to exist.
O! Through my resting in the natural state,
Everything is liberated as reality.
The teacher, uncontrived, is liberated as reality;
The teaching, uncontrived, is liberated as reality;
The retinue too, uncontrived, is liberated as reality.
O! Since everything is declared to be liberated,
Do not contrive your body: do not meditate on a deity.
Do not contrive spoken words or voice.
Do not practice meditative absorption: do not contrive the mind.
Since through contriving there is no union with the natural state,
Without union with the natural state there is no liberation,
And without liberation the equality of reality is not attained.
O great being, it is like this:
The All-Creating King of the awakening mind
Rests in a natural state primordially transcendent,
Yet those holding views do not know how to rest in the natural state.
When the śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas hear or see
The Great Perfection, the all-creator, primordially at rest,
They say, 'Poison arises,' and reject and block their own mind.
They too do not know how to rest the view in its natural state,
And remain for countless eons not knowing how to rest.
If those of the Great Vehicle sūtras hear of the Great Perfection,
They do not realize their own mind as suchness;
Not knowing how to rest just as it is,
They remain for three eons traveling and training.
Next, if the beings of Yoga hear of it,
Not realizing their own mind as suchness
Amid the suchness of phenomena appearing like this,
And not knowing how to rest in the natural state just as it is,
They remain for seven human lifetimes in blessing the mind
Through the branches of awakening and various miraculous displays.
If those of Great Yoga hear of it,
Since it is the nature of cause and result,
Though it is primordially so, they strive anew to accomplish it.
For two thousand six hundred
They do not know how to rest in the natural state just as it is,
And remain on the ground of the three meditative absorptions.
If those of Thorough Yoga hear of it,
Though it is primordially so, they give it the names cause and result,
And, not knowing how to rest in the natural state just as it is,
They establish the cause as the pure dharmadhātu
And the result as the pure maṇḍala of wisdom;
Without interruption, they remain for a single human lifetime.
If those of Utmost Yoga hear of it,
They abide on the ground of primordial buddhahood.
Through this effortless great bliss they attain it;
Without accomplishing anything, they attain primordial buddhahood.
The faculties of gods, humans and sentient beings differ:
Some have faculties that carry on the momentum of training;
Some have faculties that are primordially all at once.
Therefore it is taught as purpose, need and meaning.
What is called Atiyoga, the Great Perfection:
Ati is taught as a word explained by its sound.
A is unborn reality resting in its natural state;
Ti teaches spontaneous accomplishment without seeking.
Yoga: in the Utmost Yoga, the Great Perfection,
Those who speak of cause and result existing
Do not possess the meaning of realizing the Great Perfection.
If one speaks of two, ultimate and conventional,
These are words of superimposition and deprecation;
Through them there is no realization of nonduality.
The realization of the buddhas of the three times, too,
Is held to be not seeing two, resting in the natural state itself."

Thus it was spoken. This is the twenty-ninth chapter, on resting realization in its natural state, from the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke to his own heart essence, Sattvavajra, this undeclining victory banner, the scripture of effortless perfection that is Sattvavajra's own nature: "O Sattvavajra, listen! To you, Sattvavajra, I shall teach your own nature. You yourself are I, the all-creator, and I am primordially the awakening mind. The awakening mind is like this." Therefore the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke this Great Space, the undeclining victory banner.

Vajrasattva, Great Space,
Samantabhadra, the vast dharmadhātu:
To free all beings by the great pure path,
Unborn and unceasing, it intends nothing at all.
Since through love it purifies the meaning itself,
Great compassion does nothing whatsoever.
The qualities of the profound, greater than the great,
Are in no way to be praised.
Things, just as they are, are unmoved;
Being free of activity, they are liberated by liberation.
Since self-arisen wisdom is not sought,
When liberated, it also shows the path of liberation.
The great elements, the Blessed One,
Abide as the nature in all beings.
Though imagined in an inverted way,
Liberation is self-arisen, not from another.
Great wisdom, hard to find,
Is accomplished by relying on wisdom and means.
Though in name alone it seems to rely on another,
Directly perceived bliss arises from oneself.
Great miracles are not difficult:
All the qualities and powers,
Through subtle realization just as it is,
Arise at once from oneself.
The nature of reality, without appearance,
Is meditated upon by resting without seeking.
If one searches for it here and there,
It does not arise from that in that way.
The supremely secret nature of reality
Is not heard by the ear from another;
Likewise, by the faculty of the tongue,
There is not an atom of it to be spoken.
The suffering of beings is the awakening mind;
Being wholly pervaded, it plays.
While in it there is no movement,
It abides evenly, like the limits of space.
In what is alike in every way, without distinction,
One imagines, "This is karma."
If it came under the power of karma,
There would be no self-arisen wisdom.
The cause itself is vajra, like the conditions;
Being unborn, there is no destruction.
In the essence, awakening from the very beginning,
The thought of seeking stirs the expanse in name.
The meditative concentration of great qualities,
Being concentration itself, cannot be conceived.
Like a phenomenon neither thought upon nor trained,
Wisdom is born from conceptual thought itself.
Naming it a subtle doorway,
Seeking the path in the mind's solitude,
Holding to solitude in a line of hermitages:
If examined, this is meditation that turns into conceptual thought.
Naming things cause and result,
They exclude both virtue and evil;
Saying, "It arises in this world,"
They greatly arouse a relish for accepting and rejecting.
Attached and unattached are a path of words;
Like the Middle Way, they resemble an echo.
"Pleasure and pain are concordant causes,"
Said the being who is the protector of beings.
Desire, aversion and delusion, too,
Arise from the path of great awakening.
The five qualities of all experience, too,
Are said to be ornaments of the expanse of reality.
Space gives birth to no concept,
And concept itself is like space.
From the unattached dedication of space,
Space, the great benefit for oneself, arises.
Nonconceptual equality, the dharmakāya,
Is not caught by grasping; it is like the moon in water.
Through the play of Samantabhadra
The vowels and consonants are profoundly taught.
This is like ā and the beautiful ta,
And pa with its limbs spread out;
In the domain of the whole world
The profound speech of the buddhas arises.
How wonderful! This domain of the buddhas
Has no place to be found by seeking;
Like the phenomena of the six senses, it has no object,
Like a blind man groping at space.
The pure path that rises ever higher
Does not accord with the Dharma free of activity.
If one were to travel on a path,
Like the limits of space, there would be nothing to attain.
Thus, just so, because of that,
That is taught to that, and that too is attained.
That is the essence; therefore
That arises from that, greatly wondrous.
That of before and that of now
Are likewise great in the place of that.
Thus resembling the path of that,
That is the nature of that.
Like that is the path of all,
Arising from the moon, together with its support.
Since it is the equality of all,
Nothing is established by looking toward a direction.
Thus bliss now and bliss hereafter
Arise in direct experience and later on;
Since that too is a fault of aspects,
One should not rely on it.
The three times are one, without distinction;
With no before and no after, it arises from the beginning.
Because the dharmakāya pervades, being one,
The greatest of the great abides as the nature.
Even union within the three realms of existence
Is a mere name, appearing as an illusion;
Even the great estate of a universal monarch
Is a lodging for training in illusion.
Those whose conduct depends on time:
That time will never come.
Since they practice aspiration without separation,
As the characteristic of emptiness was taught,
It is one, with no aspects at all;
The yogi abides in the bird's path through space.
In the essence, unarisen and unborn,
How could there be any phenomena superimposed?
Outer and inner both: the outer itself is inner;
The profound has no parts and no domain to be realized.
Existence is a mere name, the force of error;
Therefore it is free of absorption and equipoise.
In it, the conventions of outer and inner,
The nature, the aggregates and the elements abide as they are.
Since the three times are inseparable from this,
No conventions are designated as names.
The unmoving is the seal of the body;
The unshakable is wisdom;
Not taking up is selflessness;
Not discarding is equality beyond words.

Whoever, by whatever means, and wherever,
All that serves and all that is enjoyed arises from me.
In this, that there are "male" and "female"
The King of Equality has never said.
In this there is not even the name
Of a place to be reached through fierce ascetic discipline.
If one is fully endowed with the primal A,
Illusory bliss is held to arise.
Since its nature is not fixed as one,
However it is looked upon, so it appears.
Even the bliss of a striving mind that longs for appearances
Is a great fault that obscures it.
Though, by meditating on a guise as the gateway of all the limbs of awakening,
Like the moon in water,
What is unstained and unattached may arise,
Such meditation is like the domain of childish beings.
Though, through the wrathful, frowning guise of the maṇḍala,
One assumes the body of the great wrathful lord,
And the syllables become manifest,
That is not seeing peace itself.
On account of the afflictions,
It is taught, like the severed crown of a palm tree
And like a seed burnt by fire,
That one does not come under their power.
Countless hundreds of thousands of categories:
However they are practiced, flowers grow.
Yet by the power of signlessness,
Nothing arises from that resting place.
The yogin who abides in this, beyond all talk,
Is one of excellent fortune.
Not separating his own aim from the aim of others,
He sports in the spontaneously present domain of illusion.
Being without remainder, it is completely perfect;
It does not change, but abides straightforwardly.
Like space, its limits are equal,
And it is not a phenomenon that depends on another.
That spontaneously present great bliss,
Through incomparable wisdom,
Is from the awareness of its own power;
Phenomena do not arise from anything else.
It is easy and difficult: difficult because it is easy.
Not abiding as direct perception, it pervades everything.
To show it by a mere name, saying "this,"
Even Vajrasattva cannot point it out.
This marvelous, wondrous play
Abides like space, free of activity.
From delusion that observes nothing at all,
It arises instantly, of itself.
This is the path that is equal for all;
It abides as the nature of all beings.
Because they are confused, corrupted by contrivance,
It is like medicine itself seeking a physician.
In the domain of understanding there is great bliss;
This is the pure world.
There, by the gathering of the light of the directions,
The four directions, the intermediate directions, above and below are formed.
From the indefinite colors of the rainbow,
The distinctions of the families plainly appear.
Likewise the moving particles are unmoving;
This is the great principal, greater than the five elements.
Not abiding in the conventional names
Of past, future and present,
It is discerned as without arising or ceasing,
And that itself is joined to the great three times.
Being equal, it has no arrangement in sequence;
Being one, it is free of dedication to the ten directions.
Even the ornaments of the feast that are laid out,
Abiding as the nature itself, are not carried around.
Being spontaneously present, there is no dedicating;
Being primordially pure, it is amṛta.
The twelve sense fields in particular
Are not seized by a superior intention.
Thinking with the mind is the patron;
Looking is the laying out of the feast.
Having come to see is accomplishment,
And that itself is the perfection of meditative equipoise.
Holding it for an instant is union;
Delighting is the sacred commitment.
By setting the dance in motion through skillful means,
The nondual union is offered.
Giving without grasping is not giving;
These are actions free of activity.
Nonconceptual wisdom having cleared away the obstructors,
The unspoken equipoise is the word of mantra.
Offering to the guru, giving,
And likewise all merit,
If done without the power of nonattachment and the unmoving,
Become a great bondage.
Therefore, that scripture applied to that very thing,
That applied to that, becomes an obscuration.
If one thus conceives that in that,
In that, that itself is not established.

Thus it was spoken. This is the thirtieth chapter of the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind: the Victory Banner of the Undeclining Nature of Vajrasattva.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke this spontaneously present Effortless Perfection, the nature of the all-creator:

O great being, listen!
Though the nature of diversity is nondual,
In its very parts it is free of elaboration.
Though one does not conceive it as "just as it is,"
Vairocana is Samantabhadra.
Since it is complete, the sickness of effort is abandoned;
Abiding spontaneously, it is left as it is.

Thus it was spoken. This is the thirty-first chapter of the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind: the Six Vajra Lines.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke to Sattvavajra this chapter of the four lines of certainty, the scripture on accomplishing the aim without doing:

O great being, thus, by knowing the meaning,
However things appear, they are one in suchness.
Let no one contrive this!
In that uncontrived king of equality,
Abide in the realization of the nonconceptual dharmakāya.

Thus it was spoken. This is the thirty-second chapter of the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind: the Four Lines of Certainty.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, taught this hard-to-realize nature of himself to Sattvavajra, the wisdom of awareness of that very nature of reality which arose from himself, the self-arisen wisdom, the king who makes all phenomena known:

O great being, understand sound with the mental faculty!
Through sound, come to know its meaning.
If the ground of all is the dharmadhātu,
How do you know the meaning of the word "dhātu"?
In this element of space, the field of vision for all,
How do you know the meaning of the word "space"?
If what is manifestly clear is the five elements,
How do you know the meaning of what manifestly appears?
The sentient beings of the three realms who arise from them,
How do they know their meaning?
King of awareness, for this very reason, listen to me.

Thus it was spoken.

O Teacher of teachers, All-Creating King,
Your nature, so very hard to realize,
I realize through the words that teach it in four ways.
Through the word "dhātu" I realize it as without division.
Through the word "space" I realize it as unobscured and clear.
Through the word "elements" I realize it as the essence of all.
Sentient beings, by mind, know the meaning of that word.
Knowing that, one knows the all-creator is extremely hard to realize.
Nothing is taught as other than the all-creator itself.

Thus it was spoken.

Then the All-Creating King spoke again to the King of awareness:

If you know this hard-to-realize nature of mine,
Then, by knowing all the sūtras, roots and branches of the Dharma,
You are also given the name "King of awareness."

O king of creating, all-creator, awakening mind,
This nature of yours, the nature of reality,
Is mind, wisdom, the nature of reality, and awareness.
I see its nature in this way:
Mind itself free of elaboration: that word is the word "nature of reality."
Mind itself unobscured: that word is "element of space."
Mind itself as essence and heart: that is the word "elements."
Awareness that discerns things one by one is the mind of sentient beings.
There is nothing other than the nature of mind;
The All-Creating King is nothing other than mind.

Thus it was spoken.

O great being,
If you know that nature of mine,
That is knowing the nature of me, the all-creator.
Knowing me, one knows the one; knowing all, one knows everything.
To you, great being Sattvavajra,
I give the name "King of awareness."
O King of awareness, if you have come to know it thus:
The teachers of the three kāyas who arise from me
Brought forth three kinds of vehicle as their teaching.
Then the three who hold that the result comes from a cause,
Through the activity of practice, with their various views and conduct,
Accomplish one or another of the three results.
How does the King of awareness know that teaching?

Thus it was spoken. The King of awareness spoke again:

O All-Creating King, the teachers of the three kāyas
Who arise from you are your three essences.
The three kinds of teaching of those three are your indirect intent;
To the individual minds of the aware, an indirect intent is taught.
With what in mind were these indirect teachings given?
Because they had in mind those who seek undispersed calm abiding,
They set each one, indirectly, on whatever object suits him.
Those teachers of the three kāyas, teaching provisional scripture,
Some teach the result of the grounds and the paths;
Some teach a result that is unreal, like the moon in water;
Some teach a result in which one accomplishes oneself by oneself.
The teaching of the three kāyas is a scripture of indirect intent;
Not taught definitively, it is a provisional teaching;
It is the samādhi of seeking undispersed calm abiding.
The Great Vehicle of cause and result is taught as provisional scripture.

Thus it was spoken.

O King of awareness, Sattvavajra, listen!
What is taught as the scripture of the ultimate, definitive meaning
Cannot be known or taught, even by the King of awareness.
It does not abide; it is not an object to be observed.
In the nature that is nonconceptual, beyond concepts,
Samādhi is not cultivated; it is free of the domain of thought.
There is no mind of desire and no result to be taken up.
One who abides nonconceptually, just as it is,
Without traversing the path, has reached the ground of a buddha.
Without training awareness, he has found self-arisen wisdom.
Without seeking enlightened activity, the nature is spontaneously present.
Without keeping commitments, the nature is completely pure.
Objects and faculties are clear as suchness.
He does not see buddhas and sentient beings as two;
He sees all as one in suchness.
In suchness there is neither one nor many.
In the essence that has not occurred and is unborn,
How could there be conventions imputed by superimposition?
O great being, King of awareness,
Buddhas, sentient beings, all of appearance and existence, the world and its inhabitants,
Are the nature of me, the all-creator, free of superimposition and denial.
In my nature, the one suchness,
Even what the teachers of the three kāyas teach separately
Is, with respect to suchness, a provisional teaching.

Thus it was spoken. This is the thirty-third chapter of the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind: the Teaching of Definitive Scripture and Provisional Scripture.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke this sūtra on the gathering of the intent of all buddhas in himself:

O King of realization, listen to the sound!
In me, the All-Creating King,
The meaning of the intent
Of all the buddhas of the three times is gathered.
In this nature of mine
Body is gathered, speech is gathered, intent is gathered.
Listen to my sound and gather the intent!

Thus it was spoken.

O Teacher, All-Creating King,
The dharmakāya and the sambhogakāya,
Samantabhadra and Vajrasattva,
Vipaśyin, Śikhin and the rest,
The seven generations down to Śākyamuni,
The thousand and two buddhas,
The spontaneously present ones, and those as numerous as atoms:
Is their intent gathered in you?

Thus he asked.

Then the All-Creating King uttered the sound of what is:

O King of realization, now listen!
Ho! Ho! I utter the sound of what is.
Through the three, example, meaning and sign,
I teach the meaning of the gathering of intent.
My nature is like space;
My meaning is the nature of reality;
My essence is the awakening mind.
O, I am unobscured and without concepts;
Space without concepts is the dharmakāya.
I am nonconceptual, without accepting or rejecting;
Without accepting or rejecting, I am like space.
Just as space has no accepting or rejecting,
So in Samantabhadra there is no accepting or rejecting;
Freedom from accepting and rejecting is gathered in me.
O great being, listen!
O, I am unborn, like space.
Like unborn space,
Vajrasattva has no birth;
The unborn intent is gathered in me.
Vipaśyin, the seven generations, Śākyamuni,
The thousand and two, those as numerous as atoms,
And all the spontaneously present buddhas too:
Their intent is the nature of space,
Unborn, nonconceptual, without accepting or rejecting.
By example, it is taught that they are gathered in me.
O great being, listen!
O, the gathering, by meaning,
Of the intent of all the buddhas spoken of above:
Just as the nature of the All-Creating King
Is taught to be unborn,
So, since all the buddhas are unborn,
The unborn nature of reality is taught to be gathered.
Just as in the nature of the All-Creating King
The nature of reality has no cessation,
So in the nature of reality
Of all the buddhas spoken of above there is no cessation;
Therefore the nature of reality is taught to be gathered.
O great being, listen!
The teaching of the gathering by sign:
The sign is the sign of all, the awakening mind.
Through the mind of the All-Creating King,
The sign of the essence of all the buddhas of the three times without exception,
Of the dharmakāya and Samantabhadra,
Of Vajrasattva and the rest,
Is gathered into mind.
Therefore it is gathered into the three, example, meaning and sign.
O, in me, the All-Creating King,
Is the general gathering, the gathering of gateways, and the gathering of intent.
In the general gathering, example, meaning and sign are gathered;
In the gathering of gateways, five kinds are gathered.
The gathering of the dharmakāya's intent
Is gathered as nonconceptual equality,
And, unmoving, it is able to display miracles.
The gathering of the sambhogakāya's intent
Is gathered as the appearance of the directly perceived nature,
And as bringing forth whatever is wished through the coming together of conditions.
The gathering of Samantabhadra's intent
Is gathered as the all-creator made manifest,
In which all that appears, whatever it may be,
Is without good or bad, without accepting or rejecting.
The gathering of Vajrasattva's intent
Is gathered as the intent that neither arises nor ceases.
The seven generations down to Śākyamuni,
The thousand and two, and those as numerous as atoms,
Who act for the benefit of beings through body, speech and mind:
Though they appear, they arise from body, speech and mind.
In the nature of reality of body, speech and mind,
The way they act for beings through body, speech and mind
Is also gathered in the all-creator itself.
In the palace of the dharmadhātu
There is not a single one not gathered in me.
In the celestial mansion of the element of space
There is not a single one not gathered in me.

When this was spoken, Sattvavajra was terrified and fainted. When he recovered from his faint, he asked:

O King of gathering, listen!
If all conditioned phenomena are gathered,
Is the unconditioned also all gathered?
Is what has not arisen also all gathered?
If the dharmadhātu and the element of space,
The perverted views of the tīrthikas,
And the views that hold to the two, permanence and annihilation,
Are also your unerring intent,
On what basis are buddhas and sentient beings designated?
What seals off the tīrthikas from the buddhas?

Thus he spoke. Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, the king in whom all is gathered, declared:

O great being, listen!
If you had come to realize my nature,
You would be convinced that everything is gathered.
Great being, you
Seem not to have realized my nature.
Because you seem not to have realized it, you are not convinced.
Realize the all-creator, the awakening mind!
If you realize the all-creator, the awakening mind,
That self-arisen awakening mind
Clears away the two extremes of permanence and annihilation.
By being unborn and free of apprehended and apprehender,
It clears away the extreme of permanent entities;
Because self-arisen wisdom is unchanging,
It also clears away the extreme of nonexistence, annihilation.
In the word for tīrthikas, mu, "the edge," is the dharmadhātu;
Stegs, "the platform," is the place of realization.
Those who see buddhas and sentient beings as two
Do not see the all-creator, the awakening mind.

Thus it was spoken. This is the thirty-fourth chapter of the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind: the Scripture of the Gathering of Intent.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, taught this text of the Great Perfection, his own teaching, which is not the domain of others and transcends cause and result, effort and accomplishment:

O great being,
This domain of mine is difficult.
Gateway, view, commitment,
Activity, path to be traversed, ground to abide in,
Wisdom and the nature of reality: these eight
Do not accord in view and conduct with the other vehicles,
The five kinds of vehicle of the three kāyas.
As for the gateway, one enters through the gate of no seeking.
The view is taught as nothing to be meditated upon.
The commitment is taught as nothing to be guarded.
The activity is taught as nothing to be sought.
The path is taught as nothing to be traversed.
The ground is taught as nothing to be purified.
The wisdom is nonconceptual and unmoving.
The nature of reality, just as it is, is uncontrivable.
O great being, if this meaning
Were taught to those of the vehicles of cause and result,
They would say, in keeping with the worldly principle of cause and result,
That it is unreasonable, since a result arises from a cause.
They hold that the cause is accomplished by sentient beings of the three realms,
And that the result is the arising of the buddhas of the three times.
As for holding that, having made the cause view and meditation,
One attains whatever result one has meditated on:
By that meditation no result is attained.
Since all are phenomena just as they are,
To contrive what is just as it is
Is to deceive the truth with falsehood, a very great wrong.
Through the effortful practice of view, meditation, commitment and the rest,
The faculties are separated from distraction by objects
And led by skillful means to a solitary place;
These are taught as means toward nonconceptual equality.

Thus it was spoken. This is the thirty-fifth chapter of the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind: the Teaching of the Scripture of the Great Perfection.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke this on the gathering of the nature of all phenomena into the nature of his own body, speech and mind.

O great being,
Realize this nature of mine!
I am the nature of the awakening mind;
The awakening mind is the All-Creating King.
The nature of the All-Creating King is body, speech and mind;
There is not a single thing not made by body, speech and mind.
The buddhas of the three times are made by body, speech and mind.
The three, body, speech and mind, of the sentient beings of the three realms
Are also made by the body, speech and mind of me, the all-creator.
All that is comprised in appearance and existence, the world and its inhabitants,
Is made, as the nature of reality that is just as it is and uncontrived,
By the body, speech and mind of me, the all-creator.

When he had spoken thus, Sattvavajra asked again:

O All-Creating King, lord of creation,
Please explain this meaning, unknown and unrealized.
Please explain to me how it is made.

When he had asked this:

O great being, listen!
I am the nature of body, speech and mind.
The body, by manifesting the nature of reality,
Has brought about the wondrous arising of Samantabhadra's play.
Speech, by proclaiming its sounds and words,
Teaches the nature of reality, the meaning, so that it is understood through words.
Mind, through unceasing self-arisen wisdom,
Makes the Teacher a lamp of wisdom
And teaches without error the essence of the nature of reality, the meaning.
O great being, realize this meaning!
Apart from what thus appears as appearance and existence, the world and its inhabitants,
I have no other essence of body;
Therefore I explain and teach through my own essence.
Great being, you too
Must teach this to others who have not realized it!
O great being, listen!
The sounds of earth, water, fire, wind and space,
And these sounds and words of the sentient beings of the six classes,
Are nothing other than the speech of me, the all-creator.
Let them be explained by joining words and sounds with their meaning.
O Sattvavajra, you too
Must teach this to others who have not realized it!
O great being, listen!
All the sentient beings of the three realms and six classes,
The nonconceptual equality of the five great elements,
And the nature of reality, unborn, nonconceptual and unceasing:
In the mind of me, the all-creator, there is nothing but this.
Great being Sattvavajra,
You must teach this to others who have not realized it!
If the great being does not teach this,
My nature will not be realized at all.
If, not realizing it, they do not meet me,
The heirs of the buddhas of the three times,
All the sentient beings who dwell in the three realms,
The ten grounds that cross the nine passes and nine valleys,
The mastery over life, the mudrā, the spontaneous presence,
The three vidyādharas and the rest, these heirs,
Will never meet me, the all-creator.
If they never meet me, the all-creator,
The sentient beings who dwell in the three realms and six classes
Will not themselves recognize their own nature.
The bodhisattvas who dwell on the ten grounds,
Even if they count out three countless eons,
Will not meet me, the all-creator,
And so they too will not know their own nature.
The three vidyādharas with mastery over the body,
Having assumed with their bodies the sambhogakāya of a buddha,
Look with the mental faculty at their own mind, which is not seen.
So they too do not see the nondual nature
Of me, the All-Creating King, and of themselves.
Until they see it, they are seized by the sickness that cuts off life.
Therefore, teach my nature!

Thus it was spoken. This is the thirty-sixth chapter of the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind: the Gathering into Body, Speech and Mind.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, taught this nature of reality of the Great Perfection, which is beyond meditation:

O great being Sattvavajra and the rest,
Those who abide on the ground of the vidyādharas,
The bodhisattvas who abide on the grounds,
And the sentient beings of the three realms whose time has come:
Since I, the all-creator, am the great aim of all,
My nature is the nature of reality, the awakening mind.
Realize that in the mind that is the nature of reality there is no meditation!
The teachers of the three kāyas, who teach cause and result,
Regarding my nature, the nature of reality beyond meditation,
In keeping with the worldly principle of cause and result,
Declared that the result arises from a cause,
And so taught the scriptures on meditation separately, in this way.
In the single essence, the awakening mind, the nature of reality,
Through the fourfold truths, suffering, origin and the rest,
They say that suffering and its origin are the cause of the lower realms,
And so they abandon that awakening mind, the nature of reality.
By this, not seeing the nature of reality, they have abandoned it.
In the essence of the awakening mind, the nature of reality,
Through the two truths, ultimate and conventional,
And the ten perfections, generosity, moral discipline and the rest,
They train and journey on the path of the ten grounds;
Not seeing the nature of reality, they abide on grounds of training.
In the essence of the awakening mind, the nature of reality,
By way of planets, constellations and auspicious times,
Through the five awakenings and the four great miracles,
They bless all these phenomena that so appear
And meditate on them as the bodies of deities.
By this they do not see their own mind, which is beyond meditation.
In the essence of the awakening mind, the one phenomenon,
Through the three samādhis and the five kinds of rite,
Upon the vajra aggregates of their own purified continuum,
They meditate on the deity through the four aspects of approach and accomplishment,
And so make appearances to be without intrinsic nature.
But since, by seeking and accomplishing, they contrive their own mind into a deity,
By this too they do not see their own mind, which is beyond action.
As for one's own nature of reality, the awakening mind,
Even the All-Creating King, the Teacher of teachers,
With regard to one's own mind, primordially liberated as the nature of reality,
Did not teach the three teachers any scripture on meditation.
If one seeks meditation and accomplishment, one has abandoned one's own mind.

Thus it was spoken.

O great being, if you wish to accomplish your own mind,
Since it is accomplished through not wishing,
One does not deliberately abide in nonconceptual equality.
Without accepting or rejecting, the nature abides in its objects;
In a state without movement, it abides naturally.
The mind is that essence just as it is;
All phenomena are accomplished as suchness.
Do not contrive what is just as it is!
Do not accomplish it as anything other than its essential nature!
Since it is yourself, do not seek it elsewhere!
If it were sought, even the Victor would not find it in the expanse.
Since it has already been done, I have nothing to do.
Since it is already accomplished, without accomplishing it now,
Rest in equality, without concepts, intending nothing at all!
O great being, listen!
The buddhas who passed in former ages, too,
Sought no Dharma other than their own mind.
They never contrived what is just as it is;
They never meditated on samādhi or conceptual thought.
Their own mind was accomplished through nonconceptuality.
Those who dwell now and those who will come later, too,
Will be accomplished through nonconceptual equality.

Thus it was spoken.

This is the thirty-seventh chapter of the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind: the Sūtra Scripture on What Is Beyond Meditation.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, taught this on how the teachings of the teachers of the retinue have become deviations and obscurations:

O great being, listen!
The three teachings
Of the three kinds of teacher of the retinue are deviations and obscurations.
If you ask how this is so:
The six vehicles that have attained certainty
Are taught as deviations from the Great Perfection.
If you ask how this is so:
The sūtra collection of the bodhisattvas,
Which aspires to the ground Universal Light,
Through the investigation and analysis of the two truths,
Holds the nature of reality to be emptiness, like space.
The great bliss of Atiyoga
Is the awakening mind beyond investigation and analysis.
What is beyond investigation and analysis is obscured by the sūtra collection.
Investigation and analysis in the Great Perfection
Are explained as deviation into the sūtra collection.
Kriyā, which aspires to the ground of the Vajra Holder,
Having entered through the gateway of the three purities,
Abides in an object purified of apprehended and apprehender.
The great bliss of Atiyoga
Is the awakening mind beyond apprehended and apprehender.
What is beyond apprehended and apprehender is obscured by Kriyā.
Apprehending and grasping in the Great Perfection
Are explained as deviation into Kriyā.
The view and conduct of Upa
Practice the conduct of Kriyā and accomplish Yoga.
Because there is no meaning that links view and conduct,
They do not realize the meaning of nonduality.
The great bliss of Atiyoga
Is the awakening mind that is nondual.
What is nondual is obscured by Upa.
Dividing the Great Perfection into two
Is explained as deviation into Upa.
Yoga, which aspires to the ground Ghanavyūha,
Having entered through the gateway of with signs and without signs,
Makes the four mudrās its chief practice,
And cannot enter what is without accepting and rejecting.
The great bliss of Atiyoga
Is the awakening mind without accepting and rejecting.
What is without accepting and rejecting is obscured by Yoga.
Accepting and rejecting in the Great Perfection
Are explained as deviation into Yoga.
Mahā, which aspires to Vajradhara,
Having entered through the gateway of skillful means and wisdom,
Upon the maṇḍala of its own purified continuum
Accomplishes the four aspects of approach and accomplishment.
The great bliss of Atiyoga
Is the awakening mind beyond effort and accomplishment.
What is beyond effort and accomplishment is obscured by Mahā.
Effort and accomplishment in the Great Perfection
Are explained as deviation into Mahāyoga.
Anu, which aspires to the ground of nondifference,
Having entered through the gateway of expanse and wisdom,
With regard to these phenomena that so appear,
Views the cause as the pure expanse
And views the result as the maṇḍala of wisdom.
The great bliss of Atiyoga
Is the awakening mind beyond cause and result.
What is beyond cause and result is obscured by Anu.
If one views the Great Perfection as the two, cause and result,
That is explained as deviation into Anuyoga.
Thus the deviations and obscurations of view and conduct have been taught.
O great being,
The view and conduct of the Great Perfection
Are not like accomplishing cause and result.
The view and conduct, that awakening mind,
Has a nature like space,
And space is beyond investigation and analysis.
Whoever engages in investigation and analysis
Accomplishes awakening only as something like space.
When investigation and analysis arise, there is deviation and obscuration.
In view and conduct, whose nature is space,
Whoever engages in apprehended and apprehender
Does not accomplish that awakening which is space.
When apprehended and apprehender arise, there is deviation and obscuration.
When view and conduct arise as two:
Just as space is not two,
In awakening there is no duality.
Whoever makes it into two
Does not accomplish that awakening which is space.
When view and conduct arise as two, there is deviation and obscuration.
Awakening has the nature of space,
And in space there is no accepting or rejecting.
Whoever engages in accepting and rejecting
Does not accomplish that awakening which is space.
Accepting and rejecting are deviation and obscuration.
Awakening has the nature of space,
And in space there is no effort or accomplishment.
Whoever engages in effort and accomplishment
Does not accomplish that awakening which is space.
Striving and accomplishing are deviation and obscuration.
Awakening has the nature of space,
And in space there is no cause or result.
Whoever views the two, cause and result,
Does not accomplish that awakening which is space.
The view of cause and result is deviation and obscuration.
O great being,
All phenomena have the nature of space,
And space has no intrinsic nature.
Space has no example;
Space has no measure either.
The meaning of all phenomena without exception
Should be understood in this way.
O great being, realize this!
Each vehicle has its own view,
Yet there is no other object of view.
In this awakening mind, the nature of reality,
There is no other object to be viewed,
So what is called "view" is explained as mistaken viewing.
Each vehicle has its own commitment,
Which serves as an escort on the path to awakening.
Because awakening has no apprehended or apprehender,
Because there is nothing else to be guarded,
In commitment too there is nothing to guard.
Each vehicle has its own activity,
Which serves as a companion on the path to awakening.
Because awakening is spontaneously present,
There is no activity to be sought;
What is spontaneously present cannot come about through action.
Each vehicle has its own ground,
Which serves as the basis and support of the path to awakening.
On the ground of the Great Perfection, the awakening mind,
Since all the buddhas of the three times,
The teachers of the three kāyas who abide there,
All the sentient beings of the three realms without exception,
And appearance and existence, the world and its inhabitants, all abide,
There is no ground to be trained on or traversed.
Each vehicle has its own nature of reality.
The nature of reality of the Great Perfection is the awakening mind.
Since there is no phenomenon apart from mind,
All the phenomena of the vehicles are the awakening mind.
The self-arisen wisdom of the Great Perfection
Does not conceptualize objects;
It is nonconceptual, unmoving wisdom.

Thus it was spoken. This is the thirty-eighth chapter of the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind: the Teaching on the Deviations and Obscurations of View and Conduct.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke this on how, in the whole of his own nature, there is no deviation or obscuration, it being the essence of the Victors.

O great being, listen!
I am the awakening mind of all,
And therefore I am declared the essence of all.
The three kāyas, the three kinds of teachers,
The buddhas who abide in the three times,
All the sentient beings of the three realms,
And all that appears and exists, the world and its inhabitants:
There is no essence from which they arise other than me.

If you ask how this is so:
From nonconceptual equality itself
The unborn dharmakāya arose.
From its own essence itself
The sambhogakāya, enjoyment of all that is wished for, arose.
From compassion, the great awakening,
Emanations arose that act for the benefit of beings.

As for all the buddhas of the three times:
The buddhas who have passed in the past
Realized nonconceptually self-arisen wisdom, mind itself,
And so became buddhas long ago;
Now they abide in the state of nonconceptuality.
The buddhas who abide at present, too,
Being self-arisen wisdom, mind itself,
Act for the benefit of beings through nonconceptuality.
The buddhas who will come hereafter,
Prophesied as that awakening, self-arisen wisdom,
Nonconceptuality itself,
Are certain to appear for the benefit of beings.

As for the vidyādharas and those on the grounds:
Since that self-arisen awakening mind
Is not seen by looking for it,
They meet it right there, as the essence of their own minds.

Since the self-arisen wisdom
Of all the sentient beings of the three realms is unobstructed,
Awareness is clear upon the objects of the senses.
This unfabricated awakening mind
Is truly declared to be the essence.

Of all that appears and exists, the world and its inhabitants,
There is not a single thing that I have not produced.
There is nothing that has not arisen from me, the cause of all.
Therefore I am declared the essence of all.

I am victorious over all:
I am victorious even over the three kāyas;
I am victorious over the three buddhas.
The vidyādharas and the bodhisattvas,
The sentient beings who dwell in the three realms,
And all that appears and exists, the world and its inhabitants:
I am their maker, victorious over all.

I, the all-creating awakening mind,
Before all other teachings,
Set out the teaching of the awakening mind.
This teaching of the awakening mind
Cannot be set out by anyone at all.
If you ask, then how is it set out:
Since in phenomena there is nothing to be done,
One enters by way of not seeking.
Since there is nothing apart from the awakening mind,
And since in the nature of reality of the awakening mind
There is nothing for the mind to meditate on,
I set out the view as having nothing to meditate on.
In the nature of reality, beyond apprehended and apprehender,
I set out that there are no sacred commitments to keep.
Since it is spontaneously present from the very beginning,
I set out that there is no enlightened activity to seek.
Since all abide in the awakening mind,
I set out that there are no grounds to train through.
The awakening mind is the destination of the journey;
Since on the ground of mind there is no journeying,
I taught that there is no traveling the path.
Since the nature of reality is the awakening mind,
One does not look for the nature of reality elsewhere.
Wisdom is self-arisen wisdom;
It is not a wisdom that conceptualizes objects.
I, the all-creating awakening mind,
Because I am victorious over all views and conduct
And am the essence of all making,
Am called the essence of the Victors.

Thus it was spoken. This is the thirty-ninth chapter of the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind: the Essence of the Victors.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke this teaching on the nature of the awakening mind itself.

O great being, listen!
Because I have set out this teaching of mine,
All the buddhas of the three times,
The vidyādharas and the bodhisattvas,
And every sentient being of the three realms,
Without exertion or practice,
Are prophesied as buddhas from the very beginning.
Therefore the three teachings of exertion and practice
Of the three kinds of teachers of the retinue
Will be ruined by deviations and obscurations.

Thus it was spoken.

Then Sattvavajra asked again:
O Teacher, All-Creating King,
Your teaching is the awakening mind.
If this awakening mind is buddha,
Are the sentient beings of the three realms buddhas?
If the sentient beings of the three realms are buddhas,
Why do they wander in the abodes of the three realms?

Thus he asked. Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke again:

O great being, listen to this!
All phenomena that arise from the awakening mind,
However they may appear,
Are the nature of reality become manifest.
In the nature of reality, which is one in suchness,
Its objects appear as five.
Saying that from the five desirable and five undesirable ones
Affliction and suffering arise,
They abandon the five objects of the nature of reality.
But since that appearance of self-arisen wisdom
Is not abandoned even in three eons,
They wander in the abodes of the three realms.

In the nature of reality, which is one in suchness,
Its objects appear as five.
Saying that the five appearances, desirable and undesirable,
Are the cause of saṃsāra,
They block the five of the nature of reality.
But since that appearance of self-arisen wisdom
Is not blocked even in many eons,
They wander in the abodes of the three realms.

In the nature of reality, which is one in suchness,
Its objects appear as five,
And the five objects, desirable and undesirable, arise.
Saying that these are a basis of attachment,
They purify the five of the nature of reality.
But since for long eons they do not make use
Of that appearance of self-arisen wisdom,
They wander in the abodes of the three realms.

In the nature of reality become manifest,
Which is one in suchness,
Its objects appear as five.
Because the five, desirable and undesirable, arise,
Saying that these are a basis for blessing,
They bless outer and inner as pure
And make the five of the nature of reality pure.
But that appearance of self-arisen wisdom
They do not bless even in seven human lifetimes,
And they wander in the abodes of the three realms.

In the nature of reality made manifest,
Arisen from the awakening mind,
From what is one in suchness
Its objects appear as five,
And from them the five, desirable and undesirable, arise.
Saying that these are the causes of the higher realms and the lower realms,
They accept and reject within suchness.
But that appearance of self-arisen wisdom
Cannot be rejected by one's own conditions;
Because they try to reject what cannot be rejected, they wander.

The nature of reality become manifest,
Arisen from the awakening mind,
Is one in suchness, and from it
Its objects appear as five:
The five objects, desirable and undesirable.
Having understood them as body, speech and mind,
They set about newly accomplishing them as body, speech and mind.
Because they strive to accomplish what has been so from the beginning,
By that very accomplishing they wander.

Therefore, O great being,
Beyond abandoning the awakening mind,
There is, from the beginning, no self-arisen wisdom.
Beyond abandoning saṃsāra,
There is nothing called nirvāṇa.
Beyond abandoning the things that bear that nature,
There is, from the beginning, nothing called the nature of reality.
All is one in suchness.
In what is one in suchness,
To alter suchness is to err.
In the word for suchness, "that" is a name for what is unerring;
"Thus" means what admits no alteration;
"Itself" shows its own defining character.
Do not alter suchness!

Whoever alters suchness,
Whoever alters the awakening mind,
Thereby brings about the very essence of saṃsāra.

Thus it was spoken. This is the fortieth chapter of the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind: the Teaching on the Nature of the Awakening Mind.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, taught this: that he is the nature of all phenomena.

O holy being, Vajra,
Understand my nature in this way:
I am the awakening mind, the lamp that teaches;
I am the essence of all the buddhas of the three times,
The father and mother of all the sentient beings of the three realms,
And the cause of all that appears and exists, the world and its inhabitants.
There is not a single thing that has not arisen from me.
Because I am nonabiding and pervade all,
I am, from the beginning, the buddhas of the three times.
Because I abide nonconceptually in equality,
I am the nonconceptual sameness, the dharmakāya, awakened from the beginning.
Because my essence enjoys everything,
I myself am awakened from the beginning as the sambhogakāya.
Because I myself am the appearance of self-arisen wisdom,
I myself am fully awakened as the nirmāṇakāya of compassion.
Therefore I am declared the essence of all the buddhas of the three times.
The appearance of my wisdom, the awakening mind,
Is the five self-arisen wisdoms and the minds of the six classes of beings.
Therefore I am the father and mother of the sentient beings of the three realms.
And since the essence of all that appears and exists, the world and its inhabitants,
Appears as having arisen as my essence,
I myself, from the beginning, am appearance awakened.
In me there is no duality: all is gathered into one, and this is water;
Because it gathers, mind itself is water.
I am the sameness of all, the heat that evens out, and this is fire;
Since I abide equally in all, fire is awakened.
I am unobstructed, light and moving;
Through unobstructed movement I am fully awakened as wind.
Because I am unobscured, all-pervading and clear,
I am the buddha given the name of space.
A buddha not made by me
Does not exist among the phenomena included in the great elements and in existence.
The teaching on my nature as the five elements
Is that in each one the nature of all twenty-five is complete.
Appearance is earth, gathering is water,
Sameness is fire, movement shines forth as wind,
And unobscured clarity is the nature of space.
That all twenty-five are complete in each one
Shows the great quality of the awakening mind.
In phenomena there is no duality; they are one as the awakening mind.
The one has made all; they are made by the All-Creating King.

Thus it was spoken. This is the forty-first chapter of the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind: the One Essence.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke this: that all phenomena are awakened solely as the awakening mind.

O great being, listen!
O, the dharmakāya is the awakening mind.
From the awakening mind, the dharmakāya
Has not been fabricated by anyone, not even by an atom's worth.
Therefore there is no buddha other than mind.
The sambhogakāya is the awakening mind;
In the awakening mind is the sambhogakāya.
Apart from the forms that arise from mind,
There is no other body that is the sambhogakāya.
The nirmāṇakāya is the awakening mind;
Apart from the emanations of the awakening mind,
There is no acting for the benefit of beings anywhere else.
All the buddhas of the three times, too,
Are nothing other than the awakening mind.
The buddhas who passed in earlier ages
Saw and realized long ago their own unfabricated mind.
The buddhas who abide at present,
Through that unfabricated mind of their own,
Have realized the unfabricated just as it is,
And now act for the benefit of beings.
The buddhas who will come hereafter
Were never instructed beforehand
To alter this self-arisen mind itself;
Now, in concentration, they do not alter the mind,
And having entered the unfabricated path, they will come.
Those who do not realize phenomena, however they appear,
To be the awakening mind
Will not accomplish anything by fabricating and striving to accomplish.
Not realizing, they try to accomplish it by fabrication.
Though they count eons numbering in the many,
They will not meet with effortless bliss.
O, since the three kāyas are not fabricated from mind,
When, in the sūtras and scriptures that teach the three kāyas,
A meaning of altering appears,
That teacher, even though of the three kāyas,
Is not the teacher who teaches the nature that is himself.
However the teacher who alters suchness
May teach the way of truth,
It is not definitive scripture; it is provisional scripture.

Thus it was spoken. This is the forty-second chapter of the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind: the Unfabricated.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke this, acknowledging that he himself is the teacher of all phenomena.

O great being, listen!
I am the teacher of all phenomena.
From my threefold nature
The teachers too are shown to be threefold.
The teachings too, in three kinds, are taught by me,
And the three assemblies that hold them are also taught by me.
If you ask which are the three teachers:
The teacher who teaches the nature of phenomena,
The teacher who teaches the essence of phenomena,
And the teacher who teaches the sounds and words of the meaning.

The teacher who teaches the nature of phenomena
Brings forth blessing from the unborn
And teaches all phenomena, however they appear,
As the suchness of phenomena.
The teacher who teaches the essence of phenomena
Brings forth all phenomena, however they appear,
As the very body of the teacher,
And teaches that there is no essence of phenomena apart from that.
The teacher who teaches the sounds and words of phenomena
Gathers the meaning of all phenomena into sound,
And to the sentient beings of the three realms, ignorant and unknowing,
Teaches that meaning, composed in sounds and words.
Therefore this is declared the teaching of the nature of the teachers.

The retinues that hold these are taught to be of three kinds.
The retinue of Utmost Yoga, beyond effort and practice,
Having entered the door with faith, abides on the ground of a buddha.
The retinue of the vidyādhara ground and of the first to the tenth grounds,
Not yet attained but drawing near to it, abides on the ground of meditation.
The retinues of the generation of the mind and of the conduct of aspiration,
Taking virtue as the cause, abide on the ground of the result, which destroys the poisons.
These are taught to be the retinues of the three teachers.

O Teacher of teachers, All-Creating King,
If the three teachers and their assemblies are thus,
How are the three kinds of teaching to be taught?

When this was asked, he replied:

O great being, listen!
The teaching of the dharmakāya teaches suchness;
In suchness there are no divisions into kinds.
All phenomena, however they appear,
Are suchness, for they are the essence of mind.
Whoever meditates on it and alters it
Does not realize the teaching of the dharmakāya.
Not realizing awakening, the nature of reality without appearance,
As unerring suchness,
Whoever alters it and meditates on it
Does not know his own mind as unfabricated.
The awakening mind, in which there are no phenomena of appearance and existence,
Is the suchness of mind arising as suchness.
Not realizing it as suchness, they see things as separate.
As for those who seek suchness out in the expanse,
Even the Victors of the three times, searching, have not found it.

O great being, listen!
The teaching of the sambhogakāya is enjoyment:
The five enjoyments are the single enjoyment of the nature of reality.
In the five enjoyments, form and the rest,
Each one is enjoyed fivefold, making twenty-five.
The enjoyment of these twenty-five is the mode of the five kāyas,
And that mode is declared to be the very essence of the meaning of the nature of reality.
As the enjoyment of all buddhas and sentient beings,
My form, the sambhogakāya, is nothing to be engaged in.
This I taught to the vidyādharas and those of the ten grounds.

Thus he spoke.

O great being, listen!
The teaching of the nirmāṇakāya suits its aims through compassion:
For the differing intentions of the sentient beings of the three realms,
Through the three, body, speech and mind,
Those differing intentions are turned back by the compassion of body, speech and mind.
Having turned back the faculties' clinging to their objects,
And having taught whatever solitary place is fitting,
It serves as the guiding scripture of my compassion.

O great being, listen!
Because I am the teacher of all phenomena,
To the buddhas, too, I teach my nature;
To sentient beings, too, I teach my nature.
Because I teach the nature, I acknowledge myself as the teacher.
Understand my nature in this way:
The teacher, the teaching and the retinues
Are nothing other than my three natures.
The teacher is self-arisen wisdom, unceasing;
The teaching is my unfabricated nature;
And the retinue that holds it holds it through my essence.

Thus it was spoken. This is the forty-third chapter of the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind: Acknowledging Himself as the Teacher.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke this, on the meaning of there being nothing to be done in himself.

O great being,
This meaning of nothing to be done
Is my nature, the all-creator.
That there is nothing to be done in me
Means that, since I have from the beginning already accomplished it,
I am the nature of reality in which there is nothing to be done.
From the nature of reality, my nature,
There is nothing to be done to that nature.
What the nature does as nature
Is a nature that cannot be fabricated.
In that unfabricated awakening,
Sattvavajra, make no alteration!
If Sattvavajra alters it,
It is altering me, the all-creator.
All phenomena, however they appear,
Are my nature, the all-creator.

O great being, listen!
My nature is changeless;
To meditate on it is to alter and transform it.
I am spontaneously present from the beginning;
To strive to accomplish me is to fabricate me.
In me, there is no arriving by traveling;
In me, there is no accomplishing by seeking;
In me, there is no cleansing by purifying.
Do not look at me: there is no object.
Do not travel in me: there is no path.
Do not purify me: there is no obscuration.
Nonabiding, I am free of any object of reference;
Free of elaboration, I am beyond any object of thought.
The phenomena imagined by grasping
Display my essence.
Whoever abandons or blocks them
Will not meet me, the all-creator,
Even for the span of three eons.
The phenomena that are to be abandoned
Are declared to be one's own essence.

O great being,
The awakening mind is the All-Creating King.
What the all-creating awakening mind has made
Is made as nothing other than the awakening mind.
The awakening mind is unerring from the beginning;
Being so from the beginning, from the beginning there is no error.
In what is without error, true and false are not superimposed.
It transcends all causes and truths, results, effort and practice.
Not knowing that it is already accomplished, beyond effort and practice,
If one thinks to accomplish it by meditating with effort and practice, doer and deed,
There is no greater obstacle to awakening than that.
Since it is nonabiding, nonconceptual, intending nothing whatsoever,
Its nature abides in the domain of nonconceptual equality.
Being accomplished from the beginning, no mind of effort arises.
Whoever abides in that great bliss
Becomes the essence of all phenomena.

Such a one does not labor for the benefit of self and others;
Through that essence in which all is gathered into one,
Without looking, the minds of the buddhas of the three times are clear,
And, unobscured, the minds of all sentient beings appear as well.

O great being, listen!
I, the all-creator, have already accomplished the benefit of beings.
What I have accomplished need not be done by another.
Phenomena that depend on another are not my scripture.
In me all is complete: already accomplished and complete from the beginning.
Being accomplished from the beginning, there is no need to meditate on the view.
Being utterly pure from the beginning, there is no need to keep sacred commitments.
Since all abide from the beginning on the ground of awakening,
There is no need to train through the vidyādhara ground and the ten grounds.
Since it is spontaneously present from the beginning, without seeking,
There is never any need to perform enlightened activity that seeks.
Since self-arisen wisdom does not conceptualize objects,
The wisdom of awareness need not be cleared of objects.
Since great bliss, the dharmakāya, is one with mind,
There are no three times, earlier and later; they are one, without distinction.
The perfections and the times and places are all one,
And since everything is one, all is complete in me.
Since all is complete in me, the Great Perfection,
View, conduct, enlightened activity, sacred commitments, grounds and paths
Need not be sought and accomplished in the way explained above.
If, through ignorance, one engages in effort and practice in that way,
One contradicts the meaning that is beyond cause and result,
And will not meet that great bliss, free of action;
One is seized by that ignorance, the sickness of effort and practice.
Therefore, since the Great Perfection, beyond cause and result,
Is not the domain of those without the fortune for it,
They should practice the teachings of cause and result.

Thus it was spoken. This is the forty-fourth chapter of the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind: the Great Perfection Is of No Benefit to Those Without Fortune.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, taught this sūtra scripture on the view without meditation in the Great Perfection.

O great being,
The view without meditation that is one's own
Is I, the all-creator; in me there is nothing to meditate on.
I, the all-creator, am not an object of meditation.
I am unfabricated, abiding naturally in all.
Concerning that absence of meditation, which abides naturally,
The teachers of the three kāyas who arose from me,
Regarding the nature of reality that I made manifest,
Say that it is the suffering of the five covetous attachments;
They do not leave it unfabricated, but meditate on ascetic disciplines of abandoning and blocking.
Such teaching contradicts the meaning of the unfabricated.
Because the compassion of the emanation
Set up the four schools of the tīrthikas as a blessed retinue and taught them,
They will not attain that very bliss which is free of action.

O great being,
Regarding the nature of reality that I made manifest,
Saying that suffering arises from the five covetous attachments,
The emanation taught the bodhisattvas of the first to the tenth grounds,
Through the analysis and investigation of the two truths,
That things do not exist ultimately but exist conventionally,
And they meditate with analysis and investigation.
This contradicts my meaning, unfabricated as it is.

O great being,
Regarding the nature of reality that I made manifest,
The nature of reality, unfabricated as it is,
The sambhogakāya taught its retinue;
But not realizing it as unfabricated, just as it is,
They bless outer and inner, the world and its inhabitants, as pure,
And by way of all the rites with the branches of awakening
They make the meditated wisdom deity appear.
Having made offerings for an instant, they request the deity to depart and dissolve the concentration.
Meditating again and dissolving again, they are like children building houses of mud.
This contradicts my meaning, unfabricated as it is.

O great being, listen!
Through the instruction of the teacher who arose from me,
Regarding the nature of reality that arose from me and was made manifest,
They enter the doors of concentration with signs and without signs,
And within the single nature of reality they accept and reject;
They meditate on the wisdom being and the commitment being as separate,
And hope that these companions will grant them accomplishments.
Meditation that sees the one as two is mistaken.
In a single essence, nothing is attained through two.

O great being, listen!
Regarding my nature made manifest,
The aggregates, elements, consciousnesses, faculties and objects,
They make their own continuum pure, like a vajra,
And meditate on the rites of the four kinds of approach and accomplishment;
Through the radiating and gathering of mind they make offerings to the noble ones and act for the benefit of beings.
Gathering in the radiating and gathering, they seek to obtain accomplishments,
And so they meditate that they themselves obtain their own accomplishment.
That too is a concentration of mind that comes to an end.

The view of the Great Perfection, without meditation,
Is the quality of my mind, the all-creator.
Through the great quality of the awakening mind
There is no need to undergo the hardships of seeking and accomplishing.
Since there are no causes and conditions, there is no need for the work of seeking.
There is no need to accomplish the nature of the result from elsewhere.
Since the nature of reality is oneself, there is no need to meditate.
Since it is unborn, there is no remedy against destruction.
Not depending on another, one does not seek a place for meditation.
Whoever meditates on me,
By that very meditation will not meet me.
Since I am the nature of reality made manifest,
Suffering does not arise and need not be abandoned.
Since it is self-arisen, without arising or perishing,
There is no need to block ignorance, dependent origination or the faculties.
The self-arisen, utterly pure nature of reality need not be purified.
Being pure from the beginning, there is no need for cleanliness.
Being spontaneously present from the beginning, there is no need for effort and practice.
When one looks, there is no impure phenomenon:
It is suchness, Vairocana, Samantabhadra.
The phenomena that appear to the six faculties, too,
Have been that from the beginning; there is nothing other than that.
Whoever meditates and practices on that
Is like one trying to climb the sky on a feather.
My nature transcends every domain of experience.
The phenomena that I have made manifest
Are displayed before the various faculties
So that all may see my essence.
In them, realize the whole of my nature!
If one does not see my nature in them,
Do not teach the vehicle of me, the all-creator.
Lead those who have no fortune for the meaning of the Great Perfection
To the provisional scripture of the vehicles of cause and result.
If this, which from the beginning transcends the domain of experience,
Is taught to those without the fortune, who delight in cause and result,
Superimposition and denial will come;
So if it is taught, they deny their own minds,
Are born among the six classes, and wander forever in saṃsāra.

O great being, realize my meaning!
Since it transcends every domain of experience,
Words cannot reach it; it is beyond the domain of the senses.
My nature is gathered and abides as a single sphere (bindu),
Unelaborated, nondual, taught from the beginning as one.
There is no need for a meditation of keeping sacred commitments;
There is no need for a meditation of seeking enlightened activity.
One does not travel the path, nor practice through the grounds;
One does not seek the nature of reality anywhere other than in oneself.
Self-arisen wisdom is nonconceptual and unmoving.
It is not the scripture of another; it is the scripture of me, the all-creator.
Being beyond cause and result, one does not engage in effort and practice.

Since it has never come into being, one does not meditate on things.
Since it is unborn, there is no fear of destruction.
Since I do not appear, it is free of meditating on a reference point.
In the center of the expanse where great bliss lies spread out,
The nature of nonconceptual equality abides.
In the nature of reality, whose nature is without appearance,
Do not, with awareness, make a nonconceptual concentration!

O great being,
If you meditate, whatever you meditate on is a sickness to me.
Since the essence of all is the awakening mind,
And since the awakening mind is free of apprehended and apprehender,
There is not even an atom to be meditated on.
Just as meditating in the ways explained above did not attain the result,
So I do not teach that I am attained through meditation.

O great being, look to me!
I am the all-creating awakening mind.
Because the all-creator made it, all is made as made by all.
"All" is declared to be the word for "without remainder";
And "creating" is that essence of making, which makes everything.
"Purified" means that nothing exists to be grasped;
"Perfected" means perfected as the essence by the essence of all;
"Mind" is self-arisen wisdom, mindful of all.

Thus it was spoken. This is the forty-fifth chapter of the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind: No View and Meditation.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke this, on there being no sacred commitments to keep in the Great Perfection.

O great being, listen!
This teaching that in the Great Perfection there are no sacred commitments to keep:
The nature of the all-creating awakening mind,
Through its nature, unborn and free of elaboration,
Gave rise to the various objects, the magical displays of birth,
And those objects are called its own nature.
In that, nonconceptual, beyond the objects of thought,
Not grasping at objects, without rejecting or accepting,
It is nonexistence, the heedless, oneness, spontaneous presence,
Free of every vow and discipline to be kept.

The three teachers who arose from me, in whom there is nothing to keep,
Blessed the four schools of the tīrthikas through compassion;
And for the retinue on whom the Vinaya was conferred, the fourfold community,
The disciplines to be kept are these:
The number of root and branch precepts to be kept
Is two hundred and fifty, and five hundred and fifty precepts.
When the time comes to keep them, they know neither their number nor their meaning,
So they cannot hold the vows and have no means to keep them.
In the Great Perfection, whatever one does is beyond all conceptions.
The vows, a boundless ocean, numerous indeed,
Are gathered into three kinds, for body, speech and mind.
Suffering arises from the faculties and their objects:
For the body, in going and sitting, there is nowhere to set down one's step;
For speech, the words one utters are hard to make true in meaning;
For the mind, one's thoughts contradict all truth;
And all unerring thoughts cannot be brought about.
Through that tight knot of the vows of body and speech
It is extremely hard to meet the meaning of truth itself.
One will not meet that in which there is nothing to keep and nothing to breach.
Nothing to keep and nothing to breach: this is the scripture of the all-creator.

O great being, listen!
Those on whom the teacher who arose from me conferred his scripture,
Choosing auspicious planets, constellations and times,
Purify themselves with ablutions of outer and inner cleanliness,
And promise vows, moral discipline and the benefit of beings.
Yet whatever they pledge is not fulfilled as it should be,
And when the pledge is not fulfilled, confession does not cleanse the mind.
For a long time they fall away, far from me, in whom there is nothing to keep.

O Vajra, the sacred commitments of the three secrets
Are the three roots and the five branches.
The three roots comprise body, speech and mind.
For the body, meditating on it as the body of a deity binds the body too.
For speech, recitation and concentration are not peace.
For the mind, holding to a concentration of radiating and gathering,
One is parted from the bliss of the root commitment, nonconceptuality.

The five kinds of branch commitments are these.
First, the commitments to be known:
The five aggregates, the five elements,
The consciousnesses, the faculties, the objects and so on
Are to be known as having the nature of deities and maṇḍala.
Through this, one does not see that there is neither knowable nor knower.
The five commitments to be practiced
Are killing, sexual union, taking what is not given,
Unchaste conduct and speaking falsehood:
These five kinds are practiced with skillful means.
Seeing it as a breach not to practice them, one practices them with conceptions,
And that too is to be parted from nonconceptual equality.
The five commitments to be embraced:
Saying that from the five sacred substances, excrement, urine and the rest,
The five bodies of the Victors are accomplished,
They radiate and gather with the five heroic seed syllables
And take up the five bodies of the Victors.
They think that if they are not embraced, the five bodies will not be accomplished,
And so the realization that is beyond accepting and rejecting is not accomplished.
The five sacred commitments not to be abandoned
Are the five poisons, attachment, aversion and the rest.
In the great commitment of the secret vajra,
The five afflictions are transformed into the five wisdoms:
Not abandoning the five poisons, they take them up as the five wisdoms.
By this the bliss without accepting or rejecting is not accomplished.
The five commitments to be accomplished:
They accomplish the aggregates, the elements,
The consciousnesses and the objects as maṇḍala;
Completing the three concentrations and the five rites,
They accomplish the maṇḍala of the five families of Victors.
With concentration and the five rites the mind strives,
Which contradicts the nonconceptual, unsought, spontaneous presence.
To nonconceptuality, the meditated body is like a rainbow;
Rites, recitations and mantra chanting are like faded paint.
Through the practice of effort and accomplishment it will not become spontaneous presence.
By accomplishing what does not exist there is no true accomplishment.

In me, the all-creator, there are no sacred commitments to keep.
Since there are no causes and conditions, there is no need to strive and accomplish.
Since it is spontaneously present, there is no need to practice.
Since it is wisdom, there is no need to know.
Since it is self-arisen, there is no need of causes and conditions.
Since there is no good or bad, there is no need to accept and reject.
Since there are no things, it is declared to be nonexistence.
Since it does not engage in things directly, and wisdom is unceasing,
It is designated the heedless.
Since all is one as mind, it is designated oneness.
Since all phenomena, however they appear,
Are complete, with confidence, in the awakening mind,
I have declared it spontaneous completeness.

Heat and cold, hunger and thirst, stupidity and dumbness, within the nature of reality,
And the six rites by which the retinues of the three kāyas flee in terror,
Need not be sought and accomplished as explained above.
Since the cause itself is self-arisen wisdom,
It does not come under the power of the conditions of karma.
Since the nature of the nature of reality does not change into anything at all,
I have not taught that it is accomplished by seeking.
Since self-arisen wisdom is unequaled by anything,
The self-arisen is not accomplished through causes, conditions and karma.
It cannot be ascertained through the two truths.
For what is single and certain, two kinds of truth?
In that uncertain scripture, full of doubt,
How could there be a teaching of single certainty?
Those yogic persons who are conceited about the Dharma,
And speak relying on the scriptures of cause and result of the three teachers,
Need the two truths and the four valid cognitions as well.
But since I, the all-creator, have made all phenomena,
The phenomena I have made need no two truths.
Even were the two truths needed for the phenomena I have made,
All that I have made, I have made as the awakening mind,
And in the awakening mind there is neither ultimate nor conventional.

Thus he spoke.

From The All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, this is the forty-sixth chapter, on there being no sacred commitment to guard.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke this teaching on there being no enlightened activity to seek in the Great Perfection:

O great being, listen!
I, the all-creator, am the Great Perfection.
Because the Great Perfection is spontaneously accomplished,
I teach that from the very beginning there is no enlightened activity to seek.
Those who declare that there is activity to seek
Are the teachers of the three bodies who arose from me,
Who assign to their own retinues the task of seeking.
Each of them assigns the task of seeking separately.
The emanation body assigns the task to the cause that is to be sought:
Entering through the gates of body, speech and mind into the immeasurables,
The vow of body is to give up killing, stealing and sexual misconduct;
The vow of speech is that idle talk, harsh words,
Lies, slander and the like are given up by speech;
By mind, covetousness, malice and wrong view are given up.
Having set up the practice of the ten virtues as the cause,
And having practiced the ten perfections, generosity, moral discipline and the rest,
Those who wish to attain the grounds and the ground of Universal Light
Practice for a fixed term of three eons.
Yet that too is not a scripture assigned by me, the all-creator.
The enjoyment body, the sambhogakāya, assigns scripture to its retinue,
Assigning tasks by way of cause and result.
The action of meditative concentration for beings is this:
Purifying outer and inner through ablution and cleanliness,
Blessing through the action of miraculous display,
Regarding the wisdom being and the commitment being as two,
They make offerings and request departure through the action of concentration.
By that action the fixed term becomes seven human lives.
Arising from the dharmakāya as the sambhogakāya,
It assigns scripture to the retinue of Great Yoga (mahāyoga).
Having declared that they are the great self,
In those maṇḍalas of their own purified continuum,
They meditate on deities matched to colors and families,
And through peaceful deities perform the action of pacifying,
Through enriching deities perform the action of enriching,
Through powerful deities perform the action of bringing under control,
And through wrathful deities perform the action of annihilating.
They attain the knowledge-holder of life and the knowledge-holder of the seal.
Through the four activities, accomplishment has no fixed term,
But the action of effortless non-doing is not accomplished by that.
Because the Great Perfection has from the very beginning transcended cause and result,
Non-doing is not accomplished by seeking and practice.
What the teachers of the view of cause and result have taught
Takes the world's phenomena of signs as its measure,
Seeking from a cause and accomplishing a result.
Mind has no cause, and from the very beginning has no result.
Because in the awakening mind there is no birth,
One should not take an example from the world's phenomena of birth
And impute that destruction comes from birth.
Not examining the self-arisen that has transcended cause and result,
Taking the world's phenomena of signs as the measure,
And saying that by seeking from a cause a result will arise:
That is a provisional scripture of the vehicle of cause and result.

O great being, listen!
What is taught as "wisdom, wisdom"
Is self-arisen wisdom, unceasing and equal to all.
That incomparable wisdom, which has not arisen from a cause,
Generates everything, so no phenomenon arises from anything else.
Taking the world's phenomena of cause and result as the measure,
The result that needs no seeking will not come about.
Because the awakening mind has no causes and conditions,
Do not measure it against the world's phenomena of arising and ceasing.
Since the awakening mind is not produced by conditions,
Do not take examples from the world's phenomena produced by conditions.

O great being,
Those who wish for greatness and strive in meditation and practice
Will not achieve that greatness by seeking it through meditation and practice.
Greatness is self-arisen, great from the very beginning.
If one meditates, one alters the intent of the buddhas.
Whoever alters the intent of the buddhas
Has thereby abandoned all the buddhas.
Yet it is not the buddhas he has abandoned:
Because he has abandoned his own awakening mind,
He will not meet his own nature for eons.

O great being,
Do not meditate on concentration with the mind!
Do not conceive of wisdom as an object!
There is no need to recite mantras and heart essences with the voice.
There is no need to form contrived mudrās with the hands.
Let the mind not perform the work of emanating and gathering in concentration.
By abiding in the natural state, it is unmoving and spontaneously accomplished.
Because it abides as its own nature, no one can contrive it.
Abiding just as it is, without seeking,
Is taught to be the supreme action, which is non-action.
Realizing that meaning, one does not perform actions;
Not performing actions, one abides in suchness.
Whoever abides in suchness
Will accomplish the unmistaken and the unaltered.
In the word "suchness," "such" is the unmistaken, "-like" is the unaltered,
And "-ness" refers to the unmistaken essential nature.
Apart from suchness there is no Dharma of the buddhas.

Thus he spoke.

From The All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, this is the forty-seventh chapter, on there being no enlightened activity to seek.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke this, affirming that the basis of all phenomena is the all-creator itself:

O great being, listen!
I, the all-creator, am the awakening mind of all.
The awakening mind is the basis of all phenomena.
From my three natures, I who am the all-creator,
Every excellent purpose is complete.
The three natures should be understood like this:
The unborn, the unceasing,
And the arising of the miraculous display of unceasing birth: these three.
These three are taught as the excellences.
From the nature of the unborn, in which there is no birth,
The basis of phenomena is taught: this is an excellence.
From the nature of the unobscured and unobstructed,
Comes the nature of the three teachers: this is an excellence.
From the essence of the miraculous display of the birth of the unborn,
Comes the threefold nature of the retinue: this is an excellence.

O great being, listen!
In my nature, the single nature of reality,
Through the three teachers, my unceasing aspect,
And from the three retinues in which the miraculous display of birth arises,
For my nature of reality, the single suchness,
There are six bases of method by which it is to be realized.
The view serves as the basis for seeing the nature of reality.
It is the method for seeing;
But since the unborn has no object on which to focus,
By looking, there is no seeing of suchness.
Sacred commitment serves as the basis for clearing away one's own faults.
It is the method for being free of obstacles;
But since in suchness there is no object to guard,
By guarding, there is no mastering suchness.
Action serves as the basis for accomplishment.
It is the method for accomplishing by seeking;
But since in suchness there is no object to seek,
In suchness there is no accomplishment through action.
The path serves as the basis for arriving by traversing.
It is the method for arriving at the destination;
But because in suchness there is no traversing by a path,
Suchness is not something arrived at by traversing.
The ground serves as the basis for abiding.
It is the method by which no training is needed;
Since in suchness everything whatsoever abides,
There is no training on a ground in which to abide.
Wisdom serves as the basis for realization.
It is the method for knowing the nature of reality;
But since in suchness there is no object to realize,
Self-arisen wisdom has no conceptualizing of objects.

O great being, listen!
Because the awakening mind is the basis of phenomena,
It was taught, through the six views and conducts of the separate vehicles,
To the retinues who rely on cause and result.
So each one, with the thought that his own way is right,
Seeks within the six bases of view and conduct;
Yet since in suchness there is no object to seek,
And because seeking contrives suchness,
They do not meet that uncontrived basis, their own mind.

O great being, listen!
As for the teaching that the view is not to be meditated on:
Because the root of phenomena does not exist apart from oneself,
And because one cannot look at oneself by oneself,
I have taught no object that is other than oneself.
Therefore the view is taught as not to be meditated on.
As for the teaching that sacred commitment is not to be guarded:
Guarding cannot achieve it; one's own mind cannot be fenced in.
Because it is self-arisen wisdom, there is nothing to guard.
O, as for the teaching that enlightened activity is not to be sought:
Because the Great Perfection has from the very beginning transcended cause and result,
While others hold that activity is accomplished from a cause and becomes a result,
In the Great Perfection there is no wish for a result,
Because there is no seeking of activity.
Thus enlightened activity is taught as not to be sought.
As for the teaching that the path is not to be traversed:
O, the buddhas of the three times and the sentient beings of the three realms,
When they traverse, traverse the path of the awakening mind.
In the mind, buddhas and sentient beings are not two.
Therefore mind traverses no path toward mind.
As for the teaching that there is no training on the grounds:
To wish to attain this dharmadhātu, the awakening mind, by training is the way of cause and result.
For this ground of the awakening mind, the nature of reality,
I have taught no scripture of accomplishment by training, contriving and doing.
As for the teaching that wisdom does not conceptualize objects:
That object is, from the very beginning, self-arisen wisdom;
For wisdom, there is no object of wisdom.

Thus he spoke.

From The All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, this is the forty-eighth chapter, teaching that the basis of all phenomena is the all-creator itself.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, taught this ground in which everything abides in me.

O great being,
I myself, the All-Creating King,
Abide as the heart of awakening of all.
The nature of the awakening mind
Is the palace of the dharmadhātu.
All the buddhas of the three times
And all the sentient beings of the three realms,
In that abode of mind, the great expanse,
Not a single one fails to abide.
It is the abode and the ground of all;
Without coming together or parting, they dwell there.
In it there is no need to train or to traverse.
If one were to train and traverse in it,
One would be contriving one's own mind,
And by contriving, one's own mind is not refined.
That is the universal ground, the abode of all.

O great being, listen!
The teachers of the three bodies who arose from me
Taught to the retinues who delight in cause and result
Grounds of three kinds:
The ground of generation, the ground of conduct,
And the ground of accomplishment: these three they taught.
In the ground of generation, the four minds are generated;
In the ground of conduct, the mind practices the ten;
In the ground of accomplishment, one wishes for accomplishment.
Because that is taught as a provisional scripture,
It was not taught as truly and definitively so.
Listen to this scripture of mine, which is truly definitive.
Because the awakening mind has no causes or conditions,
The awakening mind is not generated in a ground of generation.
If the awakening mind were generated in a ground of generation,
The space of the dharmadhātu could not contain the awakening mind.
By letting mindfulness hold sway over virtuous and nonvirtuous actions,
One abandons outer objects, the nature of reality, yet they are not abandoned;
One blocks self-arisen wisdom, which cannot be blocked;
And though self-arisen wisdom does not conceptualize objects,
One destroys it by saying that there are grasped and grasper.
Therefore in the awakening mind there is no generation.
In the ground of conduct one practices the ten of awakening;
Yet all phenomena, however they appear,
Being unmoving in the awakening mind,
Are nothing but the appearance of the space-like nature of reality.
In space there is not even an atom of movement;
Likewise in the awakening mind there is no movement.
For those who wish to accomplish awakening in the ground of accomplishment,
Universal Light, the Unattached Lotus Eye,
The Vajra Holder, Ghanavyūha,
The Great Assembly of the Wheel, which is Vajradhara,
And Samantabhadra, the indivisibility of cause and result: these six grounds
Are given names as grounds for accomplishing awakening.
Because the nature of mind is free of causes and conditions,
I have taught no result that is accomplished through practice.
What I have taught are the six appearances of the nature of mind.
The six natures of mind are these:
As the sign that self-arisen wisdom is unceasing,
The five doors of the senses are clear throughout,
And this is designated "Universal Light."
Not conceptualizing objects, being without attachment,
Is designated "the Unattached Lotus Eye."
That awakening mind without birth or destruction
I have named "the Vajra Holder."
In one's own nature, the immeasurable nature of reality,
Self-arisen wisdom is arrayed evenly, without concepts;
This is designated "the ground of Ghanavyūha."
That maṇḍala, the nature of the awakening mind:
In all the phenomena that abide within it
Are the grounds of the Great Assembly of the Wheel of Syllables;
Because body, speech and mind have no birth or destruction,
It is explained as "the ground of Vajradhara."
In the awakening mind cause and result are not two;
Because in all phenomena that arise from mind
There is from the very beginning no good or bad, no adopting or rejecting,
I have explained it as "the ground of indivisibility."
Therefore, in the dharmadhātu of the awakening mind,
All the buddhas of the three times and all the sentient beings of the three realms
Arise from it and abide in it;
Hence the dharmadhātu, the awakening mind, is the ground in which all engage.

Thus he spoke.

What is designated "the ground of accomplishing buddhahood"
Comes from that nature of the awakening mind,
And, as explained above, abides in the awakening mind.

Thus he taught the ground in which there is no training.

From The All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, this is the forty-ninth chapter, on there being no training on the grounds of the Great Perfection.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke this teaching on self-arisen wisdom, the awakening mind, being free of obscuration.

O great being, listen!
What is called "wisdom," "primordial knowing,"
Is wisdom that knows from the very beginning.
Self-arisen wisdom knows from the very beginning.
That wisdom which conceptualizes objects
Arises from objects, and so it is not self-arisen;
As soon as there is no object, it does not illuminate.
Therefore that self-arisen wisdom
Is the wisdom that knows from the very beginning,
And that is what is called wisdom.
The wisdom of the teachers of the retinues
Is a wisdom that examines objects.
Whoever conceptualizes objects,
That is the wisdom that cognizes.
That object to be cognized,
Because it arises from the awakening mind,
Is the objectless awakening mind.
Because wisdom arises from itself,
One does not meditate on concentration upon it.
Because one does not meditate on concentration,
The habitual tendencies of concentration do not accumulate.
That nature of reality without habitual tendencies
Is explained as the intent of the buddhas of the three times.
The intent of all the buddhas of the three times
Does not conceptualize objects;
It abides from the very beginning in equality.

O great being, listen!
In that mind, the self-arisen wisdom
Of all the buddhas of the three times,
Because from the very beginning there is no conceptualization,
The buddhas do not contemplate;
They are free of every object of conceptualization.
Whatever person of Utmost Yoga (atiyoga),
Whoever it may be,
If he comes to abide in nonconceptuality,
He holds the intent of the buddhas.

O great being, listen!
Self-arisen wisdom holds the intent of the buddhas.
Because self-arisen wisdom does not conceptualize objects,
It is not stained by the habitual tendencies of conceptual thought.
Action is the habitual tendency of concentration that examines;
It is not taught that there are habitual tendencies in nonconceptuality.
The five self-arisen wisdoms of nonconceptuality,
The nature of mind, are explained as the five doors of the senses.
That self-arisen wisdom has no cessation.
In the manifest appearance of the essence of the awakening mind,
Self-arisen wisdom, unceasing, is distinctly clear.
Since it appears through its own conditions, it is not to be cast aside.
Nonconceptual concentration accumulates no habitual tendencies.
This too is the intent of the buddhas of the three times.
Who abides in this, where nothing is conceptualized?
It abides not in me, nor in the welfare of beings;
This abides in the dharmakāya, the awakening mind,
Where the intent of all the buddhas of the three times is gathered.

O great being, the meaning of these words
Realize directly, in the experience of awareness.
Self-arisen wisdom abides as fivefold toward objects.
The five wisdoms are not the five desirable objects;
The five desirable objects appear within the five wisdoms.
Because those objects are wisdom from the very beginning,
I have not taught objects and wisdom as two.
Therefore objects too are self-arisen wisdom.
Self-arisen wisdom is nothing but one;
Yet the teachers of the retinues who arose from me,
Relying on objects, gave wisdom its names.
The wisdoms that have arisen in various numbers
Come about when one wisdom of analysis,
Toward a single object whose nature appears in distinct ways,
Gives the name of the object to the subject.
That is not definitive; it is imputed by delusion.
It is not my self-arisen wisdom, the all-creator's.
On my single nature, the all-creator's,
Names have been imposed according to the wishes of the retinues.
Some have named it the awakening mind,
Some have named it the dharmadhātu,
Some have named it the element of space,
Some have named it self-arisen wisdom,
Some have named it the dharmakāya,
Some have named it the sambhogakāya,
Some have named it the nirmāṇakāya,
Some have named it body, speech and mind,
Some have named it omniscience,
Some have named it all aspects,
Some have designated it as four wisdoms or as three,
Some have named it as five wisdoms,
And some have named it expanse and wisdom.
All of these are names given to the one self-arisen awakening mind;
They speak of me, the self-arisen, according to what they see.

Thus he spoke.

From The All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, this is the fiftieth chapter, teaching self-arisen wisdom.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke this teaching on there being no traversing of the path of the Great Perfection.

O great being, listen!
I, the All-Creating King, am the awakening mind.
The awakening mind is the great path of all.
The abode, the object and the ground of buddhahood, these three,
Are nowhere else: here they abide by nature.
The dharmadhātu and the nature of reality abide here.
The element of space and the celestial palace abide here.
The ground of buddhahood and the object are the awakening mind.
There is no path elsewhere to traverse, and no place to reach;
By traversing, one does not arrive on the path of space.

O great being, listen!
As for those who declare that there is traversing of a path:
The teachers of the three bodies who arose from me
Taught paths to be traversed, five and three.
Those who wish to arrive by traversing them,
If they wish to attain cause and result,
Are out of accord with the Great Perfection, in which there is no traversing.
On the five, the paths of accumulation, joining, seeing, meditation and the rest,
They gather the two accumulations and conquer the duality of apprehended and apprehender;
But that result, the seeing of reality attained by meditating,
Since it never meets the path in which there is no traversing,
Does not even hear the name of the great bliss beyond effort and accomplishment.
The three paths taught as the secret result,
Suchness, All-Illumination and the rest:
Those who meditate on them to familiarize themselves with them as the path of the three bodies,
Applying to self-arisen phenomena the fourfold approach and accomplishment of cause and result,
By traversing do not arrive at the ground beyond effort and accomplishment.
Because one's own mind, free of elaboration, is the dharmadhātu,
All the buddhas dwell in that freedom from elaboration.
Therefore, do not traverse a path!
One's own mind, unobscured, is clear like space;
In it dwell all the buddhas of the three times.
Therefore, do not traverse a path!
One's own mind, unaltered, is the nature of reality just as it is;
In it the buddhas of the three times are spontaneously present.
Therefore, do not traverse a path!
If, through ignorance, one were to traverse a path,
There would be no time of arriving and no time of attaining realization;
By traversing, there is no arriving at the ground of buddhahood.
The buddha is one's own mind free of conceptualization;
By traversing, one does not arrive at one's own mind free of conceptualization.
By meditating on the view, one meets with an object of concentration;
Through that concentration one is parted from concept-free equality.
By guarding sacred commitment, one abides in holding to its being unbroken;
By holding to its being unbroken, one is parted from concept-free equality.
By seeking enlightened activity, one practices a concentration of accomplishment;
A concentration of accomplishment is parted from nonconceptual equality.
By training on the grounds, one practices a concentration of abiding;
A concentration of mastery is parted from concept-free equality.
By examining the nature of reality, one practices a concentration of clarity;
Through clarity of concentration one is parted from concept-free equality.
One practices a concentration that clearly realizes wisdom;
A concentration of clear realization is parted from nonconceptuality.

O great being, listen!
How could a concentration of desire, however clear,
Accomplish the bliss that is free of desire?
That great bliss, already free of activity,
Is taken up by the nonconceptual, the desireless.
Because one does not practice concentration and accumulates no habitual tendencies,
Self-arisen wisdom does not come under the power of karma.
That self-arisen wisdom, the heart of the dharmakāya,
Is not accomplished by seeking; it is accomplished by abiding in its nature.
It is free of all aims to be accomplished;
What is called an aim is a mere name imputed.
In reality, "buddha" too is a mere name.
To impute "buddha" as a name
Is not definitive scripture; it is taught as provisional scripture.
The teachers of the three bodies who arose from me
Named as existing the Three Jewels, the Dharma, the Buddha and the Saṅgha,
So that they might be practiced in earnest.
To the retinue that arose from me, sentient beings,
They took up "This is bliss, this is happiness, that is suffering,"
And saying, "There is no bliss other than this,"
Through views and conduct of many kinds,
They taught this scripture of accomplishment through practice.
It is not the scripture of me, the effortless all-creator.

Thus he spoke.

From The All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, this is the fifty-first chapter, on there being no traversing of the path.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke this teaching on Effortless Perfection being free of desire.

O great being,
If you wish to accomplish all phenomena,
Do not accomplish the ground of desire!
That mind which desires
Takes up nothing and rejects everything.
Because this has transcended cause and result,
One does not accomplish a cause out of desire for a result;
It is accomplished by abiding in the nonconceptual nature.
Concentration of desire rejects everything;
Freedom from desire takes up everything.
Through concentration one grasps at desire,
But that desire is not genuine;
The genuine does not exist as something to be grasped.
Through all the actions that are done,
One tries to accomplish what is unsought and spontaneously present;
But that which is unsought and spontaneously present
Is not accomplished by doing;
Undone, it is spontaneously present from the very beginning.
Because desire rejects everything whatsoever,
It is a great obstacle to accomplishment.
Through the habitual tendency of that concentration of desire,
And that habitual tendency is the grasping of mind,
Grasping rejects that bliss.
Since non-grasping takes up all bliss,
It is the nature of things just as they are.

That which is just as it is, unfabricated,
Is the single intent of all the buddhas.
Apart from great bliss,
How could there be desire and grasping?
In non-abiding there is no reference point;
In nonconceptuality there is no grasping.
That essence of the meaning, which is nonexistent,
Is attained through the absence of desire.
Whoever has no grasping or attachment to anything,
That is meditative absorption, the supreme of all holding.
Since all is already held, already accomplished from the beginning,
Letting be without conceiving is the king of accomplishment.

O Great Being!
By meditating on variety, the one does not become clear.
Whatever absorption of clarity one performs,
In that too there is no absorption.
What is one in suchness
They divide into two, buddhas and sentient beings;
Having divided bliss and suffering into two,
They teach that bliss is to be embraced,
They teach that evil is to be cast behind:
That contradicts the definitive scripture.

O Great Being!
Do not divide the one into two!
Bliss and suffering are one in awakening;
Buddhas and sentient beings are one as mind;
Appearance and existence, the world and its beings, are one in the nature of reality;
True and false, too, are equal in the nature of reality.
Do not take up bliss, do not reject suffering!
By abiding in the natural state, all is accomplished.
Attachment to bliss is suffering.
Since in that which illuminates all there is no conceptualization,
That is self-arisen wisdom.
Since wisdom does not accumulate habitual tendencies,
How could it be stained by karma?
The wisdom and absorption of cause and result are conceptual;
Conceptual wisdom has habitual tendencies.
Since habitual tendencies hold sway over that wisdom,
Do not meditate on conceptual wisdom.
By meditating on conceptual wisdom,
One becomes separated from nonconceptual equality,
Seized by the sickness of striving in absorption.

O Great Being!
I, the all-creator, am the awakening mind.
Everything is the awakening mind,
And apart from the awakening mind there is nothing at all.
The phenomena emanated from mind are various;
The various emanations that appear as anything whatsoever,
Sentient beings, forms, shapes, and colors,
Each have faith in whatever way things appear;
Teaching the Dharma suited to that faith
Is explained as the emanation of wisdom and compassion.
Yet this too does not stir from the natural state of phenomena.
Because the buddhas do not stir from the natural state of phenomena,
It is the nature of reality of the victors of the three times.
There are no buddhas apart from the nature of reality;
There are no sentient beings apart from the buddhas.
The nonconceptuality of sentient beings is the intent of the buddhas.
Until the awakening mind arose,
Neither buddhas nor sentient beings existed.
Therefore all the buddhas praise mind itself.
Mind itself, the unchanging, is from the very beginning without existence.
Though there is not an atom of stirring by altering the nature of reality,
The teacher who divides the one
Into nine stages and eighty-one thousand does not realize the meaning of the one.
By not realizing the one, one will not realize the meaning of all.
Therefore one fully realizes that meaning, the one mind.

Thus he spoke.

This is the fifty-second chapter of the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, the chapter on being unmoved and free of desire.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke this teaching on the unmoving nature of all phenomena:

O Great Being, now realize this!
Realizing me, realizing phenomena, there is no stirring.
I myself, the All-Creating King, the Teacher,
Am that which does not stir from the awakening mind;
Everything is unmoved from the awakening mind,
And self-arisen wisdom, without cause or condition,
Utterly reigns over all cause and result.
It is utterly secret from those of the vehicles of cause and result.
The unborn vajra reigns in the dharmadhātu;
It is not spoken of to those of cause and result; it is utterly secret.
Unceasing mind itself reigns in the expanse of space;
It is not spoken of to those of cause and result; it is utterly secret.
Appearance and existence, the world and its beings, reign as the unfabricated nature of reality;
It is not spoken of to those of cause and result; it is utterly secret.
The five appearances, space, wind, water, earth, and fire,
Utterly reign as the essence of the awakening mind;
It is not spoken of to those of cause and result; it is utterly secret.
The three realms and the three times reign as compassion and wisdom;
It is not spoken of to those of cause and result; it is utterly secret.

In the all-creator, the intrinsic nature of the awakening mind,
Not vanished, not pacified, the intrinsic nature has no pacification.
The nature of the dharmadhātu, free of elaboration and at peace,
Not vanished, not pacified, by nature has no elaboration.
Self-arisen wisdom, unobscured and luminous,
Like space, not vanished, not pacified, has no pacification.
Appearance and existence, the world and its beings, are the intrinsic nature of reality;
Not vanished, not pacified, the intrinsic nature has no pacification.
All phenomena, not vanished, not pacified,
Are luminous by nature, their essence unmoving.
To accomplish cause and result with regard to unmoving phenomena
Is not my scripture; it is a scripture of desire.
I, being without desire, accomplish all;
Not wishing, without desire, the natural state is accomplished.
I have never spoken of anything called "accomplished";
That belongs to the words of those who wish to accomplish through cause and result.
I am great even in the sounds of superimposition and denial:
Existence and nonexistence, accomplished and unaccomplished,
All superimpositions and denials whatsoever,
Abide, through nonconceptuality, as equality.
Anything other than equality
I, the forefather of all the buddhas, have not spoken.
An intent I have not spoken, no buddha teaches.
All that is spoken speaks my essence.
Essence and intrinsic nature are all phenomena.
That King of Equality, pure by nature,
Unchanging in nature, is free of all attachment;
It has no object to be grasped, and no place for mind either.
Whoever wishes to attain it directly,
That wish is a ground of attachment.
As for the nonconceptual intent of equality regarding it:
The nature of reality abides primordially as its own nature,
And to strive with desire, thinking to seek it,
Is to search for one's own mind elsewhere.
Though such a one should demolish the dharmadhātu and the element of space,
The abodes of the three realms and the phenomena of existence,
He will not find his own mind abiding in its nature.
One's own mind has the nature of space;
Since it reaches no limit, the expanse knows no diminishing.
He does not know that his own mind has the nature of space.
The nature of all phenomena is likewise:
In that which is just as it is, nothing at all is fabricated.
Whatever absorptions of contrived play one performs,
The essence of that which is just as it is does not change.
If it changed, and a single surpassing power grew great,
Then by a single absorption saṃsāra would be left without remainder.
Though one speaks, there is no change in that which is just as it is.
In the domain of the great nature, the nature abides.
"Nature" is a name for the unfabricated,
And the great unfabricated is called "the path."
Between the body, speech, and mind of the buddhas
And the three, body, speech, and mind, of sentient beings,
No difference of good or bad, great or small, has been taught.
They abide in the nonconceptual nature of reality as equality.
Because of equality, there is not a single thing that is Dharma or non-Dharma.
Sentient beings are no cause; buddhas are no result.
To teach cause and result is to err from the Teacher's own scripture.
Therefore, since the phenomena that arise from me are equal,
What arises from me is without good or bad, equal and one.

Thus he spoke.

This is the fifty-third chapter of the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, the chapter on the unmoving nature of all phenomena.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke the following. Its own nature is self-arisen wisdom, and that self-arisen wisdom possesses five essences of meaning. What are they? The meaning that is the heart from which all phenomena arise; the meaning that is the unmistaken, definitive essence; the meaning that is the essence transcending cause and result; the meaning that is the essence free of action; and the meaning that is the essence decisively establishing all phenomena. These are the five. Concerning that self-arisen wisdom, the condensed sūtra scriptures are five, and the differentiated sūtra scriptures are these fifty-seven, which he spoke:

O Great Being, listen to this meaning!
I, the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind,
In the single great self-arisen wisdom,
From the five condensations of self-arisen wisdom,
Have fifty-seven differentiated sūtra scriptures;
They are my fifty-seven essences.
If one asks what their essence is like:
From wisdom's essence, the heart of phenomena,
Ten sūtra scriptures that bring it to manifestation are taught.
From wisdom's unmistaken, definitive essence,
Ten unmistaken, definitive sūtra scriptures are taught.
From wisdom's essence transcending cause and result,
Ten sūtra scriptures transcending cause and result arise.
From wisdom's essence free of action,
Ten sūtra scriptures of Effortless Perfection arise.
From wisdom's essence that decisively establishes,
Ten sūtra scriptures of decisive establishment arise.
From the five aspects of the essence of the wisdom of mind itself,
Five sets of ten sūtra scriptures arose;
They teach the five convictions about the essence of the nature.
The seven are the seven chapters of definitive teaching:
One teaches the ground, one teaches the instruction,
And five teach the five individual purposes.

Teaching the particulars of their meaning:
So that the faculties may see the nature of reality,
Ten sūtra scriptures that bring it to manifestation are taught.
To clear away conceptions of "it is" and "it is not," and to bring conviction,
Ten unmistaken, definitive sūtra scriptures are taught.
Because the self-arisen wisdom of the All-Creating King
Does not depend on causes and conditions,
Ten sūtra scriptures transcending cause and result are taught.
Because the self-arisen wisdom of the All-Creating King
Has no need for doer and deed, for striving and accomplishing,
Ten sūtra scriptures of Effortless Perfection are taught.
Because the self-arisen wisdom of the All-Creating King
Decisively establishes its own nature,
Ten sūtra scriptures of decisive establishment are taught.

In this there are five purposes of meaning.
In the sūtra scriptures that bring the nature of reality to manifestation
Lies the purpose of seeing the nature of reality directly.
Through seeing the nature of reality directly and unmistakenly,
There is no need to travel the path of view, conduct, and sacred commitment.
In the sūtra scriptures teaching the unmistaken and definitive
Lies the purpose of unmistaken, definitive conviction.
Through manifest, unmistaken conviction in mind itself,
There is the purpose of not needing to accomplish buddhahood.
In the sūtra scriptures not dependent on causes and conditions
Lies the quality of not needing to strive and accomplish.
Through not needing to strive and accomplish,
There is the purpose of abiding in effortless bliss.
In teaching the sūtra scriptures of Effortless Perfection
Lies the purpose of the nature being perfect without being made.
Through the purpose of the effortless nature being perfect,
There is the purpose of being spontaneously perfect from the very beginning.
From teaching the sūtra scriptures of decisive establishment,
The buddhas of the three times, the sentient beings of the three realms,
And all that is included in appearance and existence, the world and its beings,
Are decisively established, in harmony with the five sūtra scriptures,
As causeless, definitive, self-arisen, and free of action.

Teaching the root sūtra of decisive establishment:
First, explaining "the buddhas of the three times":
In the nature of reality, whose nature is like space,
Sentient beings view earlier and later, the three times;
For the later there is no recollection, and the habitual tendencies of the earlier have ceased.
Sentient beings, not knowing the space-like nature of reality, see in this way.
The explanation of the word "buddha":
In the nature of reality, the nature of the awakening mind,
Being free of elaboration, it is cleansed of shape and of the domains of the faculties,
And like space it pervades all and unfolds;
For me, "buddha" is no mere name imputed.
Teaching the reason for the term "realm":
Its characteristic is to be many and vast;
Since the faculties of a single sentient being of the three realms
Cannot measure it, it is designated "realm."
In true certainty, it is not designated by any name.
Teaching the explanation of the term "mind":
Self-arisen wisdom, without cause or condition,
Is unceasing and luminous to all in the five doors of the faculties;
This is clearly explained as what is called "mind."
All that is included in appearance and existence, the world and its beings:
That miraculous display appearing from the unborn
Is designated "appearance and existence," since variety exists.
Teaching what is called "the world and its beings":
Within the vessel of the five elements, the six classes of beings are its contents;
Since they abide thus, it is designated "the vessel and its contents."
All the deeds of the all-creator, without exception,
Being condensed, the fifty-seven sūtra scriptures arose
And are condensed into the five natures of the All-Creating King;
It is taught that they are gathered into the five natures of the all-creator:
The nature that brings the heart to manifestation,
The nature that teaches the unmistaken and definitive,
The nature that is self-arisen, transcending cause and result,
The nature transcending doer and deed, striving and accomplishing,
And the nature that decisively establishes in harmony. These are the five.

Thus he spoke.

O Teacher, All-Creating King!
Through my awareness, I, Sattvavajra,
In the single face of the all-creator's self-arisen wisdom,
Have found five excellent purposes:
Since from self-arisen wisdom, the heart of phenomena,
All the phenomena of appearance and existence
Can be brought forth into manifestation from what does not appear,
I am convinced that it is the heart of phenomena.
That essence of the single self-arisen wisdom,
Without the duality of ultimate and relative, true and false,
Teaches the unmistaken, definitive awakening mind;
So, without hesitation or doubt, I am convinced.
That essence of the single self-arisen wisdom
Does not rely on causes and is not accomplished by conditions;
Because its nature depends on nothing else,
I am convinced that it is self-arisen wisdom.
That essence of the single self-arisen wisdom,
Because it is spontaneously present without cause or condition,
Has no need to seek activity, no need for doer and deed;
I am convinced that it is spontaneously present, unsought.
That essence of the single self-arisen wisdom:
Since self-arisen wisdom is unobscured from the very beginning,
All phenomena, however they appear, are luminous;
Because it decisively establishes all phenomena,
I am convinced that it is the Teacher's lamp.

Thus he spoke.

This is the fifty-fourth chapter of the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, the chapter teaching all the sūtra scriptures by condensing them into the very nature of the all-creator.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke this sūtra on all phenomena being nothing other than the awakening mind:

O Great Being, listen!
That all that is included in appearance and existence, the world and its beings,
And all that is expressed in meaning and in words,
Is the awakening mind, is shown as follows:
This dharmadhātu and this element of space
Are the awakening mind, unelaborated and unobscured;
I have never said that they are other than this.
The appearances of space, wind, water, earth, and fire
Are taught as the essence of the awakening mind.
The three realms, the desire realm, the form realm, and the formless realm,
Are the three appearances of the essence of the awakening mind.
The three bodies of the Teacher who teaches it
Are the three aspects of the nature of the awakening mind.
The unceasing wisdom of mind itself appears as the desire realm;
The appearance of the essence of mind itself is the form realm;
The unborn nature of mind is the formless realm.
The unborn nature, free of elaboration, is the dharmakāya;
Mind itself enjoying itself within itself is the sambhogakāya;
Mind emanating from mind, acting for the benefit of minds, is the emanation.
The heat and cold, hunger and thirst, stupidity and dumbness, falling and change
Of the six classes of beings, hell beings and the rest,
Are freed, through nonconceptual equality, into the nature of reality just as it is.
Since self-arisen wisdom does not conceive of objects,
It is free of all karmic habitual tendencies, faults and virtues alike.
Since it neither takes up virtues nor casts off faults,
It does not accomplish bliss, and there is no suffering to abandon.
In what is just as it is there is no stirring from the very beginning.
There is no phenomenon that changes by altering suchness,
And no phenomenon to be attained by grasping with desire.
The characteristic of phenomena arising as their nature
Is like self-arisen wisdom arising from mind.
"Like" is a name imputed; it is self-arisen wisdom.
In self-arisen wisdom, the single domain of the nature of reality,
Seeing other phenomena, one abandons that wisdom,
And one will never meet that self-arisen wisdom.
To those whose objects are things to abandon and prevent, its name does not resound.
Though one purifies through the two truths, ultimate and relative,
One is held back for three eons.
Entering the door of purifying the threefold purity and of signlessness,
Entering the door of all the limbs of awakening,
Through stages of absorption with complete rituals,
One abandons self-arisen wisdom for seven lifetimes.
Entering the door of the nonduality of method and wisdom,
Unborn wisdom being set in motion by method,
Meditating oneself on appearances as without intrinsic nature,
If one strives intensely in the five rites, the ancillary implements,
And the way of the sacred commitments,
Then on the vidyādhara level, for one thousand six hundred years,
One practices undistracted absorption, longing for the sambhogakāya.
Having attained the sambhogakāya, one looks to seek acceptance of one's own mind;
In one thousand years one finds the acceptance of nonconceptual equality.

O Great Being, Vajra, you yourself as well,
Unmistaken in my scripture, teach me!
O, the understanding of sentient beings, gods and humans, is coarse;
By their nature distracted by the objects of the faculties,
Sentient beings do not know, and are satisfied with whatever is taught;
Without teaching them the great unmistaken, definitive scripture,
Cause and result, the provisional scripture, ultimate and relative, are taught.
Their understanding satisfied by the two, true and false,
For long eons they dwell in the two, true and false.
Therefore first teach one's own mind unmistakenly.
Through this teaching they meet the unmistaken path that is not sought,
And from this very moment they abide on the effortless ground.
They will meet the path that cannot be sought;
They will meet absorption that need not be meditated;
Through this they attain abiding in effortless bliss.
Since they attain their own mind unmistakenly and definitively,
The qualities of what is unsought and spontaneously present are these:
No need to generate the mind of awakening, no need to travel the path,
No training on the grounds, no need to meditate on the view,
No need to strive to keep vows and moral discipline.
O Great Being, teach the scripture!
As for practicing these worldly dharmas of signs:
As the action of body, one does not meditate on deities with doer and deed;
As the action of speech, one does not recite mantras and words;
As the action of mind, one does not meditate on clear absorption.

Thus he spoke.

This is the fifty-fifth chapter of the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, the chapter on all phenomena not existing apart from the awakening mind.

Then Sattvavajra proclaimed this, announcing to his whole retinue that the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, is the essence and the maker of all phenomena, and offering his realization:

O Teacher of teachers, All-Creating King!
This is my realization of your nature:
All phenomena are your single nature.
The nature of reality, that palace of the dharmadhātu,
Is renowned as the All-Creating King.
This celestial mansion of the element of space
Is renowned as the All-Creating King.
The Teacher who is the unborn dharmakāya
Is renowned as the All-Creating King.
This sambhogakāya enjoying the objects of desire
Is renowned as the All-Creating King.
This nirmāṇakāya of compassion and wisdom
Is renowned as the All-Creating King.
This realm of attachment, the desire realm,
Is renowned as the All-Creating King.
This realm of pride, the form realm,
Is renowned as the All-Creating King.
This mental body of the formless realm
Is renowned as the All-Creating King.
That which abides in the buddhas of former ages, now passed,
Is renowned as the All-Creating King.
The buddhas now present, acting for the benefit of beings,
Are renowned as the All-Creating King.
Those yet to come, arising from it,
Are renowned as the All-Creating King.
The view of the four yogas
Is renowned as the All-Creating King.
The five elements that comprise appearance and existence, the world and its beings,
Are renowned as the All-Creating King.
Apart from the All-Creating King,
In buddhas, sentient beings, appearance and existence, the world and its beings,
And all that is gathered under the name of phenomena,
Nothing appears other than the All-Creating King.

Thus Sattvavajra proclaimed.

This is the fifty-sixth chapter of the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, the chapter of Sattvavajra's proclamation through realizing the nature of the all-creator.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke this chapter on entrusting the teaching to Sattvavajra.

O Sattvavajra, hold this!
You are self-arisen wisdom;
You are the heart that arises from me.
Hold this nature of mine!
The Teachers, the three bodies,
Who arose from my threefold nature:
You hold their nature.
Their teachings, three in kind,
Lion of Speech, proclaim them!
And time, place, retinue, and the rest,
Explain them as made by me myself!
As my nature, the all-creator's,
Or as the explanation of my name, the all-creator,
You hold my nature:
I am the nature of all.
Since I am the forefather of the buddhas,
Be the forefather of all the buddhas!
I myself, the All-Creating King,
Am father of the Teachers, the three bodies;
You, be father and mother of the three bodies,
Be forefather of the buddhas of the three times,
And great forefather of all sentient beings!
Lion of Speech, when you speak,
Do not say that all phenomena, however they appear,
Are other than you yourself, the Great Being,
And me, the All-Creating King.
Within the teaching of the three bodies,
Of the two, cause and result,
One practices the cause and longs for the result;
Labeling sentient beings and buddhas,
One strives with the mind for buddhahood as a result.
This is not my teaching.
My nature, the all-creator,
Transcends the two, cause and result.
Since sentient beings and buddhas are not two,
Do not strive for buddhahood with the mind!
I myself, the All-Creating King,
Am mind itself, self-arisen wisdom.

Thus he spoke.

This is the fifty-seventh chapter of the Sūtra of the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, the chapter on entrusting the teaching.

The All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, is complete.

It was translated, checked, and finalized by the Indian scholar Śrī Siṃhaprabha and the Tibetan translator Pagor Vairocana.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, having realized self-arisen wisdom as unborn, remained without speaking at all. Then the holy Sattvavajra made this request:

O Teacher of teachers, All-Creating King!
Now that the well-spoken sūtras have been brought to completion,
How do you yourself realize the three Teachers?
How do you yourself realize the three teachings?
How do you realize the nature of retinue, place, and time?
The meaning of the intent with which you yourself realize,
Please teach it well to us!

Thus he requested.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke this subsequent tantra called The Explanation of Realization:

O Sattvavajra, listen well to this meaning!
What the Teachers, the three bodies who arose from me,
The Teacher of teachers, the All-Creating King,
Spoke in order to train beings each in their own way:
That is realizing each Teacher, place, and retinue,
And each teaching and compilation, as abiding separately;
It is not realizing my nature, the all-creator.
O, to show the way I, the all-creator, realize:
I myself am of unborn nature;
All phenomena that arise from it, every one,
Are realized as the self-arisen, unborn expanse.
O Great Being, listen well now!
Since I realize myself as unborn,
I realize the three Teachers too as unborn,
I realize the three teachings too as unborn,
I realize retinue, place, and time too as unborn,
I realize the buddhas of the past as unborn,
The buddhas who have now appeared and are present
I fully realize as unborn,
And the buddhas not yet come, who will arise later,
I fully realize as unborn.
O, just as the Teacher, the all-creator himself, realizes,
Sattvavajra, you too must realize!
If Sattvavajra does not realize in this way,
He will not realize my nature, the all-creator.
If you do not realize my nature,
You will not realize the ten natures of the heart.
Deluded about the meaning of the ten natures,
How could one realize the ten scriptures on what is not to be meditated?
O, just as I have taught phenomena,
Sattvavajra, you must realize them!
Just as you realize my nature as unborn,
Realize the nature of all as unborn!
The three Teachers too are realized as unborn;
The three teachings too are realized as unborn;
Retinue, place, and time too are realized as unborn;
The buddhas of the three times are realized as unborn;
The sentient beings of the six classes are realized as unborn;
The five, earth, water, fire, wind, and space,
Are fully realized in the state of the unborn;
The three realms and the three existences are realized as unborn;
The five desirables and the five objects are realized as unborn.
Therefore all phenomena are the awakening mind itself;
The awakening mind is the All-Creating King himself;
The All-Creating King is realized as unborn.
The root of all phenomena is I myself, the all-creator;
Since I myself am realized as the unborn expanse,
All too will be realized as the unborn expanse.

Thus he spoke.

This is the fifty-eighth chapter of the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, the chapter on the setting of realization.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke this realization that the view is not to be meditated upon:

O Sattvavajra, now realize this well!
In the all-creator, the root of all phenomena,
There is no viewing and nothing to be viewed.
Whoever realizes the heart in which there is nothing to view
Realizes my nature, the all-creator.
O, all phenomena however they appear, without exception,
All abide in the all-creator as unborn.
Whoever wishes to realize the meaning of the heart,
Let him realize my nature, the all-creator.
O, whoever, without realizing my nature, the all-creator,
Is content with the scriptures taught separately
And engages in the meaning of the Teachers, the three bodies,
Utterly contradicts my meaning, the all-creator.
O, apart from me myself, the Teacher, the all-creator,
This unborn omniscience, the nature of the view,
The buddhas of the past, the present, and the future
Have not realized and do not realize, and do not declare it realized.
Even the unborn buddhas, who have not arisen,
Having met my scripture, the all-creator's,
They too fully realize the unborn view,
And are blessed as the essence of all the buddhas.
O, were there no scripture of the Teacher, the All-Creating King,
The buddhas of the three times, the sentient beings of the three realms,
And the yogis who view the unborn path,
Though they cross nine passes and nine valleys by way of the ten grounds,
Since they conceptually analyze the meaning of their own respective things,
They are obscured with regard to the meaning of the unborn heart;
This heart of awakening, free of cause and result,
This meaning that is the view of all, would not be realized.
O, therefore I too, the Teacher, the all-creator,
Declare to Sattvavajra, first of the retinue:
The scriptures explained by the three Teachers, the three bodies,
Do not abide in them!
If this definitive scripture were not taught in this way,
The lineage of me, the All-Creating King,
Great forefather of all the victors of the three times, would be cut off,
And all would be bent upon the path of saṃsāra.
O, therefore, Sattvavajra, you too:
In what transcends the domain of viewing and not viewing,
Since conceptual thoughts proliferate about whatever meaning is viewed,
The view of those who hold to cause and result is not your realization.
In the vehicle of Utmost Yoga, the non-abiding,
Since the meaning seen by looking and examining comes to an end,
In the unfabricated heart, the nature of the all-creator,
It is realized as the meaning in which looking sees nothing.
O, I, the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind,
Am the mirror in which all phenomena are viewed:
Since all arise clear and without intrinsic nature,
I declare the unviewed nature to be clear.
In the very domain of unborn suchness,
Where there is no object to view, it is realized as the great seeing.

Thus he spoke.

This is the fifty-ninth chapter of the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, the chapter on the unborn.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke this realization that sacred commitments are not to be guarded:

O, as for this Teacher of teachers, the All-Creating King,
The Teachers, the three bodies, who arose from me,
Giving explanations in stages according to the differences of faculties,
Realize the all-creator as something that has commitments to guard.
Some realize it as the purity of vows and of the discipline of the Vinaya;
Some realize it as the purity of the vows of the vidyādharas;
Some realize it as the enumerated root and branch commitments.
However they examine it, they have not declared it to be realization.
This sacred commitment of the All-Creating King
Is taught as free of the partiality of guarding and not guarding.
Whoever realizes that there is neither guarding nor not guarding
Realizes my sacred commitment, the all-creator's.
O, I, the Teacher of teachers, the All-Creating King:
Since all are one in the root, the awakening mind,
And all are therefore the sacred commitment of the heart,
It is realized as transcending the meaning of guarding and not guarding.
O, the sacred commitment of the view of the All-Creating King,
Being, like unborn space, beyond all direction,
Whoever realizes these things well
Realizes the sacred commitment of the All-Creating King.
O, I, the Teacher of teachers, the All-Creating King,
Have declared sacred commitment to be not something to guard;
Whatever has been heard from the root tantra of the sacred commitments,
By this, that meaning is well realized.
O, just as the All-Creating King declared before,
Because the nature of reality of the three times is unchanging,
My sacred commitment, the All-Creating King's,
Is realized as not to be guarded in the three times.
O, just as phenomena are one in their root, the mind,
The root sacred commitment is one in having nothing to guard.
That too is realizing one's own mind as unborn.
Openness, nonexistence, singularity,
And spontaneous presence: these transcend all guarding.
They are the enumerations of the sacred commitment of the All-Creating King,
The enumerations of realizing the unborn.
In the single meaning, the heart, which has nothing to guard,
With regard to the difference between realizing and not realizing,
It was divided into two, provisional and definitive,
And declared as having something to guard and having nothing to guard.
O, through this, since sacred commitment is realized as without guarding,
The intent of the victors of the three times is realized.
The yogi who does not realize this
Utterly contradicts the meaning of the heart, his own mind.
O, those who transgress my sacred commitment, the all-creator's,
And abide in the commitments of the Teachers, the three bodies,
Even over many hundreds of eons
Will find it extremely hard to meet the meaning of the heart.
Therefore, having received my scripture, the all-creator's,
Realize sacred commitment as not to be guarded!

Thus he spoke.

This is the sixtieth chapter of the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, the chapter on sacred commitment without guarding.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke this teaching that activity is not to be sought:

O Sattvavajra, understand this well!
From the intrinsic nature of me, the All-Creating King, Teacher of teachers,
Arose the Teachers, the three bodies,
Who appeared in bodies to tame each being in whatever way suits,
And hold that by seeking activity one meets the meaning.
They hold that the action of the body is forming mudrās;
They hold that speech performs mantra and recitation;
They hold that the mind cultivates samādhi of emanation and absorption.
Abiding with the three doors scattered, they do not realize accomplishment.

O, as for the activity of me, the Teacher, the all-creator,
Since I have declared an activity that needs no seeking,
Which does not contradict the meaning and brings intention to its culmination,
You should realize that activity is not to be sought.

O, as for the activity of me, the Teacher, the all-creator,
Realize that one's own aim is spontaneously accomplished, so there is nothing to do;
Realize that the aim of others is one in the expanse, so there is nothing to seek.
In this way you must realize it with certainty.

O, since the all-creator has made all things as the awakening mind,
Realize that the awakening mind needs nothing to be done.
For the essence, awakening, unmade and free of action,
By what could the conventions of doing and not doing be imputed?

O, as for the activity of me, the All-Creating King,
Realize that, as the action of the body, there are no mudrās to form;
Realize that for speech there is no recitation to perform, as the essence;
Realize that the mind is free of meditating on emanation and absorption.
Those who have passed, those yet to come, and those present now
Attained manifest buddhahood by realizing activity without action.
Since the victors of the three times depend on realization,
Sattvavajra, having heard this, realize it!

O, the Teachers, the three bodies, who arose from me,
Declared the activities of pacifying, enriching, magnetizing and wrathful action;
For those who delight in conceptual signs and enumerations,
The conceptions that tame them were taught as enumerations.

O, therefore, what I, the all-creator, have declared,
Examine without error the meaning of what you hear.
Through activity free of all effort and striving,
All aims are complete without being made, and so realization dawns.

O Sattvavajra, understand this meaning well!
Through realizing the meaning of the essence without effort and striving,
The meaning is complete without doing or not doing;
So realize this activity of the All-Creating King!

O, I, the king of Teachers, the all-creator,
Taught all that is done as the path of signs.
By realizing this definitive meaning, activity free of action,
Beings become the All-Creating King himself.
The essence free of action, the three doors unmistaken:
Realization of the meaning beyond effort and striving,
Because it is the single intent of the victors of the three times,
Is realization itself, supreme on the unmistaken path.

Thus he spoke.

This is the sixty-first chapter of the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, the chapter on realizing that the three doors have nothing to do.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke this teaching on realizing that the maṇḍala is not to be generated:

O Sattvavajra, now realize this meaning!
The Teachers, the three bodies, who arose from me,
Taught disciples who delight in signs and enumerations
That there are accumulations and maṇḍalas to generate.
Whoever, relying on the path of the three bodies,
Conceptualizes by way of signs and enumerations
Will not, for as long as hundreds of koṭis of eons,
Realize the meaning that the maṇḍala is not to be generated.

O, therefore, as I, the Teacher, the All-Creating King, teach,
In the nature of reality, accomplished as an unmade intrinsic nature,
Where accumulations and maṇḍala are spontaneously perfect by their intrinsic nature,
One realizes the meaning that there is nothing to generate or accumulate.

O, the maṇḍala that I, the all-creator, teach
Is the scripture of the maṇḍala perfected, arising from oneself.
Since all without exception is perfected in the essence, there is no adding or subtracting.
Realize the maṇḍala as ungenerated and perfected all at once.

O, its center is the essence of the unmistaken meaning;
Its surrounding circle holds saṃsāra and nirvāṇa perfected as great bliss.
That is the root of every maṇḍala essence;
Realize that all maṇḍalas without exception are gathered in this.

O, the maṇḍala of me, the All-Creating King,
Is the maṇḍala in which all without exception is perfected in the essence.
Whoever realizes those as perfected
Is skilled in the meaning that the maṇḍala has no generation.

O, I, the Teacher of teachers, the All-Creating King,
Through the maṇḍala of the unborn awakening mind,
Spread it out, all-pervading, without going or coming.
By realizing it, one enters the meaning of the unborn.

O, the maṇḍala of the five self-arisen wisdoms,
Because it depends on whether its meaning is realized or not,
Is seen as two: to be generated and not to be generated.
When the sublime is realized, there is neither generating nor not generating.
Therefore, Sattvavajra, you too
Should not dwell on the words, but realize the meaning itself.
Whoever has realization of this born in them
At that very moment comes to possess the meaning of the All-Creating King.

O, this maṇḍala, the essence where all victors without exception are gathered,
Abiding as an all-pervading intrinsic nature:
In this meaning, the unborn intrinsic nature that abides,
Take up realization at ease, and do not strive!

O, since I, the all-creator, the intrinsic nature of all victors,
Have realized the meaning that the maṇḍala is not to be generated,
Sattvavajra, you should realize its intrinsic nature
As something other than words that are heard.

O, just as the All-Creating King has taught
The five kinds of attachment and aversion as the unborn awakening mind,
Sattvavajra, once you yourself have realized it,
Teach it in the same way to the retinue!

Thus he spoke.

This is the sixty-second chapter of the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, the chapter on realizing that the maṇḍala is not to be generated.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke this teaching on realizing that empowerment is not to be conferred:

O Sattvavajra, realize this meaning with certainty!
I am the Teacher, the All-Creating King.
The Teachers, the three bodies, who arose from me,
Gave the scriptural teaching that empowerment has a method of conferral;
Because they cling attachedly to the path of conceptual signs,
They will go for eons without seeing my intrinsic nature.

O, I am the Teacher, the All-Creating King.
Since my intrinsic nature is realized as the all-creator, all-pervading,
And since in me there is no meaning of a second,
Realize it as free of the meaning of conferring and not conferring.
To persons who delight in signs and enumerations
The number of empowerments and the stages of conferral were taught;
To persons who abide in the unfabricated meaning
The stage of the intrinsic nature that abides unconferred was taught.

O, since the intrinsic nature of the All-Creating King is all-pervading,
To yogins who engage and realize in this way,
Since they realize the meaning of the intrinsic nature that abides unconferred,
Declare that in empowerment there is no conferring and no obtaining!

O, since I, the Teacher of teachers, the All-Creating King,
Have declared, having realized that there is no conferring and obtaining,
Once one has obtained the empowerment of the unborn, utterly pure mind,
One will not strive for the empowerment of the ten natures.
One places no hope in the empowerments of mantra and mudrā;
The mind does not cling to empowerments of substances and signs;
Since one's own continuum is pure, one does not meditate on deities of signs;
There is no sequence of planets, lunar mansions and seasonal junctures.

O, as I, the king of Teachers, have realized,
Since the three Teachers of the three bodies have not realized it,
And since they are obscured with regard to the meaning of the ten natures,
They have no realization of my intrinsic nature, the all-creator's.

O, I, the Teacher of teachers, the All-Creating King,
To the Teachers of the three bodies, first of the retinue,
Gave the scriptural teaching of the unfabricated intrinsic nature just as it is:
Do not rely on the worldly deities of signs;
Do not rely on the accumulations, do not depend on empowerment;
Since all is perfected in the empowerment of unborn awareness,
By resting at ease without seeking, every aim is perfected.

O, I am the Teacher, the All-Creating King.
The Teachers of the three bodies were arrayed by me;
The treatises they taught were also arrayed by me.
There is no need for oneself to set oneself upon oneself.
O, thus, whoever realizes the intrinsic nature of the all-creator
Realizes that all things are that intrinsic nature of the all-creator.
Realize the intrinsic nature of me, the all-creator,
And do not rely on empowerment that clings to grasping at signs!

Thus he spoke.

This is the sixty-third chapter of the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, the chapter on the empowerment of self-arisen realization.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke this teaching on realizing that the path is not to be traversed:

O, in the intrinsic nature of the Teacher of teachers, the All-Creating King,
Which abides as one, unborn,
Because the unborn is hard to realize directly,
The Teachers, the three bodies, first of the retinue,
In order to lead each onto the path that delights them,
Taught that there are distinctions of paths to be traversed.
In order to lead those of small intelligence to the meaning by stages,
I understand this as scripture of provisional meaning.

O, the path of accumulation, the path of preparation, the path of seeing, the path of meditation,
And the stages of culmination, over hundreds of eons,
Or three or seven lifetimes and so on, are held to be accomplished in such times;
By that, this path of mine will not be realized.

O, the path that I, the All-Creating King, explain,
In my own intrinsic nature, which is unborn,
Utterly transcends the words "entering" and "not entering."
Therefore realize that the path is not to be traversed.
What appears as five paths is an enumeration of conceptual signs;
Since I myself transcend the domain of signs,
Realize that I am not to be traversed by a path of signs.

O, in the path of me, the all-creator, there are no stages,
Because it is one in the great unborn sphere;
Because it is realization of the intrinsic nature of the one,
It is realized as one, with no traversing of the path.

O, I, the Teacher of teachers, the All-Creating King,
Have no path of stages for entering me,
Because self-arisen wisdom is perfected all at once;
By resting in the untraversed intrinsic nature, one arrives.
Therefore, Sattvavajra, you yourself
Do not traverse toward me in stages!

O, I am the intrinsic nature of space, free of limits.
Like traveling to the edge of space and collapsing in exhaustion,
Wishing to traverse my intrinsic nature by a path:
Among exhaustions there is none greater than that.
Therefore, Sattvavajra, once you have realized this,
Declare it within the retinue!
Since I have now declared my intrinsic nature,
All will realize my intrinsic nature itself.
They will not cling to the worldly path of signs;
They will not dispute on the path of enumerations and words.
Realizing by resting unfabricated and at ease, they reach the far shore;
They will meet the path that is the essence of all.

O, I am the all-creator, the ground of phenomena.
The bodies and speech that teach five paths and ten grounds
Also directly show my intrinsic nature.
Thus you should realize it well.
The unfabricated, untraversed path of all, the all-creator:
Whoever traverses it will not realize the meaning of awakening,
Will not realize the intrinsic nature of me, the all-creator.
Therefore realize the meaning that there is nothing to traverse.

Thus he spoke.

This is the sixty-fourth chapter of the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, the chapter on realizing that the path is not to be traversed.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke this teaching on realizing that the grounds are not to be trained in:

O Sattvavajra, realize this meaning well!
What the Teacher of teachers, the All-Creating King, has taught,
That the grounds are not to be trained in,
Is not the domain of all; it is extremely hard to examine.

O, the Teachers, the three bodies, who arose from me,
In order that the inferior stages be traversed and trained in,
Taught to persons who delight in conceptual enumerations
The enumerations of the grounds as ten and as six.
As it was taught, so the retinue understood it;
It is nothing but appearance in the enumerations of the conceptual intellect.

O, the intrinsic nature of the enumeration of six grounds was taught as
Universal Light, the Vajra Holder,
Vajradhara, Ghanavyūha,
Lotus Eye, and the ground of Vajrasattva.
They were spoken for the sake of yogins who traverse in stages,
And for each a distinct deity and samādhi was spoken.

O, as the Teacher, the All-Creating King, has taught,
All phenomena, however they appear, without exception,
Are one in the unborn dharmadhātu;
Therefore realize them as one, without distinctions of grounds.
Even if, applying the path to me, the all-creator,
One were to obtain every enumeration of conceptual grounds,
Since these are only the appearances of each one's own conceptions,
It is not declared that one meets me, the all-creator.

O, the intrinsic nature of the ground of the All-Creating King
Is the ground spontaneously perfected by awareness, unfabricated and untrained.
Though one trains and traverses, one does not meet the meaning;
By realizing the intrinsic nature, it is perfected all at once.

O, the intrinsic nature realized through the single vehicle,
The all-creator, the awakening mind, unborn and all-pervading:
To traverse it in any way is like searching for the edge of space;
It is taught that by resting in the untraversed intrinsic nature one is liberated.

O, I, the Teacher of teachers, the All-Creating King,
Since I myself have dawned as the very essence of the grounds,
In my ground there are no stages of high and low.
Realize this, as I, the all-creator, teach!
Though I myself appear manifestly before all,
The retinue of the three bodies understand me as conceptual enumerations.
The profound intent of the All-Creating King, too,
However they look at it, is not declared to accord with their view.

O, the three bodies, first of my retinue,
Taught the Dharma to each retinue according to its inclination:
A vehicle each of body, speech and mind,
And a ground each, a path each, a result each, they taught.
If one does not realize the grounds and paths of me, the all-creator,
Through those enumerated grounds one will not find the definitive path.
Therefore, with the mind that is mind itself, the vehicle of all,
Realize it firmly in the root ground.
If one realizes this intrinsic nature of the grounds, without appearance,
One abides in the ground of the all-creator, the awakening mind.

Thus he spoke.

This is the sixty-fifth chapter of the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, the chapter on realizing that the grounds are not to be trained in.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke this teaching on realizing conduct without accepting or rejecting:

O, I am the Teacher, the All-Creating King.
Of the Teachers, the three bodies, who arose from me,
Some hold that virtue is to be practiced and evil abandoned;
Some hold that the pure and the impure are two, to be accepted and rejected;
Some hold that both the pure and the impure are to be enjoyed.
They do not realize the meaning in which there is neither practicing nor not practicing.
Those who abide in such conduct,
Through cultivating the realization of their respective antidotes,
Will realize a focal object trained in the mind,
But will not realize conduct without accepting or rejecting.

O, the conduct taught by the All-Creating King is this:
Virtue and evil, accepting and rejecting, pure and impure, great and small,
Being all one in the unborn awakening mind,
Are realized as the meaning in which there is no accepting or rejecting.
Realize them as the very absence of pure and impure;
Realize that practicing and not practicing are not two;
Realize that there is no dividing into extremes and middle;
Realize the ground and root as the unborn awakening mind.

O, the conduct taught by the All-Creating King is this:
However one acts, there is no contradiction in that,
Because both what is done and what is not done are unborn;
Through knowing this, however one acts is the meaning of the unborn.

O, the conduct of me, the All-Creating King,
Abiding like space, has no number or measure to grasp;
Being nondual, it transcends the sides of is and is not.
Realize it as the conduct of the all-creator, the awakening mind.
Realize the five sense pleasures, too, as the conduct of mind;
Realize the five kinds of attachment and aversion, too, as the conduct of mind;
Realize the five causes of ornament as the conduct of mind;
Realize the three realms and three existences as the conduct of mind.
Conduct that does not realize the unborn
Was not taught by me, the forefather of all victors.

O, therefore, Sattvavajra, you
Realize this conduct of me, the all-creator,
And so that all may realize it as without accepting or rejecting,
Declare it within my retinue!
Without realizing the conduct of me, the all-creator,
Whoever clings to the retinue of the Teachers of the three bodies
Strays into the mistaken view and conduct of the world
And does not realize conduct without accepting or rejecting.

O, the scripture on conduct that the Teacher of teachers, the All-Creating King,
Declared within the retinue
Declares that through unattached conduct without accepting or rejecting
All things are realized as unborn and equal.
Since Sattvavajra declares it to the retinue,
The retinue too will realize it as without accepting or rejecting.
All phenomena of saṃsāra and nirvāṇa
Are unborn, like the intrinsic nature of space;
Just as you and I have realized,
You must declare it within the retinue!
Relying on hearing what was declared before,
Through realizing it they will abide in the all-creator itself.

Thus he spoke.

This is the sixty-sixth chapter of the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, the chapter on realizing conduct without accepting or rejecting.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke this teaching on realizing that wisdom has no obscuration in itself:

O, because I, the Teacher of teachers, the All-Creating King,
Taught as means to tame each being in whatever way suits,
The Teachers, the three bodies, who arose from me,
Hold that obscurations are to be removed and wisdom accomplished.
The Teachers of the three bodies taught the retinue in that way,
And the retinue understood and realized it just so;
Because they cling to the meaning that each one favors,
They do not gather into the retinue of me, the all-creator.

O, the scriptural teaching I, the all-creator, gave the retinue:
Because all phenomena that abide as appearance and sound
Are all one in unborn suchness,
They do not abide as phenomena that obscure or do not obscure.
Therefore I do not divide wisdom into obscuration;
I do not view things partially, as phenomena that obscure or do not obscure;
I do not count numbers in the meaning that has no final end;
I do not produce the meaning of the essence through cause and result.

O, since the word of me, the Teacher, the All-Creating King,
Does not accord with the scriptures of the Teachers of the three bodies,
I do not count enumerations of signs in the unborn one;
I do not look at objects and consciousness, nor rely on hosts of antidotes.
Since awareness and ignorance are one in the unborn expanse,
I do not impose on the one enumerations of signs that grasp at two;
I do not place a nonconceptual perception within.
It is not placed by that, because all is known at once.
Thus, Sattvavajra, realize it!

O, wisdom itself, of me, the Teacher, the all-creator,
Cannot be counted; if counted, it goes beyond all limit.
Since numbers of wisdoms were taught for each greatness,
It is declared that by speaking of them they are never exhausted.

O, the wisdom of me, the All-Creating King,
Is nonconceptual, free of all elaboration,
Utterly peaceful, with nothing to tame, abiding like space;
It is taught as what is called "the unborn."
Though it never stirs from the one self-arisen wisdom,
Its distinct aspects shine clearly, each one, unfabricated.
Whoever thus knows and realizes it
Is declared a son of the All-Creating King.
Just as wisdom is realized as unborn,
So, Sattvavajra, you should realize it.

O, I am wisdom, the All-Creating King.
I dispel the view of ignorance;
I cut through the whole net of obscurations;
I make the light of wisdom spread far and wide.
I, the all-creator, am self-arisen wisdom;
Since there is not a single wisdom that has not arisen from me,
And since I have arrayed all wisdoms without exception,
I am explained as the one called the King of Wisdom.
If you wish to make the afflictions clear as wisdom,
Realize the scripture of me, the all-creator.

Thus he spoke.

This is the sixty-seventh chapter of the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, the chapter on realizing that wisdom has no obscuration.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke this teaching on realizing that spontaneous presence is not to be sought:

O, I am the Teacher, the All-Creating King.
I am spontaneous presence that is not to be sought.
I shall teach my intrinsic nature to you;
Realize my meaning well!

O, I am the Teacher, the All-Creating King.
The Teachers, the three bodies, who arose from me,
Because my intrinsic nature is extremely hard to realize,
Taught that it is to be sought by their individual means.
If one asks how exactly they taught it:
Some, having entered the path of the Four Truths,
Were taught to strive at abandoning afflictions and adopting wisdom.
Some, clinging to the path of dependent origination,
Were taught to strive at refuting afflictions and establishing wisdom.
Some, having entered the path of the two truths,
Were taught that by training and striving the result is attained.
Some, by the striving of acts of apprehended and apprehender,
Hold that the accomplished state abides as clear appearance as a deity.
Some, by striving at ritual action and samādhi,
Hold that the result is attained by way of accepting and rejecting.
Some, by striving at the three kinds of samādhi,
Hold that by accepting and rejecting the result is attained.
Some, by striving at the samādhi of emanation and absorption,
Hold that a result without accepting or rejecting is attained.
Some, by striving at the samādhi of cause and result,
Attain the result of the body and speech of Vajrasattva.
O, through these results of seeking and accomplishing,
The intrinsic nature of me, the all-creator, will not be realized.

O, the intrinsic nature of me, the All-Creating King,
Though it pervades all, is unrealized and supremely secret.
Because my intrinsic nature is unseen and secret,
Those of the vehicles of striving and accomplishing denigrate it,
And so, for a long time, koṭis of niyutas of eons,
They are born in the lowest states of the lower realms,
Bereft of happiness, with incomplete faculties,
Bereft of the meaning of the essence, they pass the eons.

O, I am the Teacher, the all-creator, the awakening mind.
Because I am all-pervading like space,
Connected with all, accomplishing the aims of all,
I do not declare that there is seeking and accomplishing.
Through teaching you this meaning of mine,
Realize with certainty that there is nothing to seek or accomplish.
What Sattvavajra has realized,
You must declare to my retinue!

O, because I am spontaneously present without being sought,
Since I have declared to you just what I realized,
You will realize all phenomena as the father.
Then, having found relief without striving or accomplishing,
You are declared to be the All-Creating King himself.
Therefore, holy Sattvavajra,
To persons who have trained for eons in the unfabricated,
In order to set them on the ground without effort or striving,
Declare the realization that there is nothing to seek or accomplish!

Thus he spoke.

This is the sixty-eighth chapter of the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, the chapter on realizing that spontaneous presence is not to be sought.

Then Sattvavajra proclaimed this to the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind:

O, you are the All-Creating King!
All phenomena, however they appear,
You, the king who creates, have made.
Though you have made all, without exception,
For you, the king who creates, yourself,
There is nothing to be done: you are accomplished from the very beginning.
The buddhas of the three times you have made;
The sentient beings of the three realms you have made;
The six classes and five paths you have made;
The four modes of birth and five destinies you have made.
The three Teachers, too, you have arrayed;
The three teachings, too, you have arrayed;
The three compilers, too, you have arrayed;
The retinues of the three times, too, you have arrayed.
The five self-arisen ones, too, arose from you;
The five causes of ornament you have arrayed;
The ten natures, too, you have taught.
All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind,
You are the essence of all sūtras and scriptures,
The mirror of the teaching, the lamp that dispels darkness,
You who dispel the darkness of the retinue.
You are the forefather of all victors,
Self-arisen, supreme means, the father of all,
The source, wisdom, the mother of all.
Without elaboration, and without even peace,
Beyond speech, and without any core,
Pure like space, beyond the domain of experience,
The object that all are to realize,
Beyond superimposition and denial, the dharmadhātu,
Beyond beginning, end and middle,
Possessing the essence, the source of the wheel,
Like the heart of a precious lotus,
A body that captivates the mind, of which one never tires,
The ornament of the essence of the vehicles.
In your body, speech and mind there are no marks.
Since I have realized the meaning that is hard to realize,
Great forefather of all victors,
All-Creating King, I bow to you!

Thus he praised and proclaimed.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, made this proclamation to Sattvavajra:

How wonderful! Body of Sattvavajra,
You have gained mastery of the secret treasury
Of the body, speech and mind of all the victors,
Unsurpassed Lion of Speech!
Mind that realizes the holy secret,
Speech that proclaims the ocean of secrets,
Body perfect with spontaneously present qualities,
You are the son of the victors.
Like me, the All-Creating King,
Be the forefather of all victors!
Be the dispeller of darkness for the realms of the world!
Be the guru of yogins!
This is the king of all secrets:
This secret, the later teaching,
Declare it to the yogins!

Thus he spoke.

This is the sixty-ninth chapter of the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, the chapter of the proclamation of realization and of praise.

This completes what is called the Later Tantra, the twelve sūtra scriptures.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, seeing and contemplating all phenomena as equal in being unborn, remained without speaking at all.

At that time Sattvavajra himself, knowing that the Teacher had become so, contemplated the realization that all phenomena are unborn and sat without saying anything at all.

Then the great assemblies who had come from the ten directions and were seated there, having realized it in that way, urged Sattvavajra with these words; they urged him truly; they urged him with holy certainty: "How wonderful! Great Sattvavajra, for our benefit, the time has come to ask the Teacher how one is to meditate on the meaning of realization. The time has certainly come!"

When they had urged him thus, Sattvavajra, with a radiance of joy, showing a radiance of clear faith and devotion, rose from his seat and made this request before the Teacher:

"O Teacher of teachers, All-Creating King,
We who are gathered and seated before you,
What is the meaning that we seek?
Please teach us the meaning that we seek."

When he had asked this, the all-creator, the Awakening Mind, remained without speaking at all, and Sattvavajra made this request:

"O Teacher of teachers, All-Creating King,
In the dharmakāya, whose intrinsic nature is the unborn sphere,
How are the three Teachers themselves to be meditated on?
How is it to be meditated on as the three teachings?
How is it to be meditated on as the three compilers?
How is the intrinsic nature of retinue, place and time to be meditated on?
How is one to meditate on the meaning of all phenomena?
Please teach me the ten scriptures of meditation."

Thus he asked.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke these words:

O Sattvavajra, now meditate on the meaning!
In awakening, the nature of reality on which there is nothing to meditate,
Since there are not two, meditating and what is to be meditated on,
Resting in the intrinsic nature without meditating is meditation.
The meaning is the meaning of all, the unborn.
Having known conceptual signs as suchness,
All the memories and thoughts that move in the mind, without exception,
Do not stray from the state of the unborn.
If one knows that however one thinks it is meditation,
Then without meditating, however one rests, there is no distraction.

O, the Teachers, the three bodies, who arose from me,
To persons who delight in meditation on signs,
In accordance with scriptures suited to each,
Declared to all that there is something to meditate on.

O, in me, the Teacher, the All-Creating King,
From the Teacher, the self-arisen wisdom of awareness,
The three Teachers too, without being gathered, are gathered into it.
Without being gathered into that Teacher, they are gathered into that Teacher.
The Teacher taught that in it there is nothing to meditate on.
In the teaching, awareness that is beyond expression and beyond illustration,
The three teachings too are gathered and abide.
In the celestial palace of unfabricated awareness, Akaniṣṭha,
The three celestial palaces too are explained as gathered.
All phenomena whatsoever, however they appear,
Being all gathered in the essence, the awakening mind,
The three retinues and three times are explained as all gathered there.
Apart from that wisdom in which the uncollected intrinsic nature gathers,
There is no other compiler.
In this scripture of resting in the intrinsic nature without meditating,
I do not declare that there is or is not something to meditate on.
Do not set the mind on hope and fear, on grasping at superimposition and denial!

Thus he spoke.

This is the seventieth chapter of the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, the chapter teaching whether or not there is something to meditate on.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke this intent of the view without meditation:

O, I am the Teacher, the All-Creating King.
The teachers of the three bodies who arise from me,
For persons to be trained by way of conceptualizing signs,
Have taught samādhis of conceptualized signs in great number.
Some meditate on the samādhi of peace.
Some meditate on the profound samādhi.
Some abide in the samādhi of selflessness
In which calm abiding and insight are joined.
Some, relying on pure signs,
Focus on signs as lord and subjects.
Some, relying on view and conduct that are unconnected,
Abide in a samādhi of connection.
Some, having relied on the signless meaning,
Abide in a samādhi of signs.
Some, relying on the purity of their own continuum,
Abide in the meaning of the threefold radiating and gathering.
Some, making the purity of cause and result their chief concern,
Abide in the samādhi of light.
These are the enumerated samādhis of signs;
The Teacher, the All-Creating King, did not declare them.
O, what I, the Teacher, the all-creator, have declared
Is the samādhi of total, unborn purity,
Which does not depend on the condition of meditating or not meditating.
All phenomena that are to be meditated on, however they appear,
Without any manner of placing them anywhere in any way,
Are meditated on by leaving them, unsought, in their own place.
O, because these pith instructions on there being nothing to meditate on
Are a meaning that transcends words and sounds,
The All-Creating King of former times did not declare them,
The All-Creating King of later times will not declare them,
And the All-Creating King of the present does not declare them.
O, as the Teacher of teachers, the All-Creating King,
Has declared concerning the meaning of the unerring intent:
For the essence of the meaning, beyond superimposition and denial,
Meditating and not meditating are like arguing about the sky.
The intent is not to stray from the realized meaning.
It is not a thing, nor is it the absence of a thing.
Being neither a side nor beyond sides,
It does not abide on the side of meditating or not meditating.
O, just as I, the Teacher of teachers, the All-Creating King,
Cannot point out meditating and not meditating,
So the yogin who abides in the essential meaning,
And Sattvavajra too, should abide in that.
O, the All-Creating King, Teacher of the three times,
Has not taught, does not teach, and will not teach
The intent of the unborn awakening mind
In names, conventions and words, saying "It is this."
When one realizes the meaning that the view is not to be meditated on,
To alternate between meditating and not meditating is a mistaken path.
When a holy one has understood the unborn sublime intent,
For that holy one there is neither mistaken nor unmistaken.
O, as I, the All-Creating King, have conferred the transmission,
Any fortunate yogin whatsoever
Who, without meditating, has realized and grown familiar with there being nothing to meditate on,
I prophesy as the All-Creating King himself.

Thus he spoke.

This is the seventy-first chapter of the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, the chapter on the indivisibility of view and meditation.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke this abiding in suchness, in which there is nothing to guard:

The Teacher of teachers, the All-Creating King,
Has taught that as long as one abides on the path of concepts and signs,
Seeing guarding and not guarding as two distinct things,
One should guard the root commitments together with the branches.
O, I, the all-creator, am primordially suchness.
Because in suchness there is no apprehended and apprehender,
For whoever realizes phenomena in this way,
No perception of guarding or not guarding arises.
O, I am the uncontrived all-creator, the awakening mind.
I teach no antidotes and do not engage with objects.
In the commitment of self-arisen, spontaneously present awareness
There is no transgression or impairment, so there is no deliberate guarding.
Through the valid cognition of unerring, self-aware intelligence,
One enters the meaning of the uncontrived essence.
Because in the unborn heart all phenomena are of one taste,
I do not abide in the extremes of entering or not entering.
O Sattvavajra, grow familiar with the meaning!
If the secret awareness of the intended meaning has not dawned,
Those habituated to grasping at words and sounds
Will never meet with my scripture, the scripture of the all-creator.
This sublime intent in which there is nothing to guard
Abides always and primordially unguarded.
If one has not grown familiar with my intent, the intent of the all-creator,
However one guards, it turns into a fatal sickness.
O, the commitment of my intent, the intent of the all-creator, is this:
Through undistracted mindfulness, things are wisdom,
Uncontrived, made by no one, self-arisen;
Awareness holds sway, and there is neither guarding nor not guarding.
All phenomena whatsoever, however they appear,
Are mastered by being clear as the self-arisen essence;
Thus the phenomena made by the All-Creating King
Transcend the domain of both awareness and unawareness.
O, just as the Teacher of teachers, the All-Creating King,
Transcends the domain of guarding and not guarding,
Sattvavajra, having grown familiar with the meaning,
Explain it in this way to my retinue!
If you explain it in this way to my retinue,
They will be freed from the path of perceiving things to be done.
Because nothing is born, all will be pacified,
And it will become the heart of me, the All-Creating King.
O, I am the All-Creating King, the awakening mind.
Since in me there is no object of superimposition or denial,
Do not think about anything, do not meditate,
Do not contrive the three doors; leave them relaxed.
There are no planets, lunar mansions, dates or times;
There is no mental engagement; it is free of mantra and mudrā.
Therefore, for the all-creator, the awakening mind itself,
It is declared that there is no boundary between guarding and not guarding.

Thus he spoke.

This is the seventy-second chapter of the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, the chapter on the unwavering view and meditation.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke this realization free of activity and effort:

O, I am the All-Creating King.
The intent of me, the All-Creating King,
Transcends the domain of thought, and so there is nothing to meditate on.
O, mind is the very nature of things just as they are,
And has never contrived what is just as it is.
I, the self-arisen, am free of all causes and conditions.
Leave the three doors unsought, simply relaxed!
Whoever follows after the signs of activity and effort
Will never meet me, the all-creator.

O, as for the activity of me, the all-creator, the Teacher:
Since there is nothing to be done, I do no effortful work.
Since there is no reference point, it is free of the perception of meditating.
Since there is no meeting or parting, leave it in mind without forgetting.
Since whatever arises and is felt is unborn, it becomes clear,
And without anything at all being done, all actions are accomplished.
O, I am the Teacher, the All-Creating King.
Although I perform the activity of all the victors,
Because self-arisen wisdom is perfect without action,
I have never acted upon the action of what is just as it is.
Hero, rest unsought in what is just as it is!
In the uncontrived intent of the victors, just as it is,
Whatever mind and mental factors arise,
If one knows that they abide as the unborn itself,
One is free of all perceptions of the signs of activity and effort.
O Sattvavajra, meditate on what is just as it is!
In the view in which realization is liberated in its own place,
Resting undistracted in the intrinsic nature, there is nothing to do or seek;
All is self-arisen and liberated in its own place.
O Sattvavajra, meditate on what is just as it is!
Do not contrive your body, do not subdue your senses,
Do not direct your speech; there is no activity or effort to be made.
Leave the mind too, aimed nowhere, in the unmoving state.
By meditating on this activity of non-action,
All aims will be perfected without anything being done.
O, in the great bliss that is just as it is and free of effort,
Do not exert, contrive or fixate the three doors.
Do not engage the mind; do not follow after signs;
Rest in the meaning of the bliss of self-arisen wisdom.
This is the self-arisen, luminous intent;
It is the activity of the intent of the All-Creating King.
O Sattvavajra, meditate on what is just as it is!
To enter into the uncontrived, just as it is,
There is no need for valid cognition, no need to correct with antidotes.
Leave the realized meaning uncontrived, just so.
O, this action whose nature is non-action
Is not seized by making efforts with the three doors.
By abiding in the intent of the All-Creating King,
The activity of my intent is spontaneously accomplished.

Thus he spoke.

This is the seventy-third chapter of the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, the chapter on abiding free of activity and effort in what is just as it is.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke this abiding in the essence without meeting or parting:

O Sattvavajra, meditate on what is just as it is!
The Teacher of teachers, the All-Creating King,
In teaching that the essence cannot be laid out,
Taught it as the meaning of what is just as it is, without principal and retinue.
O, because the King of awareness holds sway as clarity,
The essence is by nature beyond effort and accomplishment,
And realization is taken up by supreme wisdom.
This is the maṇḍala of the All-Creating King.
O, the Teacher of teachers, the All-Creating King,
Arranged the maṇḍala of the body of the essence in this way:
All phenomena, however they appear and abide,
Are arranged within the state of the unborn dharmadhātu.
Because in the meaning of the essence there is nothing to adopt or reject,
That too was arranged by me, the all-creator.
O, the Teacher of teachers, the All-Creating King,
Arranged the maṇḍala of the speech of the essence in this way:
All phenomena, however they resound and abide,
Are arranged as speech out of the unborn expanse,
And gathered into the inexpressible speech of the essence.
That too was arranged by the All-Creating King.
O, the Teacher of teachers, the All-Creating King,
Arranged the maṇḍala of the awareness of the essence in this way:
All recollections, awarenesses and thoughts whatsoever
Are known as the meaning of the unborn all-creator itself.
The body, speech and mind of me, the all-creator,
Are the three maṇḍalas left uncontrived in their natural state.
By realizing the meaning that they are complete at once without being arranged,
One enters the meaning of the spontaneously present essence.
O Sattvavajra, meditate on what is just as it is!
In the supreme maṇḍala of the self-arisen essence,
If one rests in the state of just as it is, with the three doors relaxed,
One abides in the intent of me, the all-creator.
The All-Creating Kings of former times, now past,
Perfected the meaning by resting their intent in its natural state;
The All-Creating Kings of times yet to come
Will perfect the meaning by resting uncontrived in the natural state;
And the All-Creating Kings dwelling at present
Abide with their intent resting in its natural state.
O great Sattvavajra, meditate!
Instruct the yogins who belong to my retinue
To rest unsought, in the natural state,
In this very intent of the All-Creating King!
This meaning of resting the intent in its natural state
Is not the sphere of everyone, and so it is not known;
Through resting in equipoise it transcends the domain of realization.
The All-Creating King abides in it.

Thus he spoke.

This is the seventy-fourth chapter of the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, the chapter on abiding in the meaning of the essence.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke this abiding in the mastery of awareness:

O Sattvavajra, meditate on what is just as it is!
Since the King of awareness holds sway as clarity,
It does not depend on the empowerment of the bliss of signs.
Having realized, leave it just as it is in the meaning free of thought.
O, when there is the awareness that has gained the empowerment of the All-Creating King,
Things always dawn uncontrived, just as they are.
This is the supreme path of peace, without cause or condition:
However the ground is set, it is that itself.
And the empowerment of realizing the great means of the All-Creating King
Is this: when everything engages its objects just as it is,
Knowing faults and good qualities both to be equal,
It utterly transcends the meaning of engaging and not engaging.
O, this intent in which the all-creator's awareness holds sway
Cannot be spoken by speech and transcends the domain of reference.
Recollection is pacified and free of elaboration;
Like space it is all-pervading, with no sides or parts whatsoever.
O Sattvavajra, meditate on suchness!
In what transcends the domain of meditating and not meditating,
With no hope or fear of either arising or entering,
One does not enter equipoise and does not meditate on a meaning.
One does not take up empowerment or accomplishment; there is nothing to contrive.
Abide with devotion in the All-Creating King himself.
O, I myself, the Teacher, the All-Creating King,
Am the self-arisen essence in which there is nothing to meditate on:
Wisdom is clear, and there is nothing to accomplish.
Empowerment is the cutting of hope and fear through realization.
The yogin who truly enters into this
Does not declare of the scripture of Utmost Yoga, beyond cause and result,
Of uncontrived wisdom, the All-Creating King,
"Rest it in this way or in that."
It was not spoken before, will not be spoken later,
And is not spoken as something occurring now.

O, the All-Creating King, the very essence of the Teacher, declares:
In the nature of reality, which cannot be meditated on or thought about,
To abide in a blank fading-away, without thinking, is saṃsāra itself;
To seek and to meditate is the path of conceptual signs.
Therefore the all-creator does not declare
That one is liberated by abiding in a samādhi bound up with hope and fear.
O, because the Teacher of teachers, the All-Creating King,
Has through realization utterly cut the bonds of both
And abides in the meaning of equality, free of contradiction,
The intent of me, the all-creator, is spontaneously accomplished.
O, one who has realized and cut whatever binds
Does not enter into liberation and does not take up nirvāṇa;
In the meaning of the essence he fixes on no partial reference point;
He does not meditate on the meaning, and his compassion does not engage objects.
O, what I, the All-Creating King, have taught,
Sattvavajra, grow familiar with its meaning:
The state of awareness mastered by the mindfulness of wisdom.
This I declare to be the All-Creating King.

Thus he spoke.

This is the seventy-fifth chapter of the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, the chapter on abiding in the mastery of awareness.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke this path of the essential meaning:

O Sattvavajra, now meditate on the meaning!
On this path of the meaning, the nonconceptual essence,
Since it abides like the reaches of space, there is nothing it does not pervade;
Since there is nothing that is not unborn, there is no not meditating;
Since it transcends the domain of thought and analysis, there is no reference point.
In this domain of the essence, which transcends signs,
Since it transcends the domain of travel, there is no traveling.
One does not travel the path or train on the grounds;
By abiding in nonconceptual bliss, all is spontaneously accomplished.
O, the path of me, the All-Creating King,
Is one in the dharmadhātu, and so there is nothing to travel;
It keeps company with the essential meaning, and so there is no distraction;
It is gathered into a single sphere, and so it goes nowhere.
O Sattvavajra, meditate on its meaning!
If one does not meditate on the meaning in which there is nothing to meditate on,
Then even if one meditates on the enumerations of signs for a hundred eons,
One will not realize the intent of me, the all-creator.
It transcends the enumerations of signs that are the objects of concepts.
The all-creator need not be worked upon, and there is nothing to be done.
Yogins who follow after the signs of activity
Do not grasp it through activity; with thought they churn up thought,
Like stirring sediment in water: the childish are deceived.
O Sattvavajra, meditate well on the meaning!
I am the Teacher, the All-Creating King.
Since I lead the path of all the victors of the three times,
You too, enter well upon my path!
The buddhas of the past entered the unborn path;
Those yet to come will enter the unborn path;
The buddhas of the present abide in the unborn itself.
Therefore the All-Creating King is the path of all.
O, the yogin who enters and meditates on this path
Abides on the ground of the victors from this very moment.
He does not grasp at the intent, and there is no partiality to hold;
On the path of the essence he transcends entering and not entering.
O, to the fortunate yogins who enter all at once,
Teach the intent of the All-Creating King,
And then, as they grow mindful of and familiar with the scripture of realization,
Explain that there is nothing to meditate on or to enter!
O, I am the Teacher, the all-creator, the awakening mind.
I made everything without exception, and made it the awakening mind;
I made the mind itself the path of the unborn;
On the path of unborn bliss there is nothing to enter.
The fortunate ones, certain to be sublime heirs of the victors,
Having grown familiar with this path of me, the all-creator,
And having realized the meaning of the essence, without traveling and without exertion,
Are without doubt the All-Creating King himself.

Thus he spoke.

This is the seventy-sixth chapter of the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, the chapter on abiding in the path of the essence.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke this teaching that in the meaning there is nothing to train in:

O Sattvavajra, meditate on the meaning of realization!
What the Teacher of teachers, the All-Creating King,
Taught as the inconceivable, definitive time
Is explained as the ground of realizing the essence.
What was taught as the definitive time of great bliss
Is explained as the ground of familiarization.
Other distinctions of striving and conduct
Are grounds that obscure the essence, grounds of saṃsāra in the three realms.
What is called a ground is a distinction of abiding:
Wherever one abides, that is one's ground.
O, although the grounds of the teachers of the three bodies are counted in number,
In the ground of the All-Creating King there are no stages.
"There are grounds, and distinctions in the stages of the grounds":
This is what the teachers of the three bodies taught their retinues.
O, the Teacher of teachers, the All-Creating King,
Taught that in the intrinsic nature of unborn equality,
For the person who has realized the means and the essence,
There is no distinction of obscurations and grounds.
The intrinsic nature of me, the All-Creating King,
Does not arise or enter in the three times; it has no mental engagement;
It does not look partially and does not seek the objects of signs.
This is taught as the scripture of the intent of the All-Creating King.
O, the intent of me, the All-Creating King,
Cannot be found to be indicated by words and letters,
And cannot be established by sound or valid cognition.
Being unborn, it is unceasing;
It does not increase and does not diminish at all;
It is not a sign, and yet it is not other than that itself.
O, because I, the Teacher of teachers, the All-Creating King,
Have blessed all phenomena whatsoever, however they appear,
As the very essence of the unborn,
Realization turns back into that very state.
Just as Sattvavajra has realized
Through the blessing of me, the All-Creating King,
So bless those of later generations who have faith in the scripture of the all-creator,
That things may be unborn!
To hold with undistracted mindfulness
The essence that thus cannot be meditated on is the pith instruction.
This All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind,
Is not reached by explanation, nor by verses,
Not by meditating, nor yet by not meditating;
The essence transcends all mantra and mudrā.
O, the ground that I, the All-Creating King, have taught:
Since all without exception rests on it, there is no rejecting or accepting;
Since it pervades without sides or parts, there is no large or small;
Since it abides in the dharmadhātu, there is no thought or non-thought.
Therefore, on this ground on which one abides all at once,
Sattvavajra, abide well!

Thus he spoke.

This is the seventy-seventh chapter of the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, the chapter on there being nothing to train in the meaning.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke this intent in which there is nothing to adopt or reject in equality:

O, the Teacher of teachers, the All-Creating King,
Has not declared that in the intent that abides
Uncontrived, just as it is, in the nature of the unborn awakening mind,
There is any adopting or rejecting.
The unborn nature is free of superimposition and denial;
Adopting and rejecting are taught as matters of superimposition and denial.
Whoever has entered the meaning of the essence
Teaches what transcends superimposition and denial as the meaning of the essence.
Whoever goes beyond all thought
Is declared to realize that there is no adopting or rejecting.
O Sattvavajra, meditate on the meaning of the essence!
In me, the unerring essence of the meaning,
There is no object for adopting, rejecting, concepts or contrivance;
I grow familiar with it through the supreme bliss of the intrinsic nature.
When the all-creator is realized, all abides in the essence;
Therefore adopting and rejecting have not been declared in the meaning of the essence;
Nor has it been declared to be phenomena that arise and perish;
Nor has it been declared to be phenomena of good and bad, clean and unclean.
It is taught as transcending the domain of thought, analysis and reflection.
O Sattvavajra, now meditate with certainty!
Meditation and the very object to be meditated on,
In the nature of the uncontrived, all-creating mind,
Transcend the domain of an intellect that meditates or does not meditate.
O, I, the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind,
Do not teach the hosts gathered as my retinue
A nature of reality in which there is adopting and rejecting;
I do not teach a dharma that divides the one into two.
Beyond thought and analysis, I do not reflect with thought and analysis.
Do not contrive what is uncontrived, just as it is!
Having let go of all activity, in the nature of reality, whatever appears,
I teach liberation by leaving things in their own nature, without thinking.
O great Sattvavajra, now meditate!
In awakening, the nature of reality, in which there is no adopting or rejecting,
Since it abides like space, body and mind cannot be contrived;
Since there is no perception or clinging, there is nothing to meditate on.
Like space it is utterly peaceful, free of all turbidity;
It does not engage objects, and the intent is free of reference and engagement.
O Sattvavajra, now meditate well!
Do not conceive the senses in relation to objects, do not withdraw the mind,
Do not make antidotes firm, do not train the continuum of body and mind:
Realize body and mind, uncontrived, as the meaning of the essence.
O, the conduct of me, the All-Creating King:
Since it abides relaxed and unsought, all aims are perfected;
Since all is gathered into the meaning of the essence, there is no adopting or rejecting;
Since hope and fear are completely cut, one does not meditate on nirvāṇa.
For whoever realizes the conduct of me, the all-creator,
In the equality of the three times in the unborn meaning,
It utterly transcends the words "engaging" and "not engaging."
This is declared to be free of adopting and rejecting.

Thus he spoke.

This is the seventy-eighth chapter of the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, the chapter teaching that there is no adopting or rejecting in equality.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke this intent that in the state of the self-arisen essence there are no obscurations to be removed:

O, the Teacher of teachers, the All-Creating King,
Has declared the essence to be the unobscured intent:
Since all phenomena without exception, however they appear,
Are all one in the unborn dharmadhātu,
In the nature of the heart, the unborn essence,
No distinction between obscured and unobscured is taught.
O Sattvavajra, now meditate well!
In what is one in the unborn dharmadhātu,
Whoever wishes to reject or accept the obscured or the unobscured
Comes into conflict with the meaning of the sublime essence.
As the all-creator, the awakening mind, has declared,
By meditating on the unborn, birth is clear as the essence.
Whoever abides in what transcends thought and analysis, like space,
Abides in the obscured and the unobscured as the awakening mind.
O, what the All-Creating King declared to the retinue
Was taught as the meaning of wisdom, unobscured and clear:
It abides as the self-arisen essence, without cause or condition;
Through realization, it is the intent that transcends superimposition and denial.
O, therefore, Sattvavajra, now meditate!
Self-arisen wisdom transcends all reference points.
It does not set realization down as an object of reference;
Since it transcends objects, it does not guard consciousness;
Since things are clear as the essence, it does not meditate;
Since appearances are clear as the essence, there is no hope or fear.
O, what the Teacher of teachers, the All-Creating King,
Declared in the midst of the uncontrived retinue is this:
In the uncontrived essence, the root of all phenomena,
Whatever appears is resolved.
If one realizes the meaning of the essence as a single nature,
The nature of all is gathered into the all-creator itself.
Therefore, if one is skilled in the meaning of me, the all-creator,
One becomes skilled in inconceivable dharmas;
If one grows familiar with the meaning of me, the all-creator,
One abides in and is familiar with the meaning of all.
O Sattvavajra, now meditate well!
In all phenomena, however they appear and resound,
There is nothing but the single meaning of realizing and growing familiar with the essence.
Whatever is taught once the essence has been brought out into manifestation,
Realize it as the meaning of the unborn nature.
Undistracted, with hope and fear cut off: that is the intent.
O Sattvavajra, now meditate well!
Whatever is taught as phenomena, however they appear,
Is taught as a means of indicating the All-Creating King.
Do not contradict the scripture of that means!
If those who have entered as the children of me, the all-creator,
Come to realize everything as the unborn meaning,
They do not think in terms of saṃsāra and nirvāṇa:
The essence is taught as by nature free of thought and analysis.

Thus he spoke.

This is the seventy-ninth chapter of the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, the chapter on non-obscuration.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke this intent of spontaneous presence, beyond hope and fear:

O Sattvavajra, now meditate well!
I am the Teacher, the All-Creating King.
All phenomena without exception, however they appear,
I teach as the very nature of the unborn mind.
O, because the nature of mind is spontaneously present,
I do not declare it to be two, established and unestablished;
I do not conceive of it as two, happiness and suffering;
It is free of hope and fear regarding saṃsāra and nirvāṇa.
Since the spontaneously present nature of mind appears as anything at all,
I do not speak of it as meaning an unarisen emptiness;
Since the ground, the root, the essence, transcends thought and analysis,
I do not cling to it as being on the side of the established and the stable.
O Sattvavajra, now meditate well!
In the spontaneously present essence, which transcends thought and analysis,
I do not conceive of the meaning as established or unestablished;
Therefore you too, do not conceive!
O, in the spontaneously present essence, which transcends activity and effort,
Whoever has a clinging that never turns away from taking sides,
Like a child arguing with the sky,
Will never see the nature of me, the all-creator.
Whoever wishes to abandon something and turn it back
Is not free of a mind of hope, fear and dread.
How could a person who clings to extremes in what is free of extremes
Touch the meaning of the spontaneously present essence?
O Sattvavajra, now meditate well!
Since the meaning of unborn, total purity appears,
Do not view appearances as things, and do not destroy them.
Since appearances are self-liberated as unborn,
Do not meditate on emptiness: one is liberated by realizing things just as they are.
O, since I, the Teacher of teachers, the All-Creating King,
Have declared all phenomena whatsoever, however they appear,
To be unborn and totally pure,
Realize them as being exactly unborn.
O, the teachers of the three bodies who arise from me
All speak of the mind itself as unborn,
And all dispute about the meaning of such absence,
Yet none ever realizes the unborn directly.
Therefore, in this scripture of the All-Creating King,
Apart from knowing it and abiding undistracted in the meaning,
Do not enter through effort, do not train the mind with antidotes,
Do not settle it on objects, do not gather in the mind with mindfulness.
Since whatever arises is itself the meaning,
Enter into the meaning of me, the all-creator!
O, as the Teacher of teachers, the All-Creating King,
Has conferred the transmission on the retinue of uncontrived mind:
Yogin who has realized everything without exception as unborn,
Do not strive at the ten natures!
In the nature of the all-creator, the awakening mind,
Knowing has no apprehended and apprehender, and there is no unknowing to abandon;
So by abiding in the meaning in which realization holds sway,
The intent of the All-Creating King is spontaneously accomplished.

Thus he spoke.

This is the eightieth chapter of the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, the chapter on spontaneous presence that cuts off hope and fear.

Then Sattvavajra addressed the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, with these words:

O Teacher of teachers, All-Creating King,
Phenomena, however they appear, were made by you;
The sublime Dharma of the teachings, without exception, was set out by you.
Since you have taught the sublime nature,
When your nature is realized, all phenomena are realized.
Therefore, since the gathered assembly does not clearly understand
This nature of the all-creator itself,
Please explain how it abides in its meaning.

Thus he asked.

Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke this summary of the scriptures and the tantras:

O Sattvavajra, listen well and with diligence!
This summary of the scriptures that the Teacher of teachers, the All-Creating King,
Has conferred upon the retinue:
Attend with diligence to the meaning of what is well taught!
O, my nature, the single unborn meaning,
Is taught as tantra of three kinds.
Through the sequence of three kinds of instruction,
The tantras, the root tantra, the subsequent tantra,
And the subsequent of the subsequent, teach the meaning completely.
O, as for the teaching of the nature that is heard as the root:
Through my fifty-seven natures
The meaning of all things is resolved.
Sattvavajra, attend to it well and with diligence!
O, my fifty-seven natures are these:
The ten scriptures of making manifest,
The ten scriptures of definitive teaching,
The ten scriptures beyond cause and result,
The ten scriptures of effortless perfection,
And the ten scriptures of resolving;
With those that teach the purpose of each, they are fifty-five.
One teaches the ground, and one teaches the instruction.
O, the ten scriptures of making manifest taught by the Teacher of teachers, the All-Creating King,
Are the first, and the tenth, and the second,
The eleventh, and the third, and the twentieth,
The fifteenth, and the twenty-fifth,
And the twenty-first; they are completed with the sixth.
Because through the conferral of the pith instructions one sees what is made manifest,
The sixteenth teaches the purpose of making manifest.
O, the ten scriptures of definitive teaching taught by the Teacher of teachers, the All-Creating King,
Are the fourth, the thirty-third, the thirteenth,
The twelfth, and the fifth, and the thirty-second,
The thirty-sixth, and the thirty-fourth,
The twenty-ninth, and they are completed with the seventeenth.
The purpose of definitive teaching, conviction,
Is taught by the fifty-fifth.
O, the ten scriptures beyond cause and result taught by the Teacher of teachers, the All-Creating King,
Are the forty-first, the fourteenth, the thirty-eighth,
The ninth, and the thirty-fifth, the nineteenth,
The eighteenth, the eighth, and the fortieth,
And they are taught with the fifty-second.
That, being beyond cause and result, there is no need for striving and accomplishment
Is taught definitively in the seventh.
O, the ten scriptures of effortless perfection taught by the Teacher of teachers, the All-Creating King,
Are the twenty-second and the thirtieth,
The twenty-fourth, the twenty-eighth, the twenty-seventh,
The thirty-first, the twenty-third, the twenty-sixth,
The forty-fourth, and they are taught with the fifty-sixth.
In order to teach that the unmade nature is perfect,
The forty-third teaches their purpose.
O, the ten scriptures of resolving taught by the Teacher of teachers, the All-Creating King,
Are the forty-eighth and the thirty-ninth,
The forty-fifth, the thirty-seventh, the forty-sixth,
The forty-seventh, the forty-ninth, the fifty-first,
The fiftieth, and they are completed with the fifty-third.
Because once things are resolved the uncontrived has nothing to contrive,
The forty-second teaches the purpose.
The fifty-fourth teaches the root ground,
And the fifty-seventh entrusts and instructs.
That root tantra is precious to hear.
O, what I, the Teacher of teachers, the All-Creating King,
Have taught having resolved it through reflection is this:
Because the meanings spoken in the root tantra
Are taught as the ten natures of perfection,
They are taught as the ten scriptures of realization.

O, what I, the Teacher of teachers, the All-Creating King,
Have explained as deliverance through meditation is this:
What was explained with intent as the ten natures
Is taught condensed into the meaning of the unborn.
Because one does not stray from the meaning of realizing the unborn,
It is taught condensed into the ten scriptures of meditation.
Because all emanates from this and all gathers into this,
This scripture, the root of all, commenting on the meaning,
Is taught to the one who has become the heir of me, the all-creator,
Indicating the meaning through words, so that the unborn may be realized.
O Sattvavajra, with diligent care, attend well!
This scripture of the awakening mind, the root of the vehicles,
This root condensed by the All-Creating King:
If you, Sattvavajra, come to realize it well,
You will become the Victor of victors, the All-Creating King.
Therefore, Sattvavajra, to the retinue of me, the all-creator,
Proclaim this meaning!
Proclaim it as the unborn awakening mind!
Proclaim saṃsāra and nirvāṇa as one in the expanse!
Proclaim cause and result as complete at a single time!

Thus he spoke. This was the eighty-first chapter of the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, the chapter on the key to the intended meaning.

Then Sattvavajra respectfully praised the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind:

O Teacher of teachers, All-Creating King,
You are the dharmadhātu, the nature of the victors of the three times;
Not abandoning saṃsāra, your compassion is impartial.
Teacher, All-Creating King, to you I bow.
O Teacher of teachers, All-Creating King,
The buddhas of the past were made by you, the all-creator;
Those yet to come and those of the present you make and create.
Teacher, All-Creating King, to you I bow.
O Teacher of teachers, All-Creating King,
The three realms and the three existences were made by you, their maker;
The five self-arisen, the five causes of ornament,
And the five wisdoms you made. All-creator, to you I bow.
O Teacher of teachers, All-Creating King,
The three bodies and the three teachers too were made by you;
Because you made all the three bodies and three minds without exception,
Teacher, All-Creating King, to you I bow.
O Teacher of teachers, All-Creating King,
The abodes where the three bodies dwell were made by you as well;
The first gathering of the retinue, too, arose from you yourself.
Teacher, All-Creating King, to you I bow.
O Teacher of teachers, All-Creating King,
The assembled retinue, too, was surely made by you;
The three teachings, too, were surely made by you.
To you, the All-Creating King, I pay homage and bow.
O Teacher of teachers, All-Creating King,
By teaching undefiled wisdom and the three realizations,
You illuminate the retinue's maṇḍala of darkness.
To you, the All-Creating King, I pay homage and bow.
O Teacher of teachers, All-Creating King,
From your single mind, self-arisen wisdom,
You yourself are the root from which all arises and into which all is gathered.
Heart of phenomena, to you I pay homage and bow.
O Teacher of teachers, All-Creating King,
Having cleared away the path of ignorance,
You make the light of wisdom spread everywhere.
Teacher, All-Creating King, to you I bow.
O, all phenomena whatsoever that appear,
Being self-arisen, are free of all causes and conditions;
Since the uncontrived essence is clear just as it is,
It is self-arisen wisdom, the All-Creating King itself.
However it is examined, it has no depth, no edge, no center;
The source of all, it transcends the domain of thought.
Beyond arising and ceasing, beyond focus and engagement, it is utterly at peace.
Whoever enters, whoever focuses, whoever has faith,
Since here nothing is fixed upon as lesser or as supreme,
Will rest in effortless ease and find relief.
From the All-Creating King, the root of phenomena,
All abides just as it is, free of all effort and striving.

Thus he spoke. This was the eighty-second chapter of the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, the chapter of the concluding praise.

Then Sattvavajra, with earnest aspiration, made this request to the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind:

O Teacher of teachers, All-Creating King,
This sūtra that teaches all phenomena whatsoever that appear and resound
As one in self-arisen wisdom,
The tantra of the All-Creating King, root of phenomena:
To which yogins of later generations
Should it be taught and its instructions given? Please explain this well.

Thus he asked. Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke this meaning of the instructions for the fortunate.

O Sattvavajra, now listen well!
I, the Teacher of teachers, the All-Creating King,
Have made, make, and will make all phenomena whatsoever that appear and resound.
This scripture on the meaning of the essence,
The ground and root, the essence beyond all, the cause of awakening,
Which brings the intent into manifestation:
To those of great faith, commitment and diligence,
Who have compassion and rejoicing, and no weariness or aversion,
Who, attached to none of them, offer with faith and joy
Their body, children, wife, servants and wealth,
Since these are the signs of faith and commitment,
One who possesses the meaning of the essence should give it.
To those who have abandoned fame and are free of pride,
Who do not hold body and life dear for the sake of the sublime meaning of the essence,
Who do not transgress the command and possess the signs,
Give the meaning of the essence and the teaching of the unborn.
When one says, "If I obtain the teaching of the essence,
What use to me are the signs of the world?
If I obtain it, I will undertake hardships,"
And stands firmly in this pledge, give the teaching.
To one who has left home, saying,
"As long as I and my master live,
With no regard for body and life,
I will carry out your command,"
Give the All-Creating King, the heart of the teaching.
In brief, one should offer even one's body and life;
What need is there to mention goods, fields, lands and livestock?
Even if he has no need of them himself,
The holy one accepts them and offers them to the Three Jewels.
O, the persons who are unfit vessels and perverse,
As taught by me, the Teacher of teachers, the All-Creating King, are these:
Those who delight in fame and worldly concerns,
Who are proud, do not honor the holy, and are faint-hearted and fickle,
Who are haughty and possess neither offerings nor faith,
Who want to practice by their own measure and by force,
Who spread themselves about and are at odds with the teaching as a whole,
Who superimpose and deny, bear ill will toward self and others, and do not meditate:
To these it is not to be taught; it should be kept supremely secret.
In order first to examine the signs and the faith,
Respectfully receive jewels, precious substances and goods.
To one with an attached mind, with pride, with a mind set on other things, and not resolved,
To such a one the All-Creating King is not to be taught.
Although in the meaning of the essence there is no accepting or rejecting,
Nonhumans and bhūtas create obstacles,
And through the accomplished ḍākinīs both teacher and disciple
Fall under the power of untimely death and terror;
Through superimposition and denial the vehicle of the essence declines.
Therefore persons of bad disposition should be avoided.
In order to sever worldly clinging and to examine the disciple's mindstream,
The master takes all of his body and possessions;
When the signs that distinguish the capable from the incapable are present,
Give the All-Creating King, which teaches the meaning of the essence.

Thus he spoke. This was the eighty-third chapter of the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, the chapter on to whom the teaching is to be entrusted.

Then Sattvavajra, with a radiant countenance, made this request before the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind:

O Teacher of teachers, All-Creating King,
This sūtra, which from your single mind, self-arisen wisdom,
Liberates the fortunate at a single time:
Please explain to me the distinctions of its names.

Thus he asked. Then the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, spoke this distinguishing of the enumeration of names:

O, what I, the Teacher of teachers, the All-Creating King,
Explain by distinguishing the names is this:
All phenomena whatsoever that appear, without exception,
I have made, make, and will make;
Since nothing needs to be done to me, being spontaneously present,
It is taught as "the All-Creating King."
O, since I, the essence, am stainless
And possess the meaning of spontaneous presence that is not sought,
In this there is no seeking and no accomplishing;
It is definitively taught as "the awakening mind."
Oh! In the nature of reality, accomplished from the very beginning,
This essence has no cause or condition and is not sought;
Since it is unobscured and clear to all without partiality,
It is taught as "self-arisen wisdom."
O, since all phenomena whatsoever that appear
All appear within the uncontrived state just as it is,
There is no meaning of falling into the two partialities;
It is "the Commentary on the Word, the Mirror of Dharma."
O, in the genuine essence, uncontrived just as it is,
Saṃsāra and nirvāṇa arise and are gathered back,
Utterly liberated from the seal of accepting and rejecting, grasping and attachment;
It is taught as "the Vehicle of the Meaning of the Essence."
O, since the victors of the past, the future and the present
See nothing and intend nothing other than this unborn,
And since the uncontrived bindu transcends apprehended and apprehender,
It is taught as "the Mirror of the View."
O, I, the essence uncontrived just as it is,
Am beyond all meaning of superimposition and denial;
Since the victors of the three times arise from me,
I am definitively taught as "the Mother of the Victors."
O, in the uncontrived and unborn All-Creating King
The qualities that need not be sought are complete in full measure;
Since it transcends all seeking and accomplishing,
It is taught as "the King of Tantras."
O, by settling the mind unmistakenly on the uncontrived meaning,
The meaning of the essence is free of superimposition and denial;
Since it teaches both as not in contradiction with the meaning,
It is taught as "the Scripture of the Uncontrived Meaning."
O, what the Teacher of teachers, the All-Creating King, has declared,
The meaning endowed with the unborn awakening mind,
Abiding as one taste in the nondual essence,
Is taught as the sūtra, the sūtra of all.
O, it is what dispels the darkness of ignorance;
It is what makes the light of wisdom spread;
It is what cuts through the ocean of suffering;
It is what makes one familiar with the meaning of the essence.
O, when I clear away the fixations of ignorance
And directly reveal the unborn awakening mind,
Fortunate yogins who possess the eye
Will be liberated into the nature that is free of action.
This Great Perfection of the Word, the innermost essence of the vehicles:
Those who possess the scripture of the essence of the pith instructions,
If they abide at ease in the natural state, will clearly accomplish it,
Uncontrived, unchanging, entering without effort.
O, I am the Teacher, the All-Creating King.
This scripture of the essence of Dharma, the root of the vehicles,
Vast, and not spoken by the teachers of the three bodies,
This mind tantra that teaches the unborn,
The victors of the three times have not spoken in the past,
Will not speak in the future, and do not teach in the present.
Since the All-Creating King has no transition or change,
It was spoken before, it will be spoken later,
And it is spoken as arising now; and so, for the fortunate,
Being definitively taught, it is the scripture of effortless liberation.
This tantra of the Word that manifestly teaches sūtra and scripture,
Neither growing nor declining, for the holding of the essence,
The All-Creating King definitively entrusts to you.

Thus he spoke. This was the eighty-fourth chapter of the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, the chapter teaching the enumeration of names.

This completes the All-Creating King, the Awakening Mind, the tantra whose view, like space, has neither edge nor center, the scripture of the supreme secret, the supreme meaning of the heart of space, in eighty-four chapters.

It was translated, revised and finalized by the Indian preceptor, the lord Śrīsiṃha, and the monk Vairocana.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

In the final sentence the Tibetan reads བྱང་ཆུབ་ཀྱི་སེམས་དཔའ ("bodhisattva") before "All-Creating King"; since everywhere else the title is བྱང་ཆུབ་ཀྱི་སེམས, this is taken as a scribal slip and rendered "the Awakening Mind." ཐིག་ལེ is rendered "sphere," the Great Perfection sense of the single all-encompassing sphere (bindu). The sentence beginning "Dividing the families" is compressed in the Tibetan and its final phrase, ལུས་རྒྱན་གྱི་ལུས་ཅན་གྱི་ཆོས་ཉིད, is construed as "the nature of reality of embodied beings with bodies of ornament."  In Sattvavajra's question and the answer to it, ང་ཡིས་རུ ("my ... to") is read as ང་ཡི་ས་རུ, "to my ground," matching the question's ཁྱོད་སར་ ("to your ground"). The verses on the five paths gloss each affliction syllable by syllable (འདོད་ཆགས, ཞེ་སྡང, ང་རྒྱལ), and the English follows that wordplay as closely as it can.  The chapter on names glosses each word of the title ང, རང་འབྱུང, ཡེ་ཤེས, ཀུན, བྱེད, རྒྱལ་པོ, བྱང, ཆུབ, སེམས in turn; "pure" and "complete" render the two syllables of བྱང་ཆུབ, "awakening." "Perfections" renders ཕུན་སུམ་ཚོགས་པ, the excellences of teacher, teaching, retinue, place and time.  Two evident copyists' slips are read as intended: the syllables written "the fruit-d" are taken as "that fruit," and "superimposition" spelled with an extra s is read as the usual word for superimposition. The "three absorptions" and the "four phases of approach and accomplishment" are the characteristic practices of Mahāyoga, here contrasted with the effortless fruit of the Great Perfection. The "three supreme arrays" are the teacher, teaching and retinue asked about in the preceding question.  The opening lines play on the Tibetan word for yoga, literally "joining with the natural state": the one natural state is shown as four kinds of joining or yoga. The "six perishings and six supports" gloss the Tibetan word for "world," literally "perishable support," and "sorrow, sorrow" glosses the Tibetan for nirvāṇa, "passing beyond sorrow." The "two outer classes" and the "inner ones" refer to the outer tantras and the inner tantras of the Nyingma scheme of vehicles; the "three stages and the essence" likely refers to the inner tantric approaches culminating in the Great Perfection. The lines on "predictions" of three eons, seven lifetimes and so on refer to the times to awakening promised by the various vehicles; the exact force of these two lines is somewhat uncertain.  The phrase rendered "self-existing purity" is a technical term of the Nyingma tantras for the intrinsic purity of one's own continuum. The "five awakenings" are the five stages of manifest awakening used in deity generation, and the "two deities of the sattva" likely refer to the commitment being and wisdom being; both identifications follow the tantric context. The doubled syllable in "the realization of the buddhas" is a simple scribal repetition and has been read as a single word.  The "swift and slow stride" seems to describe the free and spontaneous gait of self-arisen wisdom, which the lower yogas lack; the sense is not fully certain. The phrase "the three kinds of speech" in the list of six wrong attainments is obscure as written; it is translated literally.  The recurring phrase rendered "crossed the summit" is a Tibetan idiom for crossing a mountain pass, meaning to surpass, transcend or bring to culmination. The total of three hundred and sixty is six deviations times six obscurations times the ten natures, as the text itself computes it. The "two obscurations" are here called the obscuration of karma and the obscuration of the knowable, an unusual pairing that follows this text.  "Perfection" renders ཕུན་སུམ་ཚོགས་པ, which the text glosses through its literal sense of "fully gathered together." The four groups of strayed beings (མུ་སྟེགས་, མུར་ཐུག་, རྒྱང་འཕེན་, ཕྱལ་བ) are rendered according to the usual Nyingma doxography: tīrthikas, nihilists (literally "those who reach the end"), Lokāyata materialists, and ordinary heedless worldly people; the last term is the least certain. In the line on the three poisons in equal measure the Tibetan reads ཚིག ("word") where the parallel lines have ཆིག ("one"); it is read as twenty-one thousand. The "three outer classes that engage in activity" refer to the outer tantras (Kriyā, Ubhaya or Caryā, and Yoga), though the text does not name them.  "Śākyamuni of the seven generations" refers to Śākyamuni as the seventh in the series of buddhas of the past. "Six classes of beings" renders རྒྱུད་དྲུག, literally "six lineages." The line "It tames buddhas, gods, humans, and the world of karma" renders the somewhat obscure སངས་རྒྱས་ལྷ་མི་འཇིག་རྟེན་ལས་དག; ལས་དག might also be read as a plural ("the worlds"). "Those who see the renowned teachers" renders གྲགས་ཕྱོགས་མཐོང་བ, whose exact sense is uncertain. The passage ends in mid-sentence; the following piece lists the outer, inner, and secret divisions.  "The three secret abhidharmas" renders གསང་བའི་མངོན་པ་གསུམ, the three highest (inner) classes of teaching ascribed to the dharmakāya; the sense of མངོན་པ here is not fully certain. "Nine passes, nine valleys" renders ལ་དགུ་ལུང་དགུ, a landscape image for the nine vehicles; ལུང may also play on "scripture." The lines on the awakening mind and on yoga play on the syllables of the Tibetan words: byang (purified) and chub (pervading) for awakening, rnal (natural state) and 'byor (union) for yoga. The final narrative sentence has "I" (ང་ཡིས) as speaker, as written.  The line "Since I am the root of all pith instructions" is one syllable short in the Tibetan, but its sense is clear. "The string of the rosary of the afflictions" renders ཉོན་མོངས་པ་ཡི་ཕྲེང་ཐག, the cord on which the beads of the afflictions are strung. The four groups of practitioners described appear to correspond to the śrāvaka and bodhisattva vehicles, the outer tantras, and the inner tantras respectively, though the text does not name them.  "The ground of a buddha in words" renders སངས་རྒྱས་ཚིག་གི་ས, apparently a buddhahood that exists only as a verbal label, as opposed to the direct seeing of suchness. "The ten meanings of nonexistence" refers to the ten things declared absent in the Great Perfection (view, commitment, activity, path, grounds, and so on), listed just before. "I have not imparted" renders མ་ཕོག, literally "did not let it strike or reach."  The line "Blessing them in the form of the four kinds of tīrthikas" renders an instrumental (mu stegs rnam bzhis byin brlabs nas) that could also be read "blessed by the four kinds of tīrthikas"; the context, describing retinues the All-Creating King brings forth, favors the reading given. The sixteen months, the "appointed days" (tshes) and the figure of 1,106 years (stong dang brgya drug) are given as the Tibetan states them; the line rendered "conceive an acceptance of their own mind's spontaneous presence" (bzod rtog byed) is terse and its precise sense is uncertain.  The final lines gloss the Tibetan word for suchness (de bzhin nyid) syllable by syllable: de ("that, such") as the unmistaken nature, bzhin ("thus, like") as the uncontrived nature, and nyid ("-ness, itself") as a designation for the essential nature; the English "such", "thus" and "ness" stand in for these three syllables. The passage ends mid-thought; the next piece supplies the matching line about not denying the sentient beings of the three realms.  In "the three teachers did not teach the scripture of non-effort," the text reads mi stsol ("do not bestow"), which makes little sense here; it is taken as the similar-sounding mi rtsol ("non-effort"), which fits the chapter's theme. The dialogue on the bodily remains (sku gdung ring bsrel) plays on the parts of the compound: sku "body," gdung "remains" (also "lineage"), and ring bsrel "relics."  The lines "My nature is primordially utterly pure; my pervading of all is primordially perfected; my nature, being clear, is primordially mind" gloss the three syllables of the Tibetan for awakening mind, byang chub sems: byang ("purified"), chub ("perfected, pervaded"), and sems ("mind").  This chapter is terse and in places obscure; several lines are rendered literally where their sense is uncertain, notably "It arises in the domain of the subtle portions of dedication," "Held within a single atom" (gtses, taken as "held, contained"), "a precious jewel that comes forth from all spiritual friends," and "Through dullness toward wisdom, with emerging and entering, beings are born under its sway."  The line "are spoken of as imputed by way of deprecation" renders a terse phrase; the sense seems to be that the twelve links are taught only as a conventional imputation, a provisional denial of their ultimate reality. In the colophon of the twenty-third chapter the text reads "like" where "view" is evidently meant, matching the chapter's own heading, and it is translated as "view". "Space-like realization" renders the phrase literally "the intent (or realization) of space".  The text names the teachers' bodies as earth, water, wind, space, and then space a second time; one would expect fire among the five elements, and the repeated "space" may stand where fire was intended, but it is translated as written. "The great sphere" renders thig le chen po (mahābindu), the all-encompassing single sphere of Great Perfection terminology. The line on "the account and its meaning" is terse; it seems to criticize fixing both narrative scripture and its sense within a framework of logical valid cognition.  The lines about "gold by the fistful" are obscure (the Tibetan reads literally "who sends a black fist for gold"); the rendering follows the evident sense that the true master's teaching is worth any price. Before the verse on the two truths the address "O great being, listen!" is written twice, the second time with an intrusive "own"; this looks like a copyist's repetition and is translated once. In this chapter the verb usually rendered "prophesy" means "declare" or "teach", as in "I have not declared"; the line about seven lifetimes is syntactically compressed, and its sense appears to be that such practice brings no self-arisen wisdom and is not the All-Creating King's teaching.  "The beings of Yoga" renders a phrase that reads literally "the subsequent yoga beings"; in this sequence of vehicles it evidently refers to followers of Yoga tantra, before Great Yoga (mahāyoga). The span "two thousand six hundred" is given without a unit in the Tibetan. In the list of the five perfections, only teacher, place and time are expanded here; teaching and retinue reappear in the lines on liberation. The syllable-by-syllable explanation of Atiyoga glosses A and Ti, then turns to Yoga in the following line.  This section is highly compressed and cryptic, especially the run of lines built on "that"; they are rendered closely rather than interpreted. "Stirs the expanse in name" renders a phrase that may be corrupt. The syllables a, ta and pa illustrate the letters of the alphabet as the play of Samantabhadra. In the closing lines "the unmoving" and "the unshakable" may allude to the deities Acala and Akṣobhya, but read naturally as descriptions here. "The pure path that rises ever higher" could also be read as "the path of Brahmā".  "The primal A" renders ཨ་དང་པོ (here ཨ་དང་པར), taken as the primordial syllable A; the line is terse and other construals are possible. In "corrupted by contrivance," བུས་བས is read as བྱས་པས ("by doing, by contrivance"), since the form as written makes no clear sense. The final quatrain of chapter thirty is a deliberate play on the pronoun "that" and has been kept as literal as English allows. The closing verse is the reply of the addressee, Sattvavajra, though the text does not name the speaker.  གཙུག་ཏོར, listed after Vipaśyin among the seven buddhas ending with Śākyamuni, is taken as Śikhin (usually གཙུག་གཏོར་ཅན). The reply to the opening statement and the second reply are spoken by the King of awareness (Sattvavajra); the text names him only before the second. "Indirect intent" renders ལྡེམ་དགོངས, teaching given with a hidden purpose.  In the opening verses, "sign" renders རྟོག (and later རྟོགས), read as རྟགས "sign" in line with the passage's own threefold scheme of example, meaning and sign. "What seals off the tīrthikas from the buddhas" renders the terse ཅི་ལ་རྒྱ, understood as asking what boundary distinguishes them. The explanation of mu stegs (tīrthika) is a play on the two syllables of the Tibetan word, "edge" and "platform" or "ford."  "The mastery over life, the mudrā, the spontaneous presence" lists levels of vidyādhara attainment as named in the Nyingma scheme. "The sickness that cuts off life" renders ཚི་ཆད་ནད, reading ཚི་ཆད as ཚེ་ཆད "life cut off"; the expression is unusual. The line "One does not deliberately abide in nonconceptual equality" renders a negated statement (མི་བྱེད), not a prohibition; it is understood as saying that even resting in equality is not something to be contrived.  The six vehicles are the bodhisattva sūtras, Kriyā, Upa (Upayoga or Ubhaya), Yoga, Mahāyoga and Anuyoga, each set against Atiyoga. Universal Light (Samantaprabhā), Ghanavyūha ("Densely Arrayed"), the Vajra Holder and nondifference are the grounds or fruits each vehicle aims at. In the first of the parallel verses on space the Tibetan reads "accomplishes awakening like space" without the negation that every later verse has; it is translated as it stands, understood as reducing awakening to a mere space-like emptiness, but it may be a scribal omission of མི.  The verses on being "victorious over all" play on the word for victor (jina), explaining why the awakening mind is called "the essence of the Victors."
