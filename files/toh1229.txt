The Ten Realities (Daśatattva)

This text is The Ten Realities, in Sanskrit Daśatattva and in Tibetan De kho na nyid bcu pa, a short ritual manual attributed to the Indian master Ḍombipa and translated into Tibetan by Dīpaṃkaraśrījñāna, also known as Atiśa, with the monk Gewé Lodrö. Drawing on the Hevajra Tantra, it sets out in brief the ten ritual topics a tantric master must know: the wheel of protection with its ten wrathful deities, the four empowerments, the extensive and abbreviated torma offerings, mantra recitation, the fierce rite of separation directed against those who gravely harm the teaching, repelling by meditation and by a drawn protective wheel, the practice of the maṇḍala, the rite of threatening that compels accomplishment, and the dismissal of the deities with the disposal of the sand maṇḍala. It is Toh 1229 in the Tengyur, where it belongs to the tantra commentaries as a practical ritual work of the Hevajra cycle.

In the language of India: daśatāttva. In the language of Tibet: The Ten Realities.

Homage to the bodhisattva Mañjuśrī in youthful form.

Having bowed with reverence to glorious Heruka and to the guru,
from the ten realities taught in the Hevajra
I shall explain, in brief, what they definitely are.

Protection, empowerment, torma, recitation, dance,
repelling, the practice of the maṇḍala,
threatening, application, and requesting the deities to depart:
these the buddhas have called the ten realities.

Their meaning is this. What is called the wheel of protection is as follows. On a variegated lotus arisen from the yellow seed, the letter paṃ, on a sun born from the letter raṃ, stands the yellow seed bhrūṃ, which transforms into a ten-spoked wheel. In its center, on a sun at the hub, stands the blue letter hūṃ, and from its transformation comes Vajrahūṃkāra. And, according to the rite, the ten seed letters hūṃ stand on the ten spokes, and from them are born the ten wrathful ones.

On the eastern spoke is blue Yamāntaka, holding a hammer. On the southern spoke is white Prajñāntaka, holding a white cudgel. On the western spoke is red Padmāntaka, holding a red lotus. On the northern spoke is black Vighnāntaka, holding a black vajra. On the spoke of Īśāna, the northeast, is black Ṭakkirāja, holding an iron hook. On the spoke of fire, the southeast, is Nīladaṇḍa, holding a black staff in his hand. On the spoke of Nairṛti, the southwest, is black Mahābala, holding a trident in his hand. On the spoke of wind, the northwest, is Acala, holding a black sword. On the upper spoke is Uṣṇīṣacakravartin, crowned with Vairocana, holding a white wheel. On the lower spoke is Sumbharāja, crowned with Akṣobhya, holding a black vajra in his hand. Vajrahūṃkāra, his head sealed by Akṣobhya, holds a black vajra.

The four wrathful ones of the east and the other cardinal directions are crowned with Vairocana, Akṣobhya, Amitābha and Amoghasiddhi. In just the same order, Ṭakkirāja, Nīladaṇḍa, Mahābala and Acala, who dwell in the northeast and the other intermediate directions, are crowned with Akṣobhya, Vairocana, Amitābha and Amoghasiddhi.

All the wrathful ones have one face and two arms. The fingers of their left hands make the threatening gesture, and their right hands hold their own emblems. Their faces are wrathful, round and red, and their yellow hair bristles upward. Radiating forms of wrathful ones like themselves, they threaten the māras. They are seated on suns and have the radiance of the sun disc. Yamāntaka, Prajñāntaka, Padmāntaka and Vighnāntaka are stout, with hanging bellies, adorned with snakes, their yellow eyebrows bristling upward; the others have graceful bodies. This is the first reality.

Now empowerment will be explained. In the proper order, having made the maṇḍala at the place for the maṇḍala, one leads the disciple into the maṇḍala by the rite of entering the maṇḍala. To a disciple who is free of intoxication, pride and arrogance, who has no faith in other teachers, who looks on with great faith, and who has been empowered with the seven, water, crown, vajra, lord and the rest, one should give the secret empowerment according to the rite.

After that, to the one who has the seven together with the rite of the wisdom-gnosis empowerment, who has received the secret empowerment, whose mind delights greatly in wisdom-gnosis, and to whom union with the wisdom consort (prajñā) has been shown through the sign of bliss, one should entrust the wisdom consort, as taught in scripture, by the rite of truly giving the wisdom consort. The sign of bliss that comes from accepting the wisdom consort truly entrusted is the wisdom-gnosis empowerment. Then, after that, what is to be touched through the teaching of the holy guru is the fourth empowerment. This is the second reality.

Now the torma will be explained. Having uttered the secret mantra hūṃ, one imagines oneself transformed into the form of glorious Heruka, all-protecting, and that place, and the maṇḍala in front. One visualizes, in successively higher order, a red lotus with eight petals contained within the center of three nested sources of phenomena. Above it, by means of Gaurī and the other goddesses, one invites all those of divine birth who dwell in the immeasurable worlds, together with their retinues: Viṣṇu, the Great God (Śiva), Maheśvara, the sun, the moon and the rest. From the transformation of the seed letter oṃ they are two-armed and hold vajra and bell; they are white in color, their bodies embraced by their own wisdom consorts; they are seated with the right leg extended, blessed by the four limbs, and crowned with Vairocana. They are offered argha water, drink and the rest. One should visualize them with a mind free of attachment to food.

In the middle are the guardians of the directions, each contained in the petal of its own direction; from the transformation of the seed letter hūṃ they are black and crowned with Akṣobhya. Below are Source of Strength, Ananta, Vāsuki, Takṣaka, Lineage Giver, Śaṅkha, Padma and Mahāpadma, each contained in the petal of its own direction, together with the asuras; from the transformation of the seed letter a they are red in color and crowned with Amitābha, and the rest is as before. After that, one should consecrate the food, drink, nectar and the rest as nectar itself.

The sequence for this is as follows. There is a wind maṇḍala arisen from yaṃ, smoke-colored and shaped like a bow. Above it is a red fire maṇḍala arisen from raṃ. Above that is a lotus vessel, the transformation of the letter a, filled with food and the rest, blessed by the five seeds, and melted by the blazing and the heat. Above that, the moon that is the transformation of the seed letter oṃ melts and is made into nectar. Then, together with scent, flowers, incense, victory banners, parasols, cloth, music and the rest, and drink and the rest, the master, intent on ringing the bell, should give the torma with oṃ inda ja ma ja la ja kkha and the other mantras.

Then one should make offerings by means of Gaurī and the other goddesses, who arise from the seed at the heart of the gnosis being in one's own heart and hold various offerings in their hands, and so on. After that one should praise with the verses that begin "Akṣobhya, great vajra gnosis." Immediately after the praise, one should request the deities to depart with the mantra oṃ bajrasatva samaya manupālaya bajrasatva tveno patiṣṭha dṛḍho me bhaba sutoṣyo me bhaba anurakto me bhaba supoṣyo me bhaba sarba siddhiṃ me prayaccha sarba karma su ca me cittaṃ śriyaḥ kuru hūṃ ha ha ha ha ho bhagabāna sarba tathāgata bajra mā me muñca bajri bhaba mahāsamayasatva āḥ muḥ. This is the extensive torma.

Now the abbreviated torma will be taught. The yogin endowed with deity yoga, wholly intent on ringing the bell, summons the very deities mentioned above, in their order, by means of Gaurī and the others, onto the red lotus at the center of the three sources of phenomena, and in a moment satisfies them with the torma prepared earlier and with the mantras of Indra and the rest. The remainder is as before. This is the abbreviated torma, the third reality.

Now recitation will be taught. The yogin endowed with the yoga of his own deity, preceded by the three samādhis, immediately after meditating on the innate, recollects his own deity. Light rays come forth from the seed hūṃ that stands on a sun at the heart of the gnosis being in his own heart. They reach the body, speech and mind of all beings without exception, leaving no stain at all, and having drawn them in, he should place them again within himself. Then, viewing things in the same way, he recites the mantra of his own deity, makes offerings and the rest, and then rises. This is the recitation of secret mantra, the fourth reality.

Separation will also be explained. One may see someone who harms the teaching, who has deceived the guru and the buddhas, or who is bent on killing his father and mother. The yogin who has the higher knowledge that directly knows the virtuous and nonvirtuous actions of others should, with a mind full of compassion, seek that person's welfare through gifts, respect and the like. Even if that person treats him badly, and even if he goes on acting in the same way, the yogin should seek his welfare again and again. If he still does not heed one's words, then, with a loving mind, one should perform the fierce rite taught in the glorious Hevajra.

The sequence for this is as follows. At the junctures of the day, endowed with the application of the three samādhis, one sends out light rays from the seed at the heart of the gnosis being and invites countless buddhas. In their presence one looks at the target who commits such deeds and declares it to the buddhas. After that one invites the deities of the maṇḍala, makes offerings and praises and tastes the nectar. Then, by the light rays of the seed at the heart of the gnosis being, one invites the lord of one's own circle. He melts by way of the vajra path into the lotus, and one sees him emerge from it in the form of Gaurī, holding a noose, a hook and a hammer. That Gaurī, sending out her own light rays in the form of vajras, strips away the target's mantras, mudrās and other protections, binds him with the noose, drags him with the hook, and summons him before the wheel. The yogin then entreats the gnosis being within him, saying:

"By the power of my very nature,
and by passion for the nature of Akṣobhya:
this is the source of all contemptible deeds;
this body you must abandon."

Having entreated it in this way,
with Gaurī's hook and noose
one draws out the gnosis being;
with the hook in Gaurī's hand
one draws out all the entrails
from the root of the target's navel;
and at once the ascetic
should apply him to the fierce rite.

Or else, having visualized him in the form of an animal, standing upright, vomiting blood from his mouth, trembling, without refuge, smeared with sesame oil, naked, his hair standing on end, one imagines a figure like a fiery needle entering deep into his passage, imagines the seed of fire at his heart, and immediately liberates him. The gnosis being, for its part, is made into Akṣobhya; one makes offerings and praises and tastes the nectar according to the sequence of the rite, and sees it go to the buddha field of Akṣobhya. This is the reality of separation, the fifth.

Now repelling through meditation will be taught. One visualizes the lord Uṣṇīṣacakravartin with two arms, holding his own emblem, the wheel, and making the threatening gesture, wrathful, seated with the right leg extended on a sun seat, his form radiating light, having received empowerment and the rest, threatening the host of enemies, and moving as he turns to the left. Then one recites nama samanta budhanāṃ nama samanta narmānāṃ nama samanta saṃghānaṃ oṃ sitātapatra oṃ bimala oṃ śaṃkara oṃ pratyaṃgera bajra uṣṇiṣa cakkrabarti sarba yantra mantra tantra mūla karma bandhana tāḍana kīlanaṃ ba mama kṛte yena kaina cita kṛtaṃ tat sarbantu cchinda cchinda bhinda bhinda ciri ciri giri giri mara mara hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ phaṭ phaṭ seven times at the time of lying down, and meditates. This is the reality of repelling through meditation, the sixth.

On birch bark or the like, one draws a wheel with ten spokes. On the eastern spoke one writes: namaḥ smanta buddhebhyo namaḥ smuṅgarahastāya caṇḍamaṇḍala akepakarāya protect so-and-so rakṣaṃ kuru hūṃ phaṭ. On the southern spoke: manta buddhebhyo mahādaṇḍadhara bibidhara sura asura mardhana bhūtānaṃ nirmatha protect so-and-so rakṣaṃ kuru hūṃ phaṭ. On the western spoke: manta buddhebhyo hulu hulu mahāpadma amṛtaṃ kuru mahābiśaṃ protect so-and-so rakṣaṃ kuru hūṃ phaṭ. On the northern spoke: manta buddhebhyo nama bajrakrodhaya mahādaṃṣṭotakaṭabhairabaya bajrasanto darbāya oṃ amṛtakuṇḍali protect so-and-so rakṣaṃ kuru hūṃ phaṭ. On the spoke of Īśāna: manta buddhebhyo oṃ yamadaṇḍaya nīladaṇḍaya turu turu muru muru sarba duṣṭāṃ māraya māraya protect so-and-so rakṣaṃ kuru hūṃ phaṭ. On the spoke of fire: namaḥ samanta buddhebhyo namo mahābalāya ucchuṣmakrodhāya krodhārājāya sarba nāgānāṃ hṛdaya a ṭaḍaya protect so-and-so rakṣaṃ kuru hūṃ phaṭ. On the spoke of Nairṛti: namaḥ samanta buddhebhyo namo acalakaṣāya ceṭṭa ceṭṭa moṭṭa moṭṭa tuṭṭa tuṭṭa ghaṭṭa ghaṭṭa ghāṭṭaya ghāṭṭaya protect so-and-so rakṣaṃ kuru hūṃ phaṭ. In the direction of wind: manta buddhebhyo oṃ ṭakkirājā protect so-and-so rakṣaṃ kuru hūṃ phaṭ. On the hub of the wheel: oṃ suṃbhani suṃbha hūṃ gṛhṇa gṛhṇa hūṃ ghṛhṇāpaya gṛhṇāpaya hūṃ ānaya ho bhagabān bidyārājāya for so-and-so rakṣaṃ śāntiṃ kuru hū phaṭ svāhā.

On the rim of the wheel one should write this mantra: namaḥ smanta buddhānā namaḥ smanta dharmānāṃ namaḥ smanta dharmānāṃ namaḥ smanta saṃghānāṃ oṃ sitātapratra oṃ bimala oṃ śaṃkara oṃ pratyaṃgīra oṃ bajraṣṇīṣa cakrabarti sarba yantra mantra mūla karma bandhanaṃ tāḍanaṃ kīlanaṃ ba mama kṛte yena kena citta kṛtaṃ tata sarbantu cchinda cchinda bhinda binda ciri ciri giri giri mara mara hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ phaṭ phaṭ.

This practice of protecting oneself destroys magical devices, mantras and the like. If the protection wheel, once made, is tied on the upper arm, one will not be overpowered by the three realms. This is repelling by drawing, the seventh reality.

Immediately after that, the practice of the maṇḍala will be explained. The mantra practitioner, facing west, should begin the practice of the maṇḍala. By the light rays of the seed at the heart of the gnosis being, one draws in the maṇḍala that dwells in the sky and imagines that it enters the lotus of one's own consort. From that melting, by means of one's own seed, one should send forth the lord of the circle and the rest, in their proper order, into the maṇḍala of colored powders. After that, one blesses the eyes and the rest, and performs the offerings and the praise, up to the tasting of the nectar. This is the practice of the maṇḍala, the eighth reality.

Now the application of threatening will be explained. When the yogin's mind has become somewhat stable in meditation and he has found some signs, he should meditate for up to six months. If even so there is no accomplishment, then he should meditate for eighteen months, preceded by the yoga of his own chosen deity and by the rite as described, the commitments and the rest. If one says that even so there has been no accomplishment, then at the junctures between sessions he should undertake the application of threatening. The sequence for it is this. The yogin, endowed with the application of Akṣobhya and with the three samādhis, meditates on a celestial palace that has become black, encircled by a black maṇḍala. After that he makes offerings and praise and tastes the nectar, and by the light rays of the seed at the heart of the gnosis being he invites the circle of the tathāgatas. It falls into the lotus maṇḍala, encircled by a maṇḍala of fire, and he sees them frightened, terrified and trembling violently. By the light rays of his seed the host of tathāgatas melts through passion for the wisdom consort and comes forth by the vajra path. From its transformation, Yamāntaka with two arms, from the eight directions, should drive in the ritual dagger with oṃ āḥ bajrarāja akṣobhya kīlaya hūṃ phaṭ. This is the rite of Akṣobhya. If all this is done without clinging to what is to be pinned down, the one who pins it down, or the act of pinning down, it will be accomplished, but not otherwise. This is the practice of threatening, the ninth reality.

After that, requesting the deities to depart will be taught. The sequence for it is this. Having come out of the maṇḍala house and performed a peaceful fire offering, one enters the maṇḍala house again, makes offerings to the maṇḍala with various offerings, rings the bell together with incense, and recites the verse:

oṃ You have accomplished every aim of beings;
grant accomplishments in accordance.
Having gone to the realm of the buddhas,
please come again. muḥ

After that, one imagines the circle of the maṇḍala, raised up from the maṇḍala of colored sand, departing to the buddha field. With the three syllables ending in muḥ, one sweeps up the colored sand of the maṇḍala, puts it in a fragrant vessel, and casts it into a river that flows down to the ocean. One smears the ground of the maṇḍala with cow dung, performs the purifying sprinkling by the rite of the syllables ending in muḥ, and gives the torma. This is the reality of requesting the deities to depart, the tenth.

Whatever merit I have gathered
from ascertaining the ten realities,
by it may the cataracts of unknowing
that afflict beings be dispelled.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The fifth item in the list of ten, written dgar, is read as gar, "dance". Yamāntaka's emblem is written thob pa, which makes no sense here; it is read as tho ba, "hammer". The phrase rendered "free of intoxication, pride and arrogance" reads literally "without waste of intoxication, pride and arrogance"; the sense of a disciple untainted by these faults is the evident one. Phyogs skyod in the torma section is read as phyogs skyong, "guardians of the directions", and smar ba mtha' yas as snang ba mtha' yas, Amitābha, both evident slips. Two nāga names could not be identified with confidence and are translated: stobs kyi rgyu as Source of Strength, and rigs sbyin as Lineage Giver (possibly Kulika). Nor rgyas kyi bu is the usual Tibetan rendering of Vāsuki. The Great God and Maheśvara are both common names of Śiva but are listed here as separate deities. In the empowerment section the wisdom consort (prajñā) is the female partner of the third, wisdom-gnosis, empowerment.  The fifth reality is here called dgar ba, "separation", the fierce rite in which the target's consciousness is separated from the body and sent to Akṣobhya's buddha field; the same word in the opening list of ten, rendered there as "dance" on a reading of gar, should evidently be understood as this rite of separation. "Liberate" renders the technical term of the fierce rite, killing while freeing the consciousness. The four-line verse addressed to the target's gnosis being is terse and its first two lines are uncertain; the rendering takes it as the yogin's declaration that the target's body has become a source of contemptible deeds and must be abandoned. "Passage" renders lam, the bodily channel the fiery needle enters.  Che ge mo, which recurs in the mantras, is the Tibetan placeholder "so-and-so", where the practitioner inserts the name of the person to be protected; it is translated and kept outside the Sanskrit. In the ninth reality the "threatening" is aimed at the tathāgatas themselves, who are compelled to grant accomplishment to a practitioner whose meditation has not borne fruit; the text then insists that the act succeeds only when free of clinging to its object, agent and action.
