The Dhāraṇī Called "Luster That Cannot Be Stolen Away"

The Dhāraṇī Called "Luster That Cannot Be Stolen Away," known in Tibetan as Mdangs phyir mi 'phrog pa, is a very brief protective incantation whose Sanskrit title is not securely attested. It opens with homage to Vaiḍūryaprabha, the buddha whose name means "Beryl Light," and then gives a short mantra in Sanskrit invoking protection. A simple ritual instruction follows: a paste of musk, sweet flag, and a substance called black bhisu is applied as a mark on the throat while the mantra is recited seven times. Done this way, harmful spirits known as bhūtas cannot even look at the person, let alone steal away their vitality and radiance. The work is Toh 1052 in the Kangyur, where it belongs to the collection of dhāraṇī texts, the short spells and incantations valued for protection and practical ritual benefit.

The Dhāraṇī Called "Luster That Cannot Be Stolen Away"

Homage to Vaiḍūryaprabha!

tadyathā oṃ mama rakṣa sāgare mahāsāgare buruce burucuru samane me deba hulu hulu svāhā

Make a paste of musk, sweet flag, and black bhisu. Then place a mark of it on the throat and recite the mantra seven times. The bhūtas will then be unable even to look at that person, let alone steal away his luster.

It is complete.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The title could also be understood as "Not Robbing Back the Luster." It names a rite that keeps one's luster or vitality from being stolen by spirits. Vaiḍūryaprabha, "Lapis Light," is probably the Medicine Buddha, Bhaiṣajyaguru Vaiḍūryaprabha. The ingredient "bhisu nagpo" cannot be identified with confidence and is given here as written, as "black bhisu." "Sweet flag" renders shu dag, which is calamus.
