The Dhāraṇī of the Venerable Noble Mañjuśrī Called "Increasing Wisdom and Intelligence" (Āryamañjuśrībhaṭṭārakasyaprajñābuddhivardhananāmadhāraṇī)

The Dhāraṇī of the Venerable Noble Mañjuśrī Called "Increasing Wisdom and Intelligence," known in Sanskrit as Ārya Mañjuśrī Bhaṭṭārakasya Prajñā Buddhi Vardhana Nāma Dhāraṇī and in Tibetan as Rje btsun 'phags pa 'jam dpal gyi shes rab dang blo 'phel ba zhes bya ba'i gzungs, is a brief incantation devoted to the bodhisattva Mañjuśrī, the embodiment of wisdom. After an homage to the Three Jewels and to Mañjuśrī in his youthful form, it presents a short mantra of purification and victory, then describes the benefits of holding and reciting it. These include gaining intelligence within a month, a melodious voice, a beautiful form, the memory of past lives, great learning, and turning away from cyclic existence for countless eons. It is Toh 895 in the Kangyur, where it belongs to the class of dhāraṇī texts, short works centered on sacred formulas whose recitation is believed to bring spiritual and worldly blessings.

In the language of India: ārya mañjuśrī bhaṭṭārakasya prajñā buddhi vardhana nāma dhāraṇī
In the language of Tibet: The Dhāraṇī of the Venerable Noble Mañjuśrī Called "Increasing Wisdom and Intelligence."

Homage to the Three Jewels.

namo mañjuśriye kumārabhūtāya tadyathā oṃ araje viraje śuddhe viśuddhe śodhani viśodhani śodhaya viśodhaya amale vimale jayavati ruru cala hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ phaṭ phaṭ svāhā

Whoever holds this will become endowed with intelligence within a single month. Their voice will become melodious. Their form will become beautiful. If one recites it a single time, one will turn one's back on saṃsāra for a thousand eons. Merely by keeping it, one will remember one's past births. If one recites it one hundred thousand times, one will become learned. If one recites it two hundred thousand times, one will become a holder of knowledge mantras (vidyādhara). If one recites it three hundred thousand times, one will see the face of Mañjuśrī.

If one who has committed the five deeds of immediate retribution does not attain accomplishment, then let it be I myself who commits the five deeds of immediate retribution, and may I myself have deceived the buddhas, the blessed ones.

This concludes the dhāraṇī of the Venerable Noble Mañjuśrī called "Increasing Wisdom and Intelligence."

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The closing oath does not name its speaker. It is presumably the voice of the dhāraṇī's revealer, whether the Buddha or Mañjuśrī, who stakes his own truthfulness on the dhāraṇī's efficacy. It has been kept in the first person, as in the Tibetan. In the title, the genitive "of Mañjuśrī" goes with the dhāraṇī, as the Sanskrit "mañjuśrībhaṭṭārakasya … dhāraṇī" shows. The wisdom and intelligence increased are the practitioner's, not Mañjuśrī's.
