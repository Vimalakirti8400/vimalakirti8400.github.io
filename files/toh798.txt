The Dhāraṇī Mantra That Heals Wounds

The Dhāraṇī That Heals Wounds (Tibetan: Rma 'byor bar byed pa zhes bya ba'i gzungs sngags) is a very short dhāraṇī text, a spell of protective and healing power, devoted to Mañjuśrī, the bodhisattva of wisdom. After a brief homage to the noble Mañjuśrī, it gives a mantra to be recited three times, with spittle then applied to the injury, as a remedy for wounds caused by blows from stones, by weapons, by severed sinews, or by a broken skull. One of the colophons also calls it a dhāraṇī that frightens Māra, though its practical focus is clearly on healing. It is Toh 798 in the Kangyur, where it belongs to the Tantra section as one of its many brief dhāraṇī texts. A Sanskrit title is not given in the source lines provided, so none is offered here.

The dhāraṇī mantra called "That Which Heals Wounds."

Homage to the noble Mañjuśrī.

Oṃ ubase ubase. Sum sum, sarva nanarbada svāhā. Hūṃ phaṭ. Śama śāntiṃ kuru svāhā.

Recite this three times, and apply spittle to the wound. It heals wounds caused by blows from stones, wounds from weapons, severed sinews, and a broken skull.

The dhāraṇī that heals wounds is complete.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The opening words, which seem to belong to the end of a preceding text, are fragmentary; I have rendered only the clearly legible colophon of the Māra-frightening dhāraṇī. The mantra syllables are given as they appear in the Tibetan transliteration and are not translated; some forms (ubase, sum sum, nanarbada) are corrupt or obscure and may reflect scribal variants. The phrase about severed sinews and a broken skull follows the Tibetan literally.
