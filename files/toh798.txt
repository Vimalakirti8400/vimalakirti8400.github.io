This short text, known as the Dhāraṇī for Healing Wounds (in Tibetan, the Rma 'byor bar byed pa'i gzungs sngags), is a protective and healing incantation offered in homage to the noble Mañjuśrī. Consisting of a mantra to be recited three times over saliva applied to an injury, it belongs to the genre of dhāraṇī literature concerned with practical remedies, promising the healing of wounds from stones or weapons, severed tendons, and even fractured skulls. It appears alongside another related spell, the Dhāraṇī for Frightening Māras, reflecting the common pairing of protective and curative incantations in this class of literature. This text is catalogued as Toh 798 in the Kangyur, situating it among the collection of dhāraṇī and mantra texts preserved in that section of the canon.

Fainted. The dhāraṇī for Frightening Māras is complete.

The dhāraṇī mantra called Healing Wounds.

Homage to the noble Mañjuśrī.

Oṃ ubhāsa ubhāsa. Sum sum. Sarva nan bade svāhā. Hūṃ phaṭ. Śama śāntiṃ kuru svāhā.

Having recited this three times, if one smears saliva on a wound, it heals injuries from stones, wounds from weapons, severed tendons, and fractured skulls.
