The Noble Aspiration Prayer of Maitreya (Āryamaitreyapraṇidhāna)

The Noble Aspiration Prayer of Maitreya, in Tibetan Phakpa Jampé Mönlam, is Toh 1096 in the Kangyur, where it is placed in the dhāraṇī collection among the canon's aspiration prayers; the Tibetan text gives no Sanskrit title. It is a short prayer in verse that follows the classic sequence of Mahāyāna devotion: homage to the buddhas, bodhisattvas, and śrāvakas and to the mind of awakening, confession of wrongdoing, dedication of merit as the seed of omniscience, rejoicing in the merit of others, and urging the bodhisattvas to attain awakening, turn the wheel of Dharma, and remain teaching for countless eons. It then voices aspirations to realize that phenomena are unborn, empty, and without self, and to complete each of the six perfections in turn, from generosity, moral discipline, patience, diligence, and meditative concentration through to wisdom. It closes by presenting this as the very conduct through which the renowned Maitreya perfected the six perfections and came to abide on the ten bodhisattva grounds.

The Noble Aspiration Prayer of Maitreya

I pay homage to all the buddhas.
To the bodhisattvas, sages
Endowed with the divine eye,
And to the śrāvakas I pay homage.

Turning back all the lower realms,
Clearly revealing the path to the higher realms,
Leading to the state beyond aging and death:
To the mind of awakening I pay homage.

Whatever wrongs I have committed
Through falling under the power of the mind,
Coming before the buddhas,
I shall confess them all.

Whatever accumulation of merit
I have generated through the three kinds of action,
As the seed of my omniscience,
May my awakening be inexhaustible.

In the realms of the ten directions,
Whatever offerings have been made to the buddhas,
With a rejoicing the buddhas know,
In that I rejoice.

I shall confess all wrongdoing;
I rejoice in all merit;
I pay homage to all the buddhas:
May I attain supreme primordial wisdom.

In all the quarters of the ten directions,
The bodhisattvas who abide on the ten grounds
I urge to awaken fully
To supreme awakening.

Having awakened to holy awakening
And tamed Māra together with his hosts,
For the good of all living creatures
May they turn the wheel of Dharma.

With the sound of the great drum of Dharma
May they free suffering beings;
For inconceivable tens of millions of eons
May they remain, teaching the Dharma.

Sunk in the mire of desire,
Tightly bound by the cords of existence,
Upon me, bound by every bond,
Please look, you who are supreme among two-footed beings.

Those whom the mind has stained
The buddhas do not condemn.
With loving hearts toward beings,
May they free them from the ocean of existence.

The perfect buddhas who are present now,
And those who have passed and those yet to come:
Following them may I train
And practice the conduct of awakening.

Having perfected the six perfections,
May I free the beings of the six destinies;
Having actualized the six higher knowledges,
May I reach unsurpassed awakening.

Unborn and non-arising,
Without intrinsic nature, without abode,
Without cognition, without substance:
May I realize empty phenomena.

Like the buddhas, the great sages,
Without sentient being, without life,
Without person, without nourisher,
May I realize phenomena devoid of self.

Not dwelling on any thing
Through grasping at self or at what is mine,
For the happiness of all beings
May I give freely, without stinginess.

Since things have no thingness,
May my wealth be spontaneously present;
Since all things perish,
May I perfect the perfection of generosity.

Faultless in the discipline of the precepts,
Endowed with pure moral discipline,
Through moral discipline free of conceit,
May I perfect the perfection of moral discipline.

Like earth, or else water or fire,
Or the element of wind, not abiding,
Without either patience or anger,
May I perfect the perfection of patience.

Through the diligence of undertaking diligence,
Steadfast, enthusiastic, and free of laziness,
With body and mind endowed with strength,
May I perfect the perfection of diligence.

Through the illusion-like samādhi,
The samādhi of heroic progress (śūraṅgama),
And the vajra-like samādhi,
May I perfect the perfection of meditative concentration.

By bringing to direct perception
The three gates of liberation, the equality of the three times,
And the three knowledges,
May I perfect the perfection of wisdom.

Praised by all the buddhas,
Blazing with light and splendor,
Through the diligence of a bodhisattva,
May my aspirations be fulfilled.

Practicing the conduct in this way,
Maitreya, endowed with renown,
Having perfected the six perfections,
Abides fully on the ten grounds.

The Noble Aspiration Prayer of Maitreya is complete.
