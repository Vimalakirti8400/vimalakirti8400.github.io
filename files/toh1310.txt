The Sādhana of the Single Heroine (Ekavīrāsādhana)

The Sādhana of the Single Heroine, known in Sanskrit as the Ekavīrā Sādhana and in Tibetan as Dpa' gcig ma'i sgrub thabs, is a brief tantric meditation manual devoted to a solitary wrathful goddess, black or blue in color, who holds a curved knife, a skull cup and a khaṭvāṅga and stands upon a corpse. After an homage to Vajrasattva and to the goddess, its author explains that he writes concisely, through the stages of generation and completion, and then guides the practitioner through preliminary worship of the guru, meditation on the divine abodes, dissolution into emptiness, the visualization of the deity arising from a seed syllable, and her recitation and torma mantras. It closes with a short teaching that saṃsāra itself, once fully understood, is nirvāṇa. Attributed to the master Rāhula and translated into Tibetan by the Indian preceptor Prajñendraruci and the monk Shakya Yeshé, it is Toh 1310 in the Tengyur, where it belongs to the section of tantra commentaries and ritual texts.

Herein is the Sādhana of the Single Heroine (Ekavīrā).

Homage to glorious Vajrasattva.

Innate, born together with the wisdom of the dharmadhātu,
one face, two arms, left leg extended, blue,
with the five ornaments, a garland of skulls, and yellow hair,
holding a curved knife and a skull in your hands: to you I bow.
For the sake of women of lesser understanding,
concisely, not at length,
through generation and completion,
I shall set down the Single Heroine.

First of all, the yogin, in a secluded place, sits in the sattva cross-legged posture. Having worshipped the guru in the sequence that has been taught, he cultivates the brahmavihāras, the divine abodes. Then: oṃ śūnyatājñānavajrasvabhāva ātmako'haṃ.

After that, through the power of aspiration,
in the center of a sun upon a red lotus,
from the seed of the supreme unborn
arises she who is black, with one face and two arms.
In her right hand is a curved knife, she who is greatly terrifying;
in her left is a lotus vessel;
on the side that is not the right, a khaṭvāṅga.
Her lower body is wrapped in a tiger skin.
She stands upon a corpse with her left leg extended.
Her yellow hair stands on end; with the five mudrās,
a garland of skulls, and ashes she is adorned.
After that, the wisdom being is established.
The six sense sources and body, speech and mind,
the empowerment, nectar and offerings are to be done.

oṃ āḥ aṃ hūṃ svāhā. This is the recitation mantra.

oṃ akāro mukhaṃ sarvadharmāṇāṃ ādyaṇutpannatvāta oṃ āḥ hūṃ phaṭ svāhā. This is the torma mantra.

At the navel of the goddess blazes a full moon,
and on it the syllable oṃ, the very substance of the five wisdoms,
the cause of both existence and nirvāṇa.
That very thing is called saṃsāra,
and that very thing is nirvāṇa.
When saṃsāra has been fully understood,
that is what is called nirvāṇa.
Just as a precious jewel,
when it is unpierced, cannot be strung,
and once it has been pierced can be strung,
such is the nature of saṃsāra:
so the gurus have taught.

The Sādhana of the Single Heroine, Endowed with Generation and Completion, composed by the master Rāhula the Almsman, is complete.

It was translated and finalized by the Indian preceptor Prajñendraruci and the monk Shakya Yeshé.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

"The seed of the supreme unborn" is the syllable A, the letter of non-arising, from which the deity is generated. The lotus vessel in her left hand is presumably the skull cup mentioned in the homage. The author's epithet "the Almsman" (bsod snyoms pa) is rendered by meaning; it may correspond to a Sanskrit epithet such as Piṇḍapa, which is not certain. The line about the women of lesser understanding could also be read as "for those of lesser understanding and for women".
