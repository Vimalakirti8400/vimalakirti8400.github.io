The Dhāraṇī of the Noble Compassionate Jambhala Jalendra Called "The Bestower of Happiness" (Kāruṇikasya Āryajambhalajalendrasuśaṃkaranāmadhāraṇī)

The Dhāraṇī of the Noble Jambhala Jalendra, the Compassionate One, Called The Bestower of Happiness, known in Sanskrit as Kāruṇikasya Āryajambhalajalendrasuśaṃkaranāmadhāraṇī and in Tibetan as 'Phags pa gnod 'dzin chu dbang snying rje can gyi gzungs bde byed ces bya ba, is a short dhāraṇī scripture devoted to Jambhala, the yakṣa deity associated with wealth and protection. It opens with the Buddha dwelling in the City of Jewels, in the abode of the son of the great yakṣa general Maṇibhadra, who is also called a bodhisattva. This figure offers to accompany and protect any monk, nun, layman, or laywoman who recites his heart mantra three times by day and three times by night. He promises to guard them from harmful yakṣas across a great distance and to fulfill their wishes. The work is Toh 971 in the Kangyur, where it belongs to the class of dhāraṇī texts, scriptures centered on mantras and incantations recited for blessing, protection, and the fulfillment of aims.

In the language of Tibet: The Dhāraṇī of the Noble Jambhala Jalendra, the Compassionate One, Called The Bestower of Happiness.

Homage to the Omniscient One.

Thus did I hear at one time. The Blessed One (the Buddha) was dwelling in the City of Jewels, in the abode of the son of the great yakṣa general, the bodhisattva Maṇibhadra.

Then the son of the great yakṣa general Maṇibhadra said this to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, if any monk, nun, layman or laywoman contemplates my heart mantra three times by day and three times by night, then, Blessed One, I will always follow that person. I will protect them from yakṣas for as far as a hundred leagues. I will completely fulfill all their wishes. Except for wrongdoing bound up with desire, I will grant them food, drink, snacks and meals, wealth, grain, valuables and gold."

As for the rite: if, on the fifteenth day of the waxing fortnight, one makes a maṇḍala with white sandalwood, burns aloeswood, and recites the mantra, then afterward one will obtain gold.

This concludes the dhāraṇī of the noble Jambhala Jalendra, the Compassionate One, called The Bestower of Happiness.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The opening phrase can also be read as "the son of Maṇibhadra, the great yakṣa general and bodhisattva," with the titles applying to the son, who is Jambhala, the speaker. The Tibetan is ambiguous. Jalendra ("Lord of Waters") is the Sanskrit epithet given in the Indian title (spelled there jalandra). The title "Bestower of Happiness" renders the Tibetan for Skt. Suśaṃkara and is kept in translation as the title of the work. "City of Jewels" is translated literally. Its Sanskrit form (possibly Maṇipura) is not certain.
