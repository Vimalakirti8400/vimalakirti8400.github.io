The Sūtra of the Great Assembly (Mahāsamayasūtra)

The Sūtra of the Great Assembly, known in Sanskrit as the Mahāsamayasūtra and in Tibetan as Düpa Chenpo'i Do (འདུས་པ་ཆེན་པོའི་མདོ), is a canonical Buddhist sūtra recounting a vast celestial gathering in which the gods of ten world-systems converge to behold the Buddha and his community of five hundred arhats in the Śākya land. The text celebrates this great assembly by enumerating the many divine beings who come to pay homage, making it a remarkable panoramic vision of the Buddhist cosmos and its inhabitants. It belongs to the genre of sūtra literature and is catalogued as Toh 34 in the Kangyur, the Tibetan canon of texts attributed to the Buddha himself.

Thus did I hear. At one time the Blessed One was dwelling in the great grove of the Śākya land called Sers-kya, together with a great Saṅgha of five hundred monks, all of them arhats. The gods of the ten world-systems assembled at once in order to behold the Blessed One and the Saṅgha of monks.

Then the four orders of gods of the Pure Abode spoke as follows: "Here too the Blessed One dwells in the great grove of the Śākya land called Sers-kya, together with a great Saṅgha of five hundred monks, all of them arhats. The gods of the ten world-systems have assembled at once in order to behold the Blessed One and the Saṅgha of monks. Those who wish to go before the Blessed One should go there and, having gone, recite verses separately in the presence of the Blessed One."

Then those gods, just as a strong man might extend a bent arm or bend an extended arm, vanished from the Pure Abode and appeared before the Blessed One.
