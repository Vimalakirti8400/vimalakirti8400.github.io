The Noble Dhāraṇī Called "Circumambulating the Support of the Three Jewels" (Ārya-ratnatraya-pradakṣiṇa-nāma-dhāraṇī)

The Noble Dhāraṇī Called "Circumambulating the Support of the Three Jewels" (Tibetan: ’Phags pa dkon mchog gi rten la bskor ba bya ba’i gzungs; Sanskrit: Ārya-ratnatraya-pradakṣiṇa-nāma-dhāraṇī) is a short dhāraṇī text, a brief mantra-formula presented as spoken by the Buddha Śākyamuni. It concerns a practice of recitation that stands in for physically circumambulating the Three Jewels (the Buddha, the Dharma, and the Saṅgha), and it promises that a single recitation pays homage to the Three Jewels of the ten directions and three times, purifies negative deeds accumulated since beginningless time, and brings swift awakening, along with protection from enemies and obstructors. In the Sanskrit language: Ārya-ratnatraya-pradakṣiṇa-nāma-dhāraṇī. In the Tibetan language: The Noble Dhāraṇī Called "Circumambulating the Support of the Three Jewels."

Homage to the Blessed One, the Tathāgata, the arhat, the perfectly and completely awakened buddha, Śākyamuni.

Oṃ namo daśa-dik-trikāla sarva-ratna-trayāya, mama pratyakṣa supratyakṣa, sarva-pāpaṃ viśodhani svāhā.

By reciting this dhāraṇī just once, one will have paid homage to, and circumambulated, the Three Jewels of the ten directions and the three times. All the negative deeds accumulated since beginningless time will be purified. One will swiftly attain the awakening of a buddha. Enemies and obstructors
