Ascertaining the Signs

Ascertaining the Signs, known in Tibetan as Brda nges par gzung ba, is a short tantric treatise that opens with homage to glorious Hevajra and explains how practitioners should understand the secret signs used within the Hevajra tradition. Its Sanskrit title is not given in the opening lines. The work teaches that these signs should be understood first by their literal sound and then according to the oral instructions. It explains them as the conduct of body and speech, where the body is linked with the vajra and the lotus and speech with the letters that bless the secret space. It also explores the meaning of calling fellow initiates brother and sister, reading the terms as companions who share the same samaya, as hero and yoginī, as method and wisdom, and as vajra and lotus. The text is Toh 1214 in the Tengyur, where it belongs to the commentarial literature on the tantras, specifically the body of works related to the Hevajra cycle.

Ascertaining the Signs

Homage to glorious Hevajra.

The signs are to be ascertained. First one should understand them according to their literal sound, and then one should understand them according to the sequence of the oral instructions. "Sign" means the conduct of body and speech. Body is the vajra and the lotus, and its conduct is the conduct of these. Speech is the letters, and blessing the secret space with them is its conduct. The meaning is this: if one knows such signs, one is certainly a brother or a sister. "Brother" means a man who shares the same samaya. "Sister" means a woman who shares the same samaya. Or else "brother and sister" are the hero and the yoginī. Or else "brother and sister" are method and wisdom. Or else "brother and sister" are the vajra and the lotus, and to know this is to be without doubt.

"Showing one finger" means showing the left little finger, and it says: "Are you endowed with the profound meaning?" "With two" says: "I am endowed with the definitive meaning." "Of the left" means wisdom, that is, the lotus. "Thumb" means the vajra held by the lotus, that is, entering into union. "Of well-being" says: "I am endowed with the bliss born of the vajra and the lotus." "Ring finger" is the vajra, because it makes the lotus expand. "To giving" means to showing that. "Little finger" is the lotus, because it is the lower part, and it says that this should be shown. "Middle finger" is the knowledge of the Great Seal, which frees one from both extremes, the extreme of the nirvāṇa of the śrāvakas and others and the extreme of saṃsāra. "Index finger" is the vajra, because it has the power to threaten, and it says that this should be shown. "To showing the ring finger" says: "Are you a woman endowed with devotion?" "Showing the throat" says: "I do so with reverence; I offer my head." "To showing the garment" says: "Are the obscurations purified?" "One should show the trident" says: "I am endowed with body, speech and mind; the obscurations are purified." "To showing the breast" says: "Are you endowed with the aspect of bodhicitta?" "The cleft" is the aspect of bodhicitta, and it says: "Practice at the time of the fifteenth and the fourteenth day." Or else "breast" is the vajra, because it has become the path of the aspect of semen, and the phrase means to showing that. "To the cleft" is the lotus, and it says: "open it." "Ground" is the lotus that is the support of joy and the rest. "To showing" says: "Make it expand!" "Mouth" says: "Having blessed it with hūṃ and the rest, one should make it expand." "To showing a frown" says: "Do you obey my command?" "One should loosen the topknot" says: "I obey the command." "To showing the forehead" and "one should show the back" are meant literally. "Sole of the foot" is the lotus: since the letter pa is the first letter of the word padma, one should erase the ta and understand it as ma. Therefore the eight-petalled lotus is what is called "sole of the foot." "To showing" says: "Unite with it!" "One should sport with a stone" says: "One should generate with the vajra endowed with hūṃ phaṭ."

"There are twelve grounds" refers to the path of accumulation, the path of preparation, the path of seeing, the path of meditation and the path of liberation. Therefore joy, supreme joy and the rest, which have become the four paths, are the twelve grounds. "Lord of the ten grounds" means the yogin endowed with intelligence; therefore one should proceed by means of joy, supreme joy and the rest. "The fortnight of the pretas" means the eighth and the fourteenth day of the waning half of the month. "Of the good gathering" means gathering at the places of meeting, secondary meeting and so on. Or else "the fortnight of the pretas" is the jewel portion of the vajra, and the gathering there of the aspect of bodhicitta is the gathering of the yoginīs. Or else "the fortnight of the pretas" is the joy of cessation, and the yoginīs gather there.

"Banner" means impaled on a stake. "Slain by a weapon" means struck with a sword or the like; the sense is that the yogin should eat its flesh for the sake of accomplishment. A further explanation: "banner" means that the life force has been drawn in prior to non-arising. "Slain by a weapon" means that after non-arising it has become the element of space. "Seven times" means one who has been born as a human for seven births; the sense is that its flesh, being free of fault, should be eaten. A further explanation: "seven times" is the bodhisattva. The gnosis of self-aware bliss, comprised by the joy of cessation, is awakening, and one who brings it to mind is a bodhisattva. "Should be eaten" means that one should meditate on it for the sake of the innate accomplishment.

e means the lotus, because it has the nature of the three doors of liberation. vaṃ is the vajra, meaning the lord of the family. "Should be looked upon" means: having abandoned ordinary form, bless it. e means that one should be passionately attached. Therefore "day" is conventional form, the aspect of the moon; that is the Blessed One, meaning method. "Night" is the ultimate nature, the nature of bliss; that is wisdom, meaning signless knowledge.

"The flesh of whichever ones" is the flesh of those killed by weapons in battle. "If one eats that, whatever is connected with it comes under one's power" means that their family comes under one's power. Or else "flesh" means the conception that clings to duality. "Eat" means abandoning the clinging to duality. "That family of theirs comes under one's power" means that non-dual non-apprehension becomes directly manifest.

In another explanation, "flesh" is the fluid of the bhaga. "Eat" means that the yogin should take it up with the tongue, and take it in through the samādhi of nectar. "That comes under one's power" means that the yoginī becomes compliant.

This was revised by the great lama, the royal lord Yeshé Gyaltsen.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

This is a commentary on the code-language (chomā) passages of the Hevajra Tantra; quoted words are lemmata from the root verses. "Of well-being" renders bsnyun gyi, taken as the root text's inquiry after health; the sense is somewhat uncertain. The letter-play on "sole of the foot" (Skt. pādatala) is obscure: the text says to erase ta and understand ma, apparently turning the word into padma. The passage lists five paths and then refers to "the four paths"; translated as it stands. "Secret space" renders mkha' gsang, which may also be read as "space and secret," that is, lotus and vajra.  "Bhaga" is the female sexual organ, as the term is used in the Hevajra exegetical tradition. In the colophon, the phrase "bla chen gyi la" is unclear as written. It has been taken loosely as "the great lama," but the syllable "la" may be a fragment or a scribal slip. It may also be a dative particle, which would give "for the great lama." "Lha mnga' bdag" is rendered as the royal title "royal lord," following the use of "lha" as a royal honorific. Yeshé Gyaltsen is a Tibetan, not an Indian, name, so it is given in phonetic Tibetan.
