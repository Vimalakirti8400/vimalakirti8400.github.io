A Means of Accomplishment Following the Condensed Maṇḍala of the Glorious Ḍākinī Vajra Tent (Śrīḍākinīvajrapañjarasaṃharaṇamaṇḍalānusāreṇasādhana)

A Means of Accomplishment Following the Condensed Maṇḍala of the Glorious Ḍākinī Vajra Tent, known in Sanskrit as the Śrī Ḍākinī Vajrapañjara Maṇḍala Sādhana and in Tibetan as Pal Khandroma Dorjé Gur gyi Kyilkhor Düpa la Jesu Drangwé Drubtap, is a ritual meditation manual, or sādhana, for the practice of Hevajra and his retinue of goddesses as taught in the Ḍākinī Vajrapañjara, the Vajra Tent tantra closely linked to the Hevajra cycle. After paying homage to Vajraḍāka, Hevajra and Vajraḍākinī, the author sets down the oral instructions for generating the condensed maṇḍala, inviting and empowering its deities, making offerings and praises, tasting the inner nectar, and practicing the subtle yogas, mantra recitation and the six-branched yoga, before closing with prayers of dedication. According to its colophon it was composed by an Indian master whose name in Tibetan means Divine Observance, and it was translated into Tibetan by Marpa Chökyi Lodrö. It is Toh 1322 in the Tengyur, where it belongs among the commentaries and practice texts on the tantras.

In the language of India: śrī ḍākinī vajrapañjara saṃharaṇa maṇḍala nurenā sādhanaṃ. In the language of Tibet: A Means of Accomplishment Following the Condensed Maṇḍala of the Glorious Ḍākinī Vajra Tent.

Homage to the Blessed One Vajraḍāka.

You who, through the essence practiced as one and as many, pacify confusion about your own accomplishment;
Born from union with the limbs of Wisdom, clear and ablaze with light at the opening of the vajra's tip;
Who, making compassion dawn as a pure wheel, are bound by your vowed conduct to the welfare of beings;
Adorned with the characteristics of truth: to Hevajra I bow with the crown of my head.

Playful, with lotus feet,
To Vajraḍākinī I pay homage.
The pith instructions of the sādhana
I shall write down so that I may remember them.

Then, furthermore, the mantra practitioner who, focusing on the many suffering beings, enters into the fully developed savor of compassion; who, by examining self and others, has come to be wholly expansive toward beings; whose mind stream is completely purified in the ten aspects beginning with generosity; who is free of faults and the like in pleasing the guru by the three ways; whose mind is ever driven on by the power of diligence in accomplishing perfect and complete awakening; and who has received empowerment in the maṇḍala of the Glorious Ḍākinī Tent and obtained permission according to the rite, should rise at the beginning of the period between sessions. Having arranged and set down the sublime first seed syllable upon a sun disk arisen from the letter ra in his own heart, he should, in some place such as a charnel ground, make a square maṇḍala of sandalwood and the like. Then, on a spot of ground beautified with various flowers, he sits upon a human hide or the like in the vajra cross-legged posture. Sitting with the leaves of his hands gathered perfectly in the mudrā of meditative absorption, he should visualize, by means of the masses of light rays from the syllable hūṃ, the guru, the buddhas, and the bodhisattvas abiding in the expanse of the sky, and make offerings to them and so forth.

The offering there is as follows:

Revealing themselves by way of light,
Lotus-handed and full of grace,
The goddesses of Charm and the rest
Should make offering in the proper way.

The wrongs and so forth born in beginningless existence
Should be confessed according to the rite:
In the presence of the hosts of the Sugatas
I confess my heap of wrongdoing.

In the excellent merit
Of the Sages and the others with their heirs,
And of all the beings of the three worlds,
I rejoice with faith.

To the Victors, who have utterly vanquished
All conceptual thought, I offer myself.
With one mind set on the awakening mind
That surely destroys what makes for existence,
In all ten directions, without fail,
I shall turn the wheel of Dharma.

In these peaceful Three Jewels
From this very moment I take refuge.
The precious path that benefits
The beings of the three worlds I shall follow.

Things are empty of intrinsic nature,
Signless and without cause;
Because, on examination, one is thereby freed,
One comes to be parted from all wishing.

Having thus contemplated all things
As clear and like an illusion,
Ignorance and the other faults
Cease through its cessation.

Reciting oṃ śūnyatā jñāna vajra svabhāva ātmako'haṃ, one should take on the divine pride.

If you desire Body and Speech themselves, by the power amassed through merit and wisdom as examined,
Then imagine a terrifying vajra with spreading prongs, resting on a sun, born from the seed hūṃ and marked with it;
From this, contemplate well the four, the fence and the rest, as arising.
From the rays of bhruṃ comes a ten-spoked wheel; having set it turning yet steady as though unmoving,
Upon it, on lotus and sun, generate the peerless Yamāntaka and the rest from hūṃ and from their emblems.
Imagine them sporting with the left leg extended, trampling down the deceitful ones.
In its center is Vajrahūṃkāra, born from hūṃ and a vajra,
His body beautiful like a mass the color of sapphire.

Adorned with ornaments of varied garments,
Terrifying and full of grace,
His lotus hands are crossed,
Making the supreme mudrā at his heart.
In his jeweled diadem
The Vajra of Mind is seated.

The full round breasts of the goddess Umā,
And Hatred bearing the sign, at the place drawn tight upon the sublime limb marked by the mountain of the faithless foe,
He tramples firmly with his lotus feet, the left leg extended, full of grace:
He is the wholly pure wisdom, as has been described.

Yamāntaka, Aparājita, Hayagrīva, and Amṛtakuṇḍalin: these heroes are blue, white, red, and dark as eye ointment. They have reddish yellow beards, eyebrows flashing like lightning, and hair bristling upward, and their bodies are adorned as though with a great forest. Their bellies bulge and their bodies are short; their lower bodies are wrapped in tiger skins and they carry lion skins; and their bodies are beautified by nāgas spitting blazing poison from their mouths. In their lotus hands are a hammer, a club, a lotus, and a crossed vajra. The crowns of their heads are adorned with Vairocana, the Vajra of Mind, Amitāyus, and Amoghasiddhi. They should be visualized in the east, the south, the west, and the north.

In the corners of Agni, Nairṛti, Vāyu, and Īśāna are Ṭakkirāja, Nīladaṇḍa, Mahābala, and Acala, blue in color. A hook, a blue staff, a trident, and a sword are, in order, in the leaves of their hands. Their heads are adorned with Akṣobhya and the others, and they are adorned with fine jewels. Mahābala and Nīladaṇḍa too are wrathful, baring their fangs, just like Yamāntaka.

Above is Uṣṇīṣacakravartin, arisen from bhrūṃ and a wheel, the color of refined gold, beautiful with a wheel in his hand, adorned with jewels and the like, with Vairocana abiding in his jeweled diadem. Abiding below is Sumbha, clear blue and holding his implement, with Akṣobhya abiding in his jeweled diadem, and bearing ornaments of many kinds.

All of them have the light of a sun disk. They are the purification of the ten knowledges, beginning with the knowledge of suffering, and they subdue the maṇḍala of Māra.

The left hands of all of them should be imagined holding a nāga noose and making the threatening gesture at the heart. Of these, whichever one wishes should be made the chief of the protection circle.

Then the chief of the protection circle melts and becomes a ball of moonlight. One should imagine an upward-pointing triangle, beautifully formed and brilliantly clear on all its sides, and within it, as the purified nature of inner space, the vajra lotus, the source of phenomena (dharmodaya). There, by the seed syllables yaṃ raṃ vaṃ laṃ, the maṇḍalas of wind, fire, water, and earth are each to be made in their order; the meaning is that the elements and the faculties are contemplated as pure. There one imagines Vairocana, arisen from bhrūṃ and a wheel, and from the mass into which the maṇḍalas of wind and the rest have merged comes the abode that is the purification of the qualities of all the Tathāgatas, a form of one taste with all the three realms, and the perfection of the accumulations of merit and wisdom. With oṃ sarva tathāgata jñāna alaya vajra svabhāva ātmako'haṃ one should take on the divine pride and meditate on the tiered celestial palace.

Above its center one should see the āli and the kāli doubled, and through their melting completely, the disks of the moon and the sun. These should be well understood to be by nature the mirror-like wisdom and the wisdom of equality, and the purified thirty-two marks of a great being and eighty excellent minor marks. That the lotus vessels are marked with the sublime crossed vajra born from the seed syllables hūṃ and a is by nature the wisdom that discerns all things and objects individually. Its light rays go forth as hosts of sages, utterly pacify the suffering of all beings, and return and enter: this is the wisdom that accomplishes activities. As one whose nature is utterly pure, one should take on the divine pride with vajra ātmako'haṃ. These should be understood as the elements and as the nature of the five faculties, the limbs made pure. As it is also said:

Having completed the four abodes of the Buddha,
One should meditate on the five aspects.

This statement teaches that the five faculties are endowed with the signs and so on.

Such a Blessed One is peerless. The radiance of the limbs of his body, composed by gathering together the subtle particles of the hosts of Victors, is like the autumn moon; he is everywhere equal to space, and he arises from the nature in which emptiness and compassion are inseparable. His three faces are blue, red, and white, and the five goddesses are black. One should contemplate them as the great Vajradhara, the very embodiment of supreme bliss.

With the masses of light from his heart, the hosts of Tathāgatas are invited and absorbed; then, sending them forth as the goddesses of form and the rest, one should make them enter one's own goddess. In the center of her lotus one completes the maṇḍala of glorious Heruka, and the gandharva being, known in the world to be like a shadow and an illusion, is invited and made to enter, and so is empowered. Then, abiding together in the center of the goddess's lotus, one contemplates the three syllables placed between two blazing syllables hoḥ. Seeing the syllables of great passion melt into light and become like quicksilver, one should imagine being urged on by Pukkasī and the others, established from beginningless time, who are like dreams and illusions:

You, pure Mind, who wholly guard against the faults of the vajra,
Excellent Body, richly adorned with the dharmas through and through,
Look upon me, Pukkasī, today; cast emptiness aside,
And arise, O Guide, born from the cause of the world.

You, the Body that wholly purifies the vajra of delusion,
The Dharma Body, richly adorned with dharmas that are like reflections,
Look upon me, Śavarī, O Protector; cast emptiness aside,
And arise, Protector and Guide, born from the cause of the world.

You, pure Speech, who wholly guard the vajra of passion,
Excellent Body, richly adorned with certain discernment,
Look upon me, Caṇḍālī, today; cast emptiness aside,
And arise, Protector and Guide, born from the cause of the world.

You, the form of all, who guard the buddhas,
Richly adorned with the sameness of all things,
Look upon me, Ḍombī, today; cast emptiness aside,
And arise, O Guide, born from the cause of the world.

So they sing. Then, with a mind whose intention has first given rise to such great compassion, he looks upon the yogin who has fallen from the drop. One imagines the lotus vessel marked with the seed syllables hūṃ and aṃ becoming marked with a crossed vajra. The five kinds of samādhi, beginning with the one accompanied by conceptual thought, are first set in place as the emanating and gathering that occur by their own nature, and one makes them stable there. By this there arises glorious Heruka, the body that is the purity of all the Tathāgatas. With his four feet he tramples the four māras. His sixteen hands, like the limbs of a forest, hold hosts of skull cups. His body and his root face are blue like a rain cloud at the time of the world's destruction. The lotus faces to the right and to the left have a light like that of the full moon and of the newly risen sun. The topmost one is fiercely snarling. The remaining four are utterly terrifying, black like the bodies of bees. He is beautified by a garland of twenty-four eyes and sits in the dance of the half cross-legged posture. His lower body is wrapped in a tiger skin, and his reddish yellow locks blaze and bristle upward like a forest. One should imagine him, the supremely excellent Vajradhara, adorned with the five mudrās, utterly beautiful.

Cough, ailments of the breath, and fever,
Coughing blood, leprosy, and pustules,
Fragrance sickness and liver sickness are their natures:
Elephant, horse, donkey, and ox,
Camel, human, and king of lions,
And likewise the cat:
These are the eight skull cups on the right,
To be known in order, beginning with the Twice-Drinker, the elephant.

Earth, water, and wind,
Fire, the moon, and the sun,
Antaka and Dhanada:
These are in the eight skull cups on the left,
To be known in order, beginning with earth;
They are the purification of Īśvara and the rest.

The true meditation on Samantabhadra
Is the means of accomplishing one's own chosen deity.

By this statement one should focus on the five meditative concentrations, beginning with the one accompanied by conceptual thought. Having wholly abandoned the accumulations of existence and of nirvāṇa, since emptiness and compassion are inseparable, one should take on the pride of the dharmadhātu, with oṃ dharma dhatu svabhāva ātmako'haṃ.

After that, in the eyes, the ears, the nose, the tongue, the body, and the heart are moon disks. Upon them are the seed syllables oṃ hūṃ trāṃ hrīḥ khaṃ hūṃ and curved knives, and from their complete melting arise those who are white, blue, yellow, red, green, and the color of the sky: Delusion, Hatred, Avarice, Slander, Passion, Envy, and Vajranairātmyā. They hold a curved knife and a skull cup, and are to be set in place standing upon corpses.

At the heart, the throat, and the crown are the seed syllables hūṃ aṃ oṃ. One arranges the sky-goers, the earth-goers, and those who move below the earth, arisen from them, blue, red, and white, and, having first performed the emanation and the rest, one should bless them. With oṃ khecari citta vajra svabhāva atmako'haṃ, oṃ bhūcari vāka vajra svabhāva ātmako'haṃ, oṃ pātāla vāsini kāya vajra svabhāva ātmako'haṃ, one takes on the divine pride.

Then, in one's own heart, the samaya being is just as before. With the masses of light from the emblem and the seed syllable at the heart of the wisdom being, one makes offerings to the hosts of sages. From the light radiating from them, one sends forth hosts of goddesses who hold in their lotus hands vases filled with the water of the awakening mind, and they empower oneself. One should take on the divine pride thus, reciting oṃ sarva tathāgata abhiśeka vajra svabhāva ātmako'haṃ. One should imagine being empowered by the five Tathāgatas as one's crown ornament.

Joy, supreme joy, the joy of separation, and innate joy are the body of the four limbs. The varied, the crushing, and the one free of characteristics are the liberation of the forms of those of lesser intelligence and the rest.

Before him is Vajranairātmyā, who has become the central channel. In her right, creeper-like hand she holds a curved knife and points her forefinger threateningly at the wicked. With her left creeper-like arm, holding a skull cup filled with the blood of gods and asuras, she embraces the neck of the Lord of Victors. She has one face.

Her purification is as follows. The syllables oṃ hūṃ aṃ svāhā are the very nature of the families of body, mind, jewel, red lotus, and action; with them one should bless the head, the heart, the navel, the secret place, the two thighs, and the limbs. With the seed syllables hūṃ aṃ one should purify the lotus and its center. By hūṃ aṃ the vajra and the jewel are generated. Yellow syllables phaṭ are at their openings.

Then one should take on the divine pride with oṃ sarva tathāgatānurāgaṇa vajra svabhāva ātmako'haṃ, and by the rite in which the Sugatas enter oneself, one should engage in passionate union. These make up the first yoga.

With the net of light from the seed syllable at the heart one invites the hosts of sages and absorbs them into oneself. One makes stable the wheel of yoginīs generated by churning with the churning stick at the center of the goddess's lotus; then, holding to the guru's instruction with understanding, one should emanate them everywhere.

There, from the complete melting of the seed gaṃ, comes Gaurī, like the moon. In her right hand she holds a hook, and with her left lotus hand she diligently points her forefinger threateningly at the wicked. She is the purity of the mirror-like wisdom, of the aggregate of form, and of the three kinds of faith. Gaurī should be placed at the eastern gate.

Likewise, from the seed caṃ comes Caurī, like refined gold, with the vajra noose of great life in her right hand. She is placed at the southern gate, and one should imagine her as having the form of the purified wisdom of equality, aggregate of feeling, and diligence.

From the seed vaṃ comes Vetālī, like the rising sun, beautiful of body, whose nature is the purity of the aggregate of perception and of the discerning wisdom. In the leaf of her right hand she holds a vajra chain, binding perfect and complete awakening. She is at the western gate.

From the seed ghaṃ comes Ghasmarī, her body exquisitely beautiful and clear like a green and yellow jewel. In the leaf of her right hand she rings a vajra bell. Her nature is the purity of the aggregate of formations, the wisdom that accomplishes activities, and samādhi. She is at the northern gate.

In the corners of Īśāna, Agni, Nairṛti, and Vāyu are Pukkasī, Śavarī, Caṇḍālī, and Ḍombī, colored blue, white, dark blue, and many colored.

Of these, from the seed paṃ comes Pukkasī, holding a queen of lions and the bell of the perfect awakening mind. Her nature is the earth element, great compassion, and the rest.

From the seed śaṃ comes Śavarī, holding in her two hands a monk abiding in a lotus vessel, and a hare. Her nature is the water element, great love, and the rest.

From the seed caṃ comes Caṇḍālī, in playful pose, holding in the leaves of her hands a wheel and a hearth. Her nature is the purity of the fire element and of great joy.

By the seed ḍaṃ comes Ḍombī, holding a great vajra banner in both her right and left hands. She has the form of the purity of the wind element, great equanimity, and the rest.

They stand upon sun disks on variegated lotuses. Beneath the soles of their lotus feet, pressed down in the playful posture with the left leg extended, are those whose nature is Brahmā, Śakra, Viṣṇu, Rudra, Yama, Kubera, Nairṛti, and Vemacitrin. They are adorned with ornaments of human bone; their reddish yellow hair blazes upward, and they have skulls upon their heads. With great compassion they strive supremely for the welfare of beings; they are graceful with supreme joy, and they have three eyes.

Having thus gazed upon the maṇḍala, complete in every respect, one draws in the dhāraṇī of the maṇḍala of the hosts of Victors with the hook of light from the seed syllable at the heart. Then, looking toward the side of the eastern gate, one should offer the welcoming water with oṃ arghaṃ pratīccha svāhā. With oṃ pādyaṃ pratīccha svāhā one offers water to cool the feet, and, offering flowers as seats, one should worship them properly.

Then with oṃ vajra gaurī ākarṣaya jaḥ, oṃ vajra caurī praveśaya hūṃ, oṃ vajra vetālī bandha vaṃ and oṃ vajra ghasmarī tośaya hoḥ, by these they are summoned, made to enter, bound and pleased. Having performed the blessing of the eyes and so forth, and the blessing of body, speech and mind, then, for the sake of conferring empowerment, one should imagine on the crowns of Vairocana, Ratnasambhava, Amitābha, the Vajrin, and Pukkasī and the others, as their crown ornaments, Mind, Body, Vajra Jewel and Vajra Amitāyus.

Then, with the hook of the light rays of the seed syllable at the heart, one summons Gaurī and the others and draws them in, imagines them like wish-fulfilling jewels in the center of the lotus of one's own goddess, and, holding the breath a little, contemplates them. In this way, from the pores of one's own consort the hosts of goddesses, Gaurī and the others, emerge, holding in their lotus hands transformed vessels filled with the moon and the other substances. They completely fill the whole expanse of the sky, and the wise regard them as like an illusion and so forth, and should be freed from the conceptual thought that divides. With oṃ sarva tathāgata pūja vajra svabhāva ātmako'haṃ one takes on the pride of that.

Then one should see the whole host, like echoes, offering praise:

You are surrounded by the host of yoginīs;
you are endowed with what your own mind has fully attained;
you purify all beings, the moving and the unmoving,
and toward sentient beings you exert the mind's power of compassion.
Amid the elaborations of illusion you increase the vajra.
How wonderful! We praise glorious Heruka, the Vajra of Mind, the one who has attained.

With oṃ sarva tathāgata stuti vajra svabhāva ātmako'haṃ one takes on the pride.

After that comes a wind maṇḍala arisen from aṃ and marked with yaṃ. Above it is a fire maṇḍala arisen from raṃ and marked with raṃ. Above it is a lotus vessel arisen from ā and marked with yaṃ. Above that, the five nectars and the lamps, marked by the Tathāgatas, are kindled, and by the radiating of light they are freed of their impurities. Then from hūṃ comes dhaṃ marked with hūṃ, and it is blessed by something like the appearance of crystal. Upon this, with the syllable oṃ, the nature of the nectar of wisdom is drawn in; one regards it as nondual, possessing the supreme taste of mantra, of the nature of the power to ripen. Imagining on the tongue a vajra marked with hūṃ, one fully arranges the maṇḍala in the drop of the seed syllable at the heart and should satisfy it completely. With the mantra oṃ vajra jihva hūṃ the tongue is blessed. With oṃ sarva tathāgata amṛta āsatva vajra svabhāva ātmako'haṃ one should be endowed with pride. These are the meditative absorption of the Supreme King of the Maṇḍala.

With the light rays of the heart all the Tathāgatas are made into the form of glorious Heruka and dissolve into the maṇḍala. The deities of the maṇḍala dissolve into oneself; oneself into the wisdom being; the wisdom being into the emblem; the emblem into the seed syllable; and the seed syllable into the drop. One imagines that the drop is, in a single instant, fully completed as the wheel of the maṇḍala, and the emanating and the rest are done in the same way. This is the yoga of the drop.

All sentient beings are made to enter all the Tathāgatas, and when these melt, at the tip of the center of the lotus, in the midst of one's own emblem, within something of the five jewels the size of a white mustard seed, the wheel of the maṇḍala is completed. Then, steady and firm, one should contemplate it until the five signs arise. After that, in the subsequent yoga, there is emanating and gathering and so forth. This is the subtle yoga. Accomplishing wisdom in a moment and emanating hosts of infinite light rays, one should meditate for as long as it takes until this becomes perfectly complete, so that oneself and others are seen.

If one grows weary, then, without distinction of day and night, one should do mantra recitation according to the oral instructions. If one is unable even to do that mantra recitation, one should recite with oṃ dhara dhāraya marda marda vajra dhṛka hūṃ phaṭ. If weary, one should bring delight into it: from the syllable oṃ resting on a moon at the crown of the head a stream of nectar trickles down, and one should be fully satisfied by it.

Having sported, blissful and delighting, with the songs of the goddesses,
one should imagine the offerings and the rest.
Whenever one wishes to rise,
without distraction of speech and body,
having worshipped and praised this wheel,
the wise one rises and works for the benefit of beings.
The five sense pleasures, pure from the beginning and stainless,
forms and the rest, as the very goddesses, or as the chosen deity Vajradhara,
or as Body Vajra and the others, the abodes of the Sugatas: here one should engage with them.
Drinking the supreme nectar whose essence is compassion, ever truly blissful,
do all of this without error, this which carries one truly to nirvāṇa.
To bring faults to exhaustion, oneself, mastered by the taste of compassion, should meditate on glorious Karmavajra.
At the heart, on a moon, one imagines a crossed vajra born from the syllable khaṃ and marked with it;
the goddesses who arise from the mass of light rays streaming from it confer empowerment.
Abiding in the wheel in the form of the sound of the Tathāgatas, one should utterly destroy all obstructors.
The lord, rising at dawn, should do the offerings and the rest, recitation and meditation, according to the rite.
In the evening, having drawn the wheel into the mind, one should always sleep with awareness.
All virtue too is always dedicated as the cause of perfect awakening for sentient beings.
At midday and at midnight too one rises, and likewise one should contemplate extensively.
The six branches and the three meditative absorptions should be meditated upon in this way.

If one asks how the six branches are to be meditated upon, it is said:

Having completed the four buddha abodes,
one should meditate on the five aspects.
Having meditated on the five aspects,
one should meditate on Samantabhadra.
The means of accomplishing one's own special deity,
and likewise the maṇḍala, should be set out;
offering, praise, tasting the nectar and the rest:
meditating on these in their sequence
is held to be the six-branched yoga.

So it is taught. Here too, some hold that one arises as the very nature of the maṇḍala of Samantabhadra. Others hold that one is born from the seminal fluid. Some hold that the resultant entity is the very nature of the maṇḍala. The three meditative absorptions should be understood here.

Next, if a yogin wishes to meditate on the whole of the condensed maṇḍala, then, in meditating on the five maṇḍalas, each with its own shape and seats, arising from the melting of Vairocana, the manner of the fivefold full awakening at the abodes of the five principal deities is the same, but their respective seed syllables, emblems and colors differ. From these one generates the five causal Blessed Ones. When, according to the rite, they have melted, they are roused by the goddesses of the respective intermediate directions with words of song that come from the tantra. Then again, from their respective emblems and seed syllables, their respective bodies are completed just as taught. One performs for them the separate branches of blessing the lotus and so forth and of blessing the secret place. Then, in keeping with the way the central maṇḍala is brought forth, one completes the goddesses of each maṇḍala from the first letters of their own names, possessing the hand emblems, colors and shapes just as taught, and should set them in their respective places.

Having dissolved the wisdom maṇḍala into them, everything from blessing the eyes and so forth up to tasting the nectar is in general the same. Alternatively, from the secret place of the consort of the Heruka of Aversion, one draws them forth, together with their respective principal deities, from their own seed syllables; the rest accords with the above.

The seed syllables are these. bhrūṃ āṃ jrīṃ khaṃ are those of the four principal deities. saṃ paṃ baṃ aṃ are those of the four gatekeeper goddesses. paṃ duṃ daṃ maṃ are those of the goddesses of the intermediate directions. suṃ duṃ raṃ taṃ are those of the gatekeeper goddesses of Vajrasūrya. laṃ haṃ nraṃ griṃ are those of the goddesses of the intermediate directions. paṃ daṃ ya ṣlaṃ are those of the four gatekeeper goddesses of Padmanarteśvara. baṃ biṃ muṃ muṃ are those of the intermediate directions. taṃ kuṃ kaṃ paṃ are those of the gatekeeper goddesses of Paramāśva. laṃ maṃ paṃ taṃ are those of the intermediate directions. Hevajra is very clear. If one recites a garland of mantras, there are the heart mantras and the near-heart mantras; these are taught in the tantra.

The verse on what Vajrahūṃkāra tramples is very compressed: the goddess Umā and a figure called Hatred bearing the sign, evidently her consort Śiva, are under his feet, but the second line could not be construed with confidence and is rendered closely. The "Vajra of Mind" in his diadem, and again among the crown ornaments of the four door guardians, is translated literally; it most likely refers to Akṣobhya. The seat "great field" is taken in its tantric sense of a human hide. For Sumbha the Tibetan says only "holding," without naming the object. "The ten knowledges" are the ten kinds of knowledge beginning with knowledge of suffering.  The four singers are the Hevajra goddesses Pukkasī, Śavarī, Caṇḍālī, and Ḍombī, here named by their Tibetan renderings. In their songs the verb skyong, "guard," may be a slip for sbyong, "purify," which the second stanza has in the parallel line; it has been kept as written. The "fragrance sickness" in the list of illnesses could not be identified. Antaka is Yama, and Dhanada, "Giver of Wealth," is Kubera.  Six seed syllables and six colors are given for the sense organs, but seven goddesses are named; the list is translated as it stands. "Corpses" renders a term meaning literally "those whose life is short." The Caṇḍālī in the northeastern quarter is called here by a different Tibetan rendering, "Fierce Holder," than in the songs earlier, but her seed and attributes identify her. "Its center" reads ལྟེ་བ for the written ལྕེ་བ, "tongue," as the passage on the lotus requires. Here དབུ་མ is the central channel, not the Middle Way school.  "Dhāraṇī of the maṇḍala" follows the Tibetan gzungs; the sense here seems to be the wisdom maṇḍala that is drawn in, and a reading gzugs ("the form of the maṇḍala") is possible. "The Vajrin" (rdo rje can) in the list of buddhas most likely stands for Akṣobhya. "The lamps" reads sgron ma for the written sgrol ma ("Tārās"), which makes no sense among the inner-offering substances (five nectars and five lamps). "The moon and the other substances" are coded names for the offering substances. "If one grows weary" reads skyo na for the written skyon ni ("fault"), as in the parallel sentence that follows; "upon this" reads de la for da la; "midday" reads nyin gung for nyin rang. "In the evening" is a conjectural rendering of the unclear phyi 'bred; "with awareness" renders rig pa, which may instead mean one's consort (vidyā).  "Gatekeeper goddesses" in the first seed-syllable list reads sgo ma for the written sgom ("meditation"), as in the parallel entries. "The Heruka of Aversion" renders zhe sdang he ru ka (Dveṣa Heruka), the Heruka of the vajra family. The author's name, lha'i brtul zhugs, is translated literally as "Divine Observance"; it may correspond to a Sanskrit name such as Devavrata, but this could not be confirmed. The closing verse on Śākyamuni is a scribal addition after the colophon, not part of the sādhana.
