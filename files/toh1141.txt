The Praise Ascending from One (Ekottarikastava)

The Praise Ascending from One, known in Sanskrit as the Ekottarikastava and in Tibetan as gcig las 'phros pa'i bstod pa, is a short devotional hymn addressed to the Buddha. After an opening homage to the noble Mañjuśrī, it unfolds through a numerical sequence of verses, each built around an ascending number: the one Dharma and the single eye of the world, the two truths, the three concentrations and Three Jewels, the four truths and four dhyānas, and so on. Each verse links these familiar doctrinal lists to the Buddha's qualities and closes with a refrain of homage, making the poem both a celebration of his virtues and a memorable summary of core teachings. It is Toh 1141 in the Tengyur, where it belongs to the collection of praises, the class of hymns and eulogies honoring buddhas and awakened beings.

In the language of India: ekottarigastava. In the language of Tibet: The Praise Ascending from One.

Homage to the noble Mañjuśrī.

You delight in the one Dharma,
you dwell alone in the forest,
you have become the single eye of the worlds:
homage to the single eye of the world!

Supreme among two-legged beings,
you have fully meditated on the two truths;
you have no second companion:
homage to you whose speech is free of duality!

Endowed with the three concentrations,
holding the Three Jewels in equal measure,
you have crossed the waters of the three sufferings:
homage to the sustainer of the three worlds!

Possessing the four kinds of teaching
and bearing the aspects of the four dhyānas,
you are wholly free of the four yokes:
homage to you who know the four truths!

Free from attachment to the five aggregates,
you have fully cultivated the five faculties;
you are wholly free of the five factors:
homage to you who cut off the five destinies!

Standing firm in the river of the six senses,
you abide fully in the six constant abidings;
you have perfected the six perfections:
homage to the one with the six superknowledges!

Bestower of the seven precious limbs of awakening
and of the seven riches of the noble ones,
you are wholly free of the seven factors:
homage to the lord of the seven existences!

You teach the eight persons
who arise from the eight limbs of the path;
you have abandoned the eight worldly concerns:
homage to the destroyer of the eight unfree states!

Wholly liberated from attachment to the nine abodes,
you have entirely abandoned the nine grounds of resentment;
you have attained the nine kinds of pliancy:
homage to you who know the nine levels!

Possessor of the noble Saṅgha with its ten qualities,
you cut off the ten obscurations;
you delight in the ten abodes of the noble ones:
homage to the one endowed with the ten powers!

Thus, to worship with words the noble one endowed with the ten powers,
whatever root of virtue I have gained here through this praise,
by it may it become the cause for beings to cross the ocean of existence,
pervaded by the sufferings of the hells, the hungry ghosts and the animals.

The Praise Ascending from One, composed by the master Mātṛceṭa, is complete.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

Several numbered items are terse and admit more than one identification. The "five factors" and "seven factors" from which the Buddha is said to be free, and the "six constant abidings" (rendering the Tibetan "six continua"), follow the most likely canonical lists but are not certain. "The ten qualities" of the noble Saṅgha is translated literally.
