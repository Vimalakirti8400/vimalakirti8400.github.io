The Dhāraṇī for Giving Away the Body's Outflows as an Act of Generosity

The Dhāraṇī for Giving Away the Body's Outflows is a brief ritual text in which the bodily excretions, including saliva, phlegm, nasal mucus, the dirt of the body, excrement, and urine, are offered as an act of generosity to the beings who feed on them. It opens with homage to the Three Jewels and gives a short mantra for each substance, instructing the practitioner to wish that those who eat these things may obtain them, to snap the fingers, and finally to dedicate the resulting merit to awakening for the sake of all sentient beings. According to its colophon, the mantras were extracted from the sūtras and tantras by the master Vajrāsana and translated into Tibetan by Bari Lotsāwa, and the surviving title lines do not clearly establish a formal Sanskrit or Tibetan title. It is Toh 1082 in the Kangyur, where it belongs among the dhāraṇī texts, the collection of short incantations and protective or meritorious formulas.

Homage to the Three Jewels.

The dhāraṇī for giving away the body's outflows as an act of generosity.

For saliva: oṃ āḥ śleṣmāharebhyaḥ svāhā
For phlegm: oṃ āḥ khrera āharebhyaḥ svāhā
For nasal mucus: oṃ āḥ siṃghana āharebhyaḥ svāhā
For the dirt of the body: oṃ āḥ mla āharebhyaḥ svāhā
For excrement: oṃ āḥ biṭa āharebhyaḥ svāhā
For urine: oṃ aḥ mutra āharebhyaḥ svāhā

For each of these in turn, one should say, "May those who eat these obtain them," and snap the fingers.

Whatever merit is gathered by giving these in this way should all be dedicated to awakening for the sake of all sentient beings.

These were extracted from the sūtras and tantras by the master Vajrāsana and translated by Bari Lotsāwa.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The master here, called "Lama Dorjé Denpa" in Tibetan, is the Indian teacher known as Vajrāsana, named after the seat of awakening at Bodh Gayā. He is a person, not the place.
