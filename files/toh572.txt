The text breaks off mid-sentence at the end of the passage, ending with the title formula "Blessed Lady, Noble" without completing it. This appears to be a textual truncation in the source.

The term translated as "zombie" or "great zombie" renders ro langs chen mo, literally "great risen corpse," which in the Indic tradition corresponds to vetāla, a reanimated corpse or powerful spirit entity. The doctrinal-ritual context here is its invocation as a force with the speed of lightning and wind, which is unusual; some commentators read this as a description of the mantra's power rather than an entity. This uncertainty is noted here rather than flagged in the body.

The expression pha mam pha rol pham par mdzod cig is rendered as "defeat the near enemy, defeat the far enemy" following the internal grammatical logic of pha (near/own side) versus pha rol (far/other side), both being defeated. This is a standard protective formula structure.

The identification of Gandhamādana (ri dri rgyal, literally "king of fragrant mountains") follows standard Buddhist cosmographic usage.
