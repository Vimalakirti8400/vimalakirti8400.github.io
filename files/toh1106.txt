The Auspiciousness of the Three Families (Trikulamaṅgala)

The Auspiciousness of the Three Families, known in Sanskrit as Trikulamaṅgala and in Tibetan as Rigs gsum gyi bkra shis, is a brief verse prayer of benediction addressed to the three great protector bodhisattvas. After an opening homage to the Three Jewels, it invokes Mañjuśrī, golden like a king of mountains, as the embodiment of the enlightened body; Avalokiteśvara, white as a conch, as the embodiment of enlightened speech; and Vajrapāṇi as the embodiment of enlightened mind. Each verse closes with the refrain asking that, through that auspiciousness, there may be well-being here today. These three figures are traditionally known as the protectors of the three families, and the prayer is the kind of auspicious verse recited to bring blessing and good fortune to an occasion. It is Toh 1106 in the Kangyur, where it belongs among the short dhāraṇī and prayer texts of auspiciousness.

In the language of India: trikulamaṃgala. In the language of Tibet: The Auspiciousness of the Three Families.

Homage to the Three Jewels.

Blessed One, like a golden king of mountains,
Protector of the three worlds, Mañjuśrī in youthful form:
Whatever auspiciousness there is of the Sugata's body,
Through that auspiciousness may there be well-being here today.

Blessed One, like a conch-white king of mountains,
Protector of the three worlds, Avalokiteśvara:
Whatever auspiciousness there is of the Sugata's speech,
Through that auspiciousness may there be well-being here today.

Blessed One, like a yellowish-green king of mountains,
Protector of the three worlds, Vajrapāṇi:
Whatever auspiciousness there is of the Sugata's mind,
Through that auspiciousness may there be well-being here today.

The Glorious Auspiciousness of the Three Families is complete.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

In the second verse, the word usually glossed as "empowerment" (dbang) is part of the name Avalokiteśvara. It renders the name's final element "lord" (īśvara), so it is translated as part of the name. In the closing line, the final syllable is evidently a slip for the usual ending "so" and is read as "is complete."
