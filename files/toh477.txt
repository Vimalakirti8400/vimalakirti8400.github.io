The Great King of Tantras called Glorious Candraguhyatilaka (Śrīcandraguhyatilakanāmamahātantrarāja)

The Great King of Tantras called Glorious Candraguhyatilaka, in Sanskrit Śrī Candraguhyatilaka Mahātantrarāja and in Tibetan Dpal zla gsang thig le, is a Buddhist tantra whose title may be rendered "the Secret Drop of the Moon." It is Toh 477 in the Kangyur, where it belongs to the Tantra section among the higher yoga tantras. Opening with a homage to Vajrasattva, it unfolds as a dialogue in which the assembled tathāgatas, the Perfection goddesses, and other deities entreat the Blessed One, the great Samantabhadra, also appearing as Vajrasattva and Vajradhara, to reveal the secret of nondual wisdom and great bliss. Its teachings cover the hidden pledges and pledge substances, the families of buddhas and their colors, the union of method and wisdom, the four mudrās and the binding of hand gestures, wrathful rites of subjugation, and mantras for summoning the deities, and it closes with a colophon naming the translator Rinchen Zangpo.

In the language of India: śrī candra guhya tilka nāma mahā tantra rāja. In the language of Tibet: The Great King of Tantras called Śrī Candraguhyatilaka.

Homage to glorious Vajrasattva.

Then all the Blessed Ones, the tathāgatas, were filled with great wonder. Gathering once again, with dancing gestures, they joined their palms with a hollow between them, and bowing their crowns to the Blessed One, the great Samantabhadra, they made this request:

"The secret of all the heroes,
Candraguhyatilaka, which brings joy,
The true arising of nondual wisdom:
Blessed One, please explain it.
The unborn nature of all beings,
Free of both the born and the unborn,
Which fully joins what has arisen and what will arise,
Is the application to supreme conception.
The source of all jewels,
Like stainless space,
Raising aloft the great banner,
Is empowered by the great sun.
The true arising of the enjoyment of Dharma,
The color of the supreme red lotus,
That very desire, the great secret,
Is the supreme nectar that brings bliss to all.
Truly endowed with ā and la,
With a gait truly beautiful and graceful,
Various wisdom, great in its variety,
It is the very supreme of Candraguhyatilaka."

Then the Blessed One Samantabhadra, lord of all the tathāgatas, knowing that all the tathāgatas had made this secret request, entered into the samādhi called the Vajra of the Wisdom of Great Cessation, and remained without saying anything at all.

Then all the tathāgatas were filled with marvelous wonder. Gathering once again, they roused the Blessed One, the great Samantabhadra, with the melody of reality, and made him rise:

oṃ hūṃ āḥ
Please partake of the taste endowed with the supreme bliss of unborn wisdom.
a la la ho
In order to raise that very supreme bliss of mind from the cessations,
Lord who holds the supreme bliss free of attachment, arise and partake!
Delighting in the supremely secret bliss, keep your pledge and liberate the world!
Source of awakening, never departing from the sphere of supreme bliss, perform the dance of joy!
a la la ho
Those tormented by the extremes of former clinging and by the withering afflictions,
Rouse them by skillful means, adorned with supreme bliss free of delusion, and make them understand!
At the gateway where all beings act alike, show the path of awakening,
And to the assembled host proclaim the nectar of supreme bliss, without distinction!
a la la ho

Then the Blessed One, the great Samantabhadra, the great being who pervades the whole sphere of space, entered into the samādhi called Rousing Candraguhyatilaka, the Great Heart of All the Tathāgatas. From the secret drop of his own heart he sent forth a mass of light rays called the Bliss of Great Utter Delight. He spread it out in the center of the moon drop, the secret heart of all the tathāgatas, and made it shine throughout the whole sphere of space. It entered above and below as well, illuminating all the mountains and all beings without exception, and gladdened them with the bliss of great utter delight. Then it returned back into that very place, and the Blessed One, lord of the bliss of great utter delight, dwelt blissfully in the center of the moon drop of the supreme secret heart.

Then all the Blessed Ones, the tathāgatas, being roused by that heart, the garland of the mass of light rays that had become the supreme great bliss, danced, and focusing each on their own samādhi, they accomplished, in the center of the moon drop at the heart of the Blessed One, the great Samantabhadra, a single dense mass of the supremely secret bliss of great utter delight. Generating their own bodies in the form of women, they emerged from the heart of the Blessed One.

Then some, turning and rolling their eyes like blazing lotuses in full bloom, and moving their eyebrows,

Danced in the manner of the Buddha.
In the center of the Buddha Yoginī,
The vajra, supremely delighted, without doubt,
Gave delight to the great being,
And through the bliss of the Buddha's bliss
The Blessed One Buddha came to dwell.

Turning their eyebrows and turning their feet,
Turning their eyes and pressing down hard,
Turning the tips of their fingers,
They danced in the manner of the vajra,
And gave delight to the great Vajra.
In the center of the Vajra Yoginī,
By rousing him moment by moment,
The Blessed One Vajra came to abide.

With majestic feet and jeweled heads,
Dancing with their stainless crowns,
And turning the circles of their faces,
They let him savor the great bringer of bliss.
With lotus eyes they gazed
Upon the hosts of the qualities of the charnel ground,
And for the great lord of bliss
They made beauty in the manner of the jewel.

Utterly delighted, radiant, sublime in bliss,
They opened the red lotus,
And in the slow, delighted turning of desire
They danced with nets of lotuses.
In the center of the Lotus Yoginī,
Gazing on all with majestic eyes,
They gave delight to great bliss,
And the Blessed One came to abide in the lotus.

Turning the ends of their fingers
At the tips of their hands, they danced.
With palms joined as a hollow lotus,
Glorious like lightning, and at the crown
With clouds of offerings billowing forth,
To the lord, the bringer of various bliss,
They gave delight in the manner of action,
And the Blessed One of the Various came to abide.

Then the Blessed One, Great Bliss, glorious Vajrasattva, the great Samantabhadra, entered into the samādhi called Candraguhyatilaka, the Supreme Secret of All the Tathāgatas, and once again, from the supreme vajra of body, speech and mind, he sent forth the very mass of clouds of great light called Great Bliss, which is desired by all the great secrets of the tathāgata, the vajra, the secret of the jewel, the lotus and action.

Then he illuminated all these bodies of the tathāgatas with light, and they became a single dense mass and dwelt in the moon drop of his own heart. In the center of the moon drop of his heart, that light generated delight, drew them in and made them enter, and made them one. Then he entered into the samādhi called the Undoubted Victory of the Great Delight of His Own Body, Speech and Mind, and from the vajra of body, speech and mind of that very great bliss of utter delight he sent forth the great king of maṇḍalas called Candraguhyatilaka, the Supreme Secret of All the Tathāgatas.

In the manner of the maṇḍala,
Having emanated it by applying the rite,
In accord with the vehicle, the meaning of the maṇḍala
Should be danced with the dance of union,
And it should be correctly visualized just as it is.
The maṇḍala as it truly is,
The maṇḍala of Candraguhyatilaka,
Cannot be fathomed
Even by the hero Vajrasattva.
The maṇḍala conceived by wisdom,
Sealed through the yoga of the deity
By the heart essence, the mudrā, and the vidyā mantra,
Should be drawn by one who knows the rite.
Square, with four gates,
Fully endowed with four archways,
Drawn all around with four lines,
With eight supreme pillars standing firm.
Fully round on every side;
Imagine a moon at its center,
And upon it draw the lord,
Indivisible great bliss.
That is with the visarga,
The syllable joined to the thirteenth letter,
The syllable of supreme bliss,
From which the wind maṇḍala truly arises.
In front of it the wise one
Should draw, brought forth from the indivisible mind,
The syllable with the fierce dot and the leader,
Crowned by the fourth.
To its south
Draw the supreme letter of the silent one,
The letter that accomplishes
All the supreme wisdom of mudrā.
ha joined with its own, with the visarga,
With the fourth placed at its center:
This letter of mantra form
Should be drawn in the west.
The letter of the wrathful one, of great wisdom,
The marvelous seal of letters,
Set down with splendor,
Should be drawn in the north.
So that all sentient beings may accomplish
Supreme purity and great delight,
With my speech of suchness
I have taught this very maṇḍala.
The awakening of the buddhas is great bliss;
By absorption in the dance, perfect buddhahood.
If the supreme being teaches it,
That very awakening of a buddha is accomplished.

Then all the blessed tathāgatas gathered once more as one and danced for the Blessed One, the great Vajradhara, lord of utter great delight, and with eyes opened wide in exuberance,

Pressing down foot upon foot,
They drew in, cast out, and planted their steps.
With both hands, right and left,
They loosed the tips of splendor from the edges.
With eyes opened slightly sidelong,
Shifting their beautiful gaze,
They danced with the mudrā of the eyes,
And beings, sealed by it, bowed down.
With this melody they exhorted
The great being, the hero,
And in the maṇḍala of utter great delight
All the yoginīs came to dwell.
O vajra of utter great delight,
Great bliss of all the buddhas,
Blessed One, speak yourself
The suchness of the maṇḍala of supreme delight!
You who play in bliss unborn in every way and pervade all,
Our mind, bound with the pledge of form, enjoying, unchanging,
Supreme bliss, the mind of awakening, wholly free of suffering in every way,
Endowed with the spontaneous supremacy of great wisdom, enjoying bliss: please sport in bliss!
a la la ho
By singing this as a song,
Through existence itself there is nirvāṇa.
The tathāgatas of great joy
Bless this maṇḍala.

Then the Blessed One, Vajra Great Bliss, smiling, his wide eyes like an opened blue lotus, his brows and body shifting, in a trampling stance that changed like lightning and waves, spoke this suchness from the vajra of his own body, speech and mind:

What is called "maṇḍala, maṇḍala"
Abides in the heart of vajra wisdom.
The supreme vajra maṇḍala itself
Is conceived through the yoga of oneself.
The maṇḍala of Vajra Great Delight
Is like the full moon,
Beautiful with garlands of white rays of light,
Like the supreme mind of awakening.
The maṇḍala of the Vajra Hero
Is a beautiful crescent moon,
Ablaze with dark blue all around,
As if fanned by fire and wind.
The maṇḍala of the Vajra Jewel,
Which accomplishes supreme utter delight,
Is a delightful maṇḍala of eight parts,
Like a blazing sun.
The maṇḍala that gives rise to supreme delight,
That of the Lord of the Lotus, is like a lotus,
With its eight petals spread open,
Its anthers adorned with a moon.
The maṇḍala of Vajra Action,
Which accomplishes all activities,
Is a delightful triangular maṇḍala,
Blazing intensely like fire.
These and other boundless
Maṇḍalas should be visualized
Just as they are emanated
By the protector Vajrasattva.

Then all the blessed tathāgatas rose once more and danced with splendid vajra steps. With song, cymbals, flutes, lutes, clay drums, kettledrums, paṇava drums, palikas, gentle hand-clapping and the like, all struck in many ways, and with the display of cymbals and other instruments, and with divine flavors, flavorful food, things to eat, things to chew, drinks, vehicles, seats, garments, things to lick, things to suck, soft foods and other things of many kinds, they made offerings to the Blessed One, Great Bliss, the glorious Vajradhara. Then, respectfully, holding their lotus fingers hollowed, bowing toward the left, opening their eyes with smiles, they again praised him with this melody that exhorts him to suchness:

Possessing eyes of compassion that see all with the loving-kindness of utter bliss,
You never weary of delivering beings to the heart of great awakening;
To free them from the six afflictions, you show the path that suits them;
In the manner of space, of the nature of dhāraṇī, you hold power over all the sugatas. How wonderful!

Great through bliss expanded everywhere, you draw forth and hold wisdom;
Your body, perfected by the bliss of the distinctive grounds, is like a king gathered by emptiness;
Touching the nature of reality just as it is, you display all the greatness of your body,
And so that all beings may travel to the heart of awakening, you reveal inconceivable doors.
Teaching the glorious essence as dhāraṇī, delighting the lords of the five families,
Come with delight and partake, and with utter delight make play!
Partaking of the bliss of the tathāgatas, in order to raise beings from the state of sinking,
You are endowed with the guise of a yakṣa and the mode free of arising and ceasing;
With great compassion you teach great bliss, the nature of reality, like space.
In the state of supreme delight, to the goddesses emanated as the sense sources
And to the assembly gathered in play, please bestow supreme bliss!
ha ha ha hoḥ Abiding in bliss, partake of bliss, hūṃ!
Endowed with supreme delight, please rain down unborn supreme bliss upon bliss!
In the expanse free of extremes in every way, Sugata, please take us as your disciples!
Glorious king, please take your seat in the place where you abide, just so, as is fitting!
This wondrous great awakening itself, through which beings gain bliss as their support,
Relying on nothing at all as the very substance of the six perfections,
Is always endowed with the dhāraṇī of unborn nirvāṇa.
You who abide in the expanse of phenomena with bliss free of extremes,
Bathe with the water of compassion the beings who dwell sunk in the bliss of delusion! hoḥ

Then:

By singing this as a song,
Utter supreme delight, free of attachment,
The great bliss that accords with the mind,
The siddhi, will swiftly be attained.

Then, when the praise had been offered in the melody of supplication to the Blessed One Samantabhadra, Great Bliss, the glorious Vajrasattva, lord of all the tathāgatas, he again gazed all around with eyes like stainless lotus petals, and his face broadened into a smile. Then again, toward the whole ocean of clouds, spreading through eons and leaving none out, of the many world systems as numerous as the atoms of immeasurable, boundless buddha fields: toward the tathāgatas; toward all those arisen from the families of tathāgata, vajra, precious jewel, lotus and action, namely the tathāgatas, the vajra holders, the jewel holders, the Dharma holders and the great holders of the various vajra; toward the great assemblies of lords of yoga; toward the immeasurable assemblies of attendants of the great queens of the yoginīs, such as those arisen from the heart, the weapons, the joyful ones and the coquettish ones, who lead, summon, kiss and so on; toward the assemblies of vajra sky-goers and great female sky-goers; toward the countless assemblies of vajra ravishers; and also toward the yogins arisen from the outer families of vajra, jewel, lotus and action, the great lords of yoga, and the assemblies of sky-goers: toward all of them he danced with all-seeing eyes accompanied by the transformations of great bliss. With a trampling stance full of transformations he pressed down on every side; full of transformations he lifted his feet and bared his teeth; he danced with hands intertwined all around, full of transformations; he swayed with charm full of transformations; with movements both performed and withheld, and with a trampling stance that cast out on every side, he blessed the great maṇḍala of the assembled retinue, and from the vajra of his own body, speech and mind he sent forth the great king of maṇḍalas called Great Delight, the Undoubted Vajra.

The suchness of oneself in meditative equipoise,
Likewise the suchness of the deity,
And the suchness of mantra and mudrā:
These become its supreme maṇḍala.
Beyond meditating on all the limbs,
Wholly abandoning conception and nonconception,
Perfectly beyond leading and beyond words:
This is called the supreme maṇḍala.
Engaging in what is Dharma and what is not Dharma,
One should examine all phenomena.
This maṇḍala, like pure space,
Is what has been taught.
Desire without attachment, the sameness of desire,
Like a red lotus in water;
One's own mind utterly attached in desire:
This is the supreme maṇḍala.
That which, stainless in form and in formlessness,
Enters into all the elements,
The mind, as water and as that which stirs it,
Is called "maṇḍala, maṇḍala."

Then the tathāgatas, the great lords of yoga together with their attendants, the male and female messengers, and all those maṇḍalas of the assemblies of great male sky-goers and great female sky-goers, again bowed to the Blessed One, the glorious Samantabhadra, Vajradhara, the great chief, and said this:

Oh, supreme being of utter delight!
Oh, utterly delighted one who turns the directions!
The maṇḍala of the supreme assembly of yoginīs
You yourself have spoken in secret.
Union of the buddhas, great in union,
Unsurpassed vajra union,
Through which the supreme bliss of the three worlds is attained:
Speak yourself the secret maṇḍala!
Great one of jewel, of lotus and of the various,
Who dances the dance of various unions,
In the world of the many kinds of drops,
Speak yourself the pure maṇḍala!

Then all the yoginīs, before the Blessed One, dancing, with vajra palms whose lotus-hand finger petals were intertwined all around, with the dignified grace of transforming dance, loosening the edges, pressing their lower lips with their teeth, smiling with gazes like the petals of a fresh lotus, singing melodies with the mudrā of song, urging one another on, lifting onto their own shoulders and pressing down on right and left, sang:

oṃ hūṃ a la la la ho
Appearing everywhere, yet without attachment,
With the supreme bliss of nonattachment,
You grant power to those of blissful yoga form
And bestow bliss. a la la la ho
Inconceivable, the nature of space,
Rise up and bring others to accomplishment in power!
Unstained by attachment,
Touch all the bliss of things, and please attend to us!

Then Samantabhadra, lord of the vajra of utter great delight, roused in his heart by the exhortation of the great melody of suchness, entered into equipoise in the samādhi called Vajra Glory Fully Enjoying Utter Great Delight. In this great maṇḍala of the assembly of yoginīs of the Undoubted Vajra of utter great delight, he danced in the various trampling stances of utter great delight, sang songs with the vajra melody of utter great delight, played with great vajra play, and laughed the great vajra laugh of utter great delight; and from the center of the moon drop of the vajra of his own body, speech and mind, uttering oṃ mahā sukha hūṃ, he sent forth the mistress of all the tathāgatas, Jñānadhātvīśvarī.

With a crescent moon above,
The second, like the letter ma,
Marked with a drop:
This is the yoginī of luminous wisdom.
Drawing out the vowels and consonants,
Visualize a line in the center,
And at its head and its tail
Imagine the two supreme wrathful ones.
This is the luminous one of the queen,
The maṇḍala of the Vajra Yoginī.
By merely recollecting it,
One is blessed by every merit.
Between the two vowel series draw a line,
Fully joined with the joining leader;
Above it a crescent moon and a drop;
At head and tail, further, traṃ.
This is the luminous ultimate:
The Jewel Yoginī bestows empowerment.
By recollecting it one gains merit,
And misdeeds too are destroyed.
Joining it with the two vowel series,
The visarga is explained as two letters.
This is utter joy, wholly attached,
Taught as Makarī.
The first, of great fortune,
And another with the fierce dot:
This vowel series of all the buddhas
Fully bestows the awakening of a buddha.
On oneself, or else on another,
Sealing with this mudrā,
If one accomplishes the maṇḍala to be accomplished,
Siddhi will swiftly be attained.
This is the supreme among maṇḍalas.
Whatever merit there is in holding
The manifest presence of the mudrā of letters,
Even by writing it, it becomes exceptional.
How wonderful! Bliss, great wisdom!
Its wisdom, in this very life,
Arousing desire and relying on bliss,
Is attained by way of this.
In this supreme maṇḍala of the three realms
There is nothing else more vast or supreme than this.
All beings who are practitioners
Will accomplish buddhahood in this very life.

From the great secret king Candraguhyatilaka, the extensive and supremely secret great samaya maṇḍala of the Undoubted Vajra of Utter Delight: the wisdom maṇḍala, supremely secret, of the great lords of the yoginīs, the great secret of the vajra queens of all the tathāgatas, the first chapter, is complete.

Then all the blessed tathāgatas were amazed, and, giving rise to delighted mental bliss, contentment and gladness, they danced and laughed the vajra laugh of utter great delight. Then again, having gathered into what is called the Vajra Wisdom of the Nondual Bliss of Utter Great Delight, they made a supplication to the Blessed One.

Great maker of bliss, speak the explanation!
Great bliss, speak the explanation!
Great secret, speak the explanation!
Great delight, speak the explanation!
From you desire truly arises;
From you comes great aversion;
From you come wrongdoing and suffering;
From you the three realms all arise.
From you come men and eunuchs;
From you come women tormented by desire;
From you comes liberation from desire;
From you unsurpassed awakening arises.
From you come space and wind;
From you, splendor, the element of fire;
From you, likewise, water and earth;
From you all that is done and not done.
From you the buddhas, great heroes,
Arise again and again.
From you the Dharma and the Saṅgha,
From you the host of the perfections,
The great bodhisattvas,
The mountains and all people arise.
From you comes Vajrasattva,
From you the stainless lotus,
From you the great, immeasurable jewel;
Likewise the various vajra arises.
From you come mudrās and mantras;
From you the heart of knowledge arises.

Then the Blessed One, with unblinking eyes, raised his brows and gazed, instant by instant, all around at all the tathāgatas; at the great ones, such as the bodhisattvas and the perfections; at the assemblies of great yoginīs; at the vajra sky-goers and the assemblies of sky-goers; at the worldly and those beyond the world; and also at the whole gathering of gods, nāgas, yakṣas, gandharvas, asuras, garuḍas, kiṃnaras, mahoragas, humans and nonhumans. With his gentle, majestic hand he brandished the vajra at his heart, and with the vajra of his secret sign and his gaze of passion, fully expanded and great, he outshone the entire retinue. From the center of the drop of the letter in the moon maṇḍala of the lotus of his heart he sent forth rays of light and blessed them to illuminate everything, and from the vajra of his own body, speech and mind he sent forth this great secret maṇḍala called the Undoubted Vajra of Utter Delight.

The thirteenth of the first letters,
Imagined with a drop,
Set down properly as great bliss,
With a drop that others cannot split;
The sixth letter as the leader,
Properly visualized below.
This is the great secret,
The marvelous mudrā of letters.
To give rise to great bliss as bliss,
It has been proclaimed by all the buddhas.
If, with this mudrā of wisdom,
One seals with the essence in an instant,
One becomes the expounder of maṇḍalas,
The Teacher Vajradhara.
Arousing great bliss,
It brings about the realization of the buddhas.
Through accomplishing the letter of wisdom,
One becomes the Lord of Wisdom himself.
The magical eye and great discernment,
Destroying the wrongdoing of ignorance,
Like Vajrasattva himself,
Are attained in this very life.
Great bliss, the great touch,
Destroys suffering and craving.
The great siddhi of discernment and wisdom,
Awakening, is swiftly attained.
Surrounded by ten million consorts,
That great being sports with them.
Through the equal union of vajra and lotus,
Playing with palms outstretched,
Long life, youth and good fortune
Are attained by mere recollection.
Whatever is needed through desire
Will be accomplished swiftly.
Through space below and above
One comes and goes as one wishes.
Piercing right through the earth,
That one of great power comes here.
Those who harm all the buddhas
Will without doubt be brought to awakening.
Through the form of great bliss,
Destroying and likewise tasting,
With vajra intellect all the māras
Are liberated through transformation.
By releasing great bliss,
One should offer it in an instant.
The great holder of the vajra of accomplishment,
The great hero, should summon.
The yoginīs, as one wishes,
Should be subdued in the manner of trampling.
Indra, Upendra and the great lord,
Who dwell in solitude by the power of harm,
Should be liberated in the manner of destruction,
By utterly crushing and pressing down.
Trampling with the five weapons,
One should sport with the women of others.
Oh! The great being abiding in bliss upon bliss,
Having brought about liberation,
Through the mind of awakening from which bliss arises,
Should worship the being who is himself.
Through remaining in accord with the samaya,
Yoga becomes permanent in nature.
In an instant one goes to the higher realms;
In an instant one goes to the Blissful Realm.
By the force of the vajra, everywhere,
Beings, in this very instant,
Become the abode of all the buddhas.
Beings and those of great renown,
The families of all,
And the perfections: this is their birthplace;
Likewise the families and the perfections
All abide fully in this.
Having become the lord, the great support,
The mass of qualities like space,
That very one, in all things,
Abiding equally in all three times,
Vajrasattva, supremely blissful,
Is the being without beginning or end.

This is the wisdom of the great letter mudrā called the Vajra Dharma Letter of the Utterly Great Bliss of the Blessed One, the glorious Vajrasattva.

ha with ra subjoined, crowned at its head.

Bow down to the great being

The being dances

Goddesses who move by magic,

The very self of utter delight
Abides before one and grants siddhi.

Then the Blessed One, Lord of the Bliss of Great Utter Delight, the great Samantabhadra, the great Vajra Candraguhyatilaka, being praised and exhorted by those measureless vajra sky-goers of the great secret and by those great hosts of yogins and yoginīs of the supreme perfection, entered into equipoise in this king of samādhis called Glory of Enjoying the Great Bliss of Utter Delight of All Secrets, and drew forth from his own vajra body, speech, and mind this great maṇḍala of the yoginīs called the Undoubted Samaya of Great Utter Delight.

It is square and has four gates,
Adorned with four porticoes,
With tassels of silk and of bells,
Beautifully adorned with sun and moon,
Ornamented with clusters of jewels and pearls,
Adorned with necklaces and bracelets,
With manifold parasols spread above,
Adorned with manifold garlands and cloths;
It has four cords,
And eight supreme pillars stand well placed.
On the joy-making seat in the center,
Beautifully adorned with silver and the like,
Covered with manifold lotuses and silk,
All of these are called the seats.
In the center, Vajra Utter Delight
Should be placed upon that seat,
Clad in manifold ornaments
Like countless red lotuses,
Wearing a crown ornament of wish-fulfilling jewels,
Made beautiful by a jeweled topknot;
With countless silk streamers hanging down,
The head should be made beautiful as with flowing streams.
Self of all the manifold buddhas,
In whom manifold wisdom fully abides,
His lotus eyes slightly widened,
The joy-maker bows slightly to the left;
With his right hand he holds the great vajra,
Intent on brandishing it at the heart.
Endowed with these and other qualities,
He is set out first of all.
A disciple, or else someone else,
Adorned with all the marks,
Should be blessed in the proper way
And placed in the middle of the center.
Oneself, too, in equipoise,
Foremost in such qualities,
When seated on the central seat,
Says, "I am Utter Delight."
By this the disciple is blessed,
Perfect in performing all activities,
And one empowers him as well
With the secret that arises from vajra and lotus.
Having sought out a lovely maiden
Endowed with the qualities of utter delight and the rest,
At throat, forehead, heart, and crown
Meditating on the syllables of the deity and the others,
With the sign blazing as the mudrā,
Through the mudrā of embracing oneself,
Joining vajra and lotus in equal union,
One should place them all in the proper way.
In front, the goddess Maddening One,
Biting her lower lip, delights in the taste;
Of a color mingling white and red,
Fully endowed with every ornament,
Supreme, sixteen years of age:
The one with wisdom places her in front.
On the right, the goddess Slayer,
Intent on drawing the arrow, her weapon,
Adorned with necklaces and anklets,
Twenty years of age,
Like a mountain of melting gold,
Her lotus eyes slightly blue,
She gives delight to the Great Being:
She should be placed in the proper way.
Behind, the goddess Bewilderer,
With the radiance of the red lotus's hue,
The beautiful one of the makara banner,
Twenty-one years of age,
Delights in the taste of the joy-maker's desire;
She wears manifold garments,
Is clad in manifold ornaments,
And is well trained in the arts of dance,
The manifold lotus union and the rest: so she is placed.
On the left is the Great Manifold One,
Trained in manifold purposes,
Twelve years of age,
Very diligent in yoga,
She who intoxicates all beings,
Gods, asuras, and humans;
She is clad in manifold ornaments
And wears garments of manifold silk.
In all the corners,
The goddesses of form, sound, smell, taste, and touch,
With incense, flowers, and fragrance,
Of great splendor and radiant with light,
Should be placed as is fitting.
In all the directions of the gates,
Endowed with the mudrās of their own activities,
Fortunate and devoted to joy,
The four gatekeepers should be placed.
And further, manifold others:
The lute, the flute, and percussion,
The clay drum, the great drum, and the mukunda,
The muraja and the dara,
Dance, song, and great laughter,
And manifold musical instruments:
The joy-making maṇḍala should be set out.
That is the whole of the outer maṇḍala.

Outside this maṇḍala
Place the vajra ḍākinīs,
Riding upon kings of the pretas,
Heroines who hold terrifying forms,
Fortunate ones bearing their own hand implements,
Threatening with malevolent minds,
Eating and consuming flesh and blood,
Entrails, marrow, and fat.
Seated so as to form a maṇḍala,
The maṇḍala of the host should be set out.
She Who Delights and She Who Draws,
She Who Slays and She Who Emanates,
Those of the host of vajra ḍākinīs,
Should be placed in the east.
Great Splendor and She Who Blazes,
Terrifying One and the Donkey, in the south:
By means of studying the treatises
The wise should place them in the proper way.
Great Passion and Free of Passion,
Wheel Lady and Sealing Lady,
Joining them through thorough union,
Should be placed in the west as is fitting.
Manifold Body and Protectress,
Yakṣiṇī and Thorough Ravisher,
Blessed with mantra and mudrā,
The mantra master places in the north as is fitting.
Those placed in the eastern corner
Are Crow-Faced, with eyes to match,
Terrifying Eyes and Terrifying Face;
Made to accord as is fitting,
They are seated well upon preta seats,
Holding manifold supreme colors,
Their hands holding knives and skull bowls,
Supremely eating flesh and blood.
Those placed in the southwest corner
Are Laughing One and Long Owl-Faced,
Wrathful Dog-Faced and Pig-Faced,
Great Belly and Hanging Belly.
In the northwest corner
Are Camel-Faced and Donkey-Faced,
Wrathful Goat-Faced and Cat-Faced,
Who eat entrails from the head on.
In the northeast corner
Are those holding white skeletons,
Wrathful ones clad in garlands of bone,
And Hanging Belly, who holds a vase.
Intent on fighting one another
Are the retinue of jackals,
Their terrifying faces baring fangs,
Taking on forms through transformation.
In all the directions of the gatekeepers,
To be placed at the four gates:
In the northern direction, Companion of the Cry;
Kumbhakarṇa in the east;
And in the west, the Destroyer.

In the south, King Yama is to be placed.
These and the other supreme kings
Are encircled on all sides by ḍākinīs.
Vases of various kinds,
Made of gold and of silver,
Well wrapped in various cloths,
Adorned with flowers and fruits,
Filled with the five nectars,
Furnished with the five medicines,
Furnished with the five precious substances,
And adorned with the five grains:
Sixteen such vases are to be set out,
Or twelve, or eight, or else half that,
Or at the very least just a single one,
Set out in their proper order.
In the center, a vase of the supreme taste,
Filled with the water of the pledge,
Of gold, of silver, or even of copper,
Or made of iron or of clay.
Moon and sun, urine,
And a rich, oily drink,
The substances that come from all the senses,
Thoroughly mixed with one measure of meat:
This supreme taste, surpassing all,
Is prepared in the midst of the gathering.
By tasting it as one would a drink,
The wise vajra one lives for an eon.
With various kinds of choice food,
With things to eat and things to chew, make offerings.
With the five offering foods
And the boundless five qualities of sense pleasure,
To the boundless host of yoginīs
Make offerings moment by moment.
The mantra adept, duly in meditative equipoise,
Gives torma to the bhūtas.
To each and every one of the deities,
With the mudrā of Heruka,
Bless their own torma,
And having invited them, offer it with purity.
That host of dhāraṇī goddesses,
Having been pleased, departs again.
Into the maṇḍala of the great yoginī
The supreme disciple is to be brought.
A most beautiful woman of equal conduct,
Well wrapped in various garments,
Is to be held by the left hand.
With the mudrā of the enjoyment of Utter Delight
Bring them in, and at the corner of the gate
The wise one should proclaim the pledge:
"Beyond this there is nothing at all that is not to be done.
With the very mind of skillful means and wisdom,
Always free of hesitation,
Enjoy the five qualities of sense pleasure.
Further, those who have broken the pledge,
And whatever persons oppress the sacred,
Summon them through your discipline
And drink their great blood.
Those who revile the master,
And those who do not carry out his commands,
When they flee, their great blood
The wise one should drink from their hearts.
Those who harm the Three Jewels
And those who harm the master too,
By gathering the vajra wrathful ones
And meditating on the pledge, are to be liberated."
Then the pledge is given to drink.
The vajra nectar of supreme taste
Is to be placed in a great conch.
The water of vajra nectar is given to drink:
"Alas! This is the water of hell.
It will dwell throughout your heart.
If you guard the pledge, it bestows accomplishment.
Drink this water of vajra nectar!"
oṃ vajra udaka hūṃ ṭha
Having recited this pledge a hundred times
And blessed it, have him drink.
Otherwise it would not become the pledge,
And this also becomes accomplishment itself.
"I am one who delights in the fortune of the pledge":
The disciple is made to utter this mantra.
Standing at the corner of the gate, one should ask:
"O, in what joy do you have faith?"
And the one entering should say:
"I, the fortunate one, in supreme bliss,
In the magical display of Utter Delight, I delight;
In supreme joy I delight."
Then into the center of the supreme yoginī's maṇḍala
A flower is to be cast.
The deity on whom the flower falls,
In that very deity lies the accomplishment.
Then with the mudrā of empowerment
Confer empowerment on the crown of the head.
With the four kinds of vases as well,
Evenly set out, together with the fifth,
Having entered the lotus maṇḍala,
Then confer empowerment once again.
As is fitting, the consorts
Declare, "I myself am fortunate."
Whichever deity the flower went to,
That very deity is to be relied upon.
"This consort, She Who Delights,
You must always practice."
Then the lord of victors should empower that disciple
By the gathering of the inner hidden,
By entering into secret union,
As one endowed with union.
What is drawn from the vajra and the lotus
Is to be taken up with the thick finger and the finger of power,
And placed on the vajra palate:
Permanent, virtuous, great bliss.
Saying, "Oh, the bliss of the pledge!"
And "Oh, bliss!", it is to be taken.
This is called the secret,
The supreme method of empowerment.
The great-souled vajra master
Places the vajra in his own mouth.
When the great being is drawn forth,
The mind attains the bliss of awakening.
This is supremely secret,
The bliss of empowerment.
To disciples who do not understand,
None of this is to be proclaimed.
The vajra master should examine
Again and again.
As soon as he is empowered by this,
The disciple becomes a gaṇapati, a lord of the gathering.
He will blissfully become a master
Of the buddhas and the vajra holders.
The lord in whom the consorts delight
Is declared to be the lord of the buddhas.
He becomes the consort of the sky-goers,
Lord of the mountains and of all beings.
Then the empowerment of wisdom and primordial wisdom
Is to be given to the supreme disciple.
That consort the guru himself
Blesses, and then,
So that the fruit of accomplishment is quickly attained,
Is to give her to the disciple.
Through the most hidden empowerment,
The vajra master, in the proper way,
So that the wise one may be free of doubt,
Bestows these four kinds.
From then on, to the disciple,
Whatever little there is from all the tantras,
The things hidden in the manner of letters,
The supreme secret, is to be taught.
Then the disciple rises,
Touches his head to the master's feet,
And the sovereignty of a king, sacred bliss,
Everything, is to be offered as the fee.
To all those consorts as well
Gifts are offered as they please.
Whatever the master wants, however little,
Even a gift he has only thought of in his mind,
Out of fear of transgressing the pledge,
All of these are to be offered.
To the master, the supreme of gifts:
Offering one distinguished in family,
One's own mother upon a lion throne,
Given to the buddha, is the supreme gift.
Elephants, jewels and gold,
And a sister are the supreme gifts for the guru.
This is the family of Vajrasattva;
That gift is the supreme of gifts.
A wife who is a precious lotus,
Chariots and horses richly adorned,
Various pure jewels,
Most beautiful with garlands of pearls,
And a daughter adorned with nets of jewels:
These are for the Viśvavajra.

The sovereignty of a king,
And a wife are to be offered to the guru;
Magical mounts of every kind,
Elephants and horses richly adorned.
To the lord of all the tathāgatas,
The guru, offer them with devotion.
This seal of the guru's fee
Is to be given by the wise vajra disciple,
If he desires all that brings bliss,
With a mind of joy and devotion.
Through this and similar practices,
The bliss of Utter Delight, without any doubt,
Is swiftly attained,
And one becomes the foremost master.
Victor, sovereign of all dharmas,
He becomes the supreme wise vajra one.
King of the family of all jewels,
And likewise in all the various families,
Endowed with the glory of all the lotus families,
Lord of all the vajra families,
And of all the families of the buddhas,
He becomes the supreme chief of the victors.
In the dance of the wheel of the maṇḍala,
Since he and the host of disciples have come together,
Vajrasattva, the great bliss,
The very nature of the being, is to be worshiped.
By way of mutual and other such methods,
Through the practice of offering to wisdom,
The great vajra abiding in skillful means,
One comes to make offering to all the ḍākinīs.
The outer, the hidden, the secret and so on,
The supreme inner offerings,
Abiding in the manner of the practice of the mudrā,
Are to be sealed properly and offered.
With body, speech and mind that bring bliss,
One should worship the very nature of offering.
By experiencing the play of desire,
One attains the offering of nirvāṇa.
Through the rite of all offerings,
Knowing the distinction of self-awareness,
If one accomplishes all the practices,
One swiftly attains all accomplishments.
Seeing this wheel of the maṇḍala,
With a mind joyful, pleased and satisfied,
One bows to Vajrasattva
And makes supplication to one's own maṇḍala.
The hundred postures of the great Bliss Maker,
Five by the distinction of families,
And for each of the deities as well,
Supreme and endless distinctions of mind.
What has not arisen in past times,
The vast and endless Dharma, is taught.
Bound up by attachments,
Tormented by suffering, they go to the end.
The enjoyments of supreme desire
They follow according to their inclination and enjoy,
Yet they never become attached,
And have minds risen up through bliss.
Proceeding to the heart of awakening
Is by the vows and by the mind.

Then the Blessed One, the great Samantabhadra, lord of all utter delight, laughing broadly and utterly delighted, with a mind that gazes from the corner of the eye, entered into equipoise in the meditative absorption called the Glory of the Cross-Legged Posture of Total Enjoyment, and drew forth from the vajra of the body, speech and mind of his own mudrā's primordial wisdom itself the posture called the Glory of the Great Cross-Legged Posture.

In the manner of right and left,
Both feet are raised,
So that one's own thighs are not seen,
And the root posture is to be bound.
In the manner of a dancer in union,
One dances with the practice of dance.
The great bliss of the vajra seat:
Through this one will be victorious over Māra.
All the vicious ones and enemies
Are conquered in an instant; what need is there to say more?
Through this seat of utter delight
One swiftly attains perfect awakening.
The indivisible, together with the bindu,
And in the lower part the six syllables:
The one possessed of wisdom stirs and recites them.
When it arises, phaṭ is said.
The lotus and the indivisible are drawn,
And the practice joined with the bindu is to be done.
The indivisible is fully joined with oṃ,
With the fierce visarga, in the source of bliss.
One who sports with a woman
By this means for seven days
Sees the great yoga of the buddhas
Who fill the whole of space.
They exhort the great being
Who abides in the enjoyment of Utter Delight,
And with what arises from the consort's lotus
They confer the empowerment of the supreme Bliss Maker.
Then, having attained the bliss of utter joy,
That being is accomplished.
Without beginning and without end
He enjoys all that he desires.
He is endowed with long life, glory and abundance.
From grahas, poison and fevers,
From the harassment of ḍākinīs,
And from sinful saṃsāra he is freed,
And he roams through all the realms.
His splendor is like the rising sun.
Blazing with a likeness of all kinds,
He illuminates all things,
And through this he takes away
The sickness, grief and great fear of all sentient beings.
Through practice with this mudrā,
The five wisdoms are to be taught.
This is called the Cross-Legged Posture of Play, which accomplishes all the aims of the activities of the great mudrā.

Draw one's own feet together in the center,
Bind them with the feet,
And link them as in the posture of the being.
By merely binding this,
It is held to accomplish the three realms.
The syllables are as before.
By striving at union with one's own deity,
Whether one meditates or else stirs,
By this the mantra practitioner gains accomplishment.
Holding the mind on the mantra with purity,
Having cultivated the meditation for seven days,
One becomes perfect accomplishment itself.
If, having sealed with this mudrā,
One displays the practice thoroughly,
One will without doubt obtain kingship,
And it fulfills every need, like a wish-fulfilling jewel.
This is the vajra cross-legged posture.

Taking hold of the consort and binding her,
One joins in the manner of the jewel,
And one's own feet are not seen.
This is the cross-legged posture of all jewels.
This, the jewel of all jewels,
Destroys poverty and want.
She takes away all evil,
Removes all harmful poisons,
Bewilders all the vicious,
And destroys all enemies.
She makes all the wrathful ones smile.
If one seals the vicious ones,
Undistracted for a single month,
By this one gains the accomplishment of kingship.
It performs every activity, like a wish-fulfilling jewel.
If one wishes to accomplish oneself
As a king, supremely marvelous,
The jewel mind, the jewel mark,
Pressing in the manner of the secret mudrā,
A king victorious over the three realms
Is accomplished in just half a month.
This is the jewel posture.

Likewise, placing both feet,
Joined in the likeness of a lotus,
Oneself, or another as well,
The wise one should accomplish the bliss of the lotus.
By this all sentient beings,
And in short the pure buddhas,
And all the vajra suns without exception,
Come under one's power without doubt.
Of the great palace of the Blissful Realm,
This Bliss Maker brings attainment.
Through all the realms of sentient beings
The great being goes and comes.
This is the lotus posture.

Likewise, having spread the cross-legged posture,
One should practice union in various ways.
Through the practice of the great mantra adept,
The mantra adept, most diligent in the syllables,
If he recites and meditates on them,
Will attain lasting accomplishment.
Undistracted, by the former incense,
All the activities are seized.
By abiding in the center of the various,
One will attain the unsurpassed accomplishment.
This is the various posture.

Then, spreading the cross-legged posture of the ḍākinī,
And seizing all the blood,
The two feet of the consort
One raises oneself onto the nape of the neck.
Through the approach of the practice
Of the vajra ḍākinī, all the blood is seized.
In equipoise, if one raises
One foot of the consort to the neck,
As one endowed with union,
It seizes all foods:
Drinks, things to eat and things to chew,
And with the clothing and ornaments one is covered.
Likewise, within just seven days,
It performs all activities without doubt.
It seizes the great supreme glory
Of Brahmā, of those who seek to belong, and of Indra.
In the manner of raising the hand,
One crosses over the three realms in stages.
By closing the eyes,
One destroys the three destinies.
In equipoise like a tortoise,
If one binds it, undistracted, for a moment,
It seizes all vicious creatures,
Enemies, and those who practice and those who do not.
In equipoise like a frog,
One should circle in the manner of a maṇḍala.
It bewilders all without exception,
And crosses over the three realms.
In equipoise through the desire to realize,
The three worlds are to be sealed.
In equipoise through the lord of dance,
One makes the three worlds dance.
In equipoise through the bell,
The three worlds are to be struck.
By binding the cross-legged posture of the youth,
One attains the great bliss of the buddhas.
Having taken the half cross-legged posture in equipoise,
If one presses down with the second,
All the three realms are subdued,
And all that one desires is accomplished.
Through the equipoise of She Who Delights,
The cross-legged posture attains utter joy;
She Who Delights is the enchanting one,
The accomplishment of all the three realms.
Having set out the vidyādhara posture,
The three realms are to be pressed down in an instant.
Through the binding of the consort,
Condensed, the victory over the three realms,
The three worlds are fully conquered,
And in an instant one becomes the lord of the three realms.
Always joined to one another,
The two arms are drawn in,
And by blocking all the doors,
The thick fangs are firmly joined.
In the manner of stretching and bending the right,
One threatens with the pressing of subjugation,
And urges on by the practice of the indivisible;
With a wrathful mind one is to taste.
The stretching and bending of the left is to be joined;
Swiftly, by pressing, it is to be raised,
And with the hook of bliss of the little finger,
The index finger touches the tongue.
By adorning the crown of the bindu
With the seed hri with the fierce visarga,
Nectar or the knowledge woman is to be drawn forth.
Emanating it at the tip of the tongue,
It is to be drawn in the manner of summoning.
In the equipoise of the vajra yakṣa,
In the direction of the mouth, the fangs,
Thick, are made like the tongue.
Through the practice of the wrathful fangs,
Making the fingers like a skull cup,
One should drink a great deal of blood.
The first syllable is endowed with oṃ,
With a bindu placed on its crown,
And attached below with ra,
Drawing u and adorned with the vowel.
This is my heart essence,
Supreme, performing all activities.
Killing and all summoning,
Subjugating and empowering and the rest:
These and other activities as well
Are accomplished by applying it properly.
Closing the eyes as if dead,
And drawing in one's own body,
By the practice of the five vase-breathings to the end,
All enemies are to be slain.
Fully endowed with the first vowel,
One should make oneself the nature of everything.
Meditating on the great buddha,
One emanates masses of clouds of peaceful light rays.
Through this the body becomes peaceful.
Through this, for all sentient beings,
Fevers, grahas, poisons and the like,
Suffering, the fangs of the ḍākinīs,
And all fears are removed.
In the equipoise of the perfection of wisdom,
By oneself in the proper way,
Through this the suffering and fears
Of all those within are taken away.
These and other endless
Activities in the sky and on the earth:
Placing Vajrasattva in the heart,
One performs them all, like a wish-fulfilling jewel.
Swiftly, toward the vajra master,
Those who desire the aim of mantra accomplishment
Should turn with respect and reverence,
To an exceedingly high degree.
Never should the mind
Of the vajra master be disturbed.
If one does so through corrupt wisdom,
One will swiftly fall into hell.
Through sickness, harm and enemies,
Through grahas, fevers and poison,
One who reviles the feet of the master
Dies in great delusion.
Accomplishment is obtained from the guru.
The guru is the higher realms; the guru is bliss.
Therefore with all earnestness
One should not transgress the guru's command.

This concludes the fourth chapter, the maṇḍala of the host of the Great Vajra Yoginīs of Great Utter Delight, from the king of tantras, the Candraguhyatilaka, the great hidden secret of the supreme vajra of the body, speech and mind of all the tathāgatas: the great maṇḍala of the primordial wisdom of the empowerment of the vajra pledge, the undoubted bliss of the Great Utter Delight of Vajrasattva, the Great Bliss of the glorious Great Utter Delight of the great Samantabhadra, lord of the vajra of body, speech and mind of all the tathāgatas.

Then the Blessed One, the Tathāgata, with the hosts of the wheel together with their retinues, the hosts of the vajra yogins, yoginīs and great lords of yoga, and the servants of the hosts of vajra ḍākas and ḍākinīs, were filled with great supreme joy. Raising a clamor, they sang out with a great song:

"Oh! Of Vajrasattva,
The great Vajra Utter Delight!
The various skillful means, the great Bliss Maker,
The various primordial wisdoms, the great bliss!
Oh! Of Samantabhadra,
The virtuous primordial wisdom, the great bliss!
For the sake of whatever is bliss,
He makes his abode in the knowledge woman's lotus.
Oh! The knowledge most profound,
The bliss of the perfection of wisdom!
Through wisdom, for all sentient beings,
He dispels the thick darkness.
Oh! All is a great wonder!
He dances with the net of the ḍākinīs,
He makes them smell, and taste,
And caress, supreme among those who captivate.
How wonderful! Most utterly delighted,
Giving fortune to the fortunate,
The various fortunes, the great Bliss Maker,
Viśvavajra, most beautiful!"

Then, toward the Blessed One, the great Samantabhadra, Great Utter Delight, the god of desire who enjoys everything, abiding in the great bliss of utter delight, all the yoginīs and all the great female lords of yoga, the supreme Perfection Goddess and the rest, rose from their own seats. Bowing with the graceful bow of the great bliss of utter delight, they pleased him with this great seal of song, which brings about the attainment of the bliss of Great Utter Delight:

"Filling all of space with the bliss of illusion,
You show the supreme bliss of birth whose nature is emanation.
The lotus of passion pervades all with joy,
And even the very bliss of Dharma you hold in complete equanimity.
How wonderful! Such a consort, glorious with supreme bliss, possessed of every aspect,
Not abandoning the side of the Loving One, enters through wisdom itself.
These qualities of desire are the nature of reality, wholly pure.
You master the three poisons and do not shake those of faith.
Never parted from the side of wonder, you proceed to the heart of awakening."

Then the glorious Blessed One, Samantabhadra, with wide eyes and a smile, gazed for a moment, and again for a moment, at all that assembly, and entered into equipoise in the meditative absorption called the Great Glory of the Union of Vajra and Lotus in Nonduality. Saying nothing at all, he remained in equipoise. He did not breathe in, he did not breathe out, he did not let his breath go forth. He did not look about at all, he did not look closely at all. He did not rise, he did not sit, he did not go, he did not speak, nor did he engage in conceptual thought.

In the same way, when the Blessed One, through utter great bliss, sought and spread out from the bliss of Great Utter Delight so that it utterly pervaded the whole expanse of space, he there drew in, caused to enter, bound and brought under his power all the tathāgatas, pleased them with the five qualities of sense pleasure, and they remained there.

Then all the blessed tathāgatas assembled once more and gazed again and again upon the Blessed One. These three worlds are being destroyed by those who are wrathful and greatly fierce, savage, intent on great violence, obscured by the darkness of desire, aversion and delusion, greatly violent and turbulent, taking the lives of others and stealing the minds of others, infatuated by women, tormenting others with harsh words and the other afflictions and secondary afflictions, eating one another's flesh, and abiding on paths without skillful means."

Having made them enter, he transformed all the great wrathful ones into the great mudrā, and visualized himself too in the form of the great wrathful one who sends forth all the wrathful ones: baring great fangs, with a hideous face and a thousand eyes, having the splendor of a thousand great suns, his body blue in color, driven by a fire wind that makes him utterly unbearable; the great glorious seal of the great charnel ground, feeding on human skeletons, blood, flesh, brains, marrow and fat; his back and sides covered and bound with human skin; wearing a garland of human heads upon his head. From the pores of his body he sent forth many hundreds of thousands of faces of wrathful animals, of lions, elephants, donkeys, camels, buffaloes, pigs, jackals, goats, dogs, owls, crows, geese, cats, leopards, tigers and so forth.

In the manner of the great maṇḍala, in the east was the Tamer of the Great Terrifying One, with eyes like the terror of a deep frown and a face baring great vajra fangs; with a retinue of a hundred thousand terrifying great wrathful ones, he came to be massed in the eastern maṇḍala and remained there. In the southern maṇḍala, too, the Vajra Frowner, with a retinue of many hundreds of thousands of wrathful ones, came to be massed and remained there. In the western maṇḍala, too, the Great Lotus Horse-Face, with a retinue of many hundreds of thousands of wrathful ones, came to be massed in the western maṇḍala and remained there. In the northern maṇḍala, too, the Great Vajra Roarer, with a retinue of many hundreds of thousands of wrathful ones, came to be massed and remained there.

The Blessed One Vajra Utter Delight, the great wrathful one, surrounded by many hundreds of thousands of hosts of great wrathful ones, sat at the center of the maṇḍala. Enjoying himself fully with those hosts of great wrathful ones in the great bliss of Great Utter Delight, he sent forth many hundreds of thousands of forms of great wrathful ones, as many as there are atoms in Mount Meru, filled all the boundless reaches of the expanse of space, and filled this entire great trichiliocosm world system as well.

Then all the hosts of the vicious, seeing them, were terrified, and seeing no refuge, went each to their own lord, saying: "Lord, lord, protect us yourself!" Then those lords of the gods, Maheśvara and the others, emanating their own power, raised them up again and had them circle the three worlds, commanding: "You who are tormented by the Terrifying One, go for refuge to me!" Yet they did not accept going for refuge. Once more giving rise to pride in their own lineage, they drew forth what is called the Eightfold Great Terrifying One, displayed the great nature of Mount Meru, and let out a roar of great laughter:

oṃ mahā bhe ra ba be ṣṭiṃ ṣṭiṃ ṭra ṭra ṭra ta ṭra ta ha ha i ti

But not even the tip of a single pore so much as stirred.

Then the wrathful one who tames the Great Terrifying One let out the roar of Heruka, pressed down on his head and struck him. With his own great power Heruka made the Great Terrifying One cry out, drew out the tube of his entrails, and devoured his blood, bones and marrow, crunching them. He displayed this to his retinue, and Umā and some others were also destroyed. All the hosts of mātṛkās, too, were stripped naked and brought low. The wrathful one spread forth his own power, tamed the entire retinue, made them go for refuge, and established them in the samaya, and they fully entered it.

Then the Blessed One, Great Vajra Utter Delight, the great wrathful one, laughed a fierce laugh, and from the mouth of the great wrathful one Vajra Frowner gave them relief, saying: "Do not fear! Do not fear!"

Then the Blessed One Vajra Utter Delight, the great wrathful one, spoke to all the hosts of wrathful ones:

namo ratna tra yā ya mahā caṇḍa vajra pā ṇa ye mahā yakṣa senā pa tā ye tatya thā oṃ bo dha bo dha mahā bhai ra ba da ra ba pa hā ri ha ra ha ra gṝhṇa grīhṇa mā ra mā ra bhañja bhañja śīghraṃ mahā vajra canḍa sarva duṣṭāṃ ta ka ha na ha na da ha da ha pa ca pa ca ma tha ma tha hūṃ phaṭ

As soon as this was spoken, the Tamer of the Great Terrifying One expanded his body to the measure of the expanse of space. Many hundreds of thousands of wrathful ones, their faces deeply frowning and baring their fangs, swirling and transforming, holding various weapons in many hundreds of thousands of hands, with many hundreds of thousands of various faces, with hundreds of thousands of bodies blazing like tongues of fire, went to where the Great Terrifying One was, and, letting out great fierce vajra laughter, they laughed and stood before him:

oṃ mahā vajra he ru ka vajra aṭṭāṭṭā hā sa hā sa ra ha ha ha ha hūṃ ha i ti

Then Maheśvara, hearing the great fierce vajra laughter, fell face down together with his attendants and fainted. Striking him on the head with his foot, the wrathful one let him go.

oṃ vajra bhṛ ku ṭi ha na ha na gṛhṇa gṛhṇa huṃ ma ta ma ta kiñci rā ya si sūrya sa ha sra ha sti re ke ba su rā ja la la rci pra jvā li ta mahā mūrti ā kaḍḍhya ā kaḍḍhya a bya va ma bya va ma kri ṇa pa ca kra bhai ra ba hṛ da yaṃ hūṃ bhṛ

Then the Wheel Bearer's retinue, stricken with great fear, fell face down and fainted utterly, crying, "Lord, protect us!" Seeing that they themselves had no refuge, they went for refuge to the Wheel Bearer. Then Viṣṇu too fully displayed his many powers, stood before Great Utter Delight, and then withdrew behind him: "Hey, hey! Before the heart is torn out by the frown close upon the vicious, and before it is drunk, go back to your own abode!"

Then that Great Vajra Frowner became utterly agitated and fierce, dragged him forth, bound him and tore out his heart, and with his blood he fully satisfied the great Sun Frowner, the embodiment of the great yakṣa graha.

Then the retinue of Great Brahmā fell face down and cried, "Lord, protect us! Lord, protect us!" And it was said: "Hey, hey! Vicious face, go quickly, go! Riding upon your back, shall I not tame you?"

Then the Great Vajra Horse-Face became utterly agitated and fierce. He trampled on the head of Great Brahmā, drew out his heart and set about drinking his blood; he tasted his flesh, brains, fat and bones, and scattered them in the ten directions. His wives Śirimā, Sarasvatī and Śānti were also destroyed. Having tamed his hosts as well and established them in the vow of samaya, he remained.

Then the Blessed One, the great wrathful one of Great Utter Delight, looked with a wrathful gaze and sent forth:

oṃ gu lu gu lu vajra mahā kro dha ā kārṣa ya ā karṣa ya śa kra hṛ da yaṃ mahā vajra daṃṣṭrā ya hūṃ bhakṣa ya bhakṣa ya hūṃ

Then Śakra, swaggering with great arrogance and unbearably angry, said to him, "Great yakṣa, whose servant are you?" Then the Great Vajra Yakṣa became wrathful, agitated and menacing. He pulled out his crest jewel, tore apart his heart, and set about eating his flesh, blood, brains, fat, bones and everything edible. Then his retinue, their minds terrified and panic-stricken, drew near for refuge. His hosts of goddesses were also destroyed and came to remain in his place.

Then the Blessed One Great Utter Delight, the great wrathful one, sporting with the vajra yoginīs, sat in the great maṇḍala of the great wrathful one, enjoying everything in utter delight.

oṃ sumbha ni sumbha mahā kro dha śa hūṃ phaṭ

With this host of wrathful mantras one performs the rites of summoning, causing to enter, bringing under control, and so on, upon the vajra yoginīs, the ḍākinīs, the sky-goers, the vajra snatchers, and all the rest. For the samaya of worship with torma and the like, one displays the mudrā of Heruka, the mudrā of Ṭakkirāja, and the mudrā of Hayagrīva, and applies them to all rites.

Bind the vajra palms as is proper. Make the two wrathful fingers like hooks. Join the tips of the two thick fingers to each other, and raise the two peaceful fingers straight up. Extend the remaining two and send them out like rays of light. This is the supreme mudrā.

This mudrā of great splendor
Is bowed to by gods and asuras;
The buddhas, Vajradhara, and the rest
Have praised it and worshiped it.
That very binding brings the deities down
And causes all the hosts to enter.
She slays all the vicious ones
And devours all the rākṣasīs.
Abiding in the sublime yoga,
By this one performs all rites.
One who accomplishes all rites
But lacks it will not gain accomplishment.
Through approach, even those with attachment, aversion, and delusion
Swiftly obtain accomplishment.

Make the vajra binding twofold, joining the two backs of the hands back to back. Extend the two little fingers and cross them with each other. Raise the two wrathful fingers upward, show the mudrā at the heart, and hold it in the manner of extending and contracting. This performs all rites.

By this, rites of every kind
Turn out just as the mind desires.
Whatever wrongs one has done in this life,
This steals them all away.
All of them come about swiftly;
The accomplishments are a wish-fulfilling jewel.
As far as the pathways of the sky extend,
By this yoga one flies.
It also betokens the supreme vidyādhara;
When the rite is accomplished, one comes back again.

Join the palms evenly, join the two thick fingers in the middle, and make the rest like opened petals: this is the lotus seat. Point the tips of the two thick fingers downward: this is the mudrā of the Horse-Faced One.

Whoever applies it, in an instant,
Becomes one of great accomplishment.
As soon as it is bound, one takes flight
And goes wherever one wishes.
The accomplishments of the underworld, as one wishes,
And swiftly one returns once more:
These and other results without end.
This mudrā is a virtuous application.
I have taught it; the yogin
Should summon the hosts of the victors with it.
All the yoginīs as well
Are joined by that supreme limb of mudrā.

With no conflicting mind and no attachment, in the vajra
You abide in a manner unchanging, just like space.

The name should be written in the middle.
By placing this at the heart,
One goes to the supreme bliss of the tathāgatas.
Accompanied by the four mudrās,
With this body one will succeed in this life.
Truly endowed in the union of Utter Delight,
Accompanied together with the four mudrās,
And engaging in the proud vajra posture and the rest,
By binding this yoga,
Utter Delight and all the others without exception
Are drawn in, in an instant,
By the one who sways in the taste of Utter Delight.
Likewise join it to the tip of the thumb,
And place great compassion there:
The greatly fortunate Vajra Eye,
She who reveals reality and every aim.
By binding this correctly,
One becomes the eye of the eternal vajra.
Abandoning both existence and nonexistence,
One will see all things.
Make the vajra joined palm firm;
The tips of the wrathful fingers, rounded,
Are raised high like the emblem,
And the thumbs form a great, firm vajra.
This is the supreme Blessed Lady,
Which brings the attainment of the bliss of vajra and lotus.
By binding this yoga,
One attains the delight of the great hidden."

Then the great goddesses,
With great exuberance, danced,
And together with Māra, paid homage
To the very self of the Vajra Bliss Maker.
Abiding in the method of the syllables of the Dharma,
They entreated concerning the reality of the Dharma:
"The Dharma mudrā, great bliss:
Blessed Vajra, please explain it,
By which, if the practitioner strives intently,
The deity will be accomplished;
By which, swiftly blessed,
The awakening of a buddha is fully accomplished,
And with the great exuberance of the two Bliss Makers,
The tathāgatas will enter and abide."

Then the Blessed One, knowing that all the goddesses had entreated him, drew forth from his own hidden vajra body, speech, and mind these words of the great Dharma mudrā:

"The guide whose nature is the thirteenth,
Truly endowed with the drop as deity,
At the end abides as ha ra and the drop:
Utter Delight, the delight of the vajra.
Having formed this mudrā at the heart,
One sports together with the supreme Delighter.
Those who practice and those who do not will succeed,
And the yogin goes to great bliss.
Likewise make the guide first,
Vajra desire, the supreme curved one,
Truly endowed with ha ra and the drop,
With two parts and a third joined.
If this is placed at the heart,
One goes blissfully through the sky.
Of bliss that is utterly eternal,
This is the supreme accomplishment.
Place that very guide first,
'Look! Look!' together with hūṃ.
The greatly fortunate Vajra Eye,
Urged in the middle, will be accomplished.
The five miraculous eyes, of great wealth:
By this the yogin will succeed,
And in vajra awakening, great delight,
Will see all the buddhas.
First place that very guide,
And at the end make ha ra and the drop.
Vajra delight brings the attainment of bliss;
The name should be written in the middle.
By placing this at the heart,
One goes to the supreme bliss of the tathāgatas.
Accompanied by the four mudrās,
With this body one will succeed in this life.
Truly mounted in the union of Utter Delight,
Accompanied together with the four mudrās,
And engaging in the proud vajra posture and the rest,
One will accomplish the Blessed Lady of Desire."

This is the wisdom mudrā of the meaning of the reality of the Dharma.

Then the Blessed One, the great union,
Supreme, without beginning or end,
In order to tame all the malicious,
Became the Vajra Wrathful One.
In front, as the very first deity,
Is the vajra one who sounds hūṃ, a blazing sun,
Of great splendor like twelve suns,
Endowed with fire blazing fiercely.
All those wheels of his retinue
Are adorned with the supreme ornaments of the charnel ground,
And among the hosts of the malicious
He truly sports.
This mudrā, supreme in taming, of the great wrathful one:
Meditate on the mantra at one's own heart,
And circle it before the daughter of the wrathful goddess;
Thus the great wrathful goddess is accomplished.
By this all the malicious ones,
Through sounding hūṃ, will swiftly be struck down.
Those malicious ones, greatly malicious,
Terrified and agitated, utterly perish.
Crying, "Protect us! Protect us!",
They call the Vajra Lord to mind.
This mudrā is to be held in equipoise;
All beings should be brought down,
And the practitioner of every supreme rite
Should destroy the enemies.
Endowed with union, reciting it a hundred thousand times,
When one performs all the rites,
All of them will come about without fail,
And one is able to accomplish even Vajrasattva.

So spoke the Blessed One, the glorious Vajrasattva.

This is the wisdom of the mudrā of the wrathful one.

Then those great buddhas,
Supreme among all who are born as buddhas,
All the dhāraṇī women and the yoginīs,
And the hosts of the wheel of their own retinues,
Endowed with bliss with one another,
Through joy, the retinues became exalted;
Joined with the arising of their own Bliss Maker,
The greatly fortunate women sported.

Then the Blessed One, the great Vajra Utter Delight, gazed again and again upon the hosts of the wheel of his own retinue and gathered them with this heart of the guiding knowledge-woman of all the tathāgatas:

oṃ vajra aṃ ku śa du ru du ru e hye hi kaḍḍhya vi kaḍḍhya sarvān varṣa ta ca kra sañca ya vajra aṃ ku śa hūṃ phaṭ

"With this, making firm the two bindings
Of the vajra mudrā,
One should release the vajra wrathful fingers.
The vajra wrath, fully joined,
Is called the Vajra Curve.
Beings, mountains, and sentient beings
Are all drawn in by this mudrā;
What need, then, to mention the daughters of men,
Whose minds are ever swaying with desire?
Placing the vajra binding evenly,
Set it likewise above the left,
And place the thumb in the center:
The vajra guide, Great Splendor.
Tightening the vajra binding,
Touch the tips of the vajra thumbs together.
Meditating 'I am the vajra,'
One should release that binding.
Making that same binding firm,
At the juncture of the roots of the ring finger and the little finger,
Making the vajra thumb potent,
One should set the very self of Utter Delight in motion.
From that, generate the two supreme vajras,
And join them in the manner of a knot.
It is supreme for drawing in and causing to enter;
Since through it all things are accomplished,
By this very yoga mudrā,
Drawing them in, causing them to enter, and binding them,
And bringing them under control, one should accomplish them."

Then, having drawn in, caused to enter, and bound all of them, and having filled them with joy, through the great bliss of his own Utter Delight he watched over his own kingdom, and he declared to Vajradharma:

"This great way of all the buddhas,
This secret of the supreme Delighter, of Candraguhyatilaka,
Whose nature is Utter Delight:
Great being, you must hold it."

Then he swiftly rose from his seat,
Joined his palms and paid homage,
And spoke words in the nature of the Dharma:
"I have received great bliss."
Dancing the enjoyment of his own bliss,
Vajrāyus spoke these words:
"Vajradharma is the great manifold one;
The manifold lotus dances fully."
Then all those Perfection goddesses,
Dancing the enjoyment of Utter Delight,
Following the Great Bliss Maker himself,
Gave the word "Excellent":
"Excellent, excellent, great bliss!
Excellent, excellent, great wisdom!
Excellent, excellent, great manifold one!
Excellent, excellent, great delight!"

When the Blessed One, with a delighted heart, had spoken thus, that great retinue of the Perfection goddesses rejoiced.

The great king of tantras called Candraguhyatilaka, the supremely secret, is complete.

It was translated, edited, and finalized by the chief editor-translator, the monk Rinchen Zangpo.

By the merit of translating, as fitting,
This teaching that a master who realized the meaning of the scriptures
Especially elucidated and set forth,
May sentient beings attain happiness of mind.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

In the invocation, bde mchog is taken as "supreme bliss", a common noun, not as the deity Cakrasaṃvara. The "withering afflictions" renders sngo skams, whose exact sense here is uncertain. "The Blessed One of the Various" refers to the lord of the action family, matching the preceding stanzas on the buddha, vajra and lotus families.  The verses on drawing the seed syllables use a letter code (the thirteenth letter, the fierce dot or visarga, the leader, the fourth, the silent one); the Tibetan for "thirteenth" is literally "thirteen letters," and the intended syllables are not spelled out. "The tips of splendor" renders the obscure rjid pa'i rtse mo, read as brjid; the dance gesture meant is uncertain. "Crescent moon" renders zla ba kham pa, literally "a portion of the moon." Among the instruments, ba na wa is taken as the paṇava drum, and pa li ka is unidentified and left as "palika"; the offering terms bldag pa, bzhib pa and 'jams are taken as things to lick, things to suck and soft foods. In the song, bde mchog is read as "supreme bliss," not as the deity Cakrasaṃvara.  "Bared his teeth" renders tshems kyis 'debs pa, literally "struck or set down with the teeth"; the exact dance gesture is uncertain. The line "the mind, as water and as that which stirs it" renders the terse sems ni chu dang skul byed pa, whose sense is uncertain. "Sky-goers" renders nam mkha' la 'gro ba (Skt. khecara), here the ḍākas and ḍākinīs. The maṇḍala's name, "Undoubted Vajra," takes gdon mi za ba as "undoubted," as in the earlier "Undoubted Victory," not as a reference to grahas.  The verses on the seed syllables use a letter code (the second letter, the joining leader, the visarga, the first of great fortune) whose intended syllables are not spelled out. "The three worlds" renders 'jigs gsum, taken as 'jig rten gsum; as written it would mean "three fears." "Pressing down on right and left" renders rnal par gnon pa, where rnal is obscure. Makarī renders chu srin ma, "the female makara," taken as a deity's name.  The verses on the seed syllables use a letter code (the thirteenth of the first letters, the sixth letter as the leader, ha with ra subjoined) whose syllables are not spelled out. "The vajra of his secret sign" renders mtshan ma'i phyag rdo rje, where mtshan ma probably means the sexual organ. "The great lord" renders dbang chen po in a list after Indra and Upendra; it may stand for Mahendra or Maheśvara. "Those who belong with them" renders the obscure gtogs 'dod rnams. "Ten million consorts" renders rig ma 'bum phrag brgya, a hundred times a hundred thousand.  "Hārītī" renders 'phrog ma, "the ravisher," following the house glossary; the next verse plays on the word, saying the practitioner "steals" ('phrog byed) the siddhi of the buddhas, so the name may be meant here as an epithet of the consort. "The wrestlers' contest" renders gyad kyi 'gyed pa, a figure for the sexual union. "Vighnāntaka," "the ender of obstructors," is probably part of the letter code for the mantra.  Here བདེ་མཆོག in "the supreme bliss of the lord" is read as a descriptive phrase, not as the name Cakrasaṃvara. The "thirteenth vowel" is taken as the vowel o, which with the king of drops (the anusvāra) gives oṃ. In the verses on the gods, དམིགས་གསུམ is read as "three-eyed" (Śiva) and the four-faced great father as Brahmā; ཤ་ཀ་རྒྱལ་པོ is taken as Śakra, and རྡོ་རྗེ་འཛིན in this worldly list may refer to Indra as vajra-holder, though it is kept as Vajradhara for consistency. The "great king, kinsman of all" and the "holy lord who fashions all things" could not be identified with confidence and are translated by meaning. དཔལ་དགྱེས ("Glorious Delight") may name a consort.  "Liberates" (སྒྲོལ) here carries the tantric sense of ritual slaying that frees the victim's consciousness. སྤྱོད་དང་མི་སྤྱོད is rendered "those who practice and those who do not"; its exact reference is uncertain. In the last mantra description, "the wrathful one that others cannot split" and the thunder sound at the end of the preceding one likely describe the syllable hūṃ, but the text does not spell it out. "Those of the desire realm" reads གཏོགས་འདོད as འདོད་གཏོགས.  In "if through delusion one transgresses," the transgressor is not named; it is taken as the practitioner breaking the commitment. "The indivisible" at the end of the second mantra description and "the two, with the sound of a kettledrum" at the end of the third probably denote final seed syllables (such as hūṃ), but the text does not name them. "The goddess who sweetens" renders དངར་བྱེད, whose exact sense here is uncertain. སྡེ་དབང is rendered "lords of hosts."  "Should insert it" reads གཟུགས ("form") as the similar གཞུག ("insert"), which the sense of the line requires. Here བདེ་མཆོག is read as "supreme bliss," not as the name Cakrasaṃvara. The "Autumn Goddess" renders སྟོན་ཀ་མ literally, since her Sanskrit name could not be confirmed. "Lords of hosts" renders སྡེ་དབང. "Placed at the crown of the second" (གཉིས་པའི་སྤྱི་གཙུག) is also literal and unclear. སྨེ་མ is taken as "outcaste women," which is uncertain. "The vajra holder" in the first instance seems to mean the practitioner, who in the next verse becomes Lord Vajradhara.  "Bear the banner" for the makara mudrā alludes to the makara banner (makaradhvaja) of the love god. The line "lend him their own supreme glory" renders སྟོབས་བྱེད, whose exact sense here ("strengthen, lend might") is not fully certain. "A hundred hundred-thousands" (འབུམ་ཕྲག་བརྒྱ) is given as ten million. In "the great queen and the tathāgatas" the construction of བཙུན་མོ་ཆེན་པོ་དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས is unclear and might instead mean a single figure, "the great queen, the Tathāgatī."  "Revived all the hosts of ravishers" (འཕྲོག་པར་བྱེད་པའི་ཚོགས་ལ་སླར་དབུགས་ཕྱུང) is literal; who these ravishers are within the vajra family is not clear. བདུད་རྩི་རས་ཡན is read as amṛta rasāyana, "the elixir of amṛta." བདེ་མཆོག in the song is taken as "supreme bliss," not the deity Cakrasaṃvara.  The names of the goddesses and attendants in this maṇḍala are translated by their meaning, since their Sanskrit forms cannot be identified with full confidence; the three goddesses Maddening One, Slayer, and Bewilderer may correspond to Unmādinī, Māraṇī, and Mohanī, the names of the love god's arrows. "Companion of the Cry" (འབོད་འགྲོགས) is a literal and uncertain rendering. Mukunda, muraja, and dara are kinds of drum.  "Moon and sun" in the list of substances is likely coded language for semen and menstrual blood, as commonly in the yoginī tantras. The "great conch" may likewise be a code term for a skull cup. The line rendered "When they flee" reads bros nas; its sense here is unclear and the reading may be corrupt. In the disciple's reply, bde mchog is taken in its literal sense "supreme bliss," paralleling "supreme joy," rather than as the deity name Cakrasaṃvara. "The thick finger and the finger of power" probably denote the thumb and ring finger, with which the secret substance is taken in other tantras. "Gaṇapati" here denotes a lord of the tantric gathering, not the god Gaṇeśa. "Viśvavajra" (sna tshogs rdo rje) at the close is uncertain; it may name the master as holder of the crossed vajra.  "The Bliss Maker" renders bde byed, probably standing for Śaṃkara as an epithet of the central deity or of the practice; it is kept in translation because its referent is not certain. "The indivisible" (mi phyed) and "the six syllables" in the posture instructions appear to be coded names of letters or seed syllables; they are rendered literally. "Fierce visarga" renders tsheg drag, taken as the visarga sign.  This passage is a series of yogic postures and hand gestures described in highly compressed, coded language; many lines are rendered literally because their technical referents are uncertain. "Those who seek to belong" renders gtogs 'dod, of uncertain referent in the list with Brahmā and Indra. The mantra described by its letters (oṃ, a bindu, ra attached below, the vowel u) cannot be securely reconstructed from the description.  "The supreme Perfection Goddess" renders mchog gi pha rol phyin ma, the first-named of the yoga goddesses. The line "and all the victors will bring it forth" renders rgyal ba thams cad gdon par mdzad, whose verb is uncertain here; it could also be read as "he will deliver all the victors." The syllables ye shes shis in the verse addressed to Samantabhadra appear to contain a scribal duplication and have been read as ye shes, "wisdom." "The goddess Īśvarī" renders dbang phyug mo; it might also be read as "the accomplishment of a sovereign lady."  The "great O with a bindu above" describes the seed syllable oṃ. The syllable དལ in the line about drawing the maṇḍala of all the tathāgatas is unclear and is taken as "unhurriedly." The disciple's words "Please engage in desire with her" are rendered as they stand; who is addressed is not stated.  The god named གཏོགས་འདོད, who goes between Brahmā and Maheśvara and later takes the form of Mahākāla of the wheel, could not be identified with confidence; the name is translated literally as "the One Who Longs to Belong." In Maheśvara's first speech, གནོས་ཤིག is taken as གནས་ཤིག, "be at rest."  In the list of animals, བཞད is taken as the bird of that name (a goose), and བོང་བུ as the animal, the donkey, not as the epithets used elsewhere. The western figure པདྨ་ཆེན་པོ་རྟ་གདོང is rendered literally as "Great Lotus Horse-Face"; it may be a form of Hayagrīva. The Tibetan does not say who refuses refuge after Maheśvara's command, nor who speaks the words beginning "Hey, hey"; the sense of that speech is uncertain.  The words "Riding upon your back, shall I not tame you?" are not attributed in the Tibetan; they appear to be the wrathful Horse-Face's challenge. In the last paragraph the text reads དབང་ཕྱུག་ཆེན་མོ, "Maheśvarī," where the context, the revival of the slain gods, would lead one to expect Maheśvara; it is kept as written.  "Drop of resonance" renders ང་རོ་ཐིགས, taken as the tantric pairing of nāda and bindu; the following line says nothing lies above the nāda. The line "free of dust, you have given up smiling" renders འཛུམས་པ་སྤངས literally; its exact sense is unclear. The names of the wrathful goddesses (Great Sovereign Lady, Great Vajra Blood Drinker, Vajra Sun, Lotus Net, Great Lady of Unfailing Ferocity) are translated from the Tibetan, since standard Sanskrit forms could not be identified with confidence; the mantras suggest Mahākrodheśvarī, Sūryavajrā and Amogha forms. "Musala mace" renders the transliterated mu su ra; kanaya, tomara and śakti are Indian weapon names kept as transliterated.  The verse describing the maṇḍala of the Unfailing Vajra is terse, and the assignment of the eight-armed figures riding on bhūtīs to the fourfold attendants follows the most natural reading of the lines. These are translated literally; "thick" most likely refers to the thumbs, while the assignment of the others varies in the ritual literature. The couplet "It also betokens the supreme vidyādhara; when the rite is accomplished, one comes back again" is terse, and its exact sense is uncertain. Several lines are terse and their exact sense is uncertain: "working fully at the three junctures," "the four kinds of caress upon the lotus," "the marked stamen," and "the place where three meet." "The King of Spring" is taken as the name of a melody or song. In "Delighting, giving bliss, striking," the words rendered elsewhere as the names Utter Delight and Bliss Maker are read here as a list of actions of love play.  This section gives a coded sign language between yogins and yoginīs, and much of it is terse. The fingers are named by code words (wrathful, queen, thick, little, supreme, vajra), translated literally; "thick" most likely means the thumb. The opening "Ka" is read as the syllable ka used as a coded word; its exact sense is uncertain. "Swollen flesh" renders བུས་པའི་ཤ, whose meaning is unclear. The verse on "letters without a guide," "dots and the final stress," and "the yoginī's manner of guiding" seems to describe a coded way of writing mantra syllables; its exact sense is uncertain.  The finger names follow the text's own technical vocabulary (thick fingers, vajra fingers, supreme fingers, wrathful fingers, little fingers), kept consistent with the preceding passage; their exact correspondence to particular digits is not stated here. The opening phrase "fully endowed with the power to harm" renders a terse expression that may instead name a deity or mudrā called "the Harmer." "The lord victorious over the three worlds" may allude to Trailokyavijaya.  Several lines in the hymn of epithets and the seed-syllable instructions are terse and their construal is uncertain, notably "the wrathful one who grants wishes in evil times," "has a net of thirteen at its head," and "one draws near to arising and to change from arising." "The first two strokes" and "the visarga" interpret the instructions for writing the seed syllable. The closing terms "exceeding union and great union" may correspond to atiyoga and mahāyoga. "Kneading" renders the verb literally; a sense such as crushing or subduing seems intended.  "The soft pair" renders an unspecified pair of body parts in the list for the kneading practice. "Those who are to be devoured by the wrathful" construes a terse line that may instead mean "those devoured by hatred." "A lord of serpents" is construed as the fate of the transgressor, which is uncertain. "Avīci" interprets the spelling bstir med as a variant of mnar med, the Unrelenting hell. The list beginning "Accomplishment is the union of the god" seems to give the stages of sādhana, anusādhana, mahāsādhana, upasādhana, and atisādhana.  The list beginning "With the tiger and the heroic and the fearful" appears to name the moods or sentiments (rasas) of dance and drama, though "the tiger" and "the self" do not match the standard list. The syllables "pa la ki ga ha la le" seem to name further instruments or sounds and are left untranslated. "Those who torment with fear and terror" construes the verb as "torment"; it could also be read as "make lovely."
