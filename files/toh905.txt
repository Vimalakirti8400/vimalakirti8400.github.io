The Noble Dhāraṇī Called Nīlakaṇṭha (Āryanīlakaṇṭhanāmadhāraṇī)

The Noble Dhāraṇī Called Nīlakaṇṭha, known in Sanskrit as Āryanīlakaṇṭhanāmadhāraṇī and in Tibetan as 'Phags pa nī la kaṇ ṭha zhes bya ba'i gzungs, is a short dhāraṇī text devoted to Avalokiteśvara, the bodhisattva of compassion, here invoked under the name Nīlakaṇṭha, meaning "blue-throated." After paying homage to the Three Jewels, to the Buddha Amitābha, and to the bodhisattva Mahāsthāmaprāpta, it unfolds as a series of heartfelt salutations to Avalokiteśvara. Each one is paired with an aspiration, such as attaining the eye of great wisdom, protecting all sentient beings, gaining skill in means, and boarding the ship of great wisdom. Texts of this kind combine devotional praise with the recitation of a dhāraṇī, a formula of sacred speech believed to carry protective and purifying power. This work is Toh 905 in the Kangyur, where it belongs among the dhāraṇī texts, the class of scriptures centered on such incantations.

In the language of Tibet: The Noble Dhāraṇī Called Nīlakaṇṭha.

Homage to the Three Jewels.

Homage to the Blessed One, the Tathāgata, the arhat, the perfectly complete Buddha Amitābha.

Homage to the bodhisattva, the great being Mahāsthāmaprāpta.

Venerable Avalokiteśvara, compassionate one, homage to you! May I surely attain the eye of great wisdom.

Homage to the venerable Avalokiteśvara, endowed with great compassion! May I surely protect all sentient beings.

Venerable Avalokiteśvara, compassionate one, homage to you! May I surely and swiftly attain skill in means.

Venerable Avalokiteśvara, greatly compassionate one, homage to you! May I surely and swiftly board the ship of great wisdom.

Venerable Avalokiteśvara, compassionate one, homage to you! May I surely and swiftly cross the ocean of suffering.

Venerable Avalokiteśvara, compassionate one, homage to you! May I surely possess the bases of miraculous power and swiftly attain the path.

Venerable Avalokiteśvara, compassionate one, homage to you! May I surely and swiftly climb the mountain of nirvāṇa.

Venerable Avalokiteśvara, compassionate one, homage to you! May I surely and swiftly enter the house of the unconditioned.

Venerable Avalokiteśvara, compassionate one, homage to you! May I surely and swiftly become one with the dharmakāya.

When I go to the mountain of swords, may that mountain of swords crumble and collapse of itself.

When I go into the pit of fire, may that pit of fire vanish of itself.

When I go on the path to the hells, may the hells be emptied by their very nature.

When I go to the world of Yama, may the hungry ghosts be satisfied of themselves.

When I go to the abode of the asuras, may their minds be tamed of themselves.

When I go among the animals, may they attain great wisdom of themselves.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

"Pit of fire" renders a term for a trench or mass of burning embers, one of the hell torments, set here beside the mountain of swords. In one of the homages the word for "compassion" is partly missing in the Tibetan, which reads "lord" in its place. It has been rendered "compassionate one", like the parallel lines. Nīlakaṇṭha means "Blue-Throated One"; the Tibetan title keeps the Sanskrit name and does not gloss it.
