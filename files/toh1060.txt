Dhāraṇī for Pacifying Tumors

This brief text, known in English as The Dhāraṇī for Pacifying Tumors, is a short ritual incantation preserved in Tibetan translation; a Sanskrit title is not clearly attested in the source lines. After paying homage to the Three Jewels, it presents a mantra invoking many-faced powers and calling on them to cut and break apart an affliction, so that it will return to its proper place. It then gives practical instructions. The mantra is to be recited twenty-one times over certain vegetables, including an eggplant and a long gourd, which are then split open, and this is said to make a tumor of the spleen subside without doubt. The work is Toh 1060 in the Kangyur, where it belongs to the large collection of dhāraṇī texts, the short incantations valued for healing and protection.

Homage to the Three Jewels.

oṃ mokha mukhi trimukhi trimukhi sahasramukhi tathāgatāmukhastvihana chinda chinda bhinda bhinda svathānāṃ gacchaslāhe svāhā

Recite this twenty-one times over a madsi, an eggplant, and a long gourd, and then split them open. The tumor of the spleen will then subside. There is no doubt of this.

The dhāraṇī for pacifying tumors is complete.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The three substances are uncertain. "Madsi" is left untranslated because it could not be identified. "Eggplant" takes the Tibetan bartaka as the Sanskrit vārtāka, which means eggplant. "Long gourd" is a tentative rendering of the Tibetan gagon ringpo. The text also leaves unclear what exactly is split open. It may be the substances themselves, as translated here, or it may be a lancing of the swelling.
