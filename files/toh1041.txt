The Dhāraṇī That Increases Wisdom (Prajñāvardhanī-nāma-dhāraṇī)

The Dhāraṇī for Retaining What Has Been Heard is a brief incantatory text belonging to the dhāraṇī genre, short formulas of power recited for specific spiritual and practical benefits. After paying homage to the Three Jewels and to the Buddha Śākyamuni, it gives a single Sanskrit spell invoking wisdom, the lamp of insight, and the dispelling of darkness, followed by a concise ritual instruction. Reciting the dhāraṇī in the morning is said to make one quick to learn, and reciting it over the medicinal herb brāhmī held in cupped hands, then eating the herb, is said to let one retain whatever one has heard. The text is Toh 1041 in the Kangyur, where it is found among the collected dhāraṇīs devoted to memory, learning, and related aims.

Homage to the Three Jewels.
Homage to the Tathāgata, the arhat, the perfectly and completely awakened Buddha Śākyamuni.

tadyathā mati bahulye tathāgatājñāna darśane prajñāpta gambhire bodhimaṇḍānusariṇi abiddha tamo'ndhakārabiddhamani jvala jvala prajñādīpe medhābadini sarba adhiṣṭhita suru suru dhuru dhuru medhā ānaya svāhā

The rite is this. If one recites it in the morning, one will become quick to learn. If one takes the medicinal herb brāhmī in one's cupped hands, recites the dhāraṇī over it until smoke arises, and then eats it, one will retain whatever one has heard.

The Dhāraṇī for Retaining What Has Been Heard is complete.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The herb named in the Tibetan as "bra ma hi" is taken to be brāhmī, a plant traditionally used in India to strengthen memory. The Tibetan "snyim pa" means two hands cupped together, so the herb is held in cupped hands during the recitation.
