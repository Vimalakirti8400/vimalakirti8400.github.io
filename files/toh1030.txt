This is its rite. Merely by reading it, all actions will be accomplished without any need for practice. Diseases of the brain, smoky fevers, vetālas, and grahas will be defeated. Merely by bringing it to mind, all of them will let go.

If one is hungry, one should go to a village and recite it seven times. If one then rubs one's face, one will obtain food.

If one recites it over one's tongue, one will have a winning reply when engaged in a dispute.

Whoever is given a leaf, a flower, or a fruit over which it has been recited will be pleased.

The Dhāraṇī for Accomplishing Actions is complete.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

"So-and-so's" renders a Tibetan phrase inserted into the dhāraṇī. It marks the place where the practitioner says the name of the person concerned. The phrase "smoky fevers" is a literal rendering of a fever type described as "having smoke," which cannot be identified with certainty. In the hunger rite the text does not say what is smeared on the face. It is probably the hand or breath after recitation, or simply the face itself, so the translation keeps it general. Here the glossary term for "defeat" is used in its ordinary sense of spirits being overcome, not as the monastic root downfall.
