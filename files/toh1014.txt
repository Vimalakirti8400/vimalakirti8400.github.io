The Noble Dhāraṇī Called "Thoroughly Pacifying All Illnesses" (Āryasarvarogapraśamanīnāmadhāraṇī)

The Noble Dhāraṇī Called "Thoroughly Pacifying All Illnesses," known in Sanskrit as the Ārya Sarvaroga­praśamani Nāma Dhāraṇī and in Tibetan as Pakpa Né Tamché Rabtu Shiwar Jepa Shejawé Sung, is a brief dhāraṇī text that offers a protective incantation for healing a wide range of bodily ailments. Set in Prince Jeta's Grove at Śrāvastī, it opens with the Buddha urging the assembled monks to take up, retain, recite, and master this teaching and to explain it faithfully to others. He then gives a short mantra and describes it as a basis of secret mantra against afflictions such as indigestion, swelling, bile disorders, hemorrhoids, itching, and diseases of phlegm. The work is Toh 1014 in the Kangyur, where it belongs among the dhāraṇī texts, the class of scriptures devoted to sacred formulas recited for protection and well-being.

In the language of India: ārya sarba roga praśamani nāma dhāraṇī
In the language of Tibet: The Noble Dhāraṇī Called "Thoroughly Pacifying All Illnesses."

Homage to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas.

Thus did I hear at one time. The Blessed One was staying at Śrāvastī, in Prince Jeta's Grove, the park of Anāthapiṇḍada. Then the Blessed One said to the monks:

"Monks, take up this Dharma discourse! Retain it! Recite it! Master it completely! And teach it correctly and extensively to others as well!

tadyathā cilimi cilimi panna kokili śrīmati kuṇḍali dundubhi indrani mukhe svāhā

"These are the bases of secret mantra for indigestion, for swelling, for blood mixed with bile, for those afflicted by hemorrhoids, for those with diseases of the anus, for itching, for those with phlegm disorders, for those with fever, for headache, for pain in one side of the body, and for those seized by hungry ghosts (pretas).

"Ānanda, in the world with its gods I have not seen anyone who could go against these bases of secret mantra, except one whose lifespan has run out.

"These bases of secret mantra were spoken by the Tathāgata, the arhat, the perfectly complete Buddha. Here, the Buddha is supreme among all sentient beings. Freedom from attachment is supreme among all phenomena. The Saṅgha is supreme among all communities.

"By these words of truth and this speech of truth, may whatever I have eaten, drunk, chewed and tasted be well and easily digested! svāhā"

When the Blessed One had spoken thus, those monks rejoiced and praised what the Blessed One had said.

The Noble Dhāraṇī Called "Thoroughly Pacifying All Illnesses" is complete.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The Buddha addresses the monks at the outset and then turns to Ānanda in mid-discourse. Both addressees are kept as the Tibetan gives them. The Buddha says he sees no one "who could go against" these mantra bases, apart from one whose lifespan is exhausted. This means the mantra will help everyone except a person whose life has run its course.
