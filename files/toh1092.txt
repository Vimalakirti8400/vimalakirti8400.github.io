Praise of the Glorious Goddess Sarasvatī (Śrīsarasvatīstotra)

Praise of the Glorious Goddess Sarasvatī, known in Tibetan as Pal Lhamo Drayang la Töpa, is a short devotional hymn addressed to Sarasvatī, the goddess of speech, learning, and eloquence, whom Tibetans call Yangchenma or Drayangma. It opens by taking refuge in the many maṇḍalas of wisdom through which the buddhas arise and the bodhisattva grounds unfold. It then praises Sarasvatī as a sage who has mastered speech and gone beyond the knowledge of the three Vedas. The verses also present her as a bodhisattva who takes up great activity for the sake of beings, practicing the ten grounds and the perfections. The hymn goes on to extol her radiant form, describing her face as a stainless full moon and her eyes as clear with compassion. It is Toh 1092 in the Kangyur, where it belongs to the category of praises or hymns found among the shorter tantric and dhāraṇī-related works of the collection.

Praise of the Glorious Goddess Sarasvatī

By whatever path the buddhas come to be,
And the Victors with their heirs became the masters of the Dharma,
I go for refuge to the many maṇḍalas of wisdom
That lay out the grounds in the stages of knowing.

Homage to the glorious goddess Sarasvatī,
Renowned as one who has mastered speech in this world realm!
A sage, you have gone to the far shore of the knowledge of the three Vedas,
And this has come from your vast deeds.

For the sake of beings you take up the burden of great activity,
And every task that is hard to undertake you accomplish.
By practicing the ten grounds and the perfections,
You have turned a steadfast store of virtue into a wish-fulfilling body.

The maṇḍala of your face is the stainless full moon;
The clear eyes of your compassion open the eyes of beings.
Long have you gazed upon all the world with happiness,
And to you Indra, lord of the gods, and the hosts of Brahmā bow deeply.

Mother who gives birth to love, merit and compassion,
Supreme goddess, you delight in protecting all beings.
Your topknot is like a mountain peak, your eloquence exalted;
Your wisdom is profound, and you are the support of knowledge.

You have mastered the fields of learning, lady of words;
In the world, too, you have become the goddess of gods.
Because you are the supreme friend who strives to dispel suffering,
All the tīrthikas and logicians bow to you as well.

Graceful are your well-formed limbs, and sweet your voice;
Your stainless qualities stand majestic, blazing with merit.
You who bestow the accomplishment of supreme knowledge,
Grant me quick intelligence, retention of words, and the highest eloquence!

Ever intent on the welfare of all beings,
Supreme undeceiving one, you dwell on the ground of liberation.
Upon those who call on you, holding firm to your heart's pledge,
Look with your glory, and fulfill all their aims!

Thus all that appears in the worlds
Has its meaning shown by words that set sounds apart,
And mutual understanding and correct communication as well
Are your power, Sarasvatī, beyond all thought.

You have mastered the power of dhāraṇī and Dharma;
Sage whose pledges are accomplished in truth, endowed with knowledge,
You bless body, mind and speech,
And with your lovely voice you satisfy the world.

Whoever is devoted, for the sake of the conduct of supreme awakening,
And longs for a supremely pure body, speech and mind
That brings to perfection the aims of self and others,
To them the Blessed Lady always grants the highest goal.

I pray: make me courageous and diligent on the path of awakening,
With a pure faculty of the tongue and disciplined words,
Possessed of the treasure of knowledge, unhindered in speech,
And bring about in me the eloquence of the Sugatas.

Your nature, mother of knowledge, is free of conceptual thought;
Your banner of fame is waved by the force of joy.
As with one who would inquire into the manifold forms of the sky,
Your characteristics are hard to fathom; your qualities have no bottom and no end.

namo bhagavate brahmane namaḥsvarasvatyai devi siddhantu mantra pādataṃ brahmānu manta svāhā

The Praise of the Glorious Goddess Sarasvatī is complete.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

Several readings in this short hymn are uncertain. In the sixth stanza, "mu su" is not a recognizable word. It has been taken as a slip for the term for non-Buddhists (tīrthikas), since they are paired with logicians. In the ninth stanza, "sound-destroying" makes little sense and has been rendered loosely as "words that set sounds apart." In the tenth stanza, "shes nyen can" is unclear and has been read as "endowed with knowledge." The line rendered "as with one who would inquire into the manifold forms of the sky" is an interpretation of an obscure simile.
