The Noble One Hundred and Eight Names of Samantabhadra, together with the Dhāraṇī-Mantra (Ārya-samantabhadra-nāmāṣṭaśataka-dhāraṇī-mantra-sahita)

The Noble One Hundred and Eight Names of Samantabhadra, together with the Dhāraṇī-Mantra, known in Tibetan by a title beginning "Phakpa Kuntu Zangpo," the Noble Samantabhadra, and corresponding to a Sanskrit title along the lines of Ārya Samantabhadra Nāmāṣṭaśataka, is a short devotional and incantatory work centered on the great bodhisattva Samantabhadra, whose name means "All-Good." It opens with an homage praising his great compassion, his clear realization of the Dharma, his nature as vast as space, and his manifold forms. It then relates how Samantabhadra enters a bodhisattva meditative absorption, setting the stage for the recitation of his one hundred and eight names and an accompanying dhāraṇī-mantra, a sacred formula valued for recitation and spiritual protection. The text is Toh 877 in the Kangyur, where it belongs to the class of dhāraṇī and name-litany texts associated with the tantric section of the canon.

The Noble One Hundred and Eight Names of Samantabhadra, together with the Dhāraṇī-Mantra

Homage to the bodhisattva, the great being Samantabhadra, who is endowed with great compassion; who has attained the clear illumination of the Dharma, whose essential nature is equal to the whole of the vast expanse of space without remainder; who has attained an undivided body that radiates throughout the sphere of the earth, and the equality of the bodies of the three times; who has manifold forms; who is supreme among bodhisattva great beings and gives what is most excellent and sublime; whose body of great qualities holds sway over all the grounds.

Then at that time the supreme great bodhisattva Samantabhadra, he of manifold forms, entered into the meditative absorption of the bodhisattvas called the Great Lord of the All-Pervading Grounds.

The very moment the bodhisattva, the great being Samantabhadra, entered into that absorption, bodies as numerous as the atoms in the particles of a great thousandfold world system spread out fully in every direction, and these bodies, contained within the element of space, came to be stationed throughout many spheres of the earth.

Then the tathāgatas within all the infinite buddha fields stretched out their right hands, stroked the head of the bodhisattva, the great being Samantabhadra, and said, "Excellent, excellent, son of noble family! Speak the great dhāraṇī that is the essence of every source of buddhas!"

Then the supreme bodhisattva great being made his body shine with a hundred thousand rays of light, and by this he made all the buddha fields shake at that time. Then he spoke this dhāraṇī:

tadyathā oṃ namo namastri bhuvaneśvare mahābhogāya asamasama amitasama anantasama gaganasama tribhuvanasama aśeṣasama veṣasama parama arthasama svabhāvasama tathātāsama arajaḥsma śuddhisama buddhisama dharmatathātāsama abhiṣamasama samasama ekānāya sāgara viśuddhe āloka hī hī labdha labdha vijaya vijaya oṃ prasagata naya naya hinira adhiṣṭhāna saṃbhoga aṃ analaya laya dharma agra dhara dhara jaya jaya hūṃ hūṃ dhama dhama svāhā

Then the eldest of the Victor's children, the sole protector of all the worlds, who wishes to benefit all sentient beings, the bodhisattva Samantabhadra, looked into the face of the Blessed One, made the sphere of the earth shake, and spoke these words, filling the three worlds completely.

Then, from the maṇḍala filled with great bodhisattvas together with gods and asuras, one whose body was adorned with blazing, shining golden rays of light and with mighty tremors moved from the great vajra-flower seat that spans the whole surface of the earth, rose up from the maṇḍala, let out the sound "Ha ha!", and said:

"Ah, great dhāraṇī, vast and all-pervading ocean of great compassion and of countless qualities! Ah, great source of buddhas, equal to the three realms that have neither end nor center, abiding in order to bring countless realms of sentient beings to full ripening, great dhāraṇī of the maṇḍala of Dharma that has no abiding! Ah, great dhāraṇī that clears away the habitual tendencies of evil cast forward from endless eons, dries up the ocean of afflictions, and scatters the mountain of views!

"Anyone who has not truly gathered, over endless eons, a store of roots of virtue, who has not cultivated the seed of awakening by seeing many hundreds of thousands of sugatas face to face, and who has not attained the complete ocean of the distinctive qualities of the ground of omniscience, anyone other than this cannot even hear this great dhāraṇī, the essence of all the tathāgatas; what need is there to speak of holding it, reading it, or fully mastering it? Even the mere name of this great dhāraṇī is hard to hear in a thousand times ten million eons.

"Ah, those sentient beings in whose hands this great dhāraṇī rests have gained a good gain. They live a good life. They are a good basis for the higher knowledges. Their roots of virtue are perfectly complete. They abide in the domain of the tathāgatas. All their qualities are perfectly complete.

"By merely reciting it once, one will be freed from all the sufferings of the lower realms. By merely reciting it twice, one will be released from all evil. By merely reciting it three times, one will be released from all afflictions. Thus, by mere recitation one attains qualities higher and higher, and one will also see the Tathāgata.

"If one recites it while walking back and forth, one will be freed from all suffering. If one recites it at the foot of a solitary tree, one will obtain all accomplishments. If one recites it while traveling, all sentient beings will come under one's power. If one sits cross-legged in a great forest and recites it seven times, one will attain great meditative concentration and will also be free of afflictions.

"Sitting cross-legged before an image of noble Samantabhadra, one should make offerings of as many flowers and other things as one can, and recite this great dhāraṇī until the body grows tired. If, once rested, one lies down right there, one will see the bodhisattva Samantabhadra in a dream. With his right hand he strokes that person and fully comforts him, and he also bestows the great light of the Dharma. He also says, 'Excellent, excellent, child of the Victor! You have entered the great maṇḍala of the Tathāgata. When you have passed from this life, you will attain the great ground.' Then one will wake up endowed with ease of body, ease of mind, great joy, serene faith, and supreme great delight. One will also become renowned for great power and great might. The gods too will come to see that person.

"Rising in the morning, one should read the sūtras compiled by the bodhisattvas. One should also generate a mind intent on the benefit and happiness of all sentient beings. Then all the perfections will be brought to completion, one will be free of all evil, the forces of affliction will be vanquished, and one will be praised even by the tathāgatas.

"The gods and others will also strive to guard, protect, and shelter that person. That child of the Victors who acts in this way, just as taught, will before long enter the great ground of Samantabhadra."

Then all who had gathered in the great maṇḍala rose. There were Vajroṣṇīṣa and the other great vidyādharas, lords of the trichiliocosm; Mahālocanā and the other great female vidyādharas; Vajragarbha and the other bodhisattva great beings; Vajrapāśa and the other great gods of the vidyā-mantras; Ratnajālinī and the other great goddesses of the vidyā-mantras; the goddess Tārā and the other deities of the great bodhisattva's maṇḍala; the youths Great Silk, Surūpa, Most Lovely to Behold, and others; the maidens, the servants, and the brahmin women; Akṣobhya and the other great vināyakas; Supreme Body and the other lords of the rākṣasas; the mātṛkās and the other rākṣasīs; Maheśvara and the other lords of the gods; and Vajra Flower Crown and all the others, the bodhisattvas and so on, assembled in the great maṇḍala. This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The word rendered "seat" (Tibetan thod stan) is unusual; it is taken here as a seat or cushion on which the being was sitting. The text does not name the being who rises from the maṇḍala and praises the dhāraṇī, so the translation leaves that person unnamed, as the Tibetan does. The name of the samādhi contains the Tibetan equivalent of "Maheśvara," but here it is part of a descriptive name, "Great Lord of the All-Pervading Grounds," and does not refer to Śiva. "The three realms" renders sa gsum, the three levels (below, on, and above the earth), not the technical three realms of desire, form, and formlessness.  Several retinue names could not be identified with confidence and are translated literally: Great Silk, Most Lovely to Behold, Supreme Body, and Vajra Flower Crown. The line rendered "Possessed of knowledge" reads shes nyen can, whose second syllable is unclear; the sense given is conjectural. Some epithets, such as "dispeller of the ten" and "purifier of the great occasion," are terse in the Tibetan and are rendered literally. The line "Clearing away everywhere, throughout the infinite expanse" reads sel mdzad pa, "dispelling." If it is a scribal variant of gsal, the sense would be "illuminating." Lha chen, "the Great God," and Maheśvara are epithets of Śiva.  The second line reads ཞེས་བཟང་པོ་རྟག་ཏུ་མངའ. It is a syllable short, and ཞེས makes little sense there. It may be a slip for ཤེས, "excellent knowing," or the line may have lost a syllable. I have translated it loosely as "forever endowed with excellence."

མཆོག་རྣམས་ཀྱིས་ནི་བསྟོད་ནས་ཀྱང can also be read agentively: "having been praised by the supreme ones." The rendering "with these supreme names" supplies "names" from context.

In the final stanzas, སྤོ is read as སྤོས, "fragrance," in line with the earlier phrase ཡོན་ཏན་བརྒྱ་ཡི་སྤོས.

ཡོན་ཏན་རྒྱས is literally "whose qualities are expanded" or "flourish." "Extolled" interprets this.
