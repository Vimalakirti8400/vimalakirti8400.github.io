The Dhāraṇī for Seeing Dreams (Svapnadarśanadhāraṇī)

The Dhāraṇī for Seeing Dreams is a very brief ritual text whose Sanskrit and Tibetan titles are not given in its opening lines, though its closing line names it simply as the dhāraṇī called Seeing Dreams. It opens with homage to the Three Jewels and to Vajrapāṇi, the fierce great general of the yakṣas, and then gives a short mantra. Its rite is simple: the practitioner recites the mantra over a palmful of water, drinks it, and lies down to sleep with a wish held in mind, and Vajrapāṇi will then reveal a dream in response. The work is catalogued as Toh 952 in the Kangyur, where it belongs among the dhāraṇī texts, the short collections of protective and ritual incantations within the canon.

Homage to the Three Jewels.
Homage to the great yakṣa general, the fierce Vajrapāṇi.

oṃ mucini svāhā mukhese svāhā mohani svāhā dantiri svāhā

The rite is this: recite the mantra over a palmful of water and then drink it. If one then lies down to sleep with one's wish held in mind, Vajrapāṇi will reveal a dream.

The dhāraṇī called Seeing Dreams is complete.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The phrase rendered "with one's wish held in mind" could also mean "as one wishes" or "thinking of it." In either reading, the practitioner goes to sleep keeping the intention in mind.
