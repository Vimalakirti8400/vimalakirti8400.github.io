The Means of Accomplishment of Glorious Hevajra (Śrīhevajrasādhana)

The Means of Accomplishment of Glorious Hevajra, in Sanskrit Śrī Hevajrasādhana and in Tibetan Dpal dgyes pa rdo rje'i sgrub thabs, is a short tantric practice manual, a sādhana, for the meditative realization of the deity Hevajra. Written largely in verse, it guides the practitioner from the preliminary offerings, refuge and the four immeasurables through meditation on emptiness, the visualization of the protective enclosure and the celestial palace, and the generation of Hevajra embraced by his consort and surrounded by the eight yoginīs, on to empowerment, the completion stage and mantra recitation, closing with brief descriptions of his four-armed and six-armed forms. Its colophon presents it as drawn from the oral teaching of the master Nāropa. It is Toh 1292 in the Tengyur, where it belongs to the tantra commentaries, among the many ritual and meditative works on the Hevajra cycle.

In the language of India: śrī hevajrasādhana. In the language of Tibet: The Means of Accomplishment of Glorious Hevajra.

Homage to Vajrasattva.

With complete faith, to the lotus
Of the venerable lord's feet I bow with reverence,
And for the benefit of those of lesser understanding
I write this means of accomplishment of Hevajra.

Here, one who has the special qualities of a support, possessed of the common and the uncommon vows, in a place such as a charnel ground, rises in the eighth part of the night, bathes, and makes the water offering. Having offered a maṇḍala, one then sets out the offerings, the torma and so forth, and sits in the vajra cross-legged posture on a clean seat. By saying hevajra, one instantly visualizes oneself as Heruka with one face and two arms, and after that:

In one's own heart visualize a repha;
From it arises the disk of the sun.
Upon it is the very form of hūṃ,
Whose nature is method and wisdom.
By the light rays radiating from it,
One beholds the venerable lord in the sky:
Vajra-born, the one of great compassion,
Worshipped by the eight goddesses
Who wear all the ornaments.
Gaurī holds the deer-marked moon,
Caurī the vessel of the one victorious over Māra,
Vetālī has water in her hand,
Ghasmarī holds medicine,
Pukkasī has a vajra in her hand,
Likewise Śavarī holds the elixir,
Caṇḍālī beats the ḍamaru drum.
These worship the lord.
Ḍombinī, following in great passion,
Embraces his body.

Visualizing such a field of accumulation with the mind as though directly present, one performs prostration; the outer, inner, secret and suchness offerings; the confession of wrongdoing; rejoicing in merit; going for refuge; showing the path; the generation of the mind of awakening whose nature is conventional and ultimate; and the dedication of the roots of virtue. Then one lets the field of accumulation go with the mind, or requests them to depart.

Then first one should cultivate loving-kindness;
Second, cultivate compassion;
Third, cultivate joy;
And at the end of all, equanimity.
Then again comes the awakening of emptiness;

and this too is preceded by emanation and gathering. Saying oṃ śūnyatājñānavajrasvabhāva ātmako'haṃ, one meditates on all phenomena as mere illusion.

Having first visualized a sun from a repha,

And of that very vajra a fence

Within it, from what arises from bhrūṃ,

In the sphere of space imagine a bhaga.
In its center one visualizes,

First the wheels of earth and water,
Then, as is fitting, fire,

Above them, from bhrūṃ, a wheel,

With nets and half-nets,

Ornamented within and without with vajra lines.
At the hub visualize a corpse,
Said to be of the nature of the sphere of reality.
On the eight petals imagine
Brahmā, Indra, Upendra (Viṣṇu), Rudra,
Yama and the Lord of Wealth,
Nairṛti and Vemacitrin,

Imagine the nine corpses together with suns.
āli is the moon, kāli the sun;
The yoginī meditates on the two in the sky.
Between the sun and moon joined face to face in the center,
The nāda falls and becomes hūṃ.
From hūṃ one should emanate gaṃ and the rest:

gaṃ caṃ baṃ ghaṃ paṃ śaṃ laṃ ḍaṃ, which are Gaurī and the others.

From their emanation and gathering,
The hand emblem of the central lord
Is a vajra arising from hūṃ,
Black in color, greatly terrifying.
At the center of the vajra's hub
Again visualize that very hūṃ.
For the retinue, curved knife and small drum,
Tortoise and snake, are in the cardinal directions;
Lion and monk,
Wheel and vajra, are in the intermediate directions,
Each marked by its own seed.
Again by emanation and gathering the seeds are gathered;
From that comes the fifth: the image is complete.
Seeing it turned into the form of hūṃ,
Visualize oneself as the very self of aversion:
Vajra-born, the Great Black One,
Colored like a blue lotus,

With faith, with certainty, visualize him.
His color is like blue and the sun together;

Yellow hair coiled upward,

Wheel, earrings, necklace,

They are the five buddhas in their purity;
These are well known as the mudrās.

In his left a vajra skull cup,
And from the left as well a khaṭvāṅga;
In his right a black vajra.
The lord who sounds the syllable hūṃ,
Encircled by the eight yoginīs,

In the quarter of the Lord of Waters, Vetālī at play,

Gaurī is black in color;

Ghasmarī is like emerald;
Pukkasī is like sapphire;

On the ears Dveṣavajrī,
On the nose the one called Mātsaryā,
On the mouth Rāgavajrī,
On the body Īrṣyāvajrī,

For the purification of the senses,

At the crown Bhūcarī, at the throat Khecarī,

Pukkasī with the mudrā of aversion;

Caurī with the mudrā of delusion;
Vetālī with the mudrā of avarice;
Ghasmarī with the mudrā of passion.
One should unite in passion with the khaṭvāṅga.
Meditate on the completion stage, self-blessing and the rest.
Then the eight yoginīs
Are gathered into the hūṃ at the heart,
And one meditates on the sealing within it.
After that, one recollects the purity.
By the sequence of the four methods of recitation,
Recite the secret mantra that purifies the obscurations of speech:

oṃ trailokya ākṣepa hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ svāhā. One should perform the recitation of this mantra.

Give the torma endowed with the four suchnesses.
Make aspirations and then go about one's conduct.
Continually meditate on the yoga of the four sessions.
By that very sequence of the rite:

The four arms are the purity of being victorious over the four māras. He has the form and body color described above, and arises from hūṃ. In the first left hand is a human skull cup filled with the blood of gods and asuras; likewise in the right hand a vajra; and with the remaining two arms he embraces the wisdom consort. The wisdom consort, Vajravārāhī, has the form of the Blessed One. Her mantra is oṃ jvala jvala bhyo hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ svāhā.

He is six-armed and three-faced: the left face red, the right like the moon and the sun, and the first blue. He is naked, with the body color and form described above. The arms are the purity of the perfections. In the first left hand is a trident, in the first right a vajra; in the second left a bell, and in the second right hand a curved knife. With the remaining two arms he embraces the consort Śṛṅkhalā in the manner of union. She too, just like the Blessed One, is the wisdom consort, holding a curved knife and a skull cup in her right and left hands, and trampling a corpse that embodies the three realms. Her mantra is oṃ kiṭi kiṭi vajra hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ svāhā.

Relying on the glorious Five Hundred Thousand,
Made clear by the words of the guru Vajrasattva, I have set this out;
By this virtue may all these beings cross the ocean of existence
And become Hevajra himself, the one sole culmination of bliss.

The means of accomplishment of glorious Hevajra, the principal deity with the three beings, compiled from the oral teaching of the lord Nāropa, is complete.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

"Caurī the vessel of the one victorious over Māra": the Tibetan reads literally so; in the Hevajra Tantra Caurī holds a vessel of the sun, and "victor over Māra" may here stand for the sun. In Caurī's left hand the text writes ཕྱག་པ, which makes no sense; read as ཕག་པ, "boar", in keeping with her iconography. "Vetālī at play": the text literally says "the one at play" (རོལ་པ་མོ) in the place where the sequence requires Vetālī. The line དགུང་གཉིས་རྣལ་འབྱོར་མ་ཡིས་བསྒོམ is obscure and its rendering ("the yoginī meditates on the two in the sky") is tentative. "Showing the path" (ལམ་སྟོན་པ) in the list of preliminary practices is rendered literally; its exact sense as a limb of the practice is unclear. "Pale red" renders པནྡུ་དམར་པོ (pāṇḍu, pale) as written. "Vajra-born" (རྡོ་རྗེ་སྐྱེ་བ) is an epithet of Hevajra.  "The three nested beings" are the commitment, wisdom and concentration beings (samaya-, jñāna- and samādhisattva). "The glorious Five Hundred Thousand" refers to the root Hevajra Tantra said to be of five hundred thousand lines. The consort ལུ་གུ་རྒྱུད་མ ("the chain lady") is identified as Śṛṅkhalā, known in the Hevajra cycle as Vajraśṛṅkhalā; the text here does not include "vajra" in her name. The colophon's "the principal deity with the three beings" renders གཙོ་བོ་སེམས་དཔའ་གསུམ literally.
