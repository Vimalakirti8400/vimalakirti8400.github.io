Thus did I hear at one time. The Blessed One was residing in the realm of the Thirty-Three Gods, seated upon a flat stone like a white crystal slab. Now, Śakra, the lord of the gods (Indra, king of the gods), had been defeated and utterly routed by the asuras (demigods), and in his defeat he made his way in a shrunken and shriveled form to where the Blessed One was. Having arrived, he prostrated with his head at the feet of the Blessed One and spoke these words to him:

"Blessed One, I have engaged the asuras in battle and have been defeated and completely routed by Vemacitrin, the lord of the asuras. The gods of the Thirty-Three as well have been defeated and completely routed. Blessed One, what are we to do about this?"

The Blessed One declared: "Lord of the gods, you should take up this dhāraṇī called 'The Armlet of the Victory Banner's Peak,' which cannot be overcome by others. I too, in a former life when I was a bodhisattva, received this from a Tathāgata whose banner of invincibility could not be overcome by others, and having received it, I expounded it widely and correctly to others. From that time forward, I never once experienced fear, or dread, or the hair of my body standing on end, nor did I experience any harm to my body even for an instant."

The Blessed One was then asked: "What, Blessed One, is this dhāraṇī called 'The Armlet of the Victory Banner's Peak,' which cannot be overcome by others?"

By this dhāraṇī called the Banner Crest Shoulder Ornament of the King of Gods, which none can overcome, you shall be fearless in battle, in combat, in dispute, in conflict, and wherever you may go — you shall be victorious in all things. If it is placed at the tip of a victory banner or worn about the neck, it protects all kings among men and all heroic warriors. Taking the form of a woman and standing before one, it bestows fearlessness and protection. It produces splendour, it routs the enemy host, and it causes auspiciousness, purity, renown, glory, and all excellence to be present at all times.

When the Blessed One had spoken thus, Śakra (Indra, lord of the gods), the gods, humans, asuras, and gandharvas — all the world — rejoiced, and they praised what the Blessed One had taught.

The noble dhāraṇī called the Banner Crest Shoulder Ornament is complete.
