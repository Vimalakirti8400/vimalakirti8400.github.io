The Two-Verse Dhāraṇī (Gāthādvitīyadhāraṇī)

The Two-Verse Dhāraṇī, known in Sanskrit as the Gāthādvitīyadhāraṇī and in Tibetan as Tshigs su bcad pa gnyis pa'i gzungs, is a brief dhāraṇī text that opens with homage to the youthful Mañjuśrī. Its two verses name the obscurations that hinder sentient beings, such as contempt for the Buddha and the Dharma, laziness, complacency, desire, pride, remorse, and unsteady conduct, and they present the supreme vehicle as the antidote through which these faults are entirely abandoned. A short Sanskrit mantra follows, and the text then promises that anyone who applies the dhāraṇī, whether through its words or its meaning, will gain ten kinds of benefit, including a well-nourished constitution and a favorable passing at the time of death. It is Toh 918 in the Kangyur, where it belongs among the collected dhāraṇī texts, the incantations valued for their protective and purifying power.

In the language of India: gāthādvitīyadhāranī
In the language of Tibet: The Two-Verse Dhāraṇī

Homage to the youthful Mañjuśrī.

Contempt for the Buddha and the Dharma,
laziness, contentment with a little,
indulgence in desire and in pride,
remorse, and wavering conduct:
these are the obscurations of sentient beings.
As their antidote the supreme vehicle is taught,
and through it every fault
of those hindrances is utterly abandoned.

tadyathā oṃ vajraprakāra vajraprakāra vajracakra daṃṣṭābhayanake amale vimale nirmale culuke culuculu sarvabuddhe svāhā

Whoever applies the Two-Verse Dhāraṇī
by way of its words or its meaning,
that excellent being endowed with intelligence
will obtain ten kinds of benefit.
All one's elements will be fully nourished;
at the time of death one attains the highest, sublime joy;
one is born just as one wishes;
in every life one remembers one's former lives;
one meets the buddhas,
and likewise hears the supreme vehicle from them;
one gains devotion together with understanding;
one obtains the two doors, and swiftly attains awakening.

The Two-Verse Dhāraṇī is complete.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The title "ཚིགས་སུ་བཅད་པ་གཉིས་པ" could also be read as "the second verse." It is rendered here as "two-verse," following the sense of a dhāraṇī framed by verses. The list of obscurations corresponds to a known Yogācāra list: contempt for the Buddha and Dharma, laziness, contentment with little, conduct of desire and of pride, remorse, and the conduct of those who are not yet settled in a vehicle. "Wavering conduct" (མ་ངེས་སྤྱོད་པ) translates this last item, the conduct of the "undetermined."

"The two doors" (སྒོ་གཉིས) is not explained in the text. It most likely refers to the two gateways of dhāraṇī and samādhi. It could also refer to the word and meaning mentioned just before, but this is uncertain.

The ten benefits do not divide cleanly as written. "Devotion together with understanding" (མོས་པ་བློ་དང་བཅས་པ) may count as two items, or the two doors may count as two.

The khams (elements) that are nourished are presumably the bodily constituents, but the text does not say "of the body."
