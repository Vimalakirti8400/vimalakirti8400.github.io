The Noble King of Aspiration Prayers for Excellent Conduct (Āryabhadracaryāpraṇidhānarāja)

The Noble King of Aspiration Prayers for Excellent Conduct, known in Sanskrit as the Ārya Bhadracaryāpraṇidhānarāja and in Tibetan as Pakpa Zangpo Chöpé Mönlam gyi Gyalpo, is one of the most beloved prayers in the Mahāyāna tradition. Written in verse and opening with homage to the youthful Mañjuśrī, it voices the bodhisattva's vast aspiration. It begins by bowing to all the buddhas of the three times and ten directions, envisioning countless buddhas present on every single atom, and goes on to express the wish to make offerings, confess faults, rejoice in virtue, and dedicate all merit toward the awakening of beings. The prayer is closely associated with the bodhisattva Samantabhadra and is traditionally linked to the culmination of the Gaṇḍavyūha section of the Avataṃsaka Sūtra. Recited widely in daily practice across Tibetan Buddhism, it appears as Toh 1095 in the Kangyur, where it is included among the collected dhāraṇīs and prayers.

In the language of India: āryabhadracāryapraṇidhānarājā. In the language of Tibet: The Noble King of Aspiration Prayers for Excellent Conduct.

Homage to the noble Mañjuśrī, who has become youthful.

However many lions among humans there are,
Who have gone in the three times in the worlds of the ten directions,
To all of them, without exception,
I bow with body, speech, and mind made clear with faith.

By the power of this aspiration for excellent conduct,
With all the Victors present before my mind,
Bowing with bodies as numerous as the atoms in the realms,
I pay homage to all the Victors.

On a single atom are buddhas as numerous as atoms,
Seated amid the children of the Buddha.
Just so, I trust that the entire expanse of phenomena, without exception,
Is filled completely with the Victors.

With inexhaustible oceans of their praises,
With all the sounds of an ocean of melodious tones,
I proclaim the qualities of all the Victors
And praise all the Sugatas.

With the finest flowers and the finest garlands,
With cymbals, ointments, and the best of parasols,
With the best of lamps and the finest incense,
I make offerings to those Victors.

With the finest garments and the best of fragrances,
With heaps of powders equal to Mount Meru,
And with all the supreme and especially exalted arrays,
I make offerings to those Victors.

Whatever offerings there are, unsurpassed and vast,
I envision those too for all the Victors.
By the power of faith in excellent conduct,
I pay homage and make offerings to all the Victors.

Whatever wrongs I have committed
Under the sway of attachment, aversion, and delusion,
With body, with speech, and likewise with mind,
All of them I confess, each one.

In whatever merit there is of all the Victors of the ten directions,
Of the children of the Buddha, of the pratyekabuddhas,
Of those still in training and those beyond training,
And of all beings, in all of it I rejoice.

Those who are lamps of the worlds of the ten directions,
Who through the stages of awakening have attained unattached buddhahood,
All those protectors I urge
To turn the unsurpassed wheel.

To those who intend to show the passing into nirvāṇa,
For the benefit and happiness of all beings,
I also pray with palms joined
That they remain for as many eons as there are atoms in the realms.

Whatever slight virtue I have gathered
Through homage, offering, and confession,
Through rejoicing, urging, and praying,
All of it I dedicate for the sake of awakening.

May offerings be made to the buddhas of the past
And to those who dwell in the worlds of the ten directions.
May those who have not yet come very swiftly
Fulfill their intentions and, through the stages of awakening, attain buddhahood.

However many realms there are in all ten directions,
May they become vast and utterly pure,
And may they be completely filled with Victors who have gone to the foot of the lordly tree of awakening,
And with the children of the Buddha.

However many sentient beings there are in the ten directions,
May they always be free of illness and happy.
May the aims of all beings accord with the Dharma,
And may their hopes be fulfilled as well.

May I practice the conduct of awakening,
And remember my births in all destinies.
In all my lives, as I die, transmigrate, and am born,
May I always go forth into homelessness.

Training in the footsteps of all the Victors,
Bringing excellent conduct to perfection,
May I always practice moral discipline, stainless and utterly pure,
Never failing and without fault.

In the language of the gods, in the languages of nāgas and yakṣas,
Of kumbhāṇḍas and of humans,
And in as many sounds as there are among all beings,
In every language I shall teach the Dharma.

Gentle, and striving earnestly in the perfections,
May I never forget the mind of awakening.
Whatever wrongs have become obscurations,
May they all be purified without exception.

Freed from karma, from afflictions, and from the works of māras,
May I act among the beings of the world
Just as a lotus is not stained by water,
And as the sun and moon move unobstructed through the sky.

Throughout the whole extent of the realms and the directions,
May I fully pacify the suffering of the lower realms,
Establish all beings in happiness,
And act for the benefit of all beings.

Bringing the conduct of awakening to perfection,
Engaging in accord with the conduct of sentient beings,
Fully teaching excellent conduct,
May I practice it throughout all future eons.

May I always keep company
With those whose conduct accords with mine.
In body, in speech, and also in mind,
May we practice as one in conduct and in aspiration.

May I always meet with those friends
Who wish to benefit me
And who fully teach excellent conduct,
And may I never disappoint them.

May I always see directly the Victors,
The protectors surrounded by the children of the Buddha,
And throughout all future eons, never wearying,
May I make vast offerings to them as well.

Upholding the sacred Dharma of the Victors,
Illuminating the conduct of awakening everywhere,
And thoroughly practicing excellent conduct,
I shall practice throughout all future eons.

While wandering through all states of existence,
May I find inexhaustible merit and wisdom,
And become an inexhaustible treasury of skillful means, wisdom, concentration, the liberations,
And all good qualities.

On a single atom are realms as numerous as atoms,
And in those realms are inconceivable buddhas
Seated amid the children of the Buddha.
Practicing the conduct of awakening, I shall behold them.

Just so, in all directions without exception,
In the space of a single hair tip, as many as measure the three times,
Are oceans of buddhas and oceans of realms;
Practicing for oceans of eons, I enter them fully.

The speech of all the Victors, a single utterance with the sound of an ocean of qualities,
Its melodious qualities utterly pure,
A melody suited to the inclinations of all beings:
I shall always engage with the speech of the Buddha.

All the Victors who have gone in the three times
Turn the modes of the wheel;
Into the inexhaustible melody of their speech
I too enter fully by the power of understanding.

Into all future eons
I too shall enter in a single instant.
Whatever eons there are that measure the three times,
I shall enter them in a fraction of an instant.

Whatever lions among humans have gone in the three times,
I shall behold them in a single instant.
Always I shall enter into their sphere of activity
By the power of the liberation that is like an illusion.

Whatever arrays of realms there are in the three times,

I bring them forth upon a single atom.
Thus in all directions without exception
I enter the arrays of the realms of the Victors.

All the lamps of the world who have not yet come,
As they in sequence awaken, turn the wheel,
And reveal nirvāṇa, the final utter peace,
Into the presence of all these protectors I shall go.

By the powers of swift miraculous ability in every way,
By the powers of the vehicle that is a door on every side,
By the powers of conduct endowed with every good quality,
By the power of loving-kindness that pervades all,

By the powers of merit that is virtuous in every way,
By the power of wisdom that has become free of attachment,
And by the powers of discernment, skillful means and concentration,
I fully accomplish the powers of awakening.

I purify completely the powers of karma,
I utterly vanquish the powers of the afflictions,
I render the powers of the māras utterly powerless,
And I shall perfect the power of excellent conduct.

I purify oceans of realms,
I liberate oceans of sentient beings,
I see clearly oceans of dharmas,
I fully realize oceans of wisdom.

I purify oceans of conduct,
I fully perfect oceans of aspirations,
I make offerings to oceans of buddhas,
And for oceans of eons I shall practice without weariness.

All the particular aspirations for the conduct of awakening
Of the Victors who have gone in the three times,
I shall fulfill completely, every one,
Awakening to buddhahood through excellent conduct.

The eldest of the sons of all the Victors
Is the one whose name is Samantabhadra.
In order to act in the same way as that wise one,
I fully dedicate all of these virtues.

Pure in body, speech and mind as well,
Pure in conduct, with realms completely pure,
And with dedication like that of the wise Samantabhadra,
Just so may I too become equal to him.

In order to practice excellent virtue in every way,
I shall practice the aspirations of Mañjuśrī.
Throughout all future eons, without weariness,
I shall bring all his deeds to completion.

May my conduct never become limited;
May my good qualities too be beyond measure.
Abiding in conduct that is measureless,
I shall come to know all their emanations.

As far as the limits of space extend,
So too extend the limits of all sentient beings;
And as far as their karma and afflictions extend,
So far extend the limits of my aspiration.

One might adorn the boundless realms of the ten directions
With jewels and offer them to the Victors,
And offer too the supreme joys of gods and humans
For as many eons as there are atoms in those realms;

Yet one who, having heard this king of dedications,
Is filled with longing for supreme awakening
And gives rise to faith even a single time
Will gain from this merit more sublime and supreme than that.

Whoever makes this aspiration for excellent conduct
Will thereby leave behind all the lower realms.
They will have left behind bad companions,
And swiftly they will behold Amitābha.

They will gain great gain and live in happiness;
Even in this human life they will have come well.
Just as Samantabhadra is,
So before long will they become.

The evil deeds of the five acts of immediate retribution,
Whoever has committed them under the sway of ignorance,
If they recite this Excellent Conduct,
All will swiftly be purified without remainder.

They will be endowed with wisdom, with form and the major marks,
With noble family and with fine complexion.
The many māras and non-Buddhists (tīrthikas) will not overcome them,
And they will be honored throughout the three worlds.

Swiftly they will go to the foot of the king of bodhi trees,
And having gone, they will remain there for the benefit of beings.
Awakening to buddhahood, they will turn the wheel,
And they will subdue all the māras together with their hosts.

Whoever holds, teaches or recites
This aspiration for excellent conduct,
The buddhas know the ripening of that;
Do not doubt supreme awakening.

Just as Mañjuśrī knows and is heroic,
And just as Samantabhadra too,
I train in following after them,
And fully dedicate all of these virtues.

With that dedication which all the Victors
Who have gone in the three times praise as supreme,
I shall fully dedicate all these roots of my virtue
For the sake of excellent conduct.

When the time of my death has come,
May I dispel all obscurations,
Behold Amitābha face to face,
And go to that realm of Sukhāvatī.

Having gone there, may all these aspirations
Become manifest, every one without exception.
May I fulfill them all without remainder
And benefit beings for as long as the world remains.

In that excellent and joyful maṇḍala of the Victor,
Born from a sublime and most beautiful lotus,
May I there receive a prophecy
Directly from the Victor Amitābha.

Having received the prophecy there,
Through many emanations, a billion in number,
By the power of intelligence in the ten directions
I shall bring great benefit to sentient beings.

By making the aspiration for excellent conduct,
Whatever slight virtue I have gathered,
By that may all the virtuous aspirations of beings
Be fulfilled in a single instant.

By dedicating the aspiration for excellent conduct,
Whatever boundless sublime merit I have gained,
By that may the beings sunk in the river of suffering
Attain the supreme abode of Amitābha.

By this king of aspirations, supreme and foremost,
Bringing benefit to all the boundless beings,
May the scripture adorned by Samantabhadra be accomplished,
And may the three lower realms be emptied without remainder.

Here ends The Noble King of Aspiration Prayers for Excellent Conduct.

This recension differs at points from the most familiar version of the prayer. For example, the first verse here reads that one bows with body, speech, and mind made clear with faith. The translation follows this text as it stands. Several lines that end in a simple "become" are translated as wishes, following the dedicatory context. The verb mos, rendered "trust" and "envision," refers to imagining with conviction, as in the visualized offerings. It is distinct from bsngo, "dedicate," which appears in the dedication verse. "Mañjuśrī, who has become youthful" renders the epithet Kumārabhūta. The piece ends mid-verse, and the stanza continues in the next piece.  "The wise Samantabhadra" renders the Tibetan's short form bzang po ("Bhadra"), which here refers to Samantabhadra. "A billion" renders "a hundred koṭis" (a hundred times ten million).
