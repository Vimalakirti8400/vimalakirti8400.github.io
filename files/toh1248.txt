The Sādhana of the Blessed Hevajra Called The Stages of the Four Realities (Bhagavān Hevajra Tattvacaturkrama Sādhana)

The Sādhana of the Blessed Hevajra Called The Stages of the Four Realities, known in Sanskrit as the Bhagavān Hevajra Tattvacatuṣkrama Sādhana and in Tibetan as Chomden Dé Gyepé Dorjé Druppé Tap Dekho Nanyi Zhiyi Rimpa, is a ritual and meditation manual for the practice of Hevajra, the deity of the Hevajra Tantra, together with his consort Nairātmyā. It is attributed to the Indian master Kambala, and is said to have been translated into Tibetan by the paṇḍita Vairocanarakṣita and the translator Lan Darma Lodrö. The work is arranged in four stages of reality. It covers the preparations of compassion, empowerment and a secluded dwelling; the visualization of the maṇḍala and its goddesses; the recitation of their mantras; and meditation on the reality of the self, in which all appearances are seen as illusion-like and nonconceptual. It closes with torma offerings to the guardians of the directions, prayers of aspiration and dedication, and advice for carrying the practice into daily life. It is Toh 1248 in the Tengyur, where it belongs to the tantra commentaries, among the sādhana texts of the Hevajra cycle.

In the language of India: bhagabān hevajra tattva caturkrama sādhanaṃ nāma. In the language of Tibet: The Sādhana of the Blessed Hevajra Called The Stages of the Four Realities.

Homage to glorious Vajrasattva.

Having paid homage to Heruka, nondual skillful means and wisdom,
Together with the goddess Nairātmyā,
For those who have attained that reality and have faith and good fortune,
I shall write The Stages of the Four Realities.

First, look upon beings with great compassion.
Purified by the restoration ceremony, train your mind streams step by step.
Enter the maṇḍala of this tantra and receive the empowerments;
Knowing reality, and with permission granted, meditate on the four abodes.

In a charnel ground or another secluded and delightful place,
Well seated on a seat of crossed vajras,
To guard the three, oneself, the yoga and the site,
First bathe with the five nectars and the rest.
Meditate on the four immeasurables, and view the deity and oneself.
Reciting the protection mantra rakṣa, make the net and tent.
On a well-placed support set the five nectars and five lamps;
Make offerings of flowers and the rest, and cast the torma.
With a field-born consort or the like, a well-trained knowledge consort,
With good companions as support, or else alone, practice without fear.

From the black seed hūṃ in one's own heart
Five lights radiate, summoning and purifying, and as the support in space
The guru and the wheel of Hevajra are invited.
Having meditated on them well, as below, perform prostrations and the rest.
Oneself, emanating and gathering back through the hūṃ,
Makes offerings in sequence through the outer, inner and secret goddesses.
White Gaurī holds the emblem of the deer;
Red Caurī carries the self-arisen flower;
Yellow Vetālī has water in her hand;
Green Ghasmarī holds the secret medicine;
Blue Pukkasī has various kinds of flesh in her hand;
White Śavarī has various tastes in her hand;
Red Caṇḍālī offers sound and a mass of jewels.
The inner offering is made to the three bodies with great bliss:
Ḍombī, the seal, embraces one's own body.
The offering of reality is nonduality.
Confession of wrongdoing, rejoicing, dedication, the offering of one's body,
The teaching of the path and supplication;
Meditate on the four immeasurables, and after the deities have departed,
Phenomena are illusion, one's own mind free of dust.

Recite oṃ śūnyatājñāna vajra svabhāva ātmako'haṃ, and from recollecting the meaning of that mantra, abide in one's own reality.

From that very nature of reality, raṃ and hūṃ
Become the disc of the sun and the net and tent.
From that, out of e, meditate on the source of phenomena.
Then yaṃ raṃ vaṃ laṃ,
The blue, red, white and yellow seeds,

With nets and half nets and toraṇas,

The consort is of the same color as the father,
With one face and four arms; the first two,
With vajra and bell, embrace the father,

She is adorned with a garland of fifty dry skulls;
Her bone ornaments are like the father's.
The principal one, with four legs, the right extended,
Stands upon Śakra (Indra), Brahmā, Upendra and Rudra,
Pressed down by the disc of the sun.
Meditate on him as transformed by the nine dance moods:
Terrifying, erotic, scornful,
Laughing, fierce, compassionate, awesome and peaceful.

Bless the lotus and the vajra:

oṃ vajra mahādveṣa catur ānanda dāyaka khagamukhe karaso nātha hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ karyaṃ kuru śva me.

As soon as the maṇḍala has been emanated,
The deities enter the mouth and abide in the heart;
Reciting anurāga, they come forth.
gaṃ caṃ vaṃ ghaṃ paṃ śaṃ laṃ ḍaṃ:
These eight radiate in the directions and intermediate directions
Onto the seats of the deities,
And from the five awakenings the goddesses are generated.

Arising from gaṃ and a curved knife,
White Gaurī, with four arms,
Holds arrow, bow, curved knife, and a skull cup of blood;
On the eastern petal she presses down Brahmā.
Generated from caṃ and a ḍamaru,
Red Caurī, with four arms,
Holds wheel, hook and ḍamaru;
On the southern petal she presses down Śakra.
Formed from vaṃ and a tortoise,
Vetālī, white, with four arms,
Holds liquor, water, sword and skull cup;
On the western lotus she presses down Upendra.
Truly generated from ghaṃ and a snake,
Ghasmarī is green, with four arms,
With a hearth, an axe, a skull cup filled with blood,
And the gesture of fearlessness; she stands upon Rudra.
She is to be meditated on, on the northern petal.
Arising from paṃ and a lion,
Pukkasī, yellow, with four arms,
Has a vine of the wish-granting tree, a skull cup filled with flesh,
A jewel and the boon-granting gesture;
Meditate on her in the northeast, pressing down Yama.
Born from śaṃ and a monk,
Śavarī, white, with four arms,
Holds khaṭvāṅga, skull cup, vajra and noose;
Meditate on her in the southeast, pressing down a yakṣa.
Generated from laṃ and a wheel,
Caṇḍālī, blue, with four arms,
Holds a windcloth, a banner and a white skull cup;
Meditate on her in the southwest, pressing down a rākṣasa.
Arising from ḍaṃ and a vajra,
Ḍombī, blue, with four arms,
Holds skull cup, plow, banner,

At the four doors, from jaḥ hūṃ vaṃ hoḥdaṅ,
From vajra, jewel, lotus,
And blue sword, and through the awakenings,
Come the horse-faced, the sow-faced, the dog-faced and the lion-faced ones,
With four faces and four arms, on seats of corpses.
The first has the right face yellow, the left red, the front blue;
The upper face, a horse's head, is green.
Two hands hold a flute, and the other two
Hold a vajra and a hook.
The second has the right face blue, the left red, the front yellow;
The upper face, a sow's head, is red.
Two hands hold a vīṇā, and the other two
Hold a jewel and a noose.
The third has the right face blue, the left white, the middle red;
The upper face, a dog's head, is yellow.
Two hands hold a great drum, and the other two
Hold a lotus and an iron chain.
The fourth has the right face blue, the left white, the middle green;
The upper face, a lion's head, is white.
Two hands hold a clay drum, and likewise the other two
Hold the sword and the secret bell.
In order, beginning at the eastern door,
Meditate on them upon lotus, sun and corpse.
All have three red, round eyes,
Their reddish-yellow hair blazing upward;
Naked, with tiger skins wound about their waists,
They bear the five bone ornaments,
And a garland of dried heads hangs down.
With left legs extended, in forms erotic and terrifying,
They sit on their own seats in the directions and intermediate directions.

Entering the syllables, the principal one melts:
Above the sun, hūṃ and aṃ
Abide in the form of a drop of bliss.
Then the goddesses of the retinue,
Not seeing their own principal,
Their minds tormented with great longing, wishing him to arise,
Urge the principal with vajra songs:

"Venerable one, arise with your compassionate heart!
Join me in great bliss!
Abandon the nature of emptiness
And protect me, Pukkasī!"

"If you do not remain, I shall die:
Do not cut off the stream of your compassion; arise!
Why do you remain in the nature of emptiness?
Bring Śavarī's fruit to fulfillment!"

"Lord of joy, host of the world,
Do you remain in emptiness?
I, Caṇḍālī, make this plea:
Blessed Vajra, Vajra, arise!
I long for your heart;
You who command illusion, arise!"

"I, Ḍombī, am losing my senses:
You have cut off your great compassion; arise!"

Hearing these dreamlike sounds,
The Blessed Hevajra arises as before.
Meditate on his body color and the rest as before,
And look well upon the maṇḍalas.
At the hearts of the deities, lotus, sun and moon;
Meditate on their own hand emblems and seeds.
This is the supreme king of the maṇḍala.

With the light rays from the principal's heart,
Invite the gnosis maṇḍala;
It abides in the sky over the eastern charnel ground.
Offer well the outer, inner and secret offerings,
And through Gaurī and the other three goddesses,
With jaḥ hūṃ vaṃ hoḥ, summon, absorb and bind it;
Having generated delight, meditate on nonduality.

To generate the higher knowledges,
The six faculties, the eye and the rest, should be purified.
maṃ dhaṃ naṃ raṃ aṃ naṃ,
White, black, yellow, red, green and black;
From lotus, moon, seed and curved knife,
Delusion, Aversion, Avarice,
Attachment, Envy and Nairātmyā,
The six goddesses bearing vajra names:

With the five ornaments, curved knife and skull cup,

At the crown of the head, the heart and the throat,
From a white oṃ, a red āḥ and a black hūṃ,

Come Bhūcarī, Khecarī and Nairātmyā,
White, red and black, holding curved knives,
With the five ornaments, on seats of corpses;

oṃ sarva tathāgata abhiṣekata samaya śrīye hūṃ.

Having become stable, turn the wheel:
The deities should be radiated into every direction,

Having become stable, gather them back,
And meditate clearly on oneself as the maṇḍala.
This is the supreme wheel of the king of action;
This is the reality of the deity.

One who despises the guru, is faithless, and is proud about mantra,
Puffed up from seeing books, and lowly in empowerment:
To such a one, not even a single word should be spoken.
The faithful, who have gained the four realities, strive devotedly in practice;
Those who have received the guru's transmission, remember his kindness, and revere the guru:
The one is like someone who has fallen from the summit of the king of mountains:
Though he thinks, "I shall not fall," he falls nonetheless.
For the others, free from wrongdoing, the stages of gnosis will increase.

This is the stage of the reality of the deity, the first.

Now the reality of gnosis is explained.

Since everything is one's own mind,
The gathering of the deities into oneself
Is like breath blown upon the surface of a mirror
Fading away step by step.
The four, the horse-faced one and the rest,

Here, through the guru's instruction,

The names of the eight charnel-ground trees are given as the Tibetan spells them; several (haracuta, jalita, partipar, vete) are not securely identifiable. In Viṣṇu's left hand the text reads "smoke" (dud), taken as a slip for "conch" (dung). Among Hevajra's right-hand attributes, the word gshor is obscure; it is rendered "cup" on the assumption of a drinking vessel, which is uncertain. "Field-born" consort and "knowledge consort" render the technical terms kṣetrajā and vidyā. "Mind streams" renders rgyud in the sense of continuum, not tantra.  Vetālī is described here as white (reading dkar for kar), whereas earlier, among the offering goddesses, she was yellow. Ghasmarī's source is read as "snake" (sbrul) for "emanation" (sprul). After the six colors the text has a stray "lotus, black," apparently a repetition, which is left untranslated. The fourth door guardian's "secret" attribute is taken as the bell, following the pattern of hook, noose, chain and bell. The line on Ḍombī's plea lacks the negative found in Śavarī's ("do not cut off"); it is translated as it stands. The closing lines on preparing the nectar ("the four," "in front, a maṇḍala") are terse and their sense uncertain. "Five awakenings" renders the fivefold abhisambodhi of the generation stage.  The list of syllables transforming into the nectars and meats is terse: "from hūṃ, an emanation" and "from khaṃ, it turns to liquid, the mountain of the directions" are translated as written, and their exact referents are unclear. "Excrement" follows the technical sense of dri chen in tantric rites, where it corresponds to Vairocana among the five nectars; the line could also be read as "great fragrance." The verse on the unworthy and the worthy student lists both groups before giving the simile and the result; the simile of falling is taken to apply to the first group, and the increase of gnosis to the second.  "Northern petal" reads dab ma for the written bab ma. "Clad in white" (gos dkar ldan pa) may instead allude to the goddess Pāṇḍaravāsinī; the sense of the line is uncertain. Several lines in the section on recitation ("the seed that is vow and transforming continuum," "through constant casting off") are compressed and translated literally. The "Vajra" addressed by the Blessed One is presumably Vajragarbha, the interlocutor of the Hevajra Tantra.  The line ending "recite at each stage" renders ས་ལ་བཟླ, whose sense is uncertain. In the verse on the root and branch commitments, གཏན་མེད is taken as "never": the commitments are not to be discarded on the pretext that all is illusion. བཀའ་ཐུབ is read as དཀའ་ཐུབ "austerity", and བརྙེས "obtained" as བརྙས "contempt", both evident scribal slips. In the line on the guru, བླ་མས is instrumental, so the guru is the one who bestows. In the label after the seed mantra oṃ ḷṃ svāhā, the stray syllable ལི after Ḍombī's name is left untranslated. ཛི་ཏ་པ་ར stands on its own between two mantras and is given as written.  The line on sleep (བསམ་གཏན་མི་སྦྱོར་ཉལ་མི་ནུས) is terse. It is read as "when one is unable to keep to meditation, lie down within method and wisdom", that is, the yoga of sleep. In "done together with the conferral" (བསྐུར་བ་བཅས་པས), the sense of བསྐུར་བ is uncertain. The author's name ཀ་མྤ་ལ is taken as Kambala.
