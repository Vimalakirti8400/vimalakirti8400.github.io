The Root Downfalls of the Vajrayāna Kālacakra (Kālacakravajrayānamūlāpatti)

The Root Downfalls of the Vajrayāna Kālacakra, titled in Sanskrit Kālacakravajrayānamūlāpatti and in Tibetan Dorjé Tekpa Dükyi Khorlö Tsawé Tungwa, is a brief verse work on the vows of tantric practice according to the Kālacakra system. After paying homage to Kālacakra, Mañjuśrī and Vajrasattva, it sets out the fourteen root downfalls that a practitioner of the Vajra Vehicle must avoid, such as disturbing the mind of one's guru or abandoning the mind of awakening, numbering them in the Indian manner with symbolic number-words like the moon for one and the eye for two. It then lists eight branch offenses and closes with further groups of conduct to be given up, including the five misdeeds, the five sense attachments and the twenty-five observances of non-Buddhists. The text is Toh 1379 in the Tengyur, where it belongs among the commentaries and treatises on the tantras.

In the language of India: kālacakravajrayānamūlapati. In the language of Tibet: The Root Downfalls of the Vajrayāna Kālacakra.

Homage to the glorious Kālacakra.
Homage to the noble Mañjuśrī.

To Vajrasattva, the great being,
who through the union of wisdom and skillful means
moves with the miraculous display of the expanse of the body,
to the mind of awakening, I pay homage,
and shall explain the fourteen root downfalls
as they are taught in the Kālacakra.

My son, the root downfalls are these:
To disturb the mind of the glorious guru
becomes the hare-bearer, the moon.
Another is to transgress his word;
that likewise becomes the eye.
Anger toward one's support is the qualities.
To abandon loving-kindness is the Vedas.
To abandon the mind of awakening is the arrow.
To disparage the philosophical tenets is the faculties.
To reveal the secret to people
not fully ripened is another, the mountains.
Lack of faith in the pure Dharma
is well known by the name of the planets.
Deceitful loving-kindness becomes the directions.
To conceive of what bestows bliss, being free of name and the rest,
becomes the Fierce One, Rudra.
To speak of faults in pure sentient beings
becomes the sun.
To abandon the pledges once one has gained them
is spoken of as the limbs.
To disparage all women
is the downfall that becomes the earth.
These are for those who abide in the Vajra Vehicle.

As for the vase and the secret, at a hundred places,
by the power of the definite discipline,
for the higher ones there is no purity.
To rely on a knowledge-consort
who lacks whatever pledges are required is the moon.
A quarrel at the feast of the gathering circle
is the downfall called the eye.
To obtain nectar from a mother or sister,
who are unfit to be relied upon, becomes the qualities.
Though knowing the secret Dharma, to reject a worthy vessel
out of desire or stinginess is the Vedas.
To let the time pass for the stainless Dharma talk
sought by sentient beings out of faith is the arrow.
To stay among śrāvakas who are filled with fear
for more than seven days is the tastes.
Not knowing the wisdom that protects, yet giving it,
arrogantly presenting oneself as a mantra adept,
and teaching the secret mantra
to sentient beings who are not worthy vessels, is called the auspicious ones.
These eight are the branch offenses.

Killing, stealing the wealth of others,
enjoying the women of others, lying,
and drinking intoxicating liquor;
and also the five dharmas of other sins:
gambling, blameworthy food,
evil words, the religion of the bhūtas, and the religion of the barbarians.
These five, which are at odds with the word of the Sage,
are to be abandoned.
The cow, the child,
the woman, the man and the sage, these five:
also the five dharmas of killing and harming
practiced by cruel brahmins;
toward friends, lords, the Buddha,
the Saṅgha and those who put their trust in oneself,
the five called angers:
these five kinds, done without remorse, are to be abandoned.
Attachment to the five sense pleasures
that lie in the objects of the five senses is to be abandoned.
The twenty-five observances of the non-Buddhists (tīrthikas)
are to be abandoned by Buddhists.

The Root Downfalls of the Vajrayāna Kālacakra, together with the Branches, composed by the bodhisattva, is complete.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The root and branch downfalls are numbered by Indian number-words (bhūtasaṃkhyā), here kept as they stand: the hare-bearer or moon is one, the eye two, the qualities (guṇa) three, the Vedas four, the arrow five, the faculties six, the mountains seven, the planets nine, the directions ten, Rudra eleven, the sun twelve, and the earth (as the fourteen worlds) fourteen; among the branch downfalls the tastes are six and the auspicious ones eight. The Tibetan list of root downfalls omits the usual eighth (abusing the aggregates), and the code word for the thirteenth (limbs) is unusual. Evident readings adopted: mnga'i for mda' "arrow" (fifth root downfall); khu ba "fluid" for khro ba "anger" in the five angers; sngangs "frightened" for sngags "mantra" in "presenting oneself as a mantra adept". The opening lines of the branch-downfall section (vase, secret, a hundred places, no purity for the higher) are obscure and translated literally. "Anger toward one's support" (rten) is usually given in parallel lists as anger toward vajra brothers and sisters. The author's attribution byang chub sems dpa' may be an epithet or a name; rendered "the bodhisattva".
