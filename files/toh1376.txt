The Garland of Yogas (Yogamālā)

The Garland of Yogas, known in Sanskrit as the Yogamālā and in Tibetan as Jorwé Trengwa, is a short verse manual of tantric meditative instruction compiled by the Indian paṇḍita Buddhakīrti from the oral instructions of various gurus, and translated into Tibetan by the novice Sherab Pal. Opening with homage to Vajrasattva, it presents a set of practices for the dawning of innate, stainless wisdom, among them work with the channels and breaths described in the coded language of "male," "female" and "neuter," the stream of nectar, and meditation on the form of the deity, all grounded in generosity, moral discipline and great compassion and in devotion to the guru. It is Toh 1376 in the Tengyur, where it belongs to the section of tantra commentaries and related treatises on tantric practice.

In the language of India: yogamālā. In the language of Tibet: The Garland of Yogas.

Homage to glorious Vajrasattva.
Homage to my own deity.
The innate, the stainless, that very suchness:
of the instruction by which wisdom dawns
I shall set down a little.

Beings are bound by the afflictions.
For their liberation, by growing familiar with the yoga
of true wisdom, which destroys the afflictions,
those afflictions are destroyed.

Here, for the excellent yogin,
through training for six months,
or for a year, and without interruption,
true accomplishment will come without fail.

Having let go of all elaboration,
one who longs for yoga, striving hard,
should hold single-mindedly to the yogas
that will be explained, and meditate on them.

Observing the male, the female and the neuter, their colors,
and their modes of going, coming and abiding,
the practitioner swiftly obtains
the fruit of seeing them.

Furthermore, the cause
of good qualities and of faults is the male and the female.
By the method of slaying them,
the neuter is here to be made to expand.

When that neuter has expanded,
carry it to the summit of the golden mountain.
All excellent qualities without exception
will arise from that; of this there is no doubt.

Furthermore, the male, the female and the neuter
come forth from their places through effort.
Drawing them out, from those places
one obtains precious and excellent qualities.

Or else, the male, the female and the neuter
should be placed in this very place.
Having placed them, pour the intoxicant into them.
Having poured it, the male and the female
should with effort be placed at the mouth of the neuter.

Into the objects that rest upon the essence
the intoxicant should be poured.
Having poured it, with great effort
they should be placed at the mouth of the neuter.

When this has been done, one becomes
like a wish-fulfilling tree; of this there is no doubt.

Thus, for these yogas,
I shall here explain the support.
The three, divided according to time,
are six and five, in their sequence.
Being clearly divided into upper and lower,
each of them is held to be twofold.
These things to be known
the yogin should therefore know.

Or else, at the uvula, the yogin
should set the vajra tongue in motion.
From its movement a stream of nectar
will flow; of this there is no doubt.

Whatever is emanated by the host of seed syllables,
or else the beautiful form body,
or the innate, the stainless:
by meditating on the form of the deity,
one who aspires to it should accomplish it.

For these precious yogas,
generosity, moral discipline and great compassion
should first, with great effort,
be practiced again and again until familiar.

This, out of all the tantras
and all the sūtras,
the buddhas have taught as the essence:
it is the means of accomplishing the ultimate.

These elaborations of yoga
should be applied correctly.
To that excellent disciple
the guru himself shows what he has experienced.

To this garland of yogas
the precious reality should be thoroughly joined.
If the precious reality is not obtained,
how could accomplishment itself be obtained?

Therefore the excellent disciple,
out of longing, always honors the guru,
because it is through the guru's being pleased
that the precious reality is revealed.

Having recognized a precious person,
one should, with care, speak of reality to them.
To disciples such as the white lotus,
not even a single word should be spoken,
because if it is spoken, however great the effort,
the instruction becomes distorted.

These instructions of various gurus
were compiled by the learned Buddhakīrti.

The Garland of Yogas, composed by the paṇḍita Buddhakīrti, is complete. It was translated by the translator, the novice Sherab Pal.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The text speaks in coded yogic language: "male," "female" and "neuter" refer, on the usual tantric reading, to the right and left channels or breaths and the central channel; "slaying" the male and female is stopping their flow so that the central channel expands, and the "golden mountain" (Meru) is the summit of the central axis. The Tibetan reads "ma ni nga" in the line on observing male, female and neuter; this is taken as a scribal division of "ma ning" (neuter), which the rest of the passage uses. In "drawing them out," gdon is read as the verb "to draw out," not the noun graha. "Disciples such as the white lotus" translates padma dkar literally; it apparently names a class of unsuitable disciple, but the reference is uncertain. The verse on the "support" (three, six and five, upper and lower) is obscure and has been rendered literally.
