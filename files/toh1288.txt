The Torma Rite of Hevajra (Hevajrabaliṃtavidhi)

The Torma Rite of Hevajra, in Sanskrit Hevajra Baliṃta Vidhi and in Tibetan Kyé Dorjé Zhejawé Tormé Choga, is a brief Indian tantric ritual manual, written mostly in verse, on offering torma, the ritual cake, within the practice of the deity Hevajra. It sets out three tormas in turn: one for the deities of the great maṇḍala, one for the ḍākinīs they emanate, and one for the guardians of the directions, gods and nāgas invited as guests, explaining for each how the offering is blessed into nectar, how the recipients are visualized, and which mantras and songs accompany the offering, before closing with the benefits of the practice and a dedication. Its author is not named in the text. It is Toh 1288 in the Tengyur, where it is placed in the section of tantra commentaries among the ritual works of the Hevajra cycle.

In the language of India: he vajra baliṃta vidhi nāma. In the language of Tibet: The Torma Rite of Hevajra.

Homage to Hevajra.

To the ḍākinīs, who are accomplished
in moving throughout all of space, I bow deeply.
The activity of each and every one of them,
the torma rite, I shall explain in brief.

Here there are three tormas:
for the deities of the great maṇḍala,
for the ḍākinīs they emanate,
and for the guardians of the directions who have become guests.

For the deities of the great maṇḍala,
first, as for the blessing:
imagining oneself as one's chosen deity,
in a vessel that has become supremely pure
imagine the torma substances as nectar.
Pointing to it, recite the mantra of the three realities.
Or else, imagining it transformed into the five sense pleasures,
bless it and recite the mantra.
After that, in front, imagine in an instant
the deities of the maṇḍala as holders of the vajra.

The order of offering to them is this: oṃ mahābhūta khāhi gṛhna gṛhna bhuñja bhuñja kha kha khāhi idaṃ biliṃta khāhi amṛta rāsa khāhi kāma guṇi samārasa khāhi. Having sung this as a song, one joins it to the name of each one: oṃ he vajra nera ātmāta idaṃ mahā baliṃta khāhi, and likewise oṃ vajra gaurī idaṃ mahā baliṃta khāhi, and so forth, one recites.

When they have thus been satisfied and pleased,
the torma of the ḍākinīs,
who bring together the conditions for one's own accomplishment:
first, its blessing is the same as the mantra above.
The way of offering: before oneself as the chosen deity,
having recalled the samaya,
the ḍākinīs in an instant
fully take on a conventional form;
for the sake of their enjoying the torma,
and urging them on to action and accomplishment,
the order of offering to them
follows the order of gathering the remains:
recite this mantra together with song.

oṃ uciṣṭa baliṃta vajra ḍākinī saṃpanina vajra ḍākinī ratna ḍākinī padma ḍākinī idaṃ uciṣṭa baliṃta kha kha khāhi gṛhna gṛhna bhuñja bhujña pratīccha khāhi karma sidhi saṃpanina svāhā

Then: "Bestow action and accomplishment,"
and entrust them with the activities you desire.

The torma of the bhūtas who have become guests:
in front, from a triangular source of phenomena,
above, below, and in between,
imagine eight-petaled lotuses in sequence.
Above, from seeds that have become oṃ,
on moon and sun, Brahmā and the others,
white in color, holding vajra and bell,
on their heads the mark of the body:
imagine these eight, each with a retinue like itself.
On the eight-petaled lotus in between,
the eight guardians of the directions, become mother deities,
transformed from eight seeds, blue in color,
holding vajra and bell, with retinues like themselves:
imagine their heads marked with the mind.
Below, the eight nāgas, arising from the earth,
red in color, holding vajra and bell,
their heads marked with the vajra of speech:
meditate on them with retinues like themselves.

Then, first, the blessing:
together with the vessel of samaya nectar,
wind, fire and the rest bring it to ripeness;
after that it is mixed with nectar,
and the deities spoken of above,
through tubes of light, the tongues of the deities,
enjoy it and are satisfied.
At that time, on the three sets of anthers,
imagine Brahmā, Kāmadeva and Pṛthivī, the earth goddess,
each in its order.
Reciting their mantra with an urging,
ring the vajra and bell.

oṃ inda jama jala jakkha bhūta bāhṇi ba u rakṣa canda sujja māda pappa tala pātāla aṭṭa sappa svāhā idaṃ baliṃ bhuñja jigha phulla dhūppa māṃsa piṃgha aṃbha sappa kajja sāda khanti khuṇi pheḍa gāda oṃ akāro mukhaṃ sarbba dharmāṇāṃ ādyanutpannatvā oṃ āḥ hūṃ phaṭ svāhā

As for the dedication, preceded by the benefits:
the benefits of offering torma now and then,
and of offering it continually,
are to be declared as taught in the tantra.

Following the holy guru, with steadfast mind,
this rite, which swiftly brings realization and confers blessing,
I have written at the request of certain worthy ones,
in harmony with the oral instructions and the scriptures.

The sequence of the torma rite of the Blessed One called Hevajra is complete.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The line rendered "the eight guardians of the directions, become mother deities" reads མ་ཡི་ལྷར་གྱུར; it may instead refer to deities arising from a seed syllable ma, and its exact sense is uncertain. Likewise, the nāgas are said to arise ས་ལས, which could mean "from the earth," as rendered, or "from the syllable sa." The three realities whose mantra blesses the torma are conventionally oṃ, āḥ and hūṃ.
