In the language of India: Ārya-Mahālakṣmī-nāma-sūtra. In the language of Tibet: The Noble Sūtra of the Great Glorious One.

Homage to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas.

Thus did I hear. At one time, the Blessed One was dwelling in Sukhāvatī. Then the bodhisattva, the great being, the noble Avalokiteśvara went to where the Blessed One was, and having arrived, he bowed his head at the feet of the Blessed One, circumambulated the Blessed One three times, and sat down to one side.

Then the Blessed One, looking upon the Great Glorious One, spoke thus to the noble Avalokiteśvara: "These twelve names of the Great Glorious One — whether a monk, or a nun, or a male lay practitioner, or a female lay practitioner, or anyone else at all — whoever knows them, whoever holds them, recites them, writes them down, or has them written: that person shall abandon poverty and become wealthy. Then all the assembled spirits, having heard this, beseeched together, saying, 'May it be so!'"

Then the Blessed One proclaimed the twelve names of the Great Glorious One, which are as follows: the Glorious One, the Auspicious One, Garlanded with Lotuses, Mistress of Wealth, the White One, the Greatly Renowned One, Lotus-Eyed, the Greatly Radiant One, the Active One, Bestower of Food, Supreme Bestower of Jewels, the Great Glorious One.

Syād yathedaṃ. Jini. Ghṛṇi. Sarva-artha-sādhani. Śāśini. Alakṣmī-nāśaya. Siddhyantu me mantrapadāḥ svāhā. Abhṛkuṭī-parama-subhage svāhā.

This is to be recited three times. Whoever recites this at the three times of day — all circumstances adverse to that person shall be destroyed. They shall come to possess good fortune. Their prosperity shall become inexhaustible. When going to meet someone, if this is recited before the meeting, that person will regard them with the affection of a parent for a child, will be joyful, and will do whatever is asked. If it is recited constantly, without interruption, then even though Brahmā and the brahmins perform fierce and forceful rites against one, those rites shall not prevail, and one shall come to have served many buddhas.

When the Blessed One had spoken these words, the bodhisattva, the noble Avalokiteśvara, was overjoyed and praised what the Blessed One had said.

The Noble Sūtra of the Great Glorious One is complete.
