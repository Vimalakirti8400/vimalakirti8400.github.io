The Glorious King of Tantras of the Red Yamāri (Śrī Māṃrakta Yamāri Tantra Rāja Nāma)

Toh 475 in the Kangyur is a tantric scripture known in Sanskrit as the Śrī Māṃrakta Yamāri Tantra Rāja and in Tibetan as the Dpal ldan gshin rje gshed dmar po'i rgyud kyi rgyal po, rendered in English as The Glorious King of Tantras of the Red Yamāri. It belongs to the class of highest yoga tantras and is dedicated to Raktayamāri, the red wrathful deity who embodies the destruction of death and is a fierce manifestation central to the Yamāri cycle of practice. The text opens in the classical sūtra-style framing of a teaching given by the Blessed One, but quickly establishes its tantric character by invoking figures such as Hevajra and referencing related tantras like the Saṃpuṭa, situating it within a broader network of Vajrayāna scriptural traditions. As a king of tantras concerned with ritual methods ranging from the attainment of sovereign power to healing and the full range of worldly and liberating aims, it stands as an important canonical example of the Yamāri tantric literature preserved in the Kangyur.

In the language of India: Śrī Māṃrakta Yamāri Tantra Rāja Nāma. In the language of Tibet: The Glorious King of Tantras of the Red Yamāri. Homage to Red Yamāri, the Destroyer of the Lord of Death.

Thus did I hear at one time. The Blessed One, the Tathāgata, having merged all tathāgatas into one, dwelt within the lotus of Selflessness herself. He who clasped her tightly at the throat, who himself had entreated her for the sake of sentient beings, spoke to Hevajra, and then equally set forth the vow of liberation; and likewise, though he spoke at length again and again in the Saṃpuṭa and other tantras to fulfill the entire aspiration of sentient beings, now I shall explain this excellently.

Some desire sovereign power; some desire long life, freedom from illness, and abundance. Peaceful rites and the rest, up through the most expansive activities, have been taught in various tantras. And those actions done openly, the defeat of enemy forces, the driving away, the summoning, the slaying — the wheels of mechanical means (yantravidyā) that are difficult to find throughout the three worlds, and that are not yet spoken of in the many tantras — in order to take hold of the method that benefits you, I shall now explain them. Goddess, with reverence, listen!

By the power and strength of whichever wheel of means, one may continually attain sovereign power for as long as the sun and moon endure — if one wishes to live so. Now, within the domain called by name: the raising of vital essence, the summoning of hosts, sky-faring, and the supremely swift — the remedy for these I shall explain.

First, loving-kindness is to be cultivated. Second, compassion likewise. Third, joy is to be cultivated. Fourth, equanimity is to be thoroughly cultivated. The eight worldly concerns are to be completely relinquished, and one is to contemplate them as empty. Again, emptiness is awakening; into the second, gather the seed-syllable; and into the fourth, the letter is established.

From the syllable raṃ there arises the sun; from hūṃ there arises the variegated vajra. By that same means — a vajra fence, and likewise the ground bound by vajras.

First, meditate on emptiness. Then, from the red syllable āṃ completely transformed, arises the solar maṇḍala. Above that stands the red syllable hūṃ. Above that, at the center of a club adorned with a dripping head, stands a red hūṃ. On that too, a hūṃ on the sun. From it, rays of light stream forth in all directions of the sky, gather all three worlds into the form of the hero, draw them back and dissolve them into that very same reality, and one is to conceive of oneself as the glorious form of Red Yamāri.

His body is red. His left leg is extended and his face is in a wrathful expression, his fangs bared, his tongue curled, inspiring dread. He is adorned with the eight nāgas. His hair, red and golden, blazes upward. He is adorned at his hips with a dripping tiger skin. A dwarf's corpse hangs at his side, terrifying. He has three eyes, which are supremely dreadful, red with unceasing blazing light and every kind of ray thoroughly stirring. In his left hand he holds a skull-cup full of the blood of Nirṛti and others; in his right he holds a white club and is adorned with nāga ornaments and so forth, by which he conquers the four māras. He embraces at his shoulder a vajra vetāla. His form is that of the venerable lord who is the single and definitive full blossoming of great desire.

The venerable lady is similar to him, with the special distinction of being adorned with a dry head. The Blessed One is adorned with the ornament of a dripping head and a waistband that has come undone. He stands with the left leg extended upon a red buffalo. Externally, within the center of the white three-pointed club born from the Blessed One, one is again to conceive of a red seed-syllable.

Externally, the Blessed One's vajra is likewise born from the syllable HŪṂ. Then again, the secret lotus of the Blessed Lady is directly marked in its entirety with the eight-petaled lotus by the syllable ĀḤ.

Following this, the one who embodies the sovereign of secret mantra is: OṂ SARVA TATHĀGATA ANURĀGAṆA VAJRA SVABHĀVA ĀTMAKO'HAṂ. Below the navel is a red lotus of eight petals, red in color. Within it, the red syllable HRĪḤ is to be distinctly visualized. The white streak that is visualized at the crown is once more to be conceived of, and then inserted into the crown of the Blessed One's head. Again, with a sound mind, one is to visualize that streak entering by way of the vajra path. Through the mutual union of the two with one another, it has the form of a seed of equal taste. That seed is then taken by the four goddesses as their own form: those who dwell in the four directions beginning with the east gaze upon and rouse their own lord, and all the goddesses are seen to offer song as an offering in order to receive it.

Arise, O venerable wrathful one of compassion!
Dispel the darkness throughout all three worlds!
By the vajra carcikā!

The lord is victorious over the four māras.
The mind is distracted — arise, O venerable one!
By the Sow!

Does the lord of the world dwell in the hospitality of emptiness?
O venerable one, arise through the merit of the world!
By Sarasvatī!

From the nature of awakening, O host of the world,
You who contemplate emptiness — in what do you remain?
By Gaurī!

Having been fully roused, the Blessed One once again comes fully to rest therein. Then, below, from the gathering of the essences that arise there, one who has the nature of a deity, residing upon a red buffalo, with the nature of the self — that one too is possessed of the nine moods of dance, fierce like the bandhūka flower, with a single face, two arms, fangs bared, three eyes round and red, a fearsome reddish-yellow beard, with a stocky short body, pendulous and terrifying, reddish-yellow hair swept upward, tongue curled and dripping blood, brow furrowed and laughing fiercely, holding in the right hand a white club marked with a head, holding in the left a skull-cup filled with intoxicant and blood, with a radiance equal to ten million suns, standing with the left leg extended, causing Yama, the lord of death, to tremble and quake, dwelling behind the buffalo with a form that measures eight-and-two spans, adorned with eight nāgas, a garland of heads bound at the crown with further garlands of heads hanging freely, wearing a tiger-skin lower garment, embraced by Vajravārāhī.

Similar in form and color to the Blessed One, bearing a hooked knife and skull-cup, with a body adorned by the five mudrās, one is to meditate upon this with a mind earnestly directed downward; and then, once divine pride has been assumed, there is no doubt that the self will be realized as accomplished.

Following this, the mantra VAJRA SVABHĀVĀTMAKO'HAṂ is to be recited.

Then, with great attachment, the Blessed Lady gazes upon all the limbs and appendages, presses the vajra into the lotus, and one is to meditate upon both their tiger-skin garments being swept up. Thinking "these are what generate attachment in her," the vajra and lotus are given to the mind, and one is to fully visualize the vajra at its tip consecrated by the yellow syllable OṂ. The red syllable ĀḤ consecrates the pollen of that lotus with the syllable AḤ. Then, having recited OṂ SARVA TATHĀGATA ANURĀGAṆA VAJRA SVABHĀVA ĀTMAKO'HAṂ, one is to meditate upon oneself as arisen from the equal union of vajra and lotus — just as in the external case of the two with one another. Then, at the moment of transference, one is to meditate upon the two essences dripping simultaneously flowing together within the lotus of the goddess whom one has visualized as oneself.

Through the complete transformation of those white and red essences, one is to meditate upon delusion's Yamāntaka and the others exactly as they are, imagining them within the center of the lotus, and to cultivate the vision of their being born from the Blessed Lady. After that, one imagines them turning to face forward and each abiding in their respective places.

Then, on the solar disc of the eastern petal, delusion's Yamāntaka is embraced by the black Carcikā. On the solar disc of the southern petal, slander's Yamāntaka — the yellow one — is united with the yellow Vārāhī, she who bears the face of a sow. On the solar disc of the western petal, desire's Yamāntaka — the red one — is united with the white Sarasvatī. On the solar disc of the northern petal, envy's Yamāntaka — the dark blue one — enters into union with Gaurī.

Or alternatively, all of them may be meditated upon as the red Yamāntaka, equal to a zombie-risen corpse. The four vases of Merumunen are filled to the brim by being brought together equally. The golden vase of truth-dissolution is filled with resin. Likewise, a crystal vase for the wind is filled with sihla. And the silver vase for power is filled with camphor, exactly as described. One looks upon a skull filled to the brim — complete with both sun and moon. This very thing, entering into union with great bliss, is nothing other than the highest.

Then, from the syllable HŪṂ abiding on the lotus and solar disc at one's own heart-center, its rays of light make offerings to the entire maṇḍala wheel with every conceivable offering. Then, with JAḤ HŪṂ BAṂ HOḤ, one truly invokes, introduces, and so forth, and makes offerings. All sentient beings are as they are in accordance with the guru's oral instructions — this is the second yoga.

Then, in verse:

The yogi who dwells in the ten directions,
And those assemblies of yoginīs —
With intelligence, as before, invite them,
Make offerings, confer empowerment, and so forth.

Thus, while the Tathāgatas confer empowerment, Locanā and the others, with minds of delight, sing varied songs and perform dances, and with vases filled with the five nectars:

Grant the great precious empowerment,
Worshipped by beings of the three realms —
What arises from the place
Of the secret triad of all buddhas.

So reciting and praising, one imagines that from the heads of all those in the maṇḍala there flows forth the vital essence, and that the seal of Vajrasattva is set upon them. This is the rite.

Now, delusion, slander, desire, and envy make supplication:

Hail, hail, red Yamāntaka —
Guru, whose nature is that of a demon-lord.
I behold you and am utterly terrified.
Guru, abandon the nature of wrath.

Your dance shakes the surface of the earth,
The space above the earth, and the earth below.
You, the wrathful one adorned with passion's flame,
Who dances — you are the zombie-risen one.

Your body is stocky and short, red in color,
Manifesting in many forms and emanations.
Vajra Sarasvatī bows before you —
You who dance are the great bliss.

With the secret mantras HRĪḤ and ŚRĪḤ
You dispel the delusions of the three worlds.
Venerable wrathful one of compassion,
You who dance — look upon all beings with care.

OṂ SARVA TATHĀGATA PŪJA VAJRA SVABHĀVA ĀTMAKO'HAṂ.

Then, as before, above the wind maṇḍala is the fire maṇḍala, and above that, above the three secret maṇḍalas arisen from the complete transformation of the syllable OṂ, one is to meditate upon a skull arisen from the complete transformation of the syllable ĀḤ. In its center, the five nectars, the five meats, yogurt, milk, flesh, fish, and so forth are stirred together with a stick until the flavors are equalized, cooked, and viewed as resembling the fluid of lac-resin. Having cooled this with OṂ ĀḤ HŪṂ, one imagines a tongue in the form of a hollow tube arisen from the complete transformation of the syllable HŪṂ, and one drinks.

Each one is to offer from their own mouth into the mouth of the goddess. The maṇḍala participants too are to contemplate this and partake of it universally. Then, having tasted the nectar and become perfectly delighted, one is to recite the secret mantra aloud. Having displayed the seed syllable and the emblem dwelling at the heart, one is to recite the secret mantra:

OṂ HRĪḤ ṢṬRĪḤ BIKṚTĀNANA HŪṂ HŪṂ PHAṬ PHAṬ SVĀHĀ

One is to recite this as many times as one wishes while meditating. If one wishes to rise, one performs the welfare of sentient beings through the rays of light from one's own body, absorbs everything into oneself, and through the yoga of the co-emergent Yamāntaka, one is to abide in ease as one pleases.

As for the phase of the yoga of the co-emergent Yamāntaka: one is to declare one's pride as Yamāntaka who is naturally established, resembling one's own form, saying: "I am none other than Yamāntaka — one-faced, two-armed — who is naturally established." This is the great yoga.

By this declaration of the four yogas, whether in three sessions or in four sessions as well, one is to continue in this way at all times, from the moment of manifest aspiration, until perfect completion.

Then, at the conclusion of one hundred thousand recitations, the mantra is to be recited with direct application of the syllable, and one will have accomplished the approach. Then, for the ritual of the method for all activities, one performs a fire offering of ten thousand with butter, honey, and red flowers, reciting the secret mantra one at a time:

OṂ HRĪḤ ṢṬRĪḤ BIKṚTĀNANA MANTRA SIDDHI ME DEHI TĪ HŪṂ HŪṂ PHAṬ PHAṬ SVĀHĀ

After this, there will be the attainment of the mantra's siddhi. This is the siddhi of all activities.

This concludes the first chapter, on the deity called the Red One and so forth.

Here a question is posed to the Blessed One:

O Blessed One — please tell us
how the attainment of great seal (mahāmudrā)
is obtained, and by what means
it is to be accomplished.

With mouth pressed to mouth,
embracing one another tightly,
in the form of supreme bliss —
now hear what is not to be spoken of openly.

The form of the yogins who are the source of that movement — the supreme Blessed One — flows down uninterruptedly, like the continuous flow of a river current.

Here, one is to recite the flaw-mantra for as long as one has meditated. The form of the Blessed One will swiftly be accomplished — not otherwise. For as long as it continues, the attainment here is none other than the attainment of great seal itself. These too are all accomplished through familiarization with yoga alone, not through anything else.

As for that which becomes the practice of familiarization: for the born, the arisen, the not-yet-arisen, and those who have previously abided, the attainments of the aims of liberation and of desire in their various forms, and even the attainment of buddhahood itself, are to be obtained in this very life. Having thoroughly abandoned all activities and turned one's mind toward that goal, one comes forth oneself and speaks: "Ho, ho! O supreme practitioner, whatever it may be, I shall bestow it." Then, having offered worship and made prostration, one receives the attainment and bestows it.

OṂ HRĪḤ ṢṬRĪḤ BIKṚTĀNANA ĀGACCHA ĀGACCHA HŪṂ HŪṂ PHAṬ PHAṬ SVĀHĀ

Then, if one wishes to accomplish the Vajra-zombie (vetāla) without a female zombie-spirit, one makes an image of the Vajra-zombie woman, fashioned on cloth or on fruit, dwelling at the outset perfectly in the color and shape of the noble lady. One performs worship with whatever offerings are available, and one recites for as long as all of them are seen with their own individual forms, undistorted and unmixed. Until she comes and embraces the practitioner and says, "O practitioner, what is your command?" — the practitioner then offers worship and speaks.

"She shall become my wife" — and by that declaration she becomes his wife. By enjoying union with her, one becomes accomplished in the form of Yamāntaka (the Destroyer of Death).

OṂ DVEṢARATI ĀGACCHA ĀGACCHA NUDA NUDA TRUṬA TRUṬA HŪṂ JAḤ SVĀHĀ

Or alternatively: OṂ VAJRA VETĀLI ĀGACCHA ĀGACCHA HŪṂ JAḤ SVĀHĀ

One takes as a bride a young woman of sixteen years with beautiful limbs, or else one generates a child and, making her a fortunate one, worships her fully in the manner of a yoginī consecrated with empowerment and contemplated through mantra, embracing her with the left arm and visualizing her as a yoginī, and one recites for as long as she blazes. Then, by holding her in that way, one becomes accomplished in the form of Yamāntaka.

OṂ VAJRA VETĀLI JVALAṂ HŪṂ JAḤ SVĀHĀ — this is to be recited.

Then, if one wishes to accomplish the Yamāntaka of Delusion, one dresses in the guise of Yamāntaka of Delusion and, pressing upon the lotus with the sole of one foot and the sole of the other, one recites until she calls out, "O practitioner!" and speaks the words, "What is your command?" and "What shall I do?" Then from that point on, she bestows even sovereign dominion over all three worlds. As for other attainments, what need is there even to mention them?

In the same way, if one wishes to accomplish the Yamāntaka of Slander, the Yamāntaka of Attachment, and the Yamāntaka of Envy, the secret mantras for churning out the fruit are in exactly the same way:

OṂ MOHĀYAMĀNTAKṚTA ĀGACCHA ĀGACCHA HŪṂ HŪṂ PHAṬ PHAṬ
OṂ PIŚUNĀYAMĀNTAKṚTA ĀGACCHA ĀGACCHA HŪṂ HŪṂ PHAṬ PHAṬ
OṂ RĀGĀYAMĀNTAKṚTA ĀGACCHA ĀGACCHA HŪṂ HŪṂ PHAṬ PHAṬ
OṂ ĪRṢYĀYAMĀNTAKṚTA ĀGACCHA ĀGACCHA HŪṂ HŪṂ PHAṬ PHAṬ SVĀHĀ

These are to be recited.

Then, if one wishes to accomplish the Beloved of Delusion, one imagines the previously described form of the goddess who is the Beloved of Delusion — riding a buffalo, together with oneself — and presses as before, reciting until she comes and speaks the words "What shall I do?" From that point on, all the practitioner's desires become fully accomplished, it is said. In the same way one accomplishes the Beloved of Slander, the Beloved of Attachment, and the Beloved of Envy. The mantras for these are:

OṂ MOHĀRATI ĀGACCHA ĀGACCHA HŪṂ JAḤ
OṂ PIŚUNĀRATI ĀGACCHA ĀGACCHA HŪṂ JAḤ
OṂ RĀGĀRATI ĀGACCHA ĀGACCHA HŪṂ JAḤ
OṂ ĪRṢYĀRATI ĀGACCHA ĀGACCHA HŪṂ JAḤ

These are to be recited. Or alternatively:

OṂ VAJRACARCIKE ĀGACCHA ĀGACCHA HŪṂ JAḤ

And likewise: VAJRAVĀRĀHĪ, VAJRASVARASVASTI, VAJRAGAURĪ — these are to be recited.

Then, if one wishes to accomplish a yakṣa, at that time one makes an image of a yakṣa with the perception of accomplishing a worldly attainment, presses upon it, and performs the accomplishment. All commands become fully fulfilled. In the same way one may accomplish a yakṣiṇī, a nāga (male), a nāginī (female), a hungry ghost, a hungry ghost-woman, a flesh-eater (rākṣasa), a flesh-eating woman, a god, a goddess, a man, and a woman as well.

Furthermore, for manifest accomplishment one recites:

OṂ HRĪḤ ṢṬRĪḤ VIKṚTĀNANA CHE GE MO SĀDHAYA SĀDHAYA HŪṂ HŪṂ PHAṬ PHAṬ SVĀHĀ

Then, if one wishes to accomplish the Vajra Staff, under the asterism Puṣya (the Victorious Star) one makes a wooden vajra staff thirty finger-breadths long, anoints it with the five products of the cow, adorns it beautifully with sandalwood and garlands of flowers, holds it from the outset in the manner of a sword, and recites for as long as it blazes. Then the staff will be grasped, and one will overcome adversaries just like Yamāntaka.

OṂ HRĪḤ ṢṬRĪḤ VIKṚTĀNANA VAJRADAṆḌA JVĀLAYA JVĀLAYA HŪṂ HŪṂ PHAṬ PHAṬ SVĀHĀ

Thus one recites. Then, if one wishes to accomplish the skull, one makes a skull-shaped form of the wood of Yamāntaka, thirty-two finger-breadths in size, holds it from the outset with the left hand and covers it with the right hand, and recites for as long as it blazes. The result is just as before. The distinctive element of the mantra is the addition of the phrase KAPĀLAṂ JVĀLAYA. Then one is to accomplish the staff mudrā: at that time one makes a fist with the right hand and extends the index finger. Having bound this mudrā, one recites for as long as it blazes. Then one will obtain the manifest accomplishment of the form of Yamāntaka. The distinctive element of the mantra is the addition of DAṆḌAṂ MUDRĀṂ JVĀLAYA.

This teaching declares that through the very act of enjoying all five desired objects, one may accomplish everything. Whatever samaya substances one has obtained, one is to eat and drink them for the purpose of bringing one's intention to perfect completeness. And again and again, whatever there may be, all of it without exception one is to eat and drink. Every first portion one offers to Yamāntaka.

All of the foregoing is the second chapter: the chapter on the ritual for accomplishing great manifest attainment.

Now, if one wishes to accomplish minor accomplishments, one is to accomplish pacification and the like. On a barley cake, one writes out twelve letters, each separate and distinct, inscribing them manifestly in a wheel of twelve spokes using milk that has been empowered one hundred and eight times. Beginning from the eastern door of the wheel, one writes the twelve letters going clockwise:

OṂ HRĪḤ ṢṬRĪḤ VIKṚTĀNANA HŪṂ HŪṂ PHAṬ PHAṬ

In the center, one writes the seed syllable of water, VAṂ, and between the two VAṂ syllables facing each other, one writes the name of the person to be accomplished. On the outside one encircles it with the letters. Then, having joined the two together face to face, one wraps them in white thread, then binds them with an intoxicant plant, consecrates them one hundred and eight times, places them in a vase filled with milk, and recites the mantra. Having made offerings according to one's means, one recites:

OṂ HRĪḤ ṢṬRĪḤ VIKṚTĀNANA VADEVA DATTASYA ŚĀNTIṂ KURU HŪṂ HŪṂ PHAṬ PHAṬ SVĀHĀ

Reciting this, pacification will occur.

For the harms that afflict worldly beings, such as negative actions, sickness, and so forth, in the ten directions of the village, one consecrates seven times with this mantra:

OṂ HRĪḤ ṢṬRĪḤ VIKṚTĀNANA GRĀMA AMUKA SARVOPĀDRAVA ŚĀNTIṂ KURU KURU HŪṂ HŪṂ BALIṂ GṚHṆA GṚHṆA PHAṬ PHAṬ SVĀHĀ

and one offers it through the torma ritual. Having first performed the external rites and so forth, and having performed a fire offering with milk throughout the entire village, all harms will be pacified.

Then, if one wishes to perform increase, one writes the two wheels as before, and in the center of the twelve letters one writes BHŪṂ, the seed syllable of the maṇḍala wheel. In the center of that, one writes the two BHAUṢAṬ syllables facing each other, with the name of the person to be accomplished between them. On the outside one encircles it with three square designs drawn in saffron. One wraps this in yellow thread, consecrates it one hundred and eight times, places it in a vase filled with butter and honey, and recites the mantra. Having made offerings according to one's means, one recites:

OṂ HRĪḤ ṢṬRĪḤ VIKṚTĀNANA BHŪṂ BHAUṢAṬ LHAJIN GYI PUṢṬIṂ KURU BHAUṢAṬ HŪṂ HŪṂ PHAṬ PHAṬ SVĀHĀ

One recites this until wealth has increased and multiplied. Without interrupting the mantra recitation, one performs a fire offering with curd-rice.

Then, if one wishes to perform subjugation, one writes the twelve letters on the two wheels as before, using the juice of fingernail plant,

In the center is the seed-syllable HRĪḤ, which is the seed of Kurukullā. In the center are two HO syllables written face to face. On the outside, these are encircled with a triangular design. Then one wraps this entirely in red thread, consecrates it one hundred and eight times, and writes it with the juice of lac resin on a vase filled with butter and honey, placing it inside. Having made offerings according to one's means, one recites the mantra:

OṂ HRĪḤ ṢṬRĪḤ VIKṚTĀNANA HRĪḤ HOḤ LHAJIN GYI LHAJIN LA VAŚAMĀNAYA HOḤ HŪṂ HŪṂ PHAṬ PHAṬ SVĀHĀ

One recites this until subjugation has been accomplished. At the conclusion of the mantra recitation, one performs a fire offering using red flowers combined with salt.

Then, if one wishes to summon a person, one places the mantra and that very person's name, cut apart, in the center of a charcoal form of a burial ground and an earth effigy. Pressing it down with the big toe, one recites: "This alone is the person to be accomplished" — and one continues reciting until the person comes. As for the mantra, the distinction is that in place of VAŚAMĀNAYA one says ĀRṢAYA HŪṂ, and in place of HOḤ one says JAḤ.

OṂ MARME MARMA AVIGHATE OṂ CARTSIKE HARA HARA BUKKAṂ MĀRAYA MĀRAYA — and for the purpose of the fierce goddess: JAḤ. OṂ GAMBHĪRI CARTSIKE PA HŪṂ ŚOṆITA PRĪYE GAMBHĪRA — and toward the fierce goddess: JAḤ.

At a single tree, at a charnel ground, or on a mountain, one goes there naked, facing south, and recites one hundred and eight times. After that there is no doubt that success will be achieved.

The Blessed One then declared how the ritual procedure of the mantra should be performed. Going alone to a solitary place, one visualizes directly before oneself the gurus, buddhas, and bodhisattvas dwelling in all ten directions in the clearly vivid form of the venerable painted image. Then, in the center of a twelve-spoked wheel drawn in the form of twelve spokes using human bone fragments, great quantities of blood, and the blood of a large raven, one places the seed of wind. Within that, one folds together the enemy's name written in fragmented form, binds it to the throat of a raven, and if it has not been released, at that time the enemy is destroyed. If at that time the enemy has not been destroyed, one writes the twelve seed-syllables on it:

PHAṬ PHAṬ HŪṂ HŪṂ NA NA TĀKṚ VIṢṬRĪḤ HRĪḤ OṂ

Having made offerings according to one's means, one strikes it forcefully with venom and dispatches it in a stream. If that does not work, one praises a crow's wing seven times and, calling the enemy by name at the door of his house, one immediately drives him out and destroys him.

Likewise, in the center of a twelve-spoked wheel, at the center of the seed of fire composed of the three hot substances, poison, scorpion, great bones, and the firewood of a fuel-wood tree — with the two YA syllables written in fragmented form and facing each other, and the two THA syllables encircling them on the outside — one burns this in a fire of fuel-wood. If this is seized upon a young boy, he will die in that very instant. If that does not work, one praises a human bone of four finger-widths seven times, then conceals it at the enemy's doorway. This will drive him from his house.

Likewise, when the twelve seed-syllables are completely formed, in the center of a twelve-spoked wheel — with sandalwood, jīva, kaṣṭhikā, and that which others cannot withstand, or the seed of the water of the garuḍa vine tree, with those two written facing each other at the center, and the two THA syllables encircling them on the outside — one recites one hundred and eight times. While filling with milk, one uses one hundred thousand rājāta and one hundred thousand red flowers: there is no death, and apart from this, that very rājāta substance does not go elsewhere. At that time, even one who commits the five acts of immediate retribution will be freed from them.

Alternatively, using the red flowers of the tree bearing the fruit of the sepha together with twelve seeds filling a bowl completely, one places it thus and recites over it.

Now a question was put: "O Blessed One, what exactly is empowerment called? Lord, tell me this. The empowerment of the consort, the four varieties — listen now, for this is what leads sentient beings into entry. As for the scattering that makes one stainless — all of these cause movement. Then, seated in vajra posture, filled with the bliss of non-emission and the rest: as long as these have become stainless, the bodhicitta is what abandons. As long as the yogin does not perform it, the bodhicitta is what is released. Whoever has accomplished attainments proceeds accordingly. As long as the supreme of universal joy exists, by that very form of bliss is the form of the buddha endowed with perfect purity. As long as semen has been displayed, one should always cultivate the self. That is known as true in the world. Or else, in what is now without purpose — the fourth, again, is just like this. Here, whatever wisdom and gnosis there is — in the fourth empowerment, an ordinary person would speak of it. That is again called 'likewise.' The fourth itself, as the third — what has been done in the same way is not confirmed. From the first limb, again in repetition — whatever is said becomes a fault, in just the same way. The third of the causes belonging to the childish: whatever that is, the fourth is the fruit. Because there is no distinction between cause and fruit, the fourth also is established as the third — this is the meaning of ultimate truth, as stated before, from which, by means of the three mudrās, three fruits are accomplished. In this mantra approach, the fruit of fire-offering is devoid of the three mudrās. Wondrous! In accomplishing the three mudrās, the karma mudrā and the wisdom mudrā do not become the cause" — so it is said. From having thoroughly abandoned the cultivation of the karma mudrā and the wisdom mudrā, one is to meditate definitively on the great seal, the mahāmudrā. Here, the union of the two faculties is called the bliss of practicing all desires. The characteristic of emission and non-emission — through the practice of the essential nature — does not become bliss. The third, not arisen in the cause — the emission of the clan, the fruit of the third — does not become the fourth in the ultimate. Let this be understood and fully internalized.

The sovereign lord spoke to me of the joining of the winds. The Blessed One declared: at the heart center, the life-wind and the downward-clearing wind are described. At the region of the navel dwells the equalizing wind. At the region of the throat moves the upward-flowing wind. The all-pervading wind permeates the entire body. Through the distinction of exhalation and inhalation, the winds of vitality and movement are sixteen. By means of the joining practice, for each one individually there are four maṇḍalas, and by each single stroke descending, there are one million six hundred thousand. By touching and descending from the right side, the maṇḍala of fire is to be declared. From touching both left and right, it becomes the wheel of the great sovereign. Having attained stability and leisure, it becomes the maṇḍala of rest.

The left channel and the right channel abide on the left and on the right. The all-moving one is at the center, and it follows upon the co-arisen. There is entering, exiting, and remaining — unmoved in its essential nature. The wind called the All-Vanquishing, the wind of the thread, enters for as long as life continues. The entering is called the vase-bearing. From holding it, there is the filled one. The lifting is called the rétsaka. The unmoved becomes the vase-bearing.

The assembly of winds fully entering — numbering hundreds of thousands and so forth — in accord with the words of the Buddha, the protector, is accomplished in that very instant. Whatever the winds are, having become unified, by embracing wisdom, it is accomplished in approximately half a month.

By practicing the joining with one's own superior deity, one comes to possess the buddha's gnosis perfectly and to know the five superknowledges. By practicing the joining with one's own superior deity, one embraces one's own consort. Heart center to heart center, secret joined to secret in perfect union. Mouth also made to mouth — taking earnestly to that which is without contrivance — one should meditate that the four seeds dwelling at the heart center pervade everywhere. Through that very abiding, whatever it is, one comes to know all persons completely. Simply by reflecting in all directions on what has arisen, is arising, and is arising now, one also comes to know the minds of others, and causes truth to be spoken as truth.

Likewise, through that very same joining, one should cultivate the practice with respect to the ears. Whatever sounds there are present nearby, one hears all teachings. Likewise, having well cultivated the practice with the eyes, one perceives even all three worlds. With the nose, having likewise contemplated, one comes to know all scents. With the tongue, having likewise contemplated, one fully discerns experiences even at great distances. Likewise contemplate upon the tip of one's own mark — one comes to know all tangible objects. Likewise, having contemplated upon the center of the crown of the head, one causes all capacities to greatly increase.

Wherever and in whichever wind abides, having made the tastes equal and stopped — in that very location and by that very means, one distinctly realizes the suchness of each. Pacification, enrichment, magnetization, likewise hooking, slaying, expelling — all of these are perfectly accomplished through meditation alone.

By perfectly applying the vase-bearing and so forth, the four modes of gazing are to be definitively joined. The gazing look that gazes to the left: the vase-bearing brings it under control. The hooking from the right side: the filled one definitively joins it. Whatever gaze abides at the forehead: the rétsaka performs the slaying. The gaze abiding at the tip of the nose: the vase-bearing causes arousal. The vase-bearing for flowers of the jā plant; the rétsaka for a moist tree as well; the rétsaka for what has been eaten together; the vase-bearing for a wavering root. The gaze is to be definitively joined beforehand.

Otherwise it is the activity of the three worlds. This is the fourth body, and it is not the domain even of buddhas. Through the conventions that transcend the world, it is shown in the form of the supreme yogin. Through the form of the four wheels, I have taught it as the Hevajra vajra.

In the gathering of the practice of meditation, all the channels and ḍākinīs, through Hevajra, the method of accomplishment for the fruit — wherever the practice of the winds is carried out, in the full division of day and night, at the sixty-four petals there is the rod. She who possesses the nature of the divine form — again, through the division of body, speech, and mind, the meditative equipoise of the three syllables themselves is to be understood inwardly by the mind.

By the syllable OṂ, there is entry; by the syllable ĀḤ, dwelling in that very same state; by the syllable HŪṂ, the withdrawal as well — the yogin is to carry this out from the pith instructions.

What is called OṂ ĀḤ HŪṂ — this will reveal what has cause and what is without cause. From that again, the seed with its marks is to be contemplated above, at the tip of the nose, as supreme.

Let its nature be luminosity. Likewise, self and other are brought into equality. Without agent — in the rosary of forms, again and again, moment to moment, for the purpose of rising once more from the one — the mantra practitioner, thinking of it everywhere as like a shadow or an illusion, with that distinction, is to contemplate this.

Through this pure body, whatever one meditates upon with diligent wisdom, all sentient beings are made into the nature of reality. This very divine yoga is to be understood from the pith instructions of the lama.

Again, the mantra practitioner, for the benefit of the world, is again to meditate upon the syllables. At the stage of the saṃbhogakāya, bound with the bond of total certainty in each session, with the hands firmly joined at the top — from the mechanical device taught through syllables, through the right channel (Rasanā) and the left channel (Lalanā), bring it to enter the center without movement.

Having firmly joined it in body, speech, and mind, contemplate it as the supreme seal. In that very instant, the innate arises. There is no doubt about what is to be meditated here. Whoever knows this meaning — the excellent mind is placed in equanimity, knowing all phenomena. It is the pith instruction of the path of buddhas.

You have been bathed in the great washing place of entry, and the Tathāgata has bestowed empowerment. Moreover, wherever and whenever one goes, there the buddhas will come. Throughout, as a bodhisattva, one benefits sentient beings of every kind.

Then, the synonyms of wind — the Blessed One was requested to teach them. Having distinguished them, listen as I explain. In the thirty-two rods of the lineage, there come to be four sessions. By twice sixteen there is transformation. For one and a half sessions beyond one session, there are four rods of the half-session — thus it is transformed in sequence accordingly.

For day and night, in sequence, sixteen transferences are expressly stated.

The four — fire, wind, water, and the great sovereign maṇḍala — by their flow are well known as the four rods. From the fourfold development of the four winds, it is expressly stated as four wheels.

From the division of four aspects such as the various kinds, and the distinctions of instant, duration, and the rest, those four are described there in four aspects.

Likewise, the four joys are also expressly stated as the four sessions. The three bodies together with the fourth body — likewise all things also as four. The outer sequence is likewise. The four yogas and the fourth — the body is rightly expressed as four. By this it becomes four rods.

By their thorough division, sixty-four. In the Hevajra Tantra and here, likewise, in the explicit teaching on the body, below there are four wheels. That too is the fourth body. With reference to the four joys as well, these are expressly stated as the four bodies.

This is the brief presentation of the glorious Yamāntaka Red One Tantra, Chapter Fifteen.

The Blessed One (the Buddha) declared: What is to be spoken of as the wheel of existence? Asked this most secret of divine secrets — sentient beings bewildered in the three realms of existence, hear this essence that corrupts: "Always striving to accomplish buddhahood itself, māras have emanated this" — in this way the yogin wanders in saṃsāra. From wandering in saṃsāra, one comes to cherish cyclic existence above all else. Like a lotus petal upon water, nothing whatsoever stains it with flaw. This wheel of twelve aspects abides supremely as the twelve meanings. This very wheel is also well known as the twelve limbs.

Whoever comes to know this wheel, through it comes to know without remainder all the phenomena acclaimed by all buddhas, and turns the wheel of Dharma. Therefore, hear me explain the turning of the wheel of Dharma with twelve limbs.

Ignorance, formations, consciousness, name-and-form, the six sense sources, contact, feeling, craving, clinging, becoming, birth, and aging-and-death — these are the twelve limbs.

Ignorance is the afflictions of the past. Formations are the actions of the past. Consciousness is the individual conceptualization of the past. Name-and-form are the five embryonic stages: the gelatinous, the viscous, the oval, the solid, and the stage in which the limbs take shape. The six sense sources are the six faculties — eye and the rest — that are complete upon emergence from the womb. Contact arises from the meeting of three: object, faculty, and consciousness. Feeling is distinguished in three aspects according to its characteristics: pleasure, suffering, and equanimity. Craving is attachment born of desire for union. Clinging is the thorough grasping of desired objects. Becoming is the karmic fruit arising from the existence that is to come. Birth is the rejoining to the aggregates once again. Aging-and-death comprises the four limbs beginning with name-and-form.

Through the distinctive analysis of the twelve limbs,
the Sage (Śākyamuni) taught dependent origination.
As long as there is affliction, action, and suffering,
those twelve are gathered under three.

The first, the eighth, the ninth — affliction.
The second and the tenth become action.
The remaining seven are suffering.
Because the twelve gather into three,
the three dharmas will be taught.
Their recitation divides into two parts,
each becoming a group of seven.
That itself cycles again and again,
and by this cycling it is the wheel of existence.

Beings serve as causes and effects for one another;
there are no sentient beings that are absent here, even one.
From emptiness thus constituted, phenomena abundantly arise,
yet from phenomena there is no actual transmigration.
Read well: a lamp's flame spreading, a mirror's reflection,
the transmission of sound, the transference of sunlight —
from a seed, these aggregates arise in continuity,
yet there is no linking and no transmigration.
The habitual conduct of the mind goes on to taming.

The twelve limbs spoken of here
are the distinctive teaching of the Sage.
Just as they arise through dependent origination,
know them in their summary.

One who desires to hear the separate, definitive teaching —
all that is called the domain of the buddhas,
the master here has spoken as it abides.
"Afflictions, actions, and sufferings" — by this they are summarized.

Now, with regard to those two, the ten that are set forth separately in the superior manner —

These limbs, as spoken of here, are distinguished by their particular aspects. For example, the distinction of the entity called aṅgabhaṅga is praised. Here, aṅga means "distinction." The Sage — one who has mastered body, speech, and mind through that very mastery — has set forth the teaching, and "proclaimed it with perfect clarity" is a synonym for this.

These limbs are not to be understood as having a supreme lord, an intrinsic nature, a sovereign power, a guardian of the quarters, an essential identity, or anything of that sort. They are distinguished simply as the twelve limbs.

Nevertheless, concerning how the afflictions, actions, and sufferings mutually support one another, these three proceed together in summary — in the manner of three staves leaning upon one another — just as water flows on.

Again, one may ask: how exactly do these afflictions, actions, and sufferings, when gathered into this triad, proceed? This must be explained.

"The first, eighth, and ninth become afflictions." Among the distinct limbs of these twelve, the three — namely the first, ignorance; the eighth, craving; and the ninth, grasping — are to be understood as each individually summed up under affliction.

"The second and the tenth are karma." That is, the second is formations, and the tenth is becoming. These two are to be understood as summed up under karma.

"The remaining seven are suffering." The seven remaining distinct limbs — those left over after what has been subsumed under affliction and karma — are to be understood as summed up under suffering. These are: consciousness, name-and-form, the six sense sources, contact, feeling, birth, and old age and death as well. The particle "as well" functions here as an inclusive connector, properly gathering together all those from ignorance onward and subsuming them under suffering.

Thus, all twelve of these phenomena are gathered into three — affliction, karma, and suffering — for the purpose of making known that there is nothing lesser or greater than this. The particle "indeed" in this context conveys the meaning: "So long as these are definitively set forth in the sūtra, this enumeration stands supreme."

One may further ask: how do these become karma-and-affliction, and then karma-and-suffering? This must be explained.

"The three turning in alternation arise from the turning in alternation." The seventh, called affliction, turns in alternation through these three. What is called karma turns in alternation and arises entirely from that same turning in alternation. "The seventh with three, called suffering, arises" — that is, from the seven as definitively set forth before, these three arise. The very pair called "affliction and suffering" is what causes the wheel to revolve perpetually, and so it is the wheel of existence. Within that, becoming, in the form of karma, is the spoke. And this wheel — arising here with that triple name — causes the seven worlds to revolve continually. The particle "indeed" here carries the sense of certainty. Just as the moon revolves perpetually to the east, so this is the wheel of existence. Yet it does not arise in a perfectly complete and determined manner, for certainty is absent in the sense of the particle "not."

Now, one may ask what it is that makes all embodied beings subject to a lord and an agent. This must be explained. In the movement of all beings, there is no cause and effect between them whatsoever, nor is there any such thing here. What is attributed to sentient beings as something other than themselves arises from mere imputation. Even with respect to causes, it is merely a proximate imputation to a cause that produces any given result. Therefore, it is asserted that a sentient being is genuinely real relative to other substances.

Now, one may ask what it is that passes from this present world to the world beyond. This must be explained. Even as regards that next world — phenomena exist, but there is no transmigration.

And yet, from emptiness alone, empty phenomena arise — and there is no transmigration. From phenomena in which there is no self, the five whose names are affliction and karma arise; and from those five, the seven phenomena whose names are results, empty of self and what belongs to self, arise — and there is no transmigration. Thus those who abide in selfhood as an intrinsic nature to be grasped: from those very ones, the seven empty phenomena free of intrinsic nature arise — and there is no transmigration.

Now, what is it that all this is meant to illustrate? This is to be explained here. The verse runs:

Reading aloud from a teacher, a lamp, a seal,
A mirror, a sound, the sun's movement,
A seed, and the eye's definite demonstration —

This verse points to the fact that in suchness there is no going, and that there is no self by nature; yet conventionally, the world beyond exists.

Consider, for example, the act of reading aloud from a teacher: there is no transmigration. The teacher's act of reading is not impaired, and the student's act of reading is not impaired — and yet the student's reading is not something other than the teacher's. Likewise, if things arose altogether without cause, that would entail the fault of causelessness. In the same way, the mind at the moment of death does not travel to the world beyond — for that would entail the fault of permanence. Nor does the world beyond arise from something entirely different from itself. Just as the reason for the student's reading lies in the teacher's reading, and that which has the capacity to do so declares, "It is precisely that, not other than that" — so too it is here. In the same way, depending on the mind that has reached its final point in death, the mind that partakes of birth is itself not something other. Here too that capacity is explicitly declared.

Consider these examples: a lamp from a lamp; a reflection back from a seal; an image from a mirror reflecting a face; an echo from a sound; fire from sunlight, a jewel's rays, and tinder; a sprout from a seed. And consider how, when someone eats the fruit of a chilli plant and thereby causes another to salivate, one should understand: it is not something other than that. In the same way, wise ones should examine thoroughly that the aggregates do not transmigrate from one set of aggregates to another.

The aggregates are these — those bearing the names form, feeling, perception, formations, and consciousness. These junctions of aggregates connecting one lifetime to the next are to be examined carefully as pertaining to the five aggregates. They are neither without cause nor do they arise from something entirely other. They are not from this world to the world beyond. Accordingly, the eye too involves no actual transmigration of substance. For as long as this continues, it is the sequential revolving of formations in cyclic existence. The word "also" here marks a reversal; therefore one must not understand it in a distorted way.

Because things are always of the nature of suffering and empty of self, one will not become thoroughly confused regarding individually conceptualized things. The mind free of confusion will not continually take birth. Without bewilderment, there will be no transgression through negligence. Without malice, one will not constantly engage in sinful actions. One will not take up anything as an object of action. Without grasping and without active conditioning, there is no imputation of things as existing or non-existing. The minds will not fall into sufferings with regard to the aggregates that are unborn. In the same way, from these five modes of providing causal nourishment, seven kinds of fruit are obtained here — they do not arise from anything other than that absence of other. And that is to be called liberation.

Likewise, wrong and deluded views regarding faith, permanence, annihilation, transmigration, and the like do not continually arise. The Blessed One also spoke a verse here:

Not seen as arising from a condition,
And by reality itself there is no feeling —

Birth and cessation too —
By what do they arise? One examines.
Here there is nothing whatsoever to be removed,
Not even a fraction to be purified.
To see reality truly, with a true view —
When one sees truly, one is fully liberated.

The Blessed One's Lady then requested: Blessed One, I wish to hear something wondrous.

Combining the root of śākoḍaka with the root of barura and placing them in a house will cause quarreling. Placing the guṇḍaka that dwells within a dhatura flower inside a fragrant flower causes headaches to arise in the one against whom it is directed. Destroying the root by chewing it provides release.

Making a cavity in the shape of a vagina, filling it with a woman's excrement mixed with poison and a strip of cloth, and smearing it on someone's path harms that person.

Sesame oil prepared from sesame that has been soaked in the milk of the śnehi tree, when applied, causes white hair to grow on the head. Shaving off the hair provides release.

Fumigating with a woman's fingernails conceals the marks. Fumigating with guggul provides release.

Applying frog-rendered butter as an eye salve causes one to see snakes connected to one's house.

Throwing the powder of the mushroom known as muzi onto a sleeping lamp's flame causes it to blaze up. Eating the root of the plant shinuhi together with molasses renders one sleepless. Binding the root of kāmaci to the crown of the head renders one sleepless.

For taming nāgas: take the root of the droṇa flower, turmeric, and a large rice-ball, bind them together, shake them, then wipe — one is victorious over all exhaustion of water.

Eating the root of the śalmali tree combined with red lead causes flowers to fall. Administering kāṅguṣṭha and madirā, or else separating the dust with betel, causes separation. Eating the leaves of the shinuhi plant, the seeds of markandī, and the powder of guṇa together with molasses causes bleeding.

Applying the powder of cuṭcuṭindara to a horse's nose causes it to refuse food. Washing with sandalwood provides release.

Bind the root of the ketaka plant to the head; bind the root of the śmṛkhaṭa plant to the hand; apply the root of the tāla plant to the right elbow. Then, facing north, naked, with hair unbound, tie small amounts of all three together and drink — one will not be harmed by weapons.

Rubbing together the seeds of śoṇāka and the two substances called būrakapāduka in the skin of a deer's hide, placing them on the underside of the tongue and eating the fruit of the laṭṭapa — one will not be burned.

Now then: O Blessed One, please teach the causes and reasons for what the seasons bring about and what is determined to befall sentient beings.

The vajra-born, who has come to know all that is done,
let him offer this to me.

I see the lotus born of mud with three petals,
its filaments adorned with honey.

How wonderful — a field endowed with bliss,
beautified by the buddha of attachment nearby.
Those possessed of desire bring bliss to peace,
abandoning all conceptual thought.

The mind, stirred by desire,
lays a woman on her back,
raises her legs to the shoulders,
and examines carefully above and below.

Into the unfolded lotus of that one,
into the hollow of the lotus, one enters.
At the petal of the lotus is the essence of flesh —
the vajra is inserted there with that essence.

Just as my own fluid,
by twenty thousand hundred millions,
in the center, the mind is supremely honored.

From the essence of the wind, the embrace, the quintessence within me,
at the unsurpassed tip of the vajra,
possessed of raktabandhukā —
the wise one speaks and recites there and then.

Again one should meditate on a single hero:
the supreme mind of bliss, unmoved.

From that, granting the supreme one by one,
the moment of joy becomes twofold.
For as long as one thus recites with eloquence,
one should contemplate the form body for just an instant.

The woman who has been made to arise as a painted image
is, for beings, also threefold.
Know that she grants bliss and brings peace.
Here one contemplates just a little.

For the sake of fierce force she abides in sinful acts —
she of that kind is one whose depth is difficult to fathom.
She is always beaten until she cries out,
burned in many ways, screaming and wailing.
Her body remains for three aeons.

What need is there to speak of the qualities of women?
She who fully embraces all sentient beings —
more than making day of compassion —
is a woman who dwells distinctly in mind.
She knows well what has declined,
and makes all knowledge expansive.

If such a form of a beggar-woman
is none other than a vajra yoginī,
then seeing her and her decline —
ranging from ground to ground —
by the mere act of remembering her,
in that very instant bliss is experienced.

The direction itself, the place, and the woman herself
abide perfectly in the body of a deity.
Having rendered benefit to that mudrā,
one dwells with that mind-endowed one.

Therefore one is thoroughly liberated from faults,
skilled in all supreme qualities,
and possessing great, great, great merit,
with excellence and action brought to perfection.

Therefore, gazing steadily upon her,
pressing her lips and teeth together,
the yogin makes the sound "śīt,"
and at that spot a lateral pressing is to be made.
The arising of one's own bliss is to be bound.
Sambhaka itself, too,
is called the named assembly that transcends binding.
The web of bees that is bound —
from above the legs, a wheel of mechanism.
Likewise, precisely so, the tortoise-binding
is the finest of all bindings.

In the center of that, in a cross-legged posture,
on a cushion of five-by-three folded layers,
having done this, the shoulders of both are held.
Then the Blessed Lady
firmly embraces the Blessed One,
and having fully extended the lotus and the vajra,
she offers this supplication:

"What kind of meditation should arise
in the yoga of the completion stage?
For the benefit of the yogins,
please give this petition with its fruit."

The Blessed One declared:

Whatever the yoga of the completion stage may be,
the yogin devoted to yoga
should cultivate it with single-minded awareness
in a manner that is equal through day and night.

Whatever virtue and excellence one cultivates, one should constantly apply;
it is definitively expanded by the nature of existence.
Through being exceedingly stable and through direct application,
it will become clearly manifest just as it truly is.

Then one's mother, or daughter,
or yoginī, niece,
or any other female relative —
the fierce woman (caṇḍālī), likewise a brahmin woman,
and these assemblies of women — all are given.
All should be attended with proper ritual.

Just as one does not know the distinction,
a venerable master who knows the distinction through distinctions
leads the practitioner to Avīci hell.
Here, indeed, there is worldly contradiction.
The truth of the world beyond is destroyed.
Therefore, exerting oneself thoroughly,
with fine and excellent intelligence, one undertakes it properly.

Having concealed it like the mantra of a ḍākinī,
just as the buddhas have taught,
in a direction concordant with the mind,
abandoning all harm to those nearby,
one's own mind as the goddess of joyful desire —
these are to be properly taken up, the highest.

Having made it secluded and free of fatigue,
and whatever clothing is at hand,
the two should cultivate stability in meditation.
From the excellent mutual union,
the woman is made to face toward oneself.
Having entered, the two, mutually,
should examine with desire, dwelling in steadiness.

Then gazing and speaking into the mouth,
one should dwell with one-pointed mind.
Likewise, from that very reality one should declare —
this produces the splendor of truth in the highest degree.

"Her son and householder — you;
to me: brother and farmer-father;
you are my mother and my wife,
my yoginī and niece —"

"I am the servant of the seven men;
you are the servant and bondwoman of the one with a face.
Speak and recite just such words —
this is the supreme that produces the splendor of bliss.

You are my mother, my wife,
my yoginī and my niece;
I am your servant at all times —
lean supremely toward the sharp short lance.

Let the loving mother gaze upon me;
from intimate gazing, with certain understanding,
seeing me, that person,
again and again, having exclaimed 'O!' —
bestows the three letters.

The blood, equal to the vajra's nectar,
should be made to terrify the lotus ground;
seeing it, the eyes are utterly bewildered.
The face too, having been anointed with fragrance,
then one should press upon the breasts.
Showing it, universally, in that very mouth —
touching it, one gives it into the mouth,
causing it to move, and one should speak.
By that, one should declare as follows:

'My Aruṇdhatī —
seeing her, Vairocana's food turned upside down;
for the navel gone topsy-turvy six months,
it is for the sake of seeing all the openings.'

From touching and seeing the letter HA — through the three directions.
From seeing the dark marks at the rootless root — from that.
From seeing all the limbs as cool — within eighteen days.
When, right after bathing, it has dried — within ten days.
From the body's foul odor — within two months.
From the urine circling to the left — within three nights.
From seeing the body as thin — within six months.
From not seeing the tip of the nose — within one day.

For the sake of knowing these signs in their directions,
likewise the yogī should meditate,
and with diligent concentration recite mantra.
The yogī should read with effort;
the reading of the five guardians and so forth
may be done by oneself or by another.

Each day, for the assembly, food,
and likewise food for the Saṅgha, and other such things;
with loving care for the lowly and the weak,
one should always act for the sake of sentient beings."

Thus it was spoken in the Tantra of the Variegated.

This is the twenty-first chapter of the Tantra of Yamāntaka, the Red.

Kyé! Blessed One, with compassion arising in mind toward those — all tīrthikas — who are without lovingkindness and who take the purpose of desire as their chief concern, and those who pursue the killing of all with a toxic mouth — since the Lord has become compassionate toward the intentions of such beings, may the Lord speak this to me.

First, give the restoration ceremony (poṣadha);
afterward, give the five bases of training.
Train with effort in the tenets of the Vaibhāṣika;
likewise the Sautrāntika as well.
Having become habituated to the ten virtues,
show reverence to the teacher, take delight in the Vinaya.
Having had one introduced to the Middle Way as well,
one should then enter comprehensively into mantra.

A tantra more secret than the secret —
the swift cause of perfection and accomplishment —
through this ritual preparation, swiftly,
having heard the training, one's domain of conduct becomes excellent.
Abandoning the four māras, the yogī
should abide through divine practice.

Having cut with an axe and shaken with a skull-drum at the time of a solar eclipse, Akṣobhya scatters it; then, pressing down with both feet, one should recite the mantra. If one recites one hundred thousand secret mantras, the bindhu is bound. One goes into the power of Yamāntaka, and that is made luminous. This is the oral instruction for meditating at one's own navel.

Whatever one begins from the very moment —
accomplish it through that day's [practice].
Bring it to its culmination within five days.
From the world of false speakers,
what is excellently and most clearly revealed —
that is the supreme yoga of assembled suchness.
Teach that very suchness to all yoginīs.
Recite one hundred thousand recitations;
the steadfast practitioner: five hundred thousand;
whoever has been spoken of in their own tantra —
from one's own nature: four hundred thousand.
Then this most subtle one —
the goddess reveals it all clearly.

This is the twenty-second chapter, the King of Tantras, of the glorious Tantra of Yamāntaka, the Red.

Whoever does not know this very tantra,
yet asserts definitively, tantra by tantra —

If with the intention of making a living
one wanders in the land of emptiness —

This completes the King of Tantras of the glorious Tantra of Yamāntaka, the Red.

The figure Nirṛti in "the blood of Nirṛti and others" renders Sred med kyi bu, literally "son of Nirṛti," a demonic figure associated with the lord of the southwest direction in Indian mythology, here representing the kind of being whose blood fills the skull-cup.

The term "wheel of means" for "krul khor" renders the Sanskrit yantra, mechanical or ritual devices here understood as esoteric ritual instruments or configurations rather than physical machines.
