The Great King of Ritual Procedures, Victorious over the Three Worlds (Trailokya-vijaya-mahākalpa-rāja)

Toh 482 in the Kangyur is a text known in Sanskrit as the Trailokya-vijaya-mahākalpa-rāja and in Tibetan as Kham gsum rnam par rgyal ba'i rgyal po chen po, meaning "The Great King of Ritual Procedures, Victorious over the Three Worlds." It is a mahākalpa, or great tantra of ritual procedures, belonging to the class of tantric scriptures and concerned with Vajrasattva and the practices associated with conquest over the three realms of existence. The text opens in the classic sūtra style with the phrase "Thus did I hear," situating the teaching on the summit of Mount Meru before an assembly of bodhisattvas and a vast retinue of divine and semi-divine beings. It was translated from Sanskrit into Tibetan by the Indian preceptor Padmākara and the celebrated great-revision translator, the monk Rinchen Zangpo, and belongs among the tantric collections of the Kangyur.

Translated by the Indian preceptor Padmākara and the great-revision translator, the monk Rinchen Zangpo; edited and established as the final version.

In the Indian language: Trailokya-vijaya-mahākalpa-rāja. In the Tibetan language: The Great King of Ritual Procedures, Victorious over the Three Worlds. Homage to the glorious Vajrasattva, the great bodhisattva.

Thus did I hear. At one time, the Blessed One was dwelling on the summit of Mount Meru, in a palace built of vajra and precious jewels at its very peak, together with many tens of millions of bodhisattvas, and with gods, nāgas, yakṣas, scent-eaters (gandharvas), half-humans, spirits, flesh-eaters, memory-stealers, mother-spirits, and many others both pleasing and unpleasing to behold — all of them possessing fervent devotion to the teaching of the Buddha.

Then the Blessed One, the great bodhisattva Vajrapāṇi, descended from the midst of the sky, bowed with his crown at the two feet of the Blessed One, circumambulated the Blessed One three times, and spoke these words: "Blessed One, I request that you grant me permission to impose the sacred bond of commitment upon those unruly sentient beings beginning with the Great God (Śiva). Blessed One, for that purpose, please bestow your command upon me."

Hearing the words of Vajrapāṇi, the great bodhisattva, the Blessed One — the great Dharma-king, the Tathāgata — spoke to Vajrapāṇi thus: "Yakṣa, you who are supremely proud, supremely wrathful, and supremely fierce — how is it that you speak such mantra-words for the sake of sentient beings?"

Then the great bodhisattva Vajrapāṇi, the bodhisattva of great scope, addressed the Blessed One in these words: "Therefore, Blessed One, I will tame those unruly sentient beings. And why? Because I wish to bring those who are attached to the pleasures of desire under my power and by such means to tame them, so that I may place them in unsurpassable bliss. And by purifying those unruly sentient beings through means such as slaying, I wish to place them in peace."

Then the Blessed One gave his assent to the great bodhisattva Vajrapāṇi with the word "Excellent!" and said: "Excellent, excellent, Vajrapāṇi! For the sake of the whole world, for its benefit, for its happiness, recite this heart-essence."

Then the great bodhisattva Vajrapāṇi, addressing all those from the gods down to all human beings who possessed fervent devotion to the teaching of the Buddha, declared: "Friends, go for refuge to the Buddha! And uphold my command as well!"

Then Vajrapāṇi bowed with his crown at the two feet of the Blessed One and recited this heart-essence of the one Victorious over the Three Worlds:

Oṃ sumbha ni sumbha hūṃ. Gṛhṇa gṛhṇa hūṃ. Gṛhṇāpaya gṛhṇāpaya hūṃ. Ānaya ho bhagavan vajra hūṃ phaṭ.

The very moment this was recited, the entirety of all three worlds trembled, greatly trembled, and trembled throughout. They shook, greatly shook, and shook throughout. They quaked, greatly quaked, and quaked throughout. Then all sentient beings — gods and all the rest — trembled and shook, and came to where the Blessed One was. All sentient beings throughout all the world-realms of the four continents, as far as the surrounding mountains and the great surrounding mountains, filled those realms entirely.

Then the Blessed One Vajrapāṇi, surveying all the assembled maṇḍala-circles in the world-realm of the four continents, spoke these words: "Friends, agree to go for refuge to the Buddha and the rest! And uphold my command as well!"

Then all of the great assembled retinue became terrified of the Blessed One Vajrapāṇi.

Trembling with terror, they went for refuge to the Blessed One Tathāgata.

Then the great gods (devas), the mighty ones, and the Brahmā beings addressed the Blessed One with these words: "Blessed One, who is this sentient being of such great miraculous power, of such great splendor, who subdues us in this way?"

Then, in order to make this matter clear, the Blessed One uttered and spoke these verses:

For the benefit of all sentient beings,
all the buddhas have blessed him.
Samantabhadra, lord of all,
born from the very nature of all buddhas —
Vajrapāṇi, the great yakṣa,
who holds power to tame the unruly —
by the mere utterance of whose name
even buddhahood itself is attained.

Then the great bodhisattva Vajrapāṇi again declared: "Listen, you gods! Go for refuge to the Buddha, and act in accordance with my command as well! This single blazing vajra, flaring with fire, shall burn each and every one of you utterly to ashes!"

Then the Great God (Śiva) and all the rest spoke these words: "You are a yakṣa, and we are gods. Therefore we are not able to follow the command of a yakṣa."

Then Vajrapāṇi, hearing this, became enraged and addressed the Blessed One thus: "Blessed One, the Blessed One has conferred empowerment upon me and appointed me with authority to tame all the unruly. Therefore, if the Blessed One commands me, I wish to tame this Great God."

Then the Blessed One addressed the Great God with these words: "Great being, yield! Otherwise, this one shall slay you."

Then the Great God and all the rest — the lords of the three worlds, the gods — addressed the Blessed One: "Blessed One, we are not ones who follow the commands of this unruly yakṣa."

Then the Great God prostrated at the two feet of the Blessed One and addressed the Blessed One Vajrapāṇi with these words: "O yakṣa! I am the great lord of all, the creator, the destroyer, the master of living beings. I am Brahmā. I am the one who wishes to be free. I am the Great Sovereign."

Then the great bodhisattva Vajrapāṇi, filled with fury, declared: "You are a vile, putrid being, a flesh-eater, one who performs violent and savage deeds — you are fierce. While bearing a body that feeds on flesh and carrion, how can you be Brahmā, how can you be the one who wishes to be free, how can you be the Great Sovereign? The word 'Brahmā' does not apply to you. 'The one who wishes to be free' means liberation. 'Great Sovereign' means lord of the desire realm. I am the lord of all sentient beings — therefore I am able to tame them."

Then the Great Sovereign, the Great God, overcome with rage, prostrated at the two feet of the Blessed One and then, with the terrifying form of his great wrath, let out a great laughing roar, creating immense terror. He filled the thousand world-realms entirely with a blazing conflagration, fashioning boundless and varied forms throughout all world-systems. Established in the accomplishment of great blazing fire, he manifested — together with peerless vajras, triple-pointed spears, wheels, power-weapons, clubs, and incomparable weapons being hurled and thrown — surrounded by hosts of māmo spirits and troops of elemental beings. Emanating māras, obstructors, and limitless, boundless forms of every kind, he fought and scattered wildly in the company of an immense horde of utterly intolerable, furious, and unruly ones. He turned the world-realm of the four continents into the pitch darkness of night, and then, surrounding it with a mass of great blazing fire, he lit it brilliantly with radiance.

Then the Blessed One, the great Vajradhāra, bared his fangs just slightly, his teeth biting down from above; he stirred the waves of the wrathful furrow between his brows; and with his left eye opened wide, he gazed with a vajra-gaze full of wrath, and spoke these profound words, this very essence: the text named "Glorious Conqueror of the Three Worlds."

HŪṂ.

And with that syllable HŪṂ, the entire world-realm of the great trichiliocosm — the billion worlds — was filled with that sound, and it shook, violently shook, shook utterly and completely. It trembled, violently trembled, utterly and completely trembled.

Then the entire assembly of gods — the Great Lord (Śiva) and all the rest — cried out: "Blessed One, protect us! Sugata, protect us!" And they fled to the Blessed One for refuge.

Then the Blessed One said this to Vajrapāṇi: "You are not to recite the words of such a mantra."

Then Vajrapāṇi prostrated at the feet of the Blessed One, and again proclaimed this, the Conqueror of the Three Worlds:

OṂ SUMBHA NI SUMBHA HŪṂ. GṚHṆA GṚHṆA HŪṂ. GṚHṆĀPAYA GṚHṆĀPAYA HŪṂ. ĀNAYA HO BHAGAVAN VAJRA HŪṂ PHAṬ.

The moment he had spoken these words, all the lords of gods — the Great God and all the rest — together with their retinues, were turned upside down, head over heels, toward the place where the Blessed One Vajrapāṇi was. They were drawn into the great maṇḍala at the summit of Mount Meru and pressed down, and the Blessed One Vajrapāṇi placed his left foot upon them.

Then the Blessed One conferred upon Vajrapāṇi, the bodhisattva of great scope, the words "Excellent!" saying: "Excellent, excellent, Vajrapāṇi — it is well that you tame sentient beings in just this manner! Now then, be at ease and take a breath."

Then Vajrapāṇi, the bodhisattva of great scope, for the sake of those beings, spoke this vajra-binding:

HŪṂ VAJRA DṚḌHO ME BHAVA RAKṢA SARVĀN SVĀHĀ.

The moment Vajrapāṇi had uttered these words, all those gods — the Great God and all the rest — recovered their senses.

Then the Blessed One said to them: "Friends, pledge yourselves to act in accordance with the command of this great being."

Thereupon all of them replied with one voice to the Blessed One: "We submit, O Blessed One."

Then Vajrapāṇi, the bodhisattva of great scope, again spoke his own heart-essence:

HŪṂ ṬAKKI HO.

The moment he had uttered this, all the gods and the rest were brought under his power. They bowed to the Blessed One Vajrapāṇi, and with tears streaming from their eyes, they roared aloud, fled for refuge, and supplicated: "Blessed One, protect us! Now that we are obedient to your command, please tell us — what are we to do?"

Then Vajrapāṇi, the great bodhisattva, spoke to them: "Friends, pledge yourselves to take up the awakening mind and the vow."

They replied with this supplication: "Blessed One, we wish to pay homage to the buddha Blessed Ones and to give rise to the mind set upon awakening."

Then Vajrapāṇi, the great bodhisattva, introduced them properly into the maṇḍala and caused them to take the vow. The awakening mind was made firm.

Grant happiness to all sentient beings.
Vajrapāṇi, the great lord —
do not disparage desire.
This is the secret.

Then the Blessed One, having caused them to take the vow, bestowed the samaya pledge:

OṂ VAJRA SAMAYA ABHIṢIÑCA MĀ VIRĀGAYA SARVA KĀMĀN HŪṂ PHAṬ.

Then they said —

"This was spoken by Māra, and so we will not listen to it."

Then the Blessed One, the Tathāgata, said to them: "You must not disparage this." They replied with a question: "But did not the Blessed One himself declare that freedom from attachment is the awakening of a buddha?" The Blessed One spoke: "Friends, I act according to what tames each being. It is by this very means that I expose the failings of this world-realm — teaching the Dharma by which buddha Blessed Ones tame the world in the manner suited to those who are not attached to pleasure and those who are attached to suffering. For the purpose of training them in this, the Tathāgata commands all: Samantabhadra the bodhisattva must by all means not abandon desire."

Then Vajrapāṇi again declared:

OṂ VAJRA SAMAYA ABHIṢIÑCA MI VIRĀGAYA SARVA KĀMĀN HŪṂ PHAṬ.

Then they said: "As the Blessed One has declared, so shall it be."

Then Vajrapāṇi placed a vajra in their hands and bestowed this vajra empowerment:

OṂ VAJRA ABHIṢIÑCA SARVA TATHĀGATA VAJRA DHARO TTVĀ VAJRAPĀṆI HĪ. HA HA HA HA.

Then the empowerment of the vajra name was bestowed as follows. The Great Sovereign (Śiva, Mahādeva) received the name Vajra Supreme Vidyā-Mantra. The Great Mighty One received the name Vajra Illusion. Brahmā received the name Vajra Silence. The Young Lord of Sound received the name Vajra Bell. Śakra (Indra) received the name Vajra Armor. These and all other lords of the three worlds were empowered as great ministers of vidyā-mantra.

Then those who move through the atmosphere received vajra names as follows. The Whirling Ambrosia received the name Vajra Whirl. The Moon received the name Vajra Radiance. The Razor's Point received the name Vajra Razor. The Meteor received the name Vajra Meteor. These were empowered as vajra wrathful ones.

Then the gods who move through the sky received vajra names as follows. The One Drunk on Wine received the name Held by Vajra. The Maker of Wine received the name Vajra Garland. The Victor received the name Vajra Sovereignty. The Victor-Born received the name Vajra Victorious. These and the others were empowered as lords of the assembly.

Then those dwelling on earth received vajra names as follows. The Treasurer received the name Vajra Crossbeam. Wind received the name Vajra Wind. Fire received the name Vajra Fire. Kubera received the name Vajra Terror. These and the others were empowered as vajra messengers.

Then all the divine lords dwelling beneath the earth received vajra names as follows. The Pig received the name Vajra Humpback. Yama (the Lord of Death) received the name Vajra Black. The Crown of the Earth received the name Vajra Guide. Varuṇa received the name Vajra Nāga. These and the others were empowered as vajra servants.

Then the lords of the three worlds placed vajras in the hands of all the goddesses, bestowed the vajra empowerment upon them, and conferred vajra names as follows. The Afflicted One received the name Vajra Wrathful Lady. The Wealthy One received the name Vajra Golden Lady. The Brahmin Lady received the name Vajra Peaceful Lady. The Poisonous One received the name Vajra Youthful Lady. The Lady of Power received the name Vajra Fist. These and the others were empowered as great ministers of vidyā-mantra.

Then the mothers who move through the atmosphere received their empowerment as follows. The Ambrosia Lady received the name Vajra Ambrosia Lady. The Birth Lady received the name Vajra Lovely Lady. The Razor-Bearing Lady received the name Vajra Razor-Tipped. The Life-Seizer received the name Vajra Girdle. These and the others were empowered as vajra wrathful ladies.

Then the mothers who move through the atmosphere received their empowerment as follows. The Slayer Lady received the name Vajra Slayer Lady. The Devouring Lady received the name Vajra Devouring Lady. The Clothed One received the name Vajra Robing Lady. The Joyful Lady received the name Vajra Sovereign Lady. These and the others were empowered as vajra assembly ladies.

Then the mothers who move upon the earth received their empowerment as follows. The Peaceful Lady received the name Vajra Messenger Lady. The Wind Lady received the name Vajra Swift Lady. The Fire Lady received the name Vajra Blazing Lady. Kubera received the name Vajra Stout Lady. These and the others were empowered as vajra messenger ladies.

Then all the mothers who dwell beneath the earth received their empowerment as follows. The Sow-Faced Lady received the name Vajra Face. The Scalp Lady received the name Vajra Black Lady. The Nose-Cutting Lady received the name Vajra Snout Lady. The Nāga Lady received the name Vajra Makara Lady. These and the others were empowered as vajra handmaid ladies.

Then all of those who had been drawn in by the great bodhisattva Vajrapāṇi aroused the wisdom of the buddhas and were given the vajra sacred pledge.

The mantra for summoning the gods: Om balitod balitod vajra ākarṣaya hūṃ jaḥ.

The mantra for those who move through the midair: Om vajra agri piṭāya samaya hūṃ.

The mantra for those who bear garlands: Om vajra māla gram.

The mantra for those who move upon the earth: Om vajra bandha haṃ.

The mantra for those who dwell below: Om vajra pātāla bhañja hūṃ phaṭ.

The mantra for all the māmo ladies: Om heruka vajra samaya sarva duṣṭaṃ samaya muprabhandha cakra hūṃ phaṭ.

Then Vajrapāṇi paid homage at the two feet of the Blessed One and spoke these words: "Blessed One, I am one who tames all sentient beings. Blessed One, please declare what is to be done with those maṇḍalas of untamed sentient beings."

The Blessed One declared: "Let those beings be brought into this very sacred pledge — whereby, having brought benefit to all sentient beings, one will perfectly and fully awaken to unsurpassed, complete, and perfect awakening."

Then Vajrapāṇi asked again: "Blessed One, how shall one attend to these spirits such as the ḍākinīs, and how to plagues and the like, and how to those in the lower destinies such as the hells?"

The Blessed One declared: "Let them be brought into diligent practice of that very sacred pledge."

Then Vajrapāṇi asked again: "Blessed One, how shall one attend to these human beings?"

The Blessed One declared: "Having drawn them into the great maṇḍala of all the Tathāgatas, let them be brought into the vajra maṇḍala. Having drawn them into the vajra maṇḍala, let them be brought into the sacred pledge maṇḍala. Having drawn them into the sacred pledge maṇḍala, let them be brought into the activity maṇḍala."

Then the great bodhisattva Vajrapāṇi, rejoicing in what the Blessed One had declared, spoke these verses:

For the benefit of all sentient beings,
blessed by all the buddhas,
the supreme one who accomplishes all that is to be accomplished —
the great maṇḍala shall be proclaimed.

Then, having explained it truly and fully,
the great and sacred maṇḍala,
resembling the wheel of Dharma in its nature —
all maṇḍalas shall be laid out with a cord.

The ritual of stretching the cord is as follows:

With a newly made and well-prepared cord
of good measure, one endowed with wisdom
stretches the cord, remembering and honoring
the Victors of the three worlds.

At the center stake shall be set
a peg of acacia wood firmly planted.
From it one stretches the cord as declared —
made by doubling the cord —
to be divided into sixteen parts.
Everywhere the arrangement is supreme in three tiers,
and likewise the encircling boundary divisions
are marked with pegs in the same manner.

Within that, the work proceeds
with a cord possessing seven gradations,
colored yellow and endowed with fragrant perfume —

the lines too shall be drawn. At the center of it
shall be placed four heaps of the finest cleaned barley.
Upon these the Protector — an image of the buddha —
shall be placed. Directly around the buddha on all sides,
the eight awakenings (bodhis) shall in due order
be drawn or else placed,
done with the finest of the five supreme colors.

Before the buddha there,
Vajrapāṇi is likewise
to be drawn or placed,
gathered in the form of the vajra and the syllable Hūṃ.
His left foot presses down upon
the Great Lord (Śiva),
and with his right foot he tramples
the goddess who is not an affliction.
The gods who roam the six desires
are to be drawn or placed
in the seven gradations of thread-space between them.
Having departed by the force of the vajra,
emerging outside, and there also
the four continents are to be drawn,
their shapes and rites exactly as appropriate,
the square designs well and beautifully arranged.
In the center of each of the continents throughout,
the world is depicted as it is, and so forth.
Brahmā and the other three —
there the lords of the world are drawn.
In the encircling mountain range
and in the spaces between the continents,
the maṇḍala of the outer vajra lineages
is to be properly drawn.
For the drawing of the gatekeepers,
at those four vajra gates:
the vajra hook, the lasso,
the chain, and the bell likewise.
Outside the encircling mountain range
are the eight hells.
Diseases of the sky, ailments,
ḍākinīs and fierce demons —
these are to be placed or drawn
for the purpose of destroying what is contrary to the Dharma.
Outside all of that are set
the various supreme torma offerings.

Then the great maṇḍala rite proceeds. In it, from the very beginning, the master prostrates to all the Tathāgatas and enters as follows: having put on pure clean robes and taking up a red silk thread, he binds the mudrā of the Vajra-Holder Woman, and placing fragrant flowers upon it, he recites this essence mantra and enters:

Oṃ vajra pratīccha māṃ ha ha ha ha hi.

Then, placing the right knee upon the ground, with camphor incense or whatever is available he makes the offering. The Vajra-Holder Woman, with both index fingers bent inward, recites this essence mantra:

Oṃ uru gra gra viriṇi viriṇi vajraMayahi bhidaye svāhā.

If one recites this twenty-one times, the signs arising from it are such things as the hair of the body stirring, the earth trembling, the perceiving of sweet fragrance, or the seeing of rays of light.

Then flowers are scattered upon the crown of Vajrapāṇi's head.

Next the disciple is introduced. At the very beginning he is bathed, dressed in clean pure robes, made to take refuge in the Three, established in the individual observance of the vow of training, and then he arouses the awakening mind.

Then, with his face covered by a cloth and placed upon his crown, the mudrā of the Vajra-Holder Woman is bound and he is admitted. Having been admitted, flowers are scattered, and whichever deity the flower falls upon — that is his deity. Then the binding of the face is undone and his own wisdom deity is shown to him. He makes offerings to it, presents whatever offerings he wishes to the master, and then recites his own vidyā or mantra and binds the mudrā as well.

From the Great King of Tantric Thought Victorious over the Three Worlds,
this is the Great Maṇḍala called the Wheel of the Three Worlds.

Then the vajra maṇḍala proceeds.

With a uniform rosary of vajras,
the vajra maṇḍala is to be examined.
The entering of it is the same,
and the rites too are to be done in like manner.
Having entered that maṇḍala,
in the same way the vajra is to be polished.
Having trained in the excellence of the victory over the three worlds,
the supreme vajra is to be accomplished.

The rite for accomplishing the vajra is as follows. Holding the vajra mudrā with both hands, one grasps the vajra, and for as long as it moves one recites the mantra. From that point on, all activities are to be performed with that vajra. This is the vajra maṇḍala.

Then the samaya maṇḍala (Skt. samayamaṇḍala) arises.

Having drawn the entire great maṇḍala,
all the signs are to be displayed.
Entry is with the vajra-bell,
and with the mudrā of the supreme vajra-holder.

The mudrā of the vajra-bell is as follows.

Having formed the complete vajra-holder mudrā,
one is to examine everything as vajra.
Placing it to the right, with the left
one rings the bell and makes entry.
Having entered, that bell
is to be rung in a state of equipoise.
One is to say: "May you become a master,
and always guard the samaya."
Just as the sound of this bell
is declared to be its impermanence,
so what is hereby given to you
by this great vajra —
what has been utterly consumed —
is this: the faith of the vajra-holder,
the three refuges, and the vows likewise.
Thus, for those who desire the awakening mind,
you have now in this very life renounced these.

This same is the samaya mantra: Oṃ ha na sa ma ya hūṃ phaṭ.

Then, remaining in that same position, one pours this vajra-water. Then the blindfold is removed and the rite proceeds in the same way. This is the samaya maṇḍala.

Then the karma maṇḍala (Skt. karmamaṇḍala) arises.

Having drawn the maṇḍala in like manner,
many kinds of vestments are to be arranged.
After entry, the master likewise
is to give instruction in all the activities.

Then comes the supremely engaged rite of karma. Before the Blessed One, one makes a four-sided hearth and kindles fire with sweet-scented wood. The sweet-scented flowers and fruits are dipped in honey-laden butter. Sitting on a bundle of kuśa grass with the upper robe worn over the right shoulder, one recites in the vajra speech. For the peaceful activities — such as pacification, enrichment, and bringing-under-power, and the like — one binds the one's own mudrā with the left hand and burns the offerings with the right, and thereby these are accomplished.

Behind the Blessed One, at a triangular hearth, one uses sharp thorny wood of wrathful trees, and their leaves, flowers, branch-tips, trunks, and fruits — similarly anointed with one's own blood. Sitting on a hard seat, with the upper robe worn over the left shoulder, one recites in wrathful speech and burns the offerings with the left hand. Thereby the fierce activities — hooking, dispatching, binding, sealing, destroying, pressing, wiping out, intoxicating, displacing, and the like — are supremely accomplished.

Then, for accomplishing all the activities of the Blessed One and all the Tathāgatas — hooking, dispatching, binding with samaya, bringing-under-power, and the rest — one sits to the right of the Blessed One on a spread of soft grass, draped in an upper robe like a patchwork garment. One recites in meditative absorption and, at a wheel-shaped hearth with a smokeless flame, burns sesame, rice, and so forth, offering with right and left hands alternately.

To the left of the Blessed One, one makes a vajra-shaped hearth. With the upper robe binding all the limbs, one sits on a soft, pliant seat. The right hand holds the vajra and forms the vajra-fist mudrā in the fire of the blazing garland into which all kinds of wood are entwined. The left hand, holding the vajra-fist, grasps a hook, and one smiles with joy. Reciting in vajra speech and burning offerings of all kinds, one thereby performs the fire-offering rite that supremely accomplishes the activities of the vajra-holder — hooking, dispatching, binding, bringing-under-power, and the rest.

From the Great King of Tantric Thought Victorious over the Three Worlds,
this is the rite of all the maṇḍalas.

Then, from the point where the explanation of the implements begins, the Blessed One, the Vajra-Holder, declared: "All the rites of the maṇḍala shall be seen and then accomplished." Why is this so?

One who has not entered the maṇḍala,
or who has abandoned the sacred pledges,
will not accomplish Vajrapāṇi,
for they do not truly know the secret.

The mind of the Blessed One is desire,
and attachment itself is that very vajra-nature.
One who has no attachment to it
will not accomplish Vajrapāṇi.
Without truly seeing in this way,
accomplishment within the maṇḍala is not taught.
The accomplishment of one who sees truly
cannot be attained by one who is riddled with doubt.
Having fallen into terrible fear,
one will be destroyed even by illness.
Spirits, too, will exert themselves against such a one.
After death, one will fall into a place of wailing.

Therefore let all enter the maṇḍala, and display illness, spirits, and the rest.

The Blessed One, the Tathāgata, is to be painted in golden hue upon cloth. Surrounding him on all sides, eight bodhisattvas are to be painted in the following arrangement. On the right side: the noble Avalokiteśvara, Mañjuśrī, and one who, having just generated the awakening mind, turns the wheel of Dharma. On the left side: the Vajra-Fist, Maitreya, and Ākāśagarbha. Above: Gaganagarbha. Below the Blessed One: Vajrapāṇi — bearing the mudrā known as "Vajra Hūṃ," bound together with a vajra in both hands. Of his remaining two hands, the right brandishes a vajra, and the left holds a flower-bow and five arrows. His body is the color of a blue lotus. He smiles slightly, with teeth ever so lightly clenched, his face laughing with a hint of fierce wrath. He stands in the posture of extending and contracting, adorned with all ornaments. His left foot presses down upon the left thigh of the Great God (Śiva). His right foot, bent, squeezes the breast of Umā. A multicolored upper garment covers his limbs. He wears a lower garment of bark cloth. He bears all the Tathāgatas as a crown-ornament upon his head.

Then, beginning from the first day of the lunar month, one recites eight thousand times daily until the fifteenth day, or for a full month, or for a full year. Wearing clean white garments and dwelling in one's own home, one eats pure food as one wishes while reciting. After one month has passed: by the first month, outer activities will be accomplished through the syllable Hūṃ. By the second, all activities will be accomplished. By the third, the Blessed One will appear in dreams and reveal all that is past and all that is to come. By the fourth, one gains the power to hook and bring all sentient beings under one's command. By the fifth month, one may appear and disappear at will. By the sixth month, one can take on any form one desires. By the seventh month, one's own body will appear as that of a buddha, a bodhisattva, and Vajrapāṇi, and all miraculous displays will be manifest. By the eighth month, one can hook, send forth, bind, bring under command, and engage in one's service the gods and other beings of the great three-thousandfold world system. By the ninth month, one can hook, send forth, bind, and bring under command all the bodhisattvas. By the tenth month, one will attain the ten grounds. By the eleventh month, one will attain the eleventh ground, the ground of the buddhas. By the twelfth month, one will become a Vajra-Holder.

Then, for the sādhana, one paints on cloth the Blessed One known as Vajra Hūṃ just as described, and around him on all sides one paints — beginning from the Great Powerful One and continuing to the nāga Varuṇa at the outermost limit — all shown prostrating and kneeling before the Blessed One. Then one recites accordingly: the mudrā known as "Vajra Hūṃ" is to be recited innumerable times.

If one recites until the end of the month, reciting through the night of the twenty-ninth day until dawn, then in the morning all will come. Having come, they will fall under one's power and will do everything as commanded.

Then another sādhana arises. Having painted the Blessed One just as before, one paints all the goddesses of golden radiance and the rest, each holding up their own mudrā and dancing, depicted in wrathful and terrifying forms. Then, in the same way, one recites through the night until the end of one month, reciting until dawn. In the morning, all of them will gather together, and those very dancers will make offerings to the practitioner. One should think: "These are the very ones painted here in this visualization," and having thought this, one should feel no fear. Then they will come under one's power and will become attendants who constantly perform all tasks.

Then there is another rite. Having painted the Blessed One on cloth just as before, and then reciting eight thousand times in the same manner, at the final evening one will attain the state of Vajra-Holder, and one will also attain the supreme accomplishment of whatever one wishes.

Furthermore, for another sādhana, recite one hundred thousand times before Vajrapāṇi. Then the Blessed One will accomplish every need. If one burns a fire offering with all manner of appropriate woods before Vajrapāṇi for the sake of whatever one desires, doing so continuously for a full year, all will be accomplished.

Then, having painted the name "Vajra Hūṃ" and reciting it innumerable times before it until the end of a year, at the final moment the Blessed One will grant the supreme fulfillment of whatever one desires. Before the Blessed One, the Tathāgata, one binds the mudrā known as "Vajra Hūṃ," and recites until the Tathāgata stirs. Then the mudrā will be accomplished. Through that accomplishment, the minor ritual actions — summoning, bringing under power, inserting, guiding, causing to fall, bringing down, transferring, and so forth — are all bound by a ray of Hūṃ, and all of these are performed by means of "Hūṃ."

Then there is another rite. If one wishes to confer empowerment by means of a vidyā, one fashions the form and attire of Vajrapāṇi from a painting or from clay, with two arms extended and holding a vajra. Beginning from the first day of the waxing moon and continuing through the fifteenth day, one performs ablution with four ladles and eight thousand measures of ghee. Then at the final night one recites until dawn. In the morning one dissolves that image, and thereafter one will become the lord of all vidyās.

Then another sādhana arises. One paints or fashions from clay the form of the buddha, the Blessed One, with arms extended, holding a vajra, in the same manner as before. If one washes this with scented water until it dissolves, from that point on one will become invisible, moving about unseen as one wishes.

Then another sādhana arises. On cloth one paints the Blessed One Vajrapāṇi with four arms: in the upper right hand a lotus, in the upper left a vajra, in the lower right the symbol "Vajra Hūṃ," in the lower left a bow and arrow. Then, having recited one hundred thousand times before that image, one observes a fast for three days at the conclusion, and recites through the night until dawn. The next morning one will take on a form resembling his, will dwell for an aeon free from decay, will bring all sentient beings under one's power, and will be able to go everywhere.

Then there arises a rite for accomplishing a maṇḍala. Having seen the maṇḍala, one bathes with three kinds of dung, changes one's garments three times a day, binds the mudrā of the Vajra-Holding goddess, and recites eight thousand times three times a day for seven days. Then Vajrapāṇi will take on the form of a teacher and in a dream will speak everything.

Then another rite for accomplishing the maṇḍala arises. Having seen the great maṇḍala, for the duration of one month one performs ablution three times a day in the same manner, changes garments three times a day, and three times a day makes a maṇḍala with the dung of cows. One then draws a vajra on that surface with a sharp iron implement and gazes upon it —

one binds the mudrā of the Vajra-Holding goddess and recites eight hundred times. At the end of one month one will behold the Blessed One.

Then a rite for accomplishing the vajra arises. One draws a vajra on cloth, on a wall, or on a flat piece of wood, makes offerings before it, holds a vajra in the vajra-fist, and recites until, within the span of one year, the vajra blazes with fire. By that blazing one gains the ability to travel through the sky, and one's body takes on the form, strength, and color of Vajrapāṇi at sixteen years of age. One comes to possess all the enjoyments of desire and attains every happiness and mental ease. One's lifespan, moreover, becomes one hundred thousand years.

Then a rite for accomplishing the sword arises. One holds a sword in the vajra-fist and recites for the duration of one year, and one will become a vidyādhara, master of all vidyādharas.

Then a rite for accomplishing the wheel arises. One draws the maṇḍala of the wheel, takes up an iron wheel, and recites until it transforms into a vajra and then transforms back again into a wheel. By holding that wheel one will set turning the wheel of all the vidyādharas.

Then, for one who wishes to accomplish a golden censer, one fashions a golden censer before Vajrapāṇi, holds it with the vajra-fist of the left hand, and recites until it has undergone the three kinds of accomplishment. By merely its smoke one will be able to summon all beings. By merely the vapor it emits one will be able to bring all beings under one's power. When it blazes, one summons and subjugates all beings and becomes a sovereign among vidyādharas.

Then a rite for summoning all gods and the rest arises. One presses with the left foot upon any garment belonging to any of the gods — the Great God (Śiva) and the other deities — binds the mudrā called Vajra Hūṃ, makes threatening gestures, and recites for the duration of one month. At the end all the gods will come, and they become servants who perform every task.

For one who wishes to bring nāgas under power, one enters a nāga lake up to the navel in the water and recites until all the nāgas display their own forms and become servants who perform every task within the span of one year.

For one who wishes to bring yakṣas under power, one goes to the place where those yakṣas dwell, binds the mudrā called Vajra Hūṃ, and recites until those yakṣas come and say, "What is your command?" Thereafter they become servants who carry out every command.

Then, for one who wishes to bring all the mātṛs (the Mother spirits) under power, one goes to the dwelling place of the mātṛs, presses upon the heart of the wrathful goddess with the heel, and recites for the duration of one month. On the twenty-ninth day a great and terrifying sound is emitted. Thereafter they are subjugated and become female servants who act in accordance with one's commands. They carry one upon their shoulders, lead one to the higher realms, bring one back again, and install one as lord of the assembly. Worshipping one, they summon every past benefit, every wish, and every young woman. They also grant yoga of this very kind, by means of which the yogin travels into the world of wrathful beings and returns again. No one at all can do even the slightest harm to such a one.

Then, for one who wishes to accomplish the Great Glorious One, the recitation is performed before Vajrapāṇi until one sees the body of a goddess emerge from the body of Vajrapāṇi. The instruction is: do not move from that spot. Thereafter she will grant every benefit, up to and including the granting of buddhahood.

Then, for one who wishes to bring Lake-Voice (Sarasvatī) under power, one arranges a garland of syllables before Vajrapāṇi, sets out the syllables of the garland, and reads through it to its end. One performs this practice three times each day every day and then recites eight thousand times. After one month has passed, one will behold Lake-Voice.

From that point on, one will be endowed with unstoppable eloquence. One will come to know all the sciences of the world and beyond the world without having studied them, as well as all arts and crafts. Thereafter, a practice arises involving castor seeds, saffron, long pepper, and silk. One takes a castor seed, places it on a mortar, presses it with the hand, and recites until three attainments arise. The first attainment causes one to become invisible. The second causes one to take on many forms. The third, when one climbs to a high place at just the right moment, enables one to travel through the sky. One takes saffron and the rest, places them in the hollow of a mortar, presses them with the mudrā of the female holder of the vajra, and recites until dawn blazes. Then, having made pills from them, one will become master of all desires, a holder of awareness, able to travel everywhere, and capable of living for a thousand years.

Thereafter a practice of Sālabhañjikā arises. Wherever a Sālabhañjikā has been drawn as an image or fashioned as a figure, one places a finger on that carving and recites for one month. Then, at the end of that month, one recites through the entirety of the night. On the morning that follows, she will reveal herself in her own form and become a wife who grants every wish.

For one who wishes to bring a female yakṣa under power, one sits in the place where that female yakṣa dwells and recites until she comes in her own form. She will then become a servant who bestows every wish.

For one who wishes to bring a flesh-eating demoness under power, one burns a heap of silk cloth and, holding one's own insignia with the left hand, burns offerings with the right. Thereupon, from that very heap of burning silk, a flesh-eating demoness of unpleasant face and ugly form will come and remain in her own form. She too, in the same way, becomes a servant.

For one who wishes to accomplish whatever work one desires, one fasts for one day and on the twenty-ninth of the month recites eight thousand times. Then for one month one eats only alms food and recites without count. When one month has passed, one fasts for three nights and recites through the entire night. Then in the morning, whatever comes, one must not be afraid of it. From that very dawn, accomplishment follows. From that point on, it becomes a servant that offers every wish, and even the goddesses of the high heavens can be summoned and offered. They are escorted away and summoned back again. It will also speak of everything past, present, and future. And all the wealth of others will be seized and offered.

For one without attainment who wishes to undertake the observance of awareness, one proceeds in sets of ten days for six months, supplanting the earlier practice with the new, ranging from subsisting on alms food and so on up to feeding on wind. At the very end, reciting through the entirety of the night, in the morning even buddhahood will be granted. Thereafter, each day one performs the previous practices up to the eighteenth recitation, and at the conclusion of that, reciting through the entirety of the night, in the morning all activities will be accomplished — accomplished by uttering so much as a single syllable, HŪṂ.

Thereafter a practice of raising the dead arises. One takes a human corpse that has not been stabbed or damaged, washes it well, anoints it thoroughly with unguents, adorns it with a garland of flowers, presses it with the left foot, and recites until breath comes forth. It will then rise up and stand, and riding upon it one can travel everywhere, and one's life too will extend to a hundred thousand years.

Thereafter arises the practice of the supreme attainment of raising the dead. One takes a human corpse that has not been stabbed or damaged, adorns it in the same way, rides upon it, and recites until it rises and falls again. It will then transform into the body of a Tathāgata. If one carries that on one's shoulder, one becomes a vidyādhara who is a relic of all the Tathāgatas, and one travels with miraculous power throughout all world-realms as infinite as space, and one's life too will extend for an eon.

Thus declared the Blessed One, Vajrapāṇi.

Next is set forth how the very sight of the maṇḍala, even without having performed the sādhana, becomes efficacious in itself. A single recitation will protect oneself, others, villages, towns, kings, and so forth. As for the drawing of boundaries and the like, when the boundary is established by the firm gaze, not even all the Tathāgatas can transgress it — what need be said of others? Earth-maṇḍalas, the sky, and the directions are likewise bound by that gaze.

For descent — whether by contact or without contact — a sword, mirror, vessel, thumb, living tree, painting, garment, bow, arrow, vajra, and so forth are wrapped and bound in cloth using the mudrā for vajra-descent and fumigated with gugul incense, and the descent is performed.

For the pacification of malicious spirits, one uses the syllable Hūṃ. The mudrā known as Vajra-Hūṃ destroys all sentient beings who are to be struck down. The mudrā of the vajra iron chain frees one from the harm of sorcery-spirits. By displaying the Heruka-vajra mudrā, one revives those killed by sorcery. By seizing the hair and raising the person while holding the vajra mudrā, one may suppress poison, draw out poison, pacify poison, conceal poison, destroy poison, and revive one who has died from poison — all by means of a single Vajra-Hūṃ.

For extracting a treasure: one fills a copper ball with water, fumigates it with gugul using the finger gesture of Vajra-Hūṃ, and recites. If treasure is present, that water will bubble up and overflow. If there is none, it will swirl in a circle. Then one recites the Hūṃ that is victorious over the three worlds and takes up the treasure.

For summoning light: one kindles fire using a branch of the doṅka tree and burns eight hundred flowers of the rājakoṣātakī plant anointed with curd, honey, and butter, whereupon one attains light.

For the lower realm: using the mudrā of the lower vajra, one smashes all mechanical contrivances, recites Vajra-Hūṃ, and enters — no one will be able to harm one in that place. Nor will the wheel of those who desire to seize one be able to do anything whatsoever.

In the presence of Vajrapāṇi, one recites a hundred times in each of the three periods of the day over the course of one year, directing it toward a vajra, or an emerald, or a ruby, or a pearl, or gold, or a weapon, or whatever else, or toward a man, or a woman, or an image of a buddha. When one year is completed, it will multiply a hundredfold. If one recites that many hundreds, it will multiply to that many hundreds. If one then recites a thousand times, it will multiply a thousandfold.

For drawing wealth: one recites one hundred thousand times. For summoning, one performs the summoning using the mudrā of the bowed vajra, completing it within four months. For bringing under one's power, one uses the samaya mudrā of the vajra fist.

For transferring all sentient beings: one binds the Vajra-Hūṃ mudrā and recites one hundred thousand times, and all sentient beings are transferred exactly as the mind directs them.

For suppressing water: one binds the vajra mudrā and recites until one is able to walk upon the surface of water. For suppressing fire: a single recitation suffices. For protecting crops: one uses the vajra gaze, so that no creature will damage them and even rats and the like will not cross into them. For deflecting a blow: one uses the wrathful gaze — wherever one looks, the blow is deflected there. For transferring food, drink, and all manner of things: by the blazing gaze, they are transferred into one's own vessel. For eliminating all scorpions and snakes: a single recitation suffices. For eliminating all predatory animals: a single recitation makes them disappear. For defeating all thieves: one binds the vajra fist. For delivering from warfare: one uses the mudrā of the vajra-bearer.

For bows, arrows, spears, and victory banners: one presses them down and recites until they move, and whoever carries them will become victorious over every army. For a legal dispute: a single recitation brings about the answer to the dispute. For all pacifying rituals: the fire offering of sesame. For all enriching rituals: the fire offering of white mustard seed. For all dispelling rituals: the fire offering of thorns. For all healing rituals: the fire offering of dūrvā grass leaves. For eliminating all harm, inauspicious weather, and bad dreams from their very basis: the fire offering of sesame and rice. For destroying all: the syllable HŪṂ. For inducing agitation and causing madness in any being: the syllable PHAṬ. For putting all to sleep: one curls up, closes the eyes, and recites one hundred times — all will thereby be put to sleep. For sealing all sentient beings: when one looks with the wrathful vajra gaze, they are sealed, and they will not survive beyond seven days. For stealing the voice: one grasps one's own tongue between the teeth and applies the practice up to the point of the syllable HŪṂ. For drawing out the heart: one crosses the arms with the mudrā of the vajra finger and thrusts into one's own armpit while reciting the essence, and within seven nights it will be transferred. For cutting the heart: one takes the vajra and cuts. For opening the heart: one strikes with the vajra iron spike, names the person by name, and visualizes their form. For inflating the heart: one takes the vajra in both hands, applies it seven times at the mark, and it will be transferred. Then the means of liberating oneself from these mudrās is explained: from the sealing — with a tightly clenched fist. From the stealing of the voice — by the vajra speech recitation. From the drawing of the heart — by releasing the mudrā of the vajra fist at the chest. From the cutting — one recites the vajra HŪṂ and then washes with milk. From the splitting — one withdraws the spike and anoints with vajra water. From the inflating of the heart — one is liberated through the vajra essence. In the same way, the mudrā of the curse is also to be transferred. For one who wishes to accomplish knowledge of a woman's heart: gazing at her chest and reciting once, one will hear the sound abiding in her heart. For a man's heart it is done in the same way, and likewise for every heart. Then for one who wishes to accomplish knowledge of another's mind: reciting the vajra HŪṂ one hundred times, one will know the other's mind. For knowing one's previous abodes: this is accomplished by reciting the vajra HŪṂ. For one who wishes to see in dreams: reciting the vajra HŪṂ twenty-one times and fumigating with kapīṭa incense, one then falls asleep; thereupon the Blessed One reveals knowledge of the past, future, and present. For one who wishes to make speech fully effective: reciting the vajra speech one hundred thousand times, whatever is spoken by one's own tongue with the vajra speech will become true, and whatever has been learned will be imparted. For displaying miracles: reciting the vajra speech syllable eight hundred times, one's own body becomes a miraculous display appearing in the form of reflections of all buddhas, bodhisattvas, Vajrapāṇi, gods, nāgas, yakṣas, and all manner of sentient beings. For one who wishes to transform their own form at will: reciting the vajra speech syllable one hundred thousand times, one will be able to assume whatever form one wishes, including the form of a buddha. For one who wishes to become invisible: one takes the vajra with the vajra fist, and while holding the vajra, one recites until one has become invisible — as long as saṃsāra lasts. By releasing, one is freed.

Sitting on a small throne, one takes the vajra in hand and recites until dawn. To cause a spirit to seize someone: by the syllable vajra hūṃ. To direct a spirit to perform some act: by the syllable hi. To set a spirit in motion: by the syllable hag. To destroy a spirit: by the syllable phaṭ. To strip someone naked: one binds one's own shoulder with a cord of human hair, and the person will become naked and their clothing will change. To cause aversion: by breaking the commitment mudrā of the vajra fist. To drive someone away: one hurls the vajra fist. To accomplish a needed purpose: one takes a large fresh piece of meat marked with a sign, anoints it with one's own blood, and burns it. To draw another's woman: one holds the vajra while reciting the mantra heart syllable and drawing her, or alternatively one accomplishes all things by the syllable hūṃ, the all-pervading. To enjoy all women: one binds one's own sign with the vajra binding, holds it, and recites for seven days through the three periods of the day; then reciting further until midnight, one will perpetually be aroused, never satisfied, will be full of vital essence, and one's sign will become powerfully forceful. To enjoy a girl: one holds the vajra, performs the act by the syllable hūṃ, the all-pervading, and then proceeds. To bring all women under one's power: one binds the vajra hūṃ mudrā and recites one hundred thousand times, and all women will be pleased. In the presence of Vajrapāṇi, one binds the commitment mudrā of Avalokiteśvara and recites one hundred thousand times, and all women will be pleased. To bring all sentient beings under one's power: one holds the vajra with the binding of the vajra fist and recites until the earth trembles. To bring appearances under one's power: one takes the vajra, places it on one's own crown, and recites until the vajra is firm on the crown; then, sitting before the appearance, one thereby also confers upon it the empowerment of one's own power. To draw wealth: by performing a fire offering with the powder of the chief of precious substances. To bring down rain: by the vajra hūṃ syllable, reciting one thousand times. To stop rain: by the vajra hūṃ syllable. To stop hail: by the mudrā of sharpening the vajra. To ward off spirits: by that very same mudrā. To dispel clouds: by reciting the vajra hūṃ syllable one hundred times. To clear away: one recites the vajra hūṃ syllable one hundred times. One places the vajra on top of the maṇḍala and makes offerings of flowers and incense, presses down with the mudrā of the vajra-holding woman, and recites until the vajra rises; then, holding it and circumambulating the ground, one displays illusory feats at will, and by circumambulating with the vajra one sees fruit and flowers on trees out of season. To cause a garland of flowers to appear in the sky: one holds the breath, binds the vajra fist, and scatters it, and it appears. To cause clothing to burst open: by the vajra mudrā. To cause dancing: by the vajra-holding woman. To cause speech: by the syllable hūṃ. To cause running: by the mudrā of the beckoning vajra. To cause the eyebrows to move: one holds the vajra while grasping the vajra mudrā and moves it at one's own brow-line. To cause the earth to quake: by stamping the left foot. To shake a house: by the mudrā of the descending vajra. To draw a building toward one: by binding the mudrā of the wrathful vajra's hollow form. To bring down rain without clouds: by reciting the vajra hūṃ syllable. To gather clouds instantly and make them appear: by circumambulating with the vajra. To bring down the moon and sun and make them appear: by striking the vajra. To make many suns and moons appear: by casting the vajra upward into the sky in the manner of a vajra throw. To display the four armies: by circumambulating with the vajra.

To make night into day: by circumambulating with the blazing vajra. To make day into night: by closing the eyes and circumambulating with the vajra. To make all appear from every direction: by displaying the vajra and reciting one hundred times while circumambulating with the vajra. To make bodhisattvas appear from every direction: by reciting one thousand times while circumambulating with the vajra. To make all things appear before Vajrapāṇi, the All-Encompassing: by reciting one hundred thousand times while circumambulating. To make buddhas appear from every direction: by reciting ten million times upon the vajra.

This concludes the great tantra called Vajra Hūṃ, the King of Great Tantras: Victorious over the Three Worlds.

Then the Blessed One, the Tathāgata, the arhat, the perfectly and fully awakened Buddha, spoke these words to Vajrapāṇi: "I too, for the sake of all sentient beings, bestow this essence of all the Tathāgatas upon whoever shall apply themselves diligently to this King of Great Tantras: Victorious over the Three Worlds."

Then Vajrapāṇi prostrated at the feet of the Blessed One, sat cross-legged, and recited:
Hūṃ vajra vid svāhā.

Then, by the very utterance of these words, this world-realm of the great trichiliocosm trembled and shook entirely. All sentient beings too were thoroughly shaken, and, bowing in homage, came under its sway.

Then the great bodhisattva, the bodhisattva Vajrapāṇi, prostrated with his head at the feet of the Blessed One and set forth the vajra-knowledge maṇḍala. Having examined all the maṇḍalas in the manner of the great maṇḍala, the Blessed One was seated at its center amidst four measures of grain, and Vajrapāṇi was mentally placed cross-legged before him. Then one is to admit the student in just that way. Once the student has entered, one is to declare: "From this day forward, you are a Tathāgata yourself." Then, having been instructed to say this, and when that one has spoken the words "Speak," one then says: "From this day forward you have become a Tathāgata." When that one asks, "Why?" one declares: "Because you have beheld the maṇḍala." One is to regard whoever has entered this maṇḍala as a Tathāgata himself, and declare that such a person will attain the awakening of a buddha either in this very life or in the next. Then one is to teach the mudrā. This is the Tathāgata maṇḍala.

Then Vajrapāṇi prostrated at the feet of the Blessed One and set forth the vajra maṇḍala. Having drawn the maṇḍala in just that way and admitted the student, one is to teach the practice of the vajra. Holding the vajra with both hands, one meditates on one's own mudrā. What is called the vajra — this is the meditation on the essence. If one recites the vajra syllables, that person will also attain the Tathāgata in this very life or in another. This is the vajra maṇḍala. Having made everything conform to the manner of the great commitment, one is to say these words:

With unwavering faith in Vajrapāṇi,
just so is the lineage of the Tathāgata.
For the delight of all sentient beings
you are to examine it in that way.

"From this day forward, if you do not act accordingly, you will fall into the hell of sentient beings." This is the commitment maṇḍala.

Again, having drawn the maṇḍala in just that way and admitted the student, one is to teach the four activities as well. Touching the whole body to the ground, one pays homage to all the Tathāgatas. Then, rising, one makes obeisance with the five limbs and confesses all misdeeds. Then, rising and joining the palms together, with both knees touching the ground, one rejoices in all merit. Then, rising, with the knee of the right leg and its shin pressed to the ground, and having bound the mudrā of the vajra-holder, one pays homage and recites the vajra syllables of all the Tathāgatas: Vajra vid.

By this, one comes to pay homage, show reverence, and make offering to all the Tathāgatas and to Vajrapāṇi. The intention of all the Tathāgatas is perfectly fulfilled. This is the ritual of offering.

Then follows the accomplishment of the vidyā of Vajrapāṇi. Having seen the maṇḍala, one paints the Blessed One, the Tathāgata, on cloth. To his right one paints Avalokiteśvara, and to his left a vajra fist-mudrā adorned with all ornaments.

Then, for one who holds the restoration ceremony: bathing three times each day, and wearing robes washed three times, one recites one hundred thousand times. At the conclusion of this, having bound the fist-mudrā of all the Tathāgatas and reciting while the night fades into dawn, all the Tathāgatas will come the next morning and show their faces. They will grant whatever supreme wish one desires, and will grant even buddhahood itself.

Then another ritual: one paints the Blessed One, the Tathāgata, on cloth, seated upon a lotus. Before his presence Vajrapāṇi, with palms joined, sits cross-legged and gazes upon the Blessed One. To the right side of the Blessed One, Vajrapāṇi holds a vajra in one hand, and in the left a lotus. To the left side of the Blessed One is a vajra fist-mudrā, and with both fists one holds a vajra. Then, beginning from the first day of the month, one recites in this same way for one month. When the month is complete and one recites while the night fades into dawn, in just that way the Tathāgata will come and grant whatever supreme wish one desires.

Yet another ritual: one paints the Blessed One, the Tathāgata, on cloth. Before his presence, one recites in this same way for six months, and at the conclusion performs fasting for three days and three nights. Vajrapāṇi will come and will grant the very state of being a holder of Vajrapāṇi himself. Reciting one hundred thousand times before a stūpa of the Tathāgata, at the end one will see the Tathāgata. Gazing into the sky and reciting one hundred thousand times, one will see all the Tathāgatas in the sky, and they will grant every excellence each day. Reciting one hundred thousand times in the presence of a buddha, one attains the supreme. Reciting one hundred thousand times in the presence of Vajrapāṇi, Vajrapāṇi will grant all desires. Reciting one hundred thousand times in the presence of the noble Avalokiteśvara, Avalokiteśvara will take one by the hand, lead one to the world-realm of Sukhāvatī, and then lead one back and return.

From the Great King of Mantras (Sanskrit: vidyārāja), "Victorious Over the Three Worlds," the Tathāgata mantras are complete.

Then the bodhisattva, the great being Vajrapāṇi, addressed the Blessed One again with these words: "Blessed One, in this Great King of Mantras, 'Victorious Over the Three Worlds,' by the mere utterance of its essence, all the Tathāgatas — immovable as vajra mountains — are moved and drawn hither: what need is there to speak of other sentient beings? By the mere thought of it, one is entirely liberated from all fears. By the mere utterance of it, all without exception of the unrelenting misdeeds are completely purified, all beings are protected, Vajrapāṇi grants all desires, bestows the supreme, accomplishes all aims, fulfills every need, and constantly follows in attendance. Please grant that by the mere study of it all accomplishments will be directly realized."

Then the Blessed One declared to the bodhisattva, the great being Vajrapāṇi: "Vajrapāṇi, you have spoken well. I too have accepted it." Then Vajrapāṇi, with pride of the vajra, pressed the vajra, and holding it to his own heart in the manner of praise, spoke his own essence mantra:

Ṭakki Hūṃ Ja.

Then, merely by the utterance of these words, all the world-systems everywhere shook violently. All the Tathāgatas quaked and shook greatly, leapt up and leapt up utterly. All the bodhisattvas were sent into great motion. All the śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas were rendered unable to bear it. The bodhisattva became the very essence of the earth. All the Tathāgatas rendered him firm. The attainment of Vajrasattva of all the Tathāgatas was made utterly solid. The māras and all the parties hostile to them were utterly destroyed. All harmful spirits were subdued, slain, and crushed, and trembling with fear, they went for refuge to the Blessed One, crying: "Blessed One, protect us! Sugata, protect us!" The Blessed One then declared to them: "Go for refuge now to this very Vajrapāṇi. He it is who will protect you." Then Vajrapāṇi, his heart-center blazing all around with flames, adorned with all ornaments — garlands and the rest — belonging to all the Tathāgatas, his face both smiling and wrathful, his two hands drawing a flower-bow, and his two hands forming the vajra with the syllable Hūṃ — the Blessed One brought forth the wrathful form Vajra-Desire, and having purified all sentient beings in all world-systems by means of the wrathful form of desire, he sat in the presence of Vajrapāṇi and received instruction. The Blessed One declared: "Even as I am, so too shall you act for the benefit of all sentient beings."

Then the Blessed One spoke thus to Vajrapāṇi: "Vajrapāṇi, therefore now speak the essence mantra by which all these sentient beings who have been stunned, subdued, and shattered will be revived, and by which others too will attain the vajra body."

Oṃ Ṭuṃ. If one armors oneself with this mudrā of the vajra armor, one will attain a vajra body and a vajra life, and will be protected; for as long as one lives, none will be able to harm one.

Then the bodhisattva Vajrapāṇi proclaimed this great Vajra-Wheel maṇḍala. And then he explained it further:

The great Vajra-Wheel maṇḍala
is four-sided with four gates,
furnished with four archways,
and endowed with sixteen parts.
Beautified by a vajra-fence,
at the corners of the walls and gates,
and likewise at the intervals of the archways,
let vajras and jewels be placed.
Adorned with banners, garlands, and bells,
first let this outer maṇḍala
be written while reciting the essence mantra.
Within it is also the inner wheel,
provided with vajra threads of equal measure,
having eight vajra pillars,
and at the position of seven vajra pillars,
in the manner of eight maṇḍalas,
a maṇḍala of light is arranged.
At its center, the great bodhisattva
Vajrapāṇi is to be placed:
crowned with all the precious buddhas as his diadem,
his face smiling ever so slightly,
bearing the pride of the vajra,
holding a flower-bow and arrow.
Beyond that, the other form has four arms —
the very heart of Vajrasattva —
his face wearing a frowning yet smiling expression:
with two hands he draws the flower-bow,
and with two hands he makes the Vajra Hūṃ.
To the right of Vajrapāṇi,
let Vajra-Joy be depicted,
holding a vajra firm at his heart
in a posture of embrace.
Behind Vajrapāṇi,
bearing the victory banner of the crocodile,
stands the bodhisattva Vajraśrī,
who rains down a shower of jewels.
To the left of Vajrapāṇi,
let the red Vajra-Pride be drawn,
holding the vajra-pride with both hands.

she makes offerings with grace and bows in reverence. In the corner niches of the maṇḍala, depict the offering goddesses. Vajra-Spring, Vajra-Cloud, Vajra-Autumn, Vajra-Snow — in the outer corner niches, depict the sensuous forms and the other desirable qualities. At all the gate-pillars, depict Vajra-Yearning and the rest. Then, in that great maṇḍala, the vajra master himself should bind the heart mudrā (mudrā), enter, and speak these words:

Pervading the minds of all sentient beings,
grant bliss to every sentient being.
I, who am the protector of all sentient beings,
today am accomplished as Vajrasattva.
Ṭakki Hūṃ Jaḥ.

Then, when those words have been spoken, descent will occur. By that very descent, the form of Vajrapāṇi will be seen. Since seeing the Blessed One has been shown, all attainments are also bestowed: some will hold the vajra, and some will come to full awakening. Then the disciples are admitted. The heart mudrā is bound, and each disciple, taking a flower-garland, is bound at the limbs with red cloth and both eyes are covered with red cloth. Having admitted them, speak these words:

You must not reveal these secrets to any unlearned person who has not seen the maṇḍala. You would be slain by the possession of elementals, succubi, and the like, and even after death you would fall into hell. With this charge given, reveal their faces. Then speak thus:

This one who stands at the center is the Blessed One Vajrapāṇi. He is the primordial bodhisattva, lord of desire, king of desire, Samantabhadra, Vajrasattva. This one who stands before him is the mind of this Blessed One — the wrathful deity of desire. This one who stands to the right is the bliss of touch for all sentient beings — the bodhisattva known as Vajra-Joy. This one who stands behind is the great victory banner of lordship — he who rouses loving-kindness in all bodhisattvas and bestows wealth. This one who stands to the left is the great māra of lordship — he who causes all sentient beings to become attached. These four in the corners are the offering deities of the season: flowers, incense, lamps, and fragrance. Those stationed in the outer corner niches are the desirable qualities of sensuous form and so forth. Those gate-guardians are the eye, attachment, and affection — Yearning, Binding, Tether, Fetter, and Hurling — and they will never become adversaries. You too must not disparage the Blessed One, the lord of all desires. For within the teaching of the Buddhas, freedom from attachment is taught in order to purify all sentient beings. Why so? Because non-Buddhists (tīrthikas) are liberated by that very Dharma of freedom from attachment. The Tathāgatas, however, are themselves attached — because they are attached to all sentient beings. Liberation, therefore, is of this kind: bodhisattvas gather sentient beings even through their own spouses, and desire is revealed as awakening.

Then bestow the heart-essence:

Ṭakki Hūṃ Jaḥ.

If this is accomplished, the Blessed One, the supreme bodhisattva, will be attained. When the Blessed One is attained, the wrathful deity of desire will be attained. Why? Because this heart-essence is itself of great lordship. Ṭakki means desire. Hūṃ means the wrathful one. Jaḥ, which draws those two together — this you must accomplish. Then learn the great seal (mahāmudrā). Then, having trained in the samaya mudrā, the vajra mind of the Blessed One, bestow the vajra upon both hands. The heart-essence of that bestowal is this:

Oṃ vajradhara vajrapratāṃ dhāraya sindhya ṭakki hūṃ jaḥ.

Then the garland is offered to Vajrapāṇi, who places it upon the head of the recipient. After that, the empowerment of the vajra name is given by means of this heart-essence:

Oṃ vajra abhiṣiñca sarva kāmeśvara iti idaṃ vajra nāma nāmādhiṣṭhite dṛḍho sya bhava oṃ ṭakki hūṃ jaḥ.

Then, remaining in place, one seals the great seal and recites the vajra syllables eight thousand times, whereupon the earth shakes. All accomplishments are achieved by a single recitation. Then, sealing the samaya mudrā and reciting one hundred times, the mind of that one comes under one's power. From that point onward one knows the hearts of all sentient beings, and one exercises power over the hearts of all sentient beings. One becomes dear in the hearts of all. All become firmly attached. From every heart, words will be heard. Words of the past, the future, and the present become true. From one's own heart one sees miraculous manifestations of four kinds, and one displays them to others as well. From that one's heart a vajra will arise. By this power, all sentient beings become a vajra body — one that they cannot consume, cannot withstand, cannot cut, and cannot split. So spoke the Blessed One, Vajrapāṇi.

Then the secret offering is to be made, and one praises with a song of praise to the vajra mind. From that point onward all accomplishments become complete, all wishes and all secret offerings are swiftly accomplished.

Then, having made offerings equally with all offerings, and having satisfied with every kind of food, flavor, and enjoyment as much as there is, one departs. This is the elaborate rite of the great vajra wheel.

Then, having drawn the mudrās of the wrathful deity of desire (vajrakāma) and the others just as they are within the vajra maṇḍala, and having entered, the deity goddesses of offering perform the dance offering so that the cupped hands remain full. One seals the great seal of the vajra being (Vajrasattva) and, in the presence of the Blessed One, recites one hundred thousand times with vajra recitation of vajra song. Then the vajra becomes heavy. Then again it becomes light. Then, if one recites thus through a single night until dawn, by morning one either holds the vajra — or one becomes the wrathful vajra, with the form of a deity who is lord of all world-realms of the great trichiliocosm, with the color, ornaments, and three eyes, and four arms of the wrathful one of desire, resembling the splendor of vajra desire — and one becomes the retinue of Vajradhara. This is the elaborate rite of the vajra maṇḍala.

Then comes the samaya maṇḍala. With a cord one draws the lines of the great maṇḍala. Then one draws the goddesses, and having placed the Blessed One just as he is in the center, one speaks these words:

Ho vajrasattva surata tvaṃ.

Before him one draws a bow with the bow of vajra drawn, and its mudrā is this: Vajra aśtro hūṃ. To the right one places the vajra of joyful embrace, and its mudrā is this: Vajra kili hūṃ. To the rear one draws the vajra of loving kindness bearing the makara victory banner, and its mudrā is this: Vajra agriho. To the left one draws the vajra of allure, and the vajra is joined to the left side. Its mudrā is this: Vajra garbhe hani.

Then, at the corners and gateways as well, one draws the goddesses corresponding to one's own deity. Then the vajra master, perfectly attired, holding a vajra in hand, adorned also with a woman, clasps the vajra fist with both hands, embraces with both shoulders, enters, and speaks these words:

Samaya tvaṃ ahaṃ. Surata ho ṭakki hūṃ jaḥ.

Then by the mere utterance of these words, the Blessed One, as Teacher, grants the accomplishment of all mudrās and the accomplishment of all samayas.

Then the student likewise enters with that very vajra of the heart-essence and speaks these words: "Do not transgress this samaya. If you abandon not what is unpleasant, you will swiftly die and be reborn amid great wailing and lamentation." Then the samaya is recited:

The great mudrā of all that I am
arises in perfection beyond thought and wish.
Therefore, do not abandon
desire and intention that are free of fault.

This is the mantra for guarding the samaya: Oṃ ṭakki hūṃ phaṭ. With this, the vajra ablution is performed. For the sake of all other things as well, one presses and affirms them, and pours the vajra ablution upon them also by holding the vajra mudrā. As for whoever transgresses this: "In half a month, the heart will be rent" — thus was it declared by the Blessed One, Vajrapāṇi, with the great samaya.

Then one trains in the binding of the mudrā. Then likewise it proceeds in that way. This is the secret maṇḍala called the Vajra of Supreme Delight.

Then, having drawn the action maṇḍala in the same way, one places one's own image or emblem within it. Having entered therein, one trains in the four kinds of action.

In the first of these, all wishes are granted. One binds the great mudrā of Vajrapāṇi and recites — until one sees oneself in the form of Vajrapāṇi (Skt. Vajradhara), the four-armed one, adorned with the garland of all the Tathāgatas and such ornaments — for as long as saṃsāra lasts. After that, all wishes become possible.

Then one trains in wrathful action. Binding the mudrā called Vajra Hūṃ and reciting it, one recites together with all the Tathāgatas; and by threatening with the syllable Hūṃ, even a Tathāgata will be cast down. After that, one becomes able to cast down all beings wherever they dwell.

Then arises the action of Vajrapāṇi. If one wishes to tame all sentient beings by means of both desire and wrath, one makes offerings of flowers and so forth with the mudrā of the vajra-holder woman, binds the vajra-mind mudrā at one's own heart, and recites — in the presence of Vajrapāṇi, until one has attained the vajra-hold — for as long as saṃsāra lasts.

One who wishes to perform the deeds of all the Tathāgatas should, in the presence of Vajrapāṇi, arouse the awakening mind toward awakening, practice generosity, guard ethical discipline, cultivate patience, undertake diligence, enter into meditative concentration, and perfect wisdom — doing whatever one can according to one's ability — and recite continuously until one sees the form of Vajrapāṇi or the form of a Tathāgata. Then one will attain the ten grounds and become a bodhisattva performing the deeds of the Tathāgatas. This is the ritual of the action maṇḍala.

Then accomplishment arises. The inexhaustible heart-essence is not to be given to others who have not entered the maṇḍala, who do not know the samaya, and who are not disciples.

As for accomplishment through seeing the maṇḍala: reciting one hundred thousand times in the presence of Vajrapāṇi is equivalent to having previously performed approach practice.

Then one draws on cloth. In the center of the cloth, draw the Blessed One, the Tathāgata. To his right, draw in the same way Vajrapāṇi, four-armed, as awareness arising from the heart. To his left, draw Avalokiteśvara, four-armed: with two hands holding the lotus samādhi, and with two hands drawing a bow of flowers. Draw him in red. Before him, in the same way, draw the wrathful Vajra-Desire. Above, draw Ākāśagarbha. At the corners, display the offering deities with their own offerings, and place them at the head as well. Then, having recited four thousand times in each of the four seasons before the image painted on cloth — in the manner of monthly recitation — at the completion of the month a Tathāgata will also appear and grant boons. One should paint the Blessed One on cloth in the manner of the maṇḍala, and likewise include the great bodhisattvas and the goddesses.

Before that image, one recites uncountable times throughout a single month, consuming only what one desires, and diligently practices. Then, when the month has elapsed, if one binds the great mudrā of Vajrapāṇi and recites at midnight, by the following dawn one will attain the state of Vajrapāṇi.

Paint the Blessed One Vajrapāṇi on cloth in the same manner as before. Before that image, one recites continuously for half a month without sleeping or resting. Then, when the half-month has elapsed, if one recites again at midnight, by the following dawn one will become equal to Vajrapāṇi, and one will also attain great enjoyments of desire, all happiness and mental ease, and will be able to go everywhere.

Paint the Blessed One in his wrathful form on cloth in the same manner. Before that image, having bound the mudrā of Vajra-Force and recited one hundred thousand times, one will attain accomplishment: whatever one is instructed, all activities will be performed accordingly. One will be carried to all the buddha-fields and brought back again. One will be brought all the pleasures of desire of every buddha-field and those will be given to oneself. If one is capable of enjoying them, all the goddesses need not be mentioned — even Śakra (Indra) himself will grant them. From the holder of Vajrapāṇi up to the buddhas, they will grant them as directed.

If one recites one hundred thousand times before the image of the buddhas, all Tathāgatas will be summoned and brought under one's power. If one binds the mudrā of Vajra-Mind before the image of Vajrapāṇi and recites one hundred thousand times, then Vajrapāṇi himself will lead the goddess of glory and come, and cast her before the practitioner. Then the practitioner should say: "Blessed One, I myself have achieved you — what does this one desire?" Thereupon the Blessed One, being pleased, will grant whatever is supremely wished for. All accomplishments will also be achieved.

If one recites one hundred thousand times before the image of Avalokiteśvara, Avalokiteśvara will grant the supreme. If one recites one hundred thousand times before the image of Ākāśagarbha, empowerment will be granted, and all wishes will be perfectly fulfilled. If one places a vajra and recites one hundred thousand times before that image, then all activities will be accomplished just as one wishes. If one binds the great mudrā and recites one hundred thousand times, the holder of Vajrapāṇi will grant the supreme. If one binds the samaya mudrā and recites one hundred thousand times, one will directly behold the vajra-holder, and all the activities of the mudrā will also be granted as accomplishments. If one holds a vajra in hand and recites one hundred thousand times, by merely grasping the vajra with the vajra-fist one will become invisible. If one holds a wheel and recites one hundred thousand times, one will become a holder of the wheel's vidyā. If one holds a hook and recites one hundred thousand times, that hook will be able to summon all. If one holds a lasso and recites one hundred thousand times, that lasso will be able to draw all. If one clasps both hands together bound in the vajra-bond and recites one hundred thousand times, one will be able to bind all sentient beings. If one binds the mudrā of descending vajra and recites one hundred thousand times, one will be able to bring down all those stationary and all those moving. If one binds the mudrā called Vajra-Hūṃ and recites one hundred thousand times, by a single utterance of "hūṃ" one will be able to strike down all those stationary and all those moving in a single instant.

If one binds the mudrā of victorious conquest over the three worlds — with the left foot pressing down upon the Great God (Śiva) — and recites one hundred thousand times, within a single month he will come and be brought under one's power. If one presses the left foot upon the great Tumbu and recites one hundred thousand times, one will obtain the wheel, and upon holding that wheel all things will be accomplished. If one presses upon Brahmā and recites one hundred thousand times, one will abide in the Brahmā-conduct. If one presses the left foot upon Śakra and recites one hundred thousand times, vajras will be bestowed: by sending vajras wheeling, one cuts, strikes down, and simultaneously brings down thunderbolts and hail.

If one presses upon the young woman of disease and recites one hundred thousand times, one will become master over all young women, and all tasks will be accomplished. If one gazes at the sun and recites one hundred thousand times, the nectar earring will be bestowed; by that, one will live for a thousand divine years. If one gazes at the moon with a burning gaze, looking at it with delight, and recites one hundred thousand times, one will see the moon as two; and from that point on one will come to have a radiance and complexion equal to the moon, beautiful in the eyes of all sentient beings and dear to the hearts of all sentient beings.

If one gazes upon the form of the great planet and recites one hundred thousand times, one will thereupon obtain a sword; and by fighting with that sword one will be victorious over enemies and obtain all wealth. If one goes to the dwelling place of the sky-goer and recites one hundred thousand times, the sky-goer will accomplish all tasks. If one presses upon one intoxicated with wine and recites one hundred thousand times, all vessels of ale will become inexhaustible for as long as one lives.

If one gazes upon the Madhukara and recites one hundred thousand times, one will obtain an empowerment. If one gazes upon the Victor and recites one hundred thousand times, one will be victorious in all battles and disputes. If one presses upon one who has obtained victory and recites one hundred thousand times, one will be victorious over all. If one presses the left foot upon the treasure-guardian and recites one hundred thousand times, food and drink will become inexhaustible, and no being whatsoever will be able to exhaust them.

If one makes the wind manifest and recites one hundred thousand times, the wind will act in accordance with that and carry out all one's wishes. If one gazes at fire and recites one hundred thousand times, one will not be burned whether sitting, lying, or moving in the midst of fire; rather, one will be a master of fire, and each day will bestow a tola of gold. If one presses upon the Weary Resting One and recites one hundred thousand times, one will become master over all yakṣas and will likewise bestow all wealth.

If one makes the sow manifest and recites one hundred thousand times, one will draw up the treasure seated within the earth. If one presses upon Yama, the Lord of Death, and recites one hundred thousand times, one will become free from death. If one gazes upon the form of the one who draws the sky and recites one hundred thousand times, all those drawn therein will become one's servants.

If one presses upon Varuṇa the nāga and recites one hundred thousand times, all nāgas will become servants who accomplish every task. If one goes to the dwelling place of humans and recites one hundred thousand times, all humans will become one's servants and all spirits will accomplish tasks. If one sits upon the earth and recites one hundred thousand times, one will become lord of the earth. If one gazes into the sky and recites one hundred thousand times, one will overturn and bring under power all sentient beings that travel in the sky, and one will travel through the sky. If one makes the subterranean realm manifest and recites one hundred thousand times, one will gain power over all that abide beneath the earth.

If one holds a precious jewel and recites one hundred thousand times, it will multiply a hundredfold. The same applies to golden coins and the like. If one holds a man and recites one hundred thousand times, it will multiply a hundredfold. If one holds a woman and recites one hundred thousand times, it will multiply a hundredfold. If one mounts a horse and recites one hundred thousand times, it will multiply a hundredfold. If one mounts an elephant and recites one hundred thousand times, it will multiply a hundredfold. If one grasps the two horns of cattle and the like and recites one hundred thousand times, it will multiply a hundredfold. If one is adorned with clothing and ornaments and recites one hundred thousand times, it will multiply a hundredfold.

If one gazes upon Śrī and recites one hundred thousand times, one will bring her under one's power. If one gazes upon the Lake-Voiced One and recites one hundred thousand times, one's speech will become perfectly pure; and from that point on, whatever one requests, all of it will be given. One will come to know all branches of traditional learning and all forms of consciousness.

If one holds the Accomplishment Woman and recites one hundred thousand times, one will know the past, the present, and

the future, and will spontaneously bestow wealth without being asked.

If one gazes upon Sālabhañjikā and recites one hundred thousand times, each and every day one will receive eight gold coins. If one gazes upon the Silk Garland and recites one hundred thousand times, it will become inexhaustible. Whatever vessel one gazes upon and recites one hundred thousand times, that vessel will become inexhaustible. If one gazes upon the garments of a yakṣa and recites one hundred thousand times, the yakṣa will bestow whatever one desires. If one gazes upon a rākṣasa and recites one hundred thousand times, that being will become one's servant. If one gazes upon an elemental spirit and recites one hundred thousand times, one will become master over all elemental spirits. If one gazes upon a painted image of a flesh-eating being and recites one hundred thousand times, that being will become one's servant. In the same way, the female yakṣa and others may also be accomplished.

If one ascends a mountain and recites one hundred thousand times, all mountains will come under one's power. If one sits among the gods of the livestock and recites one hundred thousand times, one will gain power over all livestock. If one recites one hundred thousand times at the foot of a great tree, the deities of that tree will bestow excellent things. If one sits in a field and recites one hundred thousand times, food will multiply a hundredfold. If one recites one hundred thousand times in a town that includes merchants, all wealth will be obtained without effort. If one recites one hundred thousand times in a place of blessing, one will gain power over everything. If one recites one hundred thousand times in a village, within a month one will possess that village. If one recites one hundred thousand times in a temple, whatever one wishes for by dwelling in that temple, all wealth will be given. If one recites one hundred thousand times at a divine shrine, the deity will bestow all wealth. If one recites one hundred thousand times in an empty house, all flesh-eating beings will become one's servants and will provide everything one needs. If one recites one hundred thousand times in a place of light, that light will come under one's power. If one recites one hundred thousand times in a pleasure garden, all the flowers and fruits of that garden will perpetually appear. If one recites one hundred thousand times at a lake, one will gain power over the deity of that lake. If one recites one hundred thousand times at a place of buried treasure, the treasure will arise spontaneously. If one recites one hundred thousand times while laughing, one will be forever joyful, and all tasks will be accomplished by laughter alone. If one recites one hundred thousand times in a state of anger, one will be able to utterly destroy everything. If one recites one hundred thousand times in complete silence, all sentient beings will come under one's power. If one recites one hundred thousand times while observing the discipline of silence, one will gain mastery over all speech.

If one marks a woman's intimate place as a sign and recites one hundred thousand times, one will become lord of all desires and will achieve the ability to travel through the sky. If one places a finger on the intimate place and recites one hundred thousand times, by doing so one will attract, draw, send away, fell, and rouse all beings in every place and in every movement. If one gazes upon the intimate place and recites one hundred thousand times, all women will be delighted. If one inserts a pill into the intimate place and recites one hundred thousand times, one will become invisible. If one anoints the intimate place with saffron and recites one hundred thousand times, whoever is anointed with it will take on whatever form is desired. If one inserts a feather into the intimate place and recites one hundred thousand times, the ability to travel through the sky will be achieved. If one inserts red arsenic into the intimate place and recites one hundred thousand times, one will become a vidyādhara, a holder of awareness.

If one takes up a sword and recites one hundred thousand times, one will become a vidyādhara of the sword. If one takes up a wheel and recites one hundred thousand times, one will become a vidyādhara of the wheel. If one takes up a kyabatang and recites one hundred thousand times, that kyabatang will be able to summon all. If one polishes a vajra and recites one hundred thousand thousand times, one will become a vajra vidyādhara. If one holds Vajrapāṇi in one's hands and recites one hundred thousand times, one will become the vidyādhara who holds the vajra of all Tathāgatas.

If one takes a Tathāgata in one's hands and recites one hundred thousand times, one will become a vidyādhara of the holy Dharma who makes offering to all Tathāgatas. If one recollects the buddhas with mindfulness and recites one hundred thousand times, one will attain the realization of seeing all buddhas. If one bows to Vajrapāṇi and recites one hundred thousand times, even Vajrapāṇi himself venerates that person — what need is there to speak of others? If one takes hold of the feet of Avalokiteśvara, the Powerful One, and recites one hundred thousand times, one will attain meditative absorption. If one takes up a vajra-essence and recites one hundred thousand times, one will come to grant all wealth. If one forms the vajra-fist mudrā and recites one hundred thousand times before the vajra-fist, one will become capable of accomplishing all actions by the mere forming of that fist. If one gazes upon Mañjuśrī the Youthful and recites one hundred thousand times, letters will appear in the sky, and one will attain the ability to speak of all things and an unobstructed eloquence. If one circumambulates a bodhisattva who, the very moment the awakening mind arose, turned the wheel of Dharma, and recites one hundred thousand times, one will enter into all maṇḍalas. If one gazes upon the Treasury of Space and recites one hundred thousand times, one will be shown everything that moves through space, all aims, and everything up to and including the buddhas and bodhisattvas. If one takes hold of the feet of Maitreya and recites one hundred thousand times, one will come to experience all the pleasures of desire in the higher realms. If one draws a maṇḍala and recites one hundred thousand times, one will become a master teacher who is lord of all maṇḍalas, and all things will be accomplished through mind alone. If one reflects upon the great maṇḍala, enters it, and recites one hundred thousand times, one will come to behold all Tathāgatas together with the vajra-holders. If one forms the great seal mudrā before Vajrapāṇi, sits within the great maṇḍala, and recites one hundred thousand times with the vajra recitation and one hundred thousand times with vajra speech, one will become capable of all attainments, all excellences, all spiritual friends, and all actions. One will experience the sovereign of desire, the powerful lord, the agent, the bestower, the inexhaustible and undiminishing — experiencing all desires, one will find and attain all happiness and mental joy, and one will become a gift that accomplishes benefit and happiness for all sentient beings. One will be seen by all one's own kinsmen, spiritual friends, and companions, and one will accomplish things for them as well. By mere thought one will display all illusions, display the forms of all Tathāgatas, display the miraculous powers of all Tathāgatas, and display the magical transformations of all Tathāgatas. In an instant one will appear in manifold forms, and one will become equal in lifespan, strength, form, youthful vigor, and extent of knowledge to the vajra-holder. Thus spoke the Blessed One, Vajrapāṇi.

If one sits in the great maṇḍala, forms the samaya seal mudrā, gazes upon the Blessed One with wrathful speech, and recites one hundred thousand times with purpose, one's own body will transform to resemble a wrathful vajra being. And by the syllable HŪṂ, grasping all Tathāgatas, becoming the great wrathful one who is lord of the three realms and has three eyes, one will see and make offering to all Tathāgatas without exception, fulfill all the needs of all sentient beings, and thus become inexhaustible and undiminishing. Thus spoke the Blessed One, Vajrapāṇi.

This has been the contemplation of Vajrapāṇi from the King of Great Contemplations, Triumphant Over the Three Worlds.

Then the noble bodhisattva mahāsattva Avalokiteśvara, the Powerful One, addressed the Blessed One with these words:

"Blessed One, I too shall bestow my own essence here in this King of Great Contemplations, Triumphant Over the Three Worlds. By the mere utterance of what I shall speak, the meditative absorption of all Tathāgatas will be directly accomplished, and all fears, thunderbolts, harm, and diseases will be destroyed."

Then the Blessed One praised the noble bodhisattva mahāsattva Avalokiteśvara, the Powerful One, with the words "Excellent!" — "Excellent, excellent! Avalokiteśvara, the Powerful One, speak!"

Then the noble bodhisattva mahāsattva Avalokiteśvara, the Powerful One, proclaimed his own essence:

ĀROLIK.

By the mere utterance of this, all the Tathāgatas assembled together, and the Blessed One Vairocana, the Tathāgata, came to the summit of Mount Meru. The Blessed One entered and was absorbed into the maṇḍala of the noble Avalokiteśvara, the Powerful One.

Then the noble bodhisattva mahāsattva Avalokiteśvara, the Powerful One, proclaimed the maṇḍala of his own essence, and spoke truly and well:

This is the supreme and sacred great maṇḍala.
Draw the outer maṇḍala as befits one who has conquered the three worlds.
In its center, on equal cords,
lay it out as a square.
The second side is shown as the gateway —
draw this supreme maṇḍala.
In the manner of eight maṇḍalas,
draw four lines in the center as well.
With red pigments,
draw a lotus of eight petals.
In the center of its heart,
place the Protector of the World.
On all the petals,
draw the image of a buddha.
The great supreme lotus mudrā —
seated thus, the master,
having entered the maṇḍala and taken his place,
should enter into yogic absorption.

Just as a lotus, though dwelling amid water
and tainted by many habitual tendencies of fault,
is not defiled by them — so too here,
beings who are defiled are thereby purified.

Having recited this once and meditated, one then draws both index fingers together inward, and releases the great lotus before the presence of Avalokiteśvara, the Powerful One. The summoning mudrā of the great lotus is this: OṂ MAHĀPADMA HŪṂ JAḤ HRĪḤ.

Then the Blessed One Avalokiteśvara, the Powerful One, himself comes forth and reveals his face to the master, and may also emit rays of light. Then the disciple is introduced by means of that very mudrā. Covered in white and dressed in white garments, with a white turban placed upon the head as a crown protrusion, the disciple is introduced. This verse is recited and its meaning is taught. Then a lotus is placed in the left hand, and a jewel of mantra is bestowed in the right hand, and one says:

All buddhas — the great lotus,
the supreme sacred great maṇḍala —
are to be contemplated as permanent.
From this day forward, you are Avalokiteśvara.

Then the great mudrā is bound, and training in meditative absorption and the rest is given.

The great maṇḍala of all Tathāgatas is called the lotus maṇḍala.

From this arises the lotus maṇḍala. The lotus maṇḍala is drawn in the same way. Seated at the central heart, gazing in the manner of a lotus petal opening, and reciting one hundred thousand times the meditative absorption of all Tathāgatas — one thereby attains noble Avalokiteśvara, the Powerful One in all his aspects. This is the lotus maṇḍala.

Then arises the pledge maṇḍala (samaya-maṇḍala). That maṇḍala is made in the same way. A thousand lotuses are arranged in its center. Then, one by one, a fresh lotus is offered to the Blessed One, until a thousand lotuses have been offered. The disciple is then introduced and the pledge is proclaimed:

Not a non-disciple, not a non-son, not a non-daughter —
to one who holds the pledge, the pledge is taught.
All desire and attachment
are pure in their very nature, like a lotus.

This is the karma maṇḍala. It is drawn in the same way as the pledge maṇḍala, and worship is offered with the four kinds of offering:

All the buddhas — through the mind,
Vajrapāṇi — through word,
the Protector of the World — through the body,
Ākāśagarbha — through action.

This is the karma maṇḍala.

Then the Blessed One Avalokiteśvara, the Powerful One, proclaimed this rite of accomplishment. On cloth, one draws the Blessed One Avalokiteśvara, the Powerful One, standing at the summit of Mount Meru, abiding in his own meditative absorption, with eight Tathāgatas arrayed all around him. Then, beginning from the first day of the waxing moon, one recites continually without break, in an unbroken stream of pure meditative absorption, until a form-body of a Tathāgata emerges from the face of Avalokiteśvara and becomes firmly established at the center of the practitioner's lotus, and then another form-body emerges and becomes firmly established at the place of empowerment of the practitioner. One recites thus until that has occurred.

Then, transformed into the form-body of Avalokiteśvara, in a single instant one goes and returns to the world-realm of Sukhāvatī. All sentient beings are seen, and they come to have boundless life.

Then accomplishment arises: if one recites one hundred thousand times, all obscurations are completely purified. If one recites two hundred thousand times, all sins without exception — including the sins of immediate consequence and the like — are cleansed. If one recites three hundred thousand times, all merit becomes manifest. Then all attainments (siddhis), all Tathāgatas and the like, all excellences, and all spiritual friends become manifest.

Then, having bound the great seal (mahāmudrā), after reciting one hundred thousand times, if at the conclusion one recites while the name-powder is brought forth, then the very next morning all Tathāgatas will arrive and enter into that person's heart, and one thereby attains the Tathāgata. Or if it enters through the mouth, one attains the Powerful One Avalokiteśvara.

Moreover, as for what this accomplishes even without full completion: recited once, it protects oneself. Recited twice, it protects others, villages, and towns. Recited three times, it accomplishes all activities.

To draw down: with sandalwood incense.
To pacify all harmful spirits: with the syllable Hrīḥ.
To bring all sentient beings under one's influence: recite "vajra-flower" and bestow it.
To subjugate all sentient beings: with yellow earth.
To bring women under one's influence: with praises of the sumanā flower.
To subjugate thieves: with a fire-offering of white mustard seed.
To subjugate wild animals and birds: by scattering white mustard seed.
To subjugate illusions: with peacock tail feathers.
To have auspicious dreams: recite at the three times of day.
To dispel bad dreams: with a fire-offering of sesame.
To bring all sentient beings under one's influence: with a fire-offering of the feathers of a domestic bird.
To bring a young unmarried girl under one's influence: with a fire-offering of a girl's hair.
To bring a daughter-in-law under one's influence: with a fire-offering of a woman's flower.
To bring all women under one's influence: with a fire-offering of a woman's sense organ.
To bring all desired things under one's influence: make offering to the Blessed One Avalokiteśvara, the Powerful One, and recite with joy for three nights — from then on all desired things will ever be obtained.

If one recites three times, three times a day, in the presence of the Powerful One Avalokiteśvara, then within three months the Blessed One will bestow the supreme fulfillment of whatever one desires. If one recites three times by day and three times by night in the presence of a buddha for the duration of three months, the Tathāgata will bestow the supreme.

This concludes the chapter on Avalokiteśvara, the Lord, from the Great King of Meditations: Victor Over the Three Worlds.

Then the noble bodhisattva, the great being Ākāśagarbha, spoke these words to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, I too shall offer my own heart-essence to the Great King of Meditations: Victor Over the Three Worlds. May the Blessed One please accept it. By this, the empowerment of all sentient beings and the complete fulfillment of all wishes and intentions shall be accomplished."

Then the Blessed One said to the noble bodhisattva Ākāśagarbha, "Great being, please speak." And so the noble bodhisattva, the great being Ākāśagarbha, recited his own heart-essence:

Tram bā bā ṭa ṭa ha ha.

When this was recited, a rain fell of every empowerment of space and all things — every precious ornament, clothing, gold, wealth, precious gems, and all such things of value. The Blessed One's body was arrayed and sat adorned with every adornment.

Then the bodhisattva Ākāśagarbha recited this great maṇḍala:

In the proper form of the vajra maṇḍala,
equal in vajra and jewel alike,
with eight sides perfectly even —
let it be endowed with every precious thing.

The ācārya then enters, places the vajra-jewel mudrā at the site of empowerment, takes his position, and recites the following:

The supreme accomplishment of space —
fulfiller of every hope and wish —
accomplish, accomplish, great mind!
May all the Blessed Ones grant accomplishment!

Then the Blessed One blessed the bodhisattva Ākāśagarbha.

Next, at the center of the maṇḍala, the image of Ākāśagarbha is placed, and surrounding it on all sides eight noble beings, beginning with Vajrapāṇi, are placed. Then the student is admitted with the vajra-fist gesture. Once admitted, these words are recited:

For the benefit of all sentient beings,
and for the complete fulfillment of all intentions —
what has been taught as "the aim" —
the awakening mind is the stable foundation.

Then one trains in the four kinds of generosity: the generosity of empowerment, the generosity of wealth, the generosity of food, and the generosity of Dharma. Of these, the generosity of empowerment is directed toward the Tathāgatas. The generosity of wealth is directed toward all sentient beings. The generosity of food is directed toward all animals. The generosity of Dharma is directed toward all sentient beings who dwell in darkness.

Then the heart-essence is given, and with water from a vase consecrated with all precious substances, empowerment is conferred, reciting:

Oṃ vajraratnābhiṣekābhiṣiñca sarvādhipati hūṃ bā bā ṭa ṭa ha ha.

Then the mudrā of the vajra-jewel fist is taught, and the mantra is:

Hūṃ bā ṭa ha.

Then the precious vajra mudrā is sealed, and the mantra is:

Oṃ bā bā ṭa mu bhaṃ.

Then four kinds of offering are to be performed — this is the source thereof. This is the maṇḍala of the conferral of all empowerments.

Then comes the precious vajra maṇḍala: the precious vajra is drawn at the center; holding a jewel in hand, one recites until the precious vajra arises. Then the Tathāgata descends and confers empowerment. Through that empowerment one becomes sovereign over all things. This is the precious jewel maṇḍala.

Next comes the samaya maṇḍala: the image of Ākāśagarbha is drawn, and in front of it are placed whatever is available — wealth, gold, pearls, precious jewels, and so forth. Then the student is introduced and all is shown to them, and this samaya is given:

If one were to make a thousand offerings each day to the buddhas and bodhisattvas —

all the wealth and goods you have gathered
shall perish in every life to come.
This is the heart of the oath.
Oṃ bābāṭaṭaya svāhā.

Then empowerment is conferred with water. This concludes the samaya maṇḍala.

Next, the karma maṇḍala is drawn. The Blessed One, the Tathāgata adorned with all ornaments, is depicted therein. The student is placed at the center and introduced. Then the training in the five kinds of karma is as follows. Through the giving of empowerment, one makes offering to all the Tathāgatas. Through the giving of wealth, one gives as generously as one is able to śramaṇas, brahmins, and the like. Through the giving of food, one gives to animals. Through the giving of fearlessness, one gives to all those who are led off in fetters. Through the giving of Dharma, one gives to sentient beings who dwell in darkness. This concludes the karma maṇḍala.

Next comes the practice of accomplishment. On cloth is painted the Blessed One, the Tathāgata adorned with all ornaments, being granted empowerment with the precious vajra empowerment. To his right, the noble Ākāśagarbha is conferring empowerment upon the Blessed One by means of an empowerment garland. To his left stands the Blessed One Vajrapāṇi, adorned with all ornaments.

Then, before the image painted on cloth, after completing a hundred thousand recitations, one begins at sunrise and recites until sunrise of the following day. At that point the Blessed One Ākāśagarbha emerges from the image painted on cloth and confers upon the practitioner empowerment with the precious vajra empowerment. Having received that empowerment, one becomes a Tathāgata.

Furthermore, even without the full accomplishment practice, for those who simply perform the recitation, the following results arise from a hundred thousand recitations: if one recites a hundred thousand times before Vajrapāṇi, a great treasure is found. If one recites a hundred thousand times before the sovereign Avalokiteśvara, the lord Avalokiteśvara bestows all excellent things in abundance. If one recites a hundred thousand times before Ākāśagarbha, one attains sovereignty over a kingdom. If one recites a hundred thousand times before the vajra fist, and seals the fist toward the sky, golden tubes emerge. If one gazes upon the youthful Mañjuśrī and recites a hundred thousand times, one obtains the wealth of Dharma and dhāraṇī, and through these wealth is also generated. If one presses with the fingers of the vajra wheel fist and recites a hundred thousand times, the wheel is set in motion. If one embraces the entire treasury of space and recites a hundred thousand times, all wealth and all provisions of food from the sky become inexhaustible. If one gazes upon Maitreya and recites a hundred thousand times, the Blessed One, the bodhisattva Maitreya, bestows the supreme gift of the Tuṣita heaven and returns again. From that point onward, one becomes the sovereign of all the wealth one desires.

This is the contemplation of Ākāśagarbha from the Great King of Contemplations, the Victor Over the Three Worlds.

Then the bodhisattva Mahāsattva Vajramuṣṭi stirred the entire assembly of all bodhisattvas gathered there, and addressed the Blessed One with these words: "I too shall offer my own heart-essence to this Great King of Contemplations, the Victor Over the Three Worlds. For ninety-one thousand aeons I took the vajra Mount Meru from the Tathāgata, and by means of it fulfilled every aspiration of sentient beings as numerous as the finest dust particles of all world-realms — from the arising of that Tathāgata down to its final completion."

Then the Blessed One said "Excellent!" to the great bodhisattva Vajramuṣṭi, and spoke thus: "Excellent, excellent! Vajramuṣṭi, let it be proclaimed by you."

Then Vajramuṣṭi caused all the world-realms of the great trichiliocosm to tremble mightily, and proclaimed this his own heart-essence:

Oṃ ruṭu ruṭu hūṃ maṭ.

Then, by the mere utterance of these words, all the Tathāgatas, together with Vajrapāṇi and the entire retinue, came to the very peak of Mount Meru where the Blessed One was. With both hands he formed the mudrā of his own vajra-fist, directed his attention toward the awakening of all the Tathāgatas, and the Blessed One, transformed into the great maṇḍala of Vajramuṣṭi, was arrayed there.

Then Vajramuṣṭi, having sealed all the Tathāgatas with the mudrā, proclaimed this great maṇḍala in verse:

Furthermore, it is excellently declared:
This is the supreme great maṇḍala,
the one resembling the vajra maṇḍala —
it is called Vajramuṣṭi, the vajra fist.
At its center, with the great mind,
one shall place the vajra fist.
The vajra fist, together with the vajra,
is to be pressed with both hands.
On all its faces
one shall place the forms of the buddhas.
In all the corners thereof
all the bodhisattvas shall be drawn.

Then, in this maṇḍala of Vajramuṣṭi, the vajra-master (ācārya) forms the samaya mudrā of the vajra fist together with the vajra, enters it, and recites:

Hūṃ dakṣiṇi smara ṇa phaṭ.

Then, through the Blessed One Vajramuṣṭi, the master will achieve accomplishment, and the mudrā will become stable for him, and no one whatsoever will be able to dissolve it.

Then, with this heart-essence, one releases the mudrā at one's own heart:

Oṃ vajramuṣṭi mu.

Next follows the initiation of the disciple. The samaya mudrā of Vajramuṣṭi together with the vajra is to be made, adorned with a garland. In the same way, cover both eyes with a red cloth. Then recite the following:

"Today you have been sealed by all the Tathāgatas with the Dharma mudrā, and also with the vajra mudrā of Vajrapāṇi. Do you desire the Dharma or the vajra?"

If the disciple says both, then the vajra-master takes the vajra and the raiment of the Tathāgata, places them on the head of the garland, and with the very same liberating heart-essence releases the mudrā at the heart. Next, what is placed in the hand: into the left hand, the vajra; into the right hand, the Tathāgata. If the disciple says "the Tathāgata," then one gives the Tathāgata itself. If the disciple says "the vajra," then one gives the vajra into the left hand, and instructs with these words: "Regard these two as your accomplishment, and make offerings to them always."

Then remove the covering from the face, display the maṇḍala, and say:

The vajras of body, speech, and mind
of all the Tathāgatas
are gathered in the fist —
when sealed, it is called the vajra fist.

Then give the heart-essence, teach the forming of the mudrā, and instruct: this is the maṇḍala called the samaya fist.

Then comes the arising of the mudrā maṇḍala:

In accordance with the great maṇḍala,
draw all the maṇḍalas completely.
At its center, as is fitting,
place the bodily form of a buddha.
Adorned with all ornaments,
Vajramuṣṭi holds all.
On all its faces
draw the four mudrās.
In the centers of the lunar discs,
likewise the vajra and the jewel;
likewise in the center of the vajra-lotus,
draw the vajra cross.
In all the corner directions
draw the four kinds of offering.
Then, having entered, all the mudrās —

the binding too is to be trained in. The accomplishment secrets and the secret of the summoning are to be trained in. Having drawn all of these completely, one should say: "Accomplish them." This is the mudrā maṇḍala.

Then comes the arising of the samaya maṇḍala. In the same way, having placed the signs, one proclaims this: "If the maṇḍala is not seen, and yet the mudrās are shown to one who knows them, then the vajra, the jewel, the Victor, and the manifold ones will slay you." This is the samaya maṇḍala.

Then comes the arising of the karma maṇḍala. Having drawn that maṇḍala in the same way, one trains in the karma of the mudrās in the presence of the Blessed One Vajramuṣṭi. The bound mudrā for that is as follows:

Having formed the vajra binding completely,
all the fingers are to be hooked inward.
Then bind the mudrā of the vajra descent
and the mudrā of the vajra fist.

Then the mudrā of the hands is bound. If one is not endowed with this in the proper way, then by accomplishing the supreme of all desires, through reciting once the essence of the great samaya, the authentic primordial wisdom, all the mudrās are to be bound — but not all of them universally. This is the karma maṇḍala.

Then comes the accomplishment of the Blessed One Vajramuṣṭi. Drinking milk, and having bound the samaya mudrā of the vajra fist, if one recites one hundred thousand times, that constitutes the prior approach. Then one is to draw on cloth. On the cloth draw the Blessed One, the noble Vajramuṣṭi, adorned with all ornaments, with both hands forming the vajra fist together with vajras, bound with the silk of vajra empowerment. Around that, in all directions, one makes the maṇḍalas of the retinues of the Tathāgatas. Those Tathāgatas too — both their hands are to be formed into the vajra fist of all the Tathāgatas. All those bodhisattvas are to be placed in meditative absorption. Then, in the presence of that image, having formed the fist of all the Tathāgatas and recited one hundred thousand times, at the conclusion one fasts for seven days, meditates on oneself as the body of all the Tathāgatas, and recites until the signs dissolve into ashes. Then, from midnight onward, the manifold activities of Māra will appear. Then one should recite this:

Oṃ vajramuṣṭi hana hana sarva māraṇa phaṭ.

Then, at the time of the inner dawn, all the Blessed Ones will come and enter into the body entirely. Thereupon one becomes a Tathāgata, an arhat, a perfectly and fully awakened buddha.

Then comes the cloth rite. On the cloth, draw the Blessed One Tathāgata adorned with all ornaments. To his right, draw the Blessed One Vajramuṣṭi adorned with all ornaments, his body the color of a dark blue utpala lotus, forming the mudrā of the vajra fist, the left fist holding a vajra, the right granting the supreme boon. To the right of that one, draw the Blessed Lady with the vajra samaya fist, adorned with all ornaments, a young goddess squeezing the vajra firmly with the samaya fist, her color red. Directly beside her, draw Vajra Luminous Stake, adorned with all ornaments, three-eyed, holding the luminous vajra stake in both hands. To the left side of the Blessed One, draw Avalokiteśvara adorned with a crown-protrusion and with all ornaments, his hands formed in the lotus and supreme-boon gestures. To his left is the bodhisattva of the great lotus-vajra called Vajrasena. To his left are Vajraśrī and Vajradūtī. Vajraśrī has a golden body color, holds the form of glorious beauty, and holds a vajra in hand. Vajradūtī is blue in color and carries a vajra staff. Then, beginning from the first day of the waxing moon,

one hundred thousand recitations while holding the vajra-fist mudrā (the sacred seal of the vajra fist), if the word "Phaṭ" is heard from that painted cloth image, then from that point on the Blessed One Vajrapāṇi will be accomplished. Thereafter one will move through the midst of the sky, and if one wishes, one can also become invisible. One will travel a thousand leagues horizontally and also travel upward. One will be able to assume whatever form one desires. Taking on the body of a sixteen-year-old with curling, swirling hair, one will enjoy all the pleasures of desire just as one wishes. By holding the vajra-fist mudrā one will become capable of benefiting all sentient beings and performing all acts such as cutting with a blade and so forth. One will remain with a beautiful form for an aeon, and will not grow old or die.

Now for another ritual. On cloth, draw the Blessed One Tathāgata. To his right, draw Vajrapāṇi adorned with all ornaments, his body the color of blue utpala lotus, holding a vajra in hand, with the vajra fist clasped together and joined, bowing in reverence before the Blessed One. To his left, draw the Blessed Lady with the sacred vajra fist, adorned with all ornaments, bowing slightly in homage to the Blessed One, her right hand offering a mango fruit, her left hand holding a vajra with the vajra-fist mudrā.

Then, having fasted for three days before the painted image on that cloth, burn without smoke eight thousand bdellium pellets the size of peas that have been smeared with sesame oil. Then that image will stir, and rays of light will issue from the mouth of the Blessed One. Then the Blessed One, granting the supreme boon by means of the vajra-fist mudrā, will bestow kingship, or the turning of the wheel, or dominion over asuras (demigods), or the power of declaring the earth, or the power of declaring what is beneath the earth, or the power of declaring the realms, or invisibility within the desire realm, or the ability to travel through the sky.

Now for another accomplishment ritual. On cloth, draw the Blessed One with the vajra-fist mudrā, adorned with all ornaments. In the same way, depict both hands bearing a vajra, holding the vajra-fist mudrā, rendered in the color of blue utpala lotus. Before that image, hold the sacred vajra-fist mudrā and recite one hundred thousand times. At the completion of that recitation, if one recites continuously for three days and nights until dawn, the Blessed One himself will come and grant the supreme boon.

On cloth, a wooden board, or a wall, draw the Blessed Lady with the vajra-fist mudrā in the same way, holding a vajra and a mango fruit. Before that image, if one recites one hundred thousand times, merely by forming all mudrās one will accomplish all activities without fail. Then activities not yet accomplished will arise. If one forms the vajra sacred vajra-fist mudrā and recites one hundred thousand times, by holding the fist mudrā one will travel through the sky. If with the left hand one forms the vajra-fist mudrā along with the vajra, the forms of Vajrapāṇi and others will appear — this by means of one hundred thousand recitations. If with the left hand one forms the vajra-fist mudrā and recites one hundred thousand times, by means of that held fist mudrā one will become invisible, and at will will become visible again. If one forms the vajra-bound fist mudrā and recites one hundred thousand times, by means of holding the fist mudrā one will become capable of binding, paralyzing, and causing sleep in all cases. If one holds the vajra-fist mudrā aloft in the sky and recites one hundred thousand times, merely by making the vajra-fist mudrā in the sky, wealth, gold, pearls, ornaments, jewels, clothing, food, drink, fire, water, flowers, fruit, sprouts, letters, weapons, autumn provisions, arms,

books, concepts, sūtras, images of buddhas, and all such existing things, and whatever destinies desire — all of these will be summoned. If one holds the right vajra-fist mudrā and recites one hundred thousand times, by displaying the vajra-fist mudrā one will become capable of summoning, drawing in, and overthrowing all existing things and all destinies. By rotating the fist, one will become capable of completely transplanting everything. If one holds the right vajra-bound fist mudrā and recites one hundred thousand times, by flinging the vajra-fist mudrā one will summon all things from all actions — wealth, gold, ornaments, clothing, weapons, flowers, fruits, and all such things. One will also summon all the finest things among forms, sounds, smells, tastes, and tactile objects. One will also summon all such things as women's breasts, the womb region, eyes, nose, lips, ears, head, thighs, the waistband, and secret hair. Upon releasing, one will be set free. If one fasts for one day, holds the vajra-fist mudrā with both hands, and recites one hundred thousand times, thereafter by holding the fist mudrā one will be able to perform all actions.

Now the actions that can be performed are these: summoning, inserting, binding, bringing under control, causing erection, causing stupor, sealing, causing sleep, raising up, guiding, overturning, striking, dispersing, separating, reversing direction, transplanting, healing, displaying all illusions, reversing seasons, causing the earth and so forth to tremble, causing descent upon all, introducing into the maṇḍala, causing to dance, causing to sing, performing the union of what arose before, prophesying individually, destroying all machines and mechanisms, opening all doors, causing all spirits to enter, raising all poisons, causing harm, moving everything, destroying the mudrās of all, obstructing all, joining what is broken, driving out emanations, seizing, releasing, causing hail, bringing down rain, drawing down lightning, cutting back all, summoning clothing, ornaments, jewels, flowers, fruits, men, women, birds, and wild animals of a kind never seen before, sending them away, displaying many forms, causing invisibility, and demonstrating travel through the sky — all actions whatsoever, according to one's desire, will be performed. This concludes the chapter on the vajra-fist mudrā from the Great King of Tantras, Victorious Over the Three Worlds.

Then the bodhisattva Mañjuśrī in his youthful form addressed the Blessed One with these words: "Blessed One, I too shall offer my own heart-essence within this tantra, Victorious Over the Three Worlds, such that by the mere utterance of it, the treasury of all Tathāgatas will be proclaimed." Then the Blessed One said to youthful Mañjuśrī: "Son of good family, speak it." Then youthful Mañjuśrī spoke: "A Ra Pa Ca Na." As soon as he had said this, it was declared that by means of the equality of letters arising from the equality of all the dharmas of all Tathāgatas, all sentient beings would attain the accomplishment of the Perfection of Wisdom.

Then a maṇḍala is to arise. A wheel maṇḍala is to be drawn on the fourteenth or fifteenth day of the waxing fortnight. In its center, at the hub, Mañjuśrī is to be placed, holding a vajra sword and a book. All around him, in the surrounding circle, a garland of the letters A Ra Pa Ca Na and so forth is to be written. Then the master, having bound the samaya mudrā of the vajra sword and entered it, shall recite these five syllables called Niṣprapañcagrā.

Then the Blessed One bestowed upon the master also the unobstructed confidence. From that point on, the Blessed One wrote the letters in the sky and proclaimed them all, and in the same way, having admitted the student, spoke thus: "Today you shall obtain the Dharma of the Tathāgata. This essence you must not speak of to anyone. These are the secret syllables: A Ra Pa Ca Na. All wishes are obtained thereby. Why is that? A signifies wish; Ra signifies attachment; Pa signifies the ultimate; Ca signifies conduct; Na signifies the absence of inherent nature. Thus, by meditating on and accomplishing this essence — which is none other than wish, attachment, the ultimate, conduct, and the very absence of inherent nature — all wishes will be obtained. The Tathāgata is the vessel of all wishes; therefore, through this essence, both the Tathāgata and Vajrapāṇi will be swiftly attained."

Then the binding of the mudrā is to be practiced. This is called Niṣprapañcagrā, the Foremost Beyond Conceptual Elaboration.

Then a sword maṇḍala is to arise. In the same way, a vajra maṇḍala is to be drawn, and a sword placed at its center. By accomplishing this essence before it, one will accomplish Mañjuśrī the Youthful. This is the vajra-sword maṇḍala.

The samaya maṇḍala: one enters a great maṇḍala drawn in proper form, and recites this:

Akāro mukhaṃ sarvadharmāṇāṃ ādyanutpannatvāt.

"The syllable A is the gateway to all dharmas, because from the very beginning they are unborn." Having turned inward and reciting this once each day, one will be untainted by all the uninterrupted sins. This is the samaya maṇḍala.

Then the karma maṇḍala arises. The Perfection of Wisdom is to be placed at the center of the maṇḍala and honored with four kinds of offering: by inquiring, by reading, by recitation, and by meditating. This is the karma maṇḍala.

Then one who wishes to accomplish, having painted on cloth in the manner of a great maṇḍala, recites the entire garland of syllables and declares: "All dharmas are established by their very nature." If one then recites these syllables one hundred thousand times, one will come to possess inexhaustible confidence equal to that of Mañjuśrī and will traverse the sky.

Moreover, even without full accomplishment, they function as deeds: reciting once, one is liberated from all suffering; reciting twice, all the uninterrupted sins are exhausted without remainder; reciting three times, meditative absorption becomes manifest; reciting four times, one becomes endowed with mindfulness; reciting five times, one swiftly attains to unsurpassed, perfect, complete awakening.

At a stūpa that enshrines bodily relics, the garland of syllables is to be written, beginning from the right side with A and proceeding all the way to Na. Then, while circumambulating and reciting the syllables one hundred thousand times, when each syllable is completed one hundred thousand times, the Blessed One Tathāgata, Mañjuśrī, and Vajrapāṇi all arise together, and day by day they appear visibly, teach the Dharma, and then depart again. Even awakening within this very lifetime, and all stages short of that, are also bestowed.

This is the contemplation of Mañjuśrī the Youthful, from the great king of contemplations, the Great King of Tantras: Triumphant over the Three Worlds.

Then the bodhisattva Vajracakra addressed the Blessed One as follows: "Blessed One, may I too bestow within this Great King of Tantras, the Triumphant over the Three Worlds, this essence — by the mere recitation of which one will enter into all maṇḍalas."

Then the Blessed One urged the bodhisattva Vajracakra to do so. Thereupon the bodhisattva Vajracakra offered his essence to the Blessed One:

Om vajra cakra hūm.

By the mere recitation of this, all the Tathāgatas and all sentient beings entered into that very maṇḍala. Then the bodhisattva Vajracakra set forth this maṇḍala:

Draw it in the likeness of a vajra wheel.
The great self of the vajra wheel —
hold its mudrā in your own hand,
and place it at the center.
All around it, in a ring,
draw the assembled maṇḍalas of the family.
And the great sentient beings themselves —
depict each of them clearly there.
Then, first among the wise,
let them enter through Vajracakra.
Having entered, recite this essence:

Jaḥ hūm baṃ hoḥ.

When this is recited, the Blessed One is delighted and grants all accomplishments. Likewise, having caused the student to enter, let the accomplishment be performed in that very way. If one binds the mudrā of Vajracakra and recites one hundred thousand times, one will attain the state of a Tathāgata or of a Vajradhāra.

This is the Vajracakra. Draw a vajra at the center of the vajra wheel; reciting likewise, one becomes a Vajradhāra.

This is the vajra maṇḍala. Having drawn that maṇḍala in the same way, place its sign and recite as follows:

By entering through Vajracakra,
through seeing and likewise through accomplishment,
all one's wishes are fulfilled.
Therefore examine it well, and then accomplish it.

This is the samaya maṇḍala. In order to accomplish through knowing the mudrā within that very drawn maṇḍala, train with unsurpassed and unwavering diligence in all things.

This is the karma maṇḍala. By the mere act of entering into the maṇḍala, all activities are performed without further striving — accomplish them according to whichever ritual or contemplation that pleases you, as taught therein.

This is the contemplation of Vajracakra, from the Great King of Tantras: Triumphant over the Three Worlds.

Then the bodhisattva Ākāśagarbha (Treasury of Space) addressed the Blessed One as follows: "Blessed One, I too shall bestow my essence within this Great King of Tantras, Triumphant over the Three Worlds — an essence by whose mere recitation one becomes the receptacle of all offerings. Blessed One, please say so." The Blessed One declared: "Speak!"

Then Ākāśagarbha spoke:

Om gagana sambhava vajra vāca hoḥ.

At that very moment, offerings of every kind arose from space throughout all the Blessed One's maṇḍalas. Then the maṇḍala arose:

Draw it in the likeness of a vajra wheel.
At its center, place the great jewel —
Space himself — foremost of all,
surrounded by all the Victors.

First, the master's entry:
the mudrā of all supreme offerings.
Having entered into that maṇḍala,
recite all the vajras.

Then the Blessed One grants accomplishment. Likewise, having caused the student to enter in the same way, recite this essence of the samaya of all vajras and teach the mudrā as well.

This is the maṇḍala called All Offerings.

Then comes the space maṇḍala. Mentally draw the maṇḍala, and enter into it. Having entered, recite as follows:

"All these are space itself. By the truth through which form arises from formless space, may offerings to all the Tathāgatas, and all accomplishments of every heart's desire, arise from space."

Having recited this and remained seated, if one binds the mudrā of all vajras and recites one hundred thousand times, then from space will arise all forms of offerings, all aims, and all excellences.

This is the vajra maṇḍala.

Draw the maṇḍala, enter into it, and declare: "This is your samaya." Then recite:

Space arises from all elements —
it is the fifth great element.
From space arise all wishes;
may the offering of every wish be accomplished.

Having recited this, direct the student: "With this, offer to all the Tathāgatas." This is the samaya maṇḍala.

Now let the student mentally enter the maṇḍala drawn in space. There, let the student also be trained in mental offering. Let the student recite mentally as follows: "With the offering of space, I make offering to all the Tathāgatas." If this is done daily, accomplishment will come. This is the Space Treasury chapter from the King of the Great Deliberations, Victorious Over the Three Worlds.

Then the noble bodhisattva Maitreya (loving-kindness personified, the future Buddha) addressed the Blessed One with these words: "Blessed One, I too shall bestow upon this King of the Great Deliberations, Victorious Over the Three Worlds, my own essence — by the mere recitation of which loving-kindness will arise in the minds of all sentient beings." The Blessed One said: "Speak, son of good family." Then the Blessed One, the bodhisattva Maitreya, proclaimed his own essence:

Oṃ maitri dṛḍha cite tiṣṭha ho.

Upon the mere recitation of this, all the Tathāgatas entered into and remained in the meditative absorption of loving-kindness. Then the maṇḍala arose. In the maṇḍala drawn as a wheel, Maitreya was placed, together with four Tathāgatas and four bodhisattvas. One binds the mudrā of firm armor and recites:

Vajra dṛḍha tiṣṭha.

Then, having entered into the meditative absorption of loving-kindness, the student was introduced in the same way as one entering into all the great maṇḍalas. Having been introduced, the student is trained in the mudrā of great loving-kindness, and the armor is then donned. This is the maṇḍala called Great Firmness.

Next comes the Maitreya maṇḍala. Enter into it through Maitreya and accomplish the meditative absorption of loving-kindness. This is the Maitreya maṇḍala.

Having entered the maṇḍala, recite as follows:

Whether joyful or wrathful,
friend and foe alike —
if you would be victorious over all beings,
cultivate great loving-kindness.

This is the samaya maṇḍala.

Then, having introduced the student into the maṇḍala, the following is to be trained in:

I pay homage to all buddhas.
May all sentient beings come to virtue.
To all spirits I offer loving-kindness,
and to all animals I give fearlessness.

This is the action maṇḍala. Through the cultivation of loving-kindness, one accomplishes all accomplishments, fulfilling every wish. Perform all activities with whatever ritual the mind delights in, together with loving-kindness. This is the Maitreya chapter from the King of the Great Deliberations, Victorious Over the Three Worlds.

Then Vajra-rikpa Dampa and others bowed their heads at the feet of the Blessed One and presented their own essences to this King of the Great Deliberations, Victorious Over the Three Worlds:

Oṃ dri. Oṃ bhinda vajra śūla. Oṃ chinda hūṃ cakra phaṭ. Oṃ bhur bhuva svaḥ svāhā. Oṃ hana bhinda vajra hūṃ phaṭ. Oṃ la la la la abhiṣa ghaṇṭan.

Then their maṇḍalas arose. In the center of the cord lines of the circular maṇḍala's chamber, draw the Great Sovereign (Maheśvara). The others are to be drawn successively throughout, or placed there, and finally a colored dot is also placed. Then first the master sits having bound the mudrā of the sign of the three illuminating pegs. The vajra student is then also admitted from the left side, and the mudrā is also taught. From that point forward, all things will be accomplished, and one will become the awareness-holder of the entire world.

This concludes the chapter on the great minister of awareness from the King of the Great Tantra of Complete Victory over the Three Worlds.

Then Vajra-Coiled and the other vajra wrathful ones offered their own heart-essence to this King of the Great Tantra of Complete Victory over the Three Worlds:

Oṃ amṛta kuṇḍali kha kha kha kha krodha jvāla māla hūṃ phaṭ. Oṃ mahā prābha sphoṭaya hūṃ phaṭ. Oṃ kāla daṇḍa hana hana hūṃ phaṭ. Oṃ kāla praśāgra ākarṣa hūṃ phaṭ.

Then their maṇḍalas arose. Draw a square maṇḍala and in its center place a vajra. In each of the four directions, the wrathful ones are likewise to be set in the intervening spaces. Then the master, having entered the mudrā of Amṛta-Coiled, is to be taught how to bind the mudrā. Thereafter, the drawing in of all, bringing under power, the banishing, and the protection shall all be accomplished. This concludes the chapter on the vajra wrathful ones.

Then Vajra-Rakṣasa and the other chief leaders of the assembly offered these heart-essences:

Oṃ mahā bālāka mahā gaṇa devī bapi bapi ru hrīḥ ran sarva duṣṭāna nā phaṭ. Oṃ māla dhara cala cala ya hūṃ. Oṃ dhanu dhara vaśī kuru sarva saṃtvāna svāhā. Oṃ jaya vijaya hūṃ phaṭ.

Then their maṇḍala arose. Draw a maṇḍala in the shape of a bow and place a vajra therein. On the right side, two; on the left side, two as well. Then one is introduced through the mudrā of Vajra-Rakṣasa, and after learning the mudrā, the following rite is to be learned: Devour all non-virtuous sentient beings. Seize them. Burn them. Consume them. Then one is to be taught the binding of the mudrā. This concludes the chapter on the assembly of messengers from the King of the Great Tantra of Complete Victory over the Three Worlds.

Then the servants Vajra-Hunchback and others addressed the Blessed One with these words: "Blessed One, we too offer our own heart-essences to this King of the Great Tantra of Complete Victory over the Three Worlds."

Oṃ vārāha daṃṣṭro tkkaṭa vigato dhara sarva nakhana khana hūṃ phaṭ. Oṃ sarva māra daḍa hūṃ phaṭ. Oṃ bhikaṭaika daṃṣṭri bhindha hūṃ phaṭ. Oṃ mahā maṇi hara hara hūṃ phaṭ.

Then this is their maṇḍala. It is to be drawn as a triangle. Having entered the manifest mudrā, the binding of the dance mudrā is to be learned, and through the offering of dance one makes offering to the vajra in the center. This concludes the chapter on the servants from the King of the Great Tantra of Complete Victory over the Three Worlds.

Then all the mothers raised a great cry and, having made offering to the Blessed One with the great offering of dance, they each offered their own heart-essence. Then the great vajra queen-ministers spoke:

Oṃ vajra krodha rali hūṃ phaṭ. Oṃ vajra cakra chindha hūṃ phaṭ. Oṃ śānati hūṃ. Oṃ vajra muṣṭi hana śindha hana hūṃ phaṭ. Oṃ kumāra habi ṣa ghaṇatikara ṇa raṇa calāya sarva duṣṭāna hūṃ phaṭ.

Then, at the gate of their square maṇḍala, a wheel and the other emblems are to be drawn. In the center, upon the flame-vessel of the wrathful vajra goddess, a shining vajra spike is placed. The samaya ritual is just as before. This concludes the chapter on the great queen vidyās from the King of the Great Tantra of Complete Victory over the Three Worlds.

Then the vajra wrathful goddesses spoke their own heart-essences:

Oṃ amṛte hūṃ phaṭ. Oṃ saumya krodhe hūṃ phaṭ. Oṃ daṇḍa dhāriṇa hana hūṃ phaṭ. Oṃ piṃgali hūṃ phaṭ.

An eight-cornered maṇḍala is drawn. In its center a vajra is placed. In the four divisions, the four are placed in order. The ritual of entry and the rest is the same as before. This is the maṇḍala of the wrathful goddesses. This concludes the chapter on the vajra wrathful goddesses from the King of the Great Tantra of Complete Victory over the Three Worlds.

Then the assembly of mothers spoke:

Oṃ khargamāriṇi hūṃ. Oṃ aśasivadhehi ramaho. Oṃ pāśasamaye hūṃ. Oṃ sarvaramaṇi ratibṛye ho.

Their maṇḍala is circular. Four gates are made, a cord is stretched through the center, four lines are struck, and it is drawn in four successive layers. Furthermore, the outer maṇḍala is likewise circular; four lines are struck in succession, and in the center a yakṣa face is drawn. On the four sides, in order, beginning from the eastern direction, they are to be drawn or placed. At the four gates, vajra-messenger figures are to be drawn or placed. Their heart-essence is this:

Bhiciriṭi bhyo bhyo.

Thus it is to be done for all, including the great queen vidyā-ministers and the rest. By reciting this once, all are gathered. Then, with the face covered, grasping both big toes with both hands, one makes homage and, entering the dancing posture of the cross-legged seat, one descends. Having bound them, give the samaya:

Sarva māra khāda piba dehi vara samaya ho.

Then the samaya is given in the same manner. Having satisfied all with song, dance, music, food, and drink, one concludes with a bell. Then it is to be declared: having obtained all accomplishments (siddhi), all happiness, and all excellent qualities, one will also attain yoga and the ability to travel through the sky and so forth. This concludes the chapter on the assembly of vajra mothers from the King of the Great Tantra of Complete Victory over the Three Worlds.

Then the vajra messenger-goddesses spoke:

Oṃ bhyo. Oṃ kali māraṇi. Vajra rākṣasi hana sarva naṭīma. Oṃ bhūtanethā. Oṃ mahā kara vāktre hūṃ phaṭ.

A pentagonal maṇḍala is drawn. The vajra is placed in the center of the pentagon. At the four cardinal points, four vajra messenger-goddesses are placed, and the ritual is performed just as before. This concludes the chapter on the vajra messenger-goddesses from the King of the Great Tantra of Complete Victory over the Three Worlds.

Then the vajra servant-goddesses spoke:

Oṃ vārāhi vajramukhi dṛtoṃ tarāpataḷa samayam anusmara āviśa hūṃ phaṭ. Oṃ kālikā kāpāli māladhāriṇi kaṭa kaṭa maṭa maṭa bhīmāraya sarva māraṇi hūṃ phaṭ. Oṃ putani ucuṣma rudra khāda sarva naśoṣaya svāhā. Oṃ makaramukhi hara hara hūṃ.

A square maṇḍala with four gates is drawn, a vajra is drawn in the center, and offering is made through dance in the same manner. This concludes the chapter on the servant-goddesses from the King of the Great Tantra of Complete Victory over the Three Worlds, being the conclusion of the Root Tantra.

Then the Blessed One, the Tathāgata, spoke to Vajrapāṇi as follows: You are the keeper of the secrets of all Tathāgatas.

Therefore, in order that this King of Tantras may be meaningful and accomplished swiftly, declare the subsequent tantra by which all sentient beings will easily attain the supreme spiritual accomplishment (siddhi). Why is this? Because these sentient beings are disciplined by diligence — some lack faith and commit sins, some are liars, some are indolent, some cannot endure hardship, and some are disciplined through freedom from attachment, while others are perpetually bound by craving. For the sake of all such beings, declare — blessed by the Tathāgata — that tantra by which all these beings will accomplish everything they undertake, easily and swiftly, in this very life, without effort and without hardship. These people of Jambudvīpa delight in sense objects and long to see their companions, their fathers, their mothers, their children, and their wives; they are stirred and driven by craving, and cannot travel even from one region to another — how much less can they behold buddha-fields and the abodes of vidyādhara masters?

Then Vajrapāṇi prostrated at the feet of the Blessed One and declared the subsequent tantra.

Then the Blessed One spoke:

Whence does the Tathāgata arise?
Whence is Vajrapāṇi born?
And from where does the vajra itself come?
What is the distinction among mudrās, essence-mantras, vidyās,
and mantras?
What is the nature of secret accomplishment?
What is the nature of bound mudrā-sealing?
What is the suchness of all these?
What is the nature of blessing and the rest?
How are the practitioners to be regarded?
What is the manner of one who desires accomplishment?
How is an image to be drawn on cloth?
What are the proper proportions of a deity-image?
How is recitation to be performed, as it is taught?
With what kind of offering is worship to be made?
How does one without merit accomplish it?
How does accomplishment come about swiftly?
How does one become a master?
How are the various rites to be performed?
What is the suchness of all these?
It is fitting that all of this be explained to me.

Then the Tathāgata gave an account of his arising:

The bodhisattva has no beginning and no end —
his defining characteristic is the arising of space.
Samantabhadra is the self of all;
he is the sovereign of migrating beings' desires.
The mind that is the mind of all sentient beings:
through stability, that is called a bodhisattva.
Through meditative equipoise comes samādhi,
and one becomes assuredly vajra in nature.

To this, someone objected: How can mind, which is formless, become a solid vajra?

The reply was given: Just as, when sentient beings are born into consciousness, flesh and bones arise from the gathering of what is pleasant, what is attached to, what is pleasurable, and formless consciousness — just so, sentient beings are born from that gathering. And just as, when nāgas are roused to anger, through the certainty of their mind's samādhi, meteoric iron arises in the sky, or lightning flashes forth — just so, from certainty the vajra nature arises, and it is the application of blessing to the bodhisattva.

Thus the vajra becomes the bodhisattva,
and the Blessed One who is the very vajra nature
becomes the bodhisattva through meditation.

As for what the application of blessing to the bodhisattva consists in:

When the self is great among all bodhisattvas,
one is blessed with the recognition "It abides in the self itself,"
and then one should cultivate the pride of thinking
"I am that bodhisattva."

That very one is the Blessed One, the bodhisattva —
Vajrasattva, or the Victor.
The examination of one's own mind, in and of itself,
is the very awakening of buddhahood.
That very one is Vajrapāṇi,
in the manner of the vajra-holder.
The lord of the world, the heart of space —
others, too, are that very suchness.
The buddha-family is also that suchness,
and it is the family of the vajra-holder.
That suchness is the family of Avalokiteśvara.
That suchness is the jewel-family —
and it is taught that the three realms are that very suchness.

The three worlds, too, are that very suchness.
Suchness is the maker and the one who draws forth.
It is the forefather of all sentient beings.
That suchness is the dwelling place of sentient beings.
The great elements are that very suchness.
That suchness is the protector of all wandering beings —
it is their abiding and their going.

Thus declared the Blessed One, the bodhisattva Vajrapāṇi, who is without beginning and without end.

This concludes the first chapter of the treatise called "The Great Suchness."

Next comes the section on the distinguishing features of essence-mantra, mudrā, mantra, and awareness.

The distinguishing feature of the essence-mantra is called "the secret" and "meditative absorption." In this connection, speaking the secret and proclaiming it for the sake of sentient beings is the recitation of the essence-mantra. Through reciting the essence-mantra, one attains the accomplishment of whatever is intended in the heart — this is the distinguishing feature of the essence-mantra.

Next, the distinguishing feature of the mudrā is set forth in verse:

The applications of reflected images
of the vajra-body, speech, and mind —
these are the distinctive excellence of all mudrās,
and through them the elements, too, are shown.
Just as, when servants behold the seal of their lord,
they do not act in defiance,
so too the Victors themselves
do not pass beyond the mudrā.
Adjust the vajra-body, speech, and mind
in accordance with the place as appropriate,
and transform oneself into the great bodhisattva
endowed with the mudrā of signs.
That is the great mudrā (mahāmudrā).
The intertwined and stable pledge
of vajra-body, speech, and mind —
from the wisdom of the mind,
distinguished into two, arises the action-mudrā.
These are the root of the samaya-pledge,
the supreme cause — the binding that arises from the root —
through which the entire body becomes stable.
Therefore the mudrā is proclaimed.

That by which all is sealed,
so that it is not wasted but thoroughly concealed —
because the sovereign holds the reflected image,
the practitioners swiftly attain accomplishment.
Those who behold it come to behold the buddhas,
the bodhisattvas, and the rest.
Though one is afflicted by the violation of the samaya-pledge,
one will not be passed over — accomplishment will come.
That is the distinguishing feature of the mudrā.
The ultimate suchness itself is also called "the great mudrā."

Now, the distinguishing feature of mantra is this:

That which is utterly free of conceptual elaboration in speech alone,
established by the word of the buddhas,
urged on by the words of the Dharma —
those are what are called mantras.

And the distinguishing feature of awareness (vidyā) is this:

It eliminates ignorance
and causes awareness itself to be fully absorbed in suchness.
Because all meanings are comprehended,
it is therefore called "awareness."

This concludes the second chapter, the exposition of essence-mantra, mudrā, mantra, and awareness.

Next, the Blessed One, the unsurpassed holder of vajra-wisdom of all mudrās, declared that he would explain the chapter on the binding of mudrās.

In this connection, the great mudrā is bound by the essence-mantra that rouses suchness. The samaya-mudrā and the action-mudrā are bound by performing the vajra-binding, the striking, and so forth. By means of the essence-mantra that rouses the suchness of the great samaya-pledge, all mudrās — beginning with the great mudrā — are bound.

Furthermore, these are not to be given to the unattached, to those who are not disciples, to those who are not suitable vessels, or to those without faith. If one trains over a long period, even those may come to attainment.

The essence-mantra that rouses suchness is this: SAMAYA STVAṂ.

The essence-mantra that rouses the suchness of the great samaya-pledge is this: SAMAYA HO SURATA STVAṂ.

If the wrathful vajra-being binds all mudrās for oneself, or causes another to be bound, it is done with just a single utterance of HŪṂ.

Then the binding called "vajra-binding" and other bindings arise. Recite VAJRABANDHA and bind the vajra-binding at the heart. Then strike all the fingers with this essence-mantra.

OṂ VAJRA BANDHA ṬĀ ṬHĀH. With this, all mudrās are summoned and brought under command.

Then bind the samaya-pledge mudrā for descending vajra-wisdom. Recite VAJRĀVEŚA, and with this essence-mantra generate the descent of the vajra. AḤ — this essence-mantra of all descent is utterly infallible.

Then bind the samaya-pledge mudrā of the vajra-fist: recite VAJRAMUṢṬI and bind it.

If, however, that has not been done, then recite SAMAYA STVAṂ and bind the mudrās of all families, beginning with the great mudrā. The essence-mantra that is the fruit of all the body-mudrās, speech-mudrās, and mind-mudrās of all the Tathāgatas is not to be let pass without purpose — through a thousand recitations all bound mudrās will be accomplished. If the mudrā of the great samaya-pledge is recited just once, it will become precious to all those who have attained accomplishment.

Then the bound mudrās arise as follows. Joining the palms and interlacing the fingers with one another is the vajra-clasp. That same form, with the fingers pressed tightly into the spaces between them, is the vajra-binding. From that same position, inserting both thumbs inside, tucking them firmly between the little fingers and the ring fingers, and closing the fist — this is the samaya-pledge mudrā of the descent of the vajra. From that same position, pressing both thumbs flat and inserting them inside in the same way, likewise placing them between the little fingers and ring fingers, with the index fingers fully hooked — this is the samaya-pledge mudrā of the vajra-fist. Done with two of these separately, it becomes the other action mudrā (karma-mudrā). Recite the great samaya-pledge suchness just once, and bind them separately at the heart or in the sky.

The speech-mudrā as well: recite VAJRA JIHVĀ and bind the mudrā. The view-mudrā is bound by means of the meaning of the essence-mantra. The tooth-mudrā, the lip-mudrā, the eye-movement mudrā, and the mudrā of moving the heart and body — reciting SAMAYA STVAṂ will accomplish the binding of all of these.

Then the binding of the mudrā called "vajra HŪṂ" arises. When the wrathful vajra index finger is formed into the vajra-fist and the index finger is extended, this is called the wrathful vajra. In verse:

The two wrathful vajra index fingers
intertwined and bound at the wrists,
with the little fingers linked as hooks —
threaten with the syllable HŪṂ.

That same form, with the right side bent sideways, is the wrathful vajra sideways posture. This is the samaya-pledge mudrā.

In verse:

The vajra-binding held in the palms,
covered by the pressing of the thumbs,
this fist that is formed —
the samaya-pledge mudrā of the wrathful vajra —
is called TIRIṂ TIRI.
HŪṂ is the speech-mudrā.
The wisdom mudrā is the wrathful one who slays all.

The vajra-ground binding: the vajra-binding stretched out flat along the ground and placed there — this becomes the vajra-ground, and no one can find any opening in it. This is the mudrā of the wrathful one who desires the vajra.

The vajra-binding, once bound, with the index finger made like a vajra — this is the mudrā that exercises dominion over all.

Bind the vajra samaya-pledge with the vajra-binding, then press both index fingers evenly flat against the heart. If one transgresses this, the heart will be split and one will die.

In verse:

Bind the two vajra mudrās,
bind the index fingers sideways,
twist inward and twist outward —
this is the supreme one for summoning the gods.

Fix the vajra-binding firmly,
squeeze the hooked index fingers tightly,
with both thumbs pressed flat,
squeezed together toward the center —
holding both vajra-fists in the mid-air posture,
with both little fingers joined,
the backs of the index fingers applied —
this repels those who roam the sky.

Having firmly set the vajra-binding, press both fists upon the ground. This is the samaya-seal of the earth: it is difficult for anything to transgress it.

Bind the two vajra fists,
squeeze finger upon finger with the left index finger,
and strike the little finger and ring finger together evenly —
this summons those of the lower realms.

Make both index fingers firm,
bind them joined in the vajra-binding.
Make the middle fingers and thumbs into vajras —
this destroys all armaments.

The vajra-twining, the vajra-squeezing, the vajra-garland, the vajra-earth, the vajra-below-the-earth, and the vajra-heruka.

Now, this is the mudrā of vajra-force. When constructing maṇḍalas — whether on cord or in drawn designs — at the moment of entry and arising, one lifts it with mind through accomplishment.

Now, this is the essence-mantra of the wisdom mudrā: Oṃ vajravega Hūṃ. By this, the samaya will not be broken. Otherwise, if the samaya is broken, accomplishment will not arise.

Now, this is the mudrā of prostration to all the Tathāgatas. Bind both shoulders in the vajra-binding, form them into two vajra fists, place the knees on the ground, touch the head to the earth, and prostrate. To Vajrapāṇi specifically, with the full power of devotion, one remains present, and prostrates with a slight smile, animated expression, and eyebrows raised with the eyes. For the others, it is done on the knees alone.

Now, this is the binding of the samaya-mudrā of the Vajra-holding Lady. Join the backs of both hands together and link thumbs with little fingers. This mudrā of the Vajra-holding Lady brings all buddhas under command. Pratīccha vajra. With this essence-mantra, scatter flowers.

The mudrā of the vajra hook-and-pull: bend both index fingers of the Vajra-holding Lady's mudrā in the manner of a hook-and-pull. The samaya of summoning all Tathāgatas and so forth, and all sentient beings, is not to be transgressed — the samaya is the iron hook.

Then arises the binding of the conceptual mudrā of all the Tathāgatas:

Bind the two vajra fists,
and suppress with the right —
the left index finger of the wrathful vajra
contemplates the supreme awakening of the buddhas.

Now, this is the samaya-mudrā of all the Tathāgatas:

Both thumbs seated cross-legged,
both index fingers curled inward,
vajra palms with a light at the center of the maṇḍala,
both middle fingers opened apart —
this is the body-mudrā of all the Tathāgatas, and through summoning, introducing, and bringing all the Tathāgatas under command, one will not transgress it. By seeing this, one will behold the bodies of all the Tathāgatas.

Then, the wisdom mudrā of Vajravidyā:

Mind is said to be perfectly pure —
through steadiness it is called a bodhisattva.
Through meditative equipoise it becomes samādhi;
through certainty it becomes vajra itself.
Being of the essential nature of the buddhas,
mind itself is buddhahood.
Blessed by all the buddhas,
one will certainly become a buddha.
Bind the great mudrā and meditate on this.

Then all the Tathāgatas, by individually examining their own minds and so forth, and by fully engaging one with buddhahood, gave their blessing that one would attain buddhahood in this very lifetime — such was the blessing they bestowed.

The fist of all the Tathāgatas is the karma-mudrā.

Then, the great mudrā of Vajrapāṇi is this:

Through the vajra of body, speech, and mind,
one becomes the very self of Vajrapāṇi.
Performing the vajra-strut supremely,
and with that proud display, the vajra is refined.
By this, everything is traversed in an instant —

light radiates and rises upward.
Sovereign over all desires and all power,
one becomes equal to Vajrapāṇi.

Then comes the commitment mudrā (samaya-mudrā). Having bound the vajra-fist, one tucks the thumbs inside and places them at the heart. The thumbs and little fingers are opened level. Thus the great vajra is bound. Through the meditative absorption of all bodhisattvas, by binding this at the heart, one accomplishes the attainment of all mudrās and will obtain even the supreme siddhis.

The wisdom mudrā (jñāna-mudrā) for this is as follows. In the center of a lunar maṇḍala, through the vajra of body, speech, and mind, one meditates on the body of Vajrapāṇi as one's own body — and swiftly it will be accomplished. When the two vajra-fists are made with the proud vajra-strut, and the vajra is refined, and with the display of striding proudly upward, one holds this at the heart — it is accomplished.

The binding of the body mudrā of the Blessed One is as follows. One binds the two vajra-fists, and with the right hand performing the gesture of drawing a bow, in the manner of releasing an arrow, one pierces all — and thus attachment is aroused.

Then the garland mudrā of the empowerment of the Blessed One: the body mudrā of the Tathāgata is placed at the crown. Then it is lowered, the thumbs are made into vajras, and from there, turning them, the thumbs are made into jewels and placed to the right side. At the back, the lotus of the thumb-vajra is placed. Then turning from there, the thumbs and little fingers are separated and placed to the left. Then the two index fingers are knotted and, in the manner of binding a garland, knotted at the back — and then all the fingers are released in sequence from the little fingers onward in the manner of dissolving, which is the release in the manner of a crown. This is the mudrā of Vajrapāṇi.

Then the Blessed One, wishing to accomplish the bodhisattva who is without beginning or end, creates a goddess maṇḍala. Then for one day, having blessed oneself accordingly, at whatever time and to whatever extent possible, one makes all that is desired pleasant and comfortable. With the song and melody of the great mudrā, in vajra-words one recites Vajrasattva, and this essence-mantra is recited as many times as one wishes:

Oṃ mahāsukha vajrasattva jaḥ hūṃ baṃ hoḥ surata stvaṃ.

In this way one recites and accomplishes for one month.

At the conclusion of the month, on the fifteenth day, when one is tired, one releases the mudrā, stretches out the vajra-palms, and with the whole body performs prostration. Having rested a little while so that one is no longer fatigued, one then recites until dawn of that night. The following morning, the Blessed One arrives with a luminosity matching the color and maṇḍala of the moon, embraces the practitioner, and enters throughout the whole of that person's body. Thereupon the practitioner too becomes radiant like a lunar maṇḍala, and adorned with the garlands of all the Tathāgatas and all divine ornaments — the body, speech, and mind of the vajra-holder all become equal to every vajra. The body, speech, and mind of Vajrapāṇi become like every vajra.

All of one's own clan, relatives, friends, companions, spiritual friends, and every sentient being who sees that beautiful form will not die but will look upon it, will make offerings to it, will rely upon it, and will perform every purposeful action. Remaining in one's own home, one performs divine miracles. Having become a Tathāgata as well, one performs all supernormal powers and miracles. Through forms such as Vajra Hūṃ and the like, one also tames beings. Those sentient beings too will see, and even the untamed will be tamed. One takes on whatever form is desired, can go everywhere, come back, and rise upward —

— until one returns, having made offerings to all the Tathāgatas in all world-realms throughout the universe. Enjoying every aim, wielding power over every desire, and pursuing every desire, one becomes equal to space itself." Thus spoke the Blessed One, Vajrasattva.

Then follows the wrathful mudrā of Vajra Desire. From the two vajra-fists held together, one draws a flower-bow in the manner of shooting an arrow — this gladdens all. With the two vajra-fists at the heart, "Vajra Hūṃ" is declared; and by hurling the vajra with the right vajra-fist, the thrown vajra utterly scatters all.

Then comes the samaya mudrā. Hold the two vajra-fists together. In this way, bend the little finger and ring finger, knot them, then press the thumb over them, bind them, and place this at the heart. This is what unfailingly hooks, draws in, binds, brings under power, and leads all the Tathāgatas and the rest.

Embracing with the vajra-fists is the mudrā of Vajra Joy. The samaya mudrā arises as follows: having fastened and bound the vajra interlace, insert the tip of the left ring finger as a mark at the sign of the thumb, then bind it there, moving it gently. This gladdens all women.

Holding the two vajra-fists in the manner of grasping a victory banner is the Vajra Victory Banner. The samaya mudrā arises: having fastened and bound the vajra interlace, hook the two index fingers, cross the ring fingers, raise the little fingers, and likewise raise both thumbs. Merely by displaying this, all the Tathāgatas themselves show loving-kindness — what need is there to say more of others?

Making proud with the two vajra-fists is Vajra Pride. The samaya mudrā arises: having formed the complete vajra interlace, encircle both sides of the chin, and by gripping and moving and bowing, all the wrathful ones are pleased.

Together with the supreme Vajrasattva, through the perfected practice of the Vajra Dance, in short, the secret offering of flowers and the like is swiftly accomplished. It is also joined with the secret offering of other contemplations.

Then comes the Vajra Dance. Having bound the two vajra-fists, open them outward from the edges with a smile. Whirl and open like lightning, then place them at the cheek and at the crown of the head. This is called the Vajra Dance — it is the totality of the offering of great self-pride. By making this offering with entreaty, even that which is without substance is swiftly accomplished.

Then the binding of the mudrās of Vajra Form and the rest: stretch out the vajra interlace and gaze at the palm. With the vajra interlace, the left index finger is the neck of the vīṇā; the right, bent, is the string. Stroke one's own heart with the open palm of the outstretched hand. In the manner of a flower-cupped hand, one drinks the vajra flavor.

Having held the two vajra-fists, the left little finger is grasped by the right ring finger. With that right ring finger, one hooks all the samayas of the bodhisattvas and all sentient beings as well.

The vajra interlace with the thumbs knotted is the lasso mudrā. The interlinked fingers of the two vajra-fists bind all. With the mudrā of Vajra Descent, one causes all to descend.

Then comes the armor mudrā. With the two bound fists, perform this in the manner of tying a knot at the heart. Moving away and then back again to the shoulder blades — likewise. Moving back to the heart — likewise. In between, knot again. Moving once more, back to the heart and shoulder blades — likewise. And at the crown of the head as well — likewise — as the place of empowerment.

In the manner of a garland-binding knot, releasing all fingers beginning with the little fingers, one performs the crown mudrā.

Then, the binding of the mudrā of the noble Avalokiteśvara:

Palms joined like a budding lotus —
known as the Great Lotus —
the little fingers and thumbs parting:
this clears away all transgressions.

That same form, with the index fingers bent and curved downward, is the mudrā that summons all.

Then comes Lotus Arising. The two index fingers of the vajra-bound hands are hooked, and the two middle fingers are placed resting on top of the level thumbs. Having formed this, one opens the lotus with the right hand upon the left. Then the mudrā of the pure lotuses: Hrīḥ. The wisdom mudrā is precisely what is taught as purifying the stains of attachment and the rest through the opening of the lotus.

Then, the binding of the mudrā of Ākāśagarbha (the bodhisattva whose name means "Sky-Treasury"): with both jewel-fists one binds the garland of empowerment.

Then, the mudrā of Vajra-Jewel:

Having formed the full vajra binding,
the index fingers leveled, joined to the thumbs —
these are presented face-outward:
empowerment is granted at the place of empowerment.

That same form, shown facing upward and placed at the heart, is what is called the jewel-fist — it carries off all purposes. From that same form, pressing the level index fingers down with the vajra of the thumbs and drawing them back is the Vajra Jewel. Trāṃ. This is the Dharma mudrā. Meditating upon all wisdom mudrās in the sky is the wisdom mudrā.

Then the binding of the mudrā of the vajra-fist is exactly as already explained above. Bāṃ. This is the speech mudrā. The binding of Loving-Kindness (Maitreya) is supreme among all bindings — it is the wisdom mudrā. In front is the mudrā of the Great Vajra. To the right is precisely the mudrā of Vajra-Jewel shown outward. Behind, the vajra binding is pressed to the heart, the thumbs joined, and the two index fingers hooked. This is the mudrā of the supreme Vajra-Lotus. The two thumbs and two little fingers of the vajra binding extended at the heart — this is the vajra: all vajras. It is the samaya mudrā of all families, conferring all accomplishments. One draws it in the maṇḍala of mudrās.

Then, the binding of the mudrā of Mañjuśrī: the vajra-fist with the tips of the index fingers hooked — this is the sword. The essence is the book. This is the samaya mudrā. The little finger of the left hand, holding the book, is grasped by the right hand holding the sword. Āḥ. This is the Dharma mudrā. "All phenomena are without a syllable" — this is the wisdom mudrā.

Then, the binding of the mudrā of Vajracakra (the Vajra Wheel):

Both vajra-fists held firm,
the little fingers and index fingers bound in the vajra manner —
this is called the Vajra Wheel:
it accomplishes all maṇḍalas.

By seeing this, one will come to behold all maṇḍalas. Likewise, if one places it facing the center and recites, one will enter all maṇḍalas and accomplish them accordingly. Hūṃ. This is the Dharma mudrā.

Then, the binding of the mudrā of Ākāśakoṣa (the Sky-Treasury): from the Vajra-Jewel mudrā, one raises the little finger and ring finger level in the manner of erecting a victory banner. The Dharma mudrā is Trāṃ.

The binding of the mudrā of the noble Maitreya, the great bodhisattva, is the armor mudrā. The speech mudrā is Oṃ.

Then arises the understanding of the general mudrā of all conceptual elaboration. That which becomes the body mudrā is as follows: whether standing or sitting, the feet brought level and extended, and the arms raised — this is the vajra mudrā.

One who accomplishes the supreme becomes equal to the vajra. Rising, one grasps the vajra fist with the left hand, and draws it by hooking with the right — this is the mudrā of the Vajra Iron Hook, the mudrā that draws in all things. Rising and raising the knees, one hooks them, joins the two hands together, and stretches them upward — this is the sword that expels all things. Embracing one's own heart with both shoulders is the armor, the indivisible.

Then, the binding of one's own mudrā is: Aho Haṃ Hūṃ — the mudrā of descent, subjugation, protection, and expulsion.

Then arise the mudrās of the gaze. In verse:

The swift-acting vajra gaze —
wide open and closed, it draws in.
The wrathful gaze is a furrowed scowl.
The steady gaze is utterly unmoving.

Then, the activities that arise from these: with the vajra gaze, all living beings are to be gladdened. With the open gaze, all things are summoned, and epidemics, demons, poisons, sorcery, and all illnesses are dispelled. With the wrathful gaze, all things are expelled. With the steady gaze, all things are protected.

The essence-mantras for these are as follows: Vajradṛṣṭi Maṭ. Dīptadṛṣṭi Aṃkuśaja. Oṃ Krodhadṛṣṭi Phaṭ. Dṛḍhadṛṣṭi Haṃ.

Then arises the mudrā of the teeth: clenching the teeth performs expulsion; baring the teeth performs subjugation. Reciting the essence-mantra and reciting the wrathful meditative absorption, one performs the activity of the mudrā. Gnashing from the right is summoning. Gnashing from the left is subjugating. Pressing the upper lip down over the lower teeth performs expulsion. Pressing both lips together performs protection.

Mindfulness of the Buddha, mindfulness of Vajrapāṇi, and mindfulness of the Wrathful Vajra — these accomplish all things. This is the mudrā of the lips.

Moving the right eye performs subjugation. Moving the left eye arouses affection. Closing both performs expulsion. Opening both wide performs protection.

Reciting the all-essence mantra while gazing upward causes one to travel through the sky. Gazing downward makes one a master of the earth. Circling to the right makes offering to all the Tathāgatas. Circling to the left makes offering to Vajrapāṇi. One accomplishes all things by reciting the all-essence mantra one thousand times.

Then arise the bindings of the outer mudrās of the Vajra Family. Among these, the worldly mudrās arise from the worldly fist. The Vajra Fist arises from the vajra-binding: one tucks the left thumb inward and presses it between the little finger and ring finger, closing it tight. With this very mudrā, one recites this essence-mantra one hundred thousand times and accomplishes: Oṃ Vajramudrā Hūṃ.

Making two vajra fists, one holds the right just as the sign was made. The binding of the mudrā of all the mothers is as follows: from the left vajra fist, one raises the wrathful vajra finger. In a charnel ground or at a gaṇacakra gathering, using the wrathful vajra finger one lifts three drops of human blood to the lips and tastes them, then — without speaking of the corpse and without letting it be harmed — one presses it down with the left foot. Reciting one hundred times, one will travel through the sky, become invisible, and bring all sorcery under one's power. One will become master of all those assemblies, being capable of reaching them at once and of benefiting them. All the supreme joys of the ḍākinīs, all conceptual processes, all activities, and all mudrās will be accomplished.

Then, the essence-mantra for accomplishing the wrathful fist is this: Oṃ Vajra Ārali. This is the procedure for binding the mudrā.

Then, following upon that, the occasion of the secret accomplishment of all mudrās is to be explained.

Then, the secret accomplishment of the Tathāgata's mudrā: one draws a maṇḍala and there recites the following:

Blessed One, this noble mudrā —
may you grant it to me always,
so that, to accomplish the welfare of all sentient beings,
I may accomplish the supreme attainment.

From within the maṇḍala, Vajrapāṇi declares:

Surāte samaya stvaṃ ho. Vajrasattva adya siddhanān.

Then, the secret accomplishment of the mudrās of the other bodhisattvas:

O great bodhisattva,
grant me attainment always.

Then, the secret accomplishment of the outer vajra family: one recites the words "Vajrapāṇi, the wrathful one, is the doer of all," and performs the accomplishment.

The secret accomplishment of the mudrā of the mothers: entering the maṇḍala, one recites "Bhyo bhyo" — this is the procedure for secret accomplishment.

Then arises the suchness of the mudrā. The suchness of the Tathāgata's mudrā is this: from mind arise the qualities of a buddha. In truth, these dwell within the heart of the Tathāgata's consort. Since I too am a Tathāgata, she is my consort — having cultivated this understanding beforehand, one accomplishes the mudrā.

The suchness of Vajrapāṇi's mudrā:

Supreme among all bindings, the most exalted joy —
from that, the mudrās are fashioned.
Vajrasattva of desire
swiftly grants firm attainment.

Then, for others:

The Blessed One, desiring what is eternal —
that very thing is the Tathāgata.
From that, all take the form of selfhood.
May I too accomplish the mudrā.

Then, the suchness of the mudrā of the outer vajra family: Vajrapāṇi brought all the gods under his power and transformed them into vajras, so their mudrās too are none other than the vajra mudrā.

Then, the suchness of the mudrā of the mothers:

Created by all the gods
in order to devour all that is inauspicious —
by performing the descent of the mudrā, one cultivates that same understanding and accomplishes it. First cultivating through the application of the suchness of the Tathāgata, one then accomplishes it. This is the procedure for the suchness of the mudrā.

Then, the activities of the blessing of all conceptual processes and so forth are explained. Thus the Blessed One Vajrapāṇi declared:

Each day, in proper sequence,
with the supreme great vajra mudrā,
the heart, the brow, the side, the crown —
one shall consecrate oneself.

The essence-mantra for this blessing is: Vajrasattva, Vajraratna, Vajradharma, Vajrakarma.

And this is the essence-mantra for the empowerment of Vajraratna: Oṃ sarva tathāgata abhiṣiñca.

With this essence-mantra, one places the Vajraratna mudrā at the brow. The two index fingers are bound in the manner of a garland, then formed into a knot at the back, and rotated; beginning from the little finger, one releases them, releasing all fingers one by one, so that they spread open like unfurled silk.

Then, for stirring the gnosis of meditative absorption: one clasps the vajra binding, places it at the breast, and meditates. With this essence-mantra of meditation, one visualizes a vajra at one's own heart: Oṃ vajrātmakaḥ. One recites the vajra recitation as many times as one wishes.

Then, the procedure for the vajra offering:

Holding the two vajra fists,
let the vajra sway gracefully.
Likewise a vajra garland and song,
and vajra dance — from the crown.
There, taking up the laudatory vajra song,
one makes offering.

This is the condensed Vajrasattva:

Vajraratna, unsurpassed —
taking up Vajradharma as song,
I pray that Vajrakarma be performed.

Joy, empowerment, and —

By this song and offering, even Tathāgahood itself is not difficult to attain — what need is there to speak of other accomplishments? Why is this so? Because the bodhisattva's bliss that is comparable to this is unequalled.

Then the blessing of the earth by Vajrapāṇi and so forth arises. On the very first day, at the proper time:

With the method of the vajra blessing,
having blessed myself,
one possesses the yoga of blessing the bodhisattva.
With vajra and vajra-binding,
with the descent of the vajra and so on,
with the vajra fist joined with the vajra,
making two — through the supreme mudrā,
I shall bless my own being.
One also meditates on desire as oneself.

Then one recites the secret essence-mantra of Vajrasattva. This secret essence is recited with the vajra song as many times as one wishes: Ṭakkī jaḥ ho.

Then, for other accomplishments as well: by means of the vajra-binding, taking hold of the seal and reciting one hundred thousand times, mastery over all desires, the form of desire, the life of desire, the lord of desire, and the fulfillment of all that is universally traversed will be accomplished.

Then arises the elaborate procedure for the empowerment of Vajrapāṇi: through this essence, the garland empowerment of all the Tathāgatas is conferred exactly as it is. Oṃ sarva-tathāgata-ratna-abhiṣeka āḥ.

Then, the meditative absorption focused on generating the wisdom of all the Tathāgatas: just as the Blessed One is the identity of all sentient beings, so too am I the identity of all — meditating thus, with this essence one draws all in, causes all to enter, binds all, brings all under mastery, and meditates. The essence-mantra for this is: Oṃ sattva jaḥ hūṃ vaṃ hoḥ.

Then, making the secret offering before the Blessed One — this includes the blessing and all that follows:

Holding the two vajra fists,
joining them with the vajra dance,
one presents flowers and so forth —
the secret offering is presented by mudrā.
In the manner of one drawing a bow,
the vajra arrow is to be shot.
One embraces from all sides, raises the banner of victory,
and with mutual pride, makes obeisance.

With this vajra praise, one takes up the vajra song and makes offering:

The bliss of form and the other objects of desire,
the supreme mental joy — to you, the Blessed One, supremely joyful, I bow.
You who today offer to me every joy and bliss —
swiftly grant the accomplishment of the supreme self, just as it is.

Through this essence of praise, all accomplishments will be attained. If one praises the Blessed One Vajrapāṇi once each day, all the actions of the Blessed One will be obtained. The master, from his very tongue, will come to speak all things of past, present, and future — all the words of the Blessed One, all conceptual thoughts, all verbal streams. He will also receive divine songs never heard before. By reciting the vajra song one hundred thousand times, one will become a vajra-holder.

Having performed the blessing in this way once, thereafter let one accomplish through that. Then, performing the blessing and all that follows once each day, every day — all accomplishments, all excellences, empowerment and so forth, all forms of knowledge, and all actions will be achieved. All the Tathāgatas and the bodhisattvas will come to that place, teach the subtle matters and so forth, and speak. They will show loving-kindness. They will make offerings. Moreover, they will make offerings to all the Tathāgatas and honor them with all forms of service. One will become capable of taming and benefiting all sentient beings. Without destruction, without exhaustion, without deficiency —

one will become free from all fear. All fears of all sentient beings, all illness, and all suffering will likewise be dispelled. All miraculous displays will be shown. From oneself, from the forms of all the Tathāgatas and bodhisattvas and so forth, from all one's own bodies, from space, earth, trees, mountains, and waters — from all of these, all the forms of the Tathāgatas and so forth will be made to appear. All miraculous displays such as the emission of rays of light and so forth will be shown. Oneself too will take on the form of a buddha and so forth, and will become the form of all things. With one's eyes one will see all cognitions of past, future, and present within the syllables. One will hear everything, and speak with one's own tongue. One will explain all the concepts of what has gone before, and all the sūtras. All teachers and protectors, lutes, drums, weapons, the arts of healing, crafts, and all performing arts will be known as spontaneously accomplished. Each day one will come to possess a great body, radiant complexion, strength, courage, long life, freedom from illness, and all accomplishments. One will know and perceive the minds, conduct, speech, and words of all sentient beings. Each day one will come to directly know all things. In brief, Tathāgatahood itself, and all accomplishments beginning with Vajrapāṇi — all of these will be obtained in this very lifetime without having to strive for them separately. This is the practice of blessing and all that follows.

Now there arises the practice of accomplishment. The practice of accomplishing the Tathāgata is this: one meditates, visualizing before oneself the recollection of the body of a buddha, and accomplishes the essence-mantra, mudrā, mantra, and vidyā-mantra. The practice of accomplishing Vajrapāṇi is this: one practices the yoga of blessing the mind as a bodhisattva, or meditates on it with longing, or recollects Vajrasattva, or meditates on a vajra at one's own heart, or while maintaining the recollection of a buddha before oneself, one accomplishes the essence-mantra, mudrā, mantra, and vidyā-mantra. For all the great bodhisattvas: one recollects one's own particular bodhisattva, or meditates on a great vajra at the heart, and accomplishes the essence-mantra, mudrā, mantra, and vidyā-mantra. The practice of the outer vajra family is this: in the manner of a wrathful deity, one accomplishes the essence-mantra, mudrā, mantra, and vidyā-mantra. The practice of accomplishing the vajra-queens is this: one recollects devouring all sentient beings, or oneself becomes a vajra-rākṣasī, and with fierce wrath thinks "I too shall devour all sentient beings," and accomplishes the essence-mantra, mudrā, mantra, and vidyā-mantra. This is the practice of accomplishment.

Now there arises the practice of the means of accomplishment, in verse:

Just as one would tame the world,
so is the accomplishment said to be taught —
the supreme one who performs all attainments.
This is the practice of the means of accomplishment.

Now there arises the practice concerning cloth. Regarding this: for the Tathāgata, the cloth is made without hair and a circular maṇḍala is drawn upon it with a line. For Vajrapāṇi, a square is drawn on all sides on silk or cloth. For the great bodhisattvas, it is arranged in the form of whatever assembly has gathered. For the outer vajra families, those not included among them are arranged in the form of whatever assembly there is. For the vajra-queens, the canvas is left without a border being cut; it is painted by an artist who observes the restoration ceremony, using pigments placed in a vessel on the ground without any gift being given. This is the practice concerning cloth.

Now the practice of measuring the forms: the Tathāgata is to be made to the height of a Tathāgata. For Vajrapāṇi, since there is no limit, the measure is whatever one wishes.

Now, as to the measure of the vajra: how large should it be? It should be made sixteen finger-widths, measured by the joints of one's own thumb. As to the shape of the vajra: it should be four-sided, five-pointed, adorned with a garland of rays of light, and cast in one piece. All great bodhisattvas are to be made to the measure of sixteen finger-widths as counted by the thumb-joints, and all of them are to be depicted seated upon lotuses. Likewise, the measure of the maṇḍala is to be made proportionate to the measure of the depicted forms.

Now this is the practice of recitation to be performed. For the Tathāgatas, the words used are those of the intention-speech, the words of meditative absorption (samādhi). For the vajras, it is the same. For the generality of all ritual acts, recitation is performed by means of the vajra-word, and the purpose is accomplished. For Vajrapāṇi, the recitation is joined with the song-words of the vajra. The wrathful vajra-beings, beginning with Vajra Hūṃ and so forth, are recited using the words of the vajra-wrathful ones. The outer vajra families are recited using Dharma-words, with the great roaring sound of the wrathful ones made clearly, and recited together with the attitude of wrath. The vajra-queens are recited using subtle vajra-words. The words of meditative absorption are recited with the mind alone.

Now the vajra-speech: with the tip of the tongue remaining perfectly still, with both teeth and lips closed, awareness pervades all ritual acts — this is the vajra-speech, free of audible sound.

Now as for the vajra-song-words: taking up songs such as the Ṣaṭpada and the like, and the joyful-village songs, when these are recited with vajra-words as ritual acts, this is known as the vajra-song.

Now the words of the vajra-wrathful ones are these: like the sound of thunder from the syllable Hūṃ, fine yet resonant with sound, the great vajra, the wrathful one made vivid — this is known as the word of the wrathful ones. This is the practice of the words.

Now this is the supreme practice of recitation to be performed:

For all ritual acts whatsoever,
and for whichever bodhisattva there may be,
by accomplishing their essence, mudrā, and mantra,
invite and accomplish their very identity.
All places and all living beings
are accomplished through all rites by one's own proper manner.
Vajra-recitation accomplishes all ritual acts.
The secret recitation of Vajrasattva arises:
the essence of Vajrapāṇi, together with the mudrā,
mantra, and awareness, likewise —
by reciting with the aim of recitation, the Protector is recited,
and all ritual acts are to be accomplished.
For the outer vajra families,
it is the equipoise of the wrathful ones.
The heart of all the vajra-queens
is to be grasped also with the mind.

Regarding what is called the aim of recitation: one meditates upon the desire of Vajrapāṇi and recites. Here, Oṃ is desire, and it is great bliss. Desire is what is called Vajrasattva. Desire is for the purpose of summoning, drawing in, binding, and bringing under control all things. Jaḥ Hūṃ Baṃ Hoḥ. Desire is supreme joy. Desire is the aim of recitation.

If one recites this secret recitation, it will be accomplished even with small merit, and even all the Tathāgatas are unable to deceive one in this. One performs offerings with the palms joined and cupped hollow. Therein, the flower offering is made by opening the cupped palms upward. The incense offering is made by extending them downward. The lamp offering is made with the thumbs folded level as a cover. The perfume offering is made by extending them out fully, either empty or filled. Through these mudrās, offering is made in truth to all buddha-fields, all maṇḍalas, all samaya-being bodhisattvas (samayasattvas), all essences, mudrās, mantras, awareness, and all deities.

This completes the excellent practice of offering.

Now there arises the excellent practice of accomplishment for sentient beings who lack merit. One draws whatever maṇḍala one wishes, as large as desired, each day within the span of four watches of the sun, then enters it properly, seals oneself with the great mudrā (mahāmudrā), and recites one's own highest deity one hundred times. This completes the excellent practice of accomplishment for sentient beings who lack merit.

Next there arises the excellent practice for swift accomplishment. The swift accomplishment of the Tathāgatas is as follows:

Giving Dharma through meditative absorption and the like,
Reading the texts of the vast tradition,
Abiding in faith and making offerings accordingly —
This is the great swift bestowal of attainment.

The swift accomplishment of Vajrapāṇi is as follows:

One who is not free from attachment to desire,
Who is venerated by the noble ones attached to desire —
Being attached to desire as much as one is able:
This is the swift bestowal of attainment.

The swift accomplishment of the great bodhisattvas is as follows:

Generosity, moral discipline, and likewise patience,
Diligence, meditative concentration, and likewise wisdom —
Whatever one is able to do,
Each day according to one's wishes.

The swift accomplishment of the outer vajra family is as follows:

Making offerings to the buddhas and likewise to the Tathāgatas,
Being steadfast in the conduct of Vajrapāṇi,
Always holding the vajra in hand,
And excelling at cutting down all who are ignoble.

Then there arises the excellent practice for swiftly accomplishing the attainment of the vajra women (vajraḍākinīs). It is as follows:

Being joyfully devoted to virtue for sentient beings,
Making offerings and guarding samaya vows,
Offering through the dance of mudrā,
And acting wrathfully toward wayward sentient beings.

This completes the excellent practice of swift accomplishment.

Now there arises the excellent practice of the ācārya. The practice of becoming an ācārya of the Tathāgata's maṇḍala is as follows. Within the maṇḍala, in the presence of the Tathāgata, one recites the essence mantra called Vajravidyā one thousand eight hundred times. Then the ācārya himself recites the samaya mudrā one thousand times. The essence mantra for conferring empowerment upon the student is this:

Vajrasattva, vajraratna, vajradharma, vajrakarma. Oṃ amukha mādhitiṣṭha sarva tathāgata āryadatte svāhā.

Reciting this one hundred times, one places the mudrā at the heart and so forth, and recites everything at the crown of the head. Thereby one bestows the ācāryaship of the Tathāgata family.

Taking the insignia of Vajrapāṇi at the crown, recite this essence mantra one hundred or one hundred and eight times:

Oṃ mahāsukha vajrasattva jaḥ hūṃ baṃ hoḥ surata stvaṃ.

Reciting it one hundred times makes one a vajra ācārya of the Tathāgata. Reciting it one hundred and eight times makes one a vajra ācārya of samādhi. The essence mantra is this:

Oṃ mahāsukha vajrasattva jaḥ hūṃ baṃ hoḥ surata stvaṃ.

Then one places the vajra in his hands. Next, for the vajra family: one places a vajra in both hands and confers empowerment with a vase mixed with the scented water of the vajra family. The essence mantra is this:

Oṃ vajra adhipadhitvaṃ abhiṣiñca mi dṛḍho bhava jaḥ hūṃ baṃ hoḥ hūṃ phaṭ.

Then the practice of establishing someone as ācārya of the lotus family: one places a lotus in his hands, recites one thousand eight hundred times, and binds his head with a garland of lotuses. One recites this essence mantra:

Oṃ padmamāle abhiṣiñca lokeśvara ratve svāhā hrīḥ.

The practice of establishing someone as ācārya of the vajra-jewel family: one places the mudrā of the vajra-jewel family at the place of empowerment of the student and recites this essence mantra one thousand times:

Oṃ vajraratna abhiṣiñca ratnadhipati tve bhava ṭaṭ.

The ācārya introduces the student into all the maṇḍalas and confers empowerment. For establishing one as a Vajrapāṇi ācārya, one draws the maṇḍala oneself and confers upon oneself the empowerment of ācāryaship.

The conduct of the ācārya is as follows:

The ācārya of the Tathāgata family: for one month he undergoes the going forth (pravrajyā) and takes up all the trainings, and he accomplishes his own chosen deity at the three times of day.

The conduct of the ācārya of the Tathāgata family: for half a month, each day before the Blessed One he performs the consecration and so forth, makes offerings to the image of Vajrapāṇi or to his own deity, and recites with unwearied voice, invoking with the syllable kyau, without distinguishing between times appropriate and times not appropriate for the path. Then the conduct of the Vajrapāṇi ācārya arises: each day, rising early in the morning, he performs the self-consecration and so forth, bathes, applies fine fragrance, wears bright garments, adorns himself with ornaments, and, endowed with song, instruments, graceful arrangements, and lovely display, makes perfectly complete the secret offerings to the Blessed One — flowers and so on — and then offers to the Blessed One and to himself with objects of desire. He recites at will the suchness of the great samaya, and for up to half a night he enjoys the five qualities of desire and likewise makes offerings and recites at will. Then again at dawn he relies upon the five objects of desire and recites. This is to be done for four days.

Then the conduct of the vajra family: for half a month, feigning madness, he pays homage to all the Tathāgatas and makes offerings to Vajrapāṇi. He touches his head to the ground, appears dishevelled — or may wear garments — shakes his head, scolds people and all beings, dances with the royal mudrās, while in mind he meditates upon the Tathāgatas. Having performed the binding of the boundaries and so forth, he makes offerings to the Blessed One in the proper manner and recites one hundred and eight times.

Then the conduct of the lotus family: he makes offerings to the Blessed One, drinks a full mouthful of cow's urine, and if he eats a little of cow's dung, all the pure forms of conduct are accomplished. Then for seven days he rests in meditative absorption and recites one thousand and eight times.

Then the conduct of the jewel family: each day he confers empowerment upon himself, makes offerings to the Tathāgatas and the great bodhisattvas, meditates visualizing himself as a king, goes to the royal palace, recites one hundred at a time before the king, and does so for eight days.

By this means the ācārya will attain all accomplishments, all excellences, all forms of knowledge, veneration by all beings and so forth, and all activities. This concludes the conduct of the ācārya.

Now, the accomplishment of activities: by reciting the essence mantra one hundred and eight times, all activities that are performed will be accomplished. By forming the great vajra mudrā and reciting the vajra recitation eight thousand times, for all activities — worldly and supramundane — the mere sealing with all the mudrās causes both oneself and others to accomplish their aims. If the mantra is recited one hundred thousand times with the vajra syllables, then by reciting all mantras together at once, all activities are accomplished. If a vidyā mantra is recited one hundred thousand times, then all vidyā mantras recited together at once accomplish all activities.

Now, the essence mantra, mudrā, mantra, and vidyā arise as follows. Vajrasattva is the essence mantra. Sattva-vajra is the mudrā. Oṃ is the mantra. Khiti is the vidyā.

Then arises the essence mantra for accomplishing all essence mantras, mudrās, mantras, and vidyās:

Oṃ mahāsukha vajrasattva jaḥ hūṃ baṃ hoḥ surata tvaṃ.

The Blessed One declared: if Vajrasattva recites this before the Blessed One along with song and the vajra syllables one thousand and eight times, all the accomplishments of essence mantras, mudrās, mantras, and vidyās will be achieved — so spoke the Blessed One Vajrasattva.

This concludes the teaching on the subsequent tantra from the King of the Great Contemplation of the Victor Transcending the Three Worlds.

Then the bodhisattva Vajrapāṇi addressed the Blessed One with these words: "Blessed One, I have explained these synonyms of the Dharma of the Victor Transcending the Three Worlds. Therefore, Blessed One, I pray that you grant a blessing so that the accomplishment which the Tathāgatas have attained — in the manner that, when going to other lands to tame sentient beings, beings do not become impoverished, or troubled, or jealous, or slanderous, or afflicted with suffering, or of little desire, or attached to desire, or minded to go forth, or subject to much harm, or subject to many faults, or sorrowful, or ill, or beset by many fears, and so forth, and do not lose faith — so that a Dharma of superior intention arises within them, and their obscurations are completely purified."

Then the Blessed One spoke to Vajrapāṇi thus: "You, the great samaya, the suchness, the vajra of body, speech, and mind in all respects — because you have taught in just this way this taming of the untamed, all the Tathāgatas grant their blessing."

Then the practitioners should first recite it eight thousand times in the manner prescribed, and thereafter accomplish either that same thing or others as well. Those beings who are to be tamed by the Dharma of freedom from attachment — such ones too will arise, and to them as well this essence mantra is given:

Oṃ bhu sarva tathāgata vajrasattva hoḥ samaya stvaṃ.

This too should be recited one hundred thousand times, and thereafter that same or other accomplishments should be pursued.

Then Vajrapāṇi bowed at the feet of the Blessed One and recited these verses:

For the welfare of all sentient beings,
the supreme contemplation has been fully taught.
Called "Victor of the Three Worlds,"
it grants the highest of all accomplishments.
By the mere utterance of it
one is fully liberated from all sins,
and is venerated throughout every world;
all accomplishments will be attained.

Those who inquire, recite, retain,
and have faith likewise —
even in this very life
will attain all accomplishments.
What need be said of those who practise
with firm faith and unbroken vows?
The buddha Vajrapāṇi himself —
even he attains it in this very life.

Whatever contemplation is applied,
whatever is done by oneself as well —
if one accomplishes the king of contemplations,
it will swiftly be accomplished even without faith.

For one who dwells in my own home,
since through it I too accomplish all things,
I stand watch over that one as protector.
Accomplishments and every desire,
increase and freedom from harm,
knowledge, long life, strength,
and a supreme form — these I shall bestow without fail.

Then the Blessed One rose from his seat, pervaded the entire extent of the three realms with the subtle vajra-dust of body, speech, and mind, and together with all the Tathāgatas gave the affirmation "Excellent!" He declared, "Excellent, excellent, O great bodhisattva!" and the Blessed One Vajrapāṇi was filled with manifest delight at what had been spoken.

The King of the Great Contemplation of the Victor Transcending the Three Worlds is complete.

It is said that the great translator Rinchen Zangpo made a translation of this text. Yeshé Gyatso of Zhalu, learned in the tantras of yoga, reviewed the words and meaning carefully and made corrections. Some also hold the view that it was translated by Ma Gewa Lö.

Oṃ ye dharmā hetu prabhavā hetuṃ teṣāṃ tathāgato hy avadat, teṣāṃ ca yo nirodha evaṃ vādī mahāśramaṇaḥ svāhā. Maṅgalam.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The phrase "slaying and so forth" as a method of taming is a well-attested tantric convention in which apparent violent action is understood as a compassionate wrathful means of liberation, not literal killing. The Tibetan term here carries this technical doctrinal sense and should not be read as straightforwardly violent.

The title of the text is the Trailokya-vijaya-mahākalpa-rāja, meaning "The Great King of Ritual Procedures, Victorious over the Three Worlds," a major tantra of the Vajrayāna corpus associated with Vajrapāṇi in his wrathful Trailokyavijaya form.

The beings listed — "scent-eaters" (gandharvas), "half-humans" (mahoragas or kiṃnaras), "flesh-eaters" (rākṣasas), "memory-stealers," and "mother-spirits" (mātṛkās) — are members of the standard retinue of worldly spirits enumerated in many tantric texts. The precise identification of "baljed" (memory-stealers) and "mamo" (mother-spirits) follows standard Tibetan tantric enumeration.  The term rendered "māmo spirits" translates "ma mo," a class of fierce female spirits in the Tibetan cosmological worldview, distinct from ḍākinīs; no Sanskrit equivalent is standard, so the Tibetan-derived gloss is retained.

"Power-weapons" renders "śakti" (a spear or lance associated with Śiva's iconography); keeping it in context as a weapon-type is appropriate here.

The phrase rendered "the one who wishes to be free" for "gtogs 'dod" is unusual. The text's own gloss equates it with "thar pa" (liberation), suggesting this is a theistic epithet being reinterpreted in Buddhist terms by Vajrapāṇi. This is a significant doctrinal move in the narrative: Vajrapāṇi refutes Śiva's self-identification by redefining his own epithets against him.  The instruction "do not disparage desire" within the verse is a characteristic Vajrayāna locution asserting the transformation of desire on the tantric path rather than its renunciation; it follows naturally in the context of a maṇḍala initiation. The phrase is rendered literally as the text intends it doctrinally.

The command "You are not to recite the words of such a mantra" addressed by the Blessed One to Vajrapāṇi is likely a ritual dramatic inversion: the Blessed One formally forbids what Vajrapāṇi then nonetheless proclaims — a conventional tantric narrative device establishing the mantra's transgressive power and ultimate authorization.  The passage contains a sustained ritual sequence of empowerment and name-bestowal involving a large cast of Indic cosmological figures. Several of the vajra-name renderings involve terms that are either technical epithets or obscure cult figures. In particular, "སྐྱ་སླེང" rendered as "Meteor" and "བེ་ཅོན་རྩེ་མོ" as "Razor's Point" are plausible contextual readings for atmospheric phenomena, but the exact referents in the Indic cosmological system are uncertain. "ཕག" rendered as "The Pig" likely refers to Vārāhī or a pig-faced deity associated with chthonic realms; the Tibetan is simply "pig." "སའི་སྤྱི་བོ" rendered as "Crown of the Earth" may refer to Pṛthivī or a related earth deity. The opening exchange about Māra and the Blessed One's response is doctrinally significant: it presents the antinomian teaching that desire is not to be abandoned in this tantric context, framed as the Buddha's skillful means for taming beings — a recurring theme in the text that the preceding passage established.  The mantra strings are rendered in transliteration as they appear in the Tibetan, preserving the Sanskrit phonetics without emendation, since critical edition collation of the exact mantra forms lies outside the scope of this translation passage.

The term rendered "Vajra Stout Lady" for Kubera (Tibetan ཐག་ཐུག་མ, "stout" or "plump one") is a feminized form applied to the wealth deity Kubera, who here appears as a mother figure receiving empowerment; this gender transformation is characteristic of the tantric context.

The final lines of verse are the opening of a longer technical section on maṇḍala construction with a measuring cord; the passage breaks off mid-description. "Acacia" renders སེང་ལྡེང (Skt. khadira), the thorny acacia tree traditionally specified in ritual texts for the central peg.  The passage contains two mantra strings rendered in transcription as they appear in the Tibetan phonetic transliteration of Sanskrit: Oṃ vajra pratīccha māṃ ha ha ha ha hi and Oṃ uru gra gra viriṇi viriṇi vajraMayahi bhidaye svāhā. The Sanskrit underlying these phonetic Tibetan renderings is not always recoverable with certainty, and the transliterations are left essentially as found with minor normalization.

The figure glossed as "the goddess who is not an affliction" renders a Tibetan phrase meaning literally "the non-affliction goddess," likely referring to a worldly goddess trampled underfoot by Vajrapāṇi in the standard iconographic scene where he subdues Śiva and his consort; her precise identity in this textual tradition is uncertain.

The term rendered "Vajra-Holder Woman" translates rdo rje 'dzin ma, the feminine form of Vajradhara, a tantric mudrā-personification or female principal invoked in this initiation context.  The verse passage beginning "Just as the sound of this bell is declared to be its impermanence" and continuing through "you have now in this very life renounced these" is syntactically compressed and admits more than one reading. The rendering given understands it as a teaching delivered by the master to the initiate: the bell's impermanence symbolizes the burning away of the initiate's prior commitments to faith, the three refuges, and vows, which are now superseded and renounced in favor of the vajra samaya. This is a doctrinally significant initiatory reversal and readers should be aware the Tibetan permits alternative interpretations.

The term translated throughout as "hooking, dispatching, binding, sealing" and similar corresponds to a standard set of tantric wrathful and pacifying action-categories; the list here is somewhat extended compared to the usual four-activity schema, and the translation follows the Tibetan enumeration closely.
