The Maṇḍala Rite Called the Arising of Glorious Heruka (Śrīherukabhūtanāmamaṇḍalanopikā)

The Maṇḍala Rite Called the Arising of Glorious Heruka, whose Sanskrit title is transmitted as Śrī Heruka Bhūta Nāma Maṇḍala Nopikā and whose Tibetan title is Dpal he ru ka 'byung ba zhes bya ba'i dkyil 'khor gyi cho ga, is a ritual manual for the maṇḍala of Heruka, the wrathful "Blood-Drinker" deity of the Highest Yoga Tantras. Opening with verses of homage that liken the dancing deity to a stainless ocean, it sets out in condensed form the procedures of his maṇḍala, and by its close it covers related rites such as the consecration of images, stūpas, books and temples, the commissioning of a temple's protector Mahākāla, feast gatherings, and the dismantling of the maṇḍala, drawing especially on the Hevajra Tantra. Its colophon attributes it to the Indian scholar Divākaracandra. It is Toh 1261 in the Tengyur, where it belongs to the tantra commentaries, among the ritual works of the Highest Yoga Tantra class.

In the language of India: śrī heruka bhūta nāma maṇḍala nopikā

In the language of Tibet: The Maṇḍala Rite Called the Arising of Glorious Heruka.

Homage to glorious Heruka.

Your feet, as they move about, are like waves rolling onward that are very hard to turn back;
Your arms, as they wave, are a dance of spreading waves, waves utterly pure;
The elephant and the rest in your hands are like creatures moving in the water, and your upswept hair is like fire;
Laughing like a garland of heads, O Blood-Drinker, stainless ocean, may your body grant protection!

Bowing my head to the glorious Blood-Drinker, endowed with omniscient knowledge,
Solely compassionate, who has abandoned every fault without exception,
The innate reality itself,
I shall describe his maṇḍala in condensed form.

Apart from the rite of the great maṇḍala,
The unsurpassed supreme accomplishment is not possible,
Nor are empowerment, consecration, recitation, practice and the like.
Therefore those who desire them should apply themselves to the rite.

Maṇḍala, samādhi, mudrā and mantra,
Activity, seat, recitation, fire offering,
Worship, and the withdrawal joined to the rites:
These are the ten principles.

The master knows the ten principles
And understands the application of mantra and tantra;
He is fully endowed with all the characteristics
And knows all the meaning of the treatises.
He has been empowered with all the empowerments,
Has received the guru's command and possesses good qualities;
He keeps the commitments and keeps the vows,
Is steadfast, and holds and knows the three vehicles.
He is not carried away by worldly concerns,
Is patient, and is compassionate by nature;
He is diligent in recitation and free of avarice,
And truly devoted to faith and love.
With the rite in its full completeness
He undertakes the work of the maṇḍala.
It is done for the sake of connection with merit and so forth,
And also for the sake of faith.
One should never disparage the maṇḍala
Nor perform the rite deficiently.
Even in this life the suffering is great,
And in the world beyond the danger is great.
Therefore, with all diligence,
One who desires accomplishment should do it.
Through one's own capacity in the rite,
Following the very system taught in the tantras,
By the merit of doing it in this way,
What cannot be seen by looking will come to be.
Through worshipping the maṇḍala
It will come to be swiftly.

Here, first, either students and others who wish for auspiciousness, having first offered a maṇḍala, request the master two or three times, or else the master himself, desiring merit and with the maṇḍala rite in mind, should first perform the preliminary approach. For this, in a virtuous and conducive place free of obstacles, the yogin, focusing through the yoga of Hevajra, should perform the three samādhis and recite the mantra of the Cakravartin King and the mantra of his own chosen deity one hundred thousand times each, and should recite the mantras of the others ten thousand times. Alternatively, one may also perform the maṇḍala through devotion to it by way of a vision of the lord. This was the preliminary approach.

In mountain caves and on riverbanks,
In charnel grounds, places secluded from people, and forests,
Or only where flowing water abounds,
Accomplishment comes swiftly, without doubt.
In towns, in houses and temples,
And in the dwellings of monasteries,
Accomplishments are attained only after a long time:
So the knowers of reality have said.

This was the explanation of the site.

On a good day and under a good star,
At the time of dawn, the intelligent one,
Who has mastered the characteristics of ground,
Should examine the ground thoroughly.
Ground where ciñcā or turmeric may be gathered,
A hill thickly and evenly covered with karañja, siddhārtha, kovidāra and karṇikā,
And ground shaped like the back of a tortoise or an elephant should be avoided.
With a pippala tree to the east,
With a plakṣa tree to the south,
With a nyagrodha tree to the west,
With a bakula to the north,
With a kakubha in the southeast, the direction of fire,
With a palāśa, it is said, in the direction of Nairṛti,
In the direction of wind a śālmali tree,
And in the direction of Īśāna an udumbara;
With aśoka, kandasukha, nimba and the siddha tree,
Tamāla, macokuca, mango and pomegranate,
And with a spring in the direction of water and the direction of Indra, the ground is auspicious.
Ground with the characteristic of being square should be thoroughly examined.
There, having dug out a square pit,
One should fill it completely with the earth that was dug out.
If the pit is not filled, it is inauspicious;
If the earth and so forth are left over, it is highly praised.

This was the thorough examination of the ground.

If one undertakes a maṇḍala
On ground with ciñcā and the like,
Strife, grief and loss of wealth
Will swiftly befall the patrons.
Ground like a tortoise's back, an elephant's back, or a basin
Brings death, sickness or decline.
Therefore, in such a place
The wise should not undertake a maṇḍala.
White ground evenly endowed
With aśoka and the like is most auspicious.
If there is a spring in the northeast,
The accomplishment is unsurpassed.
If the earth that was dug out does not fill the pit,
Everything will decline.
If the earth that was dug out fills the pit,
Everything will come about.

These were the characteristics of the ground.

Then, in the center of that highly praised ground, one sits on a comfortable, high seat. Being clean and fragrant, adorned with garments covering the whole body, and wearing a crown, one arranges all the implements pleasingly and performs the protection and the recitation. Taking vajra and bell in hand, the mantrin, in meditative equipoise, presents torma and offerings, and at the time of dawn performs the three samādhis. Through the rite that will be explained, or through a fire offering of incense, one dispels the obstructors and strikes them down with the ritual dagger, and one also visualizes the vajra fence, the vajra tent and so on. Then, for the protection of the students, one performs a pacifying fire offering. After that, one purifies a vast torma in accordance with the pith instructions and offers it, with the sound of drums, to the world protectors of all directions.

Then, on the following day, in the morning, the mantrin should perform deity yoga. Having thoroughly sprinkled the place with water consecrated by the mantra of all activities, one mentally invites Pṛthivī, the earth goddess, by means of the characteristics of transformation that will be explained, and worships her fully with the five offerings. One supplicates her with the verse beginning "Goddess, you," and with the mantra oṃ tiṣṭha bajra hūṃ one should set her in the middle of the sky.

Into the ground, to about the depth of the knee,
One should dig with the action vajra.
Having carefully observed the nāga Vāsuki,
One should then purify the ground thoroughly.
In the middle and last months of autumn
The head is set toward the sunrise and Īśāna;
The nāga lies facing south,
Resting firmly on its left side.
In the middle and last months of winter
Its head rests toward Yama,
And it faces west; its human form
Is the upper body, and the rest has the form of a nāga.
In the middle and last months of spring
It faces north,
Lying with its head toward the west:
This is the mighty nāga Vāsuki.
In the middle and last months of summer
The nāga lies on its side,
Its head pointing north,
And at all these times it faces east.
If one digs throughout
At the lower part of this nāga's back,
The accomplishment the patron desires
Will come about; of this there is no doubt.
If one digs at its head,
Father, mother, siblings and kin,
Children, grandchildren, strength and so forth
Will always be destroyed.
If one digs into the body at its back,
Positions are lost, or else there is death,
And for a long time one's former possessions,
Cattle, oxen, buffalo and the like are destroyed.
One should know the nāga's position correctly
And dig at this lower part.
Having gone all around it in circumambulation,
One gazes upon the renowned goddess of the earth.
The intelligent and learned one should examine it,
And then remove the buried foreign objects.
If those foreign objects are not removed,
Faults will be produced.
If bones are found lying together with all their limbs,
And charcoal among the foreign objects, they portend danger.
Ash brings loss of wealth and death; human bones,
Snakes and other foreign objects also bring harm.
Stones, dust, potsherds, nails, hair and kreta wood,
Bricks, filth, grease, thorns and the like:
Many foreign objects arise that bring bondage, wounding and so on.
Removing the foreign objects well clears away all afflictions without exception.
If, when it has been dug to the depth of the knee,
The ground has not become pure,
The mantrin should dig again to the depth of the waist,
Purifying it completely.
If these foreign objects should keep appearing
Without end,
Then by covering them with the hand and reciting the mantra,
By that alone the ground will become pure.

The mantra is oṃ hana hana krodha hūṃ phaṭ.

Then one should purify the ground with clay made with water mixed with the five nectars and consecrated by the syllable hūṃ, and cleanse it with water over which the mantra of all activities has been recited. One should place grains, jewels and medicines firmly in the center and make that ground perfectly level.

In one's own house, in a cowshed,
In a temple or on the site of a ruined house,
In a riverbed or on a landing by the ocean,
One does not purify, dig and so forth.
By stretching out the hand and reciting the mantra,
By that, it is said, the ground is purified.
By that the ground becomes purified,
And one's own mind is completely pure.

The mantra is oṃ rakṣa rakṣa hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ svāhā.

Then the site should be filled all around with a canopy and awning set within a multi-storied pavilion that has four doors and portals, with white yak-tail whisks, trees, banners and leaves, and it should be beautifully adorned with the finest silk. It should be purified so that it seems adorned in all its parts with garments variegated by fragrant flower garlands, it should be anointed with scents, and vessels of fragrant incense should be set at its four corners; and then one should again perform the fire offering and so on. Likewise one should imagine Pṛthivī, the earth goddess, abiding in the sky. This was the rite of purifying the ground.

After that, the vajra master, together with good companions, sits on a comfortable seat on the maṇḍala ground, which has been very well bounded, and offers torma, worship and so on. He meditates on the maṇḍala of his own chosen deity, and, in order to expel the obstructors, sprinkles the whole maṇḍala ground with scented water over which the mantra of all activities has been recited. Then, with the mantra oṃ bhū khaṃ, one should make it the nature of space. With hūṃ laṃ hūṃ one should meditate on the ground as having the nature of vajra atoms. One should consecrate it with oṃ medīni vajra bhava vajra satva hūṃ. This is the purification of the ground according to the pith instructions. This is the rite of inner purification.

After that, the master, having strewn fragrant flowers, sits in the center of the maṇḍala's ground, holding vajra and bell in his hands. To him, who possesses the yoga of the three samādhis and embodies all the tathāgatas, the students, holding vajra and bell and having visualized themselves as the four yoginīs, Gaurī and the others, stationed in their directions, should make supplication with four verses:

Peaceful one, all the tathāgatas,
Abode of all the tathāgatas,
Supreme of phenomena, selfless one,
Please explain the supreme maṇḍala.

Perfectly complete in every mark,
Having perfectly abandoned all that is not a mark,
Samantabhadra, supreme of body,
Please explain the supreme maṇḍala.

Supreme peace arising from the Dharma,
Engaging in the conduct of wisdom,
Samantabhadra, supreme of speech,
Guide, proclaim the maṇḍala.

Great mind of all sentient beings,
Pure by nature and free of stain,
Samantabhadra, supreme of mind,
Guide, proclaim the maṇḍala.

Then the mantrin accepts the supplication and generates the pride of all the buddhas and bodhisattvas, with these words:

I myself am the buddha, the awakening mind;
I myself am the unfailing Vajradharma;
I am also Vajrasūrya;
I myself am all the buddhas.

After that, with the mantra hūṃ bajrod tiṣṭha, one invites the wheel of the maṇḍala that has been meditated upon and sets it in the middle of the sky. Then, with a mind of great compassion toward the realm of sentient beings, which is shrouded by the darkness of the afflictions, one becomes fiercely wrathful toward the afflictions, and then one should rise.

Holding the syllable ṭa as the moon in the left eye
And the syllable ma as the sun in the right,
One gazes upon the various worlds
With the vajra gaze in every direction.
From the soles of the feet vajras blaze;
One slowly brandishes the vajra,
Dances with the crossed-vajra step,
And rings the bell at the stations.

baṃ hvaṃ sa yaṃ pra duṣṭo ghana hūṃ

Sounding the syllable hūṃ from the mouth,
One should fix them in place with fierce hostility and threatening gestures.
The mantrin should circle to the right
And go around with the wheel.

Therefore, in order to crush the obstructors,
One should recite this verse:
"Planets, bhūtas, piśācas and the rest,
You who dwell in body, speech and mind,
Whoever you are, whose bodies are foul,
All of you, go off in every direction!
Arisen from the wisdom of great bliss,
Of the very family of Vajrasattva,
I am the glorious power of compassion,
The one who applies the maṇḍala that afflicts.
If any of you lingers here,
Your body, by the blazing vajra
Born of the three secrets and sent forth,
Shall be ground to powder through and through."
Then, after that, one should see all the obstructors, without remainder, driven off by terror.

This is called the defining procedure for expelling the obstructors and taking possession of the ground.

Then, in order to pacify the obstructors, the mantrin should visualize in the center Amṛtakuṇḍalin, his body half emerged, fierce, utterly unbearable, like the light of the half-risen sun, and with this mantra, oṃ gha gha ghātaya ghātaya sarva duṣṭāṃ phaṭ phaṭ kīlaya kīlaya sarva pāpaṃ hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ vajra kīlaya vajra dhara ājñāpayati kāya vāka citta vajra kīlaya hūṃ phaṭ, one should drive in the pegs mentally, by the practice of sending it out in every direction.

Again, in order to pacify all the obstructors without remainder, one should see the guardians of the directions, Indra and the rest, each dwelling in his own direction together with their sons, their hired servants and their retinues, and also the others who create obstacles, as gathered together as one in the center of the maṇḍala ground; and one should drive in the pegs mentally with that same mantra of Amṛtakuṇḍalin.

This is the rite of fixing with pegs.

Then, seeing the maṇḍala ground as free of obstructors and as the blazing, keen light of the family, outside the place where the pegs have been fixed is what is called the vajra fence. One should visualize a red letter ra transforming into a sun disk; in its center is a black syllable hūṃ, which transforms, and from it arises a crossed vajra endowed with five rays. From that garland of rays, garlands of blazing vajras radiate and pervade the four directions; joined closely together, they form a beautiful square vajra fence. Pervading above, they form the vajra tent, and pervading below the fence, the ground whose nature is vajra. From both the fence and the tent, too, rays radiate in every direction and join together, binding the outer directions; and the sun and the crossed vajra, too, turn entirely into rays, so that the inside is left without any gap. Then one should visualize the boundary sealed far off on the outside.

This is the rite of the protection wheel, the vajra tent and the rest.

Then one should imagine in front the earth goddess, born from the syllable baṃ and arisen from it, her body half emerged, golden in color, holding a jeweled vase in her hand and adorned with fine garments. Having consecrated her with a verse, one says:
"O goddess, for the worship of the vajra wrathful ones,
You hold the power.
Earth, holder of many jewels,
I, so-and-so, shall draw the maṇḍala."
Urging her as many as three times,
One gives the earth purifying offerings, torma offerings and the rest,
Worships her equally with offerings,
And imagines her dissolving into that very ground.

Then the master, together with his companions, should anoint the maṇḍala with sweet fragrances and fine flowers, and make sprinklings. At the places of the deities, with the five nectars, one makes nine circles, and for the maṇḍala of Nairātmyā, fifteen circles. Then, having mentally invited the maṇḍala that dwells in the sky, one should settle it evenly upon the maṇḍala anointed with the five nectars. Having completed the vajra fence and the rest, together with the sealing of the boundary, one should properly worship that circle of the maṇḍala with the outer and secret offerings, praise it, and taste the nectar.

At the time of taking possession of the ground and of the preparation, at night, one should worship the maṇḍala in the maṇḍala house with music: with the very sweet strains of the tambura, the flute, the hand drum and the clay drum, with sweet songs, with the dances of graceful people that follow along, and with special auspicious verses.

Then, seated on a well-examined, very soft and comfortable seat, one should place on the left side in front a vessel of water for sprinkling and sipping, filled with fine flowers and water and thoroughly recited over. The yogin endowed with the yoga of the three samādhis should then perform the preparation of the vase. It should not be black; it should have a wide lip, a long neck and a roomy interior. It should be very well washed and fumigated with incense, and should contain barley, barley grains, māṣa beans, rice grains, sesame and the five grains; the five medicines, namely bṛhatī, kaṇṭakārī, white aparājitā, together with sahadevā; the five fragrances, namely saffron, aguru, grantika, sandalwood and pataṅga; and the five precious substances, namely gold, silver, copper, pearl and coral, and lapis. Water should be drawn, having been very properly set out, from a river or the like that has not been crossed by elephants, horses, oxen, donkeys and so forth, and it should not be allowed to touch any other vessel. With oṃ tatpe tatpe mahā tatpe svāhā one makes it maṇḍala water. Into the vase filled with water one plants the five neck ornaments, cūtaja, jambuka, bilva and tamālu, and its neck is wrapped with a pair of new cloths. It should be marked with the emblem of one's own deity resting upon a source of phenomena, and one imagines it made into the nature of jewels by the syllable baṃ. It should be furnished with a banner and a parasol, and on top one sets the visualized lord of the maṇḍala, linking it with a vajra wrapped in garlands of flowers.

One places the victory vase together with a fitting seat. Taking up with the left hand the vajra wrapped with flowers, and with the right hand taking up the rosary, the mantrin should consecrate it a hundred and eight times with the deity's own mantra, inserted between the three syllables.

Likewise, the other maṇḍala vases should be of a size slightly smaller than that, and the rite is performed in the same way. Visualizing them as marked with the emblems of their respective deities and having fitting seats, on the maṇḍala ground one should consecrate them up to seven times with the mantras of the maṇḍala deities, inserted between the three syllables and blessed by the maṇḍala deities. Then, offering water for the feet and the rest, one should worship with the five kinds of offerings, ring the bell, and offer praise.

Likewise the rite is performed for a further vase as well. For the sake of the secret of beings one generates Ghasmarī, and one should consecrate it up to a hundred and eight times with the mantra for all activities, inserted between the three syllables. Likewise one should offer the water for the feet, the praises and the rest. After that, in order to pacify the obstructors, one should give a torma. As for this preparation of the vases, the rite is to be performed at the three times of each day.

Others say that at the doors, beginning with the east, there are eight vases, bearing the names Victory, Auspiciousness, Accomplishment, Protection, Victory, Peace, Miraculous Power and Accomplishment; they should be consecrated seven times with this mantra, oṃ suṃ kṣuṃ yuṃ hūṃ phaṭ svāhā, and prepared in the same way.

For the maṇḍala of Nairātmyā, the fifteen vases of the goddesses are likewise visualized as marked with a curved knife, and each should be consecrated with its own mantra; the rest is as before.

This is the rite of preparing the vases.

Then, joining the palms, bowing the body and holding fine flowers, one says oṃ sarva tathāgata sulaliti bilasana miter nāma mi bhagabāna taṃ jaḥ hūṃ baṃ hoḥ pratīccha kusumāñjalin nātha hoḥ, casts the flowers, and should worship the circle of the maṇḍala.

Then, planting the right knee firmly on the ground and stretching out the right hand, the mantrin should wave fine incense. Imagining on the tongue a slender vajra born from the syllable hūṃ, one should entreat the lord of the maṇḍala and the others:

"Blessed One, supreme Wrathful Vajra,
King of knowledge mantras, I bow to you and praise you.
Protector whose very nature is compassion,
I wish to ask about the maṇḍala,
In order to protect the disciples
And to make offerings to the distinguished ones.
Therefore, O Blessed One,
It is fitting that you show me your kindness.
Buddhas who turn the wheel for beings,
Bodhisattvas who abide in the fruit,
And further, the deities of the mantras themselves,
The gods who protect the world,
And the bhūtas who have truly entered the teaching of the perfect Buddha
And are devoted to the teaching:
With your true vajra eyes,
Please regard us.
I, so-and-so, great vajra,
Wish to draw the maṇḍala of the Arising of Hevajra,
By the rite, as far as I am able,
In order to purify beings.
Embracing with love
Those disciples together with me,
It is fitting that you swiftly accomplish
Everything, together with the maṇḍala."

Thus one should entreat up to three times, and should worship and praise.

This is the rite of preparing the deities.

Then the mantrin, having come forth from the maṇḍala, should properly protect the disciple and prepare him.

One with good qualities, with faith in the Sugata,
Of good family and free of pride,
Endowed with complexion, form and youth,
Desiring the goal, outstanding in diligence,
Grateful, skilled in the arts, endowed with wisdom,
Well washed and adorned with garlands of flowers:
Yet even one without good looks or good qualities,
Even an inferior one, may be prepared.
In the maṇḍala rite, the four kinds of assembly
Are the very ones permitted:
Those who keep their own respective trainings
And whose nature is devotion to the Great Vehicle.
Some enter this maṇḍala
For the sake of accomplishing mantras;
Others desire merit;
Others again desire the world beyond.
Those who, when the world beyond is taught,
Have certain faith in it,
The wise should lead into the maṇḍala;
Not with the mind of the people of this world.
With a mind set on this life
One likewise does not reach the world beyond;
For a person who desires the world beyond,
The fruits of this life come along incidentally.
Having stated all of these in full,
One should make the disciple's heart the door of entry.
Coming forth by the vajra path,
Dwelling in the center of the lotus of self-awareness,
Having entered wholly into the heart,
By the tathāgatas, who have melted,
He should be visualized as washed, by the supreme mantrin.

At the door of the maṇḍala, the request:
"You who benefit sentient beings,
Great diligent one, you are my Teacher.
From the unbearable ocean of saṃsāra
Please draw me out into great bliss;
Please draw me out by the Great Vehicle.
The steadfast vehicle, great awakening,
Great protector, grant to me.
Grant to me that very samaya;
Grant to me the mind of awakening as well.
The Buddha, the Dharma and the Saṅgha,
The Three Jewels, grant to me.
Into the supreme city of great liberation,
Protector, please lead me."
Thus the disciple should make his request.

When the disciple, with respect, outstanding and disciplined,
Has made the request three times,
The master, having summoned the chief disciple,
Should speak these words:
"This Great Vehicle is loving kindness,
The conduct of mantra, the scripture of the rite.
To you who are vessels of the Great Vehicle
I shall explain it correctly.
The buddhas who arise in the three times,
Holders of the vajra of body, speech and mind,
Through the might and power of the vajra secret mantra
Attained incomparable wisdom.
By the application of the incomparable mantra
The hero Śākyasiṃha and the others
Vanquished the mighty
And unbearable great host of Māra.
Following the ways of the world, for beings
They turned the irreversible wheel.
Therefore, with a loving mind,
Do these things in order to attain omniscience."

Having recited these, and having performed the confession of wrongdoing and the rest, one should perform in detail the going for refuge to the three, and the rest.

"The Buddha, the Dharma and the Saṅgha,
The unsurpassed Three Jewels:
The vow of the yoga of the buddhas
I shall hold from this time on.
The three kinds of moral discipline of the training,
That of gathering virtuous qualities,
And the moral discipline of working for the benefit of beings,
I take up and hold firmly.
The vajra, the bell and the mudrā too
I shall hold correctly.
In order to give rise to the great Jewel family,
I shall also take up the masters.
Making it sacred, every day
I shall give the four kinds of giving.
The samayas that join me to the great Jewel family
Are dear to my mind as well.
The outer, inner and secret, the three vehicles,
The holy Dharma, I take up fully.
For the pure and supreme Lotus family,
Arisen from great awakening,
Possessing all the vows,
I take them up fully through that very thing.
For the arising of the great Karma family,
I shall perform acts of worship as far as I am able.
The unsurpassed mind of awakening,
Holy, I shall generate.
For the sake of all sentient beings
I shall take up all the vows.
Those who have not crossed over I shall carry across;
Those not freed I shall set free;
Those not consoled I shall console;
And I shall establish sentient beings in nirvāṇa."
Thus it is said twice, and three times.

For one who is not capable:
"I confess each and every wrongdoing;
I go for refuge to the Three Jewels;
I rejoice in the virtue of beings;
I hold in mind the awakening of a buddha."
With this one confers the going for refuge to the three, and the rest.

Then the master should visualize Nairātmyā, Khecarī and Bhūcarī in the hearts, crowns and throats of the disciples. Holding a vajra by its tip with the right hand and reciting the three-syllable mantra, he should touch the heart and the other places. On the crown he places a flower, and incense and a lamp are set in front; scent should be offered at the heart.

Then a tooth stick, twelve fingers long, from a tree with milky sap such as the banyan, the udumbara or the aśvattha, not eaten by insects, not burnt, not fallen, without blemish and not crooked, is smeared with urine and a garland of flowers is tied at its tip; this is done with oṃ a ji hvā saṃ barta ni hūṃ phaṭ. Having recited this over the stick seven times, one should give it to him.

Then the disciple, facing east or north, smears with cow dung a patch of ground the size of an ox hide, and at the command of the guru chews the stick from its tip and should then cast the tooth stick away. However the tooth stick falls when cast: if it falls easily with its tip turned toward him,
Know that to be the supreme accomplishment;
He will become skilled in speech.
Likewise, toward the east the accomplishment
Is well known to be middling.
Likewise, toward the north the accomplishment
Is worldly, and of the animal kind.
When the tooth stick is cast,
If its tip faces downward,
That is accomplishment above and below;
If it falls outside, there is no accomplishment.

Then one should give him three palmfuls of water to drink, consecrated with this mantra: oṃ hrī biśuddha sarva dharmāḥ pāpaṃ nicasya saṃśodhaya sarva bikalpāṃ apanaya hūṃ.

Then three threads, recited over with the mantra for all activities, are twisted together and three knots are tied, and for the protection of the disciples they should be bound on the right arm. After that, a single stalk of kuśa grass recited over with the mantra for all activities should be given as a pillow, and likewise many more stalks of kuśa should be given for a mat.

Then one teaches the Dharma and has them listen:
"The coming of the Omniscient One into the world
Happens only rarely;
Like the udumbara flower,
It happens only rarely.
Training in the vehicle of mantra
Is rarer to come by even than that.
Practice it, so that you are able, without turning back,
To bring about the incomparable benefit of beings.
Whatever wrongdoing one has done before
Over many koṭis of eons,
By seeing a maṇḍala such as this
All of it is exhausted.
Abiding in the vehicle of secret mantra
And reciting the mantra of the protectors,
One reaches the unsurpassed state;
What need is there to speak of boundless fame?
These cut off the bad destinies
From which all sufferings arise.
Whoever, through this supreme conduct,
Has a mind that is utterly stainless:
Then, great being, you
Have gained an incomparable gain.
You who, in this teaching,
Are fully embraced by all the Victors and their heirs
Are born as great beings.
Therefore you will be born
In excellence in the Great Vehicle.
By this glorious supreme path
The supreme vehicle comes about.
That which you will travel
Is the state of a tathāgata itself:
Since it passes beyond existence and nonexistence,
It is free of stain like space,
Profound, and beyond the examination
Of logicians, without location.
Free of conceptual elaborations,
It is elaborated by elaborations.
By abiding in the scriptures of the Great Vehicle,
Through such as this one will attain the Dharma."
So it is said.

Then, with a virtuous intention, one has them rise and says: "Since the maṇḍala is to be drawn, you should go to your own places and report whatever you see in your dreams." Protecting them well with this mantra for all activities, oṃ rakṣa rakṣa hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ svāhā, one has them lie down there.

This is the preparation of the disciples.

Then the vajra master, when everything is complete, recites the mantra for all activities or the root mantra for as long as he wishes, and should lie down in the lion's posture. Then at dawn he should ask about their dreams, in which signs were seen.

Having heard whether they are good or bad, one should speak without hesitation.

Seeing the Buddha in a dream,
Seeing images and stūpas,
Listening to the Dharma, and companions in it,
Seeing monks and the Saṅgha,
Temples, heirs of the Buddha and nuns,
Mountains, staying in pleasure groves, and shrines,
Rivers, ponds and heaps of curd,
Lions, the kings of beasts, and antelopes are praised;
Excellent birds marked with wheels,
Seeing oneself in dreams ever entering meditative concentration,
Dwelling on water and on fine mountains,
Thinking oneself exalted, dwelling in the sky,
Laymen, and diligent kings,
Mantrins, lords and fair young women,
Father, mother and guru gone to the higher realms,
Supreme goddesses and the holy secret,
Supreme practitioners endowed with good qualities, and brahmins,
The great assembly of the maṇḍala of the Victors,
Being adorned with clothes, flowers in full bloom,
Performing a fire offering, fruits and flowers,
Being empowered as king and gaining a kingdom,
Victory in battle, oneself slaying a thief,
Being freed from sinking in the ocean,
And escaping from abysses hard to cross.

Dream signs such as these are said to be auspicious, and those contrary to them inauspicious. The mantrin, knowing that from the auspicious come accomplishments and from the inauspicious no accomplishments, should, if what was seen is inauspicious, recite the mantra of Vighnāntaka. Holding that all phenomena are like a dream and are attained as in a dream, devoted to that, and for the sake of accomplishment, one meditates on the pacification of the bad dream.

Then the yogin should perform the three samādhis of Hevajra, and, in order to prepare it and to invite the maṇḍala of the deities, should fix it in mind in the sky.

Then take new thread, either found in a charnel ground or spun by one who keeps the samaya, dyed separately with powders prepared in white, yellow, red, green and black, of fine scent and color. These are Vairocana, Ratnasambhava, Amitābha, Amoghasiddhi and Akṣobhya, arisen from the syllables bhrūṃ āṃ jṝṃ khaṃ hūṃ, having the nature of the mirrorlike wisdom, the wisdom of equality, the discriminating wisdom, the wisdom of accomplishing activity, and the wisdom of the utterly pure dharmadhātu. Since the mirrorlike and the others each follow the other four wisdoms as well, the measuring cords are divided into twenty-five. One should place them in the hand in order and twist them together.

Then one sees all the tathāgatas abiding in their own directions gathered together as the five tathāgatas, Vairocana and the rest, and in their hearts one sees the wisdom cord, whose nature is light rays, arisen from the seed syllables bhrūṃ and the rest. With this, oṃ a śva śva ta bajra sūtra me pra ya ccha mahā maṇḍala sūtra ya na hūṃ, one should make the request to Vairocana. Likewise, changing the name, one should make the request to Ratnasambhava and the rest.

Then, having become established in the yoga of Hevajra, the master should visualize in his two eyes a sun and a moon arising from the syllable ja. With blazing, moving eyes and the gaze of blazing eyelashes, he hooks and invites the wisdom cord, and imagines that the Blessed One urges it on and sends it. Placing the measuring cord in his hand, he inserts each into its own color. Together with the disciple endowed with the yoga of Ghasmarī, the two holders of the cord stand facing each other, and saying this, "All phenomena abide in one another; all phenomena abide one after another; all phenomena abide most thoroughly, hūṃ", they should twist the measuring cord.

Then the measuring cord, twisted and made one in this way, is twice the maṇḍala in cubits, and its thickness is one twentieth of a door. Then the mantrin moistens it with the five nectars mixed with scented water, places the three syllables in its core, sets it in a vessel of gold or the like anointed with scent, worships it with the five kinds of offerings, places it in the hand, and should guard it fully by reciting the syllable hūṃ.

Then again the mantrin anoints the maṇḍala with scent and scatters flowers. Visualizing the maṇḍala of Hevajra residing in the center of the sky, one offers purifying offerings, tormas and the rest, and should present offerings. Then the yogin, endowed with the vajra yoga of his own self, visualizes the practitioner in the north in the form of Ghasmarī, and should imagine in his right and left eyes a sun and a moon with yellow rays arisen from the syllable ja.

Then the mantrin, standing in the west and facing east, should hold the measuring cord with his left fist level with the navel, and the practitioner in the north should also hold it there. He urges it on with the three syllables jaḥ jaḥ jaḥ, and the practitioner in the north should draw it in with the three syllables jaḥ jaḥ jaḥ.

Likewise, with the companion seated in the east facing west, one says oṃ bajra samaya sūtra mātikrama hūṃ, takes up the cord with the thumb and forefinger of the right hand holding the vajra, makes the sound of a finger snap, and should strike the Brahmā line in space, level with the direction at the height of the navel, with all directions even; likewise one should strike the lower line.

Then again, with the sound of the line, "Here, for the benefit of beings, your time has come," one should urge on all the tathāgatas abiding throughout all the realms of space.

Then, moving round in circumambulation, one sits in the south facing north, while the disciple sits in the north facing south, and together with the companion one should likewise strike the Brahmā line, and likewise strike the line below as well. Then, with one seated in the fire corner facing north and one seated in the corner of Īśāna facing south, together with the companion one should strike the line of the eastern section. Then, with one seated in the wind direction facing south and one seated in the corner of Nairṛti facing north, together with the companion one should strike the line of the western section.

Again, moving round in circumambulation, with one seated in the wind corner facing east and one seated in the corner of Īśāna facing west, together with the companion one should strike the line of the northern section. Then, with one seated in the fire corner facing west and one seated in the corner of Nairṛti facing east, together with the companion one should strike the line of the southern section. Again, seated in just the same way, with one seated in the wind corner, one should strike the diagonal line. Then, with one seated in the corner of Nairṛti and one seated in the corner of Īśāna, together with the companion one should strike the diagonal line.

Then, at the middle of the four directions, on both sides of the Brahmā lines, one should strike the lines for the areas of the doors, measuring one eighth of the square circle. Then, outside the square root line, one should strike the line for the colored powders, measuring one quarter of a door. Outside that, measuring half a door, is the area of the vedikā. Then comes the line for the colored powders. Then, at the measure of the vedikā, come the lines for the band of nets and half nets and of suns and moons. Then one should strike the line for the area of the colored powders. Then on both sides of each of the four doors one should strike three lines for the areas of colored powders, and at the measure of a door one should strike the line for the area of the deities' ledge. Then, on both sides of the doors, leaving out a portion the size of the colored powder area, one should strike the lines for the archways and pillars at the size of the colored powder area. In this way the height is three times a door, and above the archways one draws in whatever way is beautiful.

Then, folding that same measuring cord double, one sits in the center of the maṇḍala, takes up the peg of acacia wood for fixing in the maṇḍala, fixes it firmly, and ties the measuring cord to it. Together with the practitioner on its north side, beginning from the direction of Īśāna and moving round in circumambulation, one should strike two circular vajra lines on the outside, beyond the maṇḍala. Again folding that same measuring cord double, beginning oneself from the direction of Īśāna and moving round in circumambulation, one should likewise strike two circular vajra lines inside the maṇḍala. Then, moving round in circumambulation, one should strike the two lines of the circle of light called the enclosure, so that the directions come out even.

For the maṇḍala of Nairātmyā, one should likewise strike the lines of eight pillars at even portions, corresponding to the eight circles. To set the wheel pillar of the eastern section, one should strike it seated in the southern section and facing north. The line of the lotus pillar of the western section is struck seated in the northern section and facing south. The line of the sword pillar of the northern section is struck seated in the western section and facing east. The line of the jewel pillar of the southern section should be struck seated in the eastern section and facing west.

If there is no practitioner on the north side to join in striking the lines, then one may strike them alone, without a companion, fixing one's own measuring cord with a peg.

At the time of striking the lines, one should also examine the signs:
Bad signs mean no accomplishment.
If the measuring cord breaks, the master declines;
If it falls short or slack, illness and the like;
If one is confused about the directions, the disciple goes astray.
Some say that these signs come from striking the two Brahmā lines. Others say that they come from striking the Brahmā lines, the direction lines and the diagonal lines. Some declare that they come from striking all the lines.

After that, beginning from the long root line, one should wipe away the four lines in the middle of the doors. Then, keeping the first line of colored powder on the side of each door, the third. Then the two lines of door measure on both sides of the upper covering. Then one should wipe away the two lines on each side, right and left, of the neck below the vedikā. Then the two lines each, at half a door's measure, below the lintel. Then one should wipe away the two lines each, at the measure of the vedikā, on the sides of the cheeks of the directions, and the remaining seven lines lying on the colored powder above the lintel.

Then that maṇḍala, laid out with lines and adorned with the proportions of its shape just as it is, should be visualized both outside and inside:
Four-cornered, with four doors,
Adorned with four archways,
Endowed evenly with the four vehicles,
Adorned with silk and garlands of flowers,
Endowed evenly with five lines;
The supreme maṇḍala, with banners on high,
Endowed evenly with bells.
All the portions of the corners,
At the corners of the doors and the rest,
Are jewels marked with vajras;
They are to be made in a single tier.
For Nairātmyā it is just the same.
Again, inside the wheel,
At half the size of the outer wheel,
Perfectly round all about,
Adorned with wheel pillars and the rest,
Beautified with vajra garlands and the rest,
Furnished with yak tail whisks and mirrors,
And spread over with a canopy.

Having thus laid out with the colored cord the maṇḍala whose nature is the dharmadhātu, one requests the measuring cord to depart with the three syllables ending in muḥ, and it should be placed in a clean vessel. Then the vajra master, seated in the maṇḍala, generates a sun and a moon in his right and left eyes with the syllable hī, should look all about with a wrathful gaze, and should open the four doors with his mind.

This is the rite of striking the lines.

Then the colors white, yellow, red, green and black, made from jewels, from iron or conch, or from śāli rice and millet, or from charnel ground bricks, charcoal or human bones, or from leaves of the plant called thief, are well ground, fumigated with incense, and placed separately in clean vessels. Generating the syllables bhūṃ āṃ jṝṃ khaṃ hūṃ, one places the colored powders in front. As before, one sees them blazing with the rays of the wisdom of the tathāgatas Vairocana and the rest, draws these in with a hook, makes them one with the nature of wisdom, sees them blazing, and consecrates them with the mantra oṃ bajra cītra samāya hūṃ.

Then, saying bajra bega, one mentally removes the wisdom lines that rest upon the maṇḍala and draws them into the center. With the syllable hūṃ one pulls out the peg that stands at the hub of the maṇḍala, and contemplates it as the vajra essence resting in one's own hand. The place where the peg was pulled out is filled evenly with the five colors. Then, in order to dispel bad omens, with the sound of the bell and with vajra songs, one says:

This dharmadhātu is utterly pure,
the liberator of the realm of sentient beings,
the great king called Hevajra,
the abode of all the tathāgatas,
utterly freed from every fault.

Thus one generates the divine pride. Then the yogin whose nature is method and wisdom takes up the powders one at a time with the left fist and, beginning from the direction of Īśāna and starting from the outside, should draw the colors white, yellow, red, green and black, going around.

It is also said:

The white comprises the nature of diamond;
the next is adorned with gold;
the next comprises the nature of ruby;
the next, likewise, is like emerald;
black is to be known as within.
This is the proper sequence of the colors.

And likewise:

As the line is laid down,
even, not crooked, and unbroken,
by a twentieth part of the door
the borders come to the measure of a barleycorn.

Then the mantra practitioner, in meditative equipoise, should examine the signs:

If it falls too thick, it turns to fever;
if too thin, wealth declines;
if crooked, or if the sky line is broken,
master and disciple will die.
If the colored powder is let fall without circling,
it must be laid down again correctly.
Otherwise it turns out auspicious,
and master and disciple are greatly exalted.

Then, reaching up to the line of the central maṇḍala, the eastern portion of ground is white; the southern portion of ground should be made yellow; the western portion of ground is red; the northern portion of ground is green; and the central portion of ground is black. In its center, on the pericarp of a red eight-petaled lotus, one should draw a skull cup, white outside and red within, facing upward and resting on a sun disk, marked in its center with a crossed vajra. For the two-armed and six-armed forms of Hevajra, it is a black vajra radiating light. On the eastern and the other petals, in their proper order, are skull cups without the crossed vajra. At the eastern door, upon a moon resting on a lotus, is a black curved knife; at the southern door, a small drum the color of madder; at the western door, a golden tortoise; at the northern door, an emerald-colored snake; in the northeastern corner, the corner of Īśāna, a sapphire-colored lion; in the southeastern corner, the corner of fire, a monk like crystal; in the southwestern corner, the corner of Nairṛti, a sky-blue wheel; and in the northwestern corner, the corner of wind, a multicolored vajra. These should be drawn upon moons resting on the pericarps of red eight-petaled lotuses. For these emblems one should again draw the seats of the four māras, Brahmā and the rest, either equal or unequal.

Then one who wishes to draw the maṇḍala of Nairātmyā should draw a red eight-petaled lotus at the heart of that maṇḍala. On its pericarp, upon a moon, one should draw a black curved knife. Likewise, on the eastern and other petals of the lotus there are four curved knives; in the northeastern and other corners of the outer ledge, again four; and at the eastern and other doors, again four. As for those at the eastern and western doors, two curved knives should be drawn on the two sides, upon moon disks resting on lotuses.

Moreover, at the times of entering upon and withdrawing from the work of drawing the colored powders, one should mentally remove the vajra lines; one does this after saying bajra bega. To do otherwise is a breach of the sacred commitment. Likewise, the Compendium of Reality (Tattvasaṃgraha) says:

The vajra lines are mentally set aside;
otherwise the sacred commitments
of entering and of withdrawing
are broken by them.

Then, in the directions and intermediate directions of the maṇḍala, one should draw the eight charnel grounds, strewn with jackals, vetālas, corpses, bhūtas and the like. In the east is the charnel ground called the Fierce and Ferocious Mountain Cave, marked by roasting. In the south is the Terrifying, filled with chopped pieces. In the west is Karaṅka, marked by corpses cast away. In the north is the Variously Blazing, marked by impaling with spears. In the northeastern corner, the corner of Īśāna, is Aṭṭahāsa, the Laughter of Ha, marked by binding. In the southeastern corner, the corner of fire, is the Glorious Forest, with its half-burnt corpses. In the southwestern corner, the corner of Nairṛti, is the Unbearable Darkness, filled with half-severed pieces. In the northwestern corner, the corner of wind, one should draw the one resounding with the cry gīlikīla, filled with the digging of pits.

Then, in proper order, one should draw the series of trees: śirīṣa, cūta, kakoli, aśvattha, pata, karañja, latāparkaṭi, and sardul. At the tops of the trees one should draw the lords of the trees together with their retinues. Of these, the hawk-faced one is surrounded by a host of hawks; the jackal-faced one is surrounded by a host of owls; the dog-faced one is surrounded by a host of dogs; the boar-faced one is surrounded by a host of boars; the cat-faced one is surrounded by a host of jackals; the tiger-faced one is surrounded by a host of tigers; the vulture-faced one is surrounded by a host of rākṣasas; and the deer-faced one is surrounded by a host of vetālas.

At the roots of the trees one should draw the guardians of the directions together with their retinues and sons, bowing with palms joined, or else without that. Of these, Indra is yellow in color, wears a jeweled diadem, holds a vajra, is adorned with fine garments, and rides a white elephant. Yama is black in color, with red eyes, his eyebrows and hair bristling upward; he holds a staff and a spear and rides a buffalo. Varuṇa is white in color, with seven serpent hoods; he holds a nāga noose and a conch and rides a makara. Kubera is golden in color and wears a jeweled diadem; he holds a hook and a staff and rides a śarabha.

Īśāna is white in color, the top of his crown of matted locks marked with a moon. He has three eyes and is adorned with a garland of skulls. In his four hands he holds a small drum, a skull cup, a trident and a khaṭvāṅga, and he rides a bull. Agni, the god of fire, is red in color, with a topknot of hair, and wears the sacred thread. In his four hands he shows the gesture of fearlessness and holds a rosary, a long-necked flask and a staff, and he rides a goat. Nairṛti is blue in color, his tawny hair standing upright, adorned with a garland of skulls. He holds a sword and a skull cup and rides a corpse. Vāyu, the god of the wind, is smoke-colored, holds a wind banner, wears a jeweled diadem, and rides a deer. These too should be drawn surrounded by hosts of gods, hungry ghosts, nāgas, yakṣas, bhūtas, sages, rākṣasas and winds.

On their left sides one should draw the eight nāgas with their hoods. Of these, Vāsuki is like the moon and has a blue utpala on the crown of his head. Padma appears like a conch, his hood marked with stars. Karkoṭa is green like dūrvā grass, his throat marked with three lines. Takṣaka is like the bandhūka flower, the crown of his head marked with a svastika. Mahāpadma is like the kunda flower, the crown of his head marked with a spear. Huluhulu is like collyrium, the crown of his head marked with a lotus. Kulika is smoke-colored, his throat marked with a half moon. Śaṅkhapāla should be drawn golden in color, his throat marked with a full moon.

Behind them one should draw clouds. Of these, the Thunderer is black in color; the Coiling is blue; the Unbearable is the color of saffron; Ghurṇita is multicolored; Ghana is white; the Wrinkled is yellow; the Raining is the color of drusara; and Candana is the color of collyrium. They should be drawn in this way. In other respects, one should lay out the wheel so that it is harmonious and beautiful.

This is the rite of laying down the colored powders.

If by chance, at the time of drawing the maṇḍala, someone quarrels and stirs up obstructing spirits, or obstruction arises for oneself, then, by the rite explained earlier, one should drive in pegs upon Indra and the others whom one has visualized, and so pacify the obstructors. If rain falls and the work of Māra arises, then one should cover the maṇḍala with charnel-ground cloth and, abiding as the wrathful one in the yoga of Hevajra, recite the mantra oṃ ārya ārya samasāna priyāya hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ svāhā. Looking upward, one should imagine that a mass of garlands of the blazing fire of wrath is sent forth, filling the expanse of the sky and burning all the clouds to ash.

Then, if one wishes to pacify the fire, one should visualize above the fire a white water maṇḍala arisen from the syllable baṃ. On it, on the pericarp of a multicolored lotus, is a white eight-spoked wheel arisen from a white bhūṃ, and the syllable bhūṃ in its center transforms into Vairocana, seated upon an autumn moon, his heart blessed by the syllable bhrūṃ. One should visualize this Vairocana as making streams of water pervade the ten directions, waters like those of the four oceans, the four great rivers and Lake Anavatapta, issuing from garlands of masses of clouds of light rays from the seed syllable at his heart and filling the whole expanse of the sky.

If one wishes to pacify the wind, then, through the yoga of one's own deity or the yoga of Vajrahūṃkāra, one should make an image of the wind god from dung that has fallen on a road, or from the earth of an anthill, consecrated by reciting the syllable hūṃ a hundred thousand times. One visualizes the body of the wind god, arisen from the syllable yaṃ, in the middle of the sky, summons him mentally, makes him enter the image and binds him there. Then one recites one hundred and eight times over a peg of human bone four finger-widths long and drives it into the image. Likewise, to pacify obstructors of water, one should drive it into the water.

Or else, following the sequence described before, carrying out the rite with the vajra fence and the rest as far as the vajra fence of the ground, one should gaze ahead with the wrathful gaze and recite the root mantra, which is: oṃ aṣṭānanāya biṃgalordhvakeśabartmani caturbiṃśatinetrāya ṣoḍaśabhujāya kṛṣṇajīmūtavapuṣe kapālamālānekādhariṇe ādhmatakrūracittāya ardhentudaṃṣṭṛni oṃ māraya māraya kāraya kāraya garjjaya garjjaya tarjjaya tarjjaya śoṣaya śoṣaya saptasāgarāna bandha bandha nāgāṣṭakāna gṛhṇa gṛhṇa śatrūṃ ha hā hi hī hu hūṃ he hai ho hau haṃ ha phaṭ svāhā.

Or else, to pacify all obstructors, one should write the names of the obstructors with the juice of arka leaves, poison, mustard seed and citraka, recite the mantra oṃ he bajra jvara jvara śatrūṃ naṃ bhrūṃ hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ svāhā ten thousand times, and wrathfully burn them in a fire of rice husks. If even then the obstructors are not pacified, one should make an image of the obstructor out of poison, salt, mustard seed, charnel-ground ashes and charcoal, pounded together with human-bone powder and the juice of datura. One kindles a charnel-ground fire on the dung of donkeys, camels and the like, cuts the image into pieces with a sharp weapon, and, with the mantra oṃ sarba bighnāṃ māraya ghu, with an angry and menacing gaze and in the stance with the left leg extended, burns the obstructors as before. Or else one trains in seeing all phenomena as mere conceptual thought, and through their intrinsic nature and selflessness one reduces all obstructors to ash.

This is the rite of pacifying the obstructors.

Then, when the colored powders have been laid down completely, the master, bathed and anointed with fragrance, his body dressed in fine garments and wearing a diadem and flowers, enters the maṇḍala and sits on a very soft and high seat. Having taken up the yoga of the deity, he dismisses the remaining colored powder with the syllable muḥ at the end of the three syllables, and the vessel of colored powder is set down in a place free of people. The ground outside the maṇḍala should be smeared with dung and sprinkled, provided with a fence of flowers, and fumigated with incense such as khanṭabinṭa, and blazing garlands of lamps should also be set out.

Then the victory vase, which is to be placed either outside or inside the maṇḍala, should be filled with lijaṃ and the like and set in the eastern or the southern part. The four vases of white and the other colors should be set in order in the eastern and other directions outside the maṇḍala. Further, the vases of Pukkasī and the others should be set in the northeastern and other corners, beginning with the corner of Īśāna. The vase for all activities should be set on the side of the eastern door of the maṇḍala. The eight door vases should be set on the sides of the eastern and other doors of the maṇḍala's palace. For the maṇḍala of Nairātmyā too, the victory vase should be set out as before. The fourteen vases of Vajrā and the others should be set out beginning from the east, according to their positions or however is beautiful. The rest is as before. Then one takes a little water from each of the goddesses' vases and places it, together with the five nectars, in the lotus vessel.

This is the rite of setting out the vases.

Then the mantra practitioner, facing the maṇḍala and having gathered the requisites, should worship and accomplish the maṇḍala according to the rite with a joyful mind. The sequence is this. Together with an outer consort sixteen years old if one is available, or with a visualized consort if not, the yogin endowed with the three samādhis invites, by the sound of that lotus, all the tathāgatas dwelling in the ten directions. They enter his own heart and melt, and he should meditate that they come by way of the vajra path to rest in the center of the lotus of wisdom. Then, through the practice of emanation, he emanates them into the expanse of the sky. Having accomplished the welfare of sentient beings, he again invites them with his mind and sets them in the sky beside the maṇḍala. Then, with the sound of hūṃ of the Eight-Faced One, he should drive off the kinds of obstructors that came along with them, and he should also fully protect his own maṇḍala.

Then, having given the purifying torma and the rest, he invites the deities in order with jaḥ hūṃ baṃ hoḥ, and makes them enter the emblems placed on the maṇḍala, binds them there and brings them under his power. Then he blesses the eyes, the body and the rest, and resolves that the aggregates and the rest are utterly pure. Again he offers the purifying torma and the rest, worships them perfectly with outer and secret offerings by the eight offering goddesses sent forth from his own heart, and offers the two garments to each one. He should praise the lord of the wheel with this verse:

You who delight in gazing on things manifold and variegated,
who rejoice in kisses and embraces manifold and variegated,
who take deep pleasure in feasts of bliss manifold and variegated,
who show forth the great vow manifold and variegated,
who abide in the sacred commitments of the things of the world:
please arise for the whole world, moving and unmoving!
You are encircled by the host of yoginīs, full of faith;
having attained the supreme, your mind is pervaded by bliss.
There, purify the whole world, moving and unmoving;
with a heart of compassion, turn the thoughts of beings toward awakening.
Through the delight of the glorious Blood-Drinker Vajra,
those beguiled by illusion fully enter into the vajras.

Thus he praises. Then he should purify the five nectars in the lotus vessel according to the oral instructions and sprinkle them. He meditates that the deities of the maṇḍala, together with the principal, are sated by that nectar and satisfied with bliss. Then, in their order one by one, beginning with the mantra of the lord of the maṇḍala, he should recite the mantras of all the deities and so please them completely.

Then, if one is available, the mantra practitioner should station a practitioner of awakening in the maṇḍala to keep other people from entering, and should go outside. In a place with white yak-tail whisks, pennants, victory banners and flags, with song, dance, the sound of music and the like, he sits on a comfortable seat. In front of him, a great copper vessel should be filled with pastries and the like, fish, meat, porridge, curd, milk, honey, liquor, and śāli rice mixed with sesame, and with various foods to chew and to eat, drinks, and things to lick and to suck; and with five lamps, nectar, offering substances, parasols, victory banners and garlands of lamps. It should be filled with other substances too. If these are not available, he should imagine that everything is filled by mere porridge and water. Looking at the torma before him, he should purify it in stages according to the rite, through the blazing and the rest, and through the nature of the five wisdoms.

The sequence for this is as follows. In the sky one visualizes a blue vajra arisen from the syllable hūṃ, its tip turned upward. Beneath it is a red skull cup arisen from the syllable a, its opening turned upward. In the middle of that skull cup one sees the torma, blessed by oṃ, transformed into the form of the five nectars. Then, from the joining together of the vajra and the skull cup, fire arises: this is mirror-like wisdom. Its heating is the wisdom of equality. Its melting from that heat and blazing with light is discriminating wisdom. Its being made the nature of wisdom and a sun disk is the wisdom that accomplishes activities. By its light rays one sees the forms of all the tathāgatas without exception who dwell in the world systems of the ten directions. With those same rays one draws the nectar of wisdom from their wisdom seeds, pours that nectar in, and makes it of one taste: this is the wisdom of the utterly pure dharmadhātu. Having thus purified it with the five wisdoms and made it the supreme nectar, one should bless it with the three syllables.

Then, in the sky in front, one visualizes a red eight-petaled lotus set within a source of phenomena. On its eastern and other petals, in order, are Indra, Yama, Varuṇa and Vaiśravaṇa. On the petals of the intermediate directions, beginning with the northeast, are Īśāna, Agni, Nairṛti and Vāyu. One should visualize them as arisen from the syllable hūṃ, black in color, with one face, holding vajra and bell crossed, embracing a consort like themselves, standing with the right leg extended, and crowned with Akṣobhya.

Above those eight within the source of phenomena are the gods of the sun, the moon, Brahmā and the rest: those of the realm of the Four Great Kings, the Thirty-Three, the Yāma gods, Tuṣita, Nirmāṇarati and Paranirmitavaśavartin, who move in the desire realm; Brahmapāriṣadya, Brahmapurohita and Mahābrahmā, who belong to the first meditative concentration; Parīttābha, Apramāṇābha and Ābhāsvara, born in the second meditative concentration; Parīttaśubha, Apramāṇaśubha and Śubhakṛtsna, born in the third meditative concentration; and Anabhraka, Puṇyaprasava, Bṛhatphala, Avṛha, Atapa, Sudṛśa, Sudarśana and those dwelling in Akaniṣṭha, born from the fourth meditative concentration, who move in the form realm; together with those who dwell in the world system of Sukhāvatī and those who dwell in the world system called Radiant. One should visualize them arisen from the syllable aṃ, white in color, holding vajra and bell, with their retinues, each with a consort like himself, standing with the right leg extended, and crowned with Vairocana.

Below that, within the source of phenomena, are the nāgas Ananta, Vāsuki, Takṣaka, Karkoṭa, Padma, Mahāpadma, Śaṅkhapāla, Kulika and the rest, and the great asuras Vemacitrin, Bali, Praha, Vairocana and the rest, who dwell beneath the earth; and the earth goddess too, with her retinue. There are those who dwell in the hot hells, called Saṃjīva, Kālasūtra, Saṃghāta, Raurava, Mahāraurava, Tapana, Pratāpana and Avīci, who are afflicted by heat; the hells that lie in the cold, called Arbuda, Nirarbuda, Huhuva, Aṭaṭa, Utpala, Padma and Mahāpadma; and the beings born in the hell realms that lie separately in water, on dry land, in the regions of the wind, on mountains and so forth. One should visualize them as red in color, holding vajra and bell, embracing a wisdom consort like themselves, standing with the right leg extended, and crowned with Amitābha.

Then the vajra master is adorned with variegated garments, a topknot, flowers, utpalas at the ears, earrings, a necklace, a long garland, armlets, anklets, rings and other ornaments. In the middle of the eight platforms anointed with perfume in the eastern and other directions and intermediate directions are the deities resting on the sources of phenomena. The platform anointed with perfume in the corner between the east and Īśāna is for those who rest above the source of phenomena; the platform between the west and Nairṛti is for the deities who rest below the source of phenomena.

Or else one summons Gaurī and the others in order, according to how near they are to oneself, makes them enter, binds them and brings them under one's power. One also gives the purifying torma and the rest, and should bless the torma with the mantra. This is the mantra for blessing the torma: oṃ inda jama jala jakkha bhūta bāhni bāu rakkha canad sujja māda pappatālaṃ pātāla aṣṭa sappa svāhā idaṃ baliṃ bhuñja jiṃghā phulla dhūpa māṃsa piṃga ambhasappakajjasādha khanti khuni pheḍagāda oṃ akāro mukhaṃ sarbadharmāṇāṃ ādyanutpannatvāta oṃ āḥ hūṃ phaṭ svāhā.

Then one should imagine on their tongues fine and beautiful vajras arisen from a white hūṃ, and through the practice of emanation make their tongues of that nature. Then one rises and, with the sound of drums and the like, faces them and, in proper order, casts the liquid of the pastries and the rest with the palm up to three times, and satisfies them with that torma, which has become the supreme nectar. Having thus offered the torma to the guardians of the directions and the rest, one should visualize that they enjoy it and, filled with joy, are favorable to the practitioner and bestow the accomplishments he longs for. Again one offers water to sprinkle and to drink and the rest, worships them with the five kinds of offering, praises them with two verses, tastes the nectar in the skull cup, and asks their forbearance:

oṃ You have accomplished every aim of beings;
grant the accomplishments that accord with them.
Though you depart to the buddha realm,
please come back once again.

With oṃ āḥ hūṃ bajra muḥ one should request them to depart. Once they have departed, one should regard them as having gone, with joyful minds, each to his own abode.

This is the rite of the torma.

Then the master, the mantra practitioner who holds vajra and bell, takes vajra and bell in his hands and stands in the posture with the right leg extended. Endowed with the yoga of the two-armed Hevajra, he takes a garland of blue flowers, utters the sound hūṃ hūṃ, and, with the terrifying manner of laughing hi hi, with the vajra posture and the force of the vajra dance, enters the maṇḍala. Ringing the bell, he circles it three times. To pacify the faults of deficiency and emptiness, he looks at the maṇḍala and offers the garland of blue flowers to the Blessed One. Then, with the movements of the dance, he takes it back again and binds it on the crown of his own head, and should worship the maṇḍala with great offerings:

Kośata and karkarcura,
mixed with butter and honey:
these incenses, ever delighting,
should be set out before the maṇḍala.
Srog, kunduru and musk,
and what comes from the gyamo:
the incense that delights the Jewel King,
mixed with molasses, goes on the southern side.
Papati, hair, prabaga,
made with sade scent and sati:
the incense that delights Amitābha,
mixed with khashar, is placed behind.
The two sandalwoods and camphor,
musk, nakha and śallakī:
the incense that delights Amoghasiddhi,
mixed with butter, is placed on the left.
Manifold śrī, the unshakable, saffron,
phalinī and the ocean-born:
the incense that delights the glorious Blood-Drinker,
mixed with sugar, is set before oneself.
Bola, musk, sarja and wang,
kosi and asreg in equal parts:
lamps steeped in sesame oil,
radiant, should be set in the corners.

If an assembly has gathered, then not far from the maṇḍala, with service and reverence first, one should recite the Blessed Mother, the Perfection of Wisdom, on the eastern side; in the south, the Ratnaketu; in the west, the Gaṇḍavyūha Sūtra; and in the north, the Candrapradīpa Sūtra.

Dance and the like, and snapping of the fingers, should also be performed in the four directions. Then, again, one should offer various fine flowers to the Blessed One with this mantra: oṃ bajra puṣpe āḥ hūṃ svāhā. With oṃ bajra dhūpe āḥ hūṃ svāhā one offers incense properly compounded of natural ingredients. With oṃ bajra dīpe āḥ hūṃ svāhā one offers the light of the flame of a lamp of boiled sesame oil. With oṃ dhvaṃ dhvaṃ bajra nebidye āḥ hūṃ svāhā one offers a food offering of fragrant śāli rice. Other offering substances, moreover, one generates from the syllable hūṃ and offers with the three syllables, and then one should offer praise and taste the nectar.

Then, in order to make up fully for whatever is deficient or missing in the rite, having first bowed to the maṇḍala, or else through aspiration alone, one should perform the fire offering for increase in the hearth for all activities. On the southern or the eastern side of the maṇḍala, on yellow ground, one digs to a width of about one cubit and a depth of twelve finger-widths, and surrounds it with a garland of vajras. In the center stands the sign of the platform, eight finger-widths in length, in the middle of which the emblem of the principal deity has been blessed. Above it a space of four finger-widths is left, and it is four finger-widths in width and height, shaped like an open lotus. In the directions it is marked with five-pronged vajras, and in the intermediate directions it is surrounded with three-pronged vajras. Having made the hearth with its rim of about a cubit, one smears it with yellow earth, and the maṇḍala too is smeared with dung. Then both of them are smeared with yellow perfume mixed with the five nectars, and fine variegated flowers, or yellow flowers, should be strewn at the three times of day. Then that auspicious hearth should be lined all around on the inside with leaves, or with the inner bark of the śāla tree, or with plantain leaves, or with fine cloth. If these are not available, one should draw the hearth with colored powders.

Then, bathed, the limbs anointed with fragrance, adorned with variegated garments, a garland of flowers and a topknot, with a mind full of joy, one sits on a fine seat; or else, having done the recitation for protection with royal splendor, facing north or facing east, the mantra practitioner, full of joy, should perform the fire offering for increase for as long as it takes for what is desired to increase.

Then, in front, one makes a maṇḍala with the five nectars mixed with water and spreads a carpet of flowers. On the left side, on stands and the like, one sets out three vessels of gold, silver, copper, stone, wood, clay, conch, mother-of-pearl, a leaf cup or the like, well washed and fumigated. They should be filled, as prescribed, with water from a bathing place, together with curd, sesame and the scent of bita. Then the water of the first vessel is consecrated seven times with the mantra for all activities ending in oṃ. The water of the second vessel should be consecrated seven times with the mantra of one's own deity, enclosed within the three syllables and ending in oṃ. Then, in front, one sets two receptacle vessels on two stands and arranges them as before. On the right side one should arrange the burnt offerings, sprinkled with the water for all activities: firewood with the three sweet substances; handfuls of fine porridge with curd, butter and honey; black sesame and barley with butter; saha flowers, bilva fruits, sālu rice, thick rice gruel, barley, wheat, peas, māṣa beans, lotuses, nāgakesara, karṇikā, red flowers and so on.

Then, to pacify the obstructors, having given the torma, the yogin who has entered the yoga of the deity takes from the hearth firewood of sacrificial trees not eaten by insects, and takes fire from a great fire in the kitchen of the Saṅgha, or produced from a jewel, or from the royal kitchen. He places it in a clean vessel and kindles it with dry firewood soaked in milk. Then he blows on it with its three syllables, holds a bundle of kuśa grass for purifying by its tip in the water for all activities, waves it around three times and sprinkles; and he should set the fire in the center of the hearth together with chips of dry wood or dry grass. Then, reciting the mantra oṃ jvala jvala hūṃ, he should kindle it with a fan, and should pour on butter consecrated seven times with the heart mantra of the family, and so make it blaze.

Then the hearth should be encircled with kuśa grass consecrated seven times with this mantra, oṃ ime kuśā śubhā dibya baputra brahma babiṭeṇa buddha dharma saṃhara kāḥ bṛthibira saṃ bajrata garbha abighṇāṃ mama śāntiṃ kuru bandya oṃ bajra satva āḥ: kuśa pulled up from its roots, fresh, unstained, unbroken, not split, not bitten by worms, and not too long. East of the platform it faces outward; from the side of the Giver and of Yama it faces west, with the tips pointing north; and from the side of Agni the tips should point east.

Then one generates the hearth from the seed syllable of one's own deity. In the center of that blazing fire, upon a triangular maṇḍala marked with the reph, the form of the mind of the First Supreme, upon a lotus that has arisen from the transformation of oṃ, one visualizes Agni arisen from the syllable hūṃ: yellow in color, with a topknot of hair, holding a rosary and making the gesture of fearlessness with the right hands, and holding a water pot and a staff in the left hands, with a pendulous belly, wearing yellow garments, in the form of a brahmin, with yellow rays of light, seated in the cross-legged posture of a hero on a yellow seat. One also sees the wisdom being in the sky and, reciting the mantra, should invite him with oṃ aganye mahāteja sarbakāmārtha prasādhakā karuṇa kṛtsatva artha asmina sannihito bhaba.

Then, having invited the wisdom Agni, one sets him on a lotus seat in the sky in front, with the Ṭakki mantra enclosed in the middle of the three suchnesses, and drives away with the syllable hūṃ the obstructors who have come along with him. From the first water vessel, with the kuśa purifier, one offers the purifying libation three times into the first receptacle. Then, with thumb and forefinger, one pours water with flowers from the second vessel and should likewise offer water for sprinkling and drinking three times.

Then, reciting the mantra oṃ jaḥ hūṃ baṃ hoḥ khaṃ raṃ prabara satkāra arghaṃ pratīccha svāhā, and opening the tip of the vajra, one should offer guest water from the third water vessel into the second receptacle, up to three times. Then, with this, oṃ niriti hūṃ khaṃ prabara satkāra pādyaṃ pratīccha svāhā, one takes up some of the water that is there, together with flowers, by joining thumb and forefinger, and likewise slowly, up to three times, one should offer it to his feet.

Then, with the mantra oṃ aganye ādyi byā di byā biṣā biṣā mahā śriye ha bhyā ka byā ba ha nā ya svāhā, one summons him, causes him to enter the pledge fire, binds him and brings him under control, blesses his eyes, body and so forth, and also confers empowerment. Again, with the purifying libation and so forth, and following the ritual, one makes the fivefold offering with the very mantras stated before, and should praise the fully accomplished fire.

After that, there is the ladle made of gold, of copper, or of sacrificial wood, having the measure of a man's finger-breadths: twenty-four finger-breadths long, marked with a vajra at the grip. On top of the yoga staff is a square of four finger-breadths, hollowed out to a depth of two finger-breadths, marked with the vajra of the First Supreme, and having a hole the size of the middle finger through which the liquid flows. From its lip downward, four finger-breadths, with a middle part reaching to the tip, is a three-pronged vajra; in the middle of the three-pronged vajra is the shape of a lotus petal. Below the staff, marked with a jewel vajra of six finger-breadths, is the pouring ladle. Then there is the filling ladle: on a staff corresponding to that one, a jewel vajra, and two finger-breadths in width, in the shape of a lotus petal, hollowed out to a depth of about one finger-breadth, and marked in the middle with the vajra of the supreme vajra.

On its mouth one sets the seed syllable of one's own deity, and on the mouth of the fire also the syllable raṃ. With the hands inside the knees, palms facing upward, one takes with the left hand the pouring ladle filled with ghee, and with the right hand turned palm down one takes the filling ladle that rests on the pouring ladle. Having filled its pouring ladle with ghee, one burns it upon the offering substances with the mantra oṃ agnaye oṃ amukaṃsya puṣṭiṃ kuru oṃ, and thereby opens the mouth of Agni a little. Then, circling the filling ladle together with the pouring ladle, one recites oṃ namaḥ smanta buddhānāṃ agnaye oṃ amukaṃsya puṣṭiṃ kuru oṃ and makes the full oblation.

Then the mantra practitioner, in meditative equipoise, should examine the characteristics of the fire, its virtuous and non-virtuous omens, by way of its color, its smell and its sound:

A fire blazing with a single tip
will accomplish every rite.
Blazing evenly, without flickering,
with two tips, it is known as middling.
If one sees it blazing with four tips,
well and evenly,
then the earth, with its girdle of the four oceans,
will be his forever.

Like jasmine and the moon, glossy,
with the fine radiance of a beryl's form,
a fire without smoke and without odor
brings freedom from illness and makes the lineage flourish.
Like crystal and gems,
resembling the appearance of snow,
like topaz,
it removes evil and grants accomplishment.
Like lhambu, crystal, Indra's bow,
lightning and coral,
refined gold and sapphire,
and sunlight, it grants kingship and wealth.

Campaka, lotus, blue utpala,
and mālatī, with cool fragrance,
camphor and the scent of aruṇa:
a treasure, a master of the fields of learning.
The sound of elephant, horse and bull,
the sound of durba and kengila:
by a fire offering at the time of entering a land
one crushes enemies and is victorious.
Lute and flute, fire and some others,
the sounds of lotus, smoke and kaha,
very deep and very smooth;
a soundless fire grants happiness.

The shapes of the śrīvatsa, parasol, conch and lotus,
and of the three tips,
of victory banner, yak-tail whisk and supreme vajra,
the shapes of nāga, horse and elephant,
the shapes of a ladle for burning ghee,
of a fine seat and of the sun;
soundless, turning to the right,
in a single mass, it grants great aims.
These are the characteristics of virtue.

Flickering, leaning toward one and flaring,
three-tipped and with smoke,
whirling and with sparks flying,
it brings illnesses of body, speech and mind.
If it rises as though burning the one who makes the homa,
its flames three-tipped like the nape of a neck,
its flames broken off and without rays,
that brings death.
A fire that quickly flickers,
quickly crackles and is harsh,
quickly scatters from the left,
and quickly touches the ground,
this means leprosy and black spots;
it foretells the decline of the lineage and quarrels,
or the loss of the king's mounts,
or else the death of their lord.

Colorless, with smoke like black clouds,
thoroughly mixed with a dark blue color,
rough, and red like the palāśa,
it destroys the aims one desires.
And also with a rancid smell, a foul odor,
having the smell of water-born creatures,
of dust, of cat and of buffalo,
the smell of the wolf and of fierce beasts,
the smells of crow and of snake,
or having the smell of much excrement:
if the fire turns out like this,
it foretells the ruin of the master.
With sounds like caṭa caṭa and so forth,
with a sound like ur ur,
of the nature of a sound like tsek tsek,
or with the sound of a thunderbolt, it destroys wealth.
Like the edge at a sword's tip,
like the head of a camel or of a cow:
whatever has such a frightful shape,
if this is told of a fire offering,
these are the characteristics of non-virtue.

Having understood it in this way, if the mantra practitioner sees inauspicious omens, he should sprinkle seven times with the water for all rites, purify and protect with the mantra for all rites, and make seven oblations of ghee; and again he should circle as before. Having thus freed it from obstructors, he should again offer into the fire the offering substances and ghee, with the pouring ladle and the filling ladle full, once, three times or seven times, as far as his means allow, using the pouring ladle together with the filling ladle, with the mantra called oṃ namaḥ.

Then, with the pouring ladle, one burns three or seven oblations of ghee. Fresh sticks of fuel wood born from milky-sapped trees, such as palāśa, udumbāra, āmra, katamba, kāsmarya, aśvattha, palakṣa and nyagrodha, each with two leaves and cut to equal length, are held by the hand at the middle; their two tips are laid evenly side by side, washed with nectar and with yellow scented water, and the two tips are dipped in curd, milk and ghee. Holding them with the ring finger and the thumb, one should burn the fuel sticks one tip at a time. Or else one should burn, each with its own mantra, fuel sticks that are quite fresh, straight and with leaves, from the khadira, the sun tree, palakṣa, pippaling, apasmana, palāśa, udumbāra, bhata, vikaṅkata, āmra, sāli, and those growing from khalce, laid in a straight direction. Those that are crooked, that are hairy, that lack tips or have damaged tips, that lack bark, that are too long or too short, too thick or too thin, that are bent, whose tips are bloody or eaten by worms, or that have living creatures on them, should be avoided.

After that, into the fire kindled with the fuel sticks one should burn the other offering substances to be burned, such as ghee, unspoiled butter oil, millet, soba grain, dūrvā grass and kuśa grass, reciting the mantras. At the end of each mantra one should cast the offering with the urging mantra: oṃ make so-and-so prosper! oṃ.

Here the words of the mantras are as follows. oṃ vajrāya jāya svāhā is for udumbāra. oṃ bodhivṛkṣāya svāhā is for aśvattha. oṃ vajralatāya svāhā is for palakṣa. oṃ vajrakuberāya svāhā is for the milky-sapped trees such as nyagrodha. oṃ sahāgāhāya svāhā is for āmra. And so too for the others: oṃ sarva pāpaṃ dahana vajrāya sarva pābaṃ daha svāhā is for sesame and the like. oṃ vajra puṣṭaye svāhā is for unspoiled millet and the like. oṃ sarva saṃbade svāhā is for soba grain and the finest foods. oṃ vajra āyuṣe svāhā is for dūrvā grass. oṃ apratihatāya svāhā is for kuśa grass. vajra vijāya svāhā is for soba grain. oṃ mahāvegāya svāhā is for barley. oṃ mahābalāya svāhā is for pulses. oṃ vajra ghasmarī svāhā is for wheat. For other substances too, it is done with those born from the three suchnesses of hūṃ.

In this, if they are present, all the substances that are gathered are offered into the mouth of Agni. Offering substances such as kuśa grass go to his halo of light; food, drink and the like to his hands; flowers to the crown of his head; incense to the blaze; scents to his heart; the purifying libation to his body and feet; lamps, guest water and food offerings in front of him. If there is no filling ladle or pouring ladle, one should burn all the offering substances by joining the thumb and forefinger of the hand. In that case, the full oblation is made by joining the five fingers, and the other oblations should be given by joining the middle finger, the ring finger and the thumb.

Then, having given Agni the water for sprinkling and drinking and so forth, one regards that fire as having become the wisdom fire of all the Tathāgatas and so as having become the sphere of reality, and as the supramundane fire, the source of everything, arising from the nature of the Perfection of Wisdom. On a seat of lotus and sun disk at its heart, from the accomplished seed syllable of one's own deity, from the complete transformation of the emblem and the seed, one meditates on glorious Hevajra, the essence of all the Tathāgatas, as lord of the maṇḍala together with the goddesses of the maṇḍala. One should also invite the wisdom maṇḍala and set it in the space in front, with the mantra oṃ ehyehi bhagavān niha bhagatyaṃ samayena ca andhadikaṃ ca praticedaṃ me.

Then one expels the obstructors and so forth, and offers the water for sprinkling and drinking, the purifying libation and the rest. With this, jaḥ hūṃ baṃ hoḥ, one summons, causes to enter, binds and brings under control, and views the wisdom wheel and the pledge wheel as having become one, like water and milk. Then one also purifies the body, eyes and so forth of that maṇḍala and confers empowerment, and again performs the rite from the purifying libation, the water for sprinkling and drinking and so on, up through offering and praise. Dissolving the syllable hūṃ into the tongue, one again offers the other oblations as before, each with its own mantra. Mixing the substances of all the requisites, one offers one, three, seven, twenty-one, one hundred and eight, or a hundred thousand oblations, as much as one has obtained, as before. Then again one should imagine that all the offering substances fall well upon the tongues of the deities.

At the time of empowerment, having made the offering, one should hold the thumb of the left hand of each of the disciples, and oneself or another should give three or seven oblations. If the means are there, one should burn oblations for the sake of the increase of the king, the lord of the land, the Saṅgha, the goddesses, the guardians of the directions, and oneself. Having thus performed the fire offering with whatever one has obtained, at the end one should kindle it closely with oblations of ghee, with the heart mantra of the family, three, seven or twenty-one times.

Then one offers the full oblation, and offers water for sprinkling and drinking, betel free of powder, clothing and so forth, and makes offerings and praises. Then all those deities, delighted and satisfied with sandalwood and the like and with substances such as ghee, emanate clouds of their own bodies, holding in their hands vases filled with jewels and nectar; they empower the one for whom the rite is accomplished and make him a vajra body. One meditates that his house too is completely filled with many jewels, and generates the accomplishments of the sword and the rest; and one views the clouds of their bodies as gathered back fully into themselves.

Then the mantra practitioner, joining his palms with flowers, in meditative equipoise and bowing his body, in order to pacify the faults of deficiency and omission and for the sake of the accomplishment he desires, should request their understanding and forbearance, with this:

Blessed One, since I lack the power,
whatever I have done without the means, without knowing,
deficient or in excess,
please forgive it.

Then one should say vajrasatva three times.

oṃ You have accomplished every aim of beings;
grant the accomplishments that accord with them.
Having gone to the realm of the buddhas,
please come again.

Saying this, and oṃ āḥ hūṃ vajra maḥ, one should request the maṇḍala of one's chosen deity to depart. Then one offers to the hearth and to Agni, as before, the purifying libation, the water for sprinkling and drinking and so forth, and worships them, and one should burn the remaining requisites in order. Then one offers the full oblation, offers the purifying libation and the rest as well, also makes offerings of betel, clothing and so forth, and should ring the bell and praise. Then, having made requests for the accomplishment one desires and having asked forbearance, one urges three times with vajrasatva, and requests Agni to depart with the mantra svānvance ba parārthañca sādhitaṃ gaccha habyabhuka agamiṣvā piyathākāle sarva siddhi kuru śvami.

Then, on the fire, in a well-washed vessel, one gathers the remainder of the offering substances into a broth with ghee, milk, honey and sugar and boils it, and one should give a little to one's relatives as well. After that one recites the hundred-syllable mantra seven times, dedicates the virtue fully to unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening, and should rise. Here, this is the hundred-syllable mantra: oṃ vajrasatva samaya manupālaya vajrasatva tvenopatiṣṭha dṛḍho me bhava sutoṣyo me bhava supoṣyo me bhava anurakto me bhyava sarva siddhimme prayaccha sarva karmasu ca me cittaṃ śreyaḥ kuru hūṃ ha ha ha ha ho bhagavān sarva tathāgata vajra mā me muñca vajrī bhava mahāsamaya satva āḥ.

Here, since it has come up in passing, I shall explain the rites of the fire offerings for pacifying and the rest.

The pacifying fire offering should be performed in order to pacify oneself or others who are seized by illness, by harm, by the harm of falling meteors, by obstructors, by afflictions or by evil, and also in order to attain the higher knowledges and liberation. For this, on white ground to the east of the maṇḍala, one digs a pit a cubit wide and twelve finger-breadths deep. In its center is an eight-petaled lotus of sixteen finger-breadths, on which rests a five-pronged vajra of eight finger-breadths with its tip pointing east. Above, leaving a space of four finger-breadths, one makes a round hearth surrounded by a rim four finger-breadths in width and in height, plasters it with white clay, and also plasters the ground of the maṇḍala with cow dung. Then one should plaster both with the scent of white sandalwood mixed with the five nectars and strew them with fine white flowers. The hearth may also be made with colored powders. One fills a vessel of gold or the like with water containing white scent, barley and milk, and should recite over it as before with mantras ending in svāhā.

One burns firewood of milky-sapped trees or the dried dung of pasture cattle, and also curd, ghee, honey, milk, barley, sesame, parched rice, priyaṅgu, sandalwood, śāli rice flour, blue dūrvā grass, crushed white flowers, kesara flowers, mango leaves, niryava and other fragrant things. The sticks of palāśa and the like, twelve finger-breadths long, not thick, about the size of thumb and forefinger, should have their tips cleansed with nectar, white scent and water, and their two tips dipped in honey, milk and ghee; the rest follows the characteristics of fuel sticks stated before.

One takes fire produced from sacrificial wood, or produced from a gem, or ordinary fire, or a brahmin's fire, and kindles the fire. Agni is white in color, wearing white garments, with a white topknot adorned with white flowers, endowed with loving-kindness and compassion, seated on a lotus seat, facing north or east, his mind at peace. Beginning on the first lunar day, the mantra practitioner rises early and, at the end of every mantra, urging properly with amukasya śīntaṃ kuru svāhā and voicing the mantras clearly, should perform the pacifying fire offering. Then the deities, delighted and satisfied with substances such as ghee and nectar, emanate hosts of clouds of their own bodies; holding jewel vases filled with the water of wisdom, they empower the one for whom the rite is done, cast off unwanted illness and the like, and accomplish the accomplishment he desires; and the clouds of their bodies gather back into themselves. One views them as abiding there, their task done. This is the rite of the pacifying fire offering.

In order to accomplish the sword, the wheel, the arrow, the jewel, the lotus, the great treasure, the elixir, the alchemical elixir of long life, kingship and the like, an ordinary person, whether oneself or another, should perform the increasing fire offering, beginning from the full moon. For this, on yellow ground to the south of the maṇḍala, two cubits wide and one cubit deep, surrounded by a rim eight finger-breadths across and eight finger-breadths high, with an eight-spoked wheel endowed with a jewel vajra in the center, one digs it out in the shape of an eight-cornered jewel or a square and makes the hearth. One plasters the hearth and the ground around it with yellow earth, scent and nectar, and should spread yellow or variegated flowers. The firewood is that already described, with fruits, excluding the dung of pasture cattle. The rest is as taught in the rite for all actions. This is the rite of the increasing fire offering.

In order to accomplish actions such as killing, drying up, expelling, causing enmity, paralyzing, confusing and wasting the body, the fierce fire offering should be performed. For this, on black ground to the north of the maṇḍala, rough and dry and like empty space, one makes a black triangular hearth twenty finger-breadths across and ten finger-breadths deep, blessed in its center by a vajra of eight finger-breadths at the tip of a trident, surrounded above by a rim three finger-breadths in width and in height with a sloping edge, its apex pointing north. One plasters it with earth, ashes from the charnel ground, and as much dung of donkeys, camels and the like as there is, and should strew it with foul-smelling things, with blue and red flowers, with soba grain and so forth. Then, in the vessel described before, foul-smelling water, kodrava, and the urine of humans, cattle and donkeys, together with flowers, is recited over thoroughly as before with mantras ending in the syllable phaṭ, and it should be placed on the right-hand side.

Yellow mustard seed, heated oil, flowers, salt, mustard seed, ashes from the charnel ground, dust from the feet, nimba leaves, the dung of donkeys, camels and the like, and hair are pounded together, and mixed with the droppings of crows and the like, the urine and excrement of cats, thorns, gravel, bone powder, blood, flesh, poison, blood, mustard seed and the juice of the leaves of the maddening plant. Mixing these with water recited over with the mantra for all actions, and with ashes, pounded matter, charcoal, poison and the like, one makes from these substances an effigy of the enemy's emanated form, and should make it the substance to be burned. A lamp of human fat is placed on the left side.

The firewood is wood born from pungent, thorny, bitter and sharp trees, or else wood from a grave, or the dried dung of donkeys and camels. The fuel sticks are made from wood born of nimba, khadira, kobhidha, bhallātaka and madana trees, or from pungent and thorny ones, or from bitter trees, or from trees of the charnel ground, cut at the root end; ten finger-breadths long, with flowers, with sharp tips, their two tips are dipped in heated oil. The fire is one born from human bones of the charnel ground, or from charnel-ground wood, or struck from stone, or taken from the house of an outcaste.

Agni is black in color, his hair blazing upward, his eyes red, his fangs bared, his face wrinkled in wrath, wrathful and terrifying, with a pendulous belly, short of stature, wearing black garments, bearing the implements of worship, blazing and with gaping mouth.

Wrathful, wearing blue garments, with a topknot adorned with flowers, his clothing moistened with blood and water, with bloodshot eyes and a face wrinkled in wrath, pressing the right foot with the left, pressing teeth against teeth, without a seat, one squats on the belly of the effigy of the target; or else, sitting in the wrathful cross-legged posture facing south, with the wrath of compassion, in order to avert enemies who harm the Three Jewels, beginning on any day of the waning fortnight, at midday or at midnight, taking wrath itself as one's measure, one places the hands outside the two knees, takes a sharp weapon with the right hand, and, cutting and cutting the thing to be burned, makes the threatening gesture with the left hand. Reciting the mantra phaṭ amukasya abhicaraṃ kuru phaṭ one should burn it. Thus one performs the fierce fire offering.

For causing enmity, specifically, at the point of urging: oṃ jrīṃ svāhā hūṃ amukaṃ bideśaya. For expelling: oṃ khaṃ svāhā hūṃ amuka ucchatranaya hūṃ. For paralyzing and confusing: oṃ hūṃ hūṃ svāhā bauṣaṭ amukaṃ stvaṃmbhaya mohaya bauṣaṭa. For killing: oṃ ghu svāhā phaṭ amukaṃ māraya phaṭ. And likewise for the others.

The deity of the fire offering is pleased, and from the seed syllable at his heart wrathful ones emanate. They fill the target with vajra nooses and a rain of embers and strike him with many weapons; deprived of life, he is joined with the Sugata himself; and one views the emanations as gathered back into oneself. This is the rite of the fierce fire offering.

In order to bring under control and to summon gods, nāgas, yakṣas, gandharvas, asuras, garuḍas and kinnaras, together with their circles of women, and lords of men and the like, one should perform the fire offering for bringing under control or the fire offering for summoning. For this, the place is to the east of the maṇḍala, or for summoning to the west itself: red ground in the shape of a bhaga or of a half moon, blessed in its center by a vajra lotus. For summoning, it is blessed in its center by a vajra hook in the form of a single-pronged vajra. Both of these are dug like the pacifying hearth, and one makes the hearth with the proper measurements of rim and so forth. One plasters it with red earth and scent, adorns it with red flowers, and should also cover it with the leaves of the plantain and the like. Over the water in the vessel described before, with red flowers and scent, one recites, on the left side, with mantras ending in the syllable ho. For summoning it should be placed on the right side.

The fuel sticks are of priyaṅgu, campaka, puṇḍarīka, nāgakesara, kesala, kucala and aśoka, forest-grown, with leaves, five finger-breadths long, free of the faults mentioned in the section on pacifying. Sour fruits, red utpalas, the fruits of red flowers, and also the offering substances taught in the respective ritual manuals, are placed on the right. For summoning those same are on the left, and they should be set out with thorns shaped like hooks. Firewood born from nimba and sour trees, and other firewood with flowers, is for bringing under control; for summoning, it is firewood with thorns. The fire is fire from the house of a courtesan, or, if not a courtesan, from the house of a family of dancers. For summoning, it is fire from a place a dancer has left, or from a highway.

Agni has the radiance of red color, is seated on a seat, wears red garments, and bears the implements of worship. Wearing red garments, one's crown wound with flowers, abandoning wrathful pride, with a mind of passion, one sits in the vajra cross-legged posture facing west. For summoning, the mantra practitioner squats and looks upward. Reciting the mantras oṃ a svāhā oṃ amukaṃ baśiṃ kuru hoḥ and oṃ hūṃ svāhā ja amukaṃ ākārṣaya jaḥ, at midnight one performs the fire offering for bringing under control or the fire offering for summoning.

Pleased by the fire offering, the deity emanates clouds from his body. Drawing to the ear a bow made of black and red flowers in bloom, he pierces the heart of the target with an arrow of utpala stem and brings him under control. For summoning, he binds him with a noose of red utpala stems, seizes the place of his heart with a hook made of red flowers, draws him in, and sets him at the feet of the practitioner. Then one should meditate that the clouds of one's own body gather back into oneself. This is the rite of the fire offering for bringing under control and summoning.

The fire offering for all actions is performed simply with the action as it is commonly known. On this it is also taught: by this, in order to increase life, one should burn dūrvā grass dipped in ghee; and for the sake of protection, likewise, one should burn kuśa grass dipped in sesame oil, and should meditate on one's own chosen deity.

At the end of the mantra one should apply the words of urging, as appropriate, and also the distinctions of substance, action, meditation, mantra, seed syllable and so forth, following in each case what was given before. Thus, on the proper lunar day and at the proper time, or as occasion allows, or according to one's own inclination, one should perform the fire offering until the desired pacification or the like has come about. And this is taught:

For the ordinary state, perform increase;
for all illnesses, pacification;
for all who are hostile, killing should be done;
for one's own desired aims, bringing under control.

Then the mantra practitioner who has performed the outer fire offering should contemplate the substances, himself and the deities by way of complete purity, should focus on loving-kindness, patience, diligence and emptiness, and should set them in conventional truth, ultimate truth and the truth of origin. Through the distinctions of liberation by the śrāvaka, the pratyekabuddha and the Great Vehicle, by the inferior, middling and superior paths, one should accomplish the state of a buddha. And this is taught:

Outwardly endowed with three aspects,
if the three abide in the three places,
the three actions of haṃ
are accomplished, the Tathāgata has said.
Those who, not knowing the action of haṃ,
perform it otherwise,
having abandoned the wisdom of mantra,
in their delusion obtain no fruit.

Thus, for the accomplishment they desire, practitioners will succeed in no long time. Moreover, in order to become quickly habituated, one should reflect as follows. On these occasions, in all fire offerings, one should be certain that all of these, the ground, the hearth, the kuśa grass, the flowers, the offering substances, the vessels, the water, the fine spout, the pouring ladle and the filling ladle, the fuel sticks and the firewood, Agni, the chosen deity, and oneself as the one who worships the maṇḍala, and so forth, are by their intrinsic nature completely pure and have become of the nature of the sphere of reality.

The fruits born from the limbs of the syllable haṃ,
and whatever else is explained besides,
even if it is not stated, the wise
should understand by themselves and take up.
The ground increases one's fields;
the hearth increases the household;
ghee makes everything prosper;
the fuel sticks generate splendor;
the wood makes one exceedingly heroic;
kuśa grass performs all protection;
white substances pacify the ground;
millet is said to bring increase;
sesame is fit to remove evil;
soba grain draws wealth and guardians;
pulses make strength great;
offering barley brings the force of wind;
dūrvā grass increases life;
wheat makes illnesses decline;
honey and milk bring wisdom;
curd with rice grants every happiness;
through the chosen deity and the fire
likewise comes the desired accomplishment;
with what remains, in accord with the action,
know that curd and the rest perform the actions.
The pouring ladle is wisdom, the upper one is method;
meditate on their union as nondual.
Then the ghee that has come forth
becomes the nectar of great wisdom.
We ourselves, the moving and the unmoving,
and Agni are to be satisfied by it.
Whoever performs the fire offering in this way
becomes a vessel of all that is desired.

This is the rite of the fire offering.

Having performed the rite of the fire offering in this way, one should enter the maṇḍala. Then, beholding the wheel of deities as if directly present, one properly worships the maṇḍala by the rite taught before, praises it, tastes the nectar and so forth, and should recite mantra. Then the mantra practitioner who wishes to gather disciples for empowerment and the rest takes the lord of the family as his guru; by the rite for bringing in disciples that will be explained, he himself first enters the maṇḍala, and the lord of the family completes for him all the empowerments up to the last. Then, having thus obtained all the actions and the authorization, in order to bring disciples into the maṇḍala, and in order to call to mind the nature and the truth of the wheel of the maṇḍala, the wisdom body, nondual, the self of all phenomena, like a reflection of suchness, the nature of Vajradhara, the great undefiled bliss, one joyfully has this verse heard:

Since you possess the marks that arise from space,
supreme treasure without beginning,
reality of the great vajra of beings,
glorious Hevajra and the rest, accomplish me!
Supreme of all the great accomplishments,
Maheśvara, highest of gods,
Vajradhara, king of all,
ultimate and unchanging one, accomplish me!
Free of every fault,
you who are passionately loved by all passion,
greatly delighting in great passion,
Blessed One, accomplish that very thing for me!
Supreme of those utterly pure,
Tathāgata, liberated from the beginning,
Samantabhadra, supreme of all,
Bodhisattva, accomplish me!
Supreme of all the great accomplishments,
the seal of the supreme great lord,
the vajra of accomplishment, the ultimate,
has become the lord of the vajra essence.
It pervades the minds of all beings,
it dwells in the hearts of all beings;
it is the very mind of all beings,
supreme desire, supreme of pledges.
By the truth through which wisdom is,
wisdom and means, in the maṇḍala,
by that truth, protector, may you
fulfill my wishes completely.
Phenomena are like reflections,
pure, clear and untroubled,
ungraspable and inexpressible,
arisen equally from cause and action;
made from that very thing, they are suchness.

By the truth called this, the reflection is clear and without stain.

Then the disciple prostrates at the two feet of the master who has come out from the maṇḍala, offering a maṇḍala first, and, in order to be led in, the disciple should make supplication. He should supplicate thus:

Protector, having granted me
the empowerment of the irreversible wheel,
please teach me the reality of the deities of the maṇḍala
and the actions of a master as well.
The pledges of all the buddhas,
the supreme of the secret vows:
for the sake of beings,
may I always become a master.

Then, having made this supplication, the guru should make supplication to the lord of the maṇḍala, with this:

I, so-and-so by name,
in order to take up the awakening mind,
endowed with the pledges and the vows,
request entry into this secret maṇḍala.

Then the master should say:

O pure one of the family of the great secret,
great being, do you wish
to take up fully
the great secret?

And the disciple should reply, "I wish it."

Then, for the disciple whose body has been bathed and who has been given fragrances, a garland of flowers, and red garments together with a diadem, the master should cover the face with a red cloth, and, the offering having been presented, have him hold a garland of flowers with the mantra a khaṃ bī ra hūṃ. He should sprinkle him with water from the vase over which the mantra of all activities has been recited, and visualize the disciple's eyes, body and so forth as blessed in the form of the chosen deity. Having screened him off with a curtain, in order to lead him into the maṇḍala, he should ask, "O dear to the heart, who are you?" And the disciple should answer, "I am the fortunate one, dear to the heart." Then the guru too should worship him properly with flowers and so forth, and with the syllable hūṃ should place him at the eastern gate.

Then at his heart, with the mantra oṃ yo ga citta utpa da ya mi, he generates the mind into yoga. With this, oṃ su ra tes ma ya stvaṃ ho siddhi bajra ya tha su khaṃ, he should place a vajra together with a flower at the heart. Then he says to him: "Today you will be blessed by all the Tathāgatas. You must not speak of this to those who have not entered the maṇḍala of the great secret, supreme among all the Tathāgatas, nor speak of it to others who lack faith."

Then, with the mantra of all activities or with the Ṭakka mantra, he should draw the disciple in, and with the mantra a khaṃ bī ra hūṃ bring him inside the curtain. Having bowed to the master and to the maṇḍala, the disciple should say this: "oṃ For the service of all the Tathāgatas I offer myself. oṃ For the service of the masters I offer myself. oṃ For the service of sentient beings I offer myself."

Then the master should say this: "Today you have entered the family of all the Tathāgatas. Therefore I too shall generate vajra wisdom, the wisdom by which one obtains even the accomplishment of all the Tathāgatas, so what need is there to speak of other accomplishments? You must not speak of this in the presence of those who have not seen the maṇḍala, lest your pledge be broken by it."

Then, placing a vajra on the crown of the disciple's head, he says:

This is the vajra pledge.
If you should repeat this way
in the presence of anyone,
your head will burst apart.

Then, placing the vajra at his heart, he binds him by oath with this:

oṃ Vajrasattva himself
today abides in your heart.
If you should speak of it,
in that very instant he will pierce it.

Then he blesses fragrant water mixed with the five nectars with the three syllables, recites this, oṃ bajra ma ho da ka, seven times, and says:

This is the water of hell.
If you transgress the pledge, it burns;
if you keep the pledge, it bestows accomplishment.
Drink the water of vajra nectar!

Having said this, he should give it to him to drink. Then, in order to make the disciple's commitment firm, he should say this: "From this day on, I am your Vajrapāṇi. Whatever I tell you, you must do, and you must not despise me, lest you die without escaping calamity and fall into hell."

Then he has him say sarba ta thā ga ta śvā dhi tiṣṭhā na ta ma bajra svā hā, and at his heart one should meditate on an earth maṇḍala arisen from the syllable laṃ, square and yellow, with three-pronged vajras at its four corners, blessed by the syllable hūṃ. At the throat one should meditate on a water maṇḍala arisen from the syllable vaṃ, round and white, marked with a vase, blessed by the syllable ā. Then at the crown of the head one should imagine a wind maṇḍala arisen from the syllable yaṃ, black and shaped like a bow, marked at both ends with hanging, fluttering banners, blessed in the center by haṃ; and on the soles of both feet one should imagine the syllable jja blazing brightly. Then below that, on top of the wind maṇḍala, one should meditate on a fire maṇḍala arisen from the syllable raṃ, red in color and triangular, and make it blaze forth with light. Ringing the bell, one should urge it on with the mantra ā bai śa ya stvaṃ bha ya ra ra ra ra ca la ya ca la ya hūṃ a jhaiṃḥ, and cause the descent to take place, continuing until it has descended. Or else, one should meditate on a wind maṇḍala, blessed by its seed syllable, beneath the disciple's feet, and meditate that it urges him on.

Then again, ringing the bell with a fierce dance, one should recite the mantra oṃ li le li le hūṃ until the descent takes place. Knowing that it has descended, one should visualize him as the vajra of desire, meditate on a red syllable āḥ on his tongue, and say, "Speak, vajra!" Then, in order to make the descent stable, one should say tiṣṭha bajra. When questioned in this way, he will speak of what is virtuous and what is non-virtuous. Then, with the mantra oṃ tiṣṭha bajra sa ma ya pra ti sa li te hūṃ hūṃ ho ho svā hā, one should make the descent stable, and with this verse one should perform the blessing of truth:

Into this true maṇḍala
I have properly led the disciple;
the order of the deities
will be in keeping with his merit.
Whatever accomplishment arises through him,
his family is the vessel for it;
whatever accords with his merit,
such will his maṇḍalas be.

Then one should ask him, "What do your eyes see?" According to what he says, by the appearance of a white, yellow, red or black maṇḍala, one should declare in order that he is fit for the accomplishment of pacifying, enriching, subjugating or fierce rites. Then, with the syllable hūṃ, he should throw the flower garland he has been holding, and one should say pra tīccha ho. Then the lesser or supreme accomplishment is indicated by the deity on which the flower falls.

According to the distinctions among the deities,
and likewise according to the place:
for one who is devoted, if the flower falls on a buddha,
on the ear, the crown protuberance or the hair,
the accomplishment of the great seal is declared,
which comes about through applying oneself to mantra.
If it falls on the eyes or the region of the face,
the accomplishments of mantra are declared.
On the upper body, it is the supreme accomplishment;
on the lower body, it is explained as middling;
on the lowest part, it is the least accomplishment.
The supreme is explained as awakening;
through the middling comes the accomplishment of lordship;
through the least, riches are accomplished.
This whole sequence holds
for the Nairātmyā goddesses as well.
Otherwise, on the seats of the deities,
it shows the three kinds of accomplishment.
If the flower that has been cast
falls far from the image,
his accomplishment will be far off;
if it falls near, it will come here.
Whatever brings benefit to mantra practitioners,
or is firmly devoted to helping them:
if the flower falls within its domain,
know him to be of its family.
If the garland of flowers that was thrown
falls in between,
whichever the garland lies nearer to,
that shows him to be of its family.
If it goes beyond the maṇḍala,
if it falls there,
his accomplishment will be otherwise;
by this, his faith is shown to be contrived.
If the garland falls
upon the lines of the maṇḍala,
pacification should be performed, and the garland
should be cast again with care.
If once more it comes near the line,
the wise one should not cast it again:
for him there is no attaining of accomplishment,
nothing is certain anywhere, O wise one.
If it lies equally between two,
or falls outside, there is no fruit;
pacification having been performed, the wise one
should cast the garland once again.
The knowing of the line itself,
nothing else: always the essence falls;
its domain is that very knowing;
this is well known as the garland.
If in these and other ways
it does not properly come to rest,
the garland should be thrown again;
if it is so three times, he should be turned away.

This is the casting of the flower.

Then one should know his deity from where the flower fell. Taking up again the flower that was thrown, and having taken it up, one should say, "This one is pleased and of great strength," and bind it on the crown of the disciple's head. This is the garland empowerment.

Then one should meditate on a blazing syllable oṃ on the disciple's eyes, and say:

oṃ Today Vajrasattva himself,
supreme among those who open eyes,
will open all eyes
to the unsurpassed vajra eye.

Having said this, one should untie the covering over the face. As for announcing the vision of Hevajra, one should show the maṇḍala, beginning with the name of the lord of the maṇḍala first, and then the names of the individual deities.

Then the guru, pleased, should say this to the disciple:

From today, give rise to faith,
and look upon this maṇḍala.
You are born into the family of the buddhas,
blessed by the knowledge mantras;
those yogas of accomplishment
and all riches have become manifest to you.
For the sake of accomplishment, guard the pledges,
diligent in the reality of mantra.

Then the disciple too, bowing at the guru's feet, should say this: "oṃ bajra maṇḍa la ma hā maṇḍa la pra ti ho'haṃ. oṃ yo ga maṇḍal ma hā maṇḍalaṃ dar si ho'haṃ. oṃ gu hya maṇḍal ma hā maṇḍal ḍa kṣi ho'haṃ."

This is the rite of leading the disciple in.

Then, in order to increase the virtue of the guru and the disciple, three verses of auspiciousness should be recited:

Whatever sublime auspiciousness, through devotion, benefits all beings without exception,
the glorious Blood-Drinker, who clears away the many faults,
whose nature is the bliss of passion, the limb of awareness, well experienced:
by that, may there be auspiciousness for the supreme empowerments as well!

Whatever auspiciousness is worshipped by gods, humans and asuras,
the unsurpassed teachings spoken by him,
and the explanation of selflessness throughout the three worlds:
by that, may there be auspiciousness for the supreme empowerments as well!

Whatever auspiciousness always upholds the supreme teachings,
the host of deities, lord of the host of the Victor's children,
the excellent auspiciousness, the four kinds of benefit to beings:
by that, may there be auspiciousness for the supreme empowerments as well!

Then one supplicates with this:

Buddhas, vajras of awakening,
as the great among the great is made clear,
in order to guide me,
grant me the space vajra and the rest.

The disciple sits on a seat of a lotus and so forth, and one visualizes him in the form of Akṣobhya. One visualizes all the Tathāgatas, in the form of glorious Hevajra, holding jewel vases filled with the water of wisdom and conferring empowerment, and says:

The vajra jewel empowerment,
to which all the three realms bow,
arising equally from the three secrets,
the buddhas bestow upon me.

Having said this, one takes up the vajra together with the water held in the fist, says oṃ bajra a bhi ṣiñca, and confers empowerment as on a universal monarch. Here the sequence of empowerment is this: first with the water of the Victory vase; after that, with the water of the vases of Gaurī and the others; after that, with the water of the vases of the Incense goddess and the others. This is called the water empowerment, the very nature of the wisdom of the utterly pure sphere of reality.

Then, meditating on the disciple in the form of the Jewel King, one places on the crown of his head, with the mantra given earlier, a diadem made of gold or the like, bearing his own deities. One meditates that the forms of the family lords of the Tathāgatas in the ten directions enter the diadem, and meditates that empowerment is conferred as before. This is called the diadem empowerment, whose nature is the wisdom of equality.

Then, meditating on him in the form of Amitābha, one takes the vajra by its tip and says:

With the empowerment of the buddha vajra
today I empower you.
This is all the buddhas themselves:
for the sake of accomplishment, take the vajra.

Having said this, one should touch his heart, the crown of his head and his throat with the vajra, give it into his hands, and confer empowerment as before. This is called the vajra empowerment, whose nature is discriminating wisdom.

Then, visualizing him in the form of Amoghasiddhi, one gives the bell into his left hand, and with his two hands holding the vajra and the bell in the gesture of embrace, the form of inseparability, one says oṃ bajra a dhi ba dhi stvāṃ a bhi ṣiñca mi tiṣṭha bajra sa ma ya staṃ and confers empowerment. This is called the bell empowerment, whose nature is the wisdom of accomplishing activities.

Then, visualizing him in the form of Vairocana, one places the vajra and the bell on the crown of his head and says oṃ bajra satva stvaṃ a bhi ṣiñca mi bajra nā ma a bhi ṣe ka ta. Then, with this formula, "O glorious so-and-so Vajra," one should confer the name empowerment of a universal monarch, in a way that accords with his family. This is called the name empowerment, whose essential nature is mirror-like wisdom.

Through these empowerments one becomes authorized to listen to, explain and practice the mantra tantras. Since these very ones are the five kinds of wisdom family of the knowledge empowerment, they are called the five empowerments.

Whatever appears, all of it
is Heruka himself.
Whatever is ordinary is the pure Dharma;
it is also the wisdom of the buddhas.

Through the general knowledge empowerment awakening is certainly attained, but not the special, perfect and complete awakening. Therefore, in order to attain with certainty, through diligence, perfect and complete awakening, one who seeks the master's empowerment for that purpose, having taken the general vows and having taken the master's vows, should be given the observance. Otherwise, the prophecy and the encouragement are not given, because through those two one becomes firmly established on the irreversible ground: through the power of conviction, thinking, "I have been prophesied; I have received encouragement," one effortlessly attains certainty. Others say: "Just as the observance is bestowed after seeing what kind of vessel the disciple is, so too the prophecy and the encouragement are not bestowed by one who knows reality." Others again say that in some cases the authorization is likewise given, with these five verses beginning "All sentient beings."

Then, furthermore, having taught the mudrā of his own deity and recited the root mantra or the heart mantra one hundred and eight times, one should give it to the disciple, saying:

I give you the Blessed One;
he has come near to you here.

And the disciple too should say:

Blessed One, I take hold of you;
you have come near to me.

Then the guru should say to the disciple: "You must not speak of this mantra and mudrā in the presence of anyone who has not seen the maṇḍala, lest on that account you go to hell."

This is the rite of the knowledge empowerment.

Then the one who holds the knowledge empowerment and the master's vows bows, offers the fee, and makes this supplication:

Buddhas, through the vajra of awakening,
as the great among the great is made clear,
in order to guide me too,
grant me the space vajra and the rest.

The one making this supplication, who has been staying at the door, should be led in. One draws an eight-petalled lotus and places him upon it. Through the yoga of one's own chosen deity, one places the disciple in the sky. From the rays of light at one's own heart, all the Tathāgatas are sent forth through the mouth, in the form of glorious Heruka, bearing the five kinds of offerings, parasols, victory banners, flags and music, with songs of auspiciousness; and one should visualize them conferring empowerment with the water of wisdom held in the jewel vases in their hands. One says:

The empowerment of the great vajra,
to which the three realms bow,
arising equally from the three secrets,
has been made clear by all the buddhas.

Having said this, one should confer empowerment with the water held in the lotus vessel.

Then one should also explain to him the reality of the maṇḍala, the reality of the deities, and the conduct of a master. Here, in order to gather the essence of utterly pure meditative absorption, the maṇḍala is comprised by the enjoyment body. Through the purity of the perfect arousing of the mind, there is the tiered palace. Because it extends equally in measure, it is square. Through the purity of great love, great compassion, joy and equanimity, there are the four lines. Through the application of recollecting the Dharma, there is the measuring cord. Through the purity of wisdom freed from the view of the transitory collection, the view that holds to extremes, the holding of views as supreme, and the view that holds ethics and observances as supreme, there is the striking of the lines. Through the purity of the discipline of vows, namely restraint, accomplishing the welfare of sentient beings, and gathering virtuous qualities, it is adorned with many ornaments. Through the faculties of faith, diligence, mindfulness, meditative absorption and discernment, there are the five kinds of wall.

Striving to destroy evil, unwholesome states that have arisen is the eastern portico. Discerning so as not to produce those that have not arisen is the western portico. Striving to maintain those that have arisen is the northern portico. The purity of the four right abandonments is the four porticoes. Through the applications of mindfulness, called body, feeling, mind and phenomena, there are the four platforms. Through the purity of the dhāraṇīs of patience, of Dharma, of meaning and of mantra, there is the host of goddesses dwelling on the terrace, linked with the offerings of song, dance and the rest.

The door of liberation that is emptiness is the eastern door. The door of liberation that is signlessness is the southern door. The door of liberation that is wishlessness is the western door. The door of liberation that is non-composition is the northern door. The basis of miraculous power that is aspiration is the eastern canopy. The basis of miraculous power that is diligence is the southern door. The basis of miraculous power that is mind is the western door. The basis of miraculous power that is investigation is the northern door.

Being moistened by compassion, and the accumulations of merit and wisdom being completely perfected, are the full vases. Through the purity of the seven limbs of awakening, namely mindfulness, discernment of phenomena, joy, pliancy, meditative absorption, diligence and equanimity, there are the flower garlands. The wisdom of the Tathāgata's path, freed from all obscurations, in which meditative absorption and discernment are perfectly united, is the yak-tail whisks. Destroying the afflictions after drawing out the seeds of the afflictions is the necklaces. Splitting the afflictions after destroying the seeds of the afflictions is the half necklaces. Through the mirror-like and the other wisdoms, there are the mirrors. Because, being declared inseparable from all beings, it is not stained by the afflictions that dwell there, there is the lotus. Because sentient beings arise as equal, through the purity of the awakening mind, luminous by nature, there is the moon. The discerning wisdom born from the vajra-like meditative absorption is the sun disc.

Being unerring with regard to form and the rest, they are the unsurpassed qualities that pass beyond the extremes. Because they accord as causes with the utterly pure sphere of reality, there are the five kinds of offerings. Through a sense of shame and a sense of embarrassment, there are the garments. Because the sublime Dharma that moves those to be trained, namely the section of discourses, the section of sung verses, the section of explanations, the section of verses, the section of inspired utterances, the section of "thus it happened," the section of extensive teachings and the section of marvels, dwells in all the words of the nine limbs of view, there is the sound of the bells at the tips of the many-colored flags, stirred by the wind. Through the purity of the vision of the flesh eye, the divine eye, the wisdom eye, the Dharma eye and the buddha eye, there are the five colors.

The maṇḍala of Nairātmyā is likewise, but this is the difference: through the purity of the noble eightfold path, namely right view, right thought, speech and the completion of action, livelihood and effort, mindfulness, meditative absorption and perception, there are the eight pillars. The turning of the wheel of the Dharma of the Vajra Vehicle by all the buddhas, everywhere and without obstruction, is the vajra garland that links the tops of the eight pillars. Through gathering all the world systems into selflessness, there are the eight charnel grounds.

This is called the reality of the maṇḍala.

Discernment of the sense base of form, become body, is Gaurī. The essence of the discernment of the sense base of sound, become mind, is Caurī. The essence of the discernment of the sense base of smell, become speech, is Vetālī. The essence of the discernment of the sense base of taste, become wisdom, is Ghasmarī. The earth element, the essence of the wisdom of the reality of body, is Pukkasī. The water element, the essence of the wisdom of the reality of mind, is Śabarī. The fire element, the essence of the wisdom of the reality of speech, is Caṇḍālī. The wind element, the essence of the true wisdom of wisdom, is Ḍombinī. The fruit of the true wisdom thus described is the state of the great Vajradhara, unsurpassed awakening, glorious Hevajra.

In the maṇḍala of Nairātmyā, the essential nature of the sense base of touch and of the faculty of discernment is Bhūcarī. The essence of the sense base of mental objects, and of faith, diligence, mindfulness, meditative absorption, discernment and the powers, is Khecarī. The essence of the faculty of faith is Pātālavāsinī. The essence of the faculty of diligence is Caurī. The essence of the faculty of mindfulness is Vetālī. The essence of the faculty of meditative absorption is Ghasmarī. The essence of the grounds Very Joyous, Stainless and Luminous is Pukkasī. The essence of the ground Manifest is Śabarī. The essence of Far Gone and Immovable is Caṇḍālī. The essence of Good Intelligence and Cloud of Dharma is Ḍombinī. The essence of the aggregate of form, the sense base of the eye, great delusion, the buddha ground, and mirror-like wisdom is Vajrā. The essential nature of the aggregate of feeling, the sense base of the nose, great pride, the buddha ground, and the wisdom of equality is Secret Gaurī. The essential nature of the aggregate of perception, the sense base of the tongue, great desire, the buddha ground, and discriminating wisdom is Vāriyoginī. The essence of the aggregate of formations, the sense base of the body, great envy, the buddha ground, and the wisdom of accomplishing activities is Vajraḍākinī. The essence of the aggregate of consciousness, the sense base of the mind, great aversion, the buddha ground, and the utterly pure sphere of reality is Nairātmyā.

This is called the reality of the deities.

There is also the outer and the inner purification, the purification of the site and so forth, and the thorough purification by the master.

Then the one who possesses the three pledges should give the vajra observance:

This vajra, born from the syllable hūṃ,
is all the buddhas themselves.
It abides in the hand of Vajrasattva,
the steadfast observance of Vajrapāṇi.
You too must always hold it.

Having said this, one has him hold it. And the disciple too should say oṃ sarba ta thā ga ta siddhi bajra sa ma ya tiṣṭha e ṣa stvaṃ dha ra ya mi ba ho hi hi hi hi hūṃ and take hold of the vajra. This is called the vajra observance.

Then the guru, with this reality,

The being without beginning or end,
Vajrasattva, great joy,
Samantabhadra, lord of all,
Vajragarbha, chief of lords,

has the disciple hold the vajra. This is the vajra observance and the vajra pledge.

After that, with this,

This bell, born from the syllable a,
is declared to follow the voice
of the wisdom of all the buddhas,
the scripture of the Victors' supreme awakening.
You too must always hold it,

one has him hold it; and with this reality of the bell,

Because existence is pure by nature,
it comes forth by its very nature;
because beings are pure by nature,
it makes sublime existence pure,

the disciple too takes hold of it and should ring it. This is the vajra observance and the pledge of the bell.

Then one should teach the mudrā of one's chosen deity with this:

You, steadfast one, the body of mind,
fully realize the mudrā.
Whatever is steadfast in every respect
is therefore called a mudrā.

The disciple too, following the guru's words, meditates on the mudrā of his own deity and should bless it with this, called "the realization of that very mudrā." The vajra observance and the realization of the mudrā: this is the vajra observance endowed with the three pledges.

Then, visualizing oneself in the form of the Buddha, one holds the corner of one's robe with the left fist and places it at the heart, and faces the disciple with the right hand extended in the great mudrā of granting the supreme boon, saying:

oṃ Here I shall give you the prophecy:
you are the Sugata Vajrasattva.
For the sake of perfecting existence,
you will draw beings out of the evil destinies of existence.

he śrī a mu kaṃ bajra nā ma siddhya bhur bhu ba svaḥ

Having said this, one should give the prophecy.

With this extended great mudrā, whoever is given the prophecy is prophesied by all the Tathāgatas and bodhisattvas, with one voice, to unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening. One should say: "This alone is the mudrā, the mantra and the blessing of the vajra holders." This is called the prophecy.

Then one should give the encouragement endowed with the three pledges:

Having seen the sublime maṇḍala of the great secret,
you have entered it in the supreme way;
you are freed from all evil,
and today you are well established.
Since, if you do not abandon great bliss,
you will never die again,
where you are not seen
practice without fear of anyone.
For existence to be perfectly accomplished,
you are freed from the ocean of the suffering of existence;
in order to strike down existence completely,
you arise truly within the supreme teaching.
Accomplishment, pledge and vow:
you must always hold to these,
the pure command of the eternal supreme ones,
spoken alike by all the buddhas.
Do not abandon the awakening mind,
which is the vajra mudrā,
by merely looking at which
one is buddhahood itself, without doubt.
Never reject
nor cast away the sublime Dharma.
If, through the delusion of ignorance,
you were ever to proclaim it,
you would abandon yourself by your own hand.
Do not torment yourself with austerities;
holding bliss as bliss, just as it is,
you will awaken in the future.
The vajra, the bell and the mudrā too
you must never cast away.
Do not despise the master:
he is equal to all the buddhas.
The guru is equal to all the buddhas;
if one despises this master,
one despises all the buddhas,
and will always meet with suffering.
Slain by plagues, harms and diseases,
by grahas, fevers and poisons,
by obstructors and terrible vināyakas,
such beings go to hell.
Therefore, with every effort,
never revile the vajra master,
the great guru,
who does not vaunt his virtue.
To the guru, with respect,
offer a fee in keeping with your means;
by this, fevers and other torments
will not arise again.
Always accomplish your own pledges;
always worship the Tathāgatas;
always make offerings to the guru as well:
the guru is equal to all the buddhas.
Therefore, to all the buddhas
it becomes a perpetual gift.
That gift is the accumulation of merit,
and from the accumulation comes supreme accomplishment.
Today you, with your birth and its fruit,
having entered here,
will abide equal to the deities
in pledge, without doubt.
By the buddhas, the vajra holders,
you are empowered today;
great king of the three realms,
you abide as the chief of kings.
Today you have conquered Māra
and entered the supreme city;
today you will attain buddhahood:
have no doubt about this.

Eat your own terrible, bliss-giving pledge substances,
and with your mind have deep faith in the vajra.
That swift bliss of beings is Vajrasattva;
into perfect and eternal equality you have entered today.

The disciple, respectful and gentle,
having bowed at the guru's two feet, says:
"Whatever the lord commands,
please speak, and so I shall do."

These are the giving of encouragement.

Then one should give him the authorization. For this, first, endowed with the yoga of one's own principal deity, one recites over a golden probe, gives it, and should say this:

As the king of physicians with a probe
removes the dim sight of the world,
so the gentle Victors remove
the cataract of your ignorance.

oṃ bajra nai tra a pa ha ra ba tra laṃ hrīḥ. Visualizing the syllable praṃ on the disciple's two eyes, one should recite this.

Then, showing the mirror, one should speak of the characteristic of phenomena:

Phenomena are like reflections,
pure, clear and free of turbidity,
ungraspable and inexpressible,
arisen together from causes and actions,
without intrinsic nature and without location.
Knowing phenomena thus with certainty,
accomplish the unequalled welfare of beings:
you will be born among the guides.

So it is said.

Then, ringing the bell born from the final vowel, one should give it with this:

All has the characteristic of space,
and space too has no characteristic.
From union equal to space,
supreme equality becomes clear.

Then, with the syllable ho, one gives the bow and says this: sarba ta thā ga ta a nu rā ga ya svā hā. One should shoot four arrows in the four directions; one is shot upward, and also downward.

With this, oṃ bajra satva, one takes up the mirror and should say:

Vajrā, like a mirror,
pure and clear, free of concepts,
supreme lord of all the buddhas,
dwells in your gentle heart.

One should make him devoted to this awakening mind of the lord of all the Tathāgatas.

Then, with oṃ bajra he tu maṃ, the wheel is placed beneath his two feet. With oṃ bajra bta ṣa raṃ, a conch is placed in his right hand and a book in his left. Having generated the bell from the first vowel, and giving it again with the final vowel, one should say this: "From this day on, by merely equalling the generation of the mind, turn the wheel of the Dharma thus:

The unsurpassed conch of the Dharma
is proclaimed with certainty in every direction,
with no desire and no wavering in it,
with a mind free of doubt.
The rite of the vehicle of mantra conduct
you must always teach to the worldly.
Doing so in gratitude, it is said,
benefits the buddhas.
Then the vajra holders
will protect you all everywhere."

Again, visualizing oneself in the very form of the Buddha as universal monarch, one says:

In all worlds without exception,
for the benefit of sentient beings,
turn the wheel of the Dharma
that trains in various ways.

Likewise:

Turn the vajra, the jewel, the lotus
and the action wheels.

With five verses one should give the authorization. This is the giving of authorization. That which culminates in the giving of the observance and the authorization should also be given at the end of the pacifying rite.

Then, having examined the disciple thoroughly
and performed all the knowledge mantras of the family,
one recites over the bracelet with the protection mantra
and should tie it on the right hand.
A parasol should be held over the crown of the head,
and, having made the circumambulations,
at the door of the maṇḍala, with palms joined,
he should prostrate together with the parasol.
To the disciple one should say this:
"You have become a holder of the tantra."
The master displays the parasol
and with it instructs him at the heart:
"Now you will become a master of the maṇḍala;
you will also hold the mantras and the tantras.
This is the word of the deities,
together with the buddhas and bodhisattvas:
in order to protect sentient beings,
you, as a practitioner, by the maṇḍala rite,
in accordance with the tantra,
should diligently draw it."

Then, having recited one's own mantra one hundred and eight times, one should give it to the disciple with these words:

"I give you the Blessed One;
here he will truly draw near."

The disciple too takes it, and taking it with the words "Blessed One, by me, you ..." he should recite.

Then, having blessed the four mudrās of one's own deity, one should say: "You must never, not even once, utter this mantra or show this mudrā to others who do not know them. Why is that? Because such a being has not seen the maṇḍala, he would become doubtful about obtaining the fruit of the practice of mudrā and maṇḍala, and without having given up his dread he would die and go to hell; and on that ground you too would go to hell."

This is the general master empowerment. The special one is:

With the wisdom consort who has reached sixteen,
one embraces in union, the two reversed;
by the union of vajra and bell
the master's empowerment is held to be conferred.

This is called the transcendent master empowerment. The master empowerment alone is also called the irreversible empowerment, the empowerment of the great Vajradhara. It is called the master empowerment, both mundane and transcendent. The knowledge empowerments and the master empowerment are called the vase empowerment, because one is consecrated with the jewel vase. Having as its nature the emanation body, with the characteristic of the complete purity of body, it is called the vase empowerment.

The supreme master is the Lord;
he truly grants the master empowerment.
He surely bestows the excellent samaya,
the supreme of wondrous vows:
elephant, human and the rest, cattle and the rest,
down to the last of them, should be eaten.
For the sake of the accomplishment of Hevajra,
likewise eat the five nectars.
You shall destroy living beings,
you shall speak what is untrue,
you shall take what is not given,
and you shall resort to the women of others.
"Life" is said to be the mind;
making the mind one is killing living beings.
"I shall liberate the world, the unsurpassed":
these words are the speaking of falsehood.
The fluid of the queen is what is not given;
the most beautiful one is the woman of another.
This very samaya
the perfect Buddha has truly taught.
You must guard the great feast;
it is a vow hard to cultivate.

Then with the five offerings,
and especially with the sense objects,
the practitioner wholly devoted to the guru,
the disciple, should make worship according to the rite.

This is the giving of the samaya and so forth.

Then the disciple performs the feast gathering, and after that should offer the guru eight knowledge consorts:

The mother who gave birth, the sister,
the daughter and the niece,
likewise the wife of the maternal uncle,
the maternal aunt and the mother-in-law,
and likewise the father's sister:
these are well known as the eight knowledge consorts.
They are the very nature of the eight goddesses,
granting accomplishment to the practitioner.

If these are lacking, one should offer knowledge consorts such as Vajrā, born of the families of the five Tathāgatas.

The dancer, the dyer, the low-caste woman,
likewise the caṇḍālī and the brahmin woman
are said to be the five knowledge consorts
born of the families of the Tathāgatas.
With passionate embraces and kisses,
then, worshipping her, the yogin,
by the union of bola and kakkola,
should sport with them.
There liquor too should be poured,
and strengthening food made from śāli rice should be eaten.
Drinking and eating
should likewise be shown again.
In the second session, outer and inner,
the disciple should be brought in there.
Having covered his eyes with a cloth,
he should be brought into that circle.
With cooked rice made from śālu grain
feed the disciple until he is satisfied.
Let him drink liquor there as well,
and the yoginīs too should be worshipped.
A great skull of a single piece
is filled to the brim with liquor
and offered to the teacher of great fortune;
then, bowing, one drinks it oneself.
The giving too is done by oneself.
Likewise the practitioner
should bow again and again.
Whether she is mother or sister,
mother-in-law or niece,
with a nonconceptual mind
accomplishment is attained before long.

Then the master, with a gathering of yoginīs and of the family of yogins, endowed with the five sense pleasures and the five offerings, together with song, the sound of music and offerings, gathers up the maṇḍala. Having made the place free of people with screens of cloth and the like, and having stretched a canopy complete with all its trimmings, with fringes hanging down, he sits near, on a soft cushion with a pair of backrests, in a place fumigated with incense and lit with lamps. Any one of those families, or one who pleases the practitioner: with fine hair and a lovely face, with wide eyes and a dark blue complexion, marked with the form of reality, adorned with youth and of good family, endowed with faith and compassion, empowered by oneself through steadiness in these, skilled in the three meditative absorptions, adorned with fine clothes and fragrant with incense and camphor; or else whoever can be found, a knowledge consort twelve years old or sixteen years old, is offered by the disciple. Such a one, adorned with clothes, with flowers, with limbs that captivate the mind and beautiful in passion, he should take onto his lap, arouse passion, and embrace.

Then, beholding the guru together with the knowledge consort, one should worship him properly with the five kinds of offerings and with many offerings: parasols, victory banners, flags, ornaments of cloth, liquor and the like, and meat and the like. One should prostrate with all the limbs and praise him:

O Blessed One, great peaceful one,
supreme and sole vajra yoga,
arising from the union of the vajra,
in which mudrā and practitioner are not divided:
just as you, great being, have done,
O Lord, do the same for me as well.
In the mire of saṃsāra
I am sunk: I beg you for refuge.

So it is said. Then, at the end of that, the master and the consort should drink, setting their teeth to each other, and he should also touch the two breasts and the lotus. With various modes of entry, with the legs bent, hanging down, and so forth, he should resort to her in the kunduru union for as long as there is fluid. At that stage, at the time of release, one praises with the verse beginning "O Blessed One," makes manifest the yoga of one's own deity, and binds the faces of the wisdom consort and the disciple. Then that which is like the full moon, filled with the melted essence of all the Tathāgatas, arisen from the kunduru union and abiding within the vajra jewel, one casts with thumb and ring finger into the space of the disciple's mouth, and one should say this:

Just as the buddhas of the past
empowered the heirs of awakening,
so by my secret empowerment
I empower your mind-stream.

So it is done. The disciple too says aho sukha and should swallow it down into the belly. The consort too is made unclothed, and from what has arisen from the union at the lotus seat the disciple, with his mouth, likewise swallows into the belly, and so remains. Having the characteristic of the complete purity of speech and the nature of the enjoyment body, this is called the secret empowerment.

Then, if master and disciple wish to receive the wisdom-gnosis empowerment, then with that same consort, or with another one, kunduru is performed likewise as before; and the disciple too, at the time of perfect awakening, having praised as before, should request the empowerment of the Victor. Then the master rises and, seeing that the disciple is wondrous, free of anger and without jealousy, should entrust the consort to him:

With a mind that guides and benefits,
the Teacher then declared:
"This goddess, giver of joy, giver of abode,
of various forms, delightful to the mind:
great being, take her,
and, vajra holder, perform kunduru.
Take her, take her, great being;
take her and make offerings."

Distinguished by the division of moments,
joy then comes to arise,
and in the moment of the gnosis of bliss
evaṃ fully abides.
The first is joy alone,
in the second is supreme joy,
the third is called the joy of cessation,
and likewise the fourth is the innate.
Joy is a little bliss;
supreme joy is greater than that;
the joy of cessation is passion;
the innate is what remains.
At first there is the wish for touch,
in the second, the wish for bliss,
the third, because passion is spent;
therefore one should meditate on the fourth.
Supreme joy is called existence,
and nirvāṇa is freedom from passion;
the middle is joy alone;
free of these is the innate.
Variety and maturation,
consummation, and likewise freedom from characteristics:
the four moments meet together,
and thus the yogin comes to know them.
Embracing, kissing and the like
are explained as variety, of various aspects.
Maturation is the reverse of that:
the very enjoying of the gnosis of bliss.
The thought "I have consumed bliss"
is said to be consummation.
Freedom from characteristics is other than the three,
free of both passion and passionlessness.
In variety is the first joy;
in maturation, supreme joy;
in consummation, the joy of cessation;
innate joy is free of characteristics.
It is not passion, not freedom from passion,
nor is it apprehended as a middle.
Only in abandoning the three
is the innate declared complete.
The three joys are abandoned,
and that too is indicated by the first joy;
supreme, final, cessation, and in the middle,
emptiness from emptiness, Heruka.

So it is said. It is taught in the order of this enumeration, and following the order of their arising, the innate should be realized in the place of the joy of cessation.

Then the consort too, as though full of desire, gazes at the practitioner and should say this:

Eating excrement and urine,
blood and semen, and likewise flesh,
ever supremely devoted to women:
gentle one, are you able?
Kissing the lotus of the bhaga,
gentle one, tell me how blissful it is.

Then the practitioner, with a smiling face,
should speak these words:

Eating semen, blood and the rest,
why, goddess, would I not delight?
Likewise I kiss the bhaga,
and ever revere women.

Then, delighted, she is made unclothed, anointed with fine scents such as saffron, musk, camphor and sandalwood, and adorned in that place with fine flowers. Sitting squatting, she reveals her own sign and should say this:

Ah, my precious lotus
is the true abode of all bliss.
Whoever resorts to it according to the rite,
before him I abide.
Serving the perfect Buddha and the rest,
do in the lotus what is to be done;
this very king of great bliss
likewise abides there.

So she says, with bhañja mokṣa ho. Then the practitioner, by the guru's instruction, visualizes her in the form of Nairātmyā through the transformation of the seed syllables and the signs, and at her heart, crown and throat he should see the syllables hūṃ, oṃ and āḥ. Then the lotus and the vajra should be purified with this: oṃ padma sukhadhara mahārāga sukhanda caturānanda bhagabiśva hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ karyaṃ kuruṣvame oṃ vajra mahādveṣa caturānanda dāyaka khagamukha ekarasvanatha hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ karyaṃ kuruṣvame. After that he should gaze at her with joy and with eyes of desire, and with embracing and kissing, as the nature of Nairātmyā abiding within the lotus, he plays upon the three channels with finger or tongue, and he should resort to her until the fluid of both comes forth.

Moreover, beginning from a thorough examination of that, the path of accumulation, the paths of seeing and of meditation, the special path, the culminating path and the uninterrupted path, that is, perfect and complete awakening, should be made known by means of signs through the guru's instruction. The awakening mind abiding at the tip of the vajra jewel is the awakening mind, the innate great bliss: great bliss, in which the proliferation of all the senses has ceased; not abiding in objects, yet through the power of nonapprehension the individually determined objects proliferate; the manner of the example of the ultimate innate. Whatever comes to be experienced, one makes it stable, through the power of familiarity with the instruction, by the rite of pressing, by mastery of the winds, or by external protection, as far as one is able; and it should be pointed out in order that the ultimate innate may be apprehended.

It is also said:

As long as the yogin
does not let go of the awakening mind,
so long he attains the unbroken;
what need is there to speak of the bliss born of joy?
It is the bliss of all blisses.
If the awakening mind should fall,
then, because the aggregate of consciousness swoons,
how could blameless accomplishment come to abide?

So it is said.

That union which has arisen in equality
the wise should not cast aside.
Then it should not be taken up with the hand,
nor with a skull cup or a conch;
having taken up the nectar with the tongue,
it surely brings increase and strength.
Then, rising, the intelligent one
should bow down at the guru's feet.
Supreme joy is truly attained
in the moment that is free of variety.
The Teacher said: "Great being,
hold fast to great bliss.
Until awakening,
be a vajra holder for the benefit of beings.
Pacifying, increasing and the rest
will all be accomplished through this."
From the nature of the five elements,
in kunduru it becomes fivefold;
from the single great joy
it comes to be divided into five.
Through the union of bola and kakkola,
by touch, there is the latent imprint of solidity;
because solidity is of the nature of delusion,
delusion is declared to be Vairocana.
Since whatever is the awakening mind
is moist, it is called the water element;
water, because it is the form of Akṣobhya,
the guide of aversion is Akṣobhya.
From the union through the friction of the two,
heat always comes to arise;
passion is the vajra Amitābha.
Whatever mind is in the kakkola,
that has the nature of wind;
jealousy is Amoghasiddhi.
The passion of bliss becomes blood;
joy has the characteristic of space;
the avarice of space becomes vajra;
avarice indeed arises from space.
In the single great mind
it is indicated in five forms.
From these very five families
many thousands have arisen.
Therefore these are of a single nature,
great bliss, supreme and permanent.
This itself is the great gnosis,
entirely free of all conceptual thought,
the manner of duality and of nonduality,
the lord whose nature is entity and nonentity.
Means and wisdom are thoroughly blended,
passion and passionlessness are blended.
That itself is the life of living beings;
that itself is the supreme imperishable syllable.
It abides pervading the stable and the moving,
and is held to have the form of an illusion.
By the means of the maṇḍala circle
one surely always comes to it.
Either it is the self of all,
or else it is free of all.
That too is indicated first in the joy of cessation,
with the three joys abandoned.
This gnosis is most subtle,
like the center of the vajra sky.
Peaceful, near to liberation, born of passion,
you yourself are its father.

So it is said. Having the characteristic of the complete purity of mind and the essence of the dharmadhātu, it is called the wisdom-gnosis empowerment.

Again, if for these two empowerments the real one is lacking, it is done by union with a companion summoned through meditation. Here this has been said:

Master, secret, wisdom,
and the fourth likewise:
by the sequence of joy and the rest,
know that these four are conferred in order.

So it is said. As to how there is a fourth empowerment: in the Hevajra Tantra the empowerments are held to be of three kinds, and "the fourth again is likewise" means that what is other than the third is the garland, the water and so forth. The completion of the empowerment was stated in summary by such texts as the Compendium of Reality. Since in the chapter of the vajra family four kinds of empowerment are stated, it comes out as one wishes; that being so, the four kinds of empowerment, made manifest from wisdom-gnosis, are applied to devotion. Likewise, again:

Purified by laughter, it is held to be the master's;
gazing likewise is the secret;
embracing with the hands is the wisdom;
and there too, two by two.

So it is said. The meaning of this is as follows. Laughing and so forth together with the wisdom consort, joy and the like, is the form of the worship of all the Tathāgatas; because it is the master, that is the master empowerment. Gazing at the lotus with joy toward the wisdom consort, because it relies on the secret, is the secret empowerment. Embracing the wisdom consort, because one relies on the wisdom consort and is gladdened, is the wisdom empowerment. And the nondual gnosis that again arises from the union of the two, together with the pointing out of the common and the supreme reality, is held to be the fourth.

Others hold them to be separate in this way, because the first chapter says, "in every case there are four." Some say that the manifest is the application of indivisible devotion; therefore, from that very mind empowerment, by dividing it into two kinds, the fourth empowerment should be taught. Likewise, the great innate bliss gathered in the limbs of the wisdom consort, having the characteristic of being relied upon, because it relies on the wisdom of the wisdom consort, is called the wisdom-gnosis empowerment. This union of innate great bliss, with a mind in meditative equipoise, which is to be meditated upon just as this samaya is, is the union of the supreme innate; the great paṇḍita, the glorious Ratnākaraśāntipāda, taught this clearly throughout The Union of the Supreme Innate. In the commentary on The Union of the Supreme Innate called The Beauty of the Innate, which I myself composed, I have also properly explained the meaning of the empowerment; here, for fear of the text growing too long, I have not elaborated on it. All of these are to be explained to the disciple at the time of the wisdom-gnosis empowerment by the guru abiding in meditative equipoise. Having the characteristic of the complete purity of body, speech and mind, and the essence of great bliss, it is called the fourth empowerment.

The three empowerments as they have been described should not be given to śrāvakas, to kings, to non-Buddhists (tīrthikas), to dry logicians, to the dull, to eunuchs, to the old, to one's own son, or to those without faith. Furthermore, no empowerment at all, whether of the garland or of the others, should be conferred on anyone by mere words. This is the rite of empowerment.

The consecration of the Dharma and likewise of the Sugata, of books, vajra and bell,
extends equally with the element of space;
since worldly people do not know this ultimate protector,
one who wishes to benefit the worlds performs the consecration.
Images, stūpas and temples,
the true Dharma, painted scrolls and books,
the excellent, those of proper measure and the lesser,
complete and endowed with the continuum of bliss:
one who knows their family and their marks
ought to perform the consecrations.
It brings about the increase of merit,
and it is also rejoicing itself.
Where an image, though complete,
remains for a long time unworshipped,
as long as it has not been worshipped,
it gives rise to what is not virtuous.
For example, if some person
is devoid of the marks of knowledge,
the whole world does not honor him;
images too become just the same.
To bow down and make offerings
to that which lacks consecration
is like a fire offering without mantra:
the burnt substances are fruitless ash.
Likewise, whoever makes offerings
to an abode that is without mantra,
through the ripening of that karma
is in all his births unworthy of honor.

For that, on an auspicious day endowed with an auspicious lunar mansion and conjunction of planets, after bathing and cleansing, one anoints the body with fine scents and, with fine ornaments, a topknot and good companions, holding vajra and bell in the hands and having gathered all the requisites, one sits on a soft seat strewn with fine flowers. Having performed the maṇḍala offering and the three meditative absorptions, facing the deity, with the three, one views the vajra masters as conforming to the one endowed with all the supreme aspects; one blesses with the seed syllable and the sign the maṇḍala, together with its sign, seat, support and supported, at the heart, and views it as endowed with the blessing on the crown that accords with its family. Having visualized the maṇḍala of wisdom beings in the sky as well, one gives the purifying torma and the rest. With the eight offering goddesses emanated from the light rays of the seed syllable at one's own heart, one makes proper offerings, and one should praise and taste the nectar.

Then, with fine flowers, joining the palms and bowing the body, one says oṃ sarvatathāgata sulalita vilāsanamiste namāmihaṃ bhagavānti jaḥ hūṃ vaṃ hoḥ pratīccha kusumāñjali nātha hoḥ samayastvaṃ samayastvaṃ samayastvaṃ and should cast the flowers from the palms. Then, setting the right knee on the ground and fumigating with incense in the right hand, one should establish the principal deity and the others in the preparatory rite:

Blessed One, So-and-so, supreme vajra,
king of knowledge, I bow and praise you.
The consecration of vajra nature
I, O protector, wish to perform.
In order to protect the disciples
and also to make offerings to you all,
Blessed One, it is right that you
show your kindness to me in this.
Just as the perfect buddhas
truly dwelt in Tuṣita
and in the womb of the goddess Māyā,
and, having dwelt there, acted here,
so, Protector, always dwell here,
and accept these flowers and the rest
for the sake of the increase
of the awakening mind of So-and-so.

Thus one should establish the preparation three times. Offerings and so forth should be made as before. Then, seated outside, one should give torma to the guardians of the directions. Then, going inside, one takes a thread twisted three-ply and with three knots, recited over with the mantra of all activities, and ties it on the right hand of each in turn; one sprinkles water recited over with the mantra of all activities and should protect the disciple. In all the activities one should move about together with the disciple who keeps this observance, and have him recite these words: "Whatever little the mantrin says, by the rite of worship and the rest, in bringing about the great fruit of offering and giving, be free of wavering." Then, in order to pacify the obstructors, one recites the root mantra in full and should sleep. This is the rite of consecration.

Then, on the next day, the mantrin, with the characteristics as described, gives the pacifying torma and sits facing the deity. Having performed the yoga of the deity, one sets the image facing east, dresses it in yellow cloth, and makes it complete through the yoga of completion with the characteristics described before; endowed with the supreme of all aspects, it is blessed at the heart, the throat and the crown with the three syllables hūṃ, āḥ and oṃ, and properly placed upon a clean seat, four-cornered and square, two cubits in height; and the attendants should carry out the tasks. Then, taking yellow mustard seed with the right and left fists, one circles it in the forward and reverse directions, empowers it three times with oṃ sarva pāpaṃ dahana vajraya svāhā and should cast it into the fire. Likewise one should cast it into water with that same mantra. Then the tasks should also be done with khampo into which soma has been poured, and with barley-flour beer. After that one thoroughly sprinkles it with this, empowered seven times with oṃ sarvatathāgata kāya viśodhane svāhā, together with sesame oil and a bundle of dūrvā grass, and wipes it. It should then be washed with water kept in a bronze vessel, with the banyan, the udumbara, the śirīṣa, the aśvattha, the plakṣa and the bakula, and with the five scents blessed with the five seed syllables. After that, by the procedure for the royal coronation, together with a man holding a white parasol, one should praise it with verses of victory and auspiciousness. Then, with the empowerment rite marked by the sound of drums, the sound of conches, the ululations of women and sweet songs, and with the vase held in the hand of the Tathāgata, one recites the verse of bathing and should perform the bathing. The verse is:

Just as at the very moment of birth
the Tathāgatas were bathed,
with pure divine water
likewise bathe me.

oṃ sarvatathāgata abhiṣekatasmaya śriye hūṃ. Paintings, books and the like should be washed in a mirror.

Likewise one should recite the verses of auspiciousness, uttered for that purpose:

The auspiciousness praised by the Dharma body,
which has fully abandoned oneness and manyness,
free of identifying and grasping, without elaboration:
by that auspiciousness may you bring peace.
The auspiciousness adorned with the minor marks, the marks and the rest,
born from abiding in the center,
the enjoyment body, of excellent Dharma speech:
by that auspiciousness may you bring peace.
The auspiciousness that blazes like fire,
whose blazing light whirls all around,
the emanation body that accomplishes the good of others:
by that auspiciousness may you bring peace.

These are the verses of auspiciousness. Having bathed it thoroughly in that sequence, one dries it of water with a clean, soft garment or cloth, smears the two robes with a saffron paste, and should clothe it with this mantra: oṃ divyā vāsame svāhā. Then, with this, oṃ vajra gandhe āḥ hūṃ, one should anoint it evenly with whatever scents are available, such as camphor, musk, sihlaka incense and sandalwood. With oṃ vajra bharaṣa vibhuṣane svāhā it should be adorned with ornaments such as earrings, necklaces, armlets and bracelets. Then one should tie a diadem on the crown with the mantra oṃ vajra ratna tramiti. With oṃ vajrasatva one should show it the mirror. With a stick made of gold, one should open the eyes with this: oṃ vajra cakṣu cakṣu samanta cakṣu viśodhane svāhā.

Then, having offered a mark of gorocana, one properly protects a little of the flowers, incense, lamps and betel with the mantra of all activities, takes them up with the syllable hūṃ, brings them into the maṇḍala with Ṭakki or with the protection mantra, and should set them out together. Then, by the rite of accomplishing the maṇḍala, one accomplishes the wisdom maṇḍala or the wisdom being and offers the scattered offerings, the purifying torma and so on. With the sound of hūṃ from the eight faces one cuts down the kinds of obstructors that have remained along with it; with ja hūṃ vaṃ hoḥ one summons it, makes it enter the completed image, binds it and brings it under control. One blesses the eyes, the body and so forth, confers empowerment, and again offers the purifying torma and the rest.

Then one should make offerings of flowers and the rest while reciting the mantras:

These sublime flowers,
born likewise from water and from dry land,
I offer with devotion;
please accept them as you please.
These sublime and supreme incenses,
natural or arising from compounding,
I offer with devotion;
please accept them as you please.
These sublime and supreme scents,
natural or likewise compounded,
I offer with devotion;
please accept them as you please.
This lamp of fine oil, beautifully blazing,
which steals away the darkness,
I offer with faith;
please accept it as you please.
Provided with food to eat and food to chew,
endowed with color, fragrance and flavor,
complete with every divine food:
please accept equally what is offered.

With each of its own verses one should recite the mantra. Then, having offered the scattered offerings, the drink and so on, one also worships with the offering of the five sense pleasures, and one should praise and taste the nectar.

Then the mantrin, in meditative equipoise, recites this verse: "With palms joined, from that very supreme one, because it has arisen from space and bears the marks"; and he should recite the hundred-syllable mantra up to seven times. Then the image should be made firm, and one should make supplication for the sake of sentient beings:

Protector, just as is fitting for the fortunate,
always act for the benefit of sentient beings,
taking on various forms
throughout all these world systems.

Thus it is said. Then, as before, having offered torma outside, one delights them with nectar, worships them properly, and should request the guardians of the directions to depart. After that, one bows to the deities who have entered the maṇḍala and to the maṇḍala and circumambulates them. In order to make up for any deficiency in the rite, at the end of the increasing fire offering as before, and in order to bring increase to the patron, one holds his right thumb with the left hand and gives, or has him give, three or seven portions of the burnt offering substances.

Then the guru should accept the fee from the disciple; and the disciple, with a joyful mind and in keeping with his own means, should offer a maiden adorned with every ornament; elephants, horses, chariots, cattle, buffaloes and the like; goods such as coral, gold, silver, copper, jewels and pearls; farmland rich in crops; houses, pleasure gardens, parks and other such places. The holy master and all the deities are delighted by these offerings and come to be pleased, and through their pleasure one should know that the desired accomplishments come. In all things one should please the guru.

Then, in a well-washed vessel, one prepares a gruel of the remainder of the burnt offering mixed with honey, milk and sugar, with the mantra of one's own deity. Its top portion is placed in a vessel of gold or the like and offered to the deity with this mantra: oṃ divyānaṃ samādhi dhyānaṃ pṛṇane svāhā.

Then the shrine room is built with great care, and on a pedestal one third the size of the image the image is set properly and upright, neither tilted too far forward, nor leaning back, nor askew, and it is requested to remain with this: oṃ supratiṣṭha vajra svāhā. Otherwise it would bring misfortune. Then, as before, one offers the purifying torma and the rest, makes proper offerings and praises, tastes the nectar and so forth, recites the hundred-syllable mantra seven times, and should ask forgiveness:

God, and your sons,
the family of the perfect Buddha who forgives,
and the hosts of gods such as Brahmā:
in the work of the rite of the vehicle,
having brought peace and auspiciousness everywhere
and made the qualities great,
depart to the realm of the buddhas,
and please come here again.
Remaining in this image
for eons and hundreds of eons,
may the gods of earth, water,
fire and wind protect it.
This having become greatly delighted,
depart with joy to your own abodes,
and protect, I pray, the master together with the disciples,
and their sons and grandsons.

Thus it is said.

Further, the particular points should be stated. Paintings and cast images are to be consecrated by the rite as it has been described. A stūpa of the Venerable One, too, should be generated by the rite for generating a multi-storied palace, and meditated on as the abode of all the buddhas and bodhisattvas. Invite the buddhas, the bodhisattvas, and all the vidyā-mantra deities without exception; make them enter into the stūpa, bind them, and bring them under control. Offer the purifying torma and the rest, perform the manifest offerings, the tasting of nectar, and so forth, and consecrate it with the mantras oṃ supratiṣṭha vajres svāhā and oṃ hūṃ hrī bhrūṃ dṛḍha tiṣṭha. The rest is as before.

A book is to be consecrated as follows. From the transformation of the syllable jṝṃ, regard the book as Amitābha; then meditate on it as rows of letters arisen from his transformation. Consecrate it with this, oṃ hūṃ hrī dhvaṃ, and meditate that it is blessed by all the Tathāgatas and all the Vajradharas. The rest is as before.

For a temple, generate with this mantra, oṃ hūṃ hrīḥ, the vast expanse of phenomena, the place of meditative absorption where all the buddhas and bodhisattvas, the śrāvakas and the pratyekabuddhas dwell. Then, having performed the preparatory rite of abiding (adhivāsana) and so on, and having offered torma, offerings, and the rest, consecrate it with the mantra oṃ supratiṣṭha vajre svāhā and oṃ hūṃ hrīḥ bhrūṃ khaṃ jṝm bhava dṛḍha teṣṭha bhu khaṃ. Meditate that the buddhas, bodhisattvas, and Vajradharas bless it, so that it is consecrated like an indestructible vajra. Wash it with the water of all the vases, and purify the temple with the mantra oṃ vajrodka abhiṣekana.

Then, having summoned with the mind Mahākāla, protector of the temple, make him enter and become one with it, bind him, and bring him under control. The master, possessing the yoga of two-armed Hevajra, should give him the command:

For as long as this temple stands,
you who are free of evil, protect it.
The tasks of the noble Saṅgha are hard;
swiftly accomplish their tasks.

Thus it is said.

Having done this, the guru, together with the joyful disciples, should call out and offer thanks:

The consecration, free of evil signs,
which you requested,
I have performed with diligence.
Now make offerings as you please.

Thus it is said.

Then the leftover broth of the mantra rite should be given:

These leftovers of the Sugatas
are to be eaten by those whose evil has waned.

To others, whatever one has obtained, a little should be given. Then the disciples, too, should worship the deities with many offerings, circumambulate three times, and prostrate. Then, seated in their own places, food should be served to the monks, nuns, laymen, laywomen, and the rest. Further, a feast should be given, as far as one is able, to the destitute who have no protector. After that, serve a feast to the artisans together with the master, satisfy them fully with gifts such as clothing, ask their forgiveness, and request them to depart.

At night, perform a feast gathering and make a great sound of music. Then the master should enter the maṇḍala and make offerings: holding a stick of incense in the left hand and ringing the bell with the right, he should offer praise, and request the deities to depart with the three syllables ending in muḥ. Put the colored powders in a clean vessel, give a little to those who want it, and cast the rest into a great river. The site of the maṇḍala, too, should be smeared with cow dung with the mantra oṃ akāro mukhaṃ sarbba dharmāṇāṃ ādyanutpannatvātta muḥ. Then a vast torma should be given to the guardians of the directions and the rest.

Since through such a maṇḍala as this
one gains accomplishments as one wishes,
in this life or in the next,
one should therefore be furnished with these.

It is also said:

Outcastes, bamboo workers and the like,
