The Dhāraṇī Given by the Nāgas, called The Heart of the Nāgas (Nāgadattadhāraṇī Nāgahṛdaya)

The Dhāraṇī Given by the Nāgas, called The Heart of the Nāgas, in Sanskrit Nāgadattadhāraṇī Nāgahṛdaya and in Tibetan Lü Jinpé Zung Lü Nyingpo, is a short dhāraṇī text catalogued as Toh 685 in the Kangyur, in its Tantra section. After a brief verse promising that whoever recites this knowledge mantra, spoken by the Buddha, will have nothing to fear from the nāgas, it consists almost entirely of long mantra formulas that invoke the great nāga kings by name and are aimed at neutralizing snake venom and protecting against serpents. A closing verse says that one who keeps it in mind day and night will not be troubled by the fear of snakes. Its colophon relates that Nāgārjuna brought it forth from beneath the earth and that it was translated into Tibetan by Tenpé Nyinjé, and it ends with the verse of dependent origination and a mantra of consecration.

In the language of India: nāgadatta ṅhāraṇi nāgahṛdaya nāma. In the language of Tibet: The Dhāraṇī Given by the Nāgas, called The Heart of the Nāgas.

Homage to the Buddha.

Whoever recites this knowledge mantra given by the nāgas,
spoken by the perfect Buddha,
for that person not the slightest fear
will arise from the hosts of nāgas.
