The Glorious Ornament of the Vajra Essence, Great King of Tantras (Śrīvajramaṇḍālaṃkāra-nāma-mahātantrarāja)

The Glorious Ornament of the Vajra Essence, Great King of Tantras, known in Sanskrit as the Śrī Vajramaṇḍālaṃkāra Mahātantrarāja and in Tibetan as Pal Dorjé Nyingpo Gyen, is Toh 490 in the Kangyur, where it belongs to the Tantra section among the tantras of the yoga class. It opens in the manner of a sūtra, with the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Body, Speech and Mind Vajra, presiding over an assembly of tathāgatas and bodhisattvas, and it goes on to unfold teachings in which mantra practice and maṇḍala ritual are joined with the Perfection of Wisdom. The text presents a series of meditations on the equality and emptiness of all phenomena, each sealed with its own mantra. Later sections describe how to choose sites for practice, how to lay out and draw the maṇḍalas of the buddha families, and how to give the consecrations that admit a disciple. According to its colophon, the Tibetan translation was begun by a translator called the glorious Chöjé, left slightly incomplete, and finished by Lodrö Tenpa.

In the language of India: śrī bajra maṇḍa a laṃ ka ra nā ma ma hā tan tra rā jā. In the language of Tibet: The Glorious Ornament of the Vajra Essence, Great King of Tantras.

With my head I bow in reverence at the lotus feet of the guru and of Mañjughoṣa.

Thus did I hear at one time. The Blessed One, the Tathāgata, Body, Speech and Mind Vajra, was dwelling there. He was endowed with the distinction of the manifold wisdom of the commitment consecrated by the vajra, the jewel, the precious gem, the lotus and the action of all the tathāgatas. With the jeweled crown of all the tathāgatas he had obtained empowerment as Dharma king of the three realms. He was the great lord of the yoga of the wisdom of the vajra commitment of all the tathāgatas, and had obtained the wisdom mudrā of all the tathāgatas. He had realized the equality of all the mudrās of all the tathāgatas, performed manifold activities, and carried out the vajra activity that fulfills every hope of all the realms of sentient beings, entirely and without exception. He was great compassion, the Great Vairocana, abiding permanently through the three times.

He dwelt in the following place. It was endowed with great precious jewels relied upon and praised by all the tathāgatas, hung with bells of many colors, and beautifully adorned with silken banners stirred by the wind, with clusters of flowers, and with nets and half nets. It was the whole expanse of reality, perfectly and thoroughly purified: the mansion of the expanse of reality, of boundless light. It was the temple of the Perfection of Wisdom, arrayed with the ornament of the supreme essence of flowering vines, its clouds spreading out oceans of world systems in succession and pervading the entire realm of space. It was consecrated by the Tathāgata. It was the center where buddhas beyond all extremes are born, the luminous celestial palace that is the essence of Vairocana, the abode of the tathāgatas, the Ornament of the Vajra Essence, the Body, Speech and Mind Vajra of all the tathāgatas. It was the sphere of activity of the limbs of the Tathāgata, which neither arise nor cease nor come to cessation, the clear limit of reality. It was the domain of the omniscient wisdom of all the tathāgatas, the mansion of the essence of the expanse of reality, the wisdom body of all the tathāgatas, the seat of awakening of all the tathāgatas, the supreme great ground. There he dwelt.

There, surrounded and attended in front by bodhisattva great beings as numerous as the atoms of all the buddha fields, and by tathāgatas, he taught the Dharma. He taught the gateway to the pure way of all phenomena, good in the beginning, good in the middle and good in the end, excellent in meaning, excellent in its words, unadulterated, complete, wholly pure and wholly immaculate.

Then again the Blessed One was the wisdom being of the nondual equality of the Body, Speech and Mind Vajra of all the tathāgatas, the sun of the wisdom of all the tathāgatas, the Vajra Essence that is the source of the commitment of complete victory. He had attained the wisdom of equality in which all phenomena are pure by their essential nature. He was deep like the ocean. He had obtained the wisdom of the mudrā of manifold activities. His body, speech and mind were vajra, like space. He was the great body, the vajra body, the body beyond extremes, the precious body, the Dharma body, the emanation body, the body of the equality of activity. He was the awakening mind, the unfailing one, the one who gathers all in, the increaser of virtue, the great king, the radiant one, the banner, the one who laughs, the one endowed with Dharma, the wheel of wisdom, the one of all languages, the manifold one, the protector, the one with the yakṣas, the one who joins, the one who has become the crown protuberance, the supreme one, the vajra bearer, the wheel bearer, the wisdom bearer, the great all pervading lord. His body, speech and mind were vajra, abiding perfectly throughout the whole expanse of space. He brought together all the tathāgatas. He was the wisdom being who comprehends the whole vajra expanse, the wisdom essence of the vajra blessing, as vast as the atoms of all the realms of space. By spreading through all the realms of space the great commitment of the unsurpassed vajra jewel empowerment of all the tathāgatas, he had become manifestly awakened in the direct knowledge of the tathāgatas. Through the pure essence of the very self of all the tathāgatas, he pervaded all of space, in which all phenomena are pure by their intrinsic nature. Through the wisdom that fully sees all forms, he was endowed with the supreme conduct of taming sentient beings entirely and without exception. Because all the tathāgatas gave him their unfailing command, he was possessed of the manifold, unsurpassed activity of the equality of all sentient beings.

Vairocana, the unchanging Akṣobhya,
Ratnaketu, the one who makes light,
Amitābha, protector of the victors,
And Amogha, who performs all activities.

He was the maṇḍala of the mirrorlike wisdom of all the tathāgatas. He was firmly established in the wisdom of equality of all the tathāgatas. He had attained the nondual discerning wisdom of all the tathāgatas, and had fully obtained the wisdom that accomplishes the deeds of all the tathāgatas. He was the pure expanse of reality of all the tathāgatas, the domain of the wisdom of the equality of all phenomena, the perfected, wholly pure wisdom that perceives the nondual equality of all the tathāgatas. Through emptiness, suchness, the limit of reality, signlessness, wishlessness and the four divine abidings, he had become the domain of the wisdom of the buddhas, and he perceived the equality of the domain of the buddhas. Having consecrated all the tathāgatas as the very Great Lord of Vajra Yoga, he abided everywhere throughout the directions and intermediate directions.

Then again the Blessed One, the Lord of Vajra Yoga, spoke to Mañjuśrī Kumārabhūta: "Mañjuśrī, invite all the buddhas and the vajra holders!"

When the Blessed One had spoken thus, Mañjuśrī Kumārabhūta, the great universal monarch, his face beaming with delight, snapped his vajra fingers in order to invite all the tathāgatas and the vajra holders, and uttered this great heart mantra that gathers all the tathāgatas: buddha samājaḥ āḥ hāḥ oṃ hūṃ vajra samājaḥ.

When he had uttered this, all the tathāgatas and the vajra holders clearly beheld the clouds of the boundless oceans of the successions of world systems arrayed with the ornament of the essence of the flower ground.

Then again Mañjuśrī Kumārabhūta, together with all the tathāgatas, united with the Blessed Vairocana, fully generated the wisdom of the vajra mind of all the tathāgatas, and abided in every place.

Then the Blessed Vairocana, the Lord of Yoga whose nature is the vajra of all the tathāgatas, consecrated himself as all the tathāgatas, and spoke to those tathāgatas and to the vajra holders: "O all you tathāgatas, I shall explain the heart of the great universal monarch of omniscient wisdom which is within me. All you tathāgatas, bestow your blessing! bhruṃ."

Then the Tathāgata Akṣobhya,
The Tathāgata Ratnasambhava,
The Tathāgata Amitābha,
And the Tathāgata Amoghasiddhi,

together with the other tathāgatas as well, entered into the śrīvatsa at the heart of all the tathāgatas. The moment they entered, all the tathāgatas fully revealed every bliss in the wheel.

Then the Blessed Vairocana took on four faces, and with his four faces he melodiously proclaimed this very examination of the way of the Dharma, the domain of all the buddhas, the expanse of reality and the infinite realm of space, as follows:

"Whatever is the state of the purity of utmost delight, that is the state of a bodhisattva. Whatever is the state of the purity of view, that is the state of a bodhisattva. Whatever is the state of the purity of joy, that is the state of a bodhisattva. Whatever is the state of the purity of craving, that is the state of a bodhisattva. Whatever is the state of the purity of attachment, that is the state of a bodhisattva. Whatever is the state of the purity of aversion, that is the state of a bodhisattva. Whatever is the state of the purity of delusion, that is the state of a bodhisattva. Whatever is the state of the purity of pride, that is the state of a bodhisattva. Whatever is the state of the purity of the unfree states, that is the state of a bodhisattva. Whatever is the state of the purity of contentment of mind, that is the state of a bodhisattva. Whatever is the state of the purity of light, that is the state of a bodhisattva. Whatever is the state of the purity of the body, that is the state of a bodhisattva. Whatever is the state of the purity of speech, that is the state of a bodhisattva. Whatever is the state of the purity of mind, that is the state of a bodhisattva. Whatever is the state of the purity of form, that is the state of a bodhisattva. Whatever is the state of the purity of sound, that is the state of a bodhisattva. Whatever is the state of the purity of smell, that is the state of a bodhisattva. Whatever is the state of the purity of taste, that is the state of a bodhisattva. Whatever is the state of the purity of touch, that is the state of a bodhisattva. Whatever is the state of the purity of all things, that is the state of a bodhisattva.

"If one asks why this is so: it is because all phenomena are pure by their intrinsic nature. Since all phenomena are pure by their intrinsic nature, the Perfection of Wisdom is completely pure."

Having spoken thus, the Blessed One, the lord of all secret mantras, the guide of all secret mantras, who has become the master of all mantras, the universal monarch of all the crown protuberances, whose teaching cannot be overridden, uttered the heart of the universal monarch: bhruṃ.

"Mañjuśrī, whoever hears even once this heart of the Perfection of Wisdom, which gathers together all phenomena, will never fall into the lower realms such as the hells, until he reaches the seat of awakening, even though he may have amassed all the obscurations and impediments, the obscuration of the afflictions, the obscuration of phenomena, and the great obscuration of karma. Even if he has committed evil deeds, he will be without suffering, and they will be exhausted. Whoever holds this, recites it each day, reads it, and continually attends to it properly, will in this very life attain the vajra samādhi of the equality of all phenomena and will become lord of all phenomena. He will experience delight, satisfaction and great joy in all phenomena. As a great bodhisattva, within sixteen births he will attain the state of a tathāgata or the state of a vajra holder. hūṃ."

Then the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Vairocana, once again taught this way of the Perfection of Wisdom, the accomplishment of the manifest awakening to the peaceful nature of reality of all the tathāgatas.

It is the great awakening of being fully and perfectly awakened to the equality of the vajra through vajra firmness; the great awakening of being fully awakened to the equality of meaning through oneness of meaning; the great awakening of being fully awakened to the equality of the nature of reality through purity of intrinsic nature; and the great awakening of being fully awakened to the equality of all actions through conceptuality and nonconceptuality.

"Mañjuśrī, if anyone listens to, holds, recites or meditates on these four bases of the Dharma, then, for as long as he has not yet come to dwell at the seat of awakening, even though he may engage in every kind of evil, he will pass beyond all evil, and he will swiftly and fully awaken to unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening. āṃ."

Then again the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Śākyamuni, tamer of all the wicked, spoke the realization of the Perfection of Wisdom called The Equality of All Phenomena Gathered by Complete Victory:

"Because attachment is free of elaboration, attachment is without elaboration. Because aversion is free of elaboration, aversion is without elaboration. Because delusion is free of elaboration, delusion is without elaboration. Because all phenomena are free of elaboration, all phenomena are without elaboration. Because the Perfection of Wisdom is free of elaboration, the Perfection of Wisdom should be known to be without elaboration.

"Mañjuśrī, if anyone listens to, holds, recites and reads this way of the Perfection of Wisdom, then even if, having taken up the power of taming, he were to slay the beings born in the three realms, he would not go to the lower realms, and he will swiftly attain unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening. hūṃ."

Then again the Blessed One, the Tathāgata who has realized the nature of reality that is pure by its intrinsic nature, spoke this way of the Perfection of Wisdom called The Wisdom Seal of Avalokiteśvara, Lord of All Phenomena:

"Because all attachment is purified, in the world all objects will come to be pure. Because all aversion is purified, in the world all evil will come to be pure. Because all phenomena are purified, in the world all phenomena will come to be pure. Because omniscient wisdom is purified, in the world omniscient wisdom will come to be pure. Because the Perfection of Wisdom is purified, in the world the Perfection of Wisdom will come to be pure.

"Mañjuśrī, if anyone listens to, holds, recites and meditates on this way of the Perfection of Wisdom, then even though he dwells amid all sensual pleasures, he will, like a lotus, not be stained by the adventitious taint of the fault of attachment, and he will swiftly and fully awaken to unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening. hrīḥ."

Then again the Blessed One, the Tathāgata who is lord of all the three realms, spoke the way of the Perfection of Wisdom called The Essence of Wisdom, Source of the Empowerment of All the Tathāgatas:

"By bestowing empowerment, one will fully obtain sovereignty over all the three realms. Through the gift of what is of benefit, the wishes of all will be completely fulfilled. Through the gift of the Dharma, one will attain the equality of all phenomena. Through the gift of material things, one will obtain bliss of body, speech and mind for all. trāṃ."

Then again the Blessed One, the Eternal Tathāgata, who has realized the wisdom seal of all the tathāgatas and holds the fist of all, spoke the way of the Perfection of Wisdom called The Vajra Consecrated by the Wisdom Seal of All the Tathāgatas:

"By fully taking up the seal of the body of all the tathāgatas, one becomes all the tathāgatas themselves. By fully taking up the seal of their speech, one comes to obtain all the teachings. By fully taking up the seal of their mind, one comes to obtain all samādhis. By fully taking up the vajra seal, one comes to accomplish all body, speech and mind as the supreme vajra.

"Mañjuśrī, if anyone listens to, holds, recites, explains and meditates on this way of the Perfection of Wisdom, he will attain every accomplishment, every excellence, every wisdom, every activity, and every accomplishment of the supreme Vajrasattva of body, speech and mind, and he will swiftly and fully awaken to unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening. aḥ."

Then again the Blessed One, the Tathāgata for whom all phenomena are free of elaboration, spoke the way of the Perfection of Wisdom called Turning the Wheel of Vajra Letters:

"Through the mode of absence of intrinsic nature, all phenomena are emptiness. Because the signlessness of all phenomena is grasped, all phenomena are devoid of signs. Because they are fully freed from wishing, all phenomena are wishless. Because the Perfection of Wisdom is completely pure, all phenomena are by their intrinsic nature luminous. aṃ."

Then again the Blessed One, the Tathāgata who dwells within the wheel of all the tathāgatas, spoke the way of the Perfection of Wisdom called Entering the Great Wheel:

"By entering the equality of the vajra, one enters the wheel of all the tathāgatas. By entering the equality of meaning, one enters the wheel of the great bodhisattvas. By entering the equality of all phenomena, one enters the wheel of the sublime Dharma. By entering the equality of action, one enters all the wheels."

hūṃ.

Then again the Blessed One, the Tathāgata who is the vessel of the vast rite of worship of all the tathāgatas, spoke the way of the Perfection of Wisdom called The Sublime Worship of All the Tathāgatas:

"Generating the awakening mind is the vast rite of worship of all the tathāgatas. Fully protecting all the realms of sentient beings is the vast rite of worship of all the tathāgatas. Fully taking up all the teachings is the vast rite of worship of all the tathāgatas. And the action preceded by asking about the Perfection of Wisdom, having others ask about it, holding it, reciting it, proclaiming it and meditating on it, is the vast rite of worship of all the tathāgatas. oṃ."

Then again the Blessed One, the Tathāgata who has the power to tame all the wicked, spoke this way of the Perfection of Wisdom called The Essence of Wisdom That Tames All Sentient Beings:

"Because all sentient beings are equality, wrath is equality. Because all sentient beings are taming, wrath is taming. Because all sentient beings are the nature of reality, wrath is the nature of reality. Because all sentient beings are vajra, wrath is vajra. If one asks why this is so: it is the awakening that tames all sentient beings. haḥ."

Then again the Blessed One, the Tathāgata who is firmly established in the equality of all, spoke this way of the Perfection of Wisdom called The Supreme Equality of All Phenomena:

"Because all is equality, the Perfection of Wisdom is equality. Because all is meaning, the Perfection of Wisdom is meaning. Because all phenomena are equality, the phenomena of the Perfection of Wisdom are equality. Because all is action, the Perfection of Wisdom should be known to be action. hrīḥ."

Then again the Blessed One, all the tathāgatas, spoke this way of the Perfection of Wisdom called The Blessing of All the Tathāgatas:

"Because the great bodhisattva Samantabhadra is the self of all, all sentient beings have the essence of the Tathāgata. Because they are empowered by the vajra essence, all sentient beings have the vajra essence. Because they are empowered by all jewels, all sentient beings have the jewel essence. Because they fully enter into all speech, all sentient beings have the Dharma essence. Through the application that performs all actions, all sentient beings have the action essence. hrīḥ."

Then again the Blessed One, the Tathāgata who has passed beyond extremes and reached the end of the limitless, in order to consecrate the Dharma that is limitless and perfected, and again its conception and its final consummation, spoke this way of the Perfection of Wisdom called The Vajra Consecrated by the Final Consummation of the Equality of All Phenomena:

"Because the Perfection of Wisdom is without end, all the tathāgatas are without end. Because the Perfection of Wisdom is without limit, all the tathāgatas are without limit. Because the Perfection of Wisdom is oneness, all phenomena are oneness. Because the Perfection of Wisdom is the final consummation, all phenomena come to their final consummation.

"Mañjuśrī, if anyone listens to, holds, recites and meditates on this way of the Perfection of Wisdom, then, by abiding in the perfected conduct of a pure and perfected bodhisattva, he will be liberated from all obscurations, and so will swiftly attain the state of a tathāgata or the state of a vajra holder. bhyoḥ."

Then again the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Vairocana, who has realized the secret nature of reality of all the tathāgatas, since all phenomena are free of elaboration, spoke this supreme and sublime gateway of the Perfection of Wisdom of the equality of vajra phenomena, free of beginning, end and middle, called The Commitment of Great Bliss, the Unfailing Vajra:

"Through the supreme accomplishment of the great attachment of the great bodhisattvas, great bliss comes to be supremely accomplished. Through the supreme accomplishment of the great bliss of the great bodhisattvas, the great awakening of all the tathāgatas comes to be supremely accomplished. Through the great bodhisattvas' supreme accomplishment of the awakening of all the tathāgatas, the utter defeat of the great hosts of Māra comes to be supremely accomplished. Through the great bodhisattvas' supreme accomplishment of the utter defeat of the great hosts of Māra, lordship over all the three realms comes to be supremely accomplished. Through the great bodhisattvas' supreme accomplishment of lordship over all the three worlds, the supreme benefit and happiness of all sentient beings, the exceedingly great bliss that fully protects the realms of sentient beings entirely and without exception, comes to be supremely accomplished. svaṃ.

"If one asks why this is so:

For as long as the supreme among the wise
Remain within the state of saṃsāra,
So long, not passing into nirvāṇa,
Can they work the incomparable good of beings.

The means of the Perfection of Wisdom
Is accomplished through the blessing of wisdom;
Because all actions are completely purified,
Existence itself comes to be pure.

The world, to be tamed through attachment and the rest,
Ever commits evil throughout existence;
In order to purify such beings,
One tames oneself for as long as existence lasts.

Just as a lotus of lovely red
Is not stained by any taint of color,
So in existence, with its faults of habitual tendencies,
One who always benefits beings is never stained.

Great attachment, completely pure,
Is great bliss, is great wealth;
Having gained lordship over the three realms,
One firmly establishes their aims.

haḥ.

"Mañjuśrī, if anyone rises each day at dawn and recites, listens to, holds and proclaims this Dharma discourse, the gateway to the way of the Perfection of Wisdom, he will obtain every bliss and every happiness of mind, and in this very life he will obtain the supreme accomplishment of the commitment of great bliss, the unfailing vajra."

hūṃ.

"This is the mudrā and mantra that tear apart evil. By merely reciting it, one becomes the great vajra holder, the supreme secret accomplishment of all the tathāgatas, or becomes the Tathāgata himself.

"Mañjuśrī, this will not resound in the ears of sentient beings who have not planted roots of virtue, and sentient beings who have not planted roots of virtue are unable to write it, read it, recite it, serve it, honor it, revere it or worship it. On the other hand, if whoever hears even so much as a single letter of this Dharma discourse, the gateway to the way of the Perfection of Wisdom, is to be known as a being who has planted roots of virtue under many buddhas, what need is there to speak of hearing it again in its entirety, complete and without omission?

"Mañjuśrī, any sentient being who has paid homage to, honored, revered and worshiped koṭis of niyutas of buddhas, as many as the sands of eighty Ganges rivers, is able to hear this way of the Perfection of Wisdom. Wherever this Dharma discourse is practiced, that spot of ground becomes a shrine, and that person too becomes worthy of homage. Whoever holds this Dharma discourse in mind or writes it down in a volume will remember his births over many hundreds of thousands of koṭis of niyutas of eons. Māra the Evil One will not obstruct him. The Four Great Kings and other gods as well will follow close behind him, and will guard, protect and shelter him. He will not die an untimely death, and all the buddhas and bodhisattvas will keep him in mind. In short, he will be born in whatever buddha field he wishes. Mañjuśrī, such are the many benefits of this Perfection of Wisdom, this Dharma discourse that is the gateway to the way of the Perfection of Wisdom."

Then the Blessed One, the Great Lord of Vajra Yoga, brought forth this heart called the Ornament of the Vajra Essence: the ornament of all vajra essences, the realization of the Great Vehicle, the supreme great secret of all the tathāgatas, the secret commitment, the great sublime lord of the vajra expanse, surrounded by the four great universal monarchs.

"Because they have a single characteristic, all phenomena are primordially unborn. Because they are equal to space, all phenomena are emptiness. Because all elaborations are abandoned, all phenomena are primordially completely pure. Because of the characteristic of the clarity of the mind of all, all phenomena are the fully expanded expanse of reality.

aḥ bhruṃ trāṃ hrīḥ hūṃ."

By the mere utterance of these words of the vajra letters of all the tathāgatas, all the domains of the buddhas, reaching to the limits of the expanse of reality, however many they are, became visible, exceedingly clear and vast.

Then the Blessed One, all the tathāgatas, having come together once more, entered into the samādhi called The Array of the Great Maṇḍala of All the Tathāgatas, and spoke to Mañjuśrī Kumārabhūta: "Mañjuśrī, I shall explain the great maṇḍala of all the tathāgatas called the Ornament of the Vajra Essence. Listen! Listen well and keep it firmly in mind, and I shall explain it."

Then the Blessed One, the Great Vairocana, spoke to Mañjuśrī Kumārabhūta: "Mañjuśrī, you too must enter the wisdom and gnosis of all the tathāgatas, and consecrate the great vajra essence of all the tathāgatas!"

Then again Mañjuśrī Kumārabhūta, having heard the words that the Blessed Vairocana had addressed to him, entered the hearts of all the tathāgatas, bestowed the consecration, came forth from there, and stood before all the tathāgatas.

Then the Blessed One entered into the meditative absorption called Source of the Pledges of All the Tathāgatas, Blessing of the Ornament of the Vajra Essence, and proclaimed the great array of the secret maṇḍala of all the tathāgatas, the Ornament of the Essence of the Great Vajra of the Dharma Realm:

the vajra essence, the great secret,
the auspicious one endowed with all the tathāgatas.
It is equal to the vajra essence,

It is the nectar of all the buddhas,

One should draw such a great maṇḍala, square, with four gates, with banners and garlands of flowers, gate recesses, parasols, victory banners, and raised flags, and covered with nets and half nets, and having drawn such a great maṇḍala, one should meditate on it. Having thus set out the maṇḍala in its stages, one offers the full assembly of all the offering articles and approaches. Having summoned the deities, caused them to enter, bound them with the pledge, and pleased them, and having performed the offering, the consecration, the blessing, the emanation, the withdrawal, and so on, one should meditate on the great mudrā of all the tathāgatas. Likewise, rising from that meditation, one should meditate, "I am Vairocana, the very self of all the tathāgatas and of Vajrasattva."

Having arisen from that, holding that pride, with compassion and loving-kindness as one's guide, and with one's eye, one fills completely all the clouds of the world systems of the ten directions, boundless and vast as the ocean, with clouds of the bodies of all the tathāgatas. Then, as the tathāgatas descend, the mudrā of Vajrasattva Hūṃkara enters one's own body, and one should make all the directions resound with the sound of the syllable hūṃ.

With the four strikes, together with their play, one should recite upon the mudrā. Binding the pledge mudrā, the vajra master, following the rite, summons the deities, causes them to enter, binds them, and establishes them in the pledge. Then, with the application of the vajra finger snap and with the mudrā of complete descent, he makes them descend upon himself, and with these four essence mantras he should think, "I am the equal of all the tathāgatas." Here, these are the four essence mantras of total descent: a ā aṃ aḥ.

Having blessed himself and so forth, the master generates the pride, "I am the equal of all the tathāgatas." Then, snapping the vajra-hook finger snap of all the tathāgatas, he should invite all the buddhas. Here the essence mantra is this: vajra samājaḥ āgaccha āgacchantu sarvva tathāgata sarvva bodhisatva parivṛtāḥ pratyekarha hate svāhā. And: oṃ vajra samājaḥ āgaccha āgaccha sarvva tathāgatā vajra yogīśvar satpa nayaiḥ svahā ahāḥ.

Then all the tathāgatas, together with all the bodhisattvas, all the maṇḍalas made fully complete, along with the families, lineages, and forms of all the tathāgatas, gather into the maṇḍala and arrive there.

Then, having visualized oneself as the great mudrā of all the tathāgatas, one recites the hundred and eight names and makes offerings according to the rite. Having made all the tathāgatas stable, one performs the rite at the four gates with Vajrāṅkuśa and the rest, and with the holy mudrā of the great action one should cause all the tathāgatas to enter: oṃ sarvva tathāgata surata vajra vajra satva jaḥ hūṃ vaṃ hoḥ adhitiṣthantu sarvva tathāgata mahā maṇḍalaṃ.

Then, having made offerings with the secret offerings and the rest, one should supplicate all the tathāgatas: "I supplicate all the buddhas and bodhisattvas. May all the buddhas and bodhisattvas please bless me. Please act for the benefit of all sentient beings. Please grant me all accomplishments. I shall bless the domains of all the buddhas, bounded only by the limits of the Dharma realm, filling them completely with clouds of offerings to all the tathāgatas, vast as the ocean. I shall completely fulfill the command of all the tathāgatas."

Next, here is the extensive rite for introducing the disciple into the Dharma realm maṇḍala of the Ornament of the Vajra Essence. Then, with a red cloth draped over his shoulder, and with palms joined in homage to all the tathāgatas, he should request their attention with this mantra: samaya hoḥ samaya stvaṃ samaya vajra adhiṣṭhānaṃ kuru māṃ sarvva tathāgata samayeṣu me adhitiṣthantu sarvva buddha māṃ praveśayantu pratipālayantu pūjāñca me prayacchantu prapiṣṭosmi sarvva tathāgata vajra maṇḍa alaṃkāre mahā vajra maṇḍale prativalayantu māṃ stujañca me prayacchantu.

Then the master ties a garland of white flowers to his joined palms with this mantra, placing four flowers inside and one flower at each fingertip, and then, reciting this mantra, he should lead him in: hūṃ dhvaṃ hroṃ kruṃ śruṃ jruṃ vaḥ kaḥ praṃ khiṃ śrīṃ jruṃ hāṃ māṃ adhitiṣṭhamāṃ sarvva tathāgata samaya satva praviśāmi.

Then the master binds a cloth over his face, leads him in by the method of the city of nirvāṇa of all the tathāgatas, which pervades the Dharma realm and the element of space, and has him cast a flower with this mantra. The mantra is: samayo haṃ samaya stvaṃ samaya hūṃ. The deity on which the flowers fall is to be given to him as his deity.

Then, according to the rite, one should cause the descent with this mantra: namaḥ sarva tathāgatebhyaḥ taṭa taṭa dhama dhama vajra hūṃ vikrita viṅca jjihvā durdānta damaka hasitalaṃ vodara kaha kaha culu culu muñca muñca aṭṭāṭṭa hāsa dara vidara vajra vajroṅgata vajra netra vajra śrotra vajra ghrāṇa vajra daṃṣṭra kiñci rāya si praviśa vajra pāṇe hrīdaya māviśa māviśa ghūrṇṇa ghūrṇṇa pūrṇṇa pūrṇṇa ghurṇṇa paya ghūrṇṇa paya bhrāmaya bhrāmaya pātaya moṭaya aveśaya jalpa jalpa jalpa paya jalpa paya laghu laghu sarvva tathāgatājñā kara pravara dharmma labdha vijaya he he vajra jhaṃ kāra hūṃ kṛta svāhā. This is the pledge of descent.

When the descent has thus been brought about, one asks, "Oh, have you seen anything virtuous or nonvirtuous?" Then he will say, "I have seen such and such a tathāgata, or a vajra holder, or something virtuous or nonvirtuous."

Then the master, having fully established all of that as the vajra nature of all the tathāgatas, unties the blindfold. For the sake of the empowerment he makes a square maṇḍala, and in its center he himself draws, in all the colors, a lovely great eight-petaled lotus, or else a lion throne. Then he honors it with silk coverings, flowers, incense, and the rest, and in particular with forms, sounds, smells, tastes, and tangibles, with parasols, victory banners, flags, and the other requisites, with conches, clay drums, great drums, conches and the like, and with dances and songs. Having made fine offerings, he confers the empowerment of the five crowns, and with this mantra he should establish the disciple in the rank of vajra master of all the tathāgatas: oṃ sarvva tathāgata vajroṣṇīṣa abhiṣiñca oṃ vajra satva abhiṣiñca oṃ vajra ratna abhiṣiñca oṃ vajra padma abhiṣiñca oṃ vajra vajra abhiṣiñca.

Having placed the seals of the syllables on the crown of the head, the forehead, the throat, the heart, the navel, and from the waist down to the shins, he should look upon him by the method of the form of his own deity. Having thus empowered him and given him a name in keeping with his family, he should train him in the vajra rites.

Here, then, are the vajra stations. Because they are by nature indivisible, one's own body is the station of Vajrasattva; this vajra station utterly destroys all the thickets of views. Because it leads into knowledge, ignorance is a vajra station; through this vajra station one comes to enter into full understanding of the objects of focus. hūṃ

Because of the forming of conditioned activity, the limit of the deeds of immediate retribution is a vajra station; through this vajra station one comes to know with certainty all that is seen. oṃ

Because of the sameness of the limit of freedom from attachment, the limit of attachment is a vajra station; this vajra station leads to freedom from attachment. ha

Because of the sameness of the limit of loving-kindness, the limit of aversion is a vajra station; this vajra station leads to the cutting off of all faults. maiḥ

Because of the sameness of the lamp of wisdom, the limit of delusion is a vajra station; this vajra station dispels the darkness. raṃ

All sentient beings are a single sentient being; this vajra station serves for entering into the sameness of sentient beings and for realizing the nature of sentient beings. hrī

All sentient beings are of a single mind; this vajra station serves for entering into certainty about the mind and for fully realizing that the mind is by nature luminous. a

All the buddhas are a single buddha; this vajra station serves for following all the tathāgatas and for realizing the wisdom of sameness. oṃ

All phenomena are the qualities of a buddha; this vajra station serves for entering into the realization of omniscient wisdom and for realizing the vajra-like meditative absorption. āṃ

All the deeds of Māra are the deeds of a buddha; this vajra station serves for entering into the realization of all actions and for passing wholly beyond all actions. hūṃ

All speech is the speech of the Tathāgata; this vajra station serves for entering into the realization of all sounds just as they are and for realizing the Dharma that is beyond expression. rdhaṃ

All phenomena are unborn; this vajra station serves for entering into deathlessness and for passing beyond the workings of birth, aging, and death. maṃ

All phenomena have never come into being; this vajra station serves for entering into non-cessation and for realizing that no phenomenon ceases. khaṃ

The yogin who abides in this
fully realizes that reality himself;
having examined that reality himself,
he becomes the equal of all buddhas, the unequaled.

By this he will swiftly accomplish all such accomplishments, all excellences, all treasures, all wheels, all vajras, the sword, the wish-fulfilling jewel, mercury, the elixirs, flight through the sky, the underworld, invisibility, the state of a knowledge holder, the state of a universal monarch, the state of Śakra, of Brahmā, of Viṣṇu, and of Maheśvara, and the grounds of a bodhisattva. hūṃ

With a mind become one-pointed,
having meditated in recollection of the Buddha,
one should meditate on the form of the Buddha
abiding in the center of the disk of the sun.
Through the grace of that practice
one becomes the equal of all buddhas, the unequaled.

Abiding in the center of the disk of the moon,
with a brilliant disk of light,
the great mudrā of Samantabhadra:
meditate on it in perfect equipoise.
By practicing it for half a year
one becomes like Samantabhadra.

The great mudrā of Vajraratna:
meditate on it in perfect equipoise.
One will attain the royal consecration,
or else sovereign power.

The great mudrā of Vajradharma:
meditate on it oneself, steadfastly.
If one attains even lordship over all the worlds,
what need is there to mention other accomplishments?

Whatever is the mudrā of Vajrakarma,
the luminosity of suchness:
by meditating on it through vajra union,
one becomes steadfast in vajra yoga.

Meditation on the supreme awakening mind in this way is itself the wisdom of the great mudrā.

The great mudrā of the Universal Monarch:
meditate on it in perfect equipoise.
One will attain the holy aim itself,
the very self of all the mudrās.

The great mudrā of Tejorāśi:
one should meditate on it for the holy aim.
If one attains the secret kingdom,
what need is there to speak of other accomplishments?

The great uṣṇīṣa of Sitātapatra:
one should meditate on it for the holy aim.
If one attains the wisdom of the uṣṇīṣa,
what need is there to speak of other accomplishments?

The great mudrā of Jayoṣṇīṣa:
meditate on it again and again.
The kingdom of the Dharma, complete victory itself,
is attained, in brief.

Of the mudrā of Vijayoṣṇīṣa
one should meditate on the holy syllable.
Well consecrated and radiant,
one will attain the wisdom of accomplishment.

These and the other supreme ones without end,
equal to the Dharma realm and without equal,
well taught by the uṣṇīṣas:
those mudrās are the supreme self.
When the yogin meditates in this way,
he will attain the one surpassing deity,
without beginning or end, peaceful,
self-arisen, supremely unchanging,
joined in sameness with all the buddhas,
the supreme rank of the lord of yogins,
the stainless wisdom of the buddhas,
the great self.

These are the mudrās of the pledge wisdom of the uṣṇīṣas of all the tathāgatas.

As for the words "Then one should train in that wisdom whose nature is the Dharma letters of all the tathāgatas": because they serve for discernment, they are the gateways to all phenomena, and they should be understood as serving for analysis. oṃ paṃ

The letter a is explained as a gateway to all phenomena because all phenomena are unborn. oṃ a ā aṃ aḥ

The letter pa is a gateway to all phenomena: one should see all the perfections of all phenomena as ultimately brought to completion. oṃ paṃ

The letter na is explained as a gateway to all phenomena because it masters the names, forms, wisdom, and qualities of all phenomena. oṃ naṃ

The letter ḍa is declared a gateway to all phenomena because it brings to completion the perfection of taming and of peace. oṃ ḍaṃ

The letter ṣa is established as a gateway to all phenomena because, having passed completely beyond what is unobstructed, the aggregates, elements, sense sources, and so on, which are taken up in relation to ultimate truth by way of thorough affliction and purification, of the contaminated and the uncontaminated, are not observed. oṃ sruṃ

The letter ta is to be seen as a gateway to all phenomena because it enters the Dharma realm, which accords with suchness. oṃ taṃ

The letter ka is to be made clear as a gateway to all phenomena because it is free from the ripening of karma. oṃ kaṃ

The letter ma is clearly explained as a gateway to all phenomena because it arises from being like an illusion, from non-recollection, from great compassion, and from sharp wisdom. oṃ maṃ

The letter ga is to be seen as a gateway to all phenomena because it is profound and hard to fathom. oṃ gaṃ

The letter ja is to be seen as a gateway to all phenomena because the nature that has fully crossed beyond birth, aging, and death is indivisible. oṃ jaṃ

The letter dha is to be seen as a gateway to all phenomena because it brings to completion the vajra of the wholly indivisible Dharma realm. oṃ rdhaṃ

The letter ṣa is to be seen as a gateway to all phenomena because it brings calm abiding and insight to completion. oṃ ṣaṃ

The letter kha is established as a gateway to all phenomena because the selflessness of persons and of phenomena is clear like space, and all phenomena are by nature luminous. oṃ khaṃ

The letter kṣa is to be seen as a gateway to all phenomena because, through the non-arising of movement, inexhaustible wisdom is perceived in each thing, and all phenomena are completely pure. oṃ kṣaṃ

The letter ṅa is to be seen as a gateway to all phenomena because the wisdom of omniscience is unobstructed. oṃ ṅaṃ

The letter tha is to be seen as a gateway to all phenomena because, through skill in what is possible and impossible, it purifies the buddha fields. oṃ thaṃ

The letter ḍha is explained as a gateway to all phenomena because it is final, because it is without end, and because, having fully abandoned all obscurations, it makes supreme the attainment of the knowledge of exhaustion and of non-arising. oṃ ḍhaṃ

The letter jha is to be seen as a gateway to all phenomena because no aspect of stain is observed. oṃ jhaṃ

The discernment of the body is to be seen as a gateway to all phenomena because, having abandoned all afflictions, the limit of freedom from attachment, one attains in particular the body of the vajra essence. oṃ kāṃ

The discernment of the mind is to be seen as a gateway to all phenomena because, having fully abandoned faults, confusion, and latent tendencies, the generation of the awakening mind is by nature luminous. oṃ aṃ

The discernment of speech is to be seen as a gateway to all phenomena because the elaborations of speech are the purity of the Dharma realm. oṃ vāṃ

The letter cha is to be seen as a gateway to all phenomena because no shadow is observed and it is clear by nature. oṃ chaṃ

The letter gha is to be seen as a gateway to all phenomena because, with no mingling into one being observed, it is ultimately nondual. oṃ ghaṃ

The letter ṭa is explained as a gateway to all phenomena because it is free from clamor and holds the vajra pride. oṃ ṭoṃ

The letter pha is explained as a gateway to the Dharma because, all phenomena being like a mass of foam, the secret wisdom becomes clear. oṃ phaṃ

The letter ḍa is to be seen as a gateway to all phenomena because it is attained through accomplishing movement through the sky, free from harm. oṃ ḍaṃ

The letter ṣa is to be seen as a gateway to all phenomena because it focuses on the meditation of heat. oṃ ṣaṃ

Pacification is established as a gateway to all phenomena because all phenomena are pacified. oṃ uṃ

The proper way is to be seen as a gateway to all phenomena because, for the sake of renunciation, it enters the noble eightfold path. oṃ yoṃ

Reality is established as a gateway to all phenomena because it abandons all elaboration. oṃ taṃ

It is explained as a gateway to all phenomena because it is the meaning of the characteristic of liberation of unborn phenomena. oṃ huṃ

Non-grasping is to be seen as a gateway to all phenomena because it is the nature of reality that has abandoned strife and dispute. oṃ hroṃ

Freedom from thorough affliction is to be seen as a gateway to all phenomena because its characteristic is completely pure.

oṃ ka kha ga gha ṅa It abides as a gateway to all phenomena because it apprehends the characteristic of being luminous by nature.

oṃ ca cha ja jha ña Appearance is a gateway to all phenomena because it is clear and blazing.

oṃ ṭa ṭha ḍa ḍha ṇa Meditation is a gateway to all phenomena because it truly gathers all phenomena as free of fetters.

oṃ ta tha da dha na Groundlessness is established as a gateway to all phenomena because all phenomena are unmixed.

oṃ pa pha ba bha ma Awakening is to be seen as a gateway to all phenomena because all phenomena are stainless.

oṃ ya ra la va Nirvāṇa is to be seen as a gateway to all phenomena because it is fully attained by the buddhas, through the characteristic of definitively casting off everything adventitious, through individually attaining the sphere of reality (dharmadhātu), and through knowing the five superknowledges and primordial wisdom.

oṃ śa ṣa sa ha It brings about the discernment of the pure wisdom of omniscience, endowed with the supreme of all aspects.

Thus it was said. This is the ascertainment of the Dharma gateways of all the tathāgatas.

Then, the extensive secret rite of all the families:

Having ascertained it as before,
And having meditated on all directions as sameness,
Visualize great Mount Meru, four-sided,
Adorned with every kind of jewel;
Upon it, a great vajra;
Upon that, place a lotus,
Eight-petaled and most radiant:
This is explained as the abode of yoga.

With the two organs in union,
If, completely and by oneself,
One meditates on all the buddhas,
Then one abides to the very end of the eon.
The great mudrā of all the buddhas,
Having a maṇḍala of bright light rays,
Should be meditated upon in every yoga;
One becomes equal to all the peerless buddhas.
The great bhaga is the great yoga,
The inexhaustible abode of the yogin.
Having meditated on oneself as all the buddhas,
One becomes equal to all the buddhas.
Great kingship over the three realms,
Over all sentient beings, in every way:
When the yogin meditates on it,
Then accomplishment rests in his hand.
Phenomena, luminous by their nature,
Are pure from the beginning and unmixed;
When wisdom sees them,
At that time one becomes pure.
All the buddhas condensed in one,
Through the application of various actions:
When the yogin meditates on this,
He will perform various actions.

This is the wisdom of the great mudrā of all the tathāgatas.

The Universal Monarch, the great king,
Supreme over all, the lord of the earth:
If one meditates on him through vajra union,
One is equal to the peerless Universal Monarch.
The great mudrā of Tejorāśi,
Meditate on it in perfect equipoise:
Tumultuous and beautiful with a garland of flames,
With light like a ruby.
Having meditated through vajra union,
One oneself becomes Tejorāśi.
If one meditates on the great mudrā
Of Sitātapatra, the uṣṇīṣa of the Victor,
Through the yoga of bright light rays
Sentient beings become pure.
The mudrā of the Radiant Uṣṇīṣa,
Tumultuous with the light of blazing swords:
Through the undefiled yoga
One is equal to the peerless Radiant Uṣṇīṣa.
The great mudrā of Jayoṣṇīṣa,
Meditate on it in perfect equipoise;
Through the yoga of Jayoṣṇīṣa
One enters the domain of his form.
The great wisdom of the vajra pledge,
Meditate on it in perfect equipoise;
Through the forms of Samantabhadra and the rest,
One is equal to the peerless Vajrasattva.
Abiding in the center of the moon disk,
Vajrasattva, the great being,
With light like the awakening mind,
Mighty, endowed with five crowns:
By meditating through vajra union
One becomes like Vajrasattva.
The great mudrā of Vajrarāja:
If one meditates on it in the ultimate sense,
And it summons all beings,
What need is there to speak of other people?
The great mudrā of Vajrarāga,
Meditate on it in perfect equipoise:
If all creatures become enamored,
What need is there to speak of other women?
The great mudrā of Vajrasādhu
Should be meditated upon for the ultimate purpose;
By proclaiming "Excellent!"
One satisfies all women.

This is the wisdom of the mudrā of the awakening mind.

The great mudrā of Vajraratna,
Its light tumultuous with a garland of flames,
Meditate on as abiding at the forehead;
Thereby one obtains the empowerment of the silk headband.
The great mudrā of Vajrateja,
With light like the disk of the sun,
Should be meditated on as abiding at the heart;
It bestows the wisdom of omniscience.
The great mudrā of Ratnaketu,
Meditate on it in perfect equipoise:
The supreme perfection of generosity
The excellent practitioner brings to completion.
The great mudrā of Vajrahāsa,
Meditate on it in perfect equipoise:
Becoming like Vajrahāsa,
He will gladden all creatures.

This is the wisdom of the mudrā of empowerment.

In the lotus, the lotus of great bliss,
Through equipoise on the pure Dharma,
One should meditate for the ultimate purpose;
One is equal to the peerless Vajradharma.
Wisdom and gnosis, the sharp vajra:
By meditating on it, one is Vajradhara.
The pure perfection of wisdom,
If one meditates on it through the yoga of emptiness,
Meditating on it for the ultimate purpose,
One is equal to the peerless Mañjughoṣa.
The great mudrā of Vajrahetu:
Having meditated in perfect equipoise,
The wheel of Dharma, the supreme summit of all,
He turns, in condensed form.
The great mudrā of Vajrabhāṣa:
Having meditated on it for the ultimate purpose,
Through the power of vajra recitation
He expounds the enigmatic speech.

This is the wisdom of the Dharma mudrā.

The great mudrā of Vajrakarma:
If one meditates on it for the ultimate purpose,
Wholly and entirely by oneself
One will perform vajra action.
Vajrarakṣa, of great loving-kindness,
Renowned as the vajra Dharma:
If one meditates through the yoga of Dharma,
One becomes equal to Vajrarakṣa.
Vajrayakṣa, of great diligence,
The great wrathful one, the mighty one,
Should be meditated on in the ultimate sense;
One becomes equal to Vajrayakṣa.
The great mudrā of Vajramuṣṭi:
Likewise, with body, speech and mind,
If one meditates through vajra union,
One will hold the vajra fist.

This is the wisdom of the karma mudrā.

Lāsyā, the goddess of grace, bestows delight;
Mālā, the goddess of the garland, confers empowerment;
Gītā, the goddess of song, makes lovely melody;
Through Nṛtyā, the goddess of dance, one attains miraculous powers;
Gandhā, the goddess of scent, is fragrance itself;
Puṣpā, the goddess of flowers, is the very limbs of awakening;
Dīpā, the goddess of the lamp, illumines all.

Dhūpā, the goddess of incense, is devoted to offerings of incense.
If the Hook goddess summons even Īśvara,
What need is there to speak of other people?
If the Noose goddess binds Indra,
What need is there to speak of malicious beings?
The Chain goddess binds Hari (Viṣṇu),
Brahmā and the others, and even Maheśvara.
If the goddess who causes descent makes all fall into possession,
What need is there to speak of lowly men?

Through this very application,
Of the supreme syllables of the Dharma,
By thoroughly applying the wisdom of the mudrās,
Joining them, one should swiftly accomplish the practice.
The application of this should be understood:
The wheel of mudrās in particular,
From the crown of the head and the heart down to the feet,
Being completely filled with syllables,
Becomes the state of a single image.
Through the concentration in which the mind becomes one-pointed,
The lotus firmly set upon the earth
Is explained as the abode of Mahendra.
Through the even union of the two organs,
Lordship too should be accomplished.
The Mahendra maṇḍala is square,
And is explained as serving the rite of Mahendra.
Water should be known as round,
Marked with the sign of a lotus,
Endowed with the action of pacifying;
It is explained for the purpose of pacification.
Fire should be known as triangular,
Set in the place of a three-way crossroads,
For the purpose of accomplishing magnetizing,
With light like a ruby.
For the fierce rite, with great diligence,
The wind maṇḍala is to be declared,
In the shape of the sword mudrā,
With light like a summer cloud.
Yellow and white and likewise red,
And black too in particular:
In the manner of the first maṇḍala
Through to the last, they should be carefully examined.
First set out the vajra,
And after it the jewel,
The lotus, and the yoga of action:
Thus the state of accomplishment is explained in sequence.

This is the wisdom of all the mudrās.

Then the ever-abiding Blessed One declared the wisdom of the mudrā of all the tathāgatas:

Being completely pure through duality and nonduality, all phenomena are sealed with the seal of liberation.

Being completely pure as permanence, all phenomena are sealed with the seal of peace, of limit, and of limitlessness.

Possessing the gateway of exhaustion, the limit of exhaustion, and the reaching of the end of exhaustion, all phenomena are sealed with the seal of exhaustion and of freedom from desire.

Because the limit of sameness is completely pure, all phenomena are sealed with the seal of not denying any phenomenon.

Through having truly transcended the five eyes, all phenomena are sealed with the seal of space.

Because all sentient beings are the very expanse of space, all phenomena are sealed with the seal of the nature of space.

Because they truly converge in the sphere of reality (dharmadhātu), all phenomena are sealed with the seal of indivisibility.

Being no different from the awakening mind, all phenomena are sealed with the seal of the awakening mind.

Being the characteristic of the indivisible sphere of reality, all phenomena are sealed with the seal of the sphere of reality.

Being without essential nature, all phenomena are sealed with the seal of emptiness.

Being without harm, all phenomena are sealed with the seal of selflessness.

Being momentary, all phenomena are sealed with the seal of impermanence.

Possessing the end of birth, aging, and death, all phenomena are sealed with the seal of suffering.

Since the prior limit and the later limit are suchness, all phenomena are sealed with the seal of suchness.

Being completely pure from the very beginning, all phenomena are sealed with the seal of the true limit.

Since the conditioned and the unconditioned are the same, all phenomena are sealed with the seal of the great emptiness.

Being entirely free from the objects of elaboration, all phenomena are sealed with the seal of signlessness.

Being liberated from all hopes, all phenomena are sealed with the seal of wishlessness.

By the characteristic of the essential nature of non-entity, all phenomena are sealed with the seal of impermanence.

By the characteristic of the complete assembly of the five aggregates, all phenomena are sealed with the seal of suffering.

Being selfless by nature, all phenomena are sealed with the seal of selflessness.

Because they are utterly unmoving, all phenomena are sealed with the seal of peace.

Because the ultimate truth truly converges, all phenomena are sealed with the seal of truth.

Because livelihood does not abide, all phenomena are sealed with the seal of stainlessness.

Because they have reached the supreme end, all phenomena are sealed with the seal of non-agitation.

Because the prior limit and the later limit are cut off, all phenomena are sealed with the seal of things as they are.

Because there is neither a measuring that exercises control nor anything brought under control, all phenomena are sealed with the seal of non-superimposition.

Because they are made by an agent, all phenomena are sealed with the seal of destruction.

Because they have not arisen by nature, all phenomena are sealed with the seal of non-arising.

Because they are unborn by nature, all phenomena are sealed with the seal of non-cessation.

Because there is no ceasing, all phenomena are sealed with the seal of being unborn from the very beginning.

Because they are free from all the workings of sophistry, all phenomena are sealed with the seal of non-elaboration.

Because there is no viewing of form, all phenomena are sealed with the seal of characteristiclessness.

Because the basis is completely cut off, all phenomena are sealed with the seal of non-engagement.

Because no adverse factor is apprehended, all phenomena are sealed with the seal of no further connection with any phenomenon.

Because there is no formative activity whatsoever, all phenomena are sealed with the seal of the absence of karmic ripening.

Because they are liberated from arising and abiding, all phenomena are sealed with the seal of the unconditioned.

Because they are no different from space, all phenomena are sealed with the seal of sameness.

Because they have a single characteristic, all phenomena are sealed with the seal of form, feeling, perception, formations, and consciousness.

Because they are without essence, all phenomena are sealed with the seal of the aggregates.

Because there is nothing that has an essence, all phenomena are sealed with the seal of the elements and sense sources.

Through attaining sameness with the bodies of all the buddhas, all phenomena are sealed with the seal of the elements and sense sources.

Because the worldly path is wholly purified, all phenomena are sealed with the seal of the four noble truths.

Because they follow the deity, all phenomena are sealed with the seal of the great mudrā.

Because they wholly benefit the Dharma, all phenomena are sealed with the seal of the Dharma.

Because they perform all the various actions of all, all phenomena are sealed with the seal of action.

Because all accomplish all the pledges, all phenomena are sealed with the seal of the pledge.

Because none of the qualities of the buddhas abides anywhere, all phenomena are sealed with the seal of the buddhas.

Because all phenomena transcend the world, they are sealed with the seal of the path, sealed with the seal of the Great Vehicle, sealed with the seal of secret mantra, sealed with the seal of the mudrā, sealed with the seal of the maṇḍala, sealed with the seal of sameness, sealed with the seal of vajra wisdom, sealed with the seal of all wisdoms, sealed with the seal of omniscient wisdom, sealed with the seal of mirror-like wisdom, sealed with the seal of the wisdom of equality, sealed with the seal of discriminating wisdom, sealed with the seal of the wisdom that accomplishes activities; and all phenomena are sealed with the seal of the wisdom of the completely pure sphere of reality.

This is the seal of the wisdom of all the tathāgatas. muḥ

Through whatever words of the mudrā, the very seal of awakening, of the blessed buddhas of the past, the future, and the present, through these words of the mudrā too the eighty-four thousand collections of the Dharma are brought forth. Then now, the wisdom of the mudrā of the great secret of all the yogas of all the tathāgatas:

In the center of the heart, always meditate
On a great eight-petaled lotus,
Its light massed with a garland of flames,
With a moon disk set in its center.
Upon that, always meditate on
The supreme chief of syllables,
Peaceful, without beginning or end,
With light like snow and frost,
The hero, white as jasmine and the moon,
Luminous like the disk of the sun,
A body whose nature is the peaceful Dharma,
Filled with omniscient wisdom,
The secret of all the tathāgatas,
Supreme, like the elixir of life (rasāyana).
The letter kha should be known as like space,
Adorned with the drop and the nāda,
The great secret, supremely beautiful,
The self without beginning or end.
Above it, great rays of light,
Light tumultuous with garlands of flame,
A vajra abiding just like a vajra,
At the crown, endowed with five prongs.
To contemplate it always upon that
Is the state of the supreme syllable.
It is to be known from the supreme oral instruction;
Otherwise I do not speak of it.
All the wise buddhas
Have taught this in condensed form.
Having attained the three liberations,
One attains this, and not otherwise.

The tongue should be joined to the palate;
At the tip of the nose, the syllable hūṃ.
Through the union of the two organs,
By means of the words of the state of the mudrā,
The one endowed with wisdom should meditate on his own mind,
Without appearance and without elaboration.
It should be known as utterly, supremely subtle,
The all-pervading lord, the very substance of merit and wisdom.
Contemplate it, striving in concentration.
Like the eye and the light of a lamp,
The letter ra, endowed with blazing:
Having contracted the throat with effort,
White, yellow, and blue light,
And red as well, one emanates oneself.
These blazing lights, with effort,
Should be correctly pointed out;
Having made them clear at the heart,
Likewise they all go to the crown.
Emerging from that place,
They fill all the senses;
Emerging from those, the world
Equally, entirely, everywhere;
Then, coming back there again,
Contemplate them entering.
If one meditates thus with the mind,
One oneself becomes like the Universal Monarch.

Through the thorough union of the two organs,
Meditate in perfect equipoise.
By focusing with the five senses,
The bodies of people become pure.
However many women endowed with good qualities
Dwell anywhere at all,
Having summoned them, drawn them in, and bound them,
One should bring them under control and accomplish the practice.
Relying fully on them together,
The mantrin attains supreme accomplishment.
When delighting in the supreme secret,
Which is the perfection of wisdom,
The supreme stage of the peaceful Dharma,
Meditate in utter equipoise.
Whatever is the accomplishment of Vajrasattva,
That is accomplished; what need is there to speak of others?
The supreme should always be known;
Through the middle one, there is excellence:
Emptiness and the supreme signless,
And what is known as wishlessness.
The supreme selflessness of all phenomena
Brings omniscient wisdom to completion.
Through the powers of the ten grounds
One attains all the perfections.
Having meditated on supreme Vajrasattva,
One becomes like Vajrasattva.
This reality itself is the extraction of the essence,
The supreme nondual wisdom,
Supremely delightful in the Great Vehicle,
Whose nature is meditation on emptiness.
The supreme nondual wisdom:
Whoever always meditates on it
Becomes a king of vajra holders,
Born from supreme qualities.

Adorned with every ornament,
Through union with a woman in body, speech, and mind,
Through touching all over, embracing, and kissing,
One will attain the supreme accomplishment.
A woman who is green in color,
Adorned with every ornament:
Then the mantrin should consort with her;
He becomes equal to the peerless secret wisdom.
A woman who is white in color,
With radiance like a lotus petal:
The mantrin who stays together with her
Will attain the excellent accomplishment.
A maiden who is red in color,
Wearing ornaments of blue lotus:
The mantrin who dwells together with her
Becomes equal to the perfection of wisdom.
A woman like a sapphire
Joins with the secret vajra.

As king of the vidyādharas
He becomes accomplished in great power.
A great secret one, like an emerald:
Whoever always consorts with her
Also becomes a king of vajra holders,
And swiftly becomes the excellent one of the secret.
A woman like a ruby,
Adorned with every ornament:
Through the union of the two organs,
Accomplish her as like a female vajra holder.
Union equal to all the buddhas,
The supreme nondual wisdom:
One who always meditates on it with resolve
Is equal to all the peerless buddhas.

The four applications of mindfulness:
If one meditates on them abiding in the supreme,
Through the supreme yoga, before long
One becomes like a vajra holder.
The four right abandonments,
Meditate on them in perfect equipoise:
Through the union of the two organs,
One is equal to the peerless Lokeśvara.
The four discriminating knowledges:
If one meditates on them in utter equipoise,
The supreme concentration of Vajrabuddha,
One is like the peerless, supreme Mañjuśrī.
The four fearlessnesses
Should be meditated upon through vajra union:
Through the union of the two organs,
One is like a vajra holder.
The four bases of miraculous power:
If one meditates on them in utter equipoise,
Through the even union of the two organs,
One becomes supreme among those with miraculous power.
The four abodes of Brahmā:
If, with the immeasurable mind,
One meditates on oneself in vajra union,
One is accomplished as like a vajra holder.
The five faculties, whose nature is union:
If one meditates on them in the ultimate sense,
Through the union of the two organs,
One is equal to the peerless Vajrabuddha.
The five powers of the mind:
If one meditates on them in utter equipoise,
One becomes like the holder of powers,
The Universal Monarch of great diligence.
The seven limbs of awakening, the seven jewels:
If one meditates on them by the meditation of secret mantra,
Through the yoga of discerning wisdom
One becomes the all-pervading lord who holds all phenomena.
The noble eightfold path
Brings omniscient wisdom to completion;
By merely meditating on it
One utterly transcends worldly wisdom.
The six recollections:
If one meditates on them single-pointedly,
Into a vajra holder
The mantrin will turn before long.
Truly meditating on the ten powers,
Through the yoga of meditation, right now
Merit and wisdom are perfected,
And one always becomes the excellent one of the ten powers.
The ten perfections are completed;
The powers, the masteries,
And all the grounds are generated by this,
And swiftly the signs become pure.

Having covered her with the garment of liberation,
Press her breasts firmly;
Likewise, with vigor,
Take her lips fully with your lips.
Embrace her tightly with effort,
And meditate on the awakening mind.
Vajra wisdom, great diligence:
Holding oneself firm in the vajra sphere,
The various kinds of postures
Should be practiced by one who knows the yoga.
The pledge of women is accomplished;
One becomes equal to Vajrasattva.
Striking in the bhaga of women,
The liṅga swiftly becomes full.
Through knowing this union
The buddhas of great power were fully accomplished.
This secret is excellent,
The protector of all the buddhas.
Through the practice of this secret
The excellent syllable will be accomplished.

Whatever are renowned
As the unshared qualities of the buddhas,
The chief who meditates on them
Is the full assembly of all accomplishments.
The eighteen unshared qualities
Are renowned as the qualities of a buddha;
One endowed with meditation on them
Bestows the awakening of a buddha.
Having attained the four meditative concentrations,
And constantly the formless ones as well,
Endowed with the attainment of cessation,
One accomplishes the awakening of a buddha.
The thirty-seven factors of awakening
Are supremely marvelous qualities;
One endowed with meditation on them
Is the protector of bodhisattvas.
All mantras are fully accomplished,
And all applications, in every way.
Therefore, with every effort,
One should meditate on the nature of reality of the buddhas.
The worldly and transcendent path,
The jewel of wisdom, is very hard to find;
Through offering, empowerment, and blessing
It is found, and not otherwise.
Confession and supplication,
And likewise offering oneself;
Rejoicing in the buddhas and the rest,
And likewise dedication;
Truly generating the awakening mind,
Especially, at all times:
At the beginning, the middle, and the end,
By meditating accordingly, just as it is,
All the buddhas are to be accomplished;
Of this there is no doubt.

Emanating beings with the mind
Is well explained as the steadfast essence;
Arisen from emptiness and suchness,
It is explained as Vajrasattva.
Having summoned all beings,
One should join them to the awakening of a buddha;
That pure and unfailing wisdom
Is explained as supreme Vajrarāja.
Having become enamored through Vajrarāga,
One becomes steadfast by nature;
All beings without exception, bodiless:
Their mind is explained as passion.
All phenomena by their nature
Are isolated and nondual;
When one sees their pure nature,
That is explained as Vajrasādhu.
Through the four vajrasattvas
Names and conventions are applied.
Union equal to all the buddhas
Is the supremely excellent vow.

Great kingship over the three realms,
Having completed the two accumulations,
Chief and supreme in the grounds and perfections,
Arisen from the vajra empowerment,
It is the wisdom of all the tathāgatas,
Untroubled and without syllables,
A maṇḍala of light, of supreme splendor:
It becomes steadfast and permanent.
Gaganagañja, the great treasury,
Holding sway over all phenomena,
The great vajra wish-fulfilling jewel,
Is the unsurpassed Vajraketu.
The great jewel, the light of wisdom,
Is seen to be truly marvelous.
The secret of all the tathāgatas
Is renowned as Vajrahāsa.
Arisen from the selflessness of phenomena,
The supreme nondual wisdom,
The excellent, inconceivable nature of reality,
Is to be remembered as Vajradharma.
Vajra intelligence, great illumination,
Supreme stainless wisdom and gnosis,
Possessor of great undefiled wisdom:
That is explained as Vajratīkṣṇa.
The wheel of Dharma of the buddhas
Is shown to arise from nonduality;
Named the twelve-limbed dependent origination,
It is definitively explained as Vajrahetu.
Intentional language, the supreme of expressions,
Taught by way of the objects,
The great recitation of all the buddhas:
That is explained as Vajrabhāṣa.
Applied to the benefit of all sentient beings,
The great offering of all the buddhas,
The great yoga pure from the beginning:
That is explained as Vajrakarma.
Great unsurpassed loving-kindness,
The unexcelled work of protection,
The pure Dharma, great diligence:
It is renowned as Vajrarakṣa.
The great wrathful one of wisdom and means,
In accord with all the buddhas,
The great devourer of all afflictions:
That is called Vajrayakṣa.
Body, speech, and mind truly joined,
Without obscuration and free of syllables,
The secret of all the tathāgatas:
That is explained as Vajrasandhi.
Vajra wisdom, the great yoga,
The great strength of all the buddhas,
Peaceful, without beginning or end:
It is explained as the supreme knowledge.

Then the blessed tathāgatas gathered together once again and entered the heart of all the tathāgatas, and the Blessed One, the great lord of yoga, uttered this inspired utterance on the meaning of the mudrā of the great maṇḍala of the Ornament of the Vajra Essence:

Next, I shall truly explain
The excellent great maṇḍala,
Renowned as the Vajra Essence,
The protector of all the buddhas.
It is square, with four gates,
Beautified by four gateway arches,
With images of vajras in the gate recesses,
And with mudrās, most beautiful.
In the center of that maṇḍala
Two wheels are set,
One eight-spoked and one sixteen-spoked;
Their divisions should be well considered.
Within the central maṇḍala,
Well arranged at the place of the hub,
Is a great eight-petaled lotus,
Most beautiful, filled with anthers.
In the center of its hub
Is the all-pervading lord, the great lord of yoga,
Vairocana of great diligence,
Loving toward all sentient beings.
All around him, in his inner circle,
Set the eight tathāgatas:
In the east set Cakravartin;
The White Uṣṇīṣa abides in the west;
Tejorāśi is in the south;
Vijaya abides in the north.
In the northeast draw the Radiant One;
In the southeast set Udgata;
Mahodgata in the southwest;
In the northwest abides Mahājaya.
These supreme ones bearing the images of buddhas
The one endowed with yoga should draw.
Akṣobhya is set in the east,
In the south is Ratnasambhava,
In the west is Amitābha,
In the north the one named Amoghasiddhi,
Each resting on a moon disk.
Beginning from the northeast,
Draw Vajrasattva and the rest,
All abiding on moon disks;
The retinues are on moon disks.
Garlands are to be arranged,
Their own emblems blazing with rays of light.
The goddesses Vajralāsyā and the rest
Should be set out with care.
Draw Vajradhūpā and the rest,
And likewise Rūpavajrā and the rest.
Vajrāṅkuśa and the rest,
The gatekeepers, should be drawn with care.
Abiding in the outer maṇḍala,
Draw Samantabhadra and the rest in order,
With the retinues of their own mudrās,
And the retinues of messengers,
The wrathful ones and the servants,
And likewise the male and female messengers.
The maṇḍala of the ocean
Is a bright maṇḍala of light.

Outside, the outer ones with vajra names
Should be drawn with care in the maṇḍala.
Beginning from the direction of Īśāna,
Draw the eight world protectors.
Brahmā, Viṣṇu, the one named Mahendra,
All the gods, Indra and the rest,
All the mātṛkās and the sisters,
The Four Great Kings and the nāgas,
And likewise all the gods,
And the garuḍas of great vigor
Should be drawn with care.
Having drawn such a maṇḍala,
One should make special offerings.
With flowers, incense, music and the like,
Likewise with food, feasts and the like,
And with various ornaments of garlands,
One should worship the tathāgatas.
By the rite of all the maṇḍalas,
With every offering, in every way,
By the rite of all the wheels,
One should worship all the buddhas.
Then all the disciples are to be brought in,
Especially those who are thoroughly pure,
Who, having generated the mind of awakening,
Follow the training for awakening.
The mantrin who knows the rite should bring them in
And have them make offerings to the Guides.
Through the application of the vajra empowerment
One then confers the empowerment.
Upon a lion throne
Adorned with silk and clusters of flowers,
With a white parasol and great jewels,
And a vast and blazing canopy,
At its right side
The wise one should set them.
Banners of various kinds
And lavish offerings of various cloths,
All of these, at the left side,
Should be set with care.
Having properly set out vases
Filled with all jewels and medicines,
With the empowerment of the vajra name
One should oneself empower him.
The empowerment with the vajra name
The wise one should perform once more.
With the crown empowerment of all,
Empower him again and again.
The Dharma crown empowerment,
The protector of all the buddhas:
Confer the empowerment of varied action,
Which is equal to the dharmadhātu.
The empowerment of the Dharma body,
The lamp of all the buddhas: confer it.
Thus the empowerment of the disciple
The mantrin should perform with special care.
Then, by the rite of the pledges,
Join him to the mantras and mudrās.
Do not despise the master,
And do not disparage any practitioner.
Toward all the conduct of mantra,
Do not transgress it and do not disparage it.
Toward the worldly gods
And all the reasonings of the tenet systems,
Do not disparage them and do not adopt them;
Give up delighting in them.
Abandon all wrongdoing entirely,
And likewise gather all merit.
Of the many foods and feasts,
Do not eat them and do not disparage them.
Through desire the Blessed One is mind;
I am born as the son of all the buddhas.
The peace that has no beginning and no end
Should never be cast aside.
All the buddhas are born from passion,
Likewise born from aversion and delusion.
All phenomena, equal and unequal,
Abiding in the state of the middle:
Having known this very pledge,
Never forsake the awakening of the buddhas.
Do not harm any embodied being.
You must not be miserly,
And you must not be jealous.
Do not abandon the true Dharma.
The mind of awakening is supreme bliss.
Give up drinking liquor,
And completely avoid the wives of others.
Never cast off omniscience.
Never kill a living creature.
Toward all sentient beings, cultivate
Compassion and loving-kindness as well.
Benefit for sentient beings
Should be gathered again and again.
These very pledges,
Which follow and protect all the buddhas,
The wise one, out of kindness to the disciples,
Should teach.
Then the binding of the mudrās
Should be taught in proper sequence.
I shall fully and rightly explain
The specific characteristics of these mudrās.
Press the palms firmly together,
Lovely in the manner of a closed flower;
All the buddha heroes
Proclaim this the lotus palms.
With all the tips well extended,
It is called the lotus mudrā.
Bound firmly, each finger in the spaces between,
That is known as the vajra palms.
All the fingers firmly clasped
Are well bound in the vajra bond.
Extending the fingers and pointing them upward
Invokes the awakening of the buddhas.
Having made the fists inward,
By joining both, it is known
As the mudrā of the Universal Monarch and the others.
It is said to bestow the sublime.
Of all the buddha heroes
This is the pledge mudrā.
With a fist with the ring fingers placed inside,
It is known as the Summit of All.
Joined well in the shape of a vajra,
It is proclaimed the Great Vajra.
From a fist of the little and ring fingers,
Extending the middle fingers well,
And joining the thumbs with the index fingers,
It invokes the Universal Monarch.
Joining the tips of the extended fingers
Is renowned as Sitātapatra.
Join the ring fingers and the thumbs,
And arrange the two middle fingers:
The great mudrā of Tejorāśi
Invokes Tejorāśi himself.
That same, joined in the form of a sword,
Is renowned as Vijaya.
That same is called the Victor,
The tathāgata possessed of the vajra.
By making the fist with its core,
It is the source of the forms of the uṣṇīṣa;
Invincible, it is called by all the buddhas
Going Upward.
Fully joining the vajra bond
Is the source of all the pledges.
Join the tips of the middle fingers supremely,
Extend the little fingers and the thumbs;
With the application of the hook and the arrow,
One should form the "Excellent!"
The middle fingers at the tip are Vajraratna;
Spread open, it is the supreme of supreme splendor.
Join them firmly at the place of the banner,
And join them at the place of laughter.
For the great mudrā of Vajradharma,
Bend the middle fingers like a lotus.
Holding a sword is Mañjughoṣa.
Joined as a wheel is the Universal Monarch.
The great mudrā opened at the mouth
Becomes Vajrabhāṣa.
The great mudrā of the action vajra
Is like a dance, bearing the crossed vajra.
By the application of gathering in all phenomena,
It is known as Vajrarakṣa.
Baring the two fangs
Is called Vajrayakṣa.
Holding the fists, the vajra palms of the fists
Are placed at the heart:
This is renowned as Vajralāsyā.
Fully joined and placed on the crown of the head,
It is called Vajramālā.
Joined palms released at the face
Are proclaimed as Vajragītā.
By the application of spreading and bowing,
That is said to be Vajranṛtyā.
Open the lotus palms at the face,
Join them fully and place them on the crown of the head.
At the heart, the thumbs held level
And fully extended: this is the unguent.

The hands fully extended upward
Become the mudrā of form.
The two hands placed at the passage of the ears
Are said to be the mudrā of sound.
Scent, by the gesture of anointing,
Is called the mudrā of scent.
Cupped hands opened at the face
Are proclaimed the mudrā of taste.
By a swift movement in the sky,
Touch is said to be the mudrā of touch.
Join the two little fingers fully;
From the index fingers in the gesture of the hook,
That same in the shape of a noose,
And in the form of a chain, it is to be known.
The fourth, called the vajra bell,
Is known as the vajra goddess who causes descent.
All the mudrās and the subsidiary seals
The intelligent one should discern for himself.
Join the palms fully and clasp them,
And, bending the two index fingers,
Bind them in the form of a jewel:
This is Samantabhadra's, for the wise one.
From that, bend the tips, tip to tip,
Having meditated in the mind on Maitreya.
Joining the index fingers and inserting them,
It is known in the form of a sword:
This all the buddhas have praised
As the mudrā of Ākāśagarbha.
Then, abiding in the form
Of the weapon that is a sword,
This mudrā is called by all
The mudrā of Viṣkambhin.
By the application of the gathering dance,
Extend the vajra palms fully,
And it is known as lotus-like:
For the wise, it is Padmapāṇi.
That same, in the manner of a closed flower,
Lovely in the form of an utpala:
This is the mudrā of the Lotus Savior,
Renowned as the Lotus Protector.
Arrange it well to resemble a mouth
With bared fangs:
This is the mudrā of Hayagrīva;
Place it near the mouth.
The lotus palms are applied
At the frown of the forehead.
The vajra palms lowered are the great one,
Śrīgarbha, possessed of jewels;
They hold the form of a wish-fulfilling gem.
With the palms well and fully extended,
It is the great mudrā of Akṣayamati,
Determined by all the buddhas.
By the application of the gathering dance,
Place the jewel vajra at the heart:
This is the mudrā of Gaganagañja;
It bestows the very treasury of the sky.
Extend the great vajra well,
Bind the little fingers and thumbs firmly,
And arrange the two hands firmly:
It becomes the mudrā of Sāgaramati.
Raise the vajra palms upward
And join the two index fingers:
This is the mudrā of the Vajra Essence,
As the Vajra Essence has declared.
Having made the fists inward,
Make points of the two index fingers:
This is the mudrā of Mañjuśrī,
Known as the vajra sword.
That same in the form of a spear
Belongs to the wise Mañjughoṣa.
Yamāntaka becomes the spearhead;
It is known as the great spearhead.
Having bent the index fingers,
Place them fully upon the middle fingers;
Being fully in the form of a noose,
It terrifies even Yama.
That same made into a sword
Is said to be Keśinī.
Binding all the fists firmly,
Bend one index finger:
This, as the mudrā of Upakeśinī,
Mañjughoṣa has declared.
Binding the vajra bond upward,
Set the two middle fingers apart,
Make the index fingers in the form of a vajra,
And extend the little fingers and the thumbs:
This is the mudrā of the great Vajradhara,
Which accomplishes every aim.
The index fingers clasped by the thumbs
Are said to be Māmakī.
From the index fingers in the form of a chain,
Make the middle of the middle fingers a vajra:
This is the mudrā of Vajraśṛṅkhalā,
Said to be like a vajra.
The great five-pronged vajra
Is said to be the wrathful Splinter goddess.
Making the supreme tip single-pointed
Is said to be that of the Vajra Point.
For the mudrā of Ratnapāṇi,
Extend both hands well,
And place the fingers in the expanse of the lotus:
It is said to be the Urging Goddess.
From the palms fully joined,
Join the index fingers and the thumbs
In the manner of an eye:
It is said to be the form of Buddhalocanā.
Take up the auspicious cross-legged posture;
The one who knows yoga straightens the body,
With the two hands as if holding a begging bowl:
This is called the meditation mudrā.
This is the mudrā of Vairocana,
Which accomplishes the aims of all the buddhas.
Touching the earth is that of Akṣobhya.
Granting the supreme boon is said to be that of Ratnaketu.
The meditation mudrā is Amitābha's,
Which realizes the meaning of essential nature.
The mudrā of Amoghadarśin,
Itself fearless, grants freedom from fear.
The wise one, abiding in the yogic posture,
Whose self is the nature of all the buddhas,
Resting in equipoise, should bind all the mudrās
Or have them bound.
For the mudrās of the worldly ones,
One hand is fully extended:
This is the trident of Īśāna.
Press the little finger with the thumb:
It becomes the vajra of Indra, lord of the gods;
It is formed with the right hand.
For Fire, the syllable oṃ: the hands
Place the thumb upon the middle finger.
The vajra staff of Yama:
Join the index finger and the thumb.
That same is the discus of Viṣṇu;
Set out the letters of the wheel.
A skull cup held in one hand
Is rightly said to be that of the mātṛkās.
Two fingers raised as a sword
Belong to the great rākṣasa.
Joining the index finger and the thumb
Is said to be the noose of Varuṇa.
The index and middle fingers
Are extended well, like a banner:
The banner mudrā of Vāyu
Is formed with both hands.
The middle finger alone well extended
Is called the mudrā of the club;
It belongs to the lord of all yakṣas,
The great Kubera.
The mudrā of holding up in offering
Is rightly said to be that of Brahmā.
Know the stake to be Rudra's;
The goddess's is called the baṭesa.
Gaṇapati's is said to be the trunk.
The moon's is the disk of nectar.

The thousand rays of the sun
Should be shown with both hands,
By forming a disk of light.
Like the vajra weapon of Indra,
This is the seal of all the gods,
Fully sanctioned by all the heroes.
The mudrā of the gandharvas
Is proclaimed to be like singing.
The great mudrā of Nandīśvara
Blazes with the sound of the clay drum;
For the sake of worshiping all the buddhas,
The Great God (Śiva) devised it.
By the gesture of holding the cymbals,
It is to be called that of the kinnaras.
By the gesture of raising the lute,
It is said to be that of Sarasvatī.
Ever sounding the kettledrum
Is called that of the vidyādharas.
The two hands made in the manner of a serpent's hood
Are rightly said to be those of the nāgas.
By the application of the gathering dance,
Likewise of spreading out and drawing together,
And by firmly binding the noose of the hands,
That is called a mudrā.
These the tathāgatas have rightly declared
To be the pledge mudrās.
Through diligent application of action,
These too are called mudrās.
Having released the mudrā,
Contemplate again and again the jewel of the mind.
Nonconceptual, at peace from the very beginning,
The dharmadhātu, equal and unequal:
Having known it to be empty, like space,
The wise one should meditate.
All of these are unborn
And abide like a dream;
They appear like a city of gandharvas.
The world of the living, again and again,
Like a magical illusion,
All beings appear vividly.
In brief, all existence,
Impermanent, suffering and empty,
Having been seen as selfless,
By all the buddhas and heroes:
Like an illusion, a mirage, an echo,
Foam, a bubble on water,
And a reflection is the world;
Thus do the buddhas regard it.
All is like a whirling firebrand,
Just like the circling of a waterwheel,
Like the setting of a dance;
View it as like a double moon.
It is ever like a garland of lightning,
And abides like the humming of bees.
Saṃsāra, intensely aroused,
Abides like the mouth of the mare.
Having known and realized it thus,
The yogin who, by the power of meditation,
Has himself well accomplished the mind:
For him the mudrā is taught.
From the nature of emptiness,
Signlessness and wishlessness,
Through body, speech and mind
The three pledges are established.
Mudrā, mantra and the deity:
These three realities fully abide.
Through the yoga of uniting with oneself,
They abide as a single characteristic.
This the buddhas have declared
To be the sublime mudrā.
The mudrā is free of letters,
Formless and without appearance.
For phenomena that are without entity,
It is said to be the very reality of entities.
The absence of entity is the nature of entity;
The absence of entity itself is also entity.
Fully freed from entity and nonentity,
The wisdom of yoga is supreme bliss.
Without speculation, without appearance,
Without confusion and without letters,
The mind is empty and like space.
When it has become just so,
Then the mudrās and mantras
Are also to be regarded as the deity itself.
Not seeing the mudrā apart from the mantra,
Nor the mantra apart from the mudrā,
The yoga of oneself as the deity
Is known as nondual mantra and mudrā.

From the Great King of Tantras called the Ornament of the Vajra Essence of All the Tathāgatas, the chapter on the stages of mudrā.

Then all the blessed tathāgatas gathered once more and supplicated the great Universal Monarch of all the tathāgatas, the great Lord of Vajra Yoga: "Please generate the family of all the tathāgatas."

Then the Blessed Śākyamuni, having heard the words of their supplication, came forth from the abode of Akaniṣṭha and dwelt in the abode of Tuṣita. There he taught the Dharma to the sons of the gods of the Tuṣita realm, and he looked upon the clan of Śuddhodana. Having gone forth into homelessness, he pressed down upon the seat of awakening and sat there. Having shown his victory over the four māras, he came to sit at the seat of awakening, and, having sat there, he entered into absorption in eighty-four hundred thousand koṭis of samādhis, preceded by the all-radiating samādhi. Having entered into absorption, with the sound of the vajra finger snap of all the tathāgatas he invited all the tathāgatas and spoke this secret mantra:

oṃ sarvva tathāgata vajra samājaḥ ākarṣaya sarvva tathāgatāna hūṃ

Then all the tathāgatas, the great maṇḍalas together with their retinues, duly arrived. Emanating into the dharmadhātu, clouds of vast oceans pervaded all the world systems of the boundless ten directions, and all who dwell in the realm of space came to be arrayed in Jambudvīpa like a pod full of sesame seeds.

Then the Blessed Lord of Vajra Yoga summoned them, drew them in, bound them and brought them under control, and placed them firmly in his own heart. Then the Blessed great Lord of Vajra Yoga, blessing them through nondual union, discerned his own mind with this mantra:

oṃ citta prativedhaṃ karomi

Immediately after that, in the heart of the Blessed Vairocana, from the enjoyment body and the emanation body, the Dharma body, naturally luminous, the precious mind, emerged for all the buddhas, and they caused it to radiate. That very mind is luminous, pervaded by great compassion, characterized by the sameness of all the tathāgatas, free of the turbidity of grasped object and grasping subject, and primordially at peace like space. Turning from that toward great compassion, he gazed upon all the realms of sentient beings. Seeing the world pervaded, through ignorance, by aging and death, he once again set forth the generation of the awakening mind with this mantra:

oṃ bodhi citta utpādayāmi

Immediately after the generation of the awakening mind, he blessed that luminous mind in the hearts of all the tathāgatas to be like a full moon disk, and set it in the hearts of all the tathāgatas.

Having made it stable like a mirror, stainless like perfectly pure space and free of all elaboration, he remained so, and over the great awakening mind he recited this mantra:

oṃ jñāna rasmi visṛja hūṃ

Immediately after that, rays of light of various kinds were sent forth from the moon disk. Having been sent forth, they entered the palace of the dharmadhātu and illuminated every maṇḍala of the knowable. They leveled the mountain peaks of the minds of all sentient beings and brought them to full realization. Then they merged into one, and the great vajra bodies entered the heart of the Blessed One and came to rest in the center of the moon disk.

Then the Blessed great Lord of Vajra Yoga blessed it with this mantra:

oṃ tiṣṭha vajra

Again he caused it to radiate with this mantra:

oṃ sphara vajra

Again he drew it back with this mantra:

oṃ saṅhara vajra

Then all the tathāgatas proclaimed this essence of the wisdom vajra:

oṃ jñāna vajra

Then the Blessed great Lord of Vajra Yoga blessed it with this mantra:

oṃ sarvva tathāgata jñāna vajra hūṃ

Then, having attained the sameness of the body, speech and mind of all the tathāgatas, he blessed it with this mantra:

oṃ vajra ātmako'haṃ

Then all the tathāgatas became stable.

Then the Blessed Vairocana, having looked upon the body, speech and mind of all the tathāgatas, blessed them with this mantra:

oṃ yathā sarvva tathāgata stathā ahaṃ

Then the Blessed One displayed throughout all the realms of sentient beings the body of all the tathāgatas, the cause of accomplishing the aims of all sentient beings, the body that is the very nature of all the buddhas. Then Śakra, Brahmā and the world protectors saw this, and gods and humans raised the cry, "He has attained full awakening; the Blessed One is fully awakened!" Then Brahmā made his request. Turning the wheel of Dharma, he tamed the beings to be trained by each particular means, through which they became irreversible. These are the stages of meditating on the awakening of a buddha.

Then the Blessed Vairocana arose from that samādhi. Having shown the awakening of a buddha and accomplished the aims of all sentient beings, he drew forth a vajra from his heart and placed it in his own hand. Then he sent forth rays of light that illuminated all world systems; they returned and entered the heart of the Blessed One. He blessed them into the body of the great bodhisattva Samantabhadra, complete with every characteristic, adorned with every ornament, empowered with silk and garlands, and blessed to have rays like the color of the moon; and so he remained.

Then, resting upon the moon disk drawn forth from the heart, in front of, behind and beside all the tathāgatas, Vajrasattva, Vajradharma, Vajraratna, Vajrakarma and the others stood arrayed as a garland of great bodhisattvas. Likewise he specially generated Lāsyā and the others and the goddesses of incense and the rest, and he generated the goddesses of form and the rest and the Hook goddess and the others. Then he declared their maṇḍala:

Next I shall rightly explain
The supreme great maṇḍala,
Which all the buddha heroes
Have called the Vajra Essence.
It is square, with four gates,
Adorned with silk and garlands of flowers,
Each embellished with gate wings,
Facades and archways.
In its center is a great throne,
Square on every side;
Upon it is a great lotus,
With anthers, exceedingly beautiful.
In the very center of its hub,
An image of the Buddha is to be set.
All around the Buddha,
All the buddhas are to be set.
In the surroundings of all the buddhas,
Draw the Universal Monarch and the rest.
In all the outer places,
Arrange the sons of the buddhas.
Yellow in color, of great splendor,
The Mahendra maṇḍala blazes brightly;
All are adorned with various ornaments:
Those adorned ones are to be set out.

From the Great Tantra of the Ornament of the Vajra Essence, the rite of the Mahendra maṇḍala.

Then the Blessed Vairocana again declared the great maṇḍala of Varuṇa, the water god:

Next I shall rightly explain
The supreme great maṇḍala.
Because it resembles the wheel of Dharma,
It is called the Wheel of Dharma.
Its second, outer part
Is to be understood as circular.
With many and various lines,
The gates and gate wings are to be laid out.
With archways of various kinds,
It is adorned with silk and garlands of flowers.
In its center is a great lotus,
With anthers, exceedingly beautiful.
Before the pillars of the eight petals,
It is adorned with the ornaments of the maṇḍala.
In the center of that maṇḍala,
All the Victors, supreme in white,
Clear white like snow and curd,
With light like crystal and the moon,
White and steady like jasmine and the moon,
Are seated upon the center of a moon disk.
In all its surroundings
Arrange the sons of the buddhas,
All white like the moon,
Abiding on moon disks,
With various weapons like the moon,
Like the blazing moon, without equal.
This is the one called by the name of water.

From the Great Tantra of the Ornament of the Vajra Essence, the rite of the maṇḍala of Varuṇa.

Then the Blessed great Lord of Vajra Yoga declared the great maṇḍala called the Fire God:

Next I shall rightly explain
The supreme great maṇḍala.
Equal in splendor to vajra wisdom,
It is called Vajra Splendor.
It is to be made in triangular form,
Like the shape of a three-way crossroads,
Terrifying with turbulent garlands of flame.

Red in color, of great power,
Like the fire at the time of destruction,
Ever like the padmarāga ruby.
In its center, of great splendor,
The color of the sun, of great power,
A varied three-pronged vajra
Is to be set at the heart of the triangle.
Like the pomegranate flower,
Like the javā flowers,
A lotus like the padmarāga ruby.
The buddha heroes, truly
Resembling fine vermilion,
Abide like the essence of lac.
In all its surroundings,
Likewise the buddhas and the buddhas' sons,
The wrathful ones and the servants,
The wise one should set out.
All of them are red in color,
Radiant in the element of splendor,
Fully radiating red rays of light,
Seated in the center of a sun disk.
All bear red weapons,
Are adorned with red ornaments,
Their bodies clothed in red garments,
Holding supreme red garlands.
This is called the Treasury of Splendor,
The supremely blazing maṇḍala.

From the Great Tantra of the Ornament of the Vajra Essence, the great maṇḍala called the Fire God is complete.

Then the great Blessed Vairocana again declared the maṇḍala of Vāyu:

Next I shall rightly explain
The supreme great maṇḍala.
Because it resembles the element of wind,
It is called the maṇḍala of wind.
It is like the shape of a half moon,
Like the light of summer clouds,
Like the atasī flower,
Like the light of the indranīla sapphire.
In its center is a great lotus,
Like the light of beryl,
Like deep black collyrium,
Abiding in a dark purple hue.
In the very center of its hub,
The buddha hero, fully blazing,
A king coiled in blazing radiance,
Abides like a black night,
In the manner of the petals of a blue utpala,
Abiding empty, like space.
In all its surroundings
Draw the Victors and the Victors' sons,
The wrathful ones and the servants,
And likewise the male and female messengers,
All adorned with various ornaments,

From the Great Tantra of the Ornament of the Vajra Essence, the rite of the maṇḍala of Vāyu.

Then all the blessed tathāgatas once again entered into absorption in the samādhi of arriving through gathering, and they supplicated the Blessed great Lord of Vajra Yoga: "Blessed One, please produce the means of accomplishing the reality of all the tathāgatas."

Having truly condensed in summary
All the offerings of Lāsyā and the others,
I should always meditate
On the great body of all the buddhas.
Truly endowed with the letters of the mantra,
Make it a body that grants what is desired.
Join sound and form in the same way,
And likewise the garland of letters,
Abiding in the center of the moon disk:
The mantrin should contemplate them all.

The sound hūṃ of an unseasonal cloud,

Then the Blessed One, the great Lord of Vajra Yoga, spoke the Precious Praise of All the Tathāgatas:

Unequaled, unmoving, you possess the unequaled, supreme nature of reality;
Embodiment of compassion, you dispel the suffering of beings;
Boundless in qualities, you bestow every accomplishment;
Stainless, unmoving, unequaled, you possess the supreme and holy Dharma.
Even the subtlest fraction of your qualities is immeasurable;
Not even space can be set beside them as a comparison.
Boundless in accomplishment, unequaled, beyond all compare,

Then the Blessed One, the great Lord of Vajra Yoga, taught the extensive rite for the accomplishment of all the tathāgatas.

There is no lunar date and no constellation to observe, and no rite of fasting observance. Having taken up the vows of the observance according to the rite as explained, make a maṇḍala, in whatever way is suitable, in any place that delights the mind. Scatter loose flowers, perfume it with incense, and make offerings to all the tathāgatas with offerings of flowers and so on, as far as one is able. With this mantra confess one's misdeeds, mentally as well:

oṃ sarbbapāpātakāyikabācikamanasodbhūtān rāgadveṣamohamadamānadhiṣṭhena yaṅ kṛrtaṃ tantrekālikaṃ hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ phaṭ phaṭ sarbbapāpāṃ sphoṭayāmi svāhā oṃ sarbbapābāṃ pratideśayāmi svāhā

Immediately after that, one should offer one's own person:

oṃ sarbbatathāgatānān ātmabhāba nirayātayāmi sarbbatathāgataḥ pratigṛhṇaṃtu māṃ svāhā

Then the rejoicing in the merit of all the tathāgatas:

oṃ sarbbatathāgatapuṇyajñānasaṃbhārān anumodayāmi svāhā

Again and again one should generate the awakening mind, with the mantra that is accomplished by its own nature:

oṃ sarbbatathāgatasamantabhadramahābajrasamayaṃ mahābodhicitta utpādayāmi svāhā

Then, immediately after generating the awakening mind, one should imagine oneself as moon-colored, seated upon a moon disc, with the accumulations of merit and wisdom perfected, the lord of the ten grounds, upon a lion throne in the heart of a lotus of great precious jewels:

oṃ buddhohaṃ

With this mantra one should purify oneself:

oṃ svabhābaśuddhāḥ sarbbatathāgatāḥ svabhābaśuddho'haṃ

With this mantra one should settle oneself firmly in the vast expanse of reality, in the radiance of luminosity:

oṃ dharmmadhātusvabhāba ātmako'haṃ

Then one should bless oneself as adorned with the thirty-two marks of a great being, and with this mantra establish oneself in the Dharma body of all the tathāgatas, which wholly transcends every world:

oṃ prabheśvarohaṃ

Likewise, by this sequence, expanding into the great and vast expanse of reality, one should generate the pride, "I myself am the Buddha," make oneself the ally of all beings without exception, and abide preeminently in the vast and luminous expanse of reality. Then, more extensively, one should begin recitation in four sessions. In three sessions, at the appropriate times, with a mind intent upon the syllables, one should recite two million times, reciting eight thousand, or one thousand, each day, until signs are obtained.

Make a maṇḍala with sandalwood, camphor, saffron and the like, and make offerings with all the requisites. Then, on the full moon of the Month of Miracles or of Vaiśākha, light eight thousand butter lamps, sit in the vajra posture, and make offerings to all the tathāgatas and to a stūpa with jasmine flowers or lotus flowers; then recite until one hears the roar of thunder. After that, one should know that the body of the Tathāgata will become manifest to oneself. Then, by completing a hundred thousand fire offerings, all the signs will become clear. By reciting forty million times, all beings, Maheśvara (Śiva) and the rest, will come under one's power.

Then one who wishes to perform the great practice should make stūpas of the Tathāgata, each the size of a tathāgata's span, and recite a thousand times before each stūpa; or else recite seventy million and a hundred thousand times beside a stūpa made of fragrant substances. One should make one stūpa, the size of a sugata's span, each day, until the recitation of seventy million is complete.

Finally, when the light of the blazing stūpa enters the practitioner's body, the gods, from Śakra (Indra), Brahmā and the world guardians up to Maheśvara of the Pure Abodes, will come and rain down flowers. Gods, nāgas, yakṣas, gandharvas, asuras, garuḍas, kinnaras and the rest will also come and sing praises, and all the hell beings, as far as Avīci, the Hell of Ceaseless Torment, will be cooled. The hungry ghosts too will be completely freed from the sufferings of hungry ghosts. Those who suffer will no longer torment sentient beings. Then all the tathāgatas will look upon the practitioner.

After that, the practitioner will have a body like the color of refined gold and the appearance of a sixteen-year-old; will attain samādhi and wisdom; will be endowed with qualities such as complexion, long life, strength, vigor and mastery; will roam the world systems; will be as great as a universal monarch; will be praised by all the buddhas and go upward; will become a king of vidyādharas, a king of the vidyādharas of the great trichiliocosm, surrounded by many hundreds of thousands of koṭis of vidyādharas and surrounded by many koṭis of divine maidens; will travel by miraculous power to the boundless world systems of the ten directions, making fine offerings to all the tathāgatas with clouds of great offerings; and all the siddhas will fall down before his light. Śakra, Brahmā and the world guardians will give him half of their seats. In splendor, form and heroism he will outshine all the vidyādharas. He will also attain, one by one, the grounds of a bodhisattva. He will become skilled in taming all sentient beings. Blessing his life until the final end, he will see all the buddhas come into being. He will surely display the deeds of all the bodhisattvas. All the clouds of offerings will arise. He will become equal to the great bodhisattvas Samantabhadra, Maitreya and Mañjuśrī.

This is the first, called the means of accomplishing the body of all the tathāgatas.

The second means of accomplishment is this. One should make offerings to all the tathāgatas, place bezoar or orpiment in a lotus vessel, and recite until the three kinds of accomplishment appear. One will bring all beings under one's power, become invisible, and hold every kind of knowledge. Having become a vidyādhara, one will have the color of a thousand rising suns and the appearance of a sixteen-year-old, with hair curling and coiled, wearing bracelets, surrounded by many koṭis of divine maidens and surrounded by all the vidyādhara siddhas; one will live until the end of a great eon and become the lord of all the vidyādharas.

If one accomplishes the trident by this sequence, one becomes Maheśvara. If one accomplishes the implement carried for worship, one becomes Brahmā. With the discus one becomes Viṣṇu. If one takes earth from the top of an anthill, makes an elephant of it, mounts it and recites, one becomes Indra. If one makes a disc of the sun and sits upon it, one becomes equal to the sun. With a disc of the moon, the moon likewise. By this very method, whatever particular god's emblem one takes up, one will enjoy that god's riches. Likewise, by this sequence, whatever one sets out to accomplish, whether mercury or an elixir, will be accomplished. If it is not accomplished, one should then perform the practice seven times. By doing it seven times it will certainly be accomplished.

Thus the Universal Monarch of all the tathāgatas declared:

By reciting three hundred thousand times, even a mountain the size of Mount Meru will fall when one says hūṃ. One dries up the ocean, uproots trees, hurls a vase into the sky, paralyzes elephants, utterly crushes an enemy army, and makes everything that dwells in the sky fall. One brings all beings down. The practitioner performs every rite he wishes to accomplish.

Again, if for about a month one keeps silence and meditates on oneself as the body of all the tathāgatas, one will see all the buddhas. One will display every net of illusion. One stands and walks in the sky. One displays blazing fire, flashing lightning and the like. Being one, one becomes many. One makes even thousands of world systems quake. The practitioner should accomplish all these after reciting seventy million times.

By this sequence, taking up any one of an alms bowl, a monastic robe, an outer patched robe or a monk's staff, one should recite until one becomes a vidyādhara. One goes to all the buddha fields, produces many emanations, dies when one wishes, goes in a single instant to all the world systems, and all the tathāgatas keep one near them. In the form of a buddha, one also performs all the deeds of a buddha.

Another means of accomplishment: make a hundred thousand stūpas, each the size of a sugata's span, and take a flawless sword. For half of the Month of Miracles make offerings with all the requisites, and then on the eighth day, the fourteenth or the full moon, sit cross-legged and recite until the sword blazes. Immediately after that, the sound of great drums resounds. A rain of flowers falls from above. Together with all his companions, he goes upward. He will be honored by all the vidyādharas. He will become a great king of vidyādharas.

He becomes a universal monarch of the great vidyādharas of the sword, wearing attire and ornaments of every kind and roaming wherever he wishes. In a single instant he ranges through all the world systems; he is free of old age and death; every day he beholds the buddhas, the great beings; with all the clouds of offerings he rains down showers of offerings in all the buddha fields; and he will live for thousands of great eons.

Then there is another means of accomplishment. Take a wheel or a paṭṭiśa spear, complete two million recitations, and go to the Gate of Glory. There perform a hundred thousand fire offerings of neem leaves smeared with hot clarified butter. All the mechanisms of the asuras will open. All the asura maidens will come. If it is not accomplished, perform two or three hundred thousand fire offerings. All the asura maidens will come as if they were burning. Having come, they make a request of the practitioner, saying, "O heroic man, please enter!" Then he should enter together with his companions. Holding that sword or wheel, he becomes a king of the asura vidyādharas: master of the treasures beneath the earth, lord of all the asura maidens, lord over all other accomplished in secret mantra and over the asura vidyādharas, lord of all the underworld, one who emanates as a great asura, who lives for a great eon, and who destroys the discus of Viṣṇu merely by uttering hūṃ. If he destroys even Viṣṇu merely by uttering hūṃ, what need is there to speak of others?

Another means of accomplishment: make three million stūpas of the Sugata and recite eight thousand times before each stūpa. Make offerings to all of them with all the requisites, and finally take a six-spoked wheel of flowered iron about a span in size, and for half of the Month of Miracles sit cross-legged and recite until it blazes. By taking it up, one becomes a universal monarch of the vidyādharas: master of all the kings of vidyādharas, tamer of all the gods, Maheśvara and the rest, living for ninety-nine hundred thousand koṭis of eons, overpowering a thousand world systems by miraculous power, making offerings with hundreds of thousands of clouds of offerings, and one will see thousands of buddhas. Every day one makes offerings to them with clouds of great offerings.

Another, extensive means of accomplishment: make six million stūpas, and first perform the approach, reciting ten million times. Then take a corpse that has all the marks, bathe it, make offerings to it with all the requisites, and recite until it rises. Once it has risen, it gives the practitioner everything: mercury, elixir, invisibility and the rest. If it does not give them, recite until its tongue comes out of its mouth. Then cut the tongue with a sharp blade. By holding it, one becomes a king of all the vidyādharas with the splendor of a thousand suns, with a retinue of sixty thousand koṭis of vidyādharas, living for sixty hundred thousand koṭis of eons. One goes by miraculous power to all the buddha fields; one shakes a thousand koṭis of Mount Merus, hurls away thousands of continents, dries up thousands of oceans, threatens hundreds of thousands of the worlds of the gods, and makes Brahmā, Viṣṇu, Maheśvara and the rest fall. One has hair curling and coiled and the appearance of a sixteen-year-old, adorned with every ornament. At the eon of burning one goes to another world system, and one is free of old age and death.

So declared the Blessed One, the great Lord of Vajra Yoga.

Likewise one should accomplish the wish-fulfilling jewel, the wish-granting tree, the excellent vase, the rain of wealth, the great sky treasury samādhi, the great jewel hand, the great rite of the stream of wealth, the fulfilling of all the hopes of sentient beings, the kingdom of a universal monarch, and all the precious possessions as well. These are as follows: the precious wheel, the precious elephant, the precious horse, the precious jewel, the precious woman, the precious householder and the precious attendant.

Then there is another means of accomplishment. Make a lotus of white sandalwood six finger-widths in size, hold that lotus in the hand, and for six months sit cross-legged, make offerings with all the requisites, and recite in three or four sessions without keeping count. When the six months are complete, perform seven hundred thousand fire offerings of lotuses. Begin the accomplishment from the full moon: make offerings with all the requisites, sit cross-legged, and recite throughout the whole night. At dawn one becomes a vidyādhara, with hair curling and coiled, with the appearance of a sixteen-year-old, adorned with every kind of ornament, equal to the light of thousands of rising suns, with a retinue of many koṭis of vidyādharas, surrounded by many hundreds of thousands of koṭis of niyutas of divine maidens, and Brahmā and the other gods will become one's servants. In a single instant one ranges through all the buddha fields and makes offerings to the tathāgatas with many clouds of offerings. The same applies to the accomplishment of the blue lotus and to the accomplishment of the lotus of red sandalwood.

There is yet another accomplishment. Take crystal, a ruby, or a pearl, examined by an appraiser of gems, and make about a hundred thousand stūpas, each the size of a sugata's span. With whichever of these one has, recite without keeping count until one sees oneself completely purified.

After that, perform a hundred thousand fire offerings of lotuses smeared with the three sweet substances, three hundred thousand of jasmine flowers, a hundred thousand of aloeswood kindling sticks, three hundred thousand of sandalwood kindling sticks, four hundred thousand of sesame and rice, a hundred thousand of yogurt and rice, three thousand fire offerings of butter, and a hundred thousand fire offerings of all the medicinal substances. Once the fire ritual is complete, one should perform the accomplishment on the fifteenth day of the bright fortnight. Sitting cross-legged, make offerings with all the requisites and recite until the precious jewel blazes. When it has blazed, one will go at the head of the youthful vidyādharas. With hair curling and coiled, with the appearance of a sixteen-year-old, adorned with every ornament, illuminating far and wide the boundless world systems of the ten directions, one becomes once more the great universal monarch of all the vidyādharas. This accomplishment of the wish-fulfilling jewel is called the vidyādhara of the great bodhisattvas. One honors and serves all the buddhas. In a single instant one circles among the beings of all the world systems and returns again to that very place. If one wishes, having perfected the accumulations of a bodhisattva, one will fully awaken in that very body to unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening.

Likewise, as in the chapter on the means of accomplishment, a painted image, wooden sandals and a garland of pearls should also be accomplished by the rite of the wish-fulfilling jewel. They will be accomplished in every case.

Another means of accomplishment: make a thousand koṭis of stūpas and recite seven hundred thousand times. When that is complete, take a five-pointed vajra of flowered iron or made of the five metals, ten finger-widths in size or about the size of a sugata's span. For half of the Month of Miracles, through the whole night, hold the vajra in the hand, with the hand covered, upon a throne in front of you, and recite until it blazes. Holding it, one becomes a great universal monarch of the vajra vidyādharas, matching a hundred koṭis of suns, splendid like Vajradhara, ranging luminous through all the world systems, and the gods, Maheśvara and the rest, will obey whatever one commands. The asuras will be terrified, the rākṣasas crushed, and all beings will do whatever one commands. So declared the Blessed One, the great Universal Monarch of all the tathāgatas.

From the Great Tantra of the Ornament of the Vajra Essence, the chapter on means of accomplishment called the Accomplishment of the Substance Rites of All the Tathāgatas is complete.

Then all the blessed tathāgatas once more entered into the equipoise of samādhi and made this request to the Blessed One without beginning or end, the great Lord of Vajra Yoga: "Blessed One, please teach the great secret of all the tathāgatas, the joining of ka kṣa, called the Union of the Boundless."

Then the Blessed One taught the great secret of all the tathāgatas, the ka kṣa pu ṭa of secret mantra:

Next is what is to be rightly explained,
Guarded by all the buddhas,
Called the secret mantra joining of ka kṣa,
The great secret, supremely marvelous.
On a square of beautiful proportion,
Wholly excellent and clean,
Having forty-nine compartments,
The mantrin should make it in brief.
In the first two fine rows,
The wise should set the letters beginning with a;
In the remaining compartments, the letters beginning with ka
The mantrin should write, gathered in brief.
According to their arrangement, all the letters
Should be set out in their proper order.
In the empty compartments
Write the letter kṣa and a vajra.
Having laid out this sequence,
Beneath its own class is the supreme blazing one.
First the vowels, and after them
The letters, the seals, in brief:
ca ṭa ta pa ya śa,
Pure, possessed of the water maṇḍala.
These bring swift accomplishment;
They are the very deities of the lotus family.
Truly accomplishing all aims,
Said to be the fire maṇḍala,
With light like blazing fire:
kha cha ṭha tha pha ra ṣa,
Possessed of the maṇḍala of Mahendra.
ga ja ḍa da ba la sa,
Accomplishing all siddhis, possessed of the form of wind,
Most fearsome, like the clouds of summer:
gha jha ḍha dha bha va ha,
Declared to bestow the siddhi of going upward.
Then again ṅa ña ṇa nam,
Supreme at bringing all phenomena under control.
Whatever are the first vowels,
They are the ten powers of the tathāgatas,
Explained in particular as twelve limbs,
The twelve grounds of the tathāgatas.
Leave aside the four neuter letters,
Those called ṛ ṝ ḷ ḹ.
Vowels, seed syllables and likewise mantras
Are all explained as letters as well.
All the classes that bring under control,
Called the dot, the sound crest and the half-moon,
Are said to be the garland of the anusvāra.
The buddhas and likewise the bodhisattvas,
Expressed as letters, and likewise the fields,
Are made clear by what are called the consonants.
Likewise ya ra la va are wind,
Fire, Mahendra and water:
The great elements, the constituents.
They are called wind, fire and the essence of nectar.
By dividing them into even and odd portions,
One should know the path of them all.
Mudrā, maṇḍala, mantra
And samādhi are explained in brief.
Impermanence, suffering, emptiness and selflessness
Are declared the summary of all phenomena.
Emptiness, signlessness and wishlessness
Are explained as the three liberations.
The aggregates, elements and sense sources
Should be understood by the wise.

Now I shall explain, for the application of the ka kṣa of mantra, the joining and separating of the extracted syllables.

Through the letters one practices awakening,
And the Buddha himself passes into nirvāṇa.
They should be joined together.
The vowels and the letters are explained in summary,
And their characteristics shall be fully explained.
The vowels are the very form of awakening;
They should be joined together with the others.
For this, the Buddha himself
Is well explained to be the giver.
First there arises the stainless
Secret mantra of all the buddhas.
First the vowel endowed with the dot,
Rightly placed upon a vajra,
With a garland of flames, utterly peaceful,
Abides radiating outward.
It should be meditated upon in the center of a moon:
The great light of the wisdom of all,
Fully attained, pervading everywhere,
Yet hard to find anywhere at all.
That itself, free, is permanent,
Without letters and indivisible;
It is fully explained as the awakening mind.
Pure like a conch, a kunda flower or the moon,
Truly endowed with the sound of the second,
It is known as very long.
It is the conduct of all the buddhas,
The grounds and the perfections.
Joining the fourth of the warm letters
Is proclaimed as nirvāṇa.
The vowels and the distinctive letters
Should be fully joined with their characteristics.
Endowed with their own mudrās and the rest,
They fully bestow the awakening of a buddha.
Now the reality of the mantras
Of all the buddhas shall be explained here.
First, having made Conduct,
The second is explained as the Speaker,
Pressed upon all over by the fourth vowel;
The third bears the name of Fire.
Being without continuation, it is slight,
Made clear by the fourth.
Not different from the sound of the sixth,
A dot rests upon it.
That all phenomena are like space
Is explained again and again.
That should be known as the fifth.
The fifth letter is of great power.
Born from the self of the five powers of the five letters, the chief,
Of great might and power, the all-pervading lord, born as the self of existence,
Of great might and power, wholly liberating from the three existences,
The five letters are the self freed from the six destinies,
And, stainless, they also bestow the six superknowledges.
That itself is the Conduct of the Blessed One,
Renowned as Akṣobhya,
The bodhisattva Vajrasattva,
The self-arisen being of the teaching,
The suchness of all phenomena.
Whatever is proclaimed, that itself in every case,
Endowed with the seed of blazing Fire,
Rests above Conduct.
That itself is the yoga of the Blessed One,
Explained as Ratnasambhava;
And the agency of all phenomena
Is explained as being nonexistent.
Truly endowed with the seed of Fire,
Pressed at the crown by the fourth vowel,
Is the Victor Amitābha.
That itself is the yoga of the Blessed One,
The Variegated, all the tathāgatas.
Truly endowed with the sixth vowel,
It is renowned as the essence of Amogha.
The fourth of the fifth class
Fully realizes things and non-things.
With the seed of blazing Fire
It rests in the center of the wheel;
Truly endowed with the sixth vowel,
Adorned with the dot and the sound,
It is the Universal Monarch, great in wisdom,
Bestowing abundant wealth.
Whatever is the end of the fifth class,
Firmly abiding in the illusory Dharma,
That itself is the yoga of the Blessed One,
Explained as Sitātapatra.
Truly endowed with the eighth vowel,
It is renowned as Maitreya.
That itself is the yoga of the Blessed One,
Pressed at the crown with the dot.
Mañjughoṣa and Great Wisdom:
It is proclaimed as Māmakī.
Truly endowed with the fifth vowel,
It is renowned as Mañjuśrī.
The Dancer of Conduct, the name of full realization,
Paralyzes every evil
And thoroughly purifies every evil;
It should be known as the Dispeller.
The third of the fifth class
Is Vajrapāṇi of great power
And Yamāntaka, the great wrathful one.
The three letters are of great power.
First the ninth vowel;
In its center place Conduct;
At the end join what is called the Variegated.
Meditate on them as the three bodies.
The third of the last class,
Whatever it is, is explained as Samantabhadra.
The third of the first class
Is called Buddhalocanā.
That itself, the Blessed protector,
Is renowned as Gaganagañja;
The vajra wrathful one of great power
Is renowned as that very one.
All of these are wholly empty,
With causes utterly abandoned.
Truly endowed with the five vowels,
It is the vajra hūṃ maker, the hūṃ maker.
Whatever is the first of the fourth class,
Made fully clear with Fire,
Truly endowed with the fourth vowel,
Is Tejorāśi of great power.
That itself, when not joined to anything,
Is renowned as Tārā.
Whatever is the second of the fourth
Is surely the Perfection of Wisdom;
Truly endowed together with the dot,
It becomes supreme, endowed with sound.
Whatever is the third of the second class,
Truly endowed with Fire itself,
Rightly pressed by the fourth vowel,
Is the supreme Trailokyavijaya, Victor over the Three Realms.
That itself is to be known as the Blessed One
Bearing the name the Dancer of Conduct.
Right after it comes Fire,
Truly endowed with the ninth vowel;
It is to be declared as fire and Mahendra.
Pressed by the third vowel,
The first letter of the first class:
That itself is the Blessed protector,
The protector of the world, the great sage.
Vast wisdom is to be accomplished;
It earnestly invokes the awakening of the buddhas.
The fifth class, the supreme jewel,
That sacred seed of the fourth,
With the limb of Fire blazing beneath,
Is renowned as Bhṛkuṭī.
On the pure letter of Mahendra,
Adorned with the dot and the sound,
Is she who is renowned as Buddhalocanā;
She steals away every evil.
The third of the first class
Is declared to be the Eye of Space.

Abiding fully in the illusory Dharma,
It earnestly invokes for the sake of slaying.
The name that is called Māmakī
Is the supremely secret vidyā;
Of phenomena whose cause is not apprehended
It bestows the stainless.
Joined with the second vowel,
It is called the noble Acala.
Joined with the seed of Mahendra,
If it paralyzes even the vajra of beings,
What need to mention all other malicious minds?
When it is joined to the wind maṇḍala,
Its own seed abides and radiates;
If it drives off all beings,
What need to mention other malicious ones?
That very one, joined with fire,
Performs the rites of subjugation and summoning,
And the pacifying of obstructors and enemies:
Acala in particular accomplishes these.
Blessed by the ultimate truth,
Vairocana is to be enacted; he arises from that place.
For the destruction of others' armies,
The cause, illuminated by fire,
Joined with the sixth vowel,
Adorned with the dot and the sound,
Summons all beings.
Drawn by the wind maṇḍala,
Kailāsa, Mandara and the other mountains
Are surely crushed by it into powder.
Abiding on the vajra, in the center
Of the wheel, it paralyzes all beings.
By driving them from their places,
It brings the bhūtas under control.
Abiding in permanent form,
Entering the heart of Acala,
And attaining equality with the dharmadhātu,
One should meditate on oneself as that very nature.
The precious seed of wind,
Joined above conduct,
Abiding in what has not arisen from fire,
Brings all beings down.
Whatever is the first of the first class
Abides upon the letter of earth;
The last of the fifth class
Abides upon the sound of the sixth;
Beneath, it is joined with the seed of fire.
In the center of the sun maṇḍala,
It sets all the three worlds ablaze.
Abiding in the direction of fire,
Meditate on all as having the nature of the mudrā.
Vāyu and the Fire God are properly joined;
The cause is equal in its function.
Vairocana, Great Wisdom:
That alone is to be accomplished.
From the union of Mahendra and fire
The state of the Universal Monarch abides.
Vast wisdom is to be accomplished,
Or else the aim of perfection:
The vidyādhara state and buddhahood itself.
Yoga and wisdom in their perfection
Will be accomplished in this very life;
Have no doubt about this.
All the vowels and letters
Are distinguished by the divisions of their combination.
The measures of words and speech,
Meditative absorption, and the arising of mantra
Are to be learned from the master's oral instructions;
It is not to be done otherwise.

From the great tantra called the Ornament of the Vajra Essence, the vajra union of the letters ka kṣa, taught in brief; the master should practice it in full.

Then, as the blessed ones had assembled once again, they requested of the Blessed One, the great Lord of Vajra Yoga, the maṇḍala of speech. Then, immediately after the words of request offered by the Blessed One Vairocana, for the sake of the complete perfection of the voice of the great Brahmā, the well-proclaimed sound of boundless thunder, a sky treasury (gaganagañja), vast and vajra-like in every way, and for the sake of the individual full realization of the maṇḍala of the wheel of speech of all the tathāgatas, the great maṇḍala called the Accomplishment of the Speech of All Tathāgatas was declared:

Next, what is to be correctly explained:
The great maṇḍala of the wheel of speech
Is round and excellent all around;
It appears like the disk of the moon.
Upon it is a great lotus
Of eight petals, with anthers.
In the very center of its hub
Is the state of the supreme letter;
There one should meditate on one's own accomplishment.
The Lord of Speech, of great light:
At the heart a moon, on it a lotus,
And upon that one should imagine a conch.
In the middle of the conch is the tongue,
Virtuous, of the nature of vajra.
Upon it is a jewel lotus,
Or else an image of the Buddha,
Blazing and pervading to the edge of the palate,
Pouring down a stream of nectar.
The letter ba, turned upside down,
Rests above the buddha;
Its dripping drops of nectar
Should be imagined in their sequence.
For half a month, for three months,
For four months, or for a single month,
Meditating supremely for eight months,
One should accomplish the excellent maṇḍala.
Seated in the cross-legged posture,
The lord of the first yoga is born.
Throughout the whole of the dharmadhātu,
Filling it completely everywhere,
One should meditate on images of the Buddha.
Equal to the dharmadhātu and unequaled,
All the vidyās there are
That follow and protect all the buddhas,
Those mudrās of body, speech and mind,
Supreme in their nature:
Imagine all of them entering
The maṇḍala of one's own body.
Imagine the clear sound of the conch,
Resting on the element of the lotus.
From the drop on the lotus anthers,
The particles whose nature is sound
Reach the throat and become sound;
That sound is the vowel itself, preeminent.
And when it reaches the tongue once more,
It is the letter, supremely virtuous.

The letter of the tongue, blazing greatly,
One after another, in the manner of vajra,
Utterly luminous all around:
Vajra speech is the sacred meaning.
Until its sound fills the whole of space,
The practitioner should recite, condensing it in brief.
Likewise, if sound at the heart,
Or light rays surely radiating on the tongue,
If light rays shining brightly
From the lips or teeth are seen,
Then know that it is accomplished.
Accomplished speech is the sacred aim.
Or else, the supreme great sādhana
Of speech of another deity:
Imagine one's own tongue as having the nature of vajra,
And oneself as Vajrasattva,
Like a blazing lamp.
In the central part of one's own heart
Is the supremely subtle letter,
The letter a, supreme sovereign,
Shining brightly like the sun.
Equal to the culmination of its own class,
Letter merges wholly into letter.
The culmination of subtle equality,
Blazing and like a drop,
Illuminates the maṇḍala of the heart;
Its exceedingly subtle blaze
Reaches as far as the palate.
Again and again it illuminates
All the letters once more,
And the blazing vajra on the tongue,
As though making them like atoms,
Sends forth thousands of blazing vajras
Again and again.
They go out to all the worlds,
Equal to the dharmadhātu and unequaled,
Shining with the nature of letters,
And by themselves they illuminate beings.
Then imagine them coming back again
And entering their own place.
The letters that have gone to all directions
Enter into the mouth.
On the body, the image of the embodied one,
The mudrā of the mind: contemplate it well.
Then the sound and the letters
Go to the navel and abide there.
At the navel is a supreme lotus;
On the lotus a stainless vajra, beautiful;
Upon it a great conch
Abides, radiating intensely.
From it a sound in blazing form
Emerges from the region of the chest;
Then, reaching the throat once more,
It touches the vajra tongue.
In the middle where the jaws join
Is a stainless image of the Buddha,
White and shining like snow or curd,
Like drops of the nectar of the elixir.
The vajra tongue in its entirety
It illuminates again and again.
Reaching the peak of meditative absorption,
It goes back again to the heart itself.
From the drop arises the hooked stroke;
The hooked stroke has a long line.
From the gathering of these four parts,
Know that it is a letter.
From the letter arises sound;
From sound, the nāda; from that, the vowel;
From the vowel it turns into speech.
From letters, collections of parts
Become lines and sentences;
From lines come verses,
And likewise gāthās,
And likewise continuous garlands.
In this way, mantras and vidyā-mantras
Enter fully into one's own heart.
Then the great yoga of recitation:
The wise one, having made it stable,
Should accomplish it through summoning, drawing in,
Binding, and bringing under control.
Through the practice of this recitation
One should be steadfast in every way:
Speech that fulfills every wish is accomplished,
Marvelous and supremely hard to find.
Or else, of another deity:
The single letter of all the protectors
For accomplishing speech, the great secret,
The supremely virtuous sādhana of speech.
The nature of all the buddhas
Is to be meditated on in well-settled equipoise.
At one's own heart, on a moon, is a great vajra,
With light of swirling garlands of flame.
From it, all the treatises
Will certainly be recited.
Through this wisdom of accomplished speech
One attains omniscience.
One recites all the treatises
And also explains their very meaning.
Whatever thing, however slight,
All sentient beings anywhere have thought of,
All of that one knows,
And speaks of it in a mere instant.

From the Ornament of the Vajra Essence, the sādhana of speech is complete.

Then all the blessed tathāgatas assembled once again and made a request to the Blessed One, the supreme lord of the great mind of all the tathāgatas: "Blessed One, please declare the sādhana of the extensive ritual of the treasury of secret mantra of all the tathāgatas." Then the Blessed One, the lord of all phenomena, having listened to the words of the request, said, "So it is." Then the Blessed One, the great king, lord of the wheel in every respect, declared the extensive ritual, divided into the stages of the sādhana of the treasury of secret mantra of all the tathāgatas:

Next, what is to be correctly explained
Is the sādhana of the Universal Monarch.
Draw a wheel of eight spokes,
Or else one of sixteen spokes.
At the hub place the Tathāgata;
Adorn it with a garland of wheels;
At the rim the skilled one
Should set the seven kinds of precious things in alternation.
At its hub, oneself
As the Universal Monarch of great strength,
The great body of all the buddhas,
Supreme, equal to the dharmadhātu.
His mudrās are beyond counting;
In a single instant they emerge.
Having illuminated all beings,
Cause them to enter the lord of mantra himself.
The accomplishment of the wheel, of great splendor:
One oneself becomes the Universal Monarch.
Minuteness and greatness, mastery,
Independence, enjoying whatever one desires,
The power of going everywhere,
And lordship over all: the fruit of perfection.
One attains freedom from aging and death,
And surely becomes equal to the Universal Monarch.
A precious wheel of gold,
Likewise of silver or of crystal,
Or even of flowered iron,
Held in one's own hand:
In the center of a Mahendra maṇḍala
One should meditate on all the buddhas.
The Universal Monarch, of great splendor,
Adorned with the major marks:
If one meditates on oneself in this way,
One becomes the Universal Monarch himself.

Here are the words of his mantra:
oṃ sarbba tathāgatoṣṇīṣa abalokiti muṣṭi cakra varti jvala jvala dhuna bidhuna trāsaya māraya ucchāṭaya hana bhañja proṃ khini kuṇḍalini aparājitāstra dhāriṇi hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ phaṭ svāhā
This is the root mantra.
oṃ oṃ aparājitādhika svāhā
This is the heart mantra.
namaḥ smanta buddhānāṃ apratihata śāsanānāṃ aparājitādhika svāhā
This is the heart mantra of the rite.

The Universal Monarch is self-accomplished.
Having attained all of buddhahood,
Wisdom, life, power and vigor,
Or else all the sublime lordships:
If the hero bestows them all,
What need to mention other accomplishments?

This is the sādhana of the Universal Monarch.

One should draw a sun maṇḍala
And meditate on it fully in the sky.
Tejorāśi, Great Wisdom:
One will attain all the buddhas.
The supreme mind that arises as nondual,
The supremely marvelous maṇḍala:
Know that it is fire.
It fully accomplishes the awakening of the buddhas.
It is also the king who holds the wheel,
The Universal Monarch, brilliantly blazing.
On it, likewise, are the seven precious things,
And it is adorned with a garland of wheels.
Abiding in the center of the wheel maṇḍala,
Meditate on the auspicious one in each and every sun.
Tejorāśi, of great body,
Bearing the thirty-two major marks,
The hero with swirling garlands of flame,
Abides like the dawning of light.
Contemplate his mudrā always,
Abiding well in the form of space,
Boundless to the limits of the dharmadhātu,
Pervading all with garlands of light rays.
Meditate on him and oneself as vajra:
It fully accomplishes the awakening of the buddhas.
Everywhere, from every side, in every way,
It will arise from all things.
Wisdom, life, power and vigor,
The power of going everywhere,
The state of all the buddhas,
The state of all the vajra holders:
Having attained all supreme splendor,
One becomes like Tejorāśi.

Here are the words of the mantra:
oṃ tathāgatoṣṇīṣa abalokite mūrtti te tejorāśi jvāla jvāla dhaka dhaka dara dara bidha bidha hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ phaṭ svāhā
This is the root mantra.
oṃ tejorāśi svāhā
This is the heart mantra.
oṃ jvāla tejorāśi hūṃ phaṭ svāhā
This is the near-heart mantra.

Through these pure mantra words,
The wisdom of the sun shines brightly.
One should accomplish the power of going everywhere,
And one will attain the state of a vidyādhara.

This is the sādhana of the secret mantra of Tejorāśi.

The supreme one called the All-Illuminating,
Of three syllables, of great power,
Brilliantly blazing, golden in color,
Luminous, reaching above the crown protuberance:
The mantrin should meditate on it as himself,
And he becomes like the All-Illuminating.
oṃ āḥ hūṃ
By merely meditating on
This sacred, peak secret mantra,
All beings everywhere, stable and moving,
Become illuminated from every side.

This is the sādhana of the All-Illuminating Uṣṇīṣa.

The great mudrā of the Wheel of Dharma
Is to be meditated on in well-settled equipoise.
All the buddhas, supreme over all,
Turn the wheel of Dharma.
This wheel has twelve spokes;
It abides with its twelve spokes.
Turned three times in three ways,
It bestows purity for the sake of what is to be done.
The great body of the buddha nature,
Abiding in the midst of space,
Pervading the dharmadhātu:
Meditate on it in well-settled equipoise.
Beings, both stable and moving,
All of these arise in dependence.
This is the great mudrā of the Dharma;
It fully accomplishes the awakening of the buddhas.
Its mantra is:
oṃ dhuna pātaya cchinda cakreṇa bajriṇi hūṃ
Through this glorious and supreme secret mantra,
The nondual wheel of Dharma dawns;
In a mere instant, everywhere,
It turns the sacred Dharma.

This is the sādhana of the Wheel of Dharma.

Like a ruby in appearance,
The form of the Buddha, brilliantly blazing:
If one accomplishes the supreme application of yoga,
One becomes like the Affliction Vajra.
At the heart abides what is like the image of a vajra,
With light of swirling garlands of flame.
In its heart the vajra fire
Arises, stainless.
The afflictions are always completely exhausted,
And one triumphs over all enemies.
Through realizing the Four Truths,
One attains the wisdom of truth.

The mantra is:
oṃ kleśa aśani hūṃ phaṭ
This is the mantra of the Affliction Vajra,
Fierce, and subduing even the others who are fierce.
If it bestows even buddhahood,
What need to mention other accomplishments?

This is the sādhana of the Affliction Vajra.

Abiding on the Mahendra maṇḍala,
Of great power, of the nature of the buddhas,
Victorious over the four māras:
Meditate in well-settled equipoise.
The great fist of the Four Truths,
Body, speech, and likewise mind,
The fist of all the buddhas,
Whose characteristic is nondual arising,
Should be meditated on in the center of a vajra.
Thinking, "I myself am a buddha,"
And that beings, stable and moving,
All of them are unborn,
If one meditates on the vajra fist,
One becomes like the vajra fist.
Its mantra is:
oṃ bijaye haḥ
The great fist of the tathāgatas
Illuminates the perfection of wisdom:
If the mantrin meditates on it constantly,
He attains the Perfection of Wisdom.

This is the sādhana of the fist of all the tathāgatas.

All things rising upward,
Primordially at peace and without characteristics,
The great body of the buddha nature,
In the center of the great maṇḍala:
Meditate on the vajra as having the nature of vajra,
With a splendor like a blaze.
Through this, as the Universal Monarch
Of all the worlds,
Throughout every one of the worlds,
All perfections from every side
Should be summoned and given with respect.

The mantra is:
oṃ jvala jvala dīpya dīpya uṅgatoṣṇīṣa dhuna dhuna hūṃ
As the one called the Upward-Rising Uṣṇīṣa
It was proclaimed by all the buddha heroes.
By merely accomplishing this,
Even buddhahood is not hard to find.

This is the sādhana of the uṣṇīṣa of all the tathāgatas.

The great mudrā of the buddha nature
Is to be meditated on in well-settled equipoise.
The great protector Sitātapatra,
Equal to the dharmadhātu and unequaled,
Abiding in the center of a moon maṇḍala,
Blazing greatly with the color of the moon,
On a completely pure water maṇḍala,
Abiding on a stainless white lotus,
Filled entirely with seven lines,
Like the supreme elixir:
One should meditate on the lord of all buddhas.
Through the practice of this accomplishment,
For sentient beings everywhere,
All hopes are completely fulfilled.
This is the great Uṣṇīṣa Sitātapatra.

The mantra is:
oṃ ma ma ma ma hūṃ ni
By merely accomplishing this,
Great wisdom, strength, and swift going,
And all heroic powers will be attained,
And even buddhahood is not hard to find.

This is the sādhana of Sitātapatra.

In the center of a moon maṇḍala,
A sword with swirling garlands of flame
The mantrin should meditate on at the heart,
Thinking, "I myself am a buddha."
With great clouds of the buddha nature
Filling all the expanses of the sky,
With oneself as nondual in essence,
One should meditate in single-pointed yoga.
The great wheel of mantra letters,
With light of swirling garlands of flame,
The mantrin should meditate on at the heart,
And he becomes a sublime Victor.

The mantra is:
oṃ jvala jvaloṣṇīṣa jvala jvala bandha bandha dama dama druma druma daha dahana hūṃ
The Victor's Uṣṇīṣa, the great sword,
Stainless wisdom and gnosis:
When the mantrin has meditated on it fully,
He becomes like the Victor's Uṣṇīṣa.

This is the sādhana of the Victor's Uṣṇīṣa.

The body that is the nature of all the buddhas,
With light swirling with garlands of flame,
Imagine upon a lion throne,
Or else abiding on a vajra seat.
The Universal Uṣṇīṣa, greatly blazing,
Should be meditated on fully at the heart.
The nature of all the buddhas
Is the abode of the sacred letter,
Peaceful, without beginning or end,
The dharmakāya, supremely great.
By merely accomplishing this,
The Universal Uṣṇīṣa of great power
Will be accomplished in that very instant,
In accordance with the word of all the vajra buddhas.

The mantra is:
oṃ bajra bandha hūṃ
This secret mantra is sacred and foremost;
All the buddhas confer empowerment through it.
If the yogin meditates on it constantly,
He is empowered by them all.

This is the sādhana of the Uṣṇīṣa of All.

From the great tantra called the Ornament of the Vajra Essence, the extensive ritual of the sādhanas of the uṣṇīṣas of all the tathāgatas, divided into stages, is complete.

Then all the blessed tathāgatas assembled once again and made a request to the Blessed One, the Lord of Vajra Yoga: "Blessed One, please declare the extensive ritual for accomplishing the secret mantras of all the bodhisattvas." Then the Blessed One, the great Lord of Vajra Yoga, having heard the words of request they had made, at that very instant blessed himself as all the tathāgatas and declared the secret mantras of all the bodhisattvas, divided into stages:

The great vajra of wisdom and gnosis
Having been meditated on fully at the heart:
Holding a blue sword in his hand,
Born from Mahendra and from fire,
Of great wisdom and great splendor,
Unerring and free of letters,
Lord of all the buddhas, supreme wisdom,
He brings all afflictions to exhaustion.
Lord of all vajras, the arranger,
The sacred aim that goes everywhere,
The indivisible, supremely secret one:
This is the nature of the supreme vidyā.
Six-faced, with six supreme arms,
Endowed with the six superknowledges, the six syllables,
Through the practice of the six recollections,
Sacred, six-armed, of great power,
Through splendor or else through strength,
He shines fully from every side.
Great of form and great of body,
He abides in the element of space.
Or else he is to be known as two-armed,
Abiding in meditative absorption, destroying evil,
The hero abiding on a lotus,
Moon-colored, of great light.
The subtle wisdom of the yogin,
The perfection of wisdom, blazes;
Contemplating, "Primordially unborn,"
He bestows the wisdom of the buddhas.
The supreme wisdom of the buddhas
Is renowned as Mañjughoṣa.

His mantra is:
oṃ bākye daṃ namaḥ
This secret mantra is supremely secret;
It is held to be the most sacred of the great secrets.
Abiding in the three doors of liberation,
The six perfections shine brightly.
Thinking, "I myself am wisdom,"
The mantrin should meditate constantly.
The six faculties are fully transformed,
And buddhahood itself comes through yoga.
Colored like a ruby,
Shining brightly like the autumn moon,
The great being, golden in color,
Of great power, should be meditated on.
By merely accomplishing this,
One's wisdom becomes stainless.
One expounds all the treatises,
And all the lords stand before one.
The mantrin himself is king of all;
He himself is equal to the king of all.
With all the vidyādharas,
He reaches the far shore of all awakening.
All lordships and great bliss,
The supreme qualities of all phenomena,
Of all the vajra holders,
Or else of all the buddhas:
If the power of going everywhere is attained,
What need to mention other accomplishments?
The elixir of mercury itself,
The stainless supreme power of swift feet,
Pills and invisibility:
It will bestow all of these.
This glorious and supreme gentle one
Was born from the supreme speech of the Dharma.
The voice of Brahmā of the buddhas
Is renowned as Mañjughoṣa.
By merely accomplishing this,
If, with all the perfections one wishes for,
One goes steadfastly to buddhahood in every way,
What need to mention other deities?

This is the sādhana of Mañjughoṣa.

At the heart, on a great lotus,
A moon maṇḍala rests.
Lokeśvara, the great hero,
Whose mind is endowed with great compassion,
Four-armed and four-faced,
Pure as a conch, as jasmine, or as the moon,
Arisen from the dharmadhātu,
On a great water maṇḍala:
Oneself as the great Lokeśvara,
Meditate on the virtuous one abiding there.
All of this is pure by its nature,
Primordially unborn and unarisen.
Of this great secret mantra,
Meditate fully at the heart.
Lokeśvara, with his great wheel
Radiating in all directions,
Thick with garlands of flame, yet peaceful,
Wears a topknot of matted locks.

In the midst of his locks is the great hero,
The lord who bears Amitābha,
Bright like the autumn moon,
Completely filling space everywhere.
One should meditate on oneself in the union of secret mantra.
Oneself, victorious over the three worlds,
Boundless to the limits of the dharmadhātu,
Completely filled with lotus bodies.
On the lotuses everywhere
Moon maṇḍalas rest.
With bodies victorious over the three realms
One should in particular fill them.
All beings, stable and moving,
Things visible and invisible,
Are to be looked upon again and again.
The minds of all those to be trained, without exception,
The mighty one should fill.
With color and shape just like this,
Blazing greatly in various forms,
Having gathered them in completely from every side,
One should place them before the mantrin.
Then, immediately, the mantrin,
Once again with single-pointed mind,
Should accomplish the summoning, the drawing in,
The binding, and the bringing under control.
When Lokeśvara, the great sage,
The Blessed One, the yogin, is accomplished,
He bestows all heroic powers,
One's own supreme accomplishments,
The holding of all yogas,
And grants abundant wealth.
The vidyādhara state, the wheel-bearer,
The grounds, the perfections and the rest:
If he bestows even buddhahood,
What need to mention other accomplishments?

His mantra is:
oṃ ārolika
This glorious and supreme secret mantra:
Oneself victorious over the three realms,
It summons all enjoyments
And bestows them in a mere instant.
Through the practice of the lotus sādhana,
Accomplish oneself as Lokeśvara.
The kingship of the three worlds
It summons into being and bestows.

This is the sādhana of Lokeśvara.

All forms have the nature of vajra.
The fire maṇḍala is virtuous;
In its center is to be made
A maṇḍala just like that of wind;
In its center, imagine likewise
The maṇḍala of the sun.
In its center, of great splendor,
With splendor like a blaze,
Is Vajrapāṇi, of great splendor,
Adorned with every ornament,
Illuminating completely from every side
All the lands of the realm of Brahmā.
The letter hūṃ, deep in sound,
With light like the clouds of summer,
Like a coiled king,
Like the flower of flax,
Thick with red flames and terrifying,
Abiding like the fire at the end of the eon,
Four-faced, with four fangs,
Like the ashes at the time of destruction,
Endowed with four hūṃ letters
That sound like thunderclouds:
The one called the vajra hūṃ maker
Is to be meditated on constantly by the yogin.

The words of the mantra are:
oṃ sumbhani sumbha hūṃ grīhṇa grīhṇa hūṃ gṛhṇapaya gṛhṇapaya hūṃ ānaya ho bhagavān bidyārājā hūṃ phaṭ
This king of vajra holders,
Supreme of all, lord of existence:
If he summons even the three worlds,
What need to mention other women?
Brahmā, Viṣṇu, Mahendra and the rest,
Together with their wives and their consorts:
If he summons all of them,
What need to mention other suffering beings?
Abiding in the center of the sun maṇḍala,
The image of the vajra reaches the heart.
Through joining the two organs in union,
The vajra holder should first accomplish it.
Fulfilling every wish,
The Blessed Vajra Holder will be accomplished.
If he bestows all kingship,
What need to mention other accomplishments?

Here the mantra is:
oṃ ṭakki hūṃ jaḥ
Through the practice of this secret mantra,
One should accomplish it for the sacred aim.
However many enjoyments there are,
It bestows every one of them.

This is the sādhana of the Vajra Holder.

Abiding on the Mahendra maṇḍala,
The body whose nature is great loving-kindness,
At the heart the light of the moon's image,
Endowed with garlands of blazing vajras.
The world systems, boundless and infinite,
Having been made one in nature,
Emanating by means of vajras,
Virtuous, of the nature of empowerment,
Brilliantly blazing, golden in color,
Wearing a topknot of matted locks,
In the midst of the locks a great stūpa
With the fine light of conch, jasmine and moon:
Having meditated thus, one should confer empowerment
On all sentient beings, so that they become steadfast.
Or else, toward all sentient beings,
Radiate as toward an only child.
If one meditates through the practice of meditation,
Nondual, one triumphs over the unconquered.
Emanation and withdrawal,
Dissolution and the rest are distinguished by the division of directions.
Radiate everywhere with loving-kindness.
Equal to the dharmadhātu and unequaled,
Everything is beyond self and what belongs to self,
And utterly stainless.
The mind steadfast like Mount Meru,
Unerring and free of letters:
Having made the mind steadfast in this way,
The unequaled is made equal with all sentient beings.
Thinking, "I myself am Maitreya,"
Assuming the manner of one who dwells in Tuṣita,
With hair slightly tawny and curled,
With light like that of a ruby,
With the precious mind completely purified,
One should meditate on oneself.

This is the secret mantra accomplished by its own nature:
oṃ ajitañjaya sarbba satva samayānugata svāhā
This king, whose nature is secret mantra,
Is renowned as Maitreya.
He pervades, equal to the dharmadhātu;
His mind of love is delightful.
Through the yoga of the first application,
One should meditate on the supreme sādhana.
oṃ maitreyatri
Even by merely accomplishing this,
One will come to dwell in Tuṣita,
One's self free of all obscurations,
Oneself equal to a son of the gods.
If one meditates through the practice of Maitreya,
One becomes like Maitreya.
In the buddhafield of Maitreya
One will be born naturally.
Having listened well to the Dharma in his presence,
And attained the grounds and the perfections,
The empowerments and the meditative absorptions,
One becomes a bodhisattva.
And in the end, all of buddhahood
Will be attained through the mind of loving-kindness.

This is the sādhana of Maitreya.

One's own body, all of it,
Should be meditated on as equal to space.
Having generated the selflessness of phenomena,
Oneself becomes Ākāśagarbha.
Through the application of this secret mantra,
One passes beyond even the example of being like space.
Having become rays of light of space,
Emanate sentient beings from one's own mind.
Having emanated them all from the mind,
Gather them back in again, step by step.
See beings as equal to space,
And join them with one's own suchness.
Dwelling in the center of a jewel maṇḍala,
Jewel in color, of great radiance,
His body adorned with every ornament,
Holding a jewel in his hand, the mighty one:
By meditating on this jewel practice,
The sublime state of space is accomplished.
The mantra is: oṃ ākāśagardhāya svāhā
Ākāśagarbha, the mighty one,
The Blessed One, will be accomplished through yoga.
He will bestow all of one's accomplishments;
Of this there is no need for doubt.
However many accomplishments
The heroes, all the buddhas, have proclaimed,
By accomplishing Ākāśagarbha
All of them are swiftly bestowed.

This is the sādhana of Ākāśagarbha.

His body the nature of all the buddhas,
Adorned with every ornament,
Samantabhadra of beautiful light,
In the center of a moon maṇḍala:
The mantra practitioner should always meditate on him,
And will attain the supreme accomplishment.
As many as are the subtlest particles
That make up one's own body,
One should make all of them alike,
Each the very body of a buddha's body.
Or else, in the pores of the skin,
Encircling them completely with one's own wheel,
Making them equal to all the buddhas,
Thinking, "I am Samantabhadra,"
One should recite his great mantra,
Which is explained as the five-syllable one.
Or else here, through another deity,
It has always become the mantra.
oṃ sāṃ hrīḥ kāya svāhā
This great king of secret mantras is secret,
Primordially pure and self-arisen.
By merely accomplishing it,
One's wisdom becomes stainless.
One's gnosis becomes stainless,
And one's beautiful form becomes equal to his.
Eloquently expounding all the treatises,
One becomes the supreme Samantabhadra.

This is the sādhana of Samantabhadra.

Dwelling in the center of a moon maṇḍala,
Adorned with ornaments,
Sarvanīvaraṇaviṣkambhin
Should be meditated on for the sake of the ultimate.
Through the application of this secret mantra
And through the practice of extracting the essence (rasāyana), easily,
And without any long delay,
One becomes equal to Sarvanīvaraṇaviṣkambhin.
The mantra arises as follows:
oṃ sarbbanivaraṇabiṣkambhini svāhā
oṃ nisarāya svāhā
The lord who holds this secret mantra
Is renowned as the Dispeller.
Through the practice of meditating on all things,
With all the mudrās, in every respect,
By their intrinsic nature primordially unborn,
They are primordially pure and unchanging.
Having understood these phenomena in this way,
And having made oneself equal to the deity,
One should practice through the subsequent yoga,
Free of turbidity and undefiled.
Like the petal of a blue utpala lotus,
He abides in a clear form.
Seated upon a Mahendra maṇḍala,
Encircled all around by fire,
Holding a sword in his hand, of great splendor:
Contemplate him again and again.
This accomplishment is of great splendor.
The supreme lord, the Dispeller,
Bestows in every way
The supreme accomplishment that is beyond the extremes.

This is the sādhana of the Dispeller.

Upon a Mahendra maṇḍala,
Seated in the center of a vajra,
Golden in color, with a great body,
With a splendor as if blazing,
A vajra mudrā placed at his heart:
Meditate on oneself as the one called Kṣitigarbha.
Through the yoga of one's own chosen deity,
One should recite one's own secret mantra.
Having meditated on oneself as Kṣitigarbha,
Recite these supreme and blissful syllables of the mantra:
oṃ kṣihaḥ rāja svāhā
Through this inconceivable supreme mantra
All accomplishments are bestowed.
The one renowned as Kṣitigarbha
Is to be practiced by the yogin for the sake of the ultimate.

This is the sādhana of Kṣitigarbha.

Inexhaustible and free of delusion,
Possessing supreme undefiled wisdom,
In the center of a moon maṇḍala,
Virtuous, the very nature of the vajra of wisdom:
Akṣayamati, so hard to find,
Should be meditated on with diligence.
In the center of a Fire maṇḍala,
A form endowed with golden color,
The great king, with the light of the moon,
His maṇḍala of light brilliantly clear.
The mantra is: oṃ akṣayamati svāhā
These secret syllables of the mantra
Make the intellect clear in every way.
One who desires inexhaustible gnosis
Should accomplish it with diligence.
Know it as the supreme of all accomplishments:
Wisdom and gnosis, stainless and beautiful.
If even buddhahood itself is attained,
What need is there to speak of other accomplishments?

This is the sādhana of Akṣayamati.

Endowed with great wisdom in every respect,
Resting upon a moon maṇḍala,
Golden in color, of great splendor,
Adorned with every ornament,
Youthful in form: always contemplate him.
He fulfills all hopes.
Through the yoga of one's own chosen deity,
Thinking, "I am the lord of space,"
With body, speech, and mind, in every way,
Make this firm through all-knowing awareness.
Imagine the utterly stainless mind of awakening
At one's own heart, like a mirror.
Then, having arranged the syllables and words,
And having meditated on oneself as "I am Gaganagañja,"
The vajra, the lord of the vajra:
In the sky, sources of jewels,
Wish-fulfilling gems and wish-granting trees,
And likewise excellent vases and the like,
All the necessities of the lords of all the world, rain down.
All the goods of all the world,
However many there may be, all of them,
Here, in a mere instant,
Are bestowed as the sublime accomplishment.
oṃ gaganagañjo gaṃ svāhā
The great accomplishment of Gaganagañja
Pours down a stream of wealth.
Gaganagañja, the mighty one,
Brings it about in a mere instant.

This is the sādhana of Gaganagañja.

Arisen from a jewel maṇḍala,
Pouring down a rain of heaps of jewels,
Sporting supremely in jeweled splendor:
That Ratnapāṇi should be accomplished.
Ratnapāṇi, of the great mudrā,
Dwelling in the center of a moon maṇḍala,
Should be meditated on through vajra yoga.
Calling oneself "Jewel,"
He bestows all jewels.
Oneself, in every way like a vajra,
With light the bluish green of priyaṅgu,
Arisen from Mahendra and from Fire,
Ratnapāṇi, brilliantly clear,
Should be meditated on with great effort.
oṃ ratnapāṇi ramu svāhā
This secret mantra is of great vigor.
If one accomplishes it with great effort,
Into the hands of all beings
It bestows every jewel.

This is the sādhana of Ratnapāṇi.

The mind that is equal to the dharmadhātu,
Completely filled with the water of nectar,
Should be imagined as the trichiliocosm,
Resting upon a single lotus.
Upon that, of great splendor,
Is the supreme form of Varuṇa.
And upon that, the great secret,
Is the sublime Fire maṇḍala.
Upon that, of great vigor,
A hundred-pointed vajra arises.
Upon that, a great lotus,
Hundred-petaled and luminous.
Upon that, always imagine
The mighty bodhisattva
Renowned as Sāgaramati,
With light massed in garlands of flame,
With the radiance of conch, of kunda jasmine, and of the moon,
Fully adorned with every ornament.
In his hand is the great mudrā,
Renowned as the vajra conch.
Through the yoga of one's own chosen deity,
Thinking, "I myself am the vajra,
Sāgaramati, of great wisdom,"
One should recite the secret mantra again and again.
The mantra is: oṃ sāgaramati sara svāhā
This mantra is supreme and foremost,
Beautiful with stainless wisdom and gnosis.
If one savors it through vajra yoga,
One's wisdom becomes equal to Sāgaramati's.
Oneself becomes a buddha, Mañjughoṣa,
A victor, Lokeśvara in compassion;
What need is there to speak of other accomplishments?

This is the sādhana of Sāgaramati.

Dwelling on a Mahendra maṇḍala,
With light like the petal of a blue utpala,
Vajragarbha, of great vigor,
In the center of a moon maṇḍala:
Pervading the whole surface of the earth, as far as it extends,
Lovely and lofty,
Solid, its essence utterly pure,
Is the supreme, unchanging syllable.
The protector, lord of the ten grounds,
Who moves upon the Cloud of Dharma,
Has truly attained the inconceivable ground.
With light like a sapphire,
His beautiful form, become mantra,
Is the supreme syllable blazing above.
At his heart is a vajra, brilliantly clear,
As though oneself were radiating out.
The mantra is: oṃ vajragarbha hūṃ
This is the supreme and foremost mantra;
It brings about the awakening of a buddha.
It bestows vast gnosis
And every excellence in every way.
Since it bestows buddhahood itself in full,
What need is there to speak of other accomplishments?
It accomplishes summoning, drawing in, binding,
And bringing under control.
Having fully generated the pride
That thinks, "I myself am the vajra,"
One should meditate on the yoga of oneself
And others as being like Vajragarbha.
Having become the very vajra of mind,
One attains equality with the dharmadhātu.
Therefore, in every way,
Vajragarbha truly arises.
Vajragarbha, the vajra of space,
The giver, is accomplished with the six-pointed one.

This is the sādhana of Vajragarbha.

Arisen from Mahendra and Fire,
With a body like a precious gem,
Dwelling on a moon maṇḍala,
With light like the disk of the sun,
Ratnasambhava, the great being,
Abides, illuminating all beings.
A lotus with a form like the sky,
His mudrā, rests at his heart.
One should meditate through the jewel yoga,
And a rain of jewels will fall.
The mantra is: oṃ ratnakāraḥ hrīḥ ratnaṃ dada svāhā
This king, Ratnasambhava,
Lord of the ground of the supreme ones,
Should be accomplished through every practice;
He will bestow vast riches.

This is the sādhana of Ratnasambhava.

Then all the blessed tathāgatas gathered once again and came, and they made a request to the Blessed One, who is without beginning or end, the great turner of the wheel of the sublime Dharma: "All the tathāgatas beseech the Blessed One to generate the Wrathful One." Then, the very moment this request had been made, the Blessed One, the great wrathful Universal Monarch, proclaimed the vajra maṇḍala of the wrathful one, more secret than the secret:

Now I shall truly explain
The supreme maṇḍala of the wrathful one.
Upon the brilliantly clear element of wind,
Fire is at the very center.
In its center is a great vajra,
Beautified by a garland of five rays of light.
In front of it, one should place
A great blazing spearhead.
On its right side
Is a great vajra with a garland of flames.
On the left is a great club,
Decorated with a garland of awareness.
Behind is the emblem of the sword,
Adorned with blazing jewels.
On the outer sides one should set out
All the wrathful ones, utterly enraged.
Its mantra syllables are secret,
A great secret, supremely blazing.

Immeasurable, countless, supreme,
Equal to the dharmadhātu and yet unequaled,
The single syllable, the great secret,
Is realized as the meaning of the cause.
Fully endowed with the five vowels,
Adorned with the bindu and its sound,
That very protector of the secret
Is explained as the seventh vowel.
Heruka, glorious great vajra,
Blazing supremely with the ashes of destruction,
The blue hero, lord of blazing,
Is like a disk of blazing fire.
With light like the rain clouds
Of the time of the great destruction,
Resembling a ruby,
He is adorned with every kind of blaze.
Mighty, with hair exceedingly bright
Like the fire of the charnel ground,
A sharp vajra blazing fiercely,
A crown of skulls blazing on his head,
He abides like the mare-mouthed fire
Of the time of the great destruction.
His brows are twisted in fury;
He stands with his left leg extended.
Endowed with the sentiments of wrath and the rest,
His faces furious in various ways,
Engaged in dancing and in gathering back,
He makes the appearances of the world his dance.
He possesses a hundred thousand arms;
He becomes one, and he takes many forms.
He has faces of many kinds,
Bordered with blue, yellow, and other colors;
His feet too are of many kinds,
Of various colors, wildly furious.
That very one is the yoga of the Blessed One,
The mighty king of the wrathful ones.
Toward the exceedingly vicious and fierce
He abides as the one called Māyājāla.
He appears with six arms and six legs,
Six faces, and a great belly.
Deep black like the finest rain cloud,
He abides like the fire at the end of time,
Like a coiled king of great hue,
Like the flower of flax.
The wrathful one wears a garland of skulls:
Yamāntaka, the mighty one.
His mantras are: oṃ āḥ hūṃ phaṭ oṃ yamamathana hūṃ phaṭ oṃ hrīḥ ṣṭīḥ bikṛtānana huṃ sarbba śatrūn nāśaya staṃbhaya hūṃ phaṭ
This one, the very king of mantras,
The all-pervading lord, tamer of those hard to tame,
Yamāntaka, the great wrathful one,
Foremost, the very nature of all the buddhas,
Invites all the buddhas
And abides bringing them under his control as well.
Ever taming those hard to tame,
As the vajra illuminator he tames them well.
Toward the exceedingly vicious and fierce
Peaceful means are not applied;
So, as the very nature of wisdom and means,
The tathāgatas take on wrathful form.
With compassion whose manner is wrath,
They surely burn even the three worlds.
Therefore, with every effort,
Generate the body that is the nature of wisdom and means,
Through compassion the supreme of all the wrathful ones,
Who tames the vicious.

This is the sādhana of Yamāntaka.

That very one is the yoga of the Blessed One,
Renowned as the Wrathful Particle:
A great wrathful one, red and blazing,
Like the time of the great destruction.
Imagine his color
As like the petal of a blue utpala.
Four-faced and greatly fierce,
With light massed in garlands of flame,
Three-eyed, with a furious frown,
Six-armed, supremely blazing,
Colored like the element of fire,
Blazing intensely like the color of vermilion.
By his furious frown
He tames the vicious and drives them out.
This second king of the wrathful ones
Should be contemplated again and again.
Since that great being, who has reached
All the lands without exception,
Summons them and brings them under control,
What need is there to speak of other people?
The mantra is: oṃ kaṇi dhuṇi hūṃ phaṭ
One who holds this wrathful one's mantra
Is supreme in the three worlds, of great power.
Since he burns all the lands,
What need is there to speak of other people?

This is the sādhana of the Wrathful Particle.

That very one is the yoga of the Blessed One:
The Tathāgata Vajrasattva,
Having become Padmanarteśvara,
Is explained as Hayagrīva.
Always like the color of the dawn,
He blazes like the color of lac.
Touching water and the radiant one,
He is endowed with Fire maṇḍalas.
For the lustful, desire arises;
For the vicious, viciousness arises;
For the peaceful, peace arises;
The powerful he meets with power.
Four-faced and four-legged,
Equally endowed with the four meditative concentrations,
Four-armed, of great vigor,
Standing like a lion, with four fangs:
Hayagrīva, the great king,
The sublime lord, should be accomplished.
Having roused him in an instant,
One should accomplish the rousing of all.
The mantras are: oṃ amṛtodbha bodbhape hūṃ phaṭ oṃ amṛtoṅbhaba hūṃ phaṭ
Through the arrangement of right and left,
Take up firmly the stance with the left leg extended.
Having assumed the wrathful posture,
One should crush the whole world.
This king, the lord of the lotus,
Is the all-supreme lord of the earth.
Hayagrīva, the mighty one,
Should be accomplished through the yoga of power.
The gods, Maheśvara and the rest,
Gaṇapati and the others,
Humans, and those of vicious intent,
All the lands should be summoned.

This is the sādhana of Hayagrīva.

That very one is the yoga of the Blessed One:
The sole chosen deity,
Having become the nature of wisdom and means,
The supremely unchanging wrathful one,
Through the yoga of the all-uniting self,
Out of compassion came under the sway of wrath.
Then, at the tip of the nose of all the buddhas,
One should imagine with the mind, unwavering,
The master Acala, the great wrathful one,
Steadfast as all the tathāgatas,
Secret, holding a sword and a noose,
Supreme, without beginning or end,
His form green like priyaṅgu,
Stocky, abiding like a child.
Throughout all the lands without exception,
Equal to the dharmadhātu and yet unequaled,
Adorned with wrathful envoys, servants,
And messengers as well,
Completely filled with all people,
In the presence of all the buddhas
He grants commands as well;
He abides like the time of destruction.
The mantra is: namaḥ smantavajrāṇāṃ caṇḍamahāroṣaṇa sphoṭaya hūṃ traka hāṃ māṃ
This wrathful one of great splendor,
Mighty, possessing many forms,
Summons all beings
And abides bringing them under control.
Arisen from wind and fire,
With a splendor as if blazing,
Supreme in all, mantra, mudrā, and the rest,
And likewise in body, speech, and mind.
Summoning, drawing in, binding,
And bringing under control: having done these, one should accomplish him.
These and the other endless supreme ones,
Supreme and boundless as the element of space,
The self-arisen mudrās of the wrathful ones,
Fierce, without beginning or end,
Abide as though radiating out.

From the Tantra of the Ornament of the Vajra Essence of All the Tathāgatas, this maṇḍala of the wrathful ones is complete.

Then all the blessed tathāgatas gathered once again and came, and they made a request to the Blessed One, the Blessed One who is the king and lord of all the dhāraṇīs of all the tathāgatas: "Blessed One, please generate the maṇḍala of all the dhāraṇīs." Then the Blessed One proclaimed the maṇḍala of the dhāraṇīs:

Now I shall truly explain
The virtuous maṇḍala of the dhāraṇīs.
Square, with four doors,
Beautified with portals,
And fully endowed with four lines,
It is proclaimed the sublime maṇḍala.
In its center is a great lotus,
Exceedingly beautiful with all its leaves,
Having twenty-five petals,
Well adorned with vajras and jewels.
In its center one should always draw
The immeasurable Prajñāpāramitā.
All around her,
On the petals blazing with various activities,
Divided into twenty-five,
One should draw Prajñāpāramitā goddesses.
Beginning from the eastern petal,
One should also determine their beginning and end.
The syllables of the mudrās, as appropriate,
Should be set out in their proper sequence.
With distinct divisions by direction,
In all the divisions of color,
Supremely varied in their distinctions,
Equal to the dharmadhātu and yet unequaled,
From them come inconceivable rays of light,
Divided into blue, yellow, and the rest.
With the maṇḍala of wisdom's light
One should illuminate all beings.
When they have gathered, all of them
Take the very form of the deity:
Great wisdom, great form,
With beautiful, blazing splendor.
In the center of her navel
Rests the mantra maṇḍala, all around.
Her body is like the color of gold,
Fully adorned with every ornament.
Entered into her own samādhi,
She whose form is a form of many forms,
Prajñāpāramitā,
Purifies the realm of sentient beings.
Mother of all sentient beings,
In every one of all sentient beings
She pervades all bodies equally,
Entering them well, like the stream of mind.
Undefiled and inconceivable,
Stainless and empty by nature,
Similar to space,
Free of existence and nonexistence,
Without beginning or end, and at peace,
The body of the sovereign of all phenomena,
Fully liberated from all wrongdoing,
Abiding in the two truths,
The sublime and the ultimate:
She is explained as the Dharma body.
She is also the consciousness of all the faculties;
Mind and all things, in every way,
Are explained as the mental consciousness.
Wisdom, intellect, understanding, and mindfulness,
Insight and all gnosis,
Thoroughly afflicted and fully purified,
Possessing the extremes of permanence and annihilation,
Yet entirely free of both extremes:
As conventional and ultimate
She is examined as twofold.
Just as, depending on firewood,
The seed of fire is present,
Just as sesame oil is in sesame,
Examine it like the taste in sugarcane.
Or as fragrance is in a flower,
As fresh butter is in curd,
The yogin churns gnosis,
And an essence like nectar emerges.
Just as, from the drill stick and its base,
Fire arises by drilling,
So the yogin should thoroughly examine gnosis

Then the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Vairocana, gazing upon the domain of all the tathāgatas, spoke this essence that unfolds the sameness of all the tathāgatas: oṃ sarbbatathāgatasamatājñānavajra hūṃ

Then, by the mere speaking of these essences of all the tathāgatas, all the maṇḍalas of all the tathāgatas became visible.

Then Vajrapāṇi, having realized himself as the essence of all the tathāgatas, spoke the essence that unfolds vajra sameness: oṃ vajrasamatājñānabuddha hūṃ

Then, by the mere speaking of this, the essences of all the tathāgatas were seen as Vajrapāṇi.

Then from the heart of Vajrapāṇi this essence that unfolds the sameness of all phenomena was drawn forth: oṃ sarbbadharmmasamatājñānavajra hūṃ

Then, by the mere drawing forth of this, once again in the heart of Vajrapāṇi all the tathāgatas blessed all the maṇḍalas, and they were seen seated in all the maṇḍalas.

Then from the hearts of all the tathāgatas this essence that unfolds the sameness of all the tathāgatas was drawn forth: oṃ sarbbasatvasamatāprapañcabuddha hūṃ

Then, by the mere drawing forth of this, Vajrapāṇi was drawn forth from the hearts of all the tathāgatas, and once again, standing before all the tathāgatas, he sat ready to receive their command:

"Blessed tathāgatas, all of you,
How shall I accomplish this?"

All the tathāgatas said, "Bless the maṇḍalas of all the tathāgatas!"

He replied, "So be it," and the Blessed One Vajrasattva, from the hearts of all the tathāgatas, blessed all the maṇḍalas so that they became stable. He then drew forth the goddesses Vajralāsyā and the rest, and those goddesses likewise took their places at the corners of all the maṇḍalas. At the doors are the female door guardians, and at the junctures, it is taught, are form, sound, smell, taste, and touch.

Their mantras are these: oṃ vajrimeghe ṭāṃ ṭāṃ This is to be placed at the junctures of the wheel. oṃ snehavajriṇi haṃ oṃ śaradvajre aṃ aṃ oṃ vajriṇi kili kili hūṃ

As the offering of great delight to all the tathāgatas, at the corners and junctures of the outer maṇḍala the goddesses Vajra Scent and the rest should be set out, dwelling close to the tathāgatas: oṃ vajragandhe hūṃ oṃ madhuvajriṇi ghuṃ ghuṃ oṃ manodbhavavajriṇi hūṃ oṃ vajriśeśire ho ho

The goddesses of the great play of all the tathāgatas, those of form and the rest, at the junctures of the doors: oṃ vajrirūpe oṃ vajriśapde oṃ vajrigandhe oṃ vajrirase oṃ vajrisparśe They bring delight.

The vajra essence of the supreme joy of all the tathāgatas: oṃ samaya hoḥ surastvaṃ

"Through this vajra essence of great joy and bliss, may all the tathāgatas, wholly pleased, become stable in all the maṇḍalas, and please grant all accomplishments!"

Summoning, and excellent consecration,
And joining each to its place as is fitting:
All the buddhas, wholly delighted,
Bestow every accomplishment, auspicious.

The mantra words are these: oṃ ehyehi bhagavān vajra aṃkuśa jaḥ oṃ praveśaya hūṃ sarbbatathāgata oṃ samaye bandha tiṣṭha bhagavān oṃ tuṣya ho bhagavān vajra āveśa aḥ

These are the activities at the doors of all the maṇḍalas.

In drawing such a maṇḍala, the bodhisattvas Maitreya, Mañjuśrī, Samantabhadra, Avalokiteśvara, and the rest should be set out. Having had the disciples enter by the maṇḍala rite explained before, and having performed the empowerment and so on, one should, together with the disciples, worship all the tathāgatas with every requisite.

There, first of all, the master of all the maṇḍalas, one who knows all the commitments, who has been blessed with all accomplishments by the Blessed One, the great Lord of Vajra Yoga, who has obtained all the empowerments, and who is like the Lord of Vajra Yoga himself, should train, through the wisdom of omniscience, in the commitment mudrās of all the tathāgatas.

Then, as for the binding of the commitment mudrās here: sitting cross-legged and anointing both hands with scents and the like, one should carry out the binding of the mudrās.

Forming the two hands into the vajra bond, the fingers interlaced with each other, and placing the vajra bond in the middle of the crossed legs, is the mudrā of all the tathāgatas. Those of Vajrasattva and the rest are done by applying the sequence of the dance.

Holding that same vajra bond at the waist is the mudrā of Vajrasattva. From the vajra bond, holding the index fingers like a hook is the mudrā of Vajrarāja. That same, applied as though shooting an arrow, is the mudrā of the vajra arrow. From that same, placing it behind while giving the gesture of "Excellent!" is that of Vajrasādhu. These are the mudrās of the awakening mind.

Placing that same vajra bond at the forehead is the mudrā of Vajraratna. From the vajra bond, applying a circle of light is the mudrā of the vajra sun's circle of light. Joining the vajra bond at the crown of the head is the mudrā of the banner. Circling it before the mouth is the mudrā of Vajrahāsa.

Circling that same vajra bond before the mouth is the mudrā of Vajradharma. That same, made at the heart, is the mudrā of the vajra sword. That same, circled about like a wheel, is the mudrā of Vajrahetu. That same, raised at the heart, is the mudrā of Vajrabhāṣa. These are the Dharma mudrās.

Displaying that same vajra bond as the dance itself is the mudrā of manifold action. Placing the two hands at the crown, the heart, and the throat is the mudrā of Vajrarakṣa. Placing the vajra bond at the two cheeks is the mudrā of Vajrayakṣa. Pressing that same vajra bond at the heart is the mudrā of Vajramuṣṭi.

Likewise, by the applications of Lāsyā, Dhūpā, and the rest, one should apply all the mudrās of all the goddesses.

Playful, with flirtatious movement and laughter,
With the moods of grace and of compassion,
The moods of heroism, wrath, and the rest,
And the mood whose very nature is peace:
All the mudrās fully assembled.
If you desire supreme accomplishment,
Either perform the vajra dance yourself,
Or apply the binding of the mudrās
Of all the buddhas, the protectors.
For those who wish to accomplish everything and the rest,
The cross-legged posture of all the buddhas.
Therefore, nondual, the vajra holder:
The upright body of the buddhas,
The vajra holder in graceful application;
The peaceful eyes of the buddhas,
The vajra holder's understood as proud.
The eyes of the buddhas and the vajra eyes
Are to be known from the divisions of samādhi.
The wrathful ones have a turbulent gaze;
The consorts, playfulness itself.
The worldly gods
And evildoers gaze upward.
The gaze that follows after all phenomena
With force is a mudrā.
The hosts, the male and the female messengers
Have gazes of manifold kinds.
Fully endowed with the lip mudrā,
Unwavering and unmoving,
The supreme mudrā of the buddhas
Bestows the awakening of a buddha.
The lips slightly joined,
Without change of expression, it abides beyond compare;
Endowed with the syllables of play,
This is the lip mudrā of the vajra holder.
With changing expression, eyes rolling,
The teeth clenched tight in wrath:
This is declared the wrathful mudrā.
The lip mudrā of beautiful form
Is the lip mudrā of the worldly ones.
Expression breaking up, devoid of form,
With desire very hard to bear,
And with hatred and delusion, in all actions,
Playful, with flirtatious movement,
Moving and radiating out:
This too is to be known as a lip mudrā.
For the goddesses, the messengers, and the hosts as well,
There are these and others, infinite and supreme.
The names of the gazes, the lips, and the rest,
All the mudrās in summary,
Should be known in full by the protectors.
As many as the divisions of the body
And the divisions of the faculties and the rest,
So many are those infinite, supreme mudrās,
Discerned by all the buddhas.
The manifold body of great splendor,
Source of immeasurable qualities,
The supreme mudrā of the buddhas,
Bestows the awakening of a buddha.

From the great Tantra of the Ornament of the Vajra Essence, the great maṇḍala called the Secret Reality of the Great Commitment is complete.

Then all the blessed tathāgatas gathered once again and came, and with the secret names of all the tathāgatas they offered excellent praise to the Blessed One, the great Lord of Vajra Yoga, and delighted the Blessed One:

From the great Tantra of the Ornament of the Vajra Essence, this extensive ritual called the Emergence of the Maṇḍala of the Perfection of Wisdom is complete.

Then all the blessed tathāgatas gathered once again and came, and made a request to the Blessed One, the great Lord of Vajra Yoga without beginning or end. Then the Blessed One, the great Lord of Vajra Yoga, having heard the words of their request, entered into equipoise in the samādhi called the Vajra of Gnosis Entering the Domain of All Buddhas. Immediately the Blessed Lady also entered into equipoise.

Then in that very instant this Blessed One, the great Lord of Vajra Yoga, became the youthful Mañjuśrī, who had entered the vajra of the wisdom and gnosis of all the tathāgatas. Having entered it, he illuminated and made firm the samādhi of wisdom and gnosis, the essence of all the tathāgatas, and came forth from the heart of all the tathāgatas. He illuminated all the realms of sentient beings, without exception and with none left out, throughout the boundless dharmadhātu that is the domain of all the buddhas. He purified the wisdom and gnosis of all sentient beings as well, caused them to generate the awakening mind, established them, gladdened them, and did them good. Abiding thus, he brought the samādhi of Vajrasattva to excellent completion and conferred empowerment. Applying it definitively to Splendor, to the Banner and to Laughter, and from the great supreme lord abiding fully, through the samādhi of Vajrasattva, in the realization of the Dharma, he made firm the minds of the beings of Sharpness, of the Cause and of Speech as well. Having become fully purified, he again joined the samādhi of the Lotus Being to the lord pure by nature, to the nature of reality that is without basis, and so the master of various actions, the protector who brings sentient beings to maturity, the wrathful one, and the vajra binding of body, speech and mind of all the tathāgatas brought all the realms of sentient beings to full maturity and blessed them. Then he returned and entered again into the heart of the Blessed One, and abiding in the wisdom and gnosis of all the tathāgatas, he uttered this inspired utterance:

How wonderful! I am the self of undefiled wisdom,
The utterly stainless wisdom mind
Of all the buddha protectors;
I have become the body of a being.
When the existence and nonexistence
Of all the stainless buddhas are examined,
How wonderful! The Dharma, inconceivable itself,
Abides under my control.

Then the bodhisattva great being Mañjuśrī came forth from the heart of the Blessed One, emanated in the bodies of sixteen bodhisattvas, and took his seat all around in the retinue of all the tathāgatas encircling the Blessed One Vairocana, upon sun disks, upon disks of light, and upon blessed moon disks. Then he said to the Blessed One, the great Lord of Vajra Yoga, "Blessed One, please speak the words of Dharma that teach in brief the Perfection of Wisdom from the Tantra of the Ornament of the Vajra Essence."

Then the Blessed One Vairocana said to the youthful Mañjuśrī, "Mañjuśrī, excellent, excellent, that you think to ask the Tathāgata about this matter.

Listen well and attentively, and keep it in mind, and I shall explain."

Then the Blessed One, the great Lord of Vajra Yoga, having blessed himself as all the tathāgatas, spoke these words of mantra:

"The four applications of mindfulness are these. With regard to the inner body, the outer body, and the body both outer and inner, one dwells contemplating the body, diligent, possessed of vigilance and mindful, having dispelled worldly attachment and mental unhappiness. One dwells contemplating origination in the body. One dwells contemplating passing away in the body. One dwells without abiding in the body, and grasps at nothing whatsoever in the world. With regard to inner feelings, outer feelings, and feelings both outer and inner; inner mind, outer mind, and mind both outer and inner; inner phenomena, outer phenomena, and phenomena both outer and inner, one dwells contemplating phenomena in phenomena, diligent, possessed of vigilance and mindful, having dispelled worldly attachment and mental unhappiness. One dwells contemplating origination in phenomena. One dwells contemplating passing away in phenomena. One dwells without abiding in phenomena, and grasps at nothing whatsoever in the world. One constantly abides in the Perfection of Wisdom through the equality of body, speech, mind, and phenomena."

oṃ kaṃ oṃ catvāri smṛtyāpasthanānā hūṃ

"The four right efforts are these. So that evil, unwholesome dharmas that have not arisen may not arise, one generates resolve and strives, in a manner that does not apprehend them; one undertakes diligence; one firmly holds the mind; one rightly makes aspirations. For the abandoning of evil, unwholesome dharmas that have arisen, one generates resolve and the rest; and in order to generate wholesome dharmas that have not arisen, one generates resolve, makes effort, undertakes diligence, firmly holds the mind, and rightly makes aspirations. For the sake of the increase, growth, and broadening of wholesome dharmas that have arisen, and for the complete perfection of their cultivation, one generates resolve, makes effort, undertakes diligence, firmly holds the mind, and rightly makes aspirations. One should realize all dharmas as, in the ultimate sense, neither abandoned nor not abandoned." Thus spoke the Blessed One, the great Lord of Vajra Yoga.

oṃ samyaka prahāṇa sarvva tathāgata samatā adhiṣṭha aḥ raḥ hūṃ

This, called the Declaration of the Right Efforts, is a gateway of the Perfection of Wisdom.

"The four bases of miraculous power are these. One cultivates the basis of miraculous power endowed with the samādhi of resolve and the formative force of exertion. One cultivates the bases of miraculous power endowed with the samādhi of diligence, of mind, and of investigation, together with the formative force of exertion. In the Perfection of Wisdom, all dharmas of the gateways of the ultimate and the relative enter into the defining mark of illusion, of illusion." Thus spoke the Blessed One, the Universal Monarch of All Dharmas.

oṃ ṛdhi pāda pāramite labāpara hūṃ hūṃ

This, called the Perfection of the Bases of Miraculous Power, the Wisdom of Equality, is the Perfection of Wisdom.

"The five faculties are the faculty of faith, the faculty of diligence, the faculty of mindfulness, the faculty of samādhi, and the faculty of wisdom." These faculties free of elaboration were spoken by the Blessed One, who has mastery over the equality of all dharmas.

oṃ indriyā babodha prajñe iśeñca nāmanya nāya gatāya āḥ hūṃ

This, called the Equality of the Faculties, is the Perfection of Wisdom.

"The five powers are the power of faith, the power of diligence, the power of mindfulness, the power of samādhi, and the power of wisdom," he said. The Blessed One, who abides in the equality of all dharmas, who has realized all the powers, who is free of all elaboration and pure from the very beginning, spoke this:

oṃ sarvva tathāgatā bala aḥ hūṃ hūṃ baḥ

This, called the Increase of Power, is the Perfection of Wisdom.

"The four abodes of Brahmā are loving-kindness, compassion, joy, and equanimity."

oṃ brahma bihara dhiṣṭhāna gaṃ maṃ druṃ kkhaṃ hūṃ trāṃ

He spoke this Perfection of Wisdom called the Equality of All Dharmas. It is the blessing of the abodes of Brahmā.

"The four analytical knowledges are the analytical knowledge of eloquence, the analytical knowledge of definitive words, the analytical knowledge of meaning, and the analytical knowledge of dharmas." So spoke the Blessed One: "Because it discerns all dharmas as having the defining mark of illusion, it is the Perfection of Wisdom."

oṃ pratisambidaḥ sva hūṃ

The Perfection of Wisdom is equality.

"What are the six recollections? Recollection of the Buddha, recollection of the Dharma, recollection of the Saṅgha, recollection of generosity, recollection of moral discipline, and recollection of the gods. The single recollection of all dharmas is called the emptiness of all dharmas in the ultimate sense." This, called the Gateway of Equality through engaging with characteristics and the absence of characteristics, was spoken by the one who has mastery over samādhi.

oṃ sarvva anusmṛti buddhaṃ saṃ padādiśi hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ

This is the wisdom of the six recollections.

"What are the seven branches of awakening? The branch of awakening that is mindfulness, the branch of awakening that is discernment of dharmas, the branch of awakening that is diligence, the branch of awakening that is joy, the branch of awakening that is pliancy, the branch of awakening that is samādhi, and the branch of awakening that is equanimity." Thus he spoke the Perfection of Wisdom called the Equality of the Branches of Awakening, the Defining Mark of Non-arising.

oṃ sarvvatā bodhyaṃga samatā supratiṣṭhite dhruvaṃ

What is the noble eightfold path? Right view, right thought, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right effort, right mindfulness, and right samādhi. It is the wisdom of omniscience that, entering the great awakening mind, the path that knows no exhaustion, discerns well.

oṃ āryāṣṭhāṃga mārga maṭa

This, called the Equality of the Noble Eightfold Path, is the Perfection of Wisdom.

The three samādhis are emptiness, signlessness, and wishlessness. Among these, what is the samādhi of emptiness? It is the one-pointed mind that realizes all dharmas as having a single characteristic, through the aspect of emptiness, the aspect of hollowness and vacuity, and the aspect of lacking intrinsic nature. This is declared to be the samādhi of emptiness. Among these, what is the samādhi of signlessness? It is the one-pointed mind in the aspect of making nothing into a sign, of the absence of desire, of the absence of cognition, of peace, and of separation. This is called the samādhi of signlessness. Among these, what is the samādhi of wishlessness? It is the one-pointed mind in the aspect of impermanence and the aspect of suffering. That is called the samādhi of wishlessness. Having realized all dharmas, one makes no wish. One does not seek after any god, or anything else besides a god, as one's aim, nor does one wish to make supplication, with regard to any thing whatsoever, even through the gateways of all the samādhis. Through this very Perfection of Wisdom one should come to understand the three gateways of samādhi.

oṃ khasamaḥ sarvva dharmmāḥ khaṃ

Among these, the ten powers are the power of knowing what is possible and what is impossible, the power of knowing the maturation of karma, the power of knowing the various elements, the power of knowing superior and inferior faculties, the power of knowing the various inclinations, the power of discerning the cessation of suffering, the power of knowing affliction and purification, the power of knowing the recollection of former lives, the power of knowing the five superknowledges, and the power of knowing the exhaustion of the defilements. Because it discerns the equality of all the tathāgatas, it is the Perfection of Wisdom.

oṃ daśabala samatā thā

This, called the Equality of the Ten Powers, is the Perfection of Wisdom.

What are the eight liberations? Possessing form, one sees forms: this is the first liberation. With the perception of having no inner form, one sees outer forms: this is the second liberation. Being intent on the beautiful: this is the third liberation. Completely transcending perceptions of form, with perceptions of resistance having subsided, and not attending to perceptions of variety, thinking, "Space is infinite," one attains and dwells in the sphere of infinite space: this is the fourth liberation. Completely transcending the sphere of infinite space in every way, thinking, "Consciousness is infinite," one attains and dwells in the sphere of infinite consciousness: this is the fifth liberation. Completely transcending the sphere of infinite consciousness in every way, thinking, "There is nothing at all," one attains and dwells in the sphere of nothingness: this is the sixth liberation. Completely transcending the sphere of nothingness in every way, one attains and dwells in the sphere of neither perception nor non-perception: this is the seventh liberation. Completely transcending the sphere of neither perception nor non-perception in every way, one attains and dwells in the absorption of the cessation of perception and feeling: this is the eighth liberation. He declared this: all dharmas have the characteristic of freedom from elaboration; they are without affliction and without purification, without meditative concentration and yet not without meditative concentration, without meditative absorption and yet not meditative absorption.

oṃ samanta bhadre sarvva svabhāva biśuddhe rdhaṃ ṣaṃ khaṃ

This, called Free of the Possible and the Impossible, is the Perfection of Wisdom.

What are the four fearlessnesses? When I declare, "I am perfectly and completely awakened," I see no ground at all on which any ascetic, brahmin, god, māra, Brahmā, or any other assembly of beings in the world could challenge me in accordance with Dharma, saying, "You have not fully awakened to these dharmas." Since I see no such ground, I dwell having attained selflessness, having attained freedom from anxiety, and having attained fearlessness. I claim the place of the great leader of the herd. I roar the lion's roar in the midst of the assembly. I set fully turning the wheel of Brahmā, which no ascetic, brahmin, god, māra, Brahmā, or any other assembly of beings whatsoever in the world can turn in accordance with Dharma.

When I declare, "My defilements are exhausted," that any ascetic, brahmin, god, māra, Brahmā, or any other assembly of beings could say to me, "These defilements of yours have not been completely exhausted," and so forth, up to "in accordance with Dharma in the world."

"Those things that I have taught to be obstructive dharmas do not become obstructive dharmas when one relies on them": this should be applied in full.

"The path that I have declared to be the path of the noble ones that leads to deliverance does not lead to deliverance; how could it bring about the true exhaustion of suffering and the true ending of suffering?" Here, seeing no ground on which any ascetic, brahmin, god, māra, Brahmā, or any assembly of beings in the world could challenge in accordance with Dharma, and all the rest, should be stated in full just as before.

Fearlessness is the equality of all dharmas, the characteristic of illusion, because it is free of grasping at "I" and grasping at "mine."

oṃ baiśāradya samatā prāpta a a aṃ aḥ

What are the nine absorptions of the equality of the successive abidings? Secluded from desires, secluded from evil, unwholesome dharmas, with thought and with analysis, possessing the joy and bliss born of seclusion, one attains and dwells in the first meditative concentration. With thought and analysis stilled, inwardly fully clear, the mind's continuum having become unified, without thought, without analysis, possessing the joy and bliss born of samādhi, one attains and dwells in the second meditative concentration. Being free of attachment to joy, one dwells in equanimity, mindful and vigilant, experiencing bliss with the body; this the noble ones describe as "equanimous, mindful, dwelling in bliss." Without joy, one attains and dwells in the third meditative concentration. Having abandoned bliss and abandoned suffering as well, mental happiness and mental unhappiness having already subsided, with neither bliss nor suffering, with equanimity and mindfulness completely pure, one attains and dwells in the fourth meditative concentration. Completely transcending perceptions of form in every way, with perceptions of resistance having subsided, and not attending to perceptions of variety, thinking, "Space is infinite," one attains and dwells in the sphere of infinite space. Completely transcending the sphere of infinite space in every way, thinking, "Consciousness is infinite," one attains and dwells in the sphere of infinite consciousness. Completely transcending the sphere of infinite consciousness in every way, thinking, "There is nothing at all," one attains and dwells in the sphere of nothingness. Completely transcending the sphere of nothingness in every way, one attains and dwells in the sphere of neither perception nor non-perception. Completely transcending the sphere of neither perception nor non-perception in every way, one attains and dwells in the absorption of the cessation of perception and feeling. Having attained the eight liberations and equality, one does not conceive of things as existent, nor conceive of them as nonexistent; one realizes all dharmas as nondual. This is the equality of the nine absorptions of the successive abidings.

oṃ anantaḥ bimokṣa mukhe śuṃ

This, called the Equality of the Liberations of the Successive Abidings of All Things, is the Perfection of Wisdom.

Among these, what are the ten perfections? They are as follows: the perfection of generosity, the perfection of moral discipline, the perfection of patience, the perfection of diligence, the perfection of meditative concentration, the perfection of wisdom, the perfection of aspiration, the perfection of primordial wisdom, the perfection of power, the perfection of skillful means, the perfection of everything, the perfection of the unequaled and the equal, the perfection of non-elaboration, the perfection of the true meaning, the perfection of the meaning of the suchness of all dharmas, the perfection of the vajra, the perfection of the jewel, the perfection of the lotus, the perfection of action, and the perfection of the equality of all dharmas. This is by way of the practice of realizing them in accordance with the teaching.

oṃ sarvva pāramita artha paripūraṇi oṃ prāṃ hūṃ taṃ

This, called the Perfection of Everything, is the Perfection of Wisdom.

Among these, what are the ten grounds? The ground called Very Joyful, the one called Stainless, the one called Luminous, the one called Radiant, the one called Hard to Conquer, the one called Manifest, the one called Gone Far, the one called Immovable, the one called Excellent Intelligence, and the one called Cloud of Dharma. All the grounds are one in meaning; that is, they are the meaning of primordial wisdom. And primordial wisdom has the meaning of being nothing whatsoever, without affliction, without letters, unfailing, pure from the very beginning, stainless, luminous, and beyond expression.

oṃ bhūmi biśodhani sarvvartha paripūraṇi baṃ bhruṃ

This, called the Purification of the Grounds, is the Perfection of Wisdom.

What are the ten abodes of the bodhisattvas? The bodhisattva who has first generated the mind, the youthful one, the beginner, the practitioner of yoga, the one endowed with application from birth to birth, the one endowed with resolve, the irreversible one, the one who has obtained consecration, the one bound to a single further birth: these are the abodes of the bodhisattvas. Abiding and non-abiding are cast aside, because in the ultimate sense there is a single abiding; that is, there is no abiding.

oṃ sarvva byabasthāna bigate hūṃ

This, called Non-abiding, is the Perfection of Wisdom.

What are the ten masteries of the bodhisattvas? Mastery over life span, mastery over karma, mastery over requisites, mastery over inclination, mastery over aspiration, mastery over miraculous power, mastery over birth, mastery over primordial wisdom, mastery over the Dharma, and mastery over mind. This is mastery over all, being by nature liberated from both mastery and non-mastery; it is having attained mastery over all lords, because of thoroughly discerning all dharmas.

oṃ sarvva baśite trāṃ trūṃ hūṃ

This, called Mastery over All, is the Perfection of Wisdom.

Among these, what are the eighteen unshared qualities of a buddha? The Tathāgata has no confusion. He has no noisiness. He has no lapse of mindfulness. He has no mind that is not in equipoise. He has no diverse perceptions. He has no equanimity that has not discerned. He has no loss of resolve. He has no loss of diligence. He has no loss of mindfulness. He has no loss of samādhi. He has no loss of wisdom. He has no loss of liberation. He has no loss of the seeing of the wisdom of liberation. His seeing of wisdom engages the past without obstruction and without attachment.

His seeing of wisdom engages the future without attachment and without obstruction. His seeing of wisdom engages the present without attachment and without obstruction. All the activities of his body are preceded by wisdom and follow after wisdom. All the activities of his speech are preceded by wisdom and follow after wisdom. All the activities of his mind are preceded by wisdom and follow after wisdom.

This is the unfinished translation of Chöjé Sakyapa. Checking it against the Indian manuscript and also in accordance with the commentary of the master Praśāntamitra, it was proofread, and only the few points that could not be left uncorrected were corrected in accordance with the commentary. Taking on the proofreading as an obligation, Butön did this.

Homage to glorious Vajrasattva.

Among these, what are the ten grounds? The ground called Very Joyful, the one called Stainless, the one called Luminous, the one called Radiant, the one called Hard to Conquer, the one called Manifest, the one called Gone Far, the one called Immovable, the one called Excellent Intelligence, and the one called Cloud of Dharma. All the grounds are one in meaning; that is, they are the meaning of primordial wisdom. And primordial wisdom, in the ultimate sense, is whatever is meant by the words: nothing whatsoever, without affliction, unchanging, unfailing, pure from the very beginning, stainless, luminous, and inexpressible.

oṃ bhūmi biśodhani sarbārtha paripūraṇi baṃ bhruṃ

This, called the Purification of the Grounds, is the Perfection of Wisdom.

What are the ten stages of the bodhisattvas? The bodhisattva who has first generated the mind, the one who is like a child, the beginner, the practitioner of yoga, the one who brings forth births, the one perfect in application, the one perfect in resolve, the irreversible one, the one who has obtained consecration, and the one bound to a single further birth. The stages of the bodhisattvas are, in the ultimate sense, free of any stage; because there is but a single stage, that is to say, there is no stage.

oṃ sarvva byabasthāna bigate hūṃ

This, called Without Stages, is the Perfection of Wisdom.

What are the ten masteries of the bodhisattvas? Mastery over life span, mastery over karma, mastery over requisites, mastery over inclination, mastery over aspiration, mastery over miraculous power, mastery over birth, mastery over the powers, mastery over the Dharma, and mastery over mind. All these masteries are, in the ultimate sense, without mastery, isolated in their intrinsic nature; conventionally they are the attainment of mastery over all lords, because of thoroughly discerning dharmas.

oṃ sarbā baśite trāṃ bhrūṃ hūṃ

This, called All Masteries, is the Perfection of Wisdom.

Among these, what are the eighteen unshared qualities of a buddha? The Tathāgata has no confusion, no noisiness, no lapse of mindfulness, no mind that is not in equipoise, no perception of diversity, no equanimity that has not discerned, no loss of resolve, no loss of diligence, no loss of mindfulness, no loss of samādhi, no loss of wisdom, no loss of liberation, and no loss of the seeing of the wisdom of liberation; his seeing of wisdom engages the past without attachment and without obstruction; his seeing of wisdom engages the future without attachment and without obstruction; his seeing of wisdom engages the present without attachment and without obstruction; all the activities of his body are preceded by wisdom and follow after wisdom; all the activities of his speech are preceded by wisdom and follow after wisdom; and all the activities of his mind are preceded by wisdom and follow after wisdom. All the tathāgatas abide perfectly in equality in all domains, at all times, on all occasions; because they truly realize emptiness, this is completely pure wisdom.

oṃ aveṇika buddha dharmma samte āḥ bira hūṃ khaṃ

This, called the Equality of the Unshared, is the Perfection of Wisdom.

Among these, what are the thirty-two marks of a great being? The palms and soles are marked with wheels: on his palms and soles are thousand-spoked wheels with hubs and rims, complete in every aspect. The palms and soles are well set: his whole sole touches the ground, and where the ground is high it becomes low and where it is low it becomes high. The fingers and toes are joined by a web like those of the king of geese: the hands and feet of the tathāgatas are beautiful with garlands of golden-colored lines, like an aṣṭāpada board. The hands and feet are soft and tender: the hands and feet of this Blessed One are like soft cotton, tender like those of an infant one month old, soft like kācalindika cloth and pleasant to the touch. Seven parts are prominent: the two hands, the two feet, the two shoulders, and the nape of the neck. The fingers are long: his toes become progressively long. The heels are broad: his heels are large and broad in proportion to his feet. The body is large and straight: not crooked, not stunted, not deficient, not coarse, well formed, a body possessing all seven aspects; his breadth is in proportion to his height. The ankles are inconspicuous: his knees and ankle bones are raised. The tips of the body hairs point upward: the tips of his body hairs point upward, and his body hairs curl to the right, coiled, the color of collyrium, and they make his golden-colored body shine forth. The calves are like those of the eṇeya antelope: the Blessed One's calves are rounded, like those of the eṇeya antelope. The arms are long and beautiful: standing upright and without bending, he touches his own kneecaps with his palms.

The private parts are withdrawn into a sheath: they are fully covered by a sheath and become like those of an elephant. The body is the color of gold: his complexion becomes like gold. The skin is fine: he has very soft skin, soft like gold leaf, and he wears garments free of dust. The hairs grow one by one: in each pore of his body there is a single hair. Between the eyebrows there is the ūrṇā hair: between his eyebrows there is a hair like fine cotton thread, the color of broken conch, curling to the right, and this very ūrṇā makes his face, which is like the color of gold, exceedingly bright. The upper body is like a lion's: his upper body becomes broad like that of a lion. The shoulders are rounded: he has a perfectly rounded neck. The shoulders are full: his shoulders are well filled out, and his neck is radiant like gold leaf. He possesses the supreme sense of taste: since he experiences tastes in each of his limbs, he possesses the supreme of tastes; these are the channels, and the supreme among them are perfectly clear, because they are not disturbed by wind, bile, phlegm, and the rest. His proportions are like those of the nyagrodha tree: the height and the breadth of the great being are equal. The head has an uṣṇīṣa: his head is rounded and covered above by an uṣṇīṣa like a parasol, and the uṣṇīṣa is very high. The tongue is long: he touches as far as the rims of his ears with the tip of his tongue, and with his tongue he covers his forehead up to the hairline. He has the voice of Brahmā: in an assembly of many thousands his voice is heard outside just as it is within, and it is as sweet as that of the kalaviṅka bird. The jaws are like a lion's: his jaws are rounded like those of a lion. The teeth are white: his teeth have the color of conch, of kunda flowers, and of the moon. The teeth are even: his teeth have tips that are neither too low, nor too high, nor uneven. The teeth are well set: his teeth have no gaps. He has forty teeth: twenty teeth below and twenty above. The eyes are deep blue: the blue of his eyes is perfectly pure, because it is unmixed with red lines, and the white is perfectly pure, white alone. The eyelashes are like those of a cow: like a bull's, his lower eyelashes stay below and his upper ones stay above, untangled and well aligned.

Some beings see the Tathāgata as golden in color, some see him as the color of crystal, some as the color of ruby, some as the color of sapphire, some as the color of red pearls, some as white like conch, kunda flowers, and the moon, some as of many colors, and some as the color of the sky. Some see him as twelve and a half cubits in height; some as a hundred thousand leagues in height, or as ten million leagues in height; some as having a maṇḍala of light that illuminates the boundless clouds of the oceans of world-systems in their vast expanse; some as filling the great trichiliocosm with light; and some as illuminating the oceans of arrays of the Ornament of the Essence of the Flower Ground, all the way to the limits of the dharmadhātu. In accordance with those to be trained, he illuminates all the domains of all the buddhas and makes all world-systems understand by his voice; illuminating with light and voice the dichiliocosm, the trichiliocosm, and everything as far as the limits of space, he makes them understand. Through the four means of attracting disciples, namely Dharma, material things, consistency of aims, and acting for others' aims, he benefits beings, and having benefited them, he establishes them in great awakening.

Through the equality of the thirty-two marks of a great being, all dharmas are equal, utterly equal. The marks of the tathāgatas are to be regarded as accomplished, but this is not the accomplishment of signs. Because awakening is without characteristics, all dharmas are without characteristics; that is, they have the characteristic of emptiness. All the tathāgatas, too, are equal in emptiness. All the tathāgatas, awakening, and all dharmas have a single characteristic; that is, they have the characteristic of space, the characteristic of emptiness, the characteristic of peace.

oṃ sarvva tathāgata bar lakṣaṇa samtā dharmmate oṃ

This, called the Equality of All the Marks, is the Perfection of Wisdom.

What are the eighty minor marks? The buddhas, the blessed ones, have nails the color of copper, glossy nails, raised nails, and rounded nails; fingers that taper gradually, and full fingers; veins that do not show, and veins without knots; ankles that do not show; feet that are even, without unevenness. They walk with the gait of an elephant, walk with the gait of a lion, walk turning to the right, walk straight, walk beautifully, and walk with measured steps. They have kneecaps that are large, broad, and well formed; private parts that are fully formed; even steps; a body that is pure, a body that is soft, a body that is clear, a body that does not droop, and a body that is not stooped. Their conduct is pure in every way; like a lordly bull they are serene on every side; their gaze is completely pure, and their radiance is even and lovely to behold. Their waist is rounded, well proportioned, and not misshapen, and the waist of their body is like the grip of a bow. Their navel is deep, steady, coiling to the right, and unobstructed. Their limbs and lesser limbs are distinctly separated; their skin is free of flaws, without dark spots or moles; their body is firm. Their hands are like cotton; their hands are broad; the lines of their hands are glossy, and the lines of their hands are not crooked. Their lips are like the bimba fruit; their face is not too long; their tongue is soft, thin, and red. They have a voice like the trumpeting of an elephant, a voice pleasant and sweet to hear, perfect sound, and a gentle voice. Their nose is prominent. Their eyeteeth are rounded, white, and tapering. Their eyes are pure, their eyes are wide, and their eyes are deep blue. Their eyelashes are black; their eyebrows are thick and soft; the whites and blacks of their eyes are distinct, like the petals of a lotus. The line of their brows is long; the hairs of their eyebrows are black and fine, and their eyebrows are well matched. Their complexion is unfaded; their ears are not uneven, and their ears are free of flaws. All their faculties are unimpaired and free of flaws. Their body is completely perfect, and their face is lofty and placed in the highest position. They have a fine forehead, and their head is very full. The hair of their head is black, thick, soft, fragrant, even, glossy, not crinkled, and not tangled. They are adorned with the śrīvatsa, the auspicious sign, and the swirling svastika, and a victory banner is well established on them. Their forehead is like a conch; their head is supremely high; their crown protuberance (uṣṇīṣa) rises upward; their head has the shape of a parasol; and they are beautiful in every way.

The tathāgatas, possessing immeasurable minor marks, are well established in equality. They are well established in the equality of what manifests and what does not manifest. They are well established in the equality in which all dharmas are without manifestation. They are well established in all the major and minor marks having the nature of illusion. They are the equality of the minor marks of the three times; they have attained vajra-like equality; they have attained the equality of all the dharmas of emptiness and of the minor marks; and they have entered well and truly into the meaning of nonduality.

oṃ anubyañjana samatā supratiṣṭhita bajrajñāna hūṃ laṃ aṃ

This, called the Equality of the Minor Marks, is the Perfection of Wisdom.

Then all the blessed tathāgatas gathered together and gladdened the Blessed One, the great Lord of Vajra Yoga, with these words of "Excellent!":

oṃ For the sake of the mind of awakening, excellent are you,
Supreme embodiment of all the buddhas!
For the sake of the Dharma of the buddhas, excellent are you!
Vajra wisdom that masters concentration, excellent are you!
Vajrasattva, excellent are you!
For the sake of the immeasurable, excellent are you!
And for the sake of sublime wisdom
You have taught this sūtra well,

Supreme of the supreme Dharma bodies,
To the very meaning of the primordial mantra,
To vajra wisdom of great power,

namastute oṃ māṃ hūṃ hūṃ namostute svāhā

By merely reciting this,

Next, from this, I shall truly explain
The unsurpassed maṇḍala of awakening,
The vajra wisdom, the supreme secret
Of all the buddhas, the protectors.

On secluded ground that is delightful,
Adorned with garlands of flowers,
Fine in every part and varied throughout,
Within the all-various vow,
The all-various rites
Should be accomplished for the ultimate aim.

On the best of mountains, or in a city
Adorned with flowers and fruits,
The mantrin who dwells there, if he desires
The supreme accomplishment, should draw it correctly.

In temples and in hermitages,
And on mountaintops everywhere,
The rites of all the buddhas
Should be accomplished for the ultimate aim.

In the abodes of the mātṛkās, in places of peace,
In charnel grounds and in the abodes of the gods,
The rites of the glorious Vajradhara
Should be accomplished in the ultimate sense.

In all the lotus pleasure groves
And at the meeting places of rivers,
The rites of the glorious Padmapāṇi
Should be accomplished for the ultimate aim.

In delightful royal palaces
And in the gardens of kings,
The jewel rites of Vajraratna
Should be accomplished.

At a solitary liṅga, in cattle pens,
And at the meeting places of streams,
All the various rites
Should be accomplished for the ultimate aim.

Glorious Vajrasattva and the deities
Are present in every supreme place.
In the sky or in any other direction,
Wherever one rests in meditative equipoise,
There, constantly, with supreme merit,
All the rites should be accomplished.

The maṇḍala, abiding in the equality of all directions,
Should be laid out with lines by the wise one:
In the directions east, south, west, and north,
The eight lines of the maṇḍala.
Outside it is a wheel
Of five colors, blazing greatly,
Square, with four gates,
Beautified by four porticoes,
Adorned with various cloths, garlands of flowers,
Yak-tail whisks, and so forth.
At the gates, the door frames, and the corners
Throughout the whole maṇḍala,
It is ornamented with vajras and jewels.
In this way all the maṇḍalas should be drawn.

In the center of that maṇḍala,
If one desires the supreme accomplishment,
One should draw the image of the Buddha
Seated upon a moon disk.
In the east, the Blessed One who holds the wheel,
Together with Vajrasattva;
All the wheel-turning monarchs
Should be drawn, together with Lāsyā.
In the south, White Uṣṇīṣa,
Endowed with a blazing jewel;
At the side of White Uṣṇīṣa
Draw Vajrateja and the others.
In the north, the one of great diligence,
Tejorāśi, the mass of splendor, of great strength,
Together with Viśvavajra and the others,
Should be drawn in full sequence.
The one who possesses vajra yoga,
To the west of the lord protector,
Should draw Jayoṣṇīṣa, of great diligence,
Together with Vajradharma and the others;
The mantrin should draw them all,
If he desires the supreme accomplishment.
In the quarter of Īśāna stands Udgata;
In the corner of Fire, Mahodgata;
In that of Nairṛti, Vijayā herself;
In that of Wind, likewise, Vikiraṇa.
Vajrasattva, the great being,
In front of all the beings,
Is seated at the center of a moon disk,
Clear and blazing, with massed rays of light.

In the maṇḍalas the wise one
Should set out the offerings:
The great offerings of Lāsyā and the others,
Likewise form, sound, and the rest,
And incense and the rest, the great secret.
The guardians of the gates are four;
In the places of the outer maṇḍala
Draw Samantabhadra and the others.
Having drawn the maṇḍala in this way,
And having made offerings as before,

From directly seeing the maṇḍala
Of the Lord of Vajra Yoga and the others,

One should afterward draw it in the same way.
According to one's merit, the maṇḍalas,
All well examined by their emblems,
Are all to be drawn
In the form of one's own special deity.
Through the yoga of being one's own deity,
All the maṇḍalas are thoroughly accomplished.
Even with a rite deficient or excessive,
Accomplishment will come; it is no fault.

Of excellent fortune, all-various throughout,
Possessing the all-various wheel,
The wheel-turning monarch, great in nature,
The great offering of the seven precious things:
Like the full moon,
Know the maṇḍala to be the source of nectar.

This maṇḍala of the Buddha,
Of Vajradhara and the others, has been explained.
That of the great Padmapāṇi
Know always to be lotus-shaped.
The Jewel's are jewels;
The maṇḍala has various forms.
One who holds the maṇḍala lowly and abandons the pledges,
If he does not know that very secret,
Will not succeed
In the means of accomplishment.
Therefore, with every effort,
Beholding all the maṇḍalas,
If one desires the supreme accomplishment,
Should always be done without fail.

One should afterward practice in stages.
All the substances there are,
Whether fixed or moving,
Roots, flowers, fruits, and so on,
A well-adorned maiden,
And a beloved wife, one should offer,
And then buy them back again at a price.
A village, and likewise a temple,
A park and the like, a supreme kingdom:
Whatever one has to give, however little,
That which sustains one, one should give.
Giving up one's own possessions completely

Having offered the fee to the guru,
One should then perform the consecration,
The sublime and marvelous secret.
Placing the disciple
On a lovely eight-petaled lotus,
His limbs all bound
With all the mudrās,
One should consecrate him with vases
Filled with roots of the nature of all the elements,
And with seeds and fruits.

On the crown and on the forehead,
On the sides and likewise on the back,
All the great precious buddhas,
The supreme, unchanging diadem,
The glorious Buddha crest jewel
Set above the white parasol.
With the consecration of all kingship
One should consecrate again and again.
This is the consecration explained
As the one called Exalted by All Glory.

Of the nature of all the elements, an elephant,
Or a horse supremely blazing,
Or the consecration seated on a lion throne,
While reciting the mantra of glory:
The consecration of the ultimate aim
Called Exalted by Glory.
Having drawn either the auspicious sign

By all the buddha heroes

For the sake of consecration in every respect,

Whatever maṇḍala one has drawn,
Whatever mudrā has been set out,

These are declared the sublime mudrās.
For the sake of consecration, the buddhas,

Sandalwood and storax,
Camphor and likewise saffron,
Aloewood and deodar,

Girikarṇa and brahmadatta:

Brings full entry into all wisdom.
The most sublime of sublime jewels,
Gold and the rest, from every source,
All medicines and all seeds,
All the excellent grains,
Through the yoga of the buddhas' blessing,
Should be carefully prepared for the consecration.
The great yoga of all the buddhas
Is the sublime consecration, blazing with light:
Through all the buddhas, one becomes a buddha.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The long series of epithets beginning "the awakening mind, the unfailing one, the one who gathers all in" appears to allude in sequence to the sixteen vajra bodhisattvas of the Vajradhātu maṇḍala (Vajrasattva, Vajrarāja, Vajrarāga, Vajrasādhu, Vajraratna, Vajrateja, Vajraketu, Vajrahāsa, Vajradharma, Vajratīkṣṇa, Vajrahetu, Vajrabhāṣa, Vajrakarma, Vajrarakṣa, Vajrayakṣa, Vajrasandhi), followed by further epithets. They are translated as descriptive epithets. "Great king, radiant one, banner" is written without separators in the Tibetan and could also be read as a single epithet. The closing sentence is ambiguous: it may instead mean that all the tathāgatas, having consecrated the Blessed One as the Great Lord of Vajra Yoga, abided throughout the directions and intermediate directions.  The world system "arrayed with the ornament of the essence of the flower ground" corresponds to the Kusumatalagarbhavyūhālaṃkāra world system familiar from the Avataṃsaka; it is rendered descriptively. The word rendered "impediments" (chod pa) is unclear as written and is translated tentatively. The litany of purified states closely parallels the opening of the Adhyardhaśatikā Prajñāpāramitā, but the items are given as they stand in this text.  The phrase rendered "having taken up the power of taming" is terse in the Tibetan; the point, as in the parallel Adhyardhaśatikā, is that one disciplined by this teaching does not fall to the lower realms even after such an act.  The third verse is terse; "one tames oneself" renders rang 'dul byed, which might also mean that the bodhisattva tames them naturally, of his own accord, throughout existence.  The text reads ཕྱ་ན་རྡོ་རྗེ, which makes no sense; it has been read as ཕྱག་ན་རྡོ་རྗེ, Vajrapāṇi. The line rendered "there comes the maṇḍala of nectar" (དཀྱིལ་འཁོར་བདུད་རྩི་ཡི་ནི་འོང་) is terse and its construction uncertain. The five uṣṇīṣa deities are given their standard Sanskrit names; the Tibetan list (White Umbrella, Uṣṇīṣa of the Victor, Mass of Splendor, Radiating, Victorious) corresponds to Sitātapatra, Jayoṣṇīṣa, Tejorāśi, Vikiraṇa and Vijaya. The Wheel Bearer (Cakrin) at the center is presumably Vairocana.  "Not Rūpavajrā and the rest" renders གཟུགས་ཀྱི་རྡོ་རྗེ་ལ་སོགས་མིན literally; the negative may be a copyist's slip for a form of "draw" or "and", in which case the line would simply list Rūpavajrā and the rest. Several deity names in this passage could not be identified with confidence and are translated by meaning: the Seven Glories, the Seven Arrows, the Reed, the Arrow-Goer, Beautiful as the Moon, Vajra Spearhead, Vajra Pride, Star Eye, Moon Tilaka, the wrathful Particle, the Terrible-Eyed, and the Vajra That Strikes with the Foot. "Within the Utmost Joy" renders རབ་དགའི་ནང་དུ as written; its reference is unclear and it may be a scribal error.  In "having visualized oneself as the great mudrā of all the tathāgatas," the Tibetan has the instrumental ཀྱིས after "all the tathāgatas," which would make them the agents; the context of the master's rite suggests the genitive, and it has been translated so. The word introducing the disciple's answer is written ད་ནས and has been read as དེ་ནས, "then."  The location written རྗེ་ངར, the last in the list of body points, is unclear; it has been taken as "shins" (cf. རྐང་ངར), but it may refer to the genitals. The seed syllables are placed exactly where they stand in the Tibetan, after each statement of the vajra stations.  The explanation for the letter ṣa, ཚ་སྒོམ་དམིགས་པ, is obscure; it has been rendered literally as "focuses on the meditation of heat," but the reading may be corrupt.
