The Means of Accomplishing the Five Ḍākas of the Ḍākinī Vajra Tent (Ḍākinīvajrapañjarapañcaḍākasādhana)

The Means of Accomplishing the Five Ḍākas of the Ḍākinī Vajra Tent, known in Sanskrit as the Ḍākinīvajrapañjarapañcaḍākasādhana and in Tibetan as Khandroma Dorjé Gur gyi Khandro Nampa Ngé Drubpé Tap, is a tantric sādhana, a ritual manual for meditative practice, connected with the Ḍākinī Vajra Tent tantra of the Hevajra cycle. Written largely in verse, it guides the practitioner through the visualization of a maṇḍala of five ḍākas with their consorts and attendant goddesses, the rites of summoning, empowerment, offering and praise, the signs of accomplishment and mantra recitation, the symbolism of the deity's body and of the aggregates, elements and senses as goddesses, and a closing torma offering to the gods, nāgas and other beings of the world. Its colophon names Durjayacandra as its author and records that it was translated into Tibetan by the Indian preceptor Līlāvajra and the Tibetan translator Sönam Gyaltsen. It is Toh 1321 in the Tengyur, where it belongs among the commentaries and practice texts of the tantra section.

In the language of India: ḍākinī vajra pañjara pañca ḍāga sādharna nāma. In the language of Tibet: The Means of Accomplishing the Five Ḍākas of the Ḍākinī Vajra Tent.

Homage to glorious Vajrasattva.

Saṃsāra, which is like the mass of branches of a great tree, is these afflictions with aversion foremost; he has conquered the births of the six classes of beings.
He who abides in a diligence hard to match, bearing a fivefold body,
The Foe of Time, Vajrasattva, the Blessed One: to him I always bow.

Not cutting off the wishes of beings without exception,
And leading them with certainty into awakening,
The wheels of the five I set down in proper order.

And as the cause for them to be endowed with merit:
In a place where water and fruit are easily found, in caves,
Or wherever is suitable, dwelling on a soft seat,
The mantra practitioner meditates on the Victor for the sake of others.

What the mind should contemplate is this:
These, hūṃ oṃ traṃ ya khaṃ,
One imagines at the heart, upon the appropriate seats.

Worship of the guru and the vajra,
And one should perfect the sevenfold practice:
Confession of wrongdoing,
And restraint from doing it again;
Rejoicing in the merits
Of the buddhas and the bodhisattvas;
One should dedicate them completely;
One relies truly on the Three Jewels;
One generates the mind of awakening,
And relies upon the path. Then,
Having meditated on the four abodes of Brahmā,
One should meditate on the manifold as empty.

When things are analyzed as true or false,
Under examination they are not to be found;
Therefore, in this way, like reflections,
One should meditate on all things.

In front, from ra pha, one should visualize a sun.
On that sun arises hūṃ, a crossed vajra; and by that very vajra come the fence and the binding of the tent.

In the center of the element of space,
One should visualize a crossed vajra.
There, arising from the syllable hūṃ,
One should imagine the protective wheel,
Spinning, yet utterly unmoving,
Like the fire at the time of destruction.

From the application of ten syllables hūṃ,
One sets out the ten wrathful ones in order.
In the east one imagines Yamāntaka;
And Prajñāntaka in the south;
Padmāntaka in the quarter of the Lord of Waters, Varuṇa;
In the north, Amṛtakuṇḍalin;
Ṭakkirāja in the quarter of Īśāna;
In Agni's quarter, Nīladaṇḍa;
In Nairṛti's quarter one imagines Acala;
In Vāyu's quarter, Mahābala;
Above, upon the crown, one should imagine Uṣṇīṣa;
Below, one visualizes Sumbha.

All of them have one face and two arms,
Vajra wrathful ones, greatly fierce,
With a fist at the heart and threatening forefinger,
The right hand brandishing its own weapon:
For the crusher of Yama, the mallet;
The staff for Vajra Aparājita;
For Hayagrīva, the red lotus;
For Amṛtakuṇḍalin, the vajra;
For Ṭakkirāja, the hook;
The blue staff for the one named after it;
For Mahābala, the triśūla;
For Acala, the vajra;
For the Uṣṇīṣa Cakravartin
One should imagine the wheel of Dharma;
For the one below, the vajra pestle.
They stand with the left leg extended.

Of a color like the indranīla sapphire
One should visualize Yamāntaka.
The one called Aparājita
Is like the rays of the moon.
The lotus wrathful one is deep red;
Amṛtakuṇḍalin is black;
The wrathful ones of the intermediate quarters are deep black.
On their crowns they bear Vajradhara:
For Yamāntaka, Vairocana himself;
For the staff-bearer, Vajrasattva;
For the one called Padma, the Eye of the World;
For Amṛtakuṇḍalin, Sattva.

In the center of the wheel of wrathful ones
One should imagine the Victor over Māra.
Having imagined a lotus below,
One should visualize the ten wrathful ones.
By their going forth for a hundred leagues
One should imagine the obstructors driven away.

In the center of the element of space
One should visualize the wind maṇḍala.
In the center of the wind maṇḍala
One should visualize the fire maṇḍala.
In the center of the fire maṇḍala
The one who keeps the observances should imagine water.
The maṇḍala of Mahendra itself
One should imagine upon that.
Upon the Mahendra maṇḍala,
Which is to be imagined as Mahendra himself,
The syllables buṃ, the five kinds of buddha,
One should imagine in five places:
Vajrin, and the Eternal One;
Third, the Vajra Sun;
Fourth, the one of Vajra Desire;
Fifth, the king Paramāśva.

The buddha maṇḍala is round;
Vajrin's is like a half moon;
The Dharma Vajra's resembles a lotus;
The Sun's resembles a wish-fulfilling jewel;
The one called glorious Paramāśva:
His is triangular all around.

Vajrin's measures three cubits;
The buddha maṇḍala, four cubits;
The Illuminator's, five cubits;
Padmanarteśvara's, six;
The maṇḍala of the king Paramāśva
Has the measure of seven cubits.

By one eighth part
The door of the wheel is well known.
The rim and the band of the deities
Should be measured at the measure of the door.
By a portion half the door
Are the sides and the jambs.
The offering band is made likewise.
For the net and half-net band as well,
Discarding two parts from the Brahmā line,
One makes it in six parts.
Discarding likewise just the measure of the door,
It is well determined in nine parts.

The maṇḍala of the Buddha Protector
Is taught to be in the east of the east;
In the south, that of the Buddha Sun;
In the west, Vajra Desire;
In the north, the King of Horses;
Vajrasattva is in the center.

In the east it is a great white;
In the south it is like gold;
In the west it should be known as red;
To its north, green.

Square, with four doors,
Adorned with four porticoes;
In the corner portions of them all,
At the doors, the door rims and the corners,
It is adorned with vajra jewels.

In the center is a white and red lotus;
Where the vowels and consonants are joined in union
Is the seat of Vajrasattva.
Having looked well at one's own seed,
One should imagine it in the form of a wheel.
The qualities of all the buddhas,
In the form of the vowels, are placed there.
As the limbs of the goddess's form,
One sees the moon in a triangle.
In the form of stars,
One sets out kā and the rest.
And in the center of the moon maṇḍala
Is a second moon, of awakening.
One should visualize a vajra arisen from hūṃ,
With garlands of white light rays.

The five emblems are distinguished for the five:
For Vajrasattva, the indestructible vajra;
For Vairocana, the wheel of Dharma;
For the Jewel King, the jewel;
For the Excellent Lotus, the red lotus;
For Amoghasiddhi, the sword of wisdom,
Whose nature is sharp wisdom.

He arises perfectly in the form of Sattva;
One should visualize him as lord of the maṇḍala.
The mudrā of the one with ḍākinīs and the rest,
The light of the moon, the light of jewels,
Embraced perfectly by Māmakī and the rest:
He is the very center of the five maṇḍalas.
The one whose name is the way of great passion
Is absorbed in the equipoise of samādhi.

Entering into one's own body,

The buddha wheel melts, each in turn.
One should imagine the eight seeds,

All those vajra ḍākinīs

In the quarters of Akṣobhya's maṇḍala,
Gaurī has the hook and the threatening forefinger;
Caurī holds the vajra noose;
Vetālī, the vajra chain;
Ghasmarī holds the vajra bell.
They are white, yellow, red and green.

Of the goddesses Pukkasī and the rest,
The signs and the colors are to be told:
The vase of bodhicitta, Mount Meru,
The hearth, and the great banner;
Black, white, and like the sky.
The wrathful one is in Akṣobhya's maṇḍala.

Flower is white; Incense,

Song is yellow, Dance green,
Laughter red; Flute

Drum Holder green; Clay Drum

The syllable ho, red, with light rays;
In the middle of the two, three letters.
With fragrance, or else, they enter into union.
As they melt, he is recalled to mind.
And the goddesses of the intermediate quarters
Urge on the chief with this song:

"You, protector of the vajra of aversion, pure in mind,

Look swiftly upon me, Pukkasī, and leave emptiness behind!

"You, protector of the vajra of delusion, pure in body,

O Guide, take birth for the sake of the world!

"You, the form body that protects all the buddhas,
Who adorn yourself with accomplished activity and the sameness of all:

The goddesses of the intermediate quarters,
Flower and the rest, dwelling in the places of Pukkasī and the others,

In the same way the other principal ones too
Are urged; and being awakened,
By the power of recalling
Their former aspirations,
One imagines them arising from hūṃ maṃ.

The Mind Ḍāka of great strength
Has eight faces and four legs,
And is adorned with sixteen arms.
Terrifying, like blue sapphire,
His forms are both graceful and wrathful;
He stands in the dance of the half cross-legged posture,
Adorned with ornaments of bone.
The first face is black;
The two are white and red;
The middle one at the crown bares its fangs;
The remaining faces are like bees.
He tramples the four māras together.
On his crown he holds a black vajra.

Elephant, horse, donkey, ox, camel,
Man, and likewise lion and cat:
In the eight right skull cups
Know them in order, beginning with the twice-drinker.
Earth, water, fire and wind,
The sun and the moon,
Antaka and Dhanada:
These are in the eight left skull cups.

With curved knife and skull cup as his emblems,
He is joined in nonduality with Māmakī.

From aṃ laṃ the one behind arises, white,
His two faces black and red.
He is seated upon a moon seat,
Embraced at the neck by Buddhalocanā.
Since the four legs and the rest are shared,
The eight faces and the rest are not described.

Hand emblems are stated:
On his crown he holds the wheel of Dharma;
Fearsome, he causes fear.
Peacock, buffalo, makara,
The king of fish, tortoise,
The great nāga, goose,

Lizard, the elder, the cloud-swift,
The one like eye salve, mouse,
Yak, the fragrant one, and the sārasa crane:
In the eight left skull cups.

From traṃ taṃ comes the one named the Jewel King;
One should visualize his body as yellow in color.
His right and left faces are black
And red; on his crown he holds a jewel.
The karaṇḍa duck and the cakravāka,
The parrot and the cakora,
The speckled bird, the hormo, the olwa,

Karkoṭa and Śaṅkhapāla,
Kulika, Padma and Mahāpadma,
Takṣaka and Vāsuki,

Like burnished gold, utterly refined,
He is embraced at the neck by Tārā.

From aṃ paṃ arises, red,
Hayagrīva, whom one should visualize.
His right face is black;
His left is like stainless moonlight.
On his crown he holds a red lotus.
He is joined in nonduality with Pāṇḍaravāsinī.
Bear and vulture, and likewise rhinoceros,
Wild ox and tiger,
Monkey, makara and jackal:
In the right hands of Vajradharma.

Crow, owl, yellow heron,
The sārasa crane, and likewise goat,
Deer and boar: these skull cups

From taṃ khaṃ arises, green,
The horse-faced King of Horses, whom one should visualize.
On his crown he holds a white sword.
He is embraced at the neck by Tārā.
His right face is black;
His left is like stainless moonlight.
Mercury, Jupiter, Ketu,
Rāhu, Mars, the Sun,

Indra, Brahmā, and likewise Rudra,

The goddesses are said to have two arms,
Holding the curved knife and the skull cup.
In the hearts of all the vajra beings
One should set the seat of wisdom.
Through union with the wisdom being
One should fully accomplish the awakening of a buddha.
What arises from one's own mantra syllable, the emblem,
And what is designated the mudrā,
The wisdom being in the heart of the pledge being:
This is called the practice of approach.

Khecarī and Bhūcarī, the two,

At heart, speech and body

The ḍāka empowerment is renowned to be given.
Endowed with the five ḍākas,
Filled with the five pledge substances,

The four hold on their crowns a wheel,

On the crown of the ḍāka of the heart

Of these, the members of the maṇḍala,
All the maṇḍalas in their vajra sequence:
The Heart, the Permanent, the Jewel,

In the skull cup, stirring and blazing,
Arising from the five seed syllables of the hook,
On the tongue a vajra the size of a barley grain:
One tastes the vajra nectar.
oṃ sarbba tathāgata amṛta svada vajra svabhāva ātmako'haṃ

From wisdom, offerings of many kinds,
The eight, are sent forth,
And the goddesses make offering to the wheel,
To the lord who is the very nature of the wheel.
oṃ sarbba tathāgata pūja vajra svabhāva ātmako'haṃ

You are encircled by the yoginīs;
You by your own mind have fully attained the supreme;
You bring about all beings, the moving and the unmoving,
And with a heart of compassion you awaken sentient beings.
Accomplisher of aims through the display of illusion,
Glorious Heruka, whose mind is joyful.
oṃ sarbba tathāgata stuti vajra svabhāva ātmako'haṃ

The celestial palace is the buddha Vairocana;
The empowerment is the tathāgata Akṣobhya;
Tasting the nectar is Amitābha;
The praise is glorious Ratnasambhava;
The offering is said to be Amoghasiddhi;
Passionate attachment is Vajrasattva.
The yogin who meditates on these six limbs
Fully accomplishes self-blessing.

Before accomplishment, the signs
Should be recognized as they appear in sequence.
The first appears like clouds;
The second is like smoke;
The third has the form of fireflies;
The fourth is a bright lamp;
The fifth, a constant radiance,
Is like a cloudless sky.
After that comes the form of illusion,
The form of a dream, momentary.

The moon that has gone within the heart:
Upon the disc of the moon, the indestructible,
Surrounded by many-colored rays of light;
From that one should visualize a vajra.
From the vajra, visualizing
The selfless ḍākinī, one rouses her by the rite.
At the tip of the ḍākinī's nose
One visualizes the form of a mustard seed,
And all beings within the mustard seed,
The three realms, the moving and the unmoving:
Thus one meditates with the mind,
And self-blessing is fully accomplished.

On rudrākṣa, conch and rhinoceros horn,
Bodhi seeds and lotus seeds,
Pearls and the like, and coral,
One endowed with meditative concentration should recite the mantras.

When the yogin grows weary of meditative concentration,
Then he should recollect the purification.
Through compassion the eyes are red.
The purification of the eyes is the three vajras.
The faces are the eight liberations.
The arms are the sixteen emptinesses.
By the four means of attraction
The four legs are spoken of.
By the seals are the five buddhas;
He is wrathful, taming the vicious.
The four kinds of truth
Are what have wholly become the bones.

The aggregate of form becomes Vajrā;
Feeling is the secret one, Guhyagaurī;
Perception likewise is Vārī;
Formations are Vajraḍākinī;
Consciousness itself is Nairātmyā.
Ultimately the aggregates do not exist.
As for what become the six sense sources:
Dveṣavajrā is Vajrā;
Vārī is the vajra of taste;
Īrṣyāvajrā is Mohavajrā;
Vajrā is Vajraḍākinī;
Mātsaryavajrā is Guhyagaurī;
On the mind is the yoginī Nairātmyā.
Earth is said to be Pukkasī;
The water element is Śavarī;
Fire should be known as Caṇḍālī;
Wind is renowned as Ḍombī.
Form is always taught to be Gaurī;
Sound is renowned as Caurī;
For the realm of smell, Vetālī;
Taste has the form of Ghasmarī;
Touch is said to be Bhūcarī;
And the element of phenomena is Khecarī.
