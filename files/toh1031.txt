Dhāraṇī for Obtaining Garments

The Dhāraṇī for Obtaining a Hundred Garments is a very brief ritual text from the Kangyur's collection of dhāraṇīs. It opens with homage to the Three Jewels and to the fierce Vajrapāṇi, called here the great general of the yakṣas. It then gives a short mantra-like incantation and explains how to use it: one recites it a thousand times over a single set of garments and then gives the garments to a noble person, a teacher, or anyone else, and within half a month one will receive a hundred sets in return. The same practice is said to bring wealth, food, and livestock, so the text belongs to the class of practical dhāraṇīs that promise worldly prosperity through generosity. It is Toh 1031 in the Kangyur, and its original Sanskrit and Tibetan titles are not stated in the opening lines given here.

Homage to the Three Jewels.
Homage to the great yakṣa general, the fierce Vajrapāṇi.

iri miri dhadaṃ me bahu phalaṃ bhavatu svāhā

Recite this a thousand times over a single set of garments. If one then gives it to a noble person, to a teacher, or to anyone else at all, within half a month one will obtain a hundred sets of garments. In the same way, wealth, food, or livestock of any kind will be obtained just like this.

The Dhāraṇī for Obtaining a Hundred Garments is complete.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The final syllable of the colophon is an irregular spelling of the usual concluding particle and has been read simply as "is complete." The term rendered "livestock" literally means "those that go on feet."
