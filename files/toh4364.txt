The Catalog of All the Dharma Translations at the Palace of Tongtang Lhenkar

The Catalog of All the Dharma Translations at the Palace of Tongtang Lhenkar, known in Tibetan as the Denkarma, is an early imperial Tibetan register of the Buddhist scriptures and treatises that had been translated into Tibetan by the time it was compiled. It is Toh 4364 in the Tengyur, where it stands among the miscellaneous works rather than among the Indian treatises, since it is a Tibetan composition with no Sanskrit original. Drawn up in a Dragon year at the palace of Lhenkar by the translator Paltsek, the monk Lui Wangpo and their colleagues, it lists the titles of the sūtras of the Great and Lesser Vehicles, dhāraṇīs, praises, prayers, Indian commentaries on philosophy and logic, and even works attributed to the emperor Trisong Detsen, recording the length of each in ślokas and sections. It also notes which works had not yet been finally edited or fully translated, which makes it a valuable witness to how the Tibetan canon took shape during the imperial period.

Here is contained the catalog of all the Dharma translations at the palace of Tongtang Lhenkar.

Homage to the Omniscient One.

From the council of the Custodian of the Tradition of the Blessed One (the Buddha) came the command: "Write a catalog of the titles of all the translations of the sacred Dharma in the kingdom of Tibet, beginning with the Perfection of Wisdom in One Hundred Thousand Lines." In the autumn of the Dragon year, when the court was in residence at the palace of Lhenkar, the translator monk Paltsek, the monk Lui Wangpo, and others determined the titles of all the Dharma translations in the land of Tibet, such as the sūtras of the sacred Dharma of the Great and Lesser Vehicles, the major and minor dhāraṇīs, the hundred and eight names, praises, aspirational prayers, benedictions, the Vinaya Basket, commentaries on the sūtras, treatises of the Middle Way, writings on meditative concentration, treatises on consciousness, works on logic (tarka), and so on, together with their numbers of sections and ślokas, and set them down in writing.
