The Heart of the Noble Ten Vajrapāṇis (Ārya Daśa Vajrapāṇi Hṛdaya)

Toh 754 in the Kangyur is a dhāraṇī text known in English as The Heart of the Noble Ten Vajrapāṇis, corresponding to the Sanskrit Ārya Daśa Vajrapāṇi Hṛdaya and the Tibetan Phags pa rdo rje lag pa bcu pa'i snying po. The text belongs to the class of dhāraṇī scriptures and concerns the protective power of knowing the names, lineages, and secret mantras of ten great yakṣa commanders associated with Vajrapāṇi. Cast as a discourse delivered by the Buddha to his attendant Ānanda at Śrāvastī, it promises that those who possess this knowledge will be shielded from curses, yakṣas, rākṣasas, and other harmful forces. As a dhāraṇī sūtra, it sits within the broader collection of canonical Buddhist scriptures preserved in the Kangyur.

Then that brahmin youth released a Brahmā-like voice and proclaimed these vajra words in all three divine abodes. The dhāraṇī of the subtle vajra, the unobstructed one, is complete.

In the language of India: Ārya Daśa Vajrapāṇi Hṛdaya. In the Tibetan language: The Heart of the Noble Ten Vajrapāṇis.

Homage to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas.

Thus did I hear. At one time the Blessed One was dwelling at Śrāvastī, in the Jeta Grove, the garden of Anāthapiṇḍada, in the park of Prince Jeta.

Then the Blessed One spoke to the Venerable Ānanda:

Ānanda, whatever son or daughter of good family knows the name, the lineage, or the secret mantra of the ten great yakṣa commanders Vajrapāṇi, no curse shall have power over that person. Yakṣas shall not harm them. Rākṣasas, those who devour flesh, and those who consume smoke shall not harm them. Those who cast spells from afar shall likewise not be able to harm them.

Thus it is: ukiri makiri, mīri capāśa pari, ulime, milime, ilime, milime, ilime, milime, ilima lime, hikke, dhikke, hukke, dhu dhure, pradhare, alaṃphale, phālaṃphale, khukhu meti kumetu me, khiri khiri, sādha sādha, sarva kvale, bandha ma kvale.

Ānanda, may these ten great yakṣa commanders Vajrapāṇi always follow behind me, and even when I am asleep, when I am intoxicated, or when I am greatly intoxicated, may they protect, utterly guard, utterly hold, utterly maintain, pacify, and bring me to wellbeing. May they receive the Blessed One's command to do so. They are as follows: Victor Most Excellent of Herds, Wilderness Dweller, Snow Dweller, the Precious Five, Good Jewel, Good Renown, the son of Ugly Body who makes beautiful, Beautiful Garland, and the great Dharma-protecting king, son of Vaiśravaṇa. May these ten great yakṣa commanders Vajrapāṇi always follow behind me, and even when I am overcome by sleep, when I am intoxicated, when I am greatly intoxicated, may they protect, utterly guard, utterly hold, utterly maintain, pacify, and bring me to wellbeing. May they receive the Blessed One's command to do so.

May the foundations of these mantras be accomplished. Svāhā. Thus it is: sara saraṇe svāhā.

As for the foundations of the mantras of those ten Vajrapāṇis, they are these: Thus it is: ugate, kete, saṃkate, uṃḍule, dhadhe, tate, tapattesvāsā, ci ce ci ce, ci khe lo chedani, bhedani, sarva vidyā chedani, sarva gṛta, kākhorda, para kṛta nāśani.

As for the supreme foundations of the mantras of those ten Vajrapāṇis, kings of knowledge-mantras, they are these: Thus it is: ale lakṣe va ke le lakṣe, ake, vake, lakṣe svāhā. Thus it is: hutu hutu, hutu tu, vitu vitu, vitu tu, gaccha gaccha, sapte, pataha taye svāhā. Namo bhagavato buddhyasya siddhyantu mantra mantra parā svāhā.

When the Blessed One had spoken these words, the Venerable Ānanda, together with the gods, humans, asuras, and gandharvas, rejoiced, and they acclaimed what the Blessed One had proclaimed.

The Heart of the Noble Ten Vajrapāṇis is complete.
