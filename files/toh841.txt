The Nature of All, the Five Nectars (Sarva Pañca Amṛta)

This is Toh 841 in the Kangyur, a tantra of the Nyingma-style Great Perfection (Dzogchen) tradition, commonly known in Tibetan as "The Nature of All, the Five Nectars" (Thamché Dütsi Ngé Rangshin). The Sanskrit title given at the head of the text is a transliterated and somewhat corrupt rendering, which the Tibetan colophon-style title lines gloss as "The Supreme Heart-Essence Near to the Great Accomplishment," and the text identifies itself as the eighth great volume of a larger tantric collection. It opens with a homage to glorious Samantabhadra, the primordial buddha of the Great Perfection, and sets its teaching in the supreme secret mandala of the Blessed One, where sovereign yogins and oceans of Victors gather in the abode of the Mother, the space in which nonduality abides spontaneously present. Within the canon it belongs to the tantra section of the Kangyur, the collection of translated words of the Buddha, and falls into the class of tantras concerned with the view and practice of the Great Perfection.

Most secret at its summit. Rendered into clear language.

In the language of India: Sarva Pañca Amṛta, Sāra Siddhi Mahā Duḥkha, Hṛdaya Nāyaka Paravidtvan Aṣṭa. In the language of Tibet: The Nature of All, the Five Nectars; the Supreme Heart-Essence Near to the Great Accomplishment; the Eighth Great Volume.

Homage to glorious Samantabhadra.

In the mandala of the Blessed One, the supreme secret, there dwelt the host of sovereign yogins, and the oceans of Victors together with their retinue, abiding together with the fortunate ones.

In the supreme abode, the mandala of the Mother, at the time when the nondual abides spontaneously present, at that time the community of hearers, the solitary realizers, the sūtras of the Great Vehicle, the community of bodhisattvas, and the great community of hearers were present, though within that state not even the name of an arhat existed.

From the mandala of Samantabhadra's mind, in his thought, he gave this scriptural teaching. At one time while it was being explained and expounded, at the very time it was being taught, the three existences were, from the beginning, the five nectars, unfabricated, buddhas perfect from the beginning.

From the mind-mandala of Samantabhadra, because the body, speech, and mind of the three times and the three existences are nectar from the beginning, he spoke from the expanse of certainty, drew it forth from the profound expanse and taught it.

Then, together with all the Sugatas who dwelt in company, his intention was this: having experienced the equality of wisdom in unsurpassed awakening, he arranged the vajra secret assembly and spoke this as the most sacred of all.

This is the supreme sacred word, the scriptural transmission, drawn forth with certainty. For the sake of the practice, what is to be practiced, and the fruit to be attained, this secret mantra, this secret instruction, is the sacred nectar, the supreme scriptural transmission. This certainty, gathered into its essence, is the supreme essence from which awakening arises. For the sake of view and conduct, commitment, practice, and activity, and for the sake of the certain accomplishing of what is to be accomplished, this profound, condensed scripture was spoken.

The teachings are inconceivable, and the eighty-four thousand categories too are spoken of as three gateways of vehicles. With the secret itself and the vehicle of inclusion, there are four. The intended meaning is provisional, and the ultimate too, and the meaning to be led onward is the buddha. Through the whispered lineage of teaching the mind is opened out, for the drop is pervaded by the dharmakāya. The scripture of certainty too is the buddha. Buddhas are equal and go into the expanse. The scripture of certainty is also of a single aspect. Through secret mantra, through secrecy, one is a buddha. This awakening mind is entirely pure, perfect wisdom, great bliss itself, the seal of the whole without remainder. From the beginning there is nothing to do and no need to do. The sounds of cause and effect are delusion; this is not the domain of nonconceptual purification. Into such a domain, through grasping with conceptuality, one has become an ocean of saṃsāra.

Within the great dharmakāya, the emergence from the world, when one rests in the bhaga just as it is, awakening itself has no place of downfall. The only child, without effort, rests in bliss.

The tantras, the scriptures, and the pith instructions too are like the common people, the minister, and the king: they differ in force and in reach, yet one rests upon one, within one state. Being of one nature, indivisible and of one mode, they are one, and categories do not exist at all. To realize within the indivisible state, this is what is called nectar.

Therefore the lord of body, speech, and mind, the bodies, the ornaments, and the wheels, are all made clear within the profound great scripture. The cycles of five and four within it, and saṃsāra as well, are nothing other than wisdom and buddhahood. Because all has become the expanse and abides in equality, it is buddhahood from the beginning, great bliss itself. This is what is called nectar. It has become the expanse without birth or death. Because the expanse's transformation is purity, equality, and spontaneous presence, this yogin who abides in this view is of excellent fortune. Whatever is practiced, meditated upon, or accomplished, that very nectar is the supreme medicine, the very heart-commitment of the Sugatas.

Blessings arise, vast and wondrous,
nourished by the oceans of Victors.
Buddhahood itself comes forth from great bliss.
This nectar, all of it without exception,
from abiding in the unelaborated bindu,
for the sake of limitless sentient beings,
one offers to oneself and one eats oneself.
Let the practitioner apply great diligence.

Thus spoke the Blessed One, glorious Samantabhadra, with the intention arising from the heart of great wisdom.

This is the first of the secret meanings, the great pith instruction that shows awakening mind, suchness itself, as the view, from the Scripture of the Supreme Great Nectar. It is the first of the eight great volumes from the great secret treasury within the ocean of the mind.

In the language of India: Amṛta-rasāyana, Tanajhaya, Praśasta-pramāṇa-śrīkara-nāpraśastaya, namo. In Tibetan: Homage to Brahmā and the other sages, to the gods, the nāgas, and the human bodhisattvas.

Then the supreme lord of the Brahmās, with his conch-white topknot and his chest of a lion, the great Brahmā Kunṭhogrol, the great sage Jangngon, Caraka and Navasokye, Thaskarbu and Lhakab, Gyünshespa and Yönchoglha, Thang-lagnä and Mezhin-jug, Kagnyi-chöd and Kalayan, Shingkhyer and Jangngonbu, Sun, Moon, and Nāga-ru, together with the goddess of trees, Ödchangma, the goddess of medicines, and Norgyi Gyün: all these, together with their retinues, paid homage to the god Samantabhadra. They received the empowerment of medicine and the commitments, and the sages were given permission.

Then Samantabhadra, the Sugata, declared this definitive scripture, the supreme nectar, king of medicines.

Then, through the miraculous display of compassion, the lands trembled greatly. Across the earth of the three-thousandfold world, everything became great medicine, rasāyana. Everything arose from the unborn. This ripening, too, came from the great ocean of compassion. The cloud of compassion pervaded, a great cloud wreathed in lightning, and in an instant, with its thunder, rain fell to fill the earth. That very protector of impartial compassion satisfied all without exception with the mass of waters that is nectar. With great drops from the ocean of nectar, the earth and all medicines were nourished. On the summit of Meru, on the mountain slopes, in the medicinal valleys and the forests, and throughout all the circles of willow groves, the great clouds moistened everything with a rain of nectar. All that water had a single taste. From the essence of the water there grew wish-fulfilling trees, gelpa trees, great trees, and mango trees, spreading over the whole earth. Becoming nectar, all of them flourished: trunks and great trunks, roots, stems, bark, heartwood, branches and leaves, petals, flowers, and fruits, and likewise all the varieties of grain. All were gathered and brought to ripeness. By drinking, one is satisfied and grows strong. According to their own places and abodes, their colors, tastes, scents, and potencies, each is brought forth from its own particular seed. In just this way all living beings make use of the substance of rasāyana. It becomes the sustenance of the six classes of beings. The door of nectar was thrown fully open. So spoke the Protector of the World.

Then the bodhisattva, the great being Mañjuśrī Sharp-Witted, condensed and declared it. To the gods, nāgas, and sages, and to the human bodhisattva, the great layman called Stainless Fame, the Licchavi Vimalakīrti, and others, he taught this nectar, rasāyana, tanajhaya.

First, the place and abode for accomplishment is called Uhusitipaca, also known as Batris: on the summit of the golden-colored king of mountains, the one called Horse-Ear Mountain.

This is called rasāyana, and it was accomplished by the sages. Some accomplish it in the space of a single moment. Some accomplish it in a single day. Some accomplish it in seven days. At the very longest, it is accomplished in twenty-one days. It is accomplished by one who has no doubt about it.

The defining characteristics of all of these are as follows.
The root, the trunk, the branches,
the leaves, the petals, the flowers,
the bark, the fruit, and the essence,
along with taste, potency, color, and function:
one root has eight limbs,
and from the eight limbs come a thousand qualities.
The qualities of all of these, moreover,
dispel the four hundred and four diseases
and certainly destroy the four māras.
This king of medicines, supreme among nectars,
was declared by the Blessed One.
All these great sages, too,
come to possess the five higher knowledges
and know the eighteen supreme fields of knowledge.

After that, the medicine is to be accomplished.
On a platform one cubit square,
smeared with the fragrance of a clean maṇḍala,
in its center place the supreme medicine,
the nectar, the three kinds of cong-zi,
together with a measure of milk
from a tawny-yellow cow.
Mix in powder of aśokakanda
and add it to what is being boiled.
Pour it into a vessel made of iron.
Having set it in the center of the platform,
recite these blessing mantras
a thousand times, or one hundred and eight times.
Oṃ vajra amṛta, pa la lig,
pe la la lig, caṇḍa lig svāhā.
This is the mantra for receiving the accomplishment:
Amṛta siddhi vicūrṇaya svāhā. Pu ca lha ha kan.
Having blessed it, it should be eaten
at midday.
From the ultimate drop, the truth,
one takes the essence from what has no essence.
As the ripened fruit, the awareness-holder is accomplished.
One lives free of illness for a hundred years.

Then twenty-one sages
became supreme accomplished awareness-holders.
After them, fifteen supreme sages,
desiring this nectar medicine,
this sacred rasāyana,
the awareness-holding of long life,
the ones called Youth Craving,
and Guide-Protector,
and Rib-Rim and others,
accomplished it on the bank of the river Gaṅgā.
Of the divine substance, the virtuous arura,
seven kinds fell to the earth.
When Śakra (Indra) drank the nectar,
seven drops fell from his mouth.
The wind carried them, and they grew from the earth.
They became renowned as the chief of nectars.
They free one from the illnesses of wind, bile, and phlegm,
from combined illnesses and seasonal illnesses,
and from untimely death.
Wind, bile, and phlegm, all of these,
are said to have five each within each one.
In order to dispel all of these completely,
the māra of the aggregates and the māra of the afflictions,
the māra of the son of the gods and the lord of death are vanquished.
This nectar is what dispels them.

In the east, south, west, or north, wherever it may be,
in an empty rock cave,
the essence that drips as nectar
is that from which the five precious substances arise.
Its name is shilajit.
This rasāyana is the supreme nectar.

Take one hundred and eight arura,
or else twenty-one,
and cook them until they become round pellets.
When they have been thoroughly cooked,
fill a vessel of iron or copper
and place that nectar medicine
in the center of the upper platform.
Bless it as before.
If it has no radiance, no smell, and no taste,
whichever of these three signs appears,
press down the left hand and lift it,
and bend the thumb and ring finger into the palm.
Taking it up, the mantra for drinking is this:
Oṃ amṛta siddhi vicūrṇaya,
and then say lha ha kan.
Its qualities cannot be fully told.
One becomes like a vajra rock,
free of illness and ever flourishing,
and becomes a supreme accomplished awareness-holder.

When existence arose, from the churning stick
came the fruit of churning, a precious thing:
the fire crystal and the water crystal, the two that appeared.

This too is method and wisdom, the two that hold sway over day and night. Taken together, that very pair comes with its retinue.

Then, as for the nectar that has dripped down: it does not grow in lowly places. The alabeta and sarkala, among gods, gandharvas, and yakṣas, in the source and dwelling place of many kinds of jewels, in the fortunate snow mountain, in the valley of the Gnyan, in mountain cliffs, in rivers and the haunts of obstructing spirits, there that which fell down and grew is the amlika and the brisitsika. Water that holds the splendor of the sun; the alabeta, the sarasra, the kalabā tree and its seeds. In the same way, water that holds the splendor of the moon. The seed of it that fell shines golden, like nectar.

It is said to be red, like fire poured upon the pūbu. Its form is lovely and beautiful, perfectly complete. Its thorns are sharp and full of glory. It bears nectar both as tree and as fruit. It destroys poisons and turns them back, and it heals many kinds of illness. It has the light of the sun and the color of the moon, and its taste too has many kinds. Combine this as the chief of the blood remedies, and one gains the lifespan of Brahmā. One obtains the accomplishment of sun and moon. It is the chief of all rasāyana.

Then the youthful sage came into being. The two, Viṣṇu and Spang, performed the deed. Holding a bow of horn, unyielding, in his hands he bore the five kinds of weapons. By him, the essence of the formation of existence, from the five kinds of nectar that were smeared on, two drops fell from his mouth to the earth. Then the supreme and sacred nectar, the cause of the later rasāyana, became a single fair maiden.

On the sands of the river Gaṅgā it exists; its manner of growth is taught: it is called the nyeu tree, or nyeu. Gantakule parpata, gnadpriya kuma, mahāgona, siṃhamukha. Its thorns look upward and look downward. All the hosts of gods and their servants surround it and make offerings. In the last autumn month, or the middle spring month, at the time of the fish, it should be sought. The method of seeking is this. A child of six or seven years, having bathed and put on fine silk clothing, faces east and makes obeisance to the gods three times. Then the mantra for drawing them out by the root:

Tadyathā śo ho dagate dare svāhā.

Homage to you, supreme nectar. Homage to you who arise from nectar, who increase strength and splendor. As the self-arisen Teacher commanded, may you, goddess, come under my power. Essence of the supreme rasāyana, you who are closest to supreme accomplishment, grant me life and strength. Make all wisdom clear.

Remove the bark and the sinews. Crush the sprouts and hide them again. Pound the heart and press its essence through a cloth into a vessel. Having bathed oneself too, and put on fine silk garments, one enters the practice of preparing the medicine. Having done the approach and the recitation meditation, recite a thousand, or a hundred thousand, or as much as one can. When eating or drinking it, drink it with fresh milk, or else mix it as a supreme powder. The mantra for eating and drinking is this:

Namo vajra agaja tu svāhā.

Whether by days or by mouthfuls, take it one measure at a time, in the proportion of one sang. These are the qualities of eating it: body, speech, and mind are clear, and the faculties become bright.

Whoever drinks this nectar becomes like the gods, deathless; to the nāgas it is like medicine; to trees it is like water; to flowers it is like the moon. For five hundred years one keeps the complexion of a youth of sixteen, free of illness, always flourishing, with strength like a great elephant, and one gains the five superknowledges. Drink it with milk, or with melted butter or honey. Eaten for one day, it gives ten qualities; eaten for seven days, a hundred. Eaten for twenty-one days, one obtains these hundred and eight. Applied, it pervades a thousand women. The seed does not fail, and the offspring too increase as sons and are full of heroic vigor. Humans cannot harm one. Eaten for a month, the qualities are such that one's face is like the full moon. Yakṣas and rākṣasas cannot harm one, and one becomes equal to all the gods.

When the defilements are exhausted, one flies through the sky. With the gait of the vajra one travels through space. One attains here the rank of a holder of awareness. But if one eats the food of humans mixed with the flesh of persons, one will certainly not attain accomplishment. Thus the scripture declared.

Then, in the time of the sage Tragsha, there was a king named Shenyencan. He made vast offerings and gifts to all the gods and sages. When Mahādeva, the Great God (Śiva), was invited to attend them all, the fierce Great God grew angry, and from the forehead of that Great God one extra eye appeared. Upon all the gods and sages he cast various disorders of wind, bile, and phlegm, and likewise epidemic diseases upon humans, entrusting them to the hand of the wind god and scattering them. Even the many gods and sages, not knowing and not feeling, became stupefied.

This king of powders, this leaf, grew spreading over the surface of the earth, and even the sages make offerings to it. This great flower, the sheep's eye, is the queen of rasāyana. Boil it with cow's milk. Drinking one swallow makes the body hair stand on end. The quality of drinking two swallows is that it cures all epidemic diseases. The quality of drinking three swallows is that the eyes and their veins become red and the complexion youthful.

Dry it in the shade and grind it to powder, then mix it with the juice of the mātulaṅga fruit. If this is administered, enemies become stupefied; even if protected by the gods, they will die. This is the mantra for blessing it. Recite it a thousand times, or one hundred and eight.

Oṃ so-u grī-bā-ha-na ha-na hūṃ, ma-ra-yam ra-ya, ce-ta-ya ce-ta-ya su-grī-ba phaṭ.

The chief of the nectars of rasāyana: make shuṅdag and ruta into powder and administer it, and kings and women come under one's power. Mixed with grihaharolo ki, if administered to an enemy it makes him deaf. Dried in the shade, the sheep's eye mixed with the milk of suti, administered to an enemy, brings death.

This that was brought forth by the fierce Great God is known to the youthful Mañjuśrī; the gods and sages do not know it otherwise. Thus the Blessed One, the youthful Mañjuśrī, declared to the lord of the Sahā world, the god Brahmā, to the sages, to the nāga king Jogpo, and to fortunate human bodhisattvas such as the Licchavis and others. Moreover, it does not wait for a later life or a later time: the fruit of good fortune is obtained directly, and the result ripens in this very life.

Then the supreme goddess of the elixirs, whose name is Wise One of Accomplishment, the maiden called Mula: her fallen seed grew in the forests of trees of Jambudvīpa. It is known here as raktā, the chief of rasāyana. Its root is raktā mula kha. Root, flowers, and fruits all alike are deep red. Its fruit is about the size of a bean or a mash pea, standing upright; this one should dig up. Dig it on the full moon day of the autumn month. It grows in the forests and lands of existence, and is known as the blood-drop. Press out the fresh juice and mix it in equal measure with the red lotus essence.

This is the mantra for accomplishing it. Oṃ namo rāga raktā, mula bune svāhā. It is the supreme means of bringing beings under one's power. Thus the goddess of elixirs declared. It must not be reduced to nothing by its name alone.

The prince called Siddhārtha, who passed beyond sorrow, left relics known as śarīra. Their seed spread and grew across the surface of Jambudvīpa. It is called Mahānīla, and its name is Thaskar Vajra. The flower is the young thaskar; its seed-pod is three-cornered, about the size of a sesame seed. It can shatter even a vajra-hard rock. Combined with śarīra and bone, it is excellent for joining. It must not be reduced to nothing by its name alone. All its qualities are such that the bones become firm and hard like a vajra. Its power equals that of Vajrapāṇi, and it is declared supreme among rasāyana.

Then, into the hand of the son of the supreme lord of the gods, Indra (Śakra), the yakṣa king shot an arrow.

Even a fragment of it, a mere finger-width long, that spreads across the surface of the earth is called Libyice, the "hand of the sovereign." It has a stalk, without branches or leaves. Its flower is three-cornered, like the feathers of an arrow. Its root is five finger-widths long. It should be dug up in the last summer month, at the time of the fish. The practitioner should place it in the left palm. It is the seal by which the accomplishments of all rasāyana are obtained. It must not be reduced to nothing by its name alone. It is a substance of accomplishment that will multiply greatly.

Combine in equal parts the great stench that arises in the body, the great stench of the slain, and white mustard. Drunk, it overcomes all poisons. Because it removes the poison that causes death, it is called "nectar" (amṛta). It must not be reduced to nothing by its name alone. By it even the Lord of Death, Māra, is conquered.

The ear called Pūrṇa is the ear of the rākṣasa Pūrṇa. Joined with the ears of the slain, it brings about the attainment of the clairvoyance of the ear. It must not be reduced to nothing by its name alone.

Joined with the flower of the bahu called "sheep's eye," and with the eyes of the garuḍa bird, and with ālavika, śaṅśaṅdeu, the peacock, the goose, and the cuckoo, and with the eyes of the slain, one attains there the clairvoyance of the eye. It is the eye of the powerful. It must not be reduced to nothing by its name alone.

Citraka and snyingzhoshá, all of these are the heart-thigh of the yakṣa. Joined with the cittas of the slain, one obtains the accomplishment of the five clairvoyances. It must not be reduced to nothing by its name alone.

Of a woman such as was described above, the brain is found in the gorocanā. That is of the lineage of the bull. Joined with all the brains of the slain, and with the root of the Tibetan wool-plant hāya, the accomplishment of radiance is attained.

Halecikiya, the blazing-mouthed one, is a tongue; therefore it is joined with tongues. One obtains the accomplishment of the iron of empowerment and the accomplishment of Avalokiteśvara. It must not be reduced to nothing by its name alone.

The lord of existence, chief of the Brahmās, is named Mgon-btsun Phya. The son of Nala is called Jihva, the "tongue" of the one who has not yet uttered speech. The one who has uttered speech is Brahmā; cut it from the palate of the one who has not yet uttered speech. For the joining that goes with it, there is a flower, a round, rolling thing like a rma-lo, named Vayasāya. Its root is Mahāgonamika. Its flower has a front of gold, and it faces east. Its entirety is halecikiya. Pound in equal parts and make a powder. When the moon is seized by an eclipse, make offerings to the personal deity, and put the powder into rice beer. Within an instant it is accomplished, and one obtains the accomplishment of the horse-neck empowerment. Joined with all the tongues of the slain, it is excellent for the joining. It must not be reduced to nothing by its name alone.

Join nose and the fruit of the nose-plant, and one becomes able to utter the melodious speech of Brahmā. Join all the noses of the slain. It must not be reduced to nothing by its name alone.

Join conch, dragon-bone, relics, and all the bones of the slain. One obtains the vajra of the body and clairvoyance. It must not be reduced to nothing by its name alone.

Foam gathered from the ocean, water of every kind, snow-water, various saltwaters and fragrant waters, join all the waters of the slain. It is the nectar of vajra water, able constantly to accomplish the benefit of beings. It must not be reduced to nothing by its name alone.

By the intended meaning, it appears to all sentient beings, and through the quality of its potency it is able to benefit all sentient beings. Intending to nourish all beings, the Blessed One, glorious Samantabhadra, out of his wisdom-mind, gathered it in a vase of song, spread it over the faculty of the tongue, and smiled. He spoke first to Mañjuśrī, the sharp one of keen knowledge, and to Vajradharma. Mañjuśrī spoke to the foremost sons of the maṇḍala, to Brahmā and the sages, to gods and nāgas, and to the human bodhisattvas, Mālagirti and the others.

This is drawn from the Great Nectar, the supreme scriptural collection, from the hundred-thousand-section scriptural transmission on the accomplishment of rasāyana medicine.

The Second Great Volume.

In the language of India: Prajñā Bhagavan Mahārāja. In the language of Tibet: Homage to the Blessed One, the great king of non-duality.

Then the conduct was taught. In the scriptural transmission of Anuyoga it was taught as the yoga of completion. The characteristics of view, of conduct, and of the dhāraṇī were spoken here.

Likewise the great samaya is also taught. If this activity is to be accomplished through the scriptural transmission, then what is not to be abandoned should be accepted. As conduct, guard the fifteen samayas, the five knowledges, and the three guardings. Because it is guarded as eight thousand and eight hundred, it is called "eight hundred and eight thousand."

And if the four great samayas are complete, all is excellent. Pacifying, enriching, empowering, and wrathful action, together with subjugating and repelling, make six, which are one.

First, concerning the woman, the scriptural transmission explains: she is endowed with a height of four forearms, and also with eleven qualities. Her face is beautiful, her eyes long, her upper and lower body balanced, her waist slender, her joints rounded, her fingers long. With eleven radiant qualities, she is said to belong to the family of wisdom. At thirteen she is accomplished as of the vajra family. At sixteen it is certain that she will be accomplished by the mantra practitioner. She is shown to be of the supreme jewel family. At twenty-one she is of the lotus family, accomplishing the supreme horse empowerment of Avalokiteśvara. At twenty-five she draws forth the awakening mind of Asaṅga. All accomplish the supreme family of action. From thirty-one onward, until the mind departs, all are suitable. One who is pleasing is supreme. It is explained that she is accomplished in the family of the Sugata, the dhāraṇī of great bliss without union or separation.

Whoever among these is found, without exception, should have her body bathed and made clean. Anoint her with fragrant sandalwood, gopan, jātipala, kuru, kakkola, camphor, agaru, turuṣka, and gibang, the scents of the elephant, and then place ornaments and garlands on her. Having dressed her in silk and soft cotton, in garments and adornments, place golden earrings and bracelets, necklaces, armlets, and pearls, and adorn her with various jewels. Seat her on a comfortable cushion as is proper, and place her on the left side.

For approach, close approach, accomplishment, and great accomplishment, make a maṇḍala one fathom square, with a raised rim four finger-widths high. Anoint the maṇḍala with vajra-scented water, and fumigate it with the incense of great flesh, guggul, and pokgi. Sit circling around that maṇḍala. Having placed her outside, on the left, bless that very Bhagavan.

Visualize at the crown a white Oṃ, radiant; at the tongue a supreme red Aḥ; at the heart, on a moon disc, a blue Hūṃ; at the navel a radiant blue Svā; and at the feet a radiant green Hā. From Ba, in the expanse of the lotus, visualize a triangle blazing with fire. In order to purify the five afflictions and to cut off the five sufferings, in the right hand is the one who cannot be overcome by others, and in the left is the great Rolangma. At her mouth is the wrathful Nyerma, and the one-braided mother is on the lotus. Having blessed the five secret consorts and the four wrathful goddesses, recite the mantra: Oṃ Aḥ Hūṃ Svāhā. The command is that of Bhagavan himself.

Having become the four mudrās, and joined in union without duality, one overcomes the māra of the gods, the māra of the aggregates, the māra of the afflictions, and the lord of death. Endowed with the four great mudrās, at the time the mind departs into space, awakening, saṃsāra, and nirvāṇa go to the ground of non-duality.

The Blessed One declared: All buddhas and all sentient beings, and everything that is designated by signs, are nothing other than great bliss. A phenomenon other than great bliss is not known even by the buddhas themselves. All outer and inner worlds and their inhabitants, being completely pure as awakening mind, are the unborn wisdom, the expanse of the consort, the great non-dual nectar, shining in the state of the bliss of father and mother. This is the essence, awakening mind. It was declared by all the Sugatas.

If one pays homage with unwavering faith, then the transmission of the Sthāna is taught.

From the primordial, pure in cause, grasping at an "I" and a self became the cause of saṃsāra in the three realms. These three realms are the field of liberation. Āsamacala dharmadhātu svabhāva ātmakoham, raṃ yaṃ khaṃ ta tha phaṭ. So the Blessed One declared.

In the state of the immovable great seal, this is the method of liberation of the great lord: the life and vital force of the lord of living beings, through the blessing of the state of compassion, wisdom, and skillful means, brings about the purification of all beings. By the activity of subjugation, with the blessing of all the buddhas, in the field of liberating the three realms entirely, for the sake of performing the Great Sthāna, the many Sugatas assemble together. Endowed with loving-kindness and compassion, they tear apart the net of affliction and saṃsāra.

Who has closed this door of the abode? Was it closed by the lord of mind? Saying "With grasping and the apprehended, with crossed legs, open the door that goes beyond all objects," one opens the door. Kata rika na praveśa. With the left foot, the form of Rudra; as this door of the abode is cut, I take refuge in the Sugatas. "This suffering of death has arisen here." Thus the eight Great Gods spoke these words at one time.

The eight Ṭākinīs declared: Arrogant and unruly Great God, now open all the doors of the mind. With the blazing vajra knife, having opened all the doors of the abode, let them be seized by the lord of mind.

The Teacher spoke: Death comes through the condition of birth. Through the condition of sin, one dies, destroyed. The self also perishes through viewing things as real. Hold to this teaching of mine. Yet the suffering of death is removed. Cast away from the object the clinging to form, limbs and hands. Male and female are crossed and pressed down. The head is directed to the southwest corner, the feet stretched out toward the northeast. The right foot of the Ṭākinī presses down upon the Rudra liṅga. Having thus pressed down the liṅga, with the stance of the great liberator, with great majesty, one speaks thus: Samaya keśa citta jvala hūṃ phaṭ. Thus one presses down. And, saying Mahādeva Rudraja:

Beings arising from the three realms, through the distinction of the vajra body, are gathered in the manner of light rays; these I seize into form. With all vajra joy, let the body and the supreme wisdom act.

In this maṇḍala that opens the path of liberation, as the arrogant ones come, so, fortunate son of the family, you are welcome; well come! Through the great lord of yogis, in this great place of the king of teachings, in the very expanse of bodhicitta, all the three realms and saṃsāra are gathered.

In the nature of reality, without arising or ceasing, "Sugatas" and "beings" are so named...

In the basic space of reality all is one, and there is no two. All things are equal in essential nature; all of them are the heart-essence, the awakening mind. Because they have never arisen, there is no ceasing and no destruction. If there is no self, there is the body of equality. Why would great compassion not act? Though the primordial state abides in just this way, beings, with minds bewildered by ignorance, cling to a self and to what belongs to a self, each separately. They have not abandoned the two extremes of permanence and annihilation. The five aggregates, imputed through error, are held and bound by wrong conceptions and inverted views. They gather the causes of affliction and suffering, and by wandering through the five realms of saṃsāra they do not pass beyond sorrow for a long time. Toward the aggregates, the elements, and the sense sources, the habitual tendencies of delusion lie dormant. For those whose path to the happy realms is obscured, in order to cut the stream of the five paths of saṃsāra, this is the purification of the lower realms.

Having thus completely purified the lower realms, in order to cut the doors of the three sufferings, one speaks the syllables raṃ, yaṃ, khaṃ. Burned by the blazing fire of wisdom, all becomes empty, without characteristics.

Those beings whose time has come, through the yogī whose action has come to fruition, are summoned into this maṇḍala of the vajra expanse by the hook of compassion. That fortunate consciousness, through the blessing of great compassion, spreads emanations of the body in the ten directions. By speech, all beings are liberated. The mind is endowed with the four immeasurables. In the maṇḍala of radiant sun and moon, having conferred the empowerment of Amitāyus, one rightly liberates the truly real. When one rightly liberates, great bliss is attained, and one surely arrives at the irreversible ground. Merely by arriving, one obtains empowerment and sees the buddha-fields as the very expanse. That consciousness, whatever suitable one it may be, is gathered by hūṃ into the secret expanse. Generated as the body, it is emanated as the wisdom body. Through the spontaneous presence of the expanse and wisdom, one goes to the limitless buddha-fields and to the palace maṇḍala of the Sugatas. Possessed of the feet of miraculous power, the wind enters the noble path, and one departs turning the wheel of Dharma. The chiefs of the gods of the world, the śrāvaka arhats, the solitary buddhas, and the bodhisattvas of the ten grounds all honor one with supramundane offerings. Passing beyond the ground adorned with density, one is set on the ground of the lotus-eyed one. One attains the deathless dharmakāya. Even to draw out the entire three-thousandfold world would not equal this, nor could even a hundred thousand rains of offerings compare. One's own mind, unaltered, is awakened from the beginning. At the body's heart is Magadha, the great charnel ground, the cool grove, the abode of the great Mamo Keuri, a land of the learned and of those with great courage. The stūpa is named Bringer of Happiness.

Toward its head, in the southern direction, the great charnel ground was established. It lies on a small island of the land of Siṅga, the abode of the great Mamo Tsouri, who bears wondrous manifestations in what is seen and heard. The stūpa is the mountain Potala.

At the right arm, in the southeast quarter, is the great charnel ground of Nepal. The great Mamo is Puskasī, who especially enjoys abundant food. The stūpa is Khasho Byagṛikha.

At the left arm, in the southwest quarter, is the great charnel ground of the land of Sala. The great Mamo is Vetali, the abode of Vajrapāṇi's authority, where power and dominion are great. In the region of the great Gyaduyul is the stūpa Malasagana.

At the right foot, in the northeast quarter, is the great charnel ground of the land of Li. The great Mamo is Caṇḍālī, known as the goddess Śaṅkinī, who has accomplished powers over water and fire and miraculous abilities. The stūpa is named Geutoshan.

At the left foot, in the northwest quarter, is the great charnel ground of the land of Kashmir. The great Mamo is Pramoha, possessed of happiness and perfect abundance. The stūpa is named Skanika.

North of the upper region of the male-marked place is the great charnel ground of the land of Oḍḍiyāna. The great Mamo is Kasmarī, who has the five enjoyments of wealth, and many who have attained spiritual accomplishments dwell there. The great stūpa of splendor is named Dharmacakra. Within it all are liberated.

The great charnel ground of the land of Zahor is the sea-gate island of Mahādo, Laṅkāpura, the abode of the rākṣasas. The great Mamo is Smaśānī, whose clairvoyance and miraculous powers are beyond thought. She holds sway over all the rākṣasas, rākṣasīs, and flesh-eaters, and is endowed with precious jewels, the wish-fulfilling tree, and every kind of wealth. The stūpa is Youthful Bringer of Happiness.

These are the eight places of the great charnel grounds taught throughout the world.

which gathers back as Amitābha, the speech of the Dharma,
abiding in the mudrā of equipoise.
At the heart, a dark blue syllable Hūṃ
sends forth five lights,
which gather back as Akṣobhya, the bodhisattva,
whose two hands hold a vajra and bell,
or else he is in the earth-touching gesture subduing Māra.
At the navel, a yellow syllable Svā
sends forth five lights,
which gather back as the chief of holy jewels.

The mudrā is that of the wish-fulfilling gem, bestowing.
At the secret place, a green syllable Ha;
from it five-colored lights stream forth
and gather back as Amoghasiddhi,
perfectly abiding in the pacifying mudrā.
All these belonging to the five families
are themselves the self-arisen maṇḍala.

Thus, by the radiating and gathering of five lights each,
in the twenty-five emanations and re-absorptions,
the work for beings of the three realms is accomplished.
Gathering back again, all is drawn into the five medicines.
The medicines themselves blaze forth clearly as the mudrās,
and the five lights gather back,
and each becomes clearly manifest as its own hand-emblem.
From the golden wheel with eight corners
the great scent, the nectar, melts away.
From Hūṃ a five-pronged vajra
blazes and melts into the awakening mind.
From Svā a jewel blazes with light,
and Mahāmāṃsa melts into nectar.
From Hrīḥ lights of many colors appear,
and the padma-rakta melts into drops.
From the green Ha, lights of many kinds radiate,
and the pure scented water melts into nectar.
In this way the radiating and gathering
should be done again and again, through familiarity.

Then, upon the five kinds of nectar,
contemplate the five sacred syllables:
Oṃ Hūṃ Svā Hrīḥ Ā.
The five root syllables too
send out their lights as before.
Then comes the recitation of the mantra to be repeated in meditation.
This ninefold mantra is the one to be drawn upon in recitation:
Oṃ Vajra Citta Samaya Hūṃ Thaḥ.

Now, the mudrā of the nectar is this:
extend the upper arm of the left hand,
bend the thumb and ring finger into the palm,
holding a vajra implement;
extend the index finger, middle finger, and little finger,
join them together, and with the palm
press down upon the vessel of nectar.
Then bless the nectar.
Let the meditative absorption be like fire,
and the recitation like the wind,
and let no breaks occur through phlegm or spitting.
Then bless the nectar:
Vajra Amṛta Oṃ Āḥ Hūṃ Hrīḥ Thā.
These are the five root maṇḍalas.
Jaḥ Hūṃ Paṃ Hoḥ.
Mahā Amṛta Āveśa Hoḥ.
Thus the Blessed One, the King of Nectar, declared.

This is the sixth great chapter of the great Nectar, the Samaya Hundred Thousand Section, which sets out the definitive transmission for extracting the five great fruits, according to the transmission of the supreme sacred nectar, and teaches the method of then compounding them as medicine.

In the language of India: Amṛta Kalaśa Siddhi. In the language of Tibet: The Transmission of the Nectar Vase.

Homage to the glorious Great Bliss of Nectar.

Having paid homage to the benefactor and protector,
I shall explain the places and sites of accomplishment:
the mountains of the four directions, east, south, west, and north;
the methods of accomplishment of the great seers;
the methods of compounding of the Phramen goddesses;
the regions where medicinal essences arise;
the abodes of the goddesses of trees and forests;
the medicinal valleys on the mountains and the sandalwood groves.
One should practice at the places where they dwell.

The means of transforming poisons into nectar:
roots together with branches,
and rasāyana and other substances,
should be gathered complete, and then one should practice.
On a mountain, amid forest leaves, by a riverbank,
or in a dense, empty cave,
so that this may be the supreme place of accomplishment,
the ground must first be carefully examined.
Level, moist, with fertile soil,
free of stakes, stones, and demonic crags,
without salt flats, and without barren or sandy ground,
unbroken, uncracked, and even:
there make the supreme place of accomplishment.

With the ten self-arisen wrathful ones,
fix the directions and intermediate points with ritual daggers,
and purify the site and the ground:
Oṃ Vajra Kīlaya Mahā Krodha Hūṃ Hūṃ Phaṭ Phaṭ.
Garja Garja Hūṃ Phaṭ.
Hana Hana Hūṃ Phaṭ.
Daha Daha Hūṃ Phaṭ.
Paca Paca Hūṃ Phaṭ.
Bandha Bandha Hūṃ Phaṭ.
Hulu Hulu Hūṃ Phaṭ.
Tiṣṭha Tiṣṭha Hūṃ Phaṭ.
Chinda Chinda Hūṃ Phaṭ.
Bhinda Bhinda Hūṃ Phaṭ.
Sphoṭaya Sphoṭaya Hūṃ Phaṭ.
Thus the boundaries are sealed in the ten directions.

When the ground has been thoroughly purified,
make the maṇḍala one fathom square, with four corners,
smeared with the five scents and with rakta,
and make it a fine maṇḍala.
With the thread of the Vasuta, the stringed line,
mark out all sixteen lines.

Upon it draw sixteen dots, or else set out sixteen hand-emblems. In the center, place a vessel: a skull cup that is whole and unbroken, or else a turtle's head will also do. Set it in the middle of that maṇḍala. Take one hundred and eight kinds of nectar, mix them, and fill the skull cup. Place it at the center of the wheel, at its hub. In the four directions and the four intermediate directions as well, set four or eight vessels, and cover their mouths with silk of the matching family and color, bound with the five-colored cloth. Or else tie a neck-cloth around the vase. Then, as far as one's means allow, arrange all the substances, the rasāyana and the rest, without exception, circling around that maṇḍala. In the same way, with white silk and sky-blue veils, circle the whole maṇḍala round. At the four corners of the maṇḍala, set four arrows, four mirrors, four jewels, four swords, and eight ritual daggers, circling around the maṇḍala.

Then meditate in order on the three samādhis. From the samādhi of the cause comes the result, and that very thing becomes the awakening mind. The maṇḍalas of the five elements, the Mother, are produced in order from the awakening mind. Raṃ, Khaṃ, Bruṃ: meditate that a celestial mansion becomes clearly manifest, equal to the expanse of space. In the center is the great awakening mind. In the east is the vajra, the great foul substance, the great. In the south is flesh of every kind, māṃsa. In the west is the lotus, the gathering of rakta. In the north is the perfumed water of many kinds. Arrange these in order. When the principal practitioner has performed the union properly, he takes the awakening mind onto the stemmed mirror, then takes the central vessel, faces the eastern direction, waves the awakening mind over it, and speaks this song:

The wisdom nature of this awakening mind,
stainless and utterly pure, descends like rain.
It is like a wish-fulfilling jewel, generating all beings.
Because it is pure from the beginning, it has the nature of nectar.
By nondual union the great vajra family is accomplished.
That very thing is declared to be mirror-like wisdom.

Having said this, place a crystal bowl within the central bhandha, and gather and pour in all the awakening mind, together with the bodily essence, gorocanā, and the rest. With the mudrā of the hook, place it above that. All the practitioners face the eastern direction, wave the awakening mind over it, and recite this mantra one hundred and eight times:

Oṃ Āḥ Hūṃ Vajrasattva Hūṃ. Sarva e śuddhe. Amṛta Sukha Mahāyoga Hūṃ. Citta Hūṃ. Oṃ Āḥ Hūṃ. Akṣobhya Dveṣa Rati Samayastvaṃ.

Having said this, pour it into the center and set it down.

Turn to face the south, take the vessel of great māṃsa, and say this:

Beings cling to place.
Deluded, they hold themselves to be clean.
If the impurity is not truly cast out,
then even a hungry ghost, withered and cut off from all merit,
cannot bear to give up his own body.
By the merit of gods and the like, what need to speak of embodied beings?
Therefore, to cast out all this impurity
and to show the self-arisen nectar,
even a god eats the self of the gods,
and likewise humans and the rest.
The yogin too uses it and eats it.
Since he draws no distinction between self and other,
liberated, without accepting or rejecting,
if one sees the meaning of equality,
all is the wisdom body of equality,
and the Sugata's supreme jewel family is accomplished.

Oṃ Āḥ Hūṃ Mahāmāṃsa Samaya Ra Ho Haṃ. Ratna Rati Samayastvaṃ.

Having said this, invite the jewel family, father and mother, recite the mantra one hundred and eight times, and place the mahāmāṃsa and the many kinds of flesh in the south.

Turn to face the west, take the vessel of the many kinds of rakta, and say this:

Rāga, rakta, the lotus arises.
These despised, unclean substances
are the abode of bliss, the object of attachment.

The pledge of the lotus family is accomplished. This is the wisdom of discernment, he says, and pours it into the vessel of the west and sets it down. Oṃ Āḥ Hūṃ Mahāpadma Rakta Mula. Amṛta Bharāga Rati. Samaya Hūṃ Su Ba Hrīḥ. Having recited this, he places it in the west.

Turning to face the north, he takes the vessel of fragrant water and speaks these words:

The origin, the outflow, saffron water,
is the water of vajra fragrance.
Nothing at all has arisen apart from water.
The pledge of the action family
and the supreme wisdom of accomplishing activity are also achieved.

Oṃ Āḥ Hūṃ Amogha Siddhi. Vajra Rati Samayastvaṃ. Having recited this, he places it in the north.

Taking the vessel facing east, with the great fragrant substance, he speaks these words:

This despised, unclean, impure refuse,
this very conceptual thought is the refuse.
It is the nectar that upholds supreme life,
for its nature is primordially pure.
It is the very heart-commitment of all the Sugatas.
Since it is joined into the nondual body,
the wisdom of the dharmadhātu and its pledge are accomplished.

Oṃ Vairocana Mohārati Samayastvaṃ. Mahāyoga Atisphāraṇa Balinte. Amṛta Ucchiṣṭha Adhitiṣṭha Oṃ. So he recites.

Then all the practitioners join their palms together, and the consort takes the vessel in the center and speaks these words:

For those who have the essence of wisdom,
this is the nature of all the buddhas.
The essence arises from the meaning of the Dharma.
This is the abode of the conduct of awakening.
The five substances to be partaken of,
each of one has five qualities each,
or each is divided into three qualities.
The root pledges are fifteen,
and the branch pledges are twenty-eight.
When vessel and contents are gathered in the center,
they are also to be shown as sixteen.
From the union of skillful means and wisdom,
see the assembly of male and female heroes.
Eight roots and eight branches,
the substances are the sixteen male heroes,
and the vessel's expanse is the sixteen female heroes.
The spokes of the wheel, too, are sixteen,
yet since their nature is nondual, one expanse,
meditate on them as endowed with mudrā and bindu.
Endowed with the thirty-two marks,
adorned with the eighty excellent features,
the maṇḍala of the vajra expanse is complete.
The cause is secret, and the result is secret.
In the maṇḍala of the vajra expanse,
the mudrā of the glorious treasury-holder
and the mudrā of the vajra opener
are placed in the center of the wheel's spokes.
The five nectars and the eight roots,
eye and ear, nose and tongue,
body and consciousness, together with their objects,
are the uncompounded vajra when gathered.
Because the intention is gathered, it is called vajra.
Fully joined with Vajrasattva,
one becomes Vajrasattva himself.

Oṃ Āḥ Hūṃ Sarva Pañca Amṛta Vajrasattva Hūṃ. Having said this, he blesses the five nectars and urges them on.

Then again, he recites these words of joining and urging, in which the eight roots and the branches gather into a thousand.

Then, into an empty vessel, he mixes grape wine, honey wine, Chinese wine, sugar wine, rice wine, and many kinds of wine, and speaks these words:

In the grains there is no wine,
and in water there is no nature of wine.
When the skilled one joins grain and water,
it becomes the wine of equality.

The mantra for blessing the wine: Oṃ Amṛta Jagat Prabhava Svāhā. Oṃ Jīva Svāhā. He pours it into the medicine wine and stirs it. The mantra for stirring: Musala Musala. One attains the supreme taste and the state of the Great Bliss.

Even in the unclean there is nothing unclean.
By the accomplished one with oral instructions,
when the skilled one joins them without duality,
the meaning of equality is attained.

Krodheśvarī Śodhaya Saṃśodhaya. Then he mixes in various kinds of milk and speaks these words:

Of horses, humans, and the rest,
however it has become milk,
if the great skilled one joins it properly,
its qualities appear like curd and butter.

Thus, by transforming the substances,
the qualities of equality are accomplished like butter.

Having said this, he pours it into the vessel of awakening mind. Then he crushes various precious substances and speaks these words:

Just as gold and jewels
may be mixed as one, or may remain apart,
and even when they remain apart,
each one's color and defining mark shine distinctly,
so too, though such precious qualities exist,
when gathered together the qualities become wondrous.
Therefore the two, adorned together, appear beautiful.
In the same way, the qualities of the substances
appear as the jewels of their own qualities.
Because they are joined in nondual equality,
the unsurpassed qualities become wondrous.
All are gathered into one.

Then he gathers the various kinds of elixir (rasāyana), pours various waters into the empty vessel, and speaks this:

Though the waters are various and different,
when they reach the ocean they have the taste of salt.
The splendor of all these gathered together
is vast in waves as the ocean itself,
and in the dharmadhātu it is of one taste.
From the compassion of the five elements
come the five medicines and the five elixirs,
which nourish all beings;
they are blessed through compassion.
From the absence of things, accomplishments are taken,
for this is a cloud of awakening mind,
and so it will be accomplished just as one visualizes it.

Having spoken thus, he takes the medicine in hand and, one by one, proclaims the great qualities of the medicine:

The medicine is compounded from eight thousand roots,
pure from the beginning, self-arisen.
Rasāyana and other substances
are the essence of a hundred thousand vast roots.
Awakening mind, arising from nondual great bliss,
is the vajra itself.
The great flesh offering is a precious jewel,
the supreme food, the great fragrance of life,
joined with the perfect lotus flower.
The vajra water is the supreme elixir.
In a crystal vessel of camphor he draws the incense offering,
a mass of precious things blazing like fire,
the essence of a hundred thousand vast qualities,
the actual embodiment of the five families and the five pledges,
the substance that perfects and accomplishes the five wisdoms,
which liberates all sentient beings of the five lineages.
It is the supreme means for dispelling the five poisons.
All of it is the great elixir of the yogin,
the drop in which everything is gathered without remainder,
for joining equally with all the buddhas.
United as one with the heroes and their tastes,
joined in equality without duality,
he makes offering to all the buddhas of the three times
and to the oceans of Victors.

So he speaks. Then the wisdom consorts place the medicine in their hands. Again he recites this auspicious verse, supreme in meaning:

A Dharma wondrous and marvelous,
an abode equal to space,
from the appearance of nonconceptuality,
is taught as merely conventional, and this is amazing.

So he speaks. Taking in hand the vessel with the essence of the medicine, he offers the elixir to the chief of the consorts. The chief takes it, transforms it into the great seal (mahāmudrā), and meditates.

Then the chief of the practitioners puts on the heroic attire. He is adorned with the drops of great ash, and with Bayaṅ Nyobati, Bayaṅ Śaṅgi Ra, and Narasira, and he takes up the pledge substances for summoning and waving, the supreme hasta, the bratasha pill, the string of great bark, the whip for striking spirits, and the flute of bone. Playing the flute, he sings the Kuśa song. He lays down the foundation of the supreme cāra and the great bark. He draws the guggulu with undistracted mind and makes nine drops of blood, and upon each weapon he places eight takrid. He wears the foundation of the tiger-skin cāra as a garment and, tying a banner to the knot, holds it. He draws the maṇḍala as a wheel of eight spokes, draws one red crescent in the center, and heaps the medicine upon it. The practitioner, father and mother, perform the union according to the rite. He recites the approach practice of the sixteen goddesses up to one hundred and eight times. Then he brings down the blessing, and also makes the seal of bringing down. He uses the signs of waving and various other signs, and the vajra of skillful means, Vajra Śarīraṃ and so forth, or else with the supreme left hand.

He draws forth the medicine and waves it. He mixes it together in the bowl, strains it, and pours it into the medicine vessel. Stirring it with the vajra, he praises it with song, dance, drums, and sacred words. He fumigates it with incense, and with the dance steps and the vajra gait he places the nectar in the center of the maṇḍala and circles around it. Then, from each side, the great qualities are proclaimed.

Again, the maṇḍala of the peaceful deities as well: all the syllables of the individual families are dissolved into the medicine, and this is recited.

Oṃ amṛta sarva mahā yoga hūṃ. Oṃ vajra sarva samaya hūṃ. Oṃ mahā sarva amṛta mahā sukha hrīḥ. Oṃ sarva eṣu dhe. Krodhīśvari, sarva śodhaya. Oṃ āḥ hūṃ vajra amṛta hūṃ hrīḥ tha. Thus he recites it over and over.

Then come the fire offering and the burnt offering, and the first portion of the fire is poured in as well.

Next, the medicine that purifies the mind: leṅ-leṅ-hwa, crystal and camphor, fresh heart, fresh kidney, and fresh lung; śuddha kubera, śuddha prame, śuddha nabse, rasāyana, and rasāyana tanajaya. The one who bathes adorns it with the drops of the utpala flower. Having mixed and combined the fresh medicines, he stirs them and bestows the vessel. The two vessels are joined at the mouth and bound with silk, and the join is sealed. Then he places it in the center of the crescent maṇḍala. He draws forth the nine-fold mantra, reciting it in a whisper.

At the beginning and the end of this, the offering of the gathering is not interrupted. The sequence of the twelve deeds may also be performed. The purpose of accomplishing the medicine, the scriptural source showing the method of preparation, the exhortation to the stages of the rite, and the things used as implements: this is the meaning drawn from the volume of the transmission of the nectar vase, from the transmission of the Supreme Great Nectar, as found in the Hundred Thousand Nectars. The seventh great volume.

In the language of India: Bhagavān Mañjuśrī Tīkṣṇa, namaḥ siddhaṃ. In the language of Tibet: Homage to the Blessed One, sharp Mañjuśrī.

Then the Blessed One, the glorious one whose body is like a vajra, was abiding in the very intent of awakening. The Blessed One, the awakening mind, the vajra Samantabhadra, was abiding in the great pleasure grove of the Mother, free of union and separation across the three times, his intent resting in the dharmadhātu, the great peace.

The Sugatas of the five families, the four secret Mothers, the assembly of the bodhisattva retinue, and the assembly of the female mind-beings were present as well. In the maṇḍala of the vajra expanse, the glorious Samantabhadra spoke to the supreme and holy sons who were the close retinue, to all the kings of the families without exception, to the great Brahmās, to the great seers, to gods and nāgas, and to humans such as the Licchavis and others. He said:

Through earnest effort, develop diligence.
Without doubt, success is certain.
These five great nectars
have qualities in the using and the holding.
Their nature and essence,
their power and blessing bring accomplishment.
By the samādhi of unforgotten continuity,
the quality of certain accomplishment
is taught in three certain instructions.

Having spoken thus, he entrusted the meaning of the Dharma to Mañjuśrī Tīkṣṇa, the compiler.

Then the noble Mañjuśrī addressed this to the oceanic assembly of deities of the retinue in the maṇḍala of the vajra expanse:

Emaho! Samantabhadra, buddha,
perfected in the great secret bindu itself,
lamp of wisdom dispelling the darkness of ignorance,
hero, I take refuge in you.
This self-arisen, stainless nectar,
supreme, praised by all the buddhas,
having vanquished all the stains of unknowing,
establishes beings in the definitive expanse.
With nondual devotion I pay homage.
Vairocana, Vajra Jina, Cakṣu,

Akṣobhya, Ravi-prabha, Amitābha,
Amoghasiddhi, the whirling nectar:
with delight and reverence I pay homage.
To the assemblies of the consorts, lords of the expanse,
with a joyful mind I bow and pay homage.
To Vajrapāṇi, the king of vajras,
to Samantabhadra of great intelligence,
to Arthaprabha, Kṣitigarbha, and the others,
with utmost reverence I pay homage.
To Ākāśa-lokeśvara,
and to Jagaddhara, the supreme and holy,
with nondual reverence I pay homage.
To the stainless bodies, pure by their own nature,
the goddesses of garlands, song, and dance,
of supreme conduct, delightful to behold, I pay homage.
To the goddesses of incense, flowers, lamps, and perfume,
supreme in form and pleasing to the mind,
I bow with devotion and pay homage.
To the supreme lord of the hosts of knowledge-holders,
the one who subdues the vajra-rākṣasas,
the wrathful kings, fathers and mothers with their retinues,
I praise with reverence and pay homage.

Then the retinue, together with the oceans of Victors, said, "Excellent! Excellent! A la la ho!" and gave their consent.

Then they conferred empowerment, saying: Noble bodhisattva, great being, you have made the great offering to all, whose nature is nectar, as the first of the wise. Moreover, you have offered to yourself by yourself. From here onward, the deities of wisdom dissolved the samādhi, the awakening mind, into the great nectar; made it unapparent in the expanse of equality; and remained hidden in Mahāsukha.

Then the Blessed One, glorious Samantabhadra, and the Sugatas of the five families addressed the noble Mañjuśrī: "To you, foremost in the wisdom of the bodhisattvas, we now declare this Dharma of ours in condensed form. Mañjuśrī Vajra, the Dharma is as follows:

Emaho! Arisen from the secret,
supreme lamp dispelling the darkness of ignorance,
hero, I take refuge in you.
Apply yourself with earnest diligence."

Thus it was said. And further: Mañjuśrī, the meaning of the Dharma of this nectar is like fire. Beginning small, no more than a spark, it grows great and burns even the mountains of existence. Likewise, this nectar is like the sun. The great rays of the sun, in Jambudvīpa, first illuminate the eight great kings of mountains. After that they illuminate the encircling ranges. After that they illuminate the great mountains. After that they illuminate the dark mountains. After that they illuminate the high snow peaks. After that they illuminate the low-lying lands of this Jambudvīpa. The great rays of the sun have no taking up or rejecting; they do not conceptualize and do not think. Nor do they discriminate in any way, nor do they think.

The rays of the sun, the vajra Dharma, and Mañjuśrī neither diminish nor increase. In the same way, in the consciousness of sentient beings, likewise, there is no diminishing or increasing. There is no distinguishing, no producing, and no reducing. In the same way, because this Dharma of nectar is taught in an inconceivable expanse, it is said that this great nectar is taught as the inconceivable from the very beginning.

Likewise, this scripture of the great nectar Dharma is unborn, unceasing, and free of concept; it is without name or sign, and it abides pervading the unabiding. Likewise, this great nectar Dharma abides pervading all, like the rays of the sun. For the fortunate, the five families pervade the five elements, and the five elements themselves are the great grove of the five mothers of the families. The five mothers and the five fathers are likewise pure from the beginning, and they arise like the light of the sun.

Then the Blessed One spoke to the oceans of Victors of the retinue. Then the Blessed One pervaded the oceans of Victors of the retinue.

Then it pervaded the gods and nāgas of the world of existence, together with Brahmā, the lord of the world. Then, together with the human bodhisattvas, step by step, it pervaded and rose like the sun in the sky. This nectar of compassion should likewise be made to rise like the sun in the three realms of existence.

Mañjuśrī, in the great thousand-fold world system of a trichiliocosm, to offer bodies beyond conceivable number to as many buddha fields as there are atoms is of less merit than to hold this great nectar upon one's body; that is the greater merit. Mañjuśrī, compared with wholly purifying the buddha fields of the three times, to bind this nectar about one's neck does not fall short of it even by a hundred-thousandth part. Mañjuśrī, the purpose of honoring with the crown of the head and prostrating to buddhas as numerous as the sands of the Ganges, for as many eons, is this: the merit of honoring this great nectar with the crown of one's head is the honoring of all the buddhas. Mañjuśrī, compared with working for the benefit of sentient beings for a thousand eons, by this great nectar one benefits beings in a single moment, and so leads even the world of the eight hells onto the path of liberation. Thus he spoke.

Then the Blessed One spoke in verse.

The nature of the great nectar
Has no color, no shape, and no sign.
It has no cessation and no division.
It has no root and no support.
There is nothing to be said of the great nectar.
It overcomes the poison of the afflictions and the four māras.
The stainless, pure awakening mind,
Of definitive meaning: bow to that.

Then the noble Mañjuśrī praised the Blessed One and this scriptural transmission of the sacred Dharma nectar, three times over.

The defining mark of space is buddhahood;
Being without defining mark, it is like space.
Without action, it is liberation.
I prostrate to the supreme nectar.

Like the moon in water, it is free of stain;
It has wholly abandoned all conceptual thought.
Without grasping at a self, utterly pure:
I prostrate to the victorious nectar.

Equal to the dharmadhātu of the three times,
It frees one from the māras and the three lower realms.
Without taking up and without letting go,
The stainless, pure awakening mind:
I prostrate to the supreme nectar.

The cause that brings forth the buddhas of the three times,
The glory that leads the beings of the three realms,
It purifies the obscurations of the six classes of beings:
I prostrate to the victorious nectar.

Having spoken thus, he declared that the vessel and essence, outer and inner, are gathered in the pill medicine, the rasāyana, and abide as nectar, condensing them into scriptural transmission and oral instruction.

This is the scriptural transmission of the supreme great nectar. It is the meaning of the great commentary of the eighth great volume of the scriptural transmission, within the turning of the wheel from the Supreme Conceptual Thought, the great king of scriptural transmissions.
