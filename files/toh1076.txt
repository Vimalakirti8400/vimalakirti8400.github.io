The Noble Dhāraṇī for Circumambulating the Supports of the Jewels (Āryapradakṣiṇaratnatrayā-nāma-dhāraṇī)

The Noble Dhāraṇī for Circumambulating the Supports of the Jewels, known in Sanskrit as the Ārya Pradakṣāratnatrayā Nāma Dhāraṇī, is a very brief incantation text that also closes under the simpler name The Dhāraṇī of Circumambulation. After a homage to the Buddha Śākyamuni, it presents a single short mantra addressed to the Three Jewels of the ten directions and the three times, calling for purification of all wrongdoing. It then explains the benefits of reciting it: a single recitation is said to equal prostrating to and circumambulating the Three Jewels everywhere and in all times, purifying wrongdoing accumulated since beginningless time, hastening the attainment of buddhahood, and protecting the reciter from enemies, obstructors, illnesses, and harmful spirits. The work is Toh 1076 in the Kangyur, where it belongs among the many short dhāraṇī texts gathered in that collection's section devoted to such incantations.

In the language of India: ārya pradakṣāratnatrayā nāma dhāraṇī

In the language of Tibet: The Noble Dhāraṇī for Circumambulating the Supports of the Jewels.

Homage to the Blessed One, the Tathāgata, the arhat, the perfectly and completely awakened buddha Śākyamuni.

oṃ namo daśadika trikāla sarva ratna trayāya mama pradakṣā supradakṣā sarva bāpaṃ piśodhani svāhā

Reciting this dhāraṇī a single time amounts to having prostrated to and circumambulated the Three Jewels of the ten directions and the three times. All the wrongdoing accumulated from beginningless time will be purified. One will swiftly attain the awakening of a buddha. Enemies and obstructors will find no opportunity to harm one. One will be freed from all illnesses and grahas.

The Dhāraṇī of Circumambulation is complete.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The final word is written སྷོ, which makes no sense here. It is evidently a slip for སོ, the usual closing particle, and is translated as "is complete."
