Dhāraṇī of Avalokiteśvara for Retention of What Is Heard and Remembered

The Learning of a Hundred Ślokas is a brief dhāraṇī text, a short ritual incantation for strengthening memory and learning. After paying homage to the Three Jewels and to the noble bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara, the great being endowed with great compassion, it gives a single dhāraṇī that invokes illumination, purification, the fulfillment of beings' wishes, and the retention of what is heard and remembered. It then sets out a simple rite. For seven days the practitioner recites the dhāraṇī seven times over a tooth-stick of apamārga wood and chews it each morning, which is said to enable one to learn a hundred verses a day, and after six months to retain whatever one hears. The work is Toh 1042 in the Kangyur, where it belongs among the collected dhāraṇī texts. Its Sanskrit and Tibetan titles are not given in the material shown here.

Homage to the Three Jewels!
Homage to the bodhisattva, the great being, endowed with great compassion, the noble Avalokiteśvara!

tadyathā avalokini valokini prabhālokini jīvahapariśodhani dhidaniyagame āśāpariśodhani sarvasatvāmanorathe kāyasiddhe śrutidhāraṇī smṛtidhāraṇī svāhā

The rite: for a period of seven days, recite this seven times over a tooth-stick of apamārga wood, and chew it each morning. One will then learn a hundred ślokas each day. If one does this for six months, one will retain whatever one hears.

The Learning of a Hundred Ślokas is complete.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

Apamārga is the plant Achyranthes aspera, whose twigs were used in India as tooth-sticks. The verb rendered "chew" literally means "eat," but with a tooth-stick it refers to chewing.
