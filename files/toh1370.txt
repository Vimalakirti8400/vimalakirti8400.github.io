The Accomplishment of the Eye of Gnosis (Jñānacakṣusādhana)

The Accomplishment of the Eye of Gnosis, known in Sanskrit as the Jñānacakṣusādhana and in Tibetan as Yeshé Chen Drubpa, is a short verse instruction on the completion-stage practice of the Kālacakra tradition, attributed in its colophon to the master Kālapāda and translated into Tibetan by the Indian paṇḍita Vibhūticandra himself. Drawing on the Kālacakra tantra, it teaches how to cultivate the five eyes, the flesh eye, the divine eye, the Dharma eye, the wisdom eye and the buddha eye, through the night and day yogas of gazing into darkness and the open sky, the signs that arise from smoke and mirage to the drop, breath control, and resting in the clear light. It goes on to connect these visions with the three bodies and the five gnoses of a buddha, and with the dissolution of the elements at death, the intermediate state and rebirth, closing with a lineage of transmission through Sūryadharma, Ratnaśrībhadra and Śākyaśrībhadra. It is Toh 1370 in the Tengyur, where it belongs to the section of commentaries on the tantras.

In the language of India: jñān cakṣu sādhana nāma. In the language of Tibet: The Accomplishment of the Eye of Gnosis.

I pay homage to the glorious Vajrasattva.

Perfectly complete in the three bodies, in qualities and activities,
with the five unobscured eyes, acting for the benefit of beings:
to the body of the glorious deity I pay homage.

From the glorious Kālacakra, the Supreme First,
the utterly secret ḍākinī teaching, the accomplishment of the five eyes,
free of obscurity, I write here clearly.

In a charnel ground, or a pleasing place well covered by a roof,
having made abundant offerings, outer, inner and secret,
having bathed, settled on one's seat, endowed with the four abodes of Brahmā,
seated cross-legged, with the senses opened wide,
gazing intently with the gaze of the Sage:
mirage, smoke, fireflies, a lamp, the sky,
the moon, the sun, Rāhula and lightning appear;
unobstructed appearances and whatever else arise,
beyond all telling: one sees the ground of the flesh eye.

Through the practice of breath control, purify grasped and grasper.
With the vajra-crown gaze and the key points of the body,
the mind set on the sky, the eyes unblinking,
having entered the vajra path, from the sky
there appear smoke, mirage, the firefly insect,
a lamp, a blaze, the moon and the sun,
Rāhula, lightning and the drop.
These are the appearances of the dregs arising in full.

Remaining settled, through the yoga of the sky,
white and yellow, red and green,
and blue: the lights of the buddhas dawn.
The great light shines forth everywhere,
and one sees the circle of light, the realm of Akaniṣṭha.
Through the yoga of the two reflections,
within that, reflections without objects:
one sees many enjoyment bodies.
The perfect buddhas, shimmering,
are seen clearly with the divine eye.

The single eye of gnosis, stainless,
as the Blessed One sets forth in the Samāja:
through the practice of breath control, purify grasping.
Once stable, from the vajra path
one should project it into the element of space.
The five superknowledges will be attained,
and a maṇḍala of light will dawn.

Within the dhūtī, hold the clear light:
one knows the reality of nonconceptual mind,
one sees the reality of the vajra body,
one becomes skilled in the Dharma of secret mantra.
At the gate of breath control, hold the clear light:
one knows the reality of the moving winds,
one will see the colors of the five winds,
and the activities of pacifying, enriching and the rest come under one's control.
Upon appearances, hold the clear light:
relying on the subtle light,
through being introduced to the great, coarse light,
within this nonconceptuality saṃsāra and nirvāṇa appear.
The conventional phenomena of cause and effect
will be seen with the Dharma eye.

Lamp of beings, lamp of gnosis,
the unobscured Dharma eye.
Knowing general and specific characteristics
is fully attained through the wisdom eye.
By this one accomplishes the buddha eye:
relying on breath and its control,
one will see the nonconceptual dharmakāya.
Through vajra yoga one sees the enjoyment body;
in nondual pure appearance one sees the emanation body.

Nonconceptual clarity is the mirror-like gnosis;
from the nonconceptual state the lights are discerned each in its own way;
the manifold, empty appearance is the gnosis of equality;
through complete purity it is the gnosis that accomplishes activities;
knowing phenomena as unborn is the gnosis of the dharmadhātu.
Seeing the five gnoses, one is equal to all the buddhas.

As for the buddha spontaneously present by nature:
in brief, the six aggregates
are renowned as the six buddhas.
The vajra sense bases, too,
called the Pure White One, Tārā and the rest,
are renowned as fire, wind and the others.
Of the outer, inner and secret maṇḍalas,
the buddhas abide in the realm of great bliss.

On this the Blessed One made a pledge:
"Having wholly abandoned all thoughts,
examine it thoroughly for a single day.
If confidence has not arisen,
then those words of mine are false."

Precious is one who holds the practice of the whispered lineage.
From birth, the generation stage,
one enters death, the completion stage.
As the elements dissolve in sequence,
when strength dissolves outwardly and capacity dissolves inwardly,
the welcome of the five lights, the eyes and the rest, arises.
Hold the essence of the gnosis of the union of the five families:
buddhahood arises in the essence of the clear-light dharmakāya.

What is called death is conceptual thought;
it is being led to the abode of the sky-farers.
From death, the clear-light dharmakāya,
the enjoyment body dawns in the intermediate state.
From appearance, increase and attainment,
one has the fleshly form of one's former existence,
with all senses complete, moving without obstruction,
with inconceivable miraculous powers born of karma.
The four terrifying sounds and the three dreadful abysses
appear as the drop grows unstable.
Through the decline of the dregs, deluded appearances rise up as sounds;
through the decline of the five lights, one is born in the abodes of the six classes of beings.

Know appearance and the rest as the dharmakāya;
know deluded appearances as one's own radiance.
Knowing there is nothing to fear or dread, like a person whose work is done,
free of elaboration, like space, born from the father, the dharmakāya,
from the light of the refined essence one sees the face of the enjoyment body too.
The five afflictions, purified, dawn as the five gnoses;
undefiled bliss cannot be contained even by the sky.
Birth is complete awakening as the emanation body.
Method and wisdom, the two, and body, speech and mind,
having traversed the ten grounds, through the secret door
come forth and will act for the benefit of beings.

Even if one knows Kālacakra, without this instruction
the toil is meaningless, and the fruit of buddhahood will not come.
Though the outer calculations are clear, the meaning of the essence is not.
Therefore I have written this for the benefit of all fortunate beings.

The Method for Accomplishing the Five Eyes, from the instructions of Kālacakra, the Supreme First, composed by the master Kālapāda, is complete.

Kālapāda heard it from the protectors of the three families, Great Compassion (Avalokiteśvara) and the others. He taught it to the gnosis ḍākinī Sūryadharma; she to Ratnaśrībhadra; he to Śākyaśrībhadra; and he to the paṇḍita Mahāvibhūticandra of Jagaddala in eastern India, who translated it himself.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

"The Supreme First" renders dam pa dang po, the Paramādibuddha, the name of the Kālacakra root tantra. In the line "the mind set on the sky, the eyes unblinking," the Tibetan as written reads "sky, sentient beings, the blinking of the eye"; it is read as "mind" and "unblinking," following the parallel Kālacakra verse on the night yoga (mig mi 'dzums). Rāhula is the Tibetan name for the eclipse planet Rāhu. "The Pure White One" (dkar dangs) is probably the goddess Pāṇḍarā, but the form is unusual. "The Samāja" may mean the Guhyasamāja, though the quoted phrase "the single eye of gnosis, stainless" is also found in the Chanting of the Names. The Sanskrit ratna śrai bhadra in the colophon is taken as Ratnaśrībhadra, and Jāgadhala as the monastery of Jagaddala.
