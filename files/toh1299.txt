The Sequence of the Torma Rite (Balikarmakrama)

The Sequence of the Torma Rite, known in Sanskrit as Balikarmakrama and in Tibetan as Tormé Lé kyi Rimpa, is a short ritual manual in verse on the offering of torma, the ritual cakes presented to enlightened deities, Dharma protectors, ḍākinīs, direction guardians, nāgas and spirits. Opening with homage to Vajrasattva, it explains that the torma is given to complete the two accumulations of merit and wisdom, to clear away old karmic debts and to pacify present obstacles, and it sets out seven stages of the rite: the requisites, their preparation, their arrangement, blessing, offering, empowerment and the pleasing of the guests. Working within the practice of Hevajra, it describes the vessels and substances, the visualizations and mantras of offering, the protective boundary, the dismissal of the guests, the torma for hungry ghosts, and the final carrying out of the great torma to a crossroads or charnel ground at midnight. Its colophon attributes it to the great master Ratnavajra of Kashmir. It is Toh 1299 in the Tengyur, where it belongs among the tantra commentaries as a ritual text of the Highest Yoga Tantra class.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The five syllables na go ku da ha abbreviate the five meats listed in the next line (human, cow, dog, elephant, horse), here rendered with glang taken as elephant. In the list of five nectars, "camphor" is the usual tantric code for semen; "crystal" (shel) is rendered literally and its intended referent in this list is uncertain. In "taking it up with the lotus mudrā, offer it", the text reads dbus ("center"), taken as a slip for dbul ("offer"), which the context requires.  The posture "one leg extended and one drawn in" renders brkyang bskum, the dancing or archer's stance. The movements in the dispatching rite (setting the ankles together, striding to the left) are rendered as closely as the terse wording allows.
