The Root Tantra of the Assembly of Wrathful Mantras, the Vajra (Vajramantrabhīruśāntimāratantra Nāma)

The Root Tantra of the Assembly of Wrathful Mantras, the Vajra, known in Sanskrit as the Vajramantra Bhirusanti Mara Tantra Nāma and in Tibetan as Drak ngak düpa dorje tsawé gyü, is a tantra of the Nyingma tradition that opens with the Blessed One, the Great Vajra Holder, dwelling atop Mount Meru in a splendid palace. In union with the Great Mother, sovereign lady of the five elements, he sends out rays of light that stir the malevolent and arrogant beings of the world-systems, setting the stage for teachings on wrathful mantra practice. The text carries a colophon-style framing in which the master Padma, that is Padmasambhava, is said to have given this counsel to his disciple Langpel Gyi Senge. It is Toh 843 in the Kangyur, where it belongs to the tantra section and, more specifically, to the collection of tantras transmitted in the Nyingma lineage.

Thus the master Padma spoke and gave this counsel to his disciple Langpel Gyi Senge.

In the language of India: Vajramantra Bhirusanti Mara Tantra Nāma. In the language of Tibet: The Root Tantra of the Assembly of Wrathful Mantras, the Vajra. Homage to the Blessed One, the Lord of Power and Might.

Thus did I hear at one time. The Blessed One, the Great Vajra Holder himself, dwelt on the summit of the great Mount Meru, in the supreme place of willow leaves, in a great and splendidly adorned palace. There he entered into a state of non-dual equipoise with the Great Mother, the Sovereign Lady of the five elements. From his body he emitted rays of light of many kinds, which shook and stirred every place in the world-systems where there were the malevolent, the arrogant, the splendid, and the fierce.

Moved as if by the force of the wind, these beings came rushing in, together with their chieftains, their retinues, and their servants. They were these: the Great Black Yama, Lord of Life, together with his consort; the Great Black Māra with his breath of fire, together with his consort, the Black Demoness Dripping Blood; the King of the Rākṣasas, the Black Slayer, together with his consort; the great yakṣa Pūrṇabhadra, together with his consort, the flesh-eating yakṣiṇī Single Braid; the king of the gods, the Great Lord Īśvara; the lord of the rudras, the Black Liberator; the mighty Mu king, the great monarch; Kāla the Black, lord of the gods of the outer paths; the great Viṣṇu, the All-Pervading; the youthful and fierce lord of the nāgas; the great Licchavi king of the gods; the great yakṣa Hand-Wanderer; the great Self, the Beautiful Vajra; and the great nāga-māra, Red Yamśud. Besides these there gathered, in inconceivable numbers, gods, nāgas, asuras, flesh-eaters, hungry ghosts, hosts of elemental spirits, and terrifying hosts of messengers.

Then the Blessed One, knowing how to subdue this retinue of the great evildoers, manifested the maṇḍala of a great charnel ground.

The spheres of the sky, appearing as forms, were utterly terrifying.
The sounds were harsh, fierce, and savage.
The smells were foul, rotting, and putrid.
The tastes were sour and burning, and they sickened.
The touch was hot and rough, the conduct cruel.
The fire of great hatred blazed,
pride packed the cliffs with skeletons,
delusion spread a thick darkness,
and the great rivers of blood of desire roared.
The wind of jealousy surged wildly.
In the great charnel's pleasures
tigers, leopards, beasts of prey, jackals, and wolves,
and flocks of vultures, crows, and winged creatures
devoured flesh, drank blood, and crushed bones.
There were corpses, rotting and putrid flesh,
sand, and what was crumbled and shattered,
what was eaten, scattered, and strewn about,
what was torn, oozing, and ruined,
and there was quarreling, laughter, and mockery.
Terrifying, malevolent, and ferocious,
they manifested the playful pleasures of the place.
All of them bore minds of malice,
and in deeds of the body, killing, cutting, and chopping,
they cried out in harsh and violent voices.
By the fierce ascetic conduct of wrath
they subdued and destroyed all without remainder,
and were wholly set upon wrathful suppression.
Though it was seen and appeared in this way,
it appeared only at the conventional level,
and not the slightest harm was done
even to a single hair of a single sentient being.
They move at ease in their own conceptions.
This is the very reality that is certain.

This is the first chapter, the introductory chapter, which shows the great miracle.

Then all the buddhas,
by the king who holds the vajra,
by the victors of the three times together with their spiritual sons,
from time without beginning,
in order to confer empowerment upon the great Lord of Secrets,
the protector of the teaching without exception,
the yakṣa Vajrapāṇi, holder of the vajra,
together with his five hundred companions,

From before the great chief of the Pratyekas,
he rose up from his own seat
and wrought a manifestation of wrathful conduct.
From the seed syllable abiding in his heart,
all the ten directions, to the very limits of space,
were violently stirred by a dark green wind.
An ocean of blood churned and trembled,
and from the ground, packed with flesh and bones,
the fire of the aeon, Mount Meru ablaze, blazed forth.
A blazing palace of bhandha,
fierce and terrifying,
which even the mightiest cannot approach,
the body that fulfills all activity without remainder
pervades the outer, the inner, the edge and the center.

For those who hate the teaching,
he spread out their aggregates as a footstool.
With the three pure seats of light
he made the supreme and great body,
and pressing down upon the summit the edge-dwelling knowledge-holders,
eighteen thousand pure appearances shone forth.
With his hands he slew the eight arrogant gods.
In right and left hands he held bhandha and citta.
With the first sign of the power of his mind he seized them.
Unchanging in compassion, dark blue in color,
his feet pressed down upon the fearless knowledge-holders,
subduing the four māras together with their directions.
In order to draw all beings without exception under his power,
his dark tawny hair blazes at the peak of existence.
With blazing eyes he gazes upon the three realms.
With flared nostrils he inhales the three existences.
The wind-sound of his nostrils fills the thousandfold world.
With sharp, glorious fangs he liberates the cruel.
His coiled tongue strikes the palate with a fierce sound.
By the light of his wrathful brow he draws all under his power.
He is richly adorned with heroic attire.
In his body the guardian deities are complete.

The mistress of the great elements,
who pleases that mighty one,
has three heads, six arms, and four feet.
In her right hands are the curved knife, the trident, and nectar;
in her left are bhandha, the great field, and the prosperity vessel.
The chief lord, assuming the guise, is most elegant.
From the play that brings delight,
manifestations that subdue stream forth.
In all the world-realms of the ten directions,
all the lords of wrathful mantras,
who bear the fierce and cruel aspect,
together with their own retinues,
appeared each one in their dwellings and supports,
bowing and trembling, without any freedom.

Om vajra sarva duṣṭānām ekaya mama bhera bhagṛhṇa hūṃ phaṭ.

Māras and Yamas and rākṣasas,
yakṣas, gods, and holders of wrong views,
the great Mu-rje and those of the outer direction,
those who work cruelty and travel in the sky,
animals, those who move crawling, and those who hold bodies,
the arrogant and the magical emanators,
all who roam the borderlands and frontier abodes,
the dwellings of gods, māras, humans, and non-humans,
in the snowy lands, scattered and burned,
their karmic bodily powers drawn out,
all of them without exception,
as if stirred by a violent wind,
trembled greatly and stood before him.
Though the Lord of Compassion regarded them,
with the body of the great, fierce, terrifying one,
with the sound of his voice speaking fierce secret words,
he commanded them, to their complete satisfaction:

"E ma! You lords of fierce mantras of all world-realms,
you arrogant ones,
without transgressing the command of the former great vajra-holders
and of myself,
you, together with your retinues,
quickly show in this terrifying place
your several bodies and imputed names,
your families, supports, and deeds,
your hosts, your guise, and your enjoyments.
If you transgress this,
by the pledge of my life,
your body, mind, and life
shall be destroyed forever without remainder."

Having spoken thus, he also spoke these assemblies of fierce mantras:

Om sarva duṣṭāntaka hana daha paca hūṃ phaṭ.
Yama rāja mṛtagasya mama kṣetra māraya hriṃ hriṃ jaḥ jaḥ.
Duḥprag rāja dugdunma tiṃ māraya hriṃ hriṃ jaḥ jaḥ.
Rakṣa ragmo mama rati ka māraya hriṃ hriṃ jaḥ jaḥ.
Yakṣa apasmāra rakṣa miruśampa māraya hriṃ hriṃ jaḥ jaḥ.
Mahādeva īśvara rāja māraya hriṃ hriṃ jaḥ jaḥ.
Mātaṅga rudra kāla māraya hriṃ hriṃ jaḥ jaḥ.
Mun mun paca citta māraya hriṃ hriṃ jaḥ jaḥ.
Tīrthika mate mahādeva māraya hriṃ hriṃ jaḥ jaḥ.
Biṣṭu pitra rokti ja māraya hriṃ hriṃ jaḥ jaḥ.

Nāgarāja kumāra caṇḍa māraya hriṃ hriṃ jaḥ jaḥ.
Rājā bhūlonga esa yamāraya hriṃ hriṃ jaḥ jaḥ.
Mahā khulo sabho māraya hriṃ hriṃ jaḥ jaḥ.
Vajra sādhu. So aṃ ca māraya hriṃ hriṃ jaḥ jaḥ.
Abhiśutraṃ li māraya hriṃ hriṃ jaḥ jaḥ.
Nṛ hur thumsa jaḥ. Śatruṃ māraya rbad.

Having uttered these words, the wrathful, blazing body of the Blessed One once more displayed itself in a terrifying form. All the world-realms of the great trichiliocosm, the buddha-fields of all the buddhas, were filled with a great and dreadful sound, with light and blazing lightning, and with the fierce force of wind. The arrogant ones of the ten directions, such as the māras and the Lords of Death, together with their own retinues, gathered completely. Laying down the pride of their respective classes, they came near into the very presence of the Blessed One and strove diligently to carry out his command.

This is the second chapter, "Gathering Under Power through Great Blessing," from the Root Tantra of the Gathering of Wrathful Mantras.

Then, before the Blessed One, there came swiftly the king of the māras of that world-realm, named Fire-Breath. He was black in color, with fire blazing from his mouth, holding a club and a black noose of the māras in his hands, and clad in a garment of field-hide. With him came his consort, the dark demoness named Dripping Blood, holding a club and a fresh skull-cup, wearing a garment of human skin. Both rode a black māra horse, bear and lion at once, and they arrived together with the host of gods of the family of māras.

In the same way came the Lord of Death, called Yama. Black in color, he held a skull and a curved knife. His consort, Yama's Devourer, held a curved knife and a skull in her hands. The two, clad in damp field-hide, rode a water buffalo, and together with their host of male and female Yamas, they hastened swiftly, threatening those who oppose.

The flesh-eating rākṣasas also came, father and mother, black in color and clad in field-hide. In the right hand they held a sword and a curved knife, and in the left a human head and a blood-filled skull. Riding tigers and lions, they roamed, and with their host of rākṣasas they swiftly took their places.

The yakṣa Pūrṇabhadra, together with his consort, the flesh-eater with a single braid, were dark blue, and they rode fast horses. Clad in field-hide, they held in their two hands a sword, black silk, and field-hide, and with their host of yakṣas they took their places.

These are the commanders of the lords of wrathful mantras. In the places of the east, south, west, and north, they bow to their chief and take their seats.

The one called the Great Lord (Maheśvara), by the mantras of the worldly gods, was white, holding a banner and a crystal knife, clothed in white silk, and riding a sky-ram. With his arrogant retinue of god-māras, he came swiftly and took his seat in the eastern direction.

The one called Black Liberator was black in color, holding offering cakes and a poison vessel. Riding a black bear and clad in field-hide, he took his seat in the southern direction with his host.

The one called Black Rmuje held black silk and a noose. Wearing a black felt cloak and riding a rmu horse, he took his seat in the western direction with his own host.

The great black tīrthika also held a khaṭvāṅga and a trident. Clad in human skin and riding a buffalo, he took his seat in the north with his own host.

The one called Viṣṇu, or Rokti, was dark blue, holding a club and a weapon. Clothed in black clouds and riding a blue dragon, he took his seat in the upper direction together with the planetary māras and their retinue.

The nāga king called Without Life-Force wore black clothing and a garment of snakeskin, and held a vase and a tortoise. Riding a water-monster, together with his host of nāgas, he took his seat in the lower direction.

In addition there were the nine-armed Libi, Thesé the chief, and Yamshu, the lords of their respective mantras. They were white, yellow, and red in color, holding crystal knives, with their nine arms spread out. They held long red silk spears in their hands, and, riding white horses, vultures, goat-born steeds, and red horses, they were clad in field-hide.

Each with their own hosts and attendants, they took their seats, abiding at the four gates of the directions.

In addition there were gods, nāgas, and yakṣas, the mamo goddesses, the Lords of Death, the rākṣasas, and the māras; the Rudras, the non-Buddhists, the gongpo and thé spirits, the ging and mu spirits, the planets, and the gyendren and men spirits; the proud gods and demons of the visible and the existent world; and the retinues of the great chieftains of the hunt. They were the outer, the inner, the secret, the very secret, and the wrathful ones; the binders and the clingers, the wounders; the workers of deeds, the butchers, and the Thorshong; the Chedrak, the Tsal, and the mamo; the Duntip, the Tongtrug, and the life-takers; the Lenzhing, the mighty ones, and others besides.

In accordance with the command of the Blessed One, the Vajra-Holder, and in obedience to it, they displayed their own powers at the unobstructed great charnel ground, with trembling and swift force, and they stood listening to his command, praising his words, and following after him.

Seated in this way, those proud lords of the worldly realm, endowed with the power of mantra, displayed before the Blessed One the distinctions of their retinues, their classes, their might, and their enjoyments, and then spoke these words: Blessed One, great Vajra-Holder, we who dwell in the realms of the world are a malevolent host, wicked and hateful. We are filled with thoughts of harming, of violence, of livelihood and quarrel, and we are unable to give rise to loving-kindness, compassion, or a mind of benefit. We therefore request that you declare to us teachings that accord with our own realms, functions, classes, deeds, supports, powers, and distinctions of authority, teachings that are fierce and endowed with strength.

This is the third chapter, "The Arrangement of the Maṇḍala of the Assembly," from the Root Tantra of the Gathering of Wrathful Mantras.

Then the Vajra-Holder, displaying the greatness of his body and urging them on with the essence of his speech, made the intention of his mind his chief concern, and spoke to the assembled retinue of the proud and powerful ones of the world:

Hūṃ! Great hosts of the proud ones of the world, listen and act according to my command.

I am the dharmakāya, great bliss, spontaneously accomplished from the beginning, uncontrived. In the body of appearance there is no real thing. It is the body of wisdom, the supreme of bodies.

I am the great power of mantra speech, free from the sounds of conventional expression from the beginning. There is no clinging to spoken words. It is unceasing speech, the glory of speech.

I am the supreme mind of truth, free from any sign from the beginning, perfectly without it. Non-dual, the mind is without effort. It is unborn mind, the glory of mind.

I am the great gathering of qualities, spontaneously accomplished and beyond thought. Without aspiration, every need arises, the basis from which great accomplishments are produced.

I am the source of enlightened activity, whose very nature is complete diligence. Pacifying, enriching, empowering, and wrathful, the many kinds of benefit for others are spontaneously fulfilled.

You too, proud mantra lords of the world, great chiefs and masters, are in no way different, in mind and wisdom, from me and the buddhas of the three times. You are not distinct from them in speech, and in body you are not to be regarded separately. Your qualities are fulfilled in equality, and your activity is one, accomplishing what is held in common.

From beginningless time until now, through the cause and effect of affliction and karma, you have ripened into aggregates that take on a bad rebirth. Your bodies, speech, and minds, puffed up with pride and arrogance, possess immeasurable strength and power. Though you have become endowed with greatness, ferocity, and sovereignty, you have not realized the meaning of the genuinely definitive. You have not grasped ultimate truth, your minds do not incline toward the virtue free of elaboration, you have not found the power of the Victor, self-awareness, the play of the unborn expanse has not dawned for you, you have not gained the confidence of the abode of great bliss, you are not intoxicated by the taste of the signless essence, you have not seen the form of the body of the nature of reality, and you only half-meditate on the crude surface of wisdom.

Therefore, sons of good family, realize fully your own abiding nature. Do not cling to the body as a self that truly exists.

Do not speak of it in words, and do not conceive of it in the mind as a real thing. Place, region, family, lineage, troop and army, the supports, the substances, the enjoyments, and one's share of power: none of these is to be grasped as "I" or "mine." Whether common or separate, or one, all of these are utterly empty, and emptiness itself is just these. Apart from this there is nothing else, and apart from other things, this too does not exist. It is unborn and unceasing, without defining mark. It has not been wiped clean, nor added to, nor corrupted, nor fabricated, nor altered, nor has it come into being. It is unborn, not accomplished, and not explained. That great meaning arises from oneself alone. Outer, inner, before, after, above, below, the directions and intermediate directions, saṃsāra and nirvāṇa, body and mind: in none of these is it found, and it does not exist even in this. In flesh, blood, skin, and bone, in the heart, the lungs, and the other organs, in the sense faculties and the limbs, that sublime great bliss does not dwell. Nor does it dwell anywhere apart from these. What has no established dwelling is spoken of as dwelling. If you hold that it has a dwelling, realize it there. What is it like? What is it? How is it? From where has it come, and to where has it gone? Where does it sit, and what does it do? Realize fully its shape, color, name, and mark, and its family and element. Looking at it with this very thing, there is no seeing, and that is the looking. By whom is meditation done, on what, and where? That which is free of meditation is called meditation. Who acts in general, and with what? Acting without abiding in any of the nine modes of conduct is great conduct. From whom, by whom, is it attained, and what is accomplished? The unaccomplished, self-abiding is the result. The complete perfection of all qualities, without remainder and in every aspect, is sovereign power. Pacifying, enriching, subjugating, and bringing under one's power are the enlightened activities. It is perfect in itself and accomplished by itself. Decisive certainty, instruction, the authoritative, the ultimate, repetition, the passed beyond and the not passed beyond, the teacher, explaining, listening, and compiling, and the place, time, qualities, and faults: all these are accomplished through realizing one's own suchness.

Thus he spoke.

This is the fourth chapter, the chapter that closely teaches the place of ultimate truth, from the Root Tantra, the Assembly of Wrathful Mantras.

Then the Vajra-Holder spoke these words of great import.

The heart of the meaning, the nature of reality, never arisen and unborn, is realized as meaning free of the extremes of subject and object. The Rudra who is puffed up and tormenting through the conceptual thought that grasps at "I" and self, if not liberated, is a fault. Skillful means, wisdom, and compassion, these three, in harmony and complete accord, are what the sons use to liberate him. Without realizing that ultimate meaning, those who act as though things were real fall into saṃsāra. When one acts with skill and realization, the noble ones are pleased and sentient beings are freed. The accumulations are completed and the three bodies are attained. Having seen the awareness of all, one reaches certainty. One begins the practice with fierce ascetic discipline, for the sake of bringing beings under one's power and taming them. The tantra gathering the activities of the world was taught by me and by the Teachers. In the charnel ground, the self-abiding dharmadhātu, the vidyādharas compile it for the sake of the Buddha's teaching. In the age of the degenerate times, a Dharma that depends on praise and establishment will appear in the guise of faults and qualities. Once those of evil deeds who destroy the teaching have been liberated by the power of wrathful mantra, one will come to attain perfect buddhahood. The maṇḍala is the support of those who dwell in it. Deities, mantras, and meditative absorptions, and the substances, become causes and conditions. The liṅga and the target are the supports of visualization. The name, bones, and "enlightenment-essence" are supports of the consciousness, and of the life-force and vitality that wander in saṃsāra. By summoning and drawing in, these are summoned. Messengers accomplish all actions. One increases the glory of mantras and substances, intoxicates with the substances for the rites of union, and paralyzes, strikes dumb, hurls weapons, and reduces to dust. One strikes at the vital point and draws out from the heart. One separates from the deity and cuts the root. The substance of the awakening mind is a drop of the essence of blood at the crown. The all-base, the size of a sesame seed, abides there. It resembles the root, the dwelling of the wind, like a peg or a fire-frog.

By gathering, burning, and cutting, the object of focus is made to be parted from its support. Its radiance and splendor are absorbed into oneself. For the one who is to be subdued through the four kinds of action, virtue and sin are equal in their essential nature. Those endowed with the view and the conduct take the field, purify it, and liberate it into the supreme realm beyond.

As for the consciousness in the intermediate state, through the outpouring and gathering back of Hūṃ and Phaṭ, one performs the actions of purifying and of transferring upward. One generates it as a child of the Victors of the ten directions. The mere remaining aggregates are thoroughly purified by means of the three supports. One shows the reflected image of the truth body. By this, one engages in the vast conduct of the supreme, unsurpassed emanations, the buddhas of the three times, and the bodhisattvas, the children of the Victors.

Thus the Blessed One declared. This is the fifth chapter, on the focusing of the meditative absorption of liberation, from the Root Tantra, the Assembly of Wrathful Mantras.

Then the Vajra Holder spoke this classification of rites. First, for this, there is the auspicious place: a charnel ground, an island in a lake, or the slope of a mountain, a place fierce and savage. There the practitioner who keeps the sacred bond, deeply learned and steadfast, a disciple fully accomplished, having first pleased the lama, possessing the strength of his personal deity, the Vajra Holder Heruka, being no different from one who is close to him, becomes one who has attained the proper measure. Having seen the signs of certain and genuine confirmation, he should apply himself with great effort.

At the site of maturation and fierce, auspicious ground, for those who are appointed to the rite, one prepares the support for focusing on the haughty ones, the chief and his retinue, in the proper measure, such as a fathom in size. From the center the spokes of the wheel are set, four, six, or eight in number. The intervening spaces are open toward the four directions, with four gates, and the wheel is adorned with garlands of skulls in three tiers. For the mantra practitioner, according to his own family, shape, color, ornaments, and signs, all of these are to be made complete.

For the haughty ones, the supports of the body are drawn, carved, or built, and set beautifully in their own places. The essence of speech is likewise. The supports of mind are their respective implements, or substances accumulated beforehand. The wheel of one's own mantra that has been accumulated, the corresponding forms, and the banners of life-force are drawn within as symbolic supports. Into each of these, the essence of the focus is compressed and placed within the citta. They become hidden and concealed supports.

In the northeast direction of that, in accordance with the tantra I explained before, one digs a pit, a cubit or a span deep, in the shape of the letter E, with four or eighty-two sides, and smears it with fierce and savage substances. Burning with fierceness is also done by this. The substances for setting it up are these: a cord-less ugly form, a skull cup, the wood of the lion tree and the golden tree, and limbs that have grown. They also include the glorious fierce phurba, black glass, black thunder, white giling, red-faced one, the unique moon, the srid tree, carakas, the pleasant-throated, and the bodies of land-dwellers and water-dwellers. These are adorned with the outer, inner, intermediate, and secret. The substances that please all in the great burning offering include beautiful forms, the sound of drums and flutes, the great fragrance of incense, flesh, blood, and taste, hot and rough touch, the essence of grains, and food offerings. The assembly of great flesh, sacred substances, and nectars is to be arranged with care and combined with diligence.

A hero, bold and dauntless, in accordance with my own earlier rites, pacifies the wrathful with wrath. Finally, he meditates on the five great elements that abide as fierce mantra elements. Visualize a mountain of blazing skeletons, or a fearsome charnel-ground mansion without measure, built from the three kinds of bhandha. It is filled with a mass of new and old tira. Red rakta streams down, and great fat swirls as smoke and billows. The sense organs, hearts, and essences dangle and writhe. Nectar, feces, and urine give off their stench. Limbs, hands, and feet are spread and strewn about. Skins and hides are stretched and flutter. In the four directions, the intermediate points, above and below, they hang down, contracted and extended, with banners and rag-garlands.

Banners and streamers flutter, with ritual daggers, folded cloths, and curtains, packed with kakamala, adorned with exquisitely beautiful ornaments. Such a blazing celestial mansion is taught as supreme in this mantra tradition. The lord of the arrogant, together with his consort, joins in union, and through that very purpose the nature of oneself is fully generated. Distinguish the radiating forth and the array of implements; in gathering back, rest in one's own abode. Through the characteristic and essential nature of whatever has taken form, and through the activity of the awakening mind, the one with his consort is brought to completion. As before, his activity too is carried out by the Victor on his own support for the hands. Visualize his retinue, swift in arrival, the māras, the Lords of Death, the rākṣasas, the fierce yakṣas, the nāgas and the gods, each with their own wives, as terrifying, just as I have described.

Those who abide at the four gates, the gods, the mātṛs, the kings, the gings, the btsan and 'gong spirits, the rmu, the non-Buddhists, together with their own hosts and armies, the entire assembly of the arrogant, whatever has been spoken of in this teaching, make clearly manifest in that very form. Confer empowerment of the family of wisdom upon my body, speech, and mind; bless, and seal them, and absorb into them the one who abides in his own nature. Accomplish the teaching through the perfect body. Prostrate, make offerings, and praise, and greatly please the deities, outwardly, inwardly, and secretly.

Gathering, joining, and liberating; abiding, lengthening, and sequence; generating and bringing down; the pleasing of the deities, by the spontaneously present, uncontrived abiding nature, practice the wrathful mantra's play without obstruction. What is applied to one's own rites is as follows: the māra's blow and the Lord of Death's belly, the rākṣasa's red and the yakṣa's sorcery, the god's punishment and the mātṛ's plague, the planet's cry and the nāga's poison, the cutting short of life and the closing of the pit, the beast's grin and the iron lock's fastening, illness, weapon-wounds, and possession, drinking poison, and a thunderbolt's falling, so fierce that there is no such thing as release. The māra attacks the life unexamined. The Lord of Death cuts with the wheel of saṃsāra. The rākṣasa shatters with the teeth of a sword. The yakṣa destroys with a thunderbolt of sky-iron. The god pierces with the spear of desire. The mātṛs stab like countless arrows. The planet-māras strike with mighty hail. The nāga, from beneath the earth, with swords and the like. This is the mantra that subdues and severs all.

In former times, this very world-system, relying on the aggregates and the afflictions, was intoxicated by the power of pride and arrogance.
