The Tantra of the Goddess, the Great Net of Illusion (Devī-jāla-mahāmāyā-tantra-nāma)

The Tantra Called the Goddess, the Great Net of Illusion (Sanskrit: Devī Jāla Mahāmāyā Tantra; Tibetan: Lhamo Gyuthrul Draba Chenmo zhes jawé Gyü) is a Buddhist tantra, a scripture presenting esoteric teachings and practices centered on a feminine deity associated with illusion and the interwoven "net" of manifestation. It opens with homage to the Blessed One, the Great Secret, and frames the revelation as a discourse in which the Blessed One, the Tathāgata, is present with an assembly of great yogīs and yoginīs, each bearing a symbolic name such as Wheel of Yoga and Gathered into Vajra Itself. In the Tibetan canon it is Toh 836 in the Kangyur, the collection of texts attributed to the Buddha, where it belongs to the Tantra section.

In the Indian language: Devī Jāla Mahāmāyā Tantra. In the Tibetan language: The Tantra called the Goddess, the Great Net of Illusion.

Homage to the Blessed One, the Great Secret.

Thus it was spoken. At that time the Blessed One, the Tathāgata, was present together with the great yogī who is equal to space, with the great yoginī, with the great one of supreme devotion, with the one who guards those who have devotion, with the great consort who protects and thoroughly guards, who stays close, with the great pacified one, and the great one who brings about peace, beautiful and radiant in color, endowed with knowledge and refuge. They are these: the chief called Wheel of Yoga; the great yogī called Gathered into Vajra Itself; the great yogī called Equanimity; the great yogī called the One Who Brings Under Power; and the yogī who thoroughly holds the manner of the full expanse. In whatever form the Teacher showed, they were endowed with four colors, and in the manner of sitting they wore the five-fold crown. Unobscured, not obscuring, not blocking what obscures, from the unobscured expanse, in the great, truly present celestial palace, by the great vajra manner of equality, with the body unimpeded, the Blessed One dwelt, his activity inconceivable.

Moreover, each one thus clearly distinct, the great equal one with the retinue, great in resources, abiding in the nature of holding the burden of all, unmistaken through reliance, and without any multiplicity of two, is the dhāraṇī of the great yoginī. They are: the Great Awakened Lady called Space; the Great Awakened Lady called Space-Holder; the Great Awakened Lady called Earth-Guardian; the Great Awakened Lady, the Great Moistener; the Great Awakened Lady called Dispeller of Arrogance; the Great Awakened Lady called Accomplisher of Action; the Great Awakened Lady called Holder of the Great Intense Attachment of Vajra, who also slays non-attachment; the Great Awakened Lady called Thorough Dispeller of Bewilderment; and the Great Awakened Lady called Wheel of Speech.

They too are thus: of a color like a most vivid blue lotus, and with sapphire, beryl, ruby, and emerald, and other substances praised, they dwell noble and radiant, with none unripened. These are the yoginī Samantabhadrā together with her retinue.

Moreover there is great reverence, the secret; great reverence, the manifold fragrances; and great reverence, the one who commands, who is called Lord of Secrets. There is the one who holds clarity, the one who becomes the abode of fragrance, and the one who holds the form that makes sound. There is the faculty that causes seeing, the faculty that causes hearing, the faculty of the great one who causes smelling, and the faculty of the great one who causes tasting. There is the one who thoroughly releases, the one who thoroughly conceives, the great sovereign who thoroughly holds, and the one who thoroughly accomplishes, together with the consort who never abandons her form.

There is the one who conceives what exists, the one who dispels what does not exist, the one who brings about the perception of victory, and the great one who thoroughly destroys cessation. Bound as non-dual through the action of manifest conduct, they dwell in the great celestial palace of the expanse of phenomena (dharmadhātu), the great equality.

Then the Blessed One, delighted in the celestial palace of the great secret yoginī, with the great gathering of wisdom of the unborn expanse, complete with every ornament, a maṇḍala like the full moon pervading without center or edge, complete with all jewels, with corners, sides, colors, and shapes of immeasurable extent, with inconceivable wisdom, adorned in splendid manner, richly decorated with the ornaments of the great wish-fulfilling jewel.

Bearing the lineage of the Victors through their various distinctions, possessed of the four gates of great magical display, the outer yogas of the vehicles become the Great Vehicle, together with the supreme of the great virtue and vice, the very great secret, at the center of the great lotus, beautified by the distinctions of the four colors of the tongue, it became the maṇḍala of the great secret, in the manner of the four directions and the three tiers.

This is the first chapter, "The Supreme Dispelling of Obscurations, the Maṇḍala of All the Tathāgatas."

Then the Blessed One entered into meditative absorption, the one that supremely dispels the torment of the great enjoyment. Delighted, with a great smile, to hold sway over the great passion, he caused the sky of the great secret yoginī to flourish with the color of the lotus of empowerment. By speaking "Oṃ surata sukhe," the inconceivable vajra family came to be, together with the consorts. Then he spoke this verse in full.

This is the second chapter, called "The Wheel of Dharma," the secret of the body, speech, and mind of all the Tathāgatas, the yogī.

Then the Blessed One, delighting in entering Samantabhadrī, the yoginī of great bliss, reflected on the great intimacy. And Samantabhadrī the yoginī, smiling, said:

Emaho, the great drop of the bodhisattva
is taught as the antidote to all habitual tendencies.
There is no Dharma of entering apart from this.

Thus she spoke. Then he became joined in nonduality with the Great Secret One. Having delighted her in many ways of arousing the left, and having brought forth the arisings such as the hidden lotus, he rejoiced greatly and sang this song:

Emaho, born from supreme bliss, Vajrapāṇi!
In the clarity that all phenomena lack real existence,
the great source knows that wonder arises. Emaho, how marvelous!
Because the cause itself is empty, no result is sought.
Even cause and result themselves the Victors do not find.
This is the magic of passion delighting in emptiness.
Therefore the Victors have declared here that there is no arising and no ceasing.

From the undivided, the unaging, the nature of all phenomena,
the joy of all arises, far more excellent than all the Victors.
Having spoken thus, Samantabhadra the yogi entered into the secret space, and sang this most fierce song:

Surpassing all illusions, the undivided one works the manifold display.
Samantabhadra the yogi is the nourisher of all the great Sugata mothers.
Entering the Sugata mother, who purifies all suffering, is the heart commitment, the Victors have declared.
Oṃ hūṃ. Not obstructing anything, and yet not doing what is done, is equanimity, the Victors have declared.
By this song that has been sung,
the protector of all beings, gone beyond sorrow,
will attain, in the empty maṇḍala of the Dharma,
the great austerity.
Thus he spoke of non-duality.

This is the fourth chapter, the chapter on the dance, from the Supreme Delight of All the Tathāgatas.

Then the Blessed One, together with great joy, entered into the meditative absorption called the Adornment of the Unsurpassed Samaya. He knew that all abide in the vow. He spoke these great samayas.

The vehicles of each are inconceivable.
When each is without fault, there is happiness.
Over worldly people, the cutting of the sūtras is forceful.
For one who upholds the Vinaya, there is happiness if it is guarded.
Behave purely toward those who act purely.
If non-virtue is turned back, there are the ten virtues.
By practice one upholds a world that is higher still.
Through the absorption of one's own aim,
those who abide on the ground of awakening
do not decline; if not entrusted to another,
from that which does not decline, it too is accomplished.
The yogic practice is accomplished through practicing.
By becoming accustomed to words, one overcomes the vehicles.
Do not abandon the supreme spiritual friend,
and cultivate also reverence, honor, and supreme service.
To become accustomed to absorption, meditate on the form of appearance.
Do not interrupt the recitation of words.

Give rise to sorrow over those who break the sacred bonds of their vajra siblings.
The sacred bond of the body is beyond conception.
The sacred bond of speech is one that is praised.
The sacred bond of mind has been taught as the highest.
The sacred bonds that guard the five families,
if they decline, are exceedingly difficult to restore.
The sacred bonds of conduct and of substances,
if they decline, are quickly restored.
One who lets a decline go unrepaired through laziness
becomes a great enemy of all the buddhas,
and is certain to be swiftly reborn
among the ten kinds of wanderers.
Therefore, noble sons, let the best,
the lowest, and the middling practitioners
practice, the superior without any interruption,
the middling in cycles of years, months, and six-month periods,
and the lowest never allowing a year to pass without repetition.
So the Victors have most emphatically declared.

Vajra fragrance, the awakening mind,
rakta, great fragrance, and the great elixir:
one who is endowed with a pure vessel
blesses these and drinks them.
This abides within the vow of the sacred bonds;
to transgress it brings great ruin.
All phenomena that are placed in equipoise,
practiced with the knowledge of equality,
and when one is clear in equality itself,
one is called a yogic hero, so it is taught.

As for the vessels used by the yogī,
all the substances that are praised,
wash them well and sweep them clean,
making them gleam like the color of the kalapiṅka bird.
Bowls, ritual daggers, and containers of every kind,
wash away their stains and make them beautiful.
Medicine is to be compounded at three times of day.
First compound it in a silver bowl,
then in a bowl of rāgan (brass),
then compound it in a bowl of copper.
Of the medicines compounded by the measure of a sang,
mix copper, silver, rāgan,
gold, and iron together well,
and compound the medicine for the disease of conceptual thought,
compounding it to the color of the mandārava flower.
If it has not taken on the mandārava hue,
it is never taught to be compounded.
When the sun and moon rise together at one time,
then, in accordance with the praise of the substances,
begin the compounding of the medicine properly.
When the medicines are fully assembled,
visualize a boundless maṇḍala of light
as having the color of the kalapiṅka.

Whoever is not diligent in this
has no integrity in the sacred bonds and holds no vow;
he is a demon estranged from his vajra siblings.
Whoever recklessly despises
the deity, the vajra master,
or through delusion fails to see, or is distracted,
or does not hold the sacred Dharma dearer than life,
or scorns the buddhas, the seven Victors,
or does harmful deeds against the maṇḍala,
that being becomes one of the great hell beings.
Such a one is afflicted with disease and a short life,
and in this very world suffers many harms.
After death he is born in the hells and the lower realms.
Therefore, there is nothing at all that can lead beyond these vows.
By not transgressing them,
in this very life one abides on the ground of the Victor's body,
the spontaneously accomplished ground.
Thus all the Tathāgatas themselves greatly extolled and magnified this vow of the great sacred bond.

This is the fifth chapter, called the Supreme of All Vows, the Secret Bliss of All the Tathāgatas.

Then the Blessed One, the Great Samantabhadra, together with the yoginīs, abided in a single thought. He knew the great lord of yoga, endowed with supreme practice, to be the entirety of conduct and the meaning of the profound. Knowing this, he spoke this method of the great magical display.

Emaho! From the pure sphere of the dharmadhātu,
buddhas and sentient beings both appear as a great marvelous display.
Though they appear as manifold from the gathering of wisdom,
they arise, perfectly emanated, from the illusion of all women.
That the two truths appear separately
the Victors have called the space of the Mother.
If one meditates on the great unerring path,
meditating on Samantabhadrī, one has entered the path.
The intent of all buddhas is wholly discerning wisdom, woman;
by honoring and serving her, great wisdom is accomplished.
The supreme accomplishment of the rite of drawing the maṇḍala,
through the very meaning of the vajra song,
becomes clear in the intent that is nondual.

Then Glorious Samantabhadra, the very self of all the Tathāgatas, spoke of this enjoyment of skillful means and wisdom.

The entry into the activity of manifest conduct
is to delight in one another,
abiding in the vow of the yogī.
The supreme offering to this
is the union of perfect joining,
all-pervading, joined in everything.
Passionate attachment is the supreme pledge.

Therefore, for the Karma family, one possessed of the proper color,
of sixteen years, tall in stature,
as though poured from a mold,
her body well formed and beautiful,
her forehead broad, her waist slender,
her breasts small, her fingers long,
her gaze slightly sidelong, her eyes long,
fair, with reddish veins glowing,
white teeth, the hair of her body fine,
short in her gathering, her voice deep and resonant:
such a woman is the supreme abode of Vajrasattva.
One who delights in her
will, without fail, accomplish the Vajra family,
and attain success in this very life.

Examine carefully in a secluded place.
Her flesh is fair, radiant, of fine complexion,
her body of middling size, round and well proportioned,
her teeth white and firm, her eyes long and round,
her voice fine, her complexion fair, her eyelashes long,
her forehead broad and well set,
her skin unblemished, and so forth:
such a woman constantly bows to the Dharma.
She belongs to the family of Brahmā's melody.
One who strives and practices with her,
in the samādhi of that same family,
if they delight in one another,
within a single dawn of Brahmā
will without fail attain success.
The delight of the yogī's union
is said to be accomplished through diligent union.

A woman of excellent bearing
is of short stature, slender-bodied,
her hands and feet small, her voice clear,
her eyes fine, her face round, her veins strongly red,
devoted in her attachment, and without ignorance.
Her body is adorned with auspicious marks and moles,
her speech is clear, she dreams little,
her teeth are white, her lips fine, and she is full of faith.
Through the samādhi of one who contemplates the Dharma,
make the sun and moon light in the secret space
together with the vajra and lotus,
and if they delight in one another,
in an instant of a god of the desire realm
success will come, without fail.

Her body is round and firm, with pride,
sharp in speech within, holding things in mind,
mindful of what is to be done and never neglecting it.
She trains slowly, dull of mind, heavy in flesh and bone,
her radiance shining; on examination she grows greater and greater.
She delights in giving and has faith in the Dharma.
Through the samādhi of the Jewel family,
cut through to the root with the seed-syllable of habitual tendency,
and when the samādhi is clear in the mind,
the time for accomplishment has come.
Then, with the utmost diligence,
open the lotus of the consort,
and with the play of bliss in the mind,
if they delight in one another,
the accomplishment of the Jewel family
will be attained in this very life.

By the particular quality of compassionate activity,
if one summons those of the lowest families in the world
and delights with them,
this is said to be the great conduct of the yogī
who possesses supreme compassion and skillful means.
Butchers, those of low caste, prostitutes,
those of base families, and their possessions:
it is said that if one acts swiftly
in the samādhi of the Subduer, success follows.

Hungry ghosts, yakṣas, animals,
dogs, tortoises, monkeys, and so forth,
and all the supreme abodes of humans and gods:
if one delights in each according to its fitting,
great peace comes to the arrogant.
Delight in diligence makes one diligent.
Of all the activities of skillful means this is supreme.
For the sake of these sentient beings,
whatever they practice in error,
through varied acts of taming
there is nothing that is not done.
By a most fierce appearance
one brings all the fierce under control;
by a most peaceful appearance
one brings the arrogant under control;
by a most radiant appearance
one brings the minds of the passionate under control.
Wondrous! How marvelous!
Whatever it may be, why should one not reflect on it?

When this was spoken, all became endowed with great and supreme diligence.

The Blessed One, the Tathāgata, inconceivable and equal to space, whose mind, with an aspiration as numerous as the atoms of the finest dust, was intent upon listening, made them diligent. The chapter was composed in which Vajra Dharma Supreme and the rest, of inconceivable number among the families, were wholly embraced by supreme listening and supreme reverence.

Then the Blessed One, the great yogin, acted with the most intense diligence. With a mind of intense diligence, they delighted one another with joyful union, with embrace, with laughter and pleasure, and with an unsurpassed satisfaction.

Then, from the expanse of suchness, the body, speech, and mind of the vajra spoke this:

Oṃ hūṃ. The one endowed with the supreme yoga of wisdom
bestows the supreme accomplishment on the fortunate.
To dispel utter bewilderment, he sets in motion every cessation.
That yogin, supreme and without a second, how wondrous this is!
The wish-fulfilling jewel, the dhāraṇī that holds sway over all,
the dhāraṇī of Brahmā's radiance, once drawn forth, and practiced with diligence,
will come to fruition. Joyful practice in this: a la la!
It is most possible; since it is said that practicing just so brings accomplishment,
those who abide on the grounds are surpassed by all.
Among all the Victors, Samantabhadra, the Victor, surpasses all.
And she too is Samantabhadrī, as all the Victors of old have said.
Free of all signs, never abandoning the union of utter joy,
that very one is Vajrasattva, as all the Victors have said.
Whoever practices with diligence and takes up
these songs of Brahmā Supreme
is an ascetic, a yogin, a samādhi-holder;
by a single warmth of the Brahmā Supreme,
accomplishment, it is said, comes without fail.
Thus it was spoken.

All the Tathāgatas, by the great, intense diligence, were made to become one in nature.

This is the sixth chapter, called The Secret Maṇḍala of All the Tathāgatas.

Then all the teachers of the three times, whose nature is that of the Sugatas, were pleased by the great offering, and they knew how to tame eighty-four thousand ignorances. Knowing this, they brought about the liberation of ignorance, and delighted in the complete liberation of aversion. By the nature of great equality, they liberated these beings through the means of taming, and by the union of the family of empowerment they guarded and protected them closely.

My own yoga is Samantabhadra.
Characteristics and imputations are not two.
If one unites in equality with all the Victors,
one abides on the ground of equality.
One's own mind abides in the center of the full moon;
this is the samādhi of Vajrasattva.
Through realizing the meaning, becoming clear,
it is the great and utter liberation.
The supreme abides in the lineage.
The miraculous display of the body is complete in every family;
the speech is the unsurpassed teacher;
the mind's activity is endowed with signs.
Through this samādhi becoming clear in this way,
in order to purify the bewilderment of mind and suffering,
through clarity without duality,
one gathers in with jaḥ hūṃ baṃ hoḥ.
One liberates through the activity of manifest conduct;
one comes to accomplish without duality.
With signs and knowledge perfectly complete,
the loving action, the yogin's mind,
with the offering of dhāraṇī in equality,
if one knows how to unite so as to please the Victors,
one honors oneself with great delight.
In the manner of truth, to purify the realization of mind itself,
which is inseparable,
by the force of samādhi all are liberated,
and one acts to free them through the activity of manifest conduct.
One unites everything in the sky of the secret.

This is the seventh chapter, called Offering, the very suchness, from the inconceivable deeds and conduct of all the Tathāgatas.

Then the Blessed One, delighting in protecting the retinue, received disciples of superior, middling, and lesser capacity, who had made many offerings, and admitted them into the maṇḍala. To cut off the hordes of obstructors, through the samādhi of Vajrasattva, he filled their palms with precious substance in the form of a face and had them recite this essence:

Oṃ sarva tathāgata pūjī dhi dha tiṣṭha hūṃ.

Having recited "Oṃ mahā-maṇḍala-devakara pūjaya pūjaya hūṃ a," he has them scatter the flower.

The spiritual friend then shows them where it has fallen. The mantras, the mudrās, and the samaya commitments are explained. Then, as the water of the samaya is poured, they are instructed to understand these words well:

The great form of sentient beings, action and the rest, is consecrated with empowerment. This is the empowerment as a son of the Victors. From this day onward, child, you must never give up these samaya antidotes, even for the sake of your life. Do not abandon your teacher, and make offerings to your companions. Listen to the command with reverence. If you transgress the command, you will be crushed, destroyed, tormented, and consumed by fire, and you will be born in the hells. Let this water that abides in the mind bring harmony to the four kinds of enlightened activity.

Having said this:

The vajra master, the maṇḍala, Samantabhadra, the vajra brothers and sisters, and the rest: bow at their feet and show them reverence. Do all that is taught in the tantra. Through reliance on the form of the one who accomplishes, success is attained.

Then the supreme son of the family is also given the empowerment of the secret name. That supreme son, full of reverence, offers kingship, the power of kings, his own body, his dwelling and the lands of his retinue, his sons, wife, and kin, precious jewels, crystal and silver, the seven kinds of precious things, wealth and possessions, horses, elephants, chariots, buffaloes and calves, whatever he has, treasures, goods, and whatever pleases the mind. With no doubt in his mind, he generates the supreme respect and service and presents these, and the Victors are pleased.

Then the empowerments are conferred, each according to the measure of the vehicle, in due order. The listening, the accomplishment, and the rest; the actions, the cymbals, and the victory banner; the eye, the vajra, the bearers of measure, the special appointments, and the others: of whichever secret empowerments have been received, for one who has reached the age of twenty or twelve, the robes, the crown ornament, food and drink, the mudrā, the bell, and so on, he visualizes clearly in this way as the maṇḍala, and all the Victors give their blessing. Spreading seats before them, he acts with delight. If the king confers the empowerment from above, he will surely obtain the siddhis. One should praise with excellent words.

Then that supreme son of the family bows at the feet and offers the gift of thanks. The vajra brothers and the dhāraṇī-holders declare: "You are a Victor of Victors, the great son of all of the three times. Engage in the conduct of all the three times. You are the liberator of all the three realms. Engage in the conduct of the supreme liberator. You are the one with excellent yoga, fortunate, abiding in the vows of samaya, drinking the water of signlessness, doing the work as commanded. You are renowned and distinguished above all. You uphold the lineage that will lead later generations. You abide on the ground of the net of magical illusion."

This is the eighth chapter, called Supreme Offering and Compassion, from the very suchness of the secret of the body, speech, and mind of the Tathāgatas.

Then the Blessed One entered into the samādhi called Ornament of the Arrayed Secret Vajra, and remained in it. Having done so, he entered into equipoise without obstructing the many linkages of pacification and of agent and action. Then once more, by the samādhi of unmoving, he established the sufferings that depend on karma, each directly arising. Then he tamed the non-Buddhists (tīrthikas) who hold the view of permanence, who cling to the fixed and eternal, by assuming the form of fixed permanence. To those who are called "those who do not want annihilation later," he caused them to enter equipoise through that very form. Having done so, to those whose extreme austerity of total restraint does not allow them to abide swiftly in happiness, he performed a great taming through the manner of great transformation of austerity. To those who delight in the forceful methods of violence, he tamed them by assuming the form of a spider-like weaver, a copper-patterned one. To the rulers who hold one-sided views and have no compassion for others, he performed taming through the samādhi of the wish-fulfilling jewel.

To those who do not know the virtue and vice of the four continents, he appeared as a great wheel-turning king and tamed them. Through the manner of the great antidote, the one with the face of copper, and the great executioner, he tamed those who bind and those who cut, who cook and who gnash their teeth, both great and small. And all of these he caused to abide in the great sameness of phenomena, the manner of awakening, the Vajra Vehicle, supreme above all vehicles, abiding in its own nature, the inseparability of means and wisdom, the inconceivable play. This activity of means is called the mudrā of the wrathful one's manifold transformation.

Then the Blessed One, the Great Samantabhadra, entered into the samādhi of transformation called Subduing Māra. Now there was a māra called Great Aversion, who for a thousand eons had taken on inconceivable suffering, and so had never generated the mind of awakening. He was exceedingly cruel, with a rough and stinking body, huge and hairy, harming beings with weapons and the cruelty of blood. With fangs like blades, his stench spread for a league. He killed, cut, devoured and drank, and in the midst of a blazing fire he shot cruel arrows. Shaking violently, he abode fierce and menacing on the brink of a precipice.

Then the Blessed One, saying nothing, smiled at Samantabhadrī. From the cloud of the awakening mind of the Father and Mother, with the utterance Oṃ dhu dhu dhu dhu ra dhu ra dhu ra ho ho, hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ, hrīṃ hrīṃ hrīṃ, ho ho, there arose, helpless and with perceptions of intense desire, the consorts of Brahmā, of Indra, of Viṣṇu, of Cakra, of the gods, of Rudra, of Garuḍa, of the Guardian of the Realm, of Vaiśravaṇa, of Virūpākṣa's kin, and of Virūpākṣa; and he gathered them together.

Again, from the cloud of the awakening mind, with the utterance Sattva Vajra Heruka, Mahāsamaya Sarva Siddhi, hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ, ho ho ho, he became adorned with the many forms of the Tathāgatas, and as he abode, each and every one of the assembled, the consorts of Brahmā and the rest, conceived an extremely great desire and clung to him. Clinging thus, from the playful awakening mind there arose inconceivable circles of water, which washed away and destroyed the world. Many circles of fire utterly burned the world. Many circles of wind scattered the beings of the world.

Then, from the cloud of the awakening mind, he conferred empowerment to establish their lineage. Then, arising from non-duality, with the utterance Oṃ oṃ sarva viśuddhe śaṣṭha ja ja ja, the consorts of Brahmā, Viṣṇu and the rest were bound by dhāraṇī to be servants, took a pledge, and went back to their own abodes.

Then from that bodily form he caused abiding in the great sameness, and with the utterance Oṃ bhara mudrā mahā caṇḍa caturyoginī maṇḍala krodha hala hala hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ, each of the consorts and their sons and daughters became the great Vajra Slayeress. To the fierce great māra Aversion and the others she said, "Lord, take the pledge, arise!" and smeared blood on the body of the cruel māra. Even so, he could not be made to heed the pledge, and remained heedless and utterly dark.

Then the Blessed One gathered all the emanations into one, and with three heads, six arms and four feet, holding a hammer, a vajra, a small stone, a bow and arrow, a noose, and a threatening finger, embraced Samantabhadrī in the play of a tumultuous dance, and the three heads blazed forth.

Reciting "Sarva Bhūta Hūṃ Hūṃ Phaṭ," he gathered beneath his feet the four māras, Brahmā, Indra, and the other great kings and protectors of the Dharma. With "Khāhi khāhi phaṭ phaṭ ruru ruru ruru hūṃ hūṃ" he slew them, and having filled the skull-cup (bhandha) with them, he drank them, so that none could remain.

Again, stretching out his right foot in the posture of standing embrace, he recited "Oṃ sarva tathāgata mahājñāna garbha ho dhu ho dhu ho dhu, caṇḍa bala mahā śrī heruka hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ, trūṃ trūṃ trūṃ, hrīṃ hrīṃ hrīṃ, phaṭ phaṭ phaṭ." Through this recitation, the consorts, sons and daughters of each were lifted up, helpless, and carried off. Then that great māra Aversion was likewise lifted up by his arrogant consort and carried off.

Once they had been carried off, the Blessed One, glorious Heruka, through the great qualities of body, speech, and mind, recited "Oṃ sarva tathāgata mahā samaya śrī heruka duṣṭaṃ dhaka hana, daha daha daha, paca paca paca, hā hā hā hā hā." Then the great sovereign ladies of vajra yoga came forth from the clouds of awakening mind in the secret space of the Mother: the Yoginī Who Impels, the Yoginī Who Slays, the Devourer, the Fierce Yoginī, the Great Accomplisher of Action, the Vajra Burner, and the Great Gatherer of Power. Seizing the host of wicked ones and the māras by the head, they stripped them of their weapons, slew them, tore their limbs apart, and carried them off and struck them down.

Then the Blessed One, the glorious great blood-drinking Vajra of great joy, turned to the retinue of beings, the classes of yakṣas and māras; the hungry ghosts; the great ogresses with horse bodies; the great owl-women; the great vulture-women; and tigers, lions, bears, wolves, dogs, jackals, monkeys, ravens, vultures, crows, pigs, and the like, together with the mātaṅgas and rudras, all dwelling in their places. He performed a union that clings to no sign, and recited "He he he he he, hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ, phaṭ phaṭ phaṭ." From the clouds of awakening mind there came forth great lions with mighty strides, great tigers in their forms, intoxicating great horse-headed ones, owl-headed ones, ravens, vultures, and long-beaked ones, with colors not apart from the forms of ogresses. Reciting "Phaṭ phaṭ phaṭ caṇḍa mahāmaṇḍala hūṃ phaṭ," he had the host of wicked ones slain, and with the manner of eating of birds, fanged beasts, and flesh-eaters, they seized and devoured them.
