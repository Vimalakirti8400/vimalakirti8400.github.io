The Chapter Called 'The Great Festival in Which the Ocean of Bodhisattvas from the Ten Directions Gathers and Takes Delight,' from the Noble Sūtra of the Great Cloud (Ārya-mahāmegha-sūtra-daśadig-bodhisattva-samudrasannipāti-mahotsava-vikrīḍita-nāma-parivarta)

Toh 233 in the Kangyur is a chapter from the Noble Sūtra of the Great Cloud, known in Sanskrit as the Ārya-mahāmegha-sūtra and in Tibetan as Phags pa sprin chen po'i mdo, with this particular section bearing the title "The Great Festival in Which the Ocean of Bodhisattvas from the Ten Directions Gathers and Takes Delight." It is a Mahāyāna sūtra set on Vulture Peak Mountain at Rājagṛha, where the Buddha is depicted addressing an enormous assembly of monks, nuns, bodhisattvas, and laypeople. The text belongs to the sūtra division of the Kangyur and is presented as a discrete named chapter, or parivarta, of the larger Great Cloud collection. As suggested by both its title and its vast assembly scene, the work is concerned with the gathering and rejoicing of bodhisattvas from across the ten directions, situating it within the tradition of grand Mahāyāna scriptures that celebrate the scope and splendor of the bodhisattva path.

In the language of India: Ārya-mahāmegha-sūtra-dadaśadig-bodhisattva-samudrasannipāti-mahotsava-vikrīḍita-nāma-parivarta. In the language of Tibet: The Chapter Called "The Great Festival in Which the Ocean of Bodhisattvas from the Ten Directions Gathers and Takes Delight," from the Noble Sūtra of the Great Cloud.

At that moment, from the ūrṇā-hair of the Blessed One there issued forth immeasurable circles of rays of light called 'The Lineage Free from Attachment, Free from Fear.' This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The Sanskrit title given in the colophon identifies this as a chapter (parivarta) from the Mahāmegha-sūtra. The Tibetan chapter title rendered here as "The Great Festival in Which the Ocean of Bodhisattvas from the Ten Directions Gathers and Takes Delight" follows the Tibetan closely; the Sanskrit reconstruction in the colophon supports this reading.

The name "Prajāpatī" renders སྐྱེ་དགུའི་བདག་མོ་ཆེན་མོ, which is a Tibetan calque of Mahāprajāpatī, the foster-mother of the Buddha and the first fully ordained nun; this identification is secure in context.

The phrase rendered "directly inclined toward the Great Vehicle and had properly entered the Great Vehicle" translates a standard Mahāyāna assembly formula distinguishing those who are oriented toward but not yet fully in the vehicle from those who have entered it properly; here both qualities are attributed to every member of each group.  The name rendered "Fragrant Elephant" translates the Tibetan dri za'i glang po, literally "the fragrant-one's elephant" or "elephant among the gandharvas," taken here as a proper name for the leading garuḍa king. This is somewhat unusual given that dri za ordinarily means gandharva, but in context the term clearly designates the garuḍa king's name; a slight textual irregularity in the manuscript tradition cannot be ruled out.

The name rendered "Wilderness Dweller" translates 'brog gnas, literally "one who dwells in the wilderness."

The name rendered "Fervent" translates drod ldan, literally "one who possesses heat or ardor."  The term rendered "Kacchanaka spirits" translates གཅོད་བྱེད་ཀྱི་བུ, literally "sons of the cutters" or "offspring of the slayers." This appears to be a class of spirit beings whose Sanskrit equivalent is uncertain; the Tibetan name is rendered descriptively here.

The elemental spirit king's name གླང་པོའི་སྙིང་པོ is rendered "Elephant-Heart," meaning "one whose heart or essence is like an elephant," suggesting steadfastness or greatness of spirit.

The number for the elemental spirit kings, ས་ཡ་ཕྲག་གསུམ་དང་བརྒྱད་འབུམ, is literally "three million and eight hundred thousand," which is an unusual combined figure and is rendered straightforwardly.
