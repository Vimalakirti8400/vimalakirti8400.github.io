The Questions of Vimalaprabha (Stainless Light) (Vimalaprabhaparipṛcchā)

The Questions of Vimalaprabha, known in Sanskrit as the Vimalaprabhaparipṛcchā and in Tibetan as Dri ma med pa'i 'od kyis zhus pa, is a sūtra in which the bodhisattva or deity Vimalaprabha — Stainless Light — poses questions to the Buddha in the context of a grand celestial assembly. The text opens with the Buddha teaching the Essence of the Moon sūtra on Vulture Peak, summoning gods and yakṣa kings including the Great King Vaiśravaṇa, situating the discourse within a cosmologically rich setting that suggests connections to lunar and calendrical themes found in related tantric literature. As a question-and-answer sūtra framed around divine interlocutors and protective deities, it belongs to the broader sūtra literature of the Kangyur. It is catalogued as Toh 168 in the Kangyur, where it appears among the general sūtra collections.

In the language of India: Vimalaprabhaparipṛcchā. In the Tibetan language: The Questions of Vimalaprabha — Stainless Light. First fascicle.

Homage to the Omniscient One.

At the time when the Buddha, the Blessed One, was teaching the sūtra called the Essence of the Moon, he entrusted each of the protectors of all the lands of Jambudvīpa with their respective duties, and while he was dwelling on Vulture Peak he sent forth rays of light in order to summon all the gods.

As soon as the Blessed One had directed his intention, all the kings of the gods, together with their retinues, appeared in the presence of the Blessed One. At that time the Blessed One called out to the Great King Vaiśravaṇa and to Sarvajaya, the lord of the yakṣas, and declared: "O Great Kings, attend to me in accordance with my Dharma teachings. One hundred years after I have passed into nirvāṇa, a land called Li will arise. I am entrusting it to your care." 

O King Vaiśravaṇa, you,
and Sarvajaya of great miraculous power,
and Anavatapta, king of the nāgas,
and the son of the gods of steadfast and excellent intellect —
hear my words.
This land will be tamed by the power of the Victor alone,
and not by another.
The sentient beings I have not yet tamed
will take birth in the environs of the land of Li,
in the city prophesied by the Buddha.
For the sake of my enlightenment as a buddha,
they endured great suffering
and planted vast and numerous aspirational prayers —
and by that accumulation of merit,
this land has become a sign of my presence.
When I pass peacefully into nirvāṇa,
I entrust it to you.
At the time when the land of Li,
this ground of my loving-kindness, arises,
the buddhas of the fortunate aeon
will tame beings here in that time,
at the beautiful place called Agumatira,
at the stūpa of the Dharma king.
For the sake of fully protecting this land,
I now give it into your keeping.

Then King Vaiśravaṇa addressed a petition to the Blessed One:

O guide, the command of the buddhas
is not something one can comply with singly.
The supreme Victor has declared this task to be exceedingly difficult.
Even while the Teacher, the Blessed One, dwells,
quarrels arise in these various lands and regions.
When you teach the true Dharma,
those who fully guard it are rare —
how much more so when the Teacher has passed into nirvāṇa,
in lands abandoned by the gods,
where the protectors, gods and others,
do not follow your instructions?
Have compassion upon us.
Please teach such a Dharma as, when heard,
will cause all the gods to act according to your command.
The word of the buddhas, the heroes,
cannot be overturned on this earth.
Since performing a deed never done before by any being
is exceedingly difficult,
how shall we fully guard and protect that Blessed One?
The buddhas see through the three times;
they possess the eye of omniscient wisdom.
It is for the sake of that Victor, the supreme being,
that we are present and make this request.
Therefore we will bear in mind the trust you place in us.
We beseech your blessing so that we may perform
the task exactly as the Victor previously commanded.
In the city prophesied by the Victor —
in that city prophesied by the Victor of old —
the bodhisattvas,
and three hundred monks maintaining fine form and their vows,
and two hundred nuns will appear.
And those five hundred who engage in wrongdoing,
who in former times planted aspirational prayers
mixed with evil deeds —
those too will arise.

In order to obstruct the one who creates virtue,
they will take birth in these very lands.
Yet those dear friends of the one who creates virtue
will also take birth in the land of Li.
In the lands prophesied by the Victor,
they will perform what is excellent.
How we may come to be established in this land,
and by what means we may hold fast to it —
O Blessed One, we make this supreme supplication to you.

To this the Blessed One replied, granting the supreme and holy gift:

"O lord of the yakṣas, in order to guard this land completely at all times,
hold this excellent abode of the bodhisattvas fast by my words.
That land, when it arises, will be like the finest pleasure grove of the gods,
beautified by the Saṅgha's assembly halls and fragrant bathing chambers,
filled with joy through monasteries and parks.
The hermitages and retreat places there will become like Sukhāvatī itself.
Filled with monks and nuns, they will teach the supreme vehicle.
Those who are born as humans in that excellent abode of the bodhisattvas
will perform virtuous actions and dedicate them toward awakening.
For as long as the Mighty Sage (Śākyamuni) keeps his teaching present in that land
and people make offerings to it, so long will those lands endure.
But if offerings are not made to the teaching, the guardian spirits will be disturbed.
The land will be cast aside, and its inhabitants will wander in disturbed minds.
When the guardians have gone and the gods have abandoned this land,
it will be threatened by sickness and famine, and even the kings will be short-lived.
When all the guardians are enraged, they will bring even armies to nothing."

Having thus spoken to the gods, the most holy Victor, the supreme one, then entered into the meditative absorption (samādhi) called Glorious Rays of Light. From between his eyebrows there came forth rays of light called the Form of the Tathāgata. From those rays there arose countless and immeasurable further rays of light, which traveled throughout all the buddha-fields. In whichever buddha-field a blessed buddha was present, this declaration was uttered aloud: "Does the God of Gods, the blessed buddha Śākyamuni, suffer little? Does he move about well?" — and so on in that manner of inquiry. And this too was said: "For the sake of buddhahood itself, I have endured hardship over many aeons. In order to gather the authentic Dharma, I have given up uncountable bodies and offered many gifts. I have undergone immense suffering that other bodhisattvas have not experienced, and thereby attained unsurpassed, perfect, and complete awakening. Now the time and occasion of my passing into complete nirvāṇa has arrived, and so that all the lands of Jambudvīpa may be well maintained, I have placed them in the hands of the guardians."

When I, the gods, have passed beyond sorrow,
at the time when one hundred years have passed,
a land bearing the name of Li
shall prosperously arise.
It will be a hook for the taming
of the buddhas of the Fortunate Aeon
and of the protecting bodhisattvas —
free from mere speculation.
The Victors' children of the Fortunate Aeon
will also take birth in that city at the heart of Li,
and will appear there like eyes,
to ripen sentient beings
and that the Dharma may be heard.
At that time they will all engage
in the excellent conduct of the bodhisattva.
Let the buddhas look upon me with care:
when this great sūtra, the Heart of the Sun,
this supreme sūtra, is being taught,
with a mind overcome by compassion,
in order to guard that land,
dhāraṇī mantras and rays of light —

Just as before they sent forth emanations,
from every buddha-field throughout all worlds,
those who have taken birth in this land —
may the supreme Victors send them forth.
To guard the teaching may they send them forth,
send them forth to me with joyful hearts.

When the protector, Śākyamuni, the Lion of the Śākyas,
sends out those rays of light,
they go to all the buddha-fields
and then return again to that very place.

Then, at once, the Blessed One buddhas dwelling in all the buddha-fields, wishing to fully protect the land of Li on behalf of the Blessed One Śākyamuni, and so that the teaching might long endure, sent rays of light into the land and also transmitted dhāraṇī mantras, speaking thus:

"The names of those Blessed One buddhas who sent out rays of light and transmitted dhāraṇī mantras into the hands of the bodhisattvas are as follows, and the names of those bodhisattvas are as follows.

Homage to the Tathāgata who is bold in entering among all non-Buddhists (tīrthikas).
Homage to the bodhisattva of the secret word.

The dhāraṇī mantra is: tadyathā — bara bara kāśa ara cakra koṣe guhyakabāre svāhā.

Through this dhāraṇī mantra, the holy gods will bestow blessings upon the gods and humans of the land of Li.

Homage to the Tathāgata who is bold in the Dharma of joy.
Homage to the bodhisattva who is the lord of the Dharma.

Tadyathā — bara trika buli śa siri pahāre bāre svāhā.

Through this dhāraṇī mantra one will come to recall past lives.

Homage to the speech of the Tathāgata, the supremely noble crest of one who has entered upon the Dharma.
Homage to the bodhisattva Īśvara of the world (Avalokiteśvara).
Homage to the bodhisattva of words that are luminous in one's own mind.
Homage to the Tathāgata, the supremely exalted king.
Homage to the bodhisattva of the word of space.

Tadyathā — hvākyarate ho ho ho ho phāru rara abibara mabibara ṇaruhe svāhā.

Whoever holds this dhāraṇī mantra will not go to the lower realms and the evil destinies, and will come to know how the migrations of sentient beings were in previous lives and what they will be in lives to come.

Homage to the Tathāgata Priyajaya, whose Dharma grows and flourishes.
Homage to the bodhisattva who is lord of the Dharma for gods and humans.

Tadyathā — dharasthita tathatāvajri bisare tathatā svāhā.

Through this dhāraṇī mantra one will attain the dhāraṇī.

Homage to the Tathāgata Akṣobhya (the Unshakeable).
Homage to the bodhisattva who is the stainless lord.

Tadyathā — avahi sarvatathāgatabhūtakoṭi bhatibhati paḍiraḍi svāhā.

Through this dhāraṇī mantra one will attain awakening at the foot of the bodhi tree of the Tathāgatas.

Homage to the Tathāgata, the King of the Sound of Drums.
Homage to the bodhisattva Śrīgyan (Glorious Ornament).

Tadyathā — tathatāpuḍe dravane sarvisaraḍri guhyamantra tathatādhana svāhā.

Through this dhāraṇī mantra the bodhisattva will accomplish great austerities, and sentient beings will be utterly liberated from suffering and sickness.

Homage to the Tathāgata Amitābha.
Homage to the bodhisattva Nāṅsal (Illuminated Light).

Tadyathā — raddāgate bagribati taḍibhati candre svāhā.

Through this dhāraṇī mantra the bodhisattva will fully purify buddha-fields, and the physical and mental suffering of whoever holds this mantra will be exhausted."

Homage to the Tathāgata Śākyamuni.
Homage to the bodhisattva Mañjuśrī, Youthful Prince of Wisdom.
Homage to the bodhisattva Samantabhadra.

Tadyathā — bhinare ribi samasacagaba darśana raśmi samanta darśarani svāhā.

Through this dhāraṇī mantra the bodhisattva will come to know all activities, and by it the Tathāgata Śākyamuni himself also performed great deeds. These buddhas, the Blessed Ones, transmitted this dhāraṇī mantra to the Blessed One Śākyamuni so that he might guard it in every land.

At that time the Blessed One, the buddha, entered into the meditative absorption known as the Courageous Advance, and from the first arising of his aspiration all the way to the heart of awakening, he fully and rightly contemplated with devotion all the conduct of a bodhisattva and all the Dharma. Then, for his own benefit and for the benefit of the gods and others, he entrusted and committed those lands to them.

As for the dhāraṇī mantra of all the gods, beginning with Brahmā, it too should be recited:

Namo brahmaṇe. Namaḥ sarvadebānān. Tadyathā — brahmaṇa indre devatāpratiṣṭhite aśvasthāne bara indre svaparapari harakṣiṃ maba indre santathāsmara bara hare ama bhakumbhaka krī grī śrī ālaṃkāra sarba deba grāma svāhā.

Through this dhāraṇī mantra gods and humans will attain victory; enemies will not be able to harm the one who holds it, and he will become lord of all sovereigns. Whoever is to become a king in the land of Li will think to himself: "This land of Li is filled with people, with men and women in great number, and abounds in wealth and grain. It is the highest and most excellent of all lands, and so no enemy army can come against it." At that time that king will cause the monks, nuns, laymen, and laywomen, together with all the people of the land, throughout every hermitage, every garden of the Saṅgha, every city, every village, every district, and all the surrounding areas of the royal palace, to read out the words of the dhāraṇī secret mantra described above, to take it up and to make it thoroughly known. And those monks, nuns, laymen, and laywomen, and all the people dwelling in the land, shall be served and honored with flour, cloth, parasols, victory banners, flags, food, various drinks, various acts of offering, and all manner of requisites. They are to be treated with reverence, shown respect, and venerated. If this is done, that land of Li will be free of all harm, and no hostile king, with all his armies, will be able to destroy that king's land of Li.

Then again, from the buddha-fields of the ten directions, the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, sent forth their respective names, their rays of light, and the words of their dhāraṇī mantras. The Blessed One Śākyamuni placed them into the hands of the thirty-two divine kings — Śakra (Indra, king of the gods), Brahmā, and the rest — and the four world-protectors, and the divine son Sarvatrojavijaya and his companions, and the twenty-eight great lords of the yakṣa hosts, and the bodhisattva Vimalaprabha (Stainless Light), and those twenty thousand bodhisattvas who offer worship at the stūpa of Gomasalagandha, and the eighteen thousand nāga kings headed by Takṣaka the Proud — into all their hands — along with the abode of all the bodhisattvas of this Fortunate Aeon.

It was entrusted to them so that they might protect, fully sustain, and keep from harm the region of Li, the stūpa of Gomasalagandha — which has become the emblem of all the buddhas of the Fortunate Aeon, the Blessed Ones, and the abode of the great sage — together with the communities of monks and nuns there, so that none would be scattered, and so that the Buddha's teaching established in the region of Li might long endure. These names of the buddhas, these rays of light, and these dhāraṇī mantra syllables were presented as gifts, filled with compassionate intent and sent forth with great supplication. And so, in accordance with the command of those Blessed Ones the buddhas, Śakra, Brahmā, and the rest — all thirty-two divine kings, those world-protectors, those lords of the yakṣa hosts, those nāga kings, the bodhisattvas headed by Vimalaprabha, and all those in that assembly — joined their palms in reverence and pledged to protect the region of Li, the stūpa of Gomasalagandha which is the abode of the great sage, and those teachings of the Buddha. They likewise pledged to protect those names of the buddhas, those rays of light, and those dhāraṇī mantra syllables for the sake of guarding the land. And those rays of light came to abide within the body of the bodhisattva Vimalaprabha.

Tadyathā: jyo ti jyo ti, va jre a, a kṣa man tra ve, pra ga ā ve, va jri mā va, ka ka su re svāhā. This is called the Queen of Sovereign Dhāraṇī Mantras, and this dhāraṇī mantra pacifies the assembled forces of adversaries and brings to perfect completion the accumulation of virtue.

Tadyathā: di śā ka re, stha kā rai, in dra va te, va prā tau sa prā u sthā te svāhā. This dhāraṇī mantra eliminates famine and eliminates poverty. Moreover, the holy one will obtain all that is desired: if one recites it twenty-one times at each of eight sessions — four times by day and four times by night, making twenty-one recitations per session — one will obtain a treasure or other wealth hidden beneath the earth.

Tadyathā: oṃ he mo he jambha ya, ma śa man tra kā, da ma kā re la bhā kā re, vi kā re sa di jñā kā re, da ka di a dhā ka, pra śa ma na phu a ka na pra ma ne phu svāhā. This pacifies all illness, liberates one from rebirth as a woman, and thoroughly pacifies the illnesses specific to women. No ascetic discipline is required; one should recite it three times a day according to one's wish. For any woman who has an illness of the monthly sign, one should recite it seven times each day.

Tadyathā: ka ka, ko śa phu ma ma, ra ra ra ra, sta re phu, sa ka re phu svāhā. This dhāraṇī mantra is declared to eliminate harm that has arisen and all suffering. No ascetic discipline is required.

Now, those blessed buddhas whose names were mentioned above, and who taught these dhāraṇī mantras, were all companions of the Blessed One Śākyamuni during his practice of the bodhisattva conduct. Those buddhas dwelt in the world-system of Endurance, and it is within this land of Liyul, the realm of the Blessed One Śākyamuni, and for the benefit of the many beings there. Taking birth as bodhisattvas of the Fortunate Aeon, those respective blessed buddhas, for the sake of causing the Blessed One Śākyamuni's Liyul to endure for a long time, sent gifts of those dhāraṇī mantras, those rays of light of the buddhas, and those names of the buddhas. The sons of the buddhas requested them, and then the noble bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara, for the sake of all sentient beings, for the protection of Liyul, and in order that the Dharma teaching might endure for a long time, took those gifts from the blessed buddhas and brought them here.

Furthermore, many bodhisattvas dwelling in various buddha-fields, in order that this land prophesied by the buddhas might not be destroyed and that the teaching might likewise endure for a long time, came here and took birth.

When harm befalls that land, the names of the buddhas, the dhāraṇī mantras, and the rays of light described above should be written and placed at the gates of the city. They should also be written and set upon the standards and weapons of the army. When the people are caused to read and to carry them, protection will be given from fear of enemy armies and from fear of famine; karma will be purified; the wholesome side will be fully perfected; and sentient beings will be benefited according to whatever they have wished for. They will also become a refuge for sentient beings who are sick. Even for those who bear very heavy evil karma, if they recite the names of the buddhas, the dhāraṇī mantras, and the rays of light for twenty-one days and call to mind the blessed buddhas of the ten directions, then before a stūpa that contains bodily relics, or in the presence of a buddha image, if this recitation is performed three times a day, whatever wholesome aim one desires will swiftly be fully perfected, and those rays of light will come to rest upon that person. If enemy armies approach, the recitation should be performed. Alternatively, it should be read for seven days. For anyone at all who has been harmed, who is sick, or who wishes to recite or to read according to any purpose whatsoever — these should be done. If one is unable to read, then the mantra should be recited seven times over a cord, knotted, and tied on, and one will be freed from all harm. Whatever city has the names of the buddhas, the dhāraṇī mantras, and the rays of light written and placed at its gates cannot be destroyed by enemy armies. For one who recites constantly, there is no need to abstain from meat, and there is no need to abstain from other things either.

Then, to those sons of good family who had gone forth in the wake of the buddhas and who were very scrupulous and filled with shame, the noble bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara spoke these words: Children, do not be filled with shame. For your sake, many assembly halls of the Saṅgha, and perfume-houses and the like built by Dharma kings, together with stūpas containing relics, will arise in that land. For as long as the teaching of the blessed buddhas endures in that land, no king capable of destroying that entire land will arise. Likewise, those three — the one called Dngarldanpa, the beautiful one marked by the pig, and the old city — for as long as the teaching of the buddhas endures, and offerings are made to the entire Saṅgha, and the buddha stūpa is at Kapila, they will not be destroyed.

Then the noble bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara called together all the sons of the Victors and enjoined them: Friends — all the bodhisattvas and gods and others who are guardians of that land — when a king without Dharma destroys that land, do not abandon it in despair. If some among you do not heed the word of the buddhas and, at the time of destruction, forsake the land, you will be obstructed for many aeons from attaining the unsurpassed, perfectly complete awakening.

Then this dhāraṇī mantra was spoken: Tadyathā: kāle, akāle, nikṣramebhapraveśā, ākālikrame, aniṣkrame, apraveśā, paresamepa risami ti, sasase, asasase, sakute, varure, parure, agame, asamamo te, siddhe siddhāya, namo vairocaṇāya svāhā, phaḍaḍeṇe svate, āsāte, sumanne, saruste, siddhe, siddhāya svāhā.

Then the noble bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara questioned the one who had become king of Liyul:

"This is your birth. What you have done — offering up your body and life for the sake of the Three Jewels — is the very deed of the children of the buddhas. You have done well. Whenever a great and fearsome enemy threatens the land, this sūtra should be read, kept, and venerated. All fears will be pacified, and Sahāpati too will come to your aid. For the protection of the land, write out this sūtra according to the proper rite and keep it in the royal palace. Write it out as well and place it at the outer walls of the palace and at the gates of the city. Place it likewise on the great roads, the streets, at crossroads and intersections, and in the houses of householders. Whenever it is read many times, learned, or kept, that land will be freed from epidemic disease and all harm."

When the recitation is to begin, drums shall be beaten and musical sounds proclaimed, and offering shall be made to the Three Jewels. The rite shall be commenced and the secret mantra recited at the time of sunset on the first fifth day of the month. Alternatively, it may be commenced on the sixth or seventh day. At dawn, the god of fire shall first be invited, and dūrvā grass fire offerings shall be performed just as was taught before. The food offerings that have been set out shall be removed and fresh ones placed. For three or seven days following the completion of the fire offering, fresh food offerings shall be placed. Within the center of that maṇḍala, a buddha image, the sacred Dharma, and relics shall also be placed. In that maṇḍala shall be set ten canopies, ten victory banners, ten pennants, ten great drums, ten bells, three hundred and sixty garlands of flowers, and three hundred and sixty butter lamps.

After performing the rite in this way, one shall begin in that same maṇḍala on the eighth day of the month, and up through the fifteenth day shall commence the elaborate rite of the noble Avalokiteśvara, reciting the mantra. When one desires to undertake a great enterprise by means of these rites of the Hayagrīva dhāraṇī mantra, or desires to go into great battle, or desires to attain lordship and sovereignty over a great king, one shall recite this Hayagrīva dhāraṇī mantra according to the elaborate rite described here. At that time, the gods will bestow the consecration and the empowerment. When one is about to begin reciting with the elaborate rite of the noble Avalokiteśvara, and performs it in that same maṇḍala according to the earlier tradition, at that time the Blessed One, a buddha, will first send forth rays of light, and from his mind — which is the source of whatever dhāraṇī mantras he has bestowed — the bodhisattva, the noble Avalokiteśvara himself, together with many gods, will come to that place and also bestow the consecration. The Inquiry of Vimalaprabha, the Third Chapter.

The Blessed One, the Buddha, then spoke to the noble bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara: "Dear brother, call this time to mind. When the Blessed One, the Buddha Ratnagarbha, entered the city, the two of us assembled there upon hearing the prophecy, and you made this great aspiration: 'May I deliver from fear and terror all those sentient beings, however many there may be who are afraid and frightened.' "Thereupon the Blessed One spoke to the bodhisattvas Samantabhadra, Mañjuśrī the youthful, Ākāśagarbha, Mahābalavāpta, the King of Medicine, Pradānasūra, Kṣitigarbha, and the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara, saying: 'Those who were formerly friends and brothers, and those who will become our companions — the lord of the lordly devas, the sovereign king of trees, the lord of compassion Thāskar, the lord of cutting, the lord of fragrance, the lord of assemblies, the lord of the world, the lord of light, the lord of birth, and the god of all — these are the companions of the noble bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara.' Then the bodhisattva Samantabhadra spoke thus: "At whatever time this goddess first comes to comprehend the dhāraṇī mantra, I will lend her assistance, so that she may truly comprehend it exactly as it is. And in order that she may come to comprehend it through obtaining the dhāraṇī mantra called Coiled Conch from the synonyms of the Dharma of the White Lotus of the Sacred Dharma (Skt. Saddharmapuṇḍarīka), which carries out the actions of all sentient beings — and so that having comprehended the dhāraṇī mantra, she may bestow it upon all sentient beings; and so that the youthful Vajrasena may be made to recollect the word of the Buddha; and so that through these dhāraṇī mantras the Dharma may be recollected — all those words of the Buddha that the blessed buddhas are presently uttering shall be remembered; and I have ensured that attachment therein will be exhausted."

Then the blessed Buddha called out to the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara and declared these words: "Dear younger brother, remember this time — when the Blessed One Jewel Heart was proceeding to the city, at the gate of that city there sat a blind woman, severely afflicted by leprosy, and with her sat a small boy who served as the eyes for the blind one. Now, when the Blessed One Jewel Heart was setting out to go to the city, sentient beings were running swiftly back and forth in order to make offerings to the Blessed One, and the woman was seized with great fear and dread, thinking: 'Has a great army come to this city? Or has something arrived — many wild beasts, elephants, lions, tigers, and the like, rushing about in great numbers?' At that moment she cried out: 'Is there anyone of compassionate nature? Let someone speak to me, for I am utterly terrified — has an army come to slay me, or have predators come to tear me apart?' At that very time, Avalokiteśvara, you had taken the form of an aged brahmin, and you said to that sick woman: 'Do not be afraid. There is no danger of an army in this city, no danger from many wild beasts, nor anything else at all — it is many people running back and forth in order to make offerings to the Blessed One, precious Jewel Heart, who is coming to the city.' Then the woman said to the small boy: 'Take my hand, child, go ahead and lead me to where the Blessed One abides.' And that child, by the power of the rays of the Buddha's light, went together with his mother to that city gate.

When the Blessed One entered the city, all the sentient beings present who were sick, blind, lame, deaf, hunchbacked, insane, and so forth were entirely healed. At the very place where the great brahmin called Ocean-Dust had made aspiration prayers, and where many bodhisattvas had received the prophecy of unsurpassed, perfect, and complete awakening, that woman too was healed. There she had made an aspiration to attain buddhahood, praying: 'At whatever time this brahmin Ocean-Dust attains full awakening, may I at that time abide in his teaching — and just as the brahmin who took me up from the aggregate of births and led me for the sake of the Buddha's teaching has himself received the prophecy as foretold, may I too thereby receive the prophecy of attaining buddhahood.' Then the brahmin who was skillfully revealing the Buddha to that woman —

saying, 'May I become a refuge for all sentient beings who are afraid and terrified' — at that very moment the woman also spoke thus: 'When you have become the refuge of all those sentient beings who are afraid and terrified, remember me then, for I too shall be your helper.' Having spoken these words, the woman then made an aspiration to attain buddhahood right there.

Now, when the goddess Vimalaprabha heard the account of past lives from the Blessed One, the buddha, she made a great aspiration and spoke thus: 'All those bodhisattvas have become buddhas, while I am still appearing in a woman's form — I am deeply ashamed of this. Yet the great karmic destiny declared by the Blessed One cannot be otherwise. If I do not take birth as a woman, how could I carry out those very deeds declared by the Blessed One? Therefore, just as other women who are possessed of attachment, aversion, and delusion, so too shall I appear here in the form of a woman. And when the time arrives for me to recall all my buddha-activities and my aspirations, may I take birth together with this youth in the land of Li, and may things come to pass exactly as the Blessed One has declared. When harm befalls the land of Li, may we become the deliverers there. Then, coming to this world-realm of Endurance from every direction — wherever I may go, taking birth as a woman among gods and humans — in all those lives may I remember my past existences. After the Blessed One has uttered this declaration and I have died, may I be born in the world-realm Sukhāvatī, where even the name of woman does not exist. And from there, without long delay, may I return here and utterly liberate all suffering sentient beings belonging to the five destinies. When Maitreya (the future buddha Loving-Kindness) arrives here, may I go forth in order to contemplate the bodhisattva's sky-like words (his teaching, as vast as the sky) in their proper measure. In all my lives, however many sentient beings shall have accumulated merit together with me, or shall do so, or shall have seen me, or shall merely have heard my name — may I utterly liberate every one of them.'

Such was the aspiration she made.

When the goddess Vimalaprabha had spoken these words, the Blessed One declared the following: 'The one who in times long past was the brahmin called Ocean-Dust — that is I, the Blessed One Śākyamuni. The old brahmin who skillfully revealed the Blessed One, the buddha, to that woman — that is the noble bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara, the Sovereign of Compassion. The woman who fell ill — that is the goddess Vimalaprabha, daughter of King Ajātaśatru. The child who was her son at that time — that is the youth Vajrasena here. Now those who were to come afterward must also be identified. Among the stars and those of the golden lineage, the first to arise from the laywoman Huśa, giving rise to the human lineage, was the laywoman Huśa herself, who came to dwell in that land of the golden lineage. That laywoman Huśa was of the divine lineage. Among the gods, a certain goddess fell into wrongdoing and was expelled from their company and cast down onto the golden mountain, and a curse was also laid upon her: "May you become a man-eating demoness there!"'

Then she showed the way to the many merchants who had come to that mountain of the golden lineage in search of gold, and that demoness enjoyed sensual pleasures together with those merchants. But when, at another time, other travellers arrived there, she ate those with whom she had previously enjoyed sensual pleasures. That demoness who had been cast down by the gods' curse also made companions of five hundred other demonesses with whom travellers would arrive and with whom sensual pleasures were experienced. Now there was a bodhisattva named Ogpakchen who had become the caravan leader at that time. When the time came for the gods to purify her of her wrongdoing, she gave birth to a daughter who was given the name Huśa. This Huśa was one who had previously dwelt in the buddha-field of the Tathāgata whose name is Flower-Fully-Bloomed, Superknowledge, King of Stars, Petal of the Lotus, Free from Stain, and from that realm she had said: "This demoness, formerly born among the gods, has been my mother for many lifetimes. Now the bodhisattva Ogpakchen intends to eat her. I shall become her daughter and thus prevent her from committing that evil act." So she repeated this with a voice of compassion. At that time other travellers arrived there. Then Ogpakchen, after cutting off all the gates with his retinue, prepared to eat them, and gathered together with the newly arrived travellers. Then the small girl called Huśa, who had come to dwell among the demonesses, said to her father Ogpakchen: "Father, my lord, let us flee and escape from here before these demonesses eat us." At that time Ogpakchen was saved by his own daughter. Ogpakchen also saved all five hundred travellers, and together they all fled. Then the demoness went there and, not finding Ogpakchen, abandoned the newly arrived travellers there and ran in pursuit of Ogpakchen and the other travellers in order to find them. Previously, when she had dwelt in the world of the gods, she had gone outside, and for that reason had been punished: the gods had cut off her ears and left her on the mountain of the golden lineage, and the gods had laid a curse upon her, saying: "Whoever asks you, 'Who cut off your ears?', you shall be freed from the curse by that person, and you shall return to the world of the gods and become a goddess again as before." Then the time came for her to return to the world of the gods. While she was running in pursuit of the many travellers, she let down the long robe she was wearing, and put on the garment that had previously been made from the fur of a mountain deer, and bound her hair at the crown of her head. When the merchants who had fled came along behind her, she caught the caravan leader Ogpakchen and those five hundred merchants, stuffed them into her robe, and loaded them onto her shoulders. Then Ogpakchen laughed, and the demoness asked: "Son of man, why do you laugh? If you do not speak truthfully, I will pour your blood upon the ground." Then the caravan leader Ogpakchen said to her: "You who are so powerful — what being could have cut off your ears like this?" At that moment she released those travellers, stood in mid-air, and declared: "The name of this country shall henceforth be called the Golden Lineage, on account of the ears." Having said this, her ears became just as they had been before, and she went to the world of the gods and became a goddess once more. The caravan leader Ogpakchen then returned together with those travellers to the mountain of the golden lineage, and there he raised and cared for that girl. All those demonesses became pregnant,

Among all of them, there was not a single one who, after experiencing the pleasures of desire together with her, did not kill that very being afterward. Then all those demonesses were filled with shame, and they raised those children, venerating Husha as they would a deity. Among all the sons they had borne, the one born first became the husband of Husha, and from that time forward the human lineage of the Golden Lineage came into being. Although Husha engaged in the pleasures of desire together with all the sons of those travelers, she was unable to satisfy them all. And so, because she had vowed to give sensual pleasure yet was unable to satisfy them all, through that karma she was reborn continuously as a woman. A son was born from Husha, and the laywoman Hushar came to be; the one who became her son became king there, and from that time forward the human lineage was established. The laywoman Hushar dwelt within a divine palace on the mountain of the Golden Lineage and taught the Dharma to gods, humans, and demonesses. At the time of Hushar's death, those five hundred demonesses, together with the gods and demons who had heard the Dharma from her, became the guardians of the land of the Golden Lineage, and so they remain to this day. The one who at that time became the laywoman Hushar is now Vimalaprabha, the Stainless Light. The one who became the first king of the land of Li, who proposed marriage and offered himself and his kingdom, is this Crystal King. The one who was to become king of Kashmir, who declared before the Blessed One, the buddha, "May I be reborn there," is the Crystal King Śakravarman, reborn as a disciple of Vimalaprabha. The one who, being the queen of the king of Kashmir, declared, "May I be reborn as his mother," will become the noble queen of King Śakravarman. The one who, being the elder brother of the goddess, made this aspiration — "May I become a beloved elder brother, and having obtained great renunciation through that grief, may I attain samādhi on impurity through that renunciation" — will become that elder brother. The one who, being the youngest sister, made this aspiration — "When I die of grief, may you go to the virtuous friend, the monk, and there attain liberation from saṃsāra" — will become that youngest sister. The youngest brother of Vajrasena, who together with him made aspirations on account of the land of Li, will become an elder brother and will become the Crystal King. The one who governed the city of Ajātaśatru will become king of the Golden Lineage. The woman who gave generously but did not uphold ethical discipline will become the queen of that king.

to abandon their divine birth and take a human birth. The gods will urge in the same way both the one who governs the city of Ajātaśatru and the one who has become the son of that city's governor. The woman of noble birth who attained the grounds will become the queen of the land of Li for five successive lives. When the time comes for the Azha people and the gathering to arrive, the goddess Vimalaprabha will bring the one who had governed the city of Ajātaśatru and was her husband, lead him to the land of Gold, and they will come there together.

At that time, those military forces will arrive, and her father the king of Meteor Stone and two of her brothers will die in that battle. At that time Vimalaprabha, pierced with grief, will enter a chamber of sorrow, and without asking anyone else she will attain the meditative absorption of impurity on her own.

When the time arrives for the Red-Faces to make the land of Li into their vassal, the king known as Vijayakīrti will be slain in that battle. His son, a king known as Vijayayaśas, will arise. The present Vajrasena will become the son of Vijayavarmā and will become the king of Meteor Stone. He too will hold royal power for one year. Vijayayaśas will arise in the land of Li; Vijayavarmā will arise in Meteor Stone.

At that time, the one who was formerly the father of both Śubhakara and Pāpakara — the one who set loose the five hundred bandits — will become king, and two great ministers will arise. The prince known as Vijayayaśas will become king of the land of Li; the one known as Vijayavarmā will become king of Meteor Stone.

Of those two youths, Śubhakara was raised by the chief great minister, and the other was raised by Pāpakara. When Śubhakara was born, five hundred merchants came and, having been born in the land of Li, performed virtuous deeds in that land. When Pāpakara was born, those five hundred bandits who had been set loose were born in the land of Li and committed evil deeds.

When the youth Pāpakara was born, those five hundred bandits were sent before the king's father. Then, at that time, having given orders to certain others, saying "Do you carry out whatever task there is," the chief of those great ministers had his hands cut off, and the other was killed. This too arose from the time when Pāpakara had formerly gouged out Śubhakara's eyes: those great ministers had come to be directly opposed to one another, and from that point forward, in every lifetime, each became the enemy of the other. Of those great ministers, the chief one will become king of Meteor Stone, and the other will become king of the land of Li. Those two will later wage war against each other.

Those who were on Pāpakara's side when the eyes of Śubhakara were gouged out will kill the king of Li. When the time comes for the Red-Faced Ones to seize the land of Li, those two will fight one another. At that time, the one now known as Vajrasena, who is the maternal nephew of the goddess Vimalaprabha, will become king of Meteor Stone under the name Vijayavarmā, and on account of that nephew he will be tormented by grief. When the time comes for a buddha's ray of light to rest upon him, he will defeat the king of Meteor Stone. Yet if he lacks merit, those who make aspiration prayers to the Blessed One, the Buddha, saying "May the perfumery and the Saṅgha's pleasure grove be destroyed," will not become his allies.

When the Red-Faced Ones act as allies, seize the land of Li, and destroy the Saṅgha's pleasure grove and the perfumery, the king of Meteor Stone will be defeated. When, before long, the time comes for the Red-Faced Ones to arrive, Vimalaprabha will be overcome with the deepest grief. At that time the Blessed Buddhas and the guardian deities of the land of Li will offer their care. Then Vimalaprabha, sitting alone in a chamber of grief, will make supplication to the buddhas. Having also recited the dhāraṇī mantra called Nectar Drum, she will supplicate the Tathāgata Amitābha, saying: "May I either meet my nephew, or may I die, or may I pass beyond saṃsāra entirely."

Then, in the early morning hours, the Blessed Buddhas will send rays of light upon her, and she will also attain the dhāraṇī mantra and the patience. At that time the buddhas and the gods will pronounce "Excellent!" The sons of the gods will ring bells and sound great drums and music, saying: "A great deed has been performed by a non-regressing bodhisattva."

At that time, knowing within herself that she had repaid the kindness of her parents and that offerings would therefore be made to her, she will act for the first time. Formerly, when Śubhakara caused a rain of jewels to fall from the wish-fulfilling jewel, the great minister made an aspiration prayer on behalf of Śubhakara at that very first moment; and then certain others said: "If all the aspirations we have made do not turn away from any of this, we will serve as allies so that even the slightest part of the teaching is not later destroyed." The youth Vijayavarmā, who will become king of Meteor Stone, will also act as an ally. Those great ministers on Pāpakara's side who also made aspiration prayers on Śubhakara's behalf will later turn back.

When the Blessed One had spoken those words, Avalokiteśvara addressed him thus: "I too shall encourage them at that time. I shall teach the Dharma to all of them, so that they all come to retain it and can in turn teach it to others — this I shall do. My sister as well has been practicing the conduct of awakening together with me."

Then all the gods rose and said: "We too wish to go there." At that moment, a rain of divine flowers fell upon the buddha, the Blessed One, and divine cymbals were sounded.

Then the buddha, the Blessed One, spoke these words: "Those buddhas, those Blessed Ones, sent forth rays of light upon him. From those rays of light, words arose that expressed the intended meaning just as it is, so that he would obtain the words of dhāraṇī-mantras; and so that, for the sake of the matter of the brother Vijayavarmā being defeated in battle, he would obtain dhāraṇī-mantras; and so that, by his obtaining those dhāraṇī-mantras, others too would obtain them; and so that he who sat dejected in a house of grief, in order to carry out the tasks the Blessed One had declared for the sake of the land of Li, would obtain dhāraṇī-mantras from those buddhas, those Blessed Ones."

those Blessed One buddhas who had been emitting rays of light from near the Kapilā stūpa will come together with the Saṅgha of monks from the land of Gold. This will be in order to liberate from that servitude of the herdsmen those people who were formerly enslaved, freeing them from the condition of having taken what was not given. When that report spreads, the herdsmen will turn back. Then, at that time, through that decisive resolution, the great mass of sentient beings destined to return to cyclic existence will be tamed.

Thereupon the Blessed One, the buddha, declared these words: "Why did you wonder whether, in a future time, herdsmen would come into my land and destroy the fragrant shrines and the pleasure gardens of the Saṅgha? Or why, you may ask, are these called 'herdsmen'? In a past age there arose a king named Brahmadatta. In his land there appeared a wandering ascetic called Bodhisattva. King Brahmadatta was driven out of his kingdom and went to the land of another king. Thinking, 'Here I shall eat roots and fruits in turn,' he made himself a shelter of leaves and stayed there. Then another woman who was tending cattle for her master happened to see that wandering ascetic, developed faith in her heart, and each day invited him as a guest to her meal, saying, 'Please enjoy my food.' Those cattle were not under that woman's own authority, since they belonged to her master. Therefore, the food she gave to the wandering ascetic was charged against her account there. The milk she gave to the wandering ascetic, over which she had no authority, counted as taking what was not given. Then the man who was the owner of those cattle was reborn in the land of the Śākyans. And he made this aspiration: 'In a future time, may I destroy the teachings of the Blessed One.' He is none other than the king who destroys the land of Li — do not think he is anyone else. The one who became the wandering ascetic — that is I, the Blessed One Śākyamuni. The woman who gave milk to the wandering ascetic is the noble woman who, after the buddha had performed his austerities and risen, was the first to offer him food. Thereupon the Blessed One declared: 'The reason that herdsmen and the Red-Faced Ones will come into my land is this: there arose a king named Rūpasāra. At that time, in that king's land, another woman had a son, and she went outside to till her field. Another man also ploughed beside her field. Then the son of that woman stole that man's yoke, wooden trough, flour, and grain. Because of the theft of the flour and the rest, the cattle-owners, the herdsmen, and the Red-Faced Ones will come to the land of Li. The son of that woman who carried away the flour, the yoke, the wooden trough, and the grain — he will become the king of Li. The man whose yoke and other goods were stolen — he will become the king of the Red-Faced Ones at that time. Those cattle will become soldiers at that time. The mother of that man's reborn form will become the mother of the king of Li. The mother of the man who committed theft — she took that flour and offered it as alms to a monk named Citybeautiful, a śrāvaka of the buddha. By that root of virtue she will attain rebirth as a human being. That man gave flour to his mother, and his mother offered it to the monk Citybeautiful; through the merit accumulated by that, in a future time in the land of Li there will be one called Brave Nurture, and

by that cause he will also become one called Armor of the Victor, and having obtained the royal throne, he will do battle at a meteor-stone. The one who gave the flour — by that cause he will attain rebirth as a human being, and that man will cultivate rice beside a road. Those people who allowed rice belonging to him to be consumed and wasted will at that time become monks, and for that reason they will have to pay restitution from the Saṅgha's wealth for the damage to that region. Those various birds that ate the rice will at that time become the servants of the king of Li. At that time the Blessed One spoke these words: "In this way many wild men will arise in the land of Li. They will be of benefit to Brave Nurture when he is born, and they will do him harm when the Evildoer is born. At that time those five hundred wild men will be sent into the presence of the king, the royal father. Those wild men too will take rebirth in the land of Li and will consume and waste the wealth of the Saṅgha and the wealth of the stūpas. Those wild men, because they commit theft — taking what is not given — will be put to death with their limbs cut off, and by that they will in turn kill the king of Li, Armor of the Victor. In a future time a monk called Pure Moon will arise who will liberate those wild men from cyclic existence. He too is the one who acts as the father of the former buddhas — the bodhisattva Mañjuśrī. Likewise, this bodhisattva Mañjuśrī will in a future time bear the bodily relics of those blessed buddhas of the Fortunate Aeon who are yet to come. And when the blessed buddhas pass completely beyond sorrow into nirvāṇa, the bodhisattva Mañjuśrī will become a brahmin of the Droṇa Gotra, endowed with the Dharma. When the kings make ready to do battle with one another, the brahmin Droṇa Gotra will take up a fine vessel and divide the bodily relics. When the time comes that in the land of Li there have not yet arisen a great number of bodhisattvas, a great many stūpas, images of the buddhas, or beautiful monastic gardens of the Saṅgha, at that time the bodhisattva Mañjuśrī will appear in a city and become a woman of noble lineage.

What are the activities of the bodhisattva Mañjuśrī? He has a form that transforms into whatever is desired in every situation. He appears just as a bodhisattva by nature ought to appear; he appears just as a king appears; he appears just as a queen appears; he appears in the form of an ascetic or a brahmin; and he enters among all sentient beings of the desire realm in every way. It is like a skilled musician who, being expert in the performance of music, displays every variety of appearance and form. He displays this especially intensely in relation to sentient beings possessed of a mind of passionate attachment. Why does the bodhisattva Mañjuśrī appear in the form of a woman? Because a being possessed of a mind of passionate attachment who is greatly attached to heightened sensual desire for women is exceedingly dull and exceedingly miserly — for that reason the bodhisattva Mañjuśrī appears especially as a woman. And because there are also women who practise the conduct leading to awakening, for that reason too the bodhisattva Mañjuśrī acts in such a way. There are also times when, having been born as a woman, he attains great compassion. Women too will come to feel shame, will come to perceive the faults of cyclic existence, and

will come to renounce men. When the time of the third age arrives and the great stūpas, the many fragrant rest-houses, and the fine pleasure gardens of the Saṅgha are about to be set on fire, the goddess Vimalāprabhā (Immaculate Light) perceives this, and at that moment the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara speaks to that goddess thus: "Dear sister, at the time when the Blessed One goes to the city of Ratnagarbha, I shall become your spiritual friend. Let us two, in that place, recall the aspiration we made together for the sake of awakening and for the sake of fully protecting all sentient beings who are fearful and terrified.

"Dear sister, recall how, at the very time you came to be called Vimalāprabhā, you and Vajrasena together accepted the word of the Blessed One. As for those dhāraṇī mantras, those names of buddhas, and those rays of light which you beheld here while you were practising the conduct of a bodhisattva — proclaim that these are the buddhas, memorize them and write them down, and carry them before your brother. Those rays of light sent forth by those buddhas in the world-realm of Endurance are the hallmark of the Blessed One Śākyamuni in this land of Li, and those bodhisattvas of the Fortunate Aeon will take birth there for the benefit of sentient beings.

"Many bodily relics of those abundant buddhas, those Blessed Ones, that reside in those stūpas of the dharma-kings will come into that goddess's hands, and she will distribute them among those sentient beings for the purpose of veneration. In that land compassion will arise in that goddess, and she will cause it to become a place of worship. Then she shall make an exceedingly beautiful dwelling within the city, so that all the people living in that city may thereby accumulate merit, uphold the teaching, venerate it, take delight in it, and come to engage in that conduct."

Why is it that the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, discuss the true Dharma for the sake of the land of Li? It is because the land of Li is the hallmark of the Blessed One Śākyamuni, and the hallmark of all the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, of the Fortunate Aeon; because all the buddhas of the Fortunate Aeon will gather at the stūpas of Gośṛṅga, Gomasāla, and Gandhā; and because the land of Li will become the abode of bodhisattvas.

Then the Blessed One spoke to the noble bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara: "When the time of red faces and warfare arrives, that is when the time has come for you, my younger one, to act. Even with certainty you will knowingly take rebirth again for the benefit of sentient beings. Those buddhas, the Blessed Ones, will send rays of light here and dispatch gifts to the Blessed One Śākyamuni, and those gifts too will be brought here by the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara, and out of compassion for fearful and terrified sentient beings, buddha-fields as well will have arisen. Those dhāraṇī mantras and those rays of light the buddhas will accept for the sake of the land of Li, and they will become companions to its people. Bodhisattvas too, so that the land of Li may not be destroyed, will pass away from various buddha-fields and take birth there. When harm comes to the land of Li, the names of those buddhas and

Those names of the dhāraṇī mantras and those rays of light should be written down and placed at the gates of cities, and written upon banners in the midst of armies, and affixed to victory standards. Alternatively, those names should be held, recited, and kept in mind. They will bring benefit for the pacification of enemy armies and of famine, for the increase of virtue, for the purification of misdeeds, and for liberation from illness.

If someone bears a heavy burden of misdeeds, let that person, for twenty-one days, make offerings to the buddhas of the ten directions, and then, before a stūpa that contains relics or before an image of a buddha, recite these names three times a day, morning, noon, and evening. Whatever wholesome aim one desires, one will swiftly attain it, and those rays of light will come to rest upon that person. These names should also be recited when an enemy army approaches. Or alternatively, they may be read seven times each day, once a day. For whoever is in suffering, or is ill, or has any wish whatsoever, they should be recited or read. If someone is unable to read them, let seven knots be tied in a cord, let the names be recited seven times over it, and then let it be tied on — that person will be liberated from suffering. If it is affixed to the gate of a city, no enemy will be able to enter that city.

Then the Blessed One spoke these words: "When the time comes for the outbreak of the red-faced war and battle, and when the one whom the Blessed One Vajra-host has appointed to duty, the youth Vajra-host, takes up what is called the Armor of Complete Victory, this letter shall be carried to the land of the Golden Lineage, and this letter shall be shown to the king of the Golden Lineage in private — it must not be shown before the eyes of a multitude. The ransom-offering of that land shall also be carried there; some of it shall be dispatched to that place, and some shall be set down upon a meteorite."

Then the Blessed One, having previously declared: "This is how it was before; this is how it will be in the time to come," at that moment entered into meditative absorption into the samādhi called the Vajra Maṇḍala. Then, from that state, he entered into the samādhi called Crest of the Moon, the samādhi called Stainless Light, the samādhi called Immovable, the samādhi called Utterly Without Appearance, the samādhi called Empowerment of Joy, the samādhi called Ornament of All, the samādhi called Heroic Victory Banner, the samādhi called Great Radiance, and the samādhi called Proceeding with Courage. Having entered into these ten samādhis, the Blessed One the buddha emitted from between his brows a ray of light called Great Illumination. From that ray of light ten further rays of light issued forth and went throughout all buddha-fields. Having gone there, they spread out to countless buddha-fields. In all those fields of all the blessed buddhas, in every single one of them, there arose spoken declarations just as had previously been told — concerning the youth Vajra, Vajra-host, and the goddess of Stainless Light — in connection with the land of Li.

Then, at that time, all those blessed buddhas together with the Blessed One Śākyamuni, in mutual accord of intention, pronounced this dhāraṇī mantra called the Vajra Maṇḍala:

Tadyathā — bodhi bodhi, sarva alaṃkāra, vara gata, dharma cakra vara gata, māra sena paragata, aviṣaya paragāta, avidyā pratyāya —

bhava pratyāya, niruddha pratyāya, tathāgata, maraṇa hara, buddhya satyena, nāga satyena, asura satyena, sarva vara satyena —

This dhāraṇī mantra called the Vajra Maṇḍala was proclaimed by all the buddhas. At the very moment the goddess of Stainless Light heard it, she came to understand the dhāraṇī mantra of the Vajra Maṇḍala.

Then, at that time when the buddhas proclaimed this teaching for the sake of the land of Li, the goddess of Stainless Light joined her palms and spoke these words: "I am known to the Blessed One, the buddha himself. From the pure buddha-field — the world-system individually adorned by the light-rays of Vairocana, of the Blessed One, the buddha named Abundantly Blooming Flowers of Omniscient Knowledge, King of Stars, Unstained Petal of the Lotus — I came to this world-system of Endurance to benefit my mother, to seek the sacred Dharma of the White Lotus, and to make aspirational prayers before the Tathāgata Śākyamuni. Having become the laywoman (upāsikā) named Huśa, and having met the brahmin Ocean-of-Dust, I thereafter received a prophecy. At that time I made this aspiration: 'In whatever world-system you shall attain buddhahood, may I too practice the conduct of awakening there.' Having done so, I then came from that world-system — the one called Individually Adorned by the Light-rays of Vairocana — the buddha-field of the Tathāgata Abundantly Blooming Flowers of Omniscient Knowledge, King of Stars, Unstained Petal of the Lotus — to this world-system of Endurance, into the presence of the Blessed One Śākyamuni. And the Blessed One declared: 'The people of this world-system of Endurance are attached and clinging, filled with hatred and swollen with pride and arrogance. For their sake, bodhisattvas take upon themselves great deeds toward one another, clearing away the misdeeds of those beings in every lifetime.'

Having heard this, I then made this aspiration: 'In just the same manner and by the same means by which others come here through the aspiration I have made, may I too come here in that very way.' At that time, for the sake of her who had been my mother in past lives, and in order to prevent her from falling into the lower realms, I took birth. In that earlier time in this world-system of Endurance, having become the laywoman named Huśa, and after the youth of Fine Wealth had questioned me concerning liberation — from that time onward I took birth after birth among human beings in human form. I practiced desire together with sentient beings, and other people saw me as little better than a fool. For the complete liberation of sentient beings, I took on many unbearable births among women — as a queen, as a king's daughter, as an ordinary woman, as a prostitute, as one afflicted by hardship, and as a poor woman — and I worked for the benefit of those born as women, liberating women entirely. Though, driven by the force of my own karma, I practiced sensual desire as I wished, I was unable to bring satisfaction to those sentient beings. Over many lifetimes in this world-system of Endurance I experienced great sufferings for the sake of sentient beings. Though I became utterly weary of being born as a woman, still the Blessed One enjoined upon me a great task. And so, for countless lifetimes, I shall willingly endure suffering. In order to please the Blessed One Śākyamuni and to accomplish buddhahood itself, I made this great aspiration:

For the benefit of all sentient beings, I shall willingly endure whatever sufferings arise for me in other lifetimes. As for the command the Blessed One gave me — "Protect that realm!" — to this day there are still no men there, nor has the realm yet come to be. Nevertheless, I shall take the excellent vows from among all those buddhas and Blessed Ones, their retinues and assemblies gathered there: gods, nāgas, yakṣas, asuras, garuḍas, gandharvas, kinnaras, great serpents, rākṣasas, humans, and non-humans alike. For as long as I take birth in those lifetimes, even if I am seen to have attachment or aversion as my basis, may I not become sinful on that account. May I obtain an appropriate time of birth, and may I hold to such a course as this. When the time comes for the realm of Li to first arise, may I then, in order to govern that realm, be the very first to become its king. In Li, when the names of those kings are established and the first royal dynasty arises, may I reign as king for seven lifetimes. And I shall build the city called Dngarldan. In the Nepali se'u grove, I shall build a great buddha stūpa. In the realm of Rtā 'jug I shall build a stūpa for the dharmic king. In 'Gumti I shall build a great pleasure garden for the Saṅgha. In Rtsa rma ba na ra as well I shall build a great pleasure garden for the Saṅgha. In Pāyādika I shall build a great fragrant house and a great stūpa, and for as long as I dwell in royal rule I shall perform great deeds. When the time comes that the king passes away, may I be reborn among the gods where the bodhisattva Maitreya (Loving-Kindness) dwells, and may I there recall the meditative absorptions I had attained. Then, taking birth once more in this realm of Li, and becoming a woman's form just as the Blessed One commanded, I shall be born as the daughter of the king of Skar rdo, and be called Rab nges. At that time, may I recall all the dhāraṇī mantras that all the blessed buddhas have taught and that I have understood. May I also recall this very sūtra with great clarity, so that everything may be proclaimed in full. When two armies come to battle and there is work to be done for the sake of the realm of Li, and when the youth Vajrasena, king of Skar rdo, known as the Completely Victorious Armor, reaches the time of giving ransom for the realm of Li — at that time, as I who am called Rab nges, may all those blessed buddhas and all the gods assembled within that retinue become my allies. May I recall, in all lifetimes, this sūtra, all those dhāraṇī mantras taught along with dhāraṇī explanations, and all the meditative absorptions.

When the goddess Vimalaprabha, the Light of Stainless Purity, had spoken those words, she then attained meditative absorptions — and specifically she attained the meditative absorption called the Swirling Expanse of the White Lotus, the Supreme Dharma of the Mahāyāna Sūtra, by which no one can cause harm to this sūtra. At that moment all the buddhas gave their approval, saying "Excellent!" and declared: "Whenever you set out to compose it, those light rays will abide above, and then whoever recalls this sūtra and practices it most earnestly will attain the meditative absorption called Buddhas Brought to Mind."

The blessed buddhas then spoke a dhāraṇī called the Vajra Maṇḍala. Through the intrinsic nature of this dhāraṇī one comes to understand dhāraṇīs, one purifies meditative absorption, and one recalls everything one has previously heard as Dharma from the blessed buddhas. The goddess Vimalaprabha, the Light of Stainless Purity, likewise comes to understand meditative absorption and dhāraṇīs just as the bodhisattvas understand them. Through that which is called the Armor of Perfect Victory, sovereignty is attained, and no one else is able to create obstacles to it. The function of these is as follows: after reciting them seven times, one should also recite the vidyā-mantra of Hayagrīva, by which sins are purified. If one recites it three times by day and three times by night, within seven years one will come to understand the dhāraṇī of the Vajra Maṇḍala.

When the blessed Buddha had taught this dhāraṇī of the Vajra Maṇḍala, the gods rained down divine flowers upon the Blessed One and sounded the music of the gods, and said these words: "We too shall bestow the dhāraṇīs we have previously heard from the blessed buddhas."

Namas sarva atyudgata. Vajramaṇḍalodtamasya. Tathāgatasya. Kanakābha. Satyasya. Bodhisattvasya. Tadyathā. Buddhasattvena. Dasabiri. Dena hayari. Kurciru pratipadari pabhavatu. Citumatu galara yakadamapatu. Sapajama sarata svāhā.

This dhāraṇī is called the Dhāraṇī that Cuts with the Severance of the Gods. If one recites it one hundred and eight times and makes a fire offering of one hundred and eight stalks of dūrvā grass, then whatever one does, the gods will be unable to obstruct it.

Having made a circular maṇḍala, if one recites it one hundred and eight times and then casts one hundred and eight knots, spirits will be unable to cause harm wherever that recitation takes place, and one will also be unable to be afflicted by sorcery.

Then at that time all those in the assembly spoke this dhāraṇī:

Tadyathā. Sarva devanān. Sarva varṣanaṇa. Nirjita brjite. Skandha nacavan. Nata mana pana gasara. Dada caca papa. Cada mima. Bhasthimi. Daṇḍakaraṇahara. Savara. Deva mara svāhā. Sava pāvarṣakaraṇa svāhā.

This dhāraṇī spoken by all the assembly should be recited one hundred and eight times. One should tie one hundred and eight knots, and then burn the cord, whereupon sorcery will be unable to gain a foothold in that household and illnesses caused by spirits will also be entirely dispelled.

Recite this dhāraṇī seven times over a young boy, tie seven knots in a yak-tail whisk, and then fasten it on him.

Tadyathā. Sarva buddha dama vaṣakarava vara cacarasa sara svāhā.

For all illnesses that arise in the body, recite this dhāraṇī seven times and tie seven knots in a cord, then fasten it on the person.

Then at that time all those in the assembly knelt upon their right knees and made this request: "When the young Vajrasena travels from the land of Karadu to the land of Li, we wish to bestow the dhāraṇī through which heroic deeds will be accomplished in his wake."

Enemies will then be unable to cause them harm, and they will accomplish heroic deeds.

When Mahāśvara, Vaiśravaṇa's son, Sarvajayin, and Harītī together with her children had recited these dhāraṇī mantras, they then made this petition: "Just as the Blessed One has appointed us to accomplish heroic deeds, we now wish to recite those dhāraṇī mantras that others cannot withstand. For the sake of the land of Lī, we will accept ransoms, and we will give and receive brides in exchange with the enemy, making peace."

Then at that time the Blessed Buddha entrusted the land of Lī to both the divine goddess Nirmalāprabhā and the young Vajrasena. At that time the Blessed Buddha declared to the young Vajrasena: "You shall become king of the land of Lī, and the divine goddess Nirmalāprabhā shall be your companion and ally. Do everything just as it has been taught in this sūtra. Now, at the time when you return to the land of the golden lineage, when you hear the news that the enemy's army has come to the land of Lī —"

Go to the land of Lī without hesitation, and if you send a messenger there, he will not come to you — he will go to the land of Lī and the land of Guzan. Send word by letter saying: 'Make alliance with me and give me the territory.' Then at that time you shall become king of the land of Lī. The one called Indra's Armor shall become king of the land of Karṇa, and he too shall become your ally in becoming king of the land of Lī. When harm comes to the land of Karṇa, do everything in your power to prevent that harm from occurring. First make an alliance with Indra's Armor as well — he will become an ally in these undertakings. All the people of Karṇa, however many there may be, shall also become allies.

There is a hero named Akuna. In a former life, when he was known as Jñānakīrti, he built a temple called Dharmatīra. Therefore you should seek out Akuna, for he will become your ally in taking the territory. Why is this? Because the two of you made aspirations together in a former life. This man named Akuna was formerly a king whose wife was abducted by barbarians and was never recovered. You were the king's great minister, and you were one who brought joy.

Then at that time the Blessed Buddha declared to the young Vajrasena: "Young Vajrasena, you shall become king of the land of Lī, bearing the title King of Victorious Armor, and you shall hold royal power for thirty years. Having become a lay practitioner, you shall attain the fruit of fully giving your body for the sake of the land of Lī."

Then at that time the Blessed One declared to the divine goddess Nirmalāprabhā: "When you become known in the land of Lī as Rabnge, take up this sūtra. Enter into it, spread it widely, and send it to those without family in the land of Karṇa as well. Rays of light shall abide upon you.
