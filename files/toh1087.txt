The Dhāraṇī of the Goddess Mahākālī (Devīmahākālīnāmadhāraṇī)

The Dhāraṇī of the Goddess Mahākālī, known in Sanskrit as Devī Mahākālī Nāma Dhāraṇī and in Tibetan as Lha mo nag mo chen mo'i gzungs, is a short scripture centered on a powerful protective incantation. It is set shortly after the Buddha's awakening, as he stays at the foot of the tree of awakening, when the goddess Mahākālī approaches him. She is described as Sovereign Lady of the Desire Realm, mother of Māra, and sister of Yama, and she offers the Buddha a knowledge-mantra in homage and asks him to accept it. The Buddha responds by charging her with binding and protective duties, framing a once-formidable deity as a guardian in service of the Dharma. The work is Toh 1087 in the Kangyur, where it belongs to the class of dhāraṇī texts, scriptures devoted to the recitation of potent spells for protection and blessing.

In the language of India: devī mahākālī nāma dhāraṇī. In the language of Tibet: The Dhāraṇī of the Goddess Mahākālī.

Homage to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas.

Thus did I hear at one time. Not long after the Blessed One had become fully and perfectly awakened, he was staying at the foot of the tree of awakening. Then the goddess Mahākālī, Sovereign Lady of the Desire Realm, mother of Māra, and sister of Yama, circumambulated the Blessed One three times and sat down to one side.

Then the goddess Mahākālī said to the Blessed One, "Venerable Blessed One, I offer this knowledge-mantra in homage at the feet of the Blessed One. May the Blessed One accept it from me."

Then the Blessed One declared, "Mother of Māra, sister of Yama, goddess Mahākālī, you should bind everything above, as far up as the Paranirmitavaśavartin heaven. You should bind everything below, as far down as the hells. And on the level, all around, you should bind everything as far as the encircling ring of the Cakravāla mountains. These mantras of hers should also be recited:

tadyathā oṃ ruru vitiṣṭha badho hsi ruru roru vitiṣṭa badho tsi svāhā

By reciting this, epidemics, diseases, misfortunes, all harms, leprosy, boils, itching, and all other such diseases will be pacified. svāhā"

Here ends the Dhāraṇī of the Goddess Mahākālī.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The final clause of the Buddha's instruction reads "khor yug tu ni ril thug pa". The phrase kho ra khor yug names the Cakravāla, the ring of mountains around the world system. The syllable ril is unclear here. It could mean "entirely," or it may be a slip for ri, "mountain." Either way the sense, "as far as the encircling mountains," is not affected.

The instrumental case on Mahākālī's name makes her the one who is to do the binding, so the lines are rendered as instructions addressed to her.

The closing syllable after the colophon is a scribal end-mark and is not rendered.
