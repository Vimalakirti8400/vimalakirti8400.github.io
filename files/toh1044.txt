Dhāraṇī for Not Forgetting (The Dhāraṇī of Avalokiteśvara for Retention)

The Dhāraṇī for Learning a Thousand Ślokas is a brief incantation text that pairs a short mantra with a simple ritual recipe for strengthening memory and learning. After paying homage to the Three Jewels and to the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara, the great being endowed with great compassion, it gives a dhāraṇī invoking the powers of intelligence, radiance, and the retention of what one has heard. It then instructs the practitioner to grind the medicinal herb brāhmī, boil it in milk, recite the mantra over it twenty-one times, and drink it for seven days, promising that one will retain whatever one hears, whether the words of the Buddha or a treatise, and will be able to learn a thousand verses in a single day and night. The work is Toh 1044 in the Kangyur, where it belongs among the dhāraṇī texts, the class of short scriptures centered on spells and mnemonic formulas for protection, blessing, and practical benefit.

Homage to the Three Jewels.

Homage to the noble Avalokiteśvara, the bodhisattva, the great being, who is endowed with great compassion.

bīre bīre mati teje teja mati dhāraṇī śruta dhāraṇī jayanti bijayanti mati dhāraṇī svāhā

Grind two measures of the medicinal herb brāhmī finely, boil it in milk, and recite this mantra twenty-one times over it. If one drinks it for seven days, one will without doubt retain whatever one hears. Whether it is the word of the Buddha or a treatise, if one hears it, one will learn a thousand ślokas in a single day and night.

The dhāraṇī for learning a thousand ślokas is complete.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The phrase rendered "without doubt" is the Tibetan idiom for certainty and has nothing to do with the term for seizing spirits (graha). The medicinal ingredient is brāhmī, an herb traditionally associated with memory. The quantity is given as two "zho," a small Tibetan unit of weight, rendered here as "two measures." The final syllable of the colophon is a scribal ending mark and is not translated.
