The Dhāraṇī for Polishing the Jewel

The Dhāraṇī for Polishing the Jewel, known in Tibetan by a title along the lines of Rinpoche Darwé Zung, is a very brief ritual instruction that pairs a short mantra with a simple devotional act. After paying homage to the Three Jewels, it directs the practitioner to recite the mantra while polishing a jewel, imagining that a boundless rain of jeweled parasols, victory banners, and similar precious offerings fills all of space, and then to offer that vision. In this way an ordinary physical action becomes a vast visualized offering, reflecting the broader Buddhist practice of multiplying gifts through the power of the mind. The work is Toh 1074 in the Kangyur, where it belongs among the dhāraṇī texts, the class of short incantations and their associated practices that are often grouped with the tantric scriptures.

Homage to the Three Jewels.

While reciting oṃ smara smara bismanaskar mahājapa hūṃ and polishing the jewel, one should imagine that a measureless rain of jewel parasols, victory banners, and the like fills the element of space, and offer it.

The Dhāraṇī for Polishing the Jewel is complete.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

"Polishing the jewel" renders rin po che bdar ba, literally the rubbing or polishing of a precious object. In practice this most likely refers to wiping or polishing an offering base, such as a maṇḍala plate, while reciting the mantra. The final syllable of the colophon is an unclear scribal flourish and has not been translated.
