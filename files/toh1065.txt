The Noble Dhāraṇī Called "The Questions of the Nāga King Manasvin" (Āryamanasvināgarājaparipṛcchānāmadhāraṇī)

The Noble Dhāraṇī Called "The Questions of the Nāga King Manasvin," whose Sanskrit title is preserved in an irregular form roughly rendered as Ārya Tapasvi Nāgarāja Paripṛcchā Nāma Dhāraṇī, and whose Tibetan title is Phakpa Lu'i Gyalpo Zichen Gyi Zhupa, is a short scripture in the form of a dhāraṇī, a protective incantation taught by the Buddha. It opens in the land of Magadha, where the Buddha is staying in a cave on Mount Videha. At nightfall the radiant nāga king Manasvin comes to him and asks what he should do if the serpents of the mountain harm the monks living there. The Buddha praises the question and responds with teachings meant to protect the monastic community from such dangers. The work is Toh 1065 in the Kangyur, and it belongs to the class of dhāraṇī texts, the incantations of protection and blessing gathered within the canon.

In the language of Tibet: The Noble Dhāraṇī Called "The Questions of the Nāga King Manasvin."

Homage to all buddhas and bodhisattvas.

Thus did I hear at one time. The Blessed One was staying in the land of Magadha, in a cave on Videha, king of mountains. Then, when night had come, the nāga king Manasvin, possessed of a surpassingly excellent complexion, went to where the Blessed One was. When he arrived, he bowed his head at the Blessed One's feet and spoke to him these words:

"Blessed One, if the long ones on this mountain Videha harm the monks, how should I act in that case?"

When he had asked this, the Blessed One spoke these words to the nāga king Manasvin: "Excellent, nāga king, excellent! Such is what you should do."

Then the Blessed One said to the monks: "Monks, therefore, commit to memory the names of these eighteen, the poisons and the great medicines:

tadyathā baṇḍare karḍe keyūre podhyadgamate bindumahe araḍa taraḍa taruḍa dahadahana etadaha jele jela phale śataśata kulen kuli ekarṣitaradhapriṣṭi

"Monks, whoever holds in mind the names of these eighteen, the poisons and the great medicines, his body will not be overcome by fever. He will not be overcome by poison, weapons, sorcery, vetālas, or by fevers that come every day, every second day, every third day, or every fourth day. The long ones will not harm him. Whoever holds these will be protected for as long as we remain."

When the Blessed One had spoken thus, the nāga king Manasvin rejoiced and praised what the Blessed One had said.

This completes the Noble Dhāraṇī Called "The Questions of the Nāga King Manasvin."
