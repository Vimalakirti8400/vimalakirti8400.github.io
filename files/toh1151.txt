Praise to All the Tathāgatas (Sarvatathāgatastotra)

The Praise to All the Tathāgatas, known in Sanskrit as the Sarvatathāgata Stotra and in Tibetan as De bzhin gshegs pa thams cad la bstod pa, is a short devotional hymn addressed collectively to all the buddhas. Opening with homage to the Three Jewels, it unfolds in verses that each close with the same refrain of bowing to all the Tathāgatas, celebrating their supreme teaching, their skill in accomplishing the welfare of others, their abandonment of every contaminated habitual tendency, their direct insight into the nature of all things, and their role as the source of the sublime Dharma and teachers of the path to nirvāṇa. In this way the poem offers both an expression of reverence and a concise portrait of the qualities that define awakened beings. It is Toh 1151 in the Tengyur, where it belongs to the collection of praises, the class of hymns and eulogies that opens that portion of the canon.

In the language of India: sarba tathāgata stotraṃ
In the language of Tibet: Praise to All the Tathāgatas

Homage to the Three Jewels.

Your teaching has become supreme for all beings,
You are thoroughly skilled at accomplishing the aims of others,
You draw out completely the great serpent of the afflictions:
To all the Tathāgatas I pay homage and bow.

You have abandoned every contaminated habitual tendency,
You see with certainty the very nature of all things,
You strive for the welfare of all living beings:
To all the Tathāgatas I pay homage and bow.

You are the vessel, the source of the precious sublime Dharma,
You teach the supreme path, nirvāṇa,
You fulfill completely every hope of the mind:
To all the Tathāgatas I pay homage and bow.

You are devoted in tender care toward the worlds,
Wholly suffused with love and compassion,
You bring to perfection the conduct that benefits all:
To all the Tathāgatas I pay homage and bow.

You fulfill completely every wish for the good of all,
You have eyes that open the lotuses of those to be trained,
You give rise on a vast scale to the virtues of excellent disciples:
To all the Tathāgatas I pay homage and bow.

You have gathered the accumulations through limitless eons,
Brahmā, lord of the pure realms, and Indra, lord of the gods, bow to you,
Your body is adorned with an aura of light a fathom wide:
To all the Tathāgatas I pay homage and bow.

You have destroyed the root of faults without remainder,
With the fire of wisdom you have burned the firewood of stains,
You bring sublime discernment to perfection:
To all the Tathāgatas I pay homage and bow.

You have vanquished all afflictions together with their imprints,
Your great renown resounds everywhere,
You are adorned with the thirty-two supreme marks,
Your lotus-like eyes are wide open,
You resemble a heap of refined gold:
To all the Tathāgatas I pay homage and bow.

By this vast and wondrous merit,
May I cast out my own afflictions together with their imprints,
And may I draw out the beings who have sunk
Into this great ocean of the afflictions of saṃsāra.

The Praise to All the Tathāgatas is complete.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The eighth stanza has six lines rather than four; it is translated as it stands. The phrase "draw out completely the great serpent of the afflictions" renders a verb that can mean either to extract or to expel; both readings fit the image of removing a poisonous serpent.
