The Stages of Putting Hevajra into Practice (Hevajrasya Hastavyavahārakrama)

The Stages of Putting Hevajra into Practice, known in Tibetan as Gyepa Dorjé Lak tu Langwé Rimpa and given a Sanskrit title along the lines of Hevajrasya Hastavyavahārakrama, is a ritual manual for the practice of Hevajra, the central deity of the Hevajra Tantra. It sets out in order the practitioner's own preparation and deity yoga, the rites of the maṇḍala, the bestowal of empowerment on disciples, and the consecration of images, books and temples, closing with the commissioning of protectors and verses on the work's composition. It is Toh 1294 in the Tengyur, where it belongs among the commentaries and ritual works of the tantra section, in the cycle of texts devoted to the Hevajra Tantra. Its closing verses name a translator, Sönam Gyaltsen, who condensed and rendered the stages of the rite into Tibetan.

In the language of India: hevajrasya hastabyabarkrama nāma. In the language of Tibet: The Stages of Putting Hevajra into Practice.

Homage to glorious Vajrasattva.

Here, first, one who is oneself the practitioner, endowed with excellent understanding, enters the meditation chamber, resolves that all phenomena are without self, and performs the outer bath and the inner bath of nectar. Then one gathers whatever articles are available, makes the maṇḍala, unrolls the painted scroll, and with scented water performs the sprinkling, the sipping and the touching. By saying oṃ he vajra atiṣṭhāna mama rakṣa rakṣa hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ svāhā, oneself and the rest become protected.

Then, sitting on a good seat, one blesses the offering articles with oṃ vajra puṣpe oṃ āḥ hūṃ, oṃ vajra dhūpe oṃ āḥ hūṃ, oṃ vajra āloke oṃ āḥ hūṃ, oṃ vajra gandhe oṃ āḥ hūṃ, oṃ vajra naividye oṃ āḥ hūṃ. The other offering substances are blessed with oṃ āḥ hūṃ, seven or twenty-one times.

Then, by the light of the hūṃ at one's own heart, one invites the Blessed One together with his retinue:

hūṃ You who are encircled by the yoginīs,
Who embrace the indivisible vajra,
Come with supreme delight
And please grant your blessing.

By saying oṃ vajra gaurī jaḥ oṃ vajra caurī hūṃ oṃ vajra vetālī baṃ oṃ vajra ghasmarī hoḥ, one sees them vividly as directly present, and should make offerings:

This flower, a fine divine substance,
Born from the water and from the land,
I offer with supreme devotion;
Please accept it as you please.
oṃ sarva tathāgata puṣpe pūja migha samudra spharaṇa samaye hūṃ

This incense, a fine divine substance,
Blended with the coemergent,
I offer with supreme devotion;
Please accept it as you please.
oṃ sarva tathāgata dhūpe pūja megha samudra spharaṇa samaye hūṃ

This lamp, which vanquishes the grahas,
Auspicious, virtuous, dispelling darkness,
I offer with supreme devotion;
Please accept it as you please.
oṃ sarva tathāgata āloke pūja megha samudra spharaṇa samaye hūṃ

The delightful essence of the forest,
This divine water, blended and fragrant,
I offer with supreme devotion;
Please accept it as you please.
oṃ sarva tathāgata gandhe pūja megha samudra spharaṇa samaye hūṃ

The delightful essence of medicinal herbs,
This food offering, a meal of the mantra,
I offer with supreme devotion;
Please accept it as you please.
oṃ sarva tathāgata nevitye pruja megha samudra spharaṇa samaye hūṃ

Then the eight goddesses make offerings:
Gaurī holds the hare,
Caurī holds the sun,
Vetālī the vajra water,
Ghasmarī holds medicine,
The incense goddess holds a stick of sandalwood,
Śavarī holds honey,
Caṇḍālī sounds the ḍamaru drum,
And, following in great passion,
Ḍombinī embraces him about the neck,
And is said to act as a man does.
With the outer, the inner and the secret,
With these they worship the lord.
oṃ sarva tathāgata anuttara pūja megha samudra sparaṇa samaye hūṃ

Then one makes confession:
Whatever wrongs I have committed,
And whatever I have rejoiced in or caused to be done,
In the presence of the perfect buddhas
I confess each and every one, without exception.

Then one rejoices:
The merit of the buddhas and bodhisattvas,
Of the noble śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas,
And of ordinary beings:
In all of it I rejoice.

Then one dedicates:
All these virtues of mine,
In common with all sentient beings,
I dedicate to the full ripening and completion
Of unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening.

Then one goes for refuge:
In the perfect Buddha, supreme among the two-legged,
In the sacred Dharma that he taught,
And in the Saṅgha of bodhisattvas,
I go for refuge until awakening.

Then one generates the aspiring mind of supreme awakening:
Having become perfectly and completely awakened
In unsurpassed awakening,
For the sake of benefiting all sentient beings,
I shall establish them in nonabiding nirvāṇa.

Then one generates the engaging mind of supreme awakening:
By perfecting all the perfections,
Generosity and the rest,
I shall rightly follow the path
Of the unsurpassed Vajra Vehicle.

Then, having meditated on the four immeasurables in order, and having contemplated the accumulation of wisdom as the awakening of emptiness, one imagines, within the fence, the tent and the rest, a square celestial palace with portals, formed from the transformation of the four elements. Inside it, on the respective seats, are the four māras in the center, Brahmā in the east, Indra in the south, Upendra (Viṣṇu) in the west, Rudra in the north, Yama in the northeast, Kubera in the southeast, Nairṛti in the southwest, and Vemacitrin in the northwest; and upon them one imagines disks of the sun, and says oṃ sarva tathāgata anilaya vajra svabhāva ātmako'haṃ.

Then, in its center, the five manifest awakenings:
The moon has the mirror-like wisdom;
The sun has the wisdom of equality;
The seed and hand emblem of one's own deity
Are said to be discernment itself;
Their becoming all one is accomplishment;
The completion is the pure sphere of reality.

By this one generates the causal Vajradhara, white in color, together with a blue consort, with sixteen faces and arms, his body adorned with the five mudrās and with ash. One blesses the secret places with padma sukhadhara and the rest, and with oṃ vajra mahādeśva and the rest, and sets them in motion by the dependent arising of siṃ siṃ and the rest. From the melting in passion brought about by oṃ sarva tathāgata unurāgaṇa vajra svabhāva ātmako'haṃ, one should emanate the wheel of goddesses through the five corresponding manifest awakenings.

Here, for Gaurī: on the eastern seat, from the previous merit and wisdom, the vowels and consonants appear, and from their transformation come the moon and the sun; this is the second manifest awakening. Within them, ghaṃ, which has melted, turns into a noose and curved knife; this, marked with ghaṃ and resting on the moon and sun, is the third awakening. Then from the seed ghaṃ light rays arise in the form of yoginīs; having accomplished the welfare of all sentient beings, they are gathered back, and the sun, moon and the rest melt; this is the fourth awakening. The completion of Gaurī's form, black in color, the great fierce one and so forth, is the fifth awakening.

In the same way, this is applied to Caurī and the others as well.

When the work of generating the retinue has thus been completed, the principal who comes about in its center through the cause is the intermediate state of becoming. The syllables oṃ āḥ hūṃ ho, set between the two red ones, abide in the manner of the gandharva of the intermediate state of becoming hovering nearby. Entering the mouth of the joyful one, it settles at the end of the lotus and, melting, becomes endowed with mindfulness; at that very moment the principal together with his consort also melts.

When the cause has thus been completed, there is the urging by the condition, song. Since the four goddesses stationed in the corners perform the action of urging, one should sing the four songs of loving-kindness and the rest:

Arise, venerable lord, whose mind is compassion!
Protect me, Pukkasī!
Abandon that very nature of emptiness,
And deign to join with me in great bliss.

If you do not stay, I shall die;
Hevajra, arise!
Abandon that very nature of emptiness,
And bring Śavarī's fruit to accomplishment.

Lord of joy, host of the world,
Do you dwell in emptiness?
Since without you I know no direction,
I, Caṇḍālī, make this request.

Since I long for your heart,
You who command magical illusion, arise!
I, Ḍombinī, have lost my mind and mindfulness;
Do not cut off the stream of your compassion!

Urged by the condition of these words, one should generate the resultant Heruka in an instant through the five manifest awakenings, in conformity with miraculous birth. That is, it melts and becomes two spheres. One sphere becomes the two, sun and moon, above and below, and between them a vajra from the seed hūṃ, which is marked with the seed. From that, light rays in the form of yoginīs are emanated and gathered back; from their becoming one, it takes on the defining features of black body color and the rest. In the same way, Nairātmyā also arises through the five awakenings, from oṃ, the curved knife and the rest. Immediately after the striding posture of the legs and the rest have formed, he becomes the lord of the maṇḍala in the central place.

Then again one makes clear the body colors, emblems and the rest of the retinue, and one makes the three nested beings. Here, in the heart of the hero, from paṃ comes an eight-petaled lotus, from raṃ a sun, and upon it from hūṃ a vajra. That is marked with hūṃ, and from it is generated the wisdom being, resembling oneself. In its heart in turn, on a lotus and a sun, is a vajra transformed from hūṃ and marked with hūṃ. Likewise for the others too, one makes the three nested vajra beings with hūṃ.

Then, from the concentration being of the principal, the wheel of wisdom is invited into the sky in front. Having worshipped it with mental offerings arising from that clear visualization, one says oṃ sarva tathāgata anuttara pūja megha samudra sparaṇa maye hūṃ.

Then, with vajra song and with mudrās, by oṃ vajra gaurī vajra aṃkuśa jaḥ oṃ vajra cautī vajra pāśa hūṃ oṃ vajra vetālī vajra sphoṭa baṃ oṃ vajra ghasmarī vajra ghaṇḍe hoḥ, they are summoned, made to enter, bound and brought under control, and they become inseparable from the wheel of commitment.

Then, so that the wheel of wisdom may rest in the commitment and to generate its power, the sense sources of all the deities are blessed, and also body, speech and mind. In the principal's eyes is the seed maṃ; from its transformation one imagines Mohavajrī vividly and says maṃ mohavajri oṃ āḥ hūṃ. In the ears, from the seed ḍaṃ, one imagines Dveṣavajrī and says daṃ dveśavajri oṃ āḥ hūṃ. In the nose, from the transformation of paṃ, one imagines Mātsaryavajrī and says paṃ beśunavajri oṃ āḥ hūṃ. In the mouth, from the transformation of raṃ, one imagines Rāgavajrī and says rāgavajre oṃ āḥ hūṃ. In the body, from the transformation of oṃ, one imagines Īrṣyāvajrī and says oṃ iraṣyavajra oṃ āḥ hūṃ. At the heart, in the mind, from the transformation of the seed naṃ, one imagines Nairātmyā and says naṃ naira ātmavajra oṃ āḥ hūṃ.

Likewise, at the principal's heart, throat and crown, from the seeds oṃ āḥ hūṃ, one vividly generates Khecarī, the vajra lady of body; Bhūcarī, the vajra lady of speech; and, dwelling beneath the feet, the vajra lady of mind; and one says oṃ sarva tathāgata khecari vajra svabhāva ātmako'haṃ, oṃ sarva tathāgata bhūcari vajra svabhāva ātmako'haṃ, oṃ sarva tathāgata pātālavasini vajra svabhāva ātmako'haṃ. In the same way this is applied in order to all the deities.

Then, by light rays issuing from the principal's heart, one invites all the bodhisattvas into the sky in front, and again worships them with light rays, saying oṃ sarva tathāgata anuttara pūja megha samudra sparaṇa samaye hūṃ. Once they are pleased by this, one supplicates them with oṃ sarva tathāgata abhiśiñcatu maṃ, whereupon those buddhas and bodhisattvas become glorious Heruka with eight faces and sixteen arms. With vases of the five tathāgatas melted, they empower all the deities, and the lord of each one's family arises upon the head; a rain of flowers and the like occurs, and one says oṃ sarva tathāgata abhiṣeka vajra svabhāva ātmako'haṃ.

Then again, from the light rays of the seed at one's own heart, one emanates hosts of offering goddesses and worships the wheel, together with oneself as the principal, saying oṃ sarva tathāgata pūja vajra svabhāva ātamko'haṃ.

Then, with the verse beginning "Supreme nature of all things," or the one beginning "You who are encircled by the yoginīs," one imagines the retinue praising the principal; one also sings oneself, and says oṃ sarva tathāgata prastute vajra svabhāva ātmako'haṃ.

Then one performs the rite of tasting the nectar. Possessing the pride of being oneself the principal, one should take pride that the deities of the maṇḍala are not different from oneself, since they arise from the appearance of one's own wisdom. Then, in front of oneself, in the manner of worldly cooking, from the transformation of kaṃ come three skulls, and from yaṃ wind. Then, upon fire transformed from raṃ, is a skull cup transformed from a. Inside it are nectar pills or powder mixed with an intoxicant; from bi ra śu ma mu and go ku da ha na they take on the nature of the five nectars and the five lamps.

Upon it, from the transformation of hūṃ, one imagines a crossed vajra like a lid. Then, as the wind stirs the fire, the nectars and lamps in the skull cup mix and boil, and light rays like steam arise. By these the nectar of wisdom is summoned from the hearts of all the tathāgatas; it dissolves into the vajra, which melts, and it dissolves into the nectar of commitment and becomes mixed with it. One should bless it by saying oṃ āḥ hūṃ seven or twenty-one times.

Then one generates and visualizes all the deities of the maṇḍala in the heart of oneself, vividly present as the principal; or else one imagines them as appearances like light rays transformed from oneself. On one's own tongue one imagines a vajra, transformed from hūṃ, the size of a barley grain. Through the straws of its light rays one tastes the flavor of the nectar, and says oṃ sarva tathāgata amṛta susatvana vajra svabhāva ātmako'haṃ.

If, in this way, the six limbs
Are meditated on with devotion
As the six universal monarchs,
One will swiftly attain awakening.
These are its signs:
First, that which is like clouds,
Up to the cloudless sky.
Then the completion, suchness:
One should kindle caṇḍālī and the rest.
When wearied by that yoga,
One should recollect suchness,
Recite mantra, give the torma,
And conduct oneself with heightened devotion.

Their meaning is this:
"The six limbs" means
The basis and union,
Empowerment, offering, praise and the tasting of nectar.
"The six universal monarchs" means
The eternal Vajradhara, and
Akṣobhya, Amoghasiddhi, Ratnasambhava,
And Amitābha, or else the wisdoms.
"Meditated on with devotion" means
That when meditating on those aspects,
Devotion has the nature of the buddhas.
"One will swiftly attain awakening" means
Through the power of supreme dependent arising,
Because Vajrasattva knows it.
"Its signs" means
The mind's being stable and entering suchness,
And the marks of accomplishment being achieved.
"First, that which is like clouds" means
That a coemergent maiden endowed with power,
Or an action seal, manifestly appears.
"Up to the cloudless sky"
Means that meditation, conduct and the power of the result,
The mudrās and the rest, are applied in order.
"The completion, suchness" means
The two stages joined into one,
Or relied upon in order to bestow blessing.
"Kindle caṇḍālī and the rest" means
That by destroying signs and conceptions,
Blazing light rays arise
From the syllable a and the drop.
"And the rest": meditate on other completion stages too.
"When wearied by that yoga" means
That if one is wearied by the two stages,
There are three kinds of respite.
"Recollect suchness" means
Recollecting oneself as the aggregates as deities,
The sense sources and the rest as goddesses,
And the attire of Heruka
As the purity of the perfections.
"Recite mantra" means:
At the time of mantra recitation,
Having made oneself appear
As the two stages gathered into one,
One devotes oneself to a meditation
Like the moon resting in the expanse of the sky;
The nature of the maṇḍala wheel
Becomes like the moon in water.
And the mantra recitation there:
Oneself having become the gathering as one,
One recites ten thousand or a hundred thousand times, in a long, clear voice;
At that time those of the maṇḍala as well
Recite in the manner of an echo.
"Give the torma" means:
Here is the activity of the torma,
From the scripture of the explanatory tantra and from the instructions.
Oneself having become the gathering as one,
In a triangular source of phenomena in front,
One makes lotuses in three tiers, above and below.
Above are Brahmā and the other gods,
Born from oṃ, white in color, marked by the Eternal One,
Holding vajra and bell,
And joined with a consort like themselves.
The middle lotus arises from hūṃ,
Blue in color, marked by Akṣobhya,
Holding vajra and bell,
And joined with a consort like themselves.
The lower lotus arises from a,
Red in color, marked by Amitābha,
Holding vajra and bell,
And joined with a consort like themselves.
From yaṃ wind, from raṃ fire;
From a, imagine nectar in a skull cup.
From the seeds of the nectars and lamps,
Boiling in order, light rays arise.
With them wisdom is summoned,
And it is stabilized with the mantra of the three suchnesses.
Then the guests already mentioned,
Through the straws of light from their tongues,
Partake of the nectar as its flavor.

oṃ inda jama jala jakkha bhrata bahni ba u rakṣa canda sujja mada pappa tala pātāle aṭṭa sappa svāhā idaṃ baliṃ bhuñja jiṃgha phulla dhūpa māṃsa piṃgha ambha sappa kajja sāda khanti khuṇi pheḍa gāda oṃ akāro mukhaṃ sarva dharmāṇāṃ ādyanutpannatvāta oṃ āḥ hūṃ phaṭ svāhā

If one performs a feast wheel at midnight,
It is the fourth respite,
To be done after the second and before the torma.
First, all enter the bath,
Anoint themselves with the five nectars and fragrances,
Adorn themselves with fine clothes, bone and other ornaments,
And place a flower on each one's head,
Or bind it with a garland of flowers.
One draws the lines and makes the marks,
And each sits comfortably on his own seat.
In the clarity of each one's yoga,
They are vividly present as deities in the directions and intermediate directions.
Then the action vajra
Distributes in order to all the yogins
The five offerings, flowers and the rest.
Then to the deities of the maṇḍala
One presents the torma of the great bali.
Gathering the first portions of food and drink,
And holding them in the hand, one says this:
oṃ vajra gaurī gaṇa cakra adhikhṣrāna;
This song, in six variations,
For the eight goddesses in order:
Having blessed them in this way,
The action vajra offers those first portions
As a great torma endowed with the three generations,
With heads of goat, sheep and human,
Adorned with streamers and banners.
With oṃ he vajrādhiśṭhāna mahā baliṃta pratīccha ho kha kha khāhi khāhi grihṇa grihṇa bhuñja bhuñja pratīccha hoḥ one sings, and one offers, joining it with all the names, oṃ vajra naira ātma, oṃ vajra gaurī and so on.
Then the action vajra
Makes a maṇḍala offering in front of all,
The vajra master and the rest,
Performs the vajra hand-turning dance,
And says this in vajra song:
You are the supreme and holy yogin,
Filled with the taste of great bliss;
Since I offer this as its condition,
Please accept it with the radiance of delight.
Then, with food, refreshments and the rest,
Special and common,
Each having become satisfied,
They sing songs in vajra dance;
The master of the feast does so first,
Or else others too.
With the three-line song oṃ vajra gaurī hūṃ vajra pūja anuttara, one should apply it to all eight goddesses together.

Further, the song of the moments and the joys:
Of many various kinds,
Kissing, embracing and the rest,
The moment of variety:
I offer the enjoyment of joy.
Of many various kinds,
The erotic, the heroic and the rest,
The moment of maturation:
I offer the enjoyment of supreme joy.
Of many various kinds,
The feast of bliss and the rest,
The moment without forms:
I offer the enjoyment of the joy free from joy.
Of many various kinds,
Great supreme bliss and the rest,
The moment free of characteristics:
I offer the enjoyment of the innate.
The entire world, without remainder,
Through the yoga of all of space,
Enters into the pledge of space;
All is enjoyed in space.
With these words one sings the song.

If one wishes to admit disciples,
The feast gathering is held beforehand,
After a little mantra recitation.
This, moreover, is the work
To be done by the vajra master:
First, by number and by signs,
And by the approach of time, one should be trained.
For the principal and the circle, a hundred thousand and ten thousand;
Likewise, six months of habituation;
And having gone to the far shore in the clear vision of mantra,
The yoga able to bring the deity to clarity.
Having there generated power through approach,
The rite of examining the ground
Should be known as outer and inner.
Outwardly, having avoided bad faults
Such as salty soil and the like,
It slopes to the north or the east of a town,
Endowed with qualities such as evenness.
Inwardly, through signs, dreams and the like,
Examine it thoroughly by the power of yoga.
Purification likewise is outer and inner.
Outwardly, dig to a fathom, to the navel,
Or to about the knee,
According to best, middling or least means,
And remove the stones, the gravel and the many cinders.
Then the inner purification
Is by samādhi, mantra and mudrā.
Then one should bless it:
Imagine the ground as the summit of Mount Meru,
Or as a blissful abode
Such as Śrīdhana.
For the yoga of dwelling there,
Perform the six limbs as before.
Then the earth goddess Pṛthivī
Is generated and invited, and when she is seated,
With words such as "Mother, holder of various jewels,"
One requests her to be witness.
After that, to the master
Who is endowed with yoga, the excellent companions
From the four directions make praise and supplication.
For that purpose he rises and guards the site:
The vajra gaze, the vajra gait,
The command "Begone!", the stances and the rest,
Are done as in the scriptures on the maṇḍala rite.
The preparation on that night
Is for deity, vase and disciple.
Of these, the task for the deity is this:
First, with the karma vajra,
One enters upon the task,
Gathers the accumulations, and the yoga
Of the six limbs is performed as before.
Then one transfers to the master
Who is himself endowed with excellence.
At that time, dots of scent,
Nine butter lamps and the rest are made perfect.
Then, as for the master's yoga:
On the images and the heaps of flowers,
One generates by oneself through samādhi and seed syllables,
Summons the wisdom beings, and merges them threefold.
Moreover, by the hook of light rays
From the seed syllable in one's own heart,
One draws in the wisdom wheel in front,
And with mentally created offerings
Makes offering as appropriate and recites this mantra:
oṃ sarbatathāgata anuttara pūja megha samudra spharaṇa samaye hūṃ
Then, with the mantras jaḥ hūṃ baṃ hoḥ
Of Gaurī and the rest,
And the mudrās accompanied by song,
Summoning and the rest, through dissolution the wisdom blazes.
One generates the buddhas and bodhisattvas,
And not upon anything else.
Blessing the six sense sources and body, speech and mind,
And the empowerment,
Are done as before, and the host of offerings
Should here be made most extensive.
Praise and the tasting of nectar,
Together with the mantras, are done as before.
Then, once again, one says this:
All-pervading lord, arisen from nondual wisdom,
Embodiment of accomplishment,
Teacher, chief Heruka,
Truly united with Nairātmyā;
Ḍākinīs of the directions and intermediate directions,
Knowledge goddesses of magical display,
Bodhisattvas and all the rest:
All of you without exception, please heed me.
To benefit the disciples
And to make offering to you,
O vajra holders, I, named such and such,
Wish to draw the maṇḍala tomorrow.
Kindly consider me with loving compassion,
And please bestow your blessing.
With these words one should make supplication to the deities.

Then, with the action vajra,
One sets about the task of the vase.
It has cause, characteristics,
Purification and blessing: the five tasks.
These are the words of the summary.
Of these, "cause" means:
Made of precious materials such as gold,
Silver or copper, or clay will also do.
"Characteristics" means:
A large belly and a long neck,
A drooping lip and the like, without blemishes.
"Purification" means:
Wash it well and smear it with white.
Place seed syllables or else emblems on it.
Strike it with white mustard seed and fumigate it with incense.
Protect it with the mantra of Amṛtakuṇḍalin.
With a fine cloth tied at the neck and an ornament at the mouth,
Place a vajra tied with a dhāraṇī thread.
Inside it, put good water
Mixed with milk.
The five medicines, hasadeba and
daṇḍa utpala, kaṇḍakar,
śatapṛha, parcid,
Are put in as the support of the five awakenings.
The five grains, rice and māṣa beans,
Barley, wheat and white sesame,
Are the nature of the deities.
The five jewels, gold and silver,
Pearl or else sapphire,
Coral and a right-coiling conch,
Are the very greatness of the deities.
Likewise the nectars, the lamps
And the five essences are put inside.
With mantras interweaving the three suchness syllables and the names,
One applies them to each substance:

For the others too, recite just the three suchness syllables.
"Blessing" means:
Oneself vivid as the deity, light rays
From the heart radiate and gather back, purifying the vase.
Then it becomes the syllable bhrūṃ,
And from that it becomes a celestial palace.
From a white syllable khaṃ
Imagine the inside of the vase as empty.
Then from a red syllable baṃ,
A lotus of eight petals as the deities' ground,
And from the letter ra, imagine a sun seat.
In the center, from hūṃ, the principal
Together with his consort is vividly generated.
From the four, gaṃ caṃ baṃ ghaṃ,
Gaurī and the rest are in the directions.

The four, paṃ saṃ laṃ ḍaṃ,
Fully transformed, are Pukkasī and the rest in the intermediate directions.
Then the wisdom wheel as well,
From the principal's heart,
Is set out in front by light rays.
To it one should offer the welcoming water,
Reciting briefly together with the mantra.
Then Gaurī and the rest
Are to be dissolved inseparably,
And offering and praise are made as before.
The tasting of nectar is not needed here.
"Task" means:
Then the recitation is performed.
First, with the mantra of the four goddesses,
ru ru sphu ru and the rest,
All the substances, the water inside the vase and the rest,
Comprised of the four elements,
Are blessed as the four goddesses.
Then the recitation of the deity as well
Is recited a hundred and eight or a thousand and eight times.
At that time, as for the thread one contemplates:
From the cord held at the heart,
One joins the hand to the syllable hūṃ.
By its light, from the lotus,
The bodhicitta is drawn upward
And, in the form of the cord, dissolves into the vajra;
The vajra dissolves into the deities within the vase;
All the deities melt, and the vase is filled.
Then, accompanied by music,
It is blessed and placed
On fine cloth spread with a carpet of flowers.
Thus, just as with the victorious vase,
On the other vases too a deity each melts.
A feast offering is made as appropriate.

Then the guru, the master,
Should perform the preparatory consecration of the disciple.
The summary is: characteristics, task,
Rite and ripening, the four kinds.
Of these, "characteristics" means:
Without pride and with senses tamed,
Of good family, endowed with the ten virtues;
A king, or one who has no son of his own;
Then admit one endowed with these characteristics.
"Task" means:
Early in the morning, bathing and the maṇḍala offering are performed.
Then one gives the generation of the mind of awakening and the vows.
During that day the disciple,
While holding a good intention,
Should gather these substances:
The medicines for the vase and the rest,
Kuśa grass, protection cords and the rest,
And other offering substances besides,
The provisions for the feast and the rest.
"Rite" means:
First, with the action vajra,
The gathering of the accumulations and the six limbs,
Oneself endowed with excellence,
Having been done as before, through the force of devotion
One transfers to the guru.
Then, to the guru endowed with devotion,
The disciple, having made the maṇḍala offering, prostrations and the rest,
Should make supplication:
O Great Delight, since you are my teacher,
Master, please consider me.
Great protector who shows the way,
Grant me that very pledge.
The Buddha, the Dharma and the Saṅgha,
These three refuges too, grant to me.
Into the supreme city of great liberation,
Protector, I shall enter.
Come, my son: of the Great Vehicle
You are a vessel of the great way.
This rite of the conduct of secret mantra
I shall properly explain to you.
The perfect buddhas who have passed,
Likewise those who have not yet come,
And the perfect buddhas present now,
Who remain to benefit beings:
Having known this excellent rite
Of secret mantra, they,
The heroes, at the foot of the tree of awakening,
Found the sign of omniscience.
The conduct of secret mantra is unequalled:
Through it the protector, glorious Heruka,
Vanquished the most terrible hosts of māras,
Even their great armies.
Therefore, to attain omniscience,
My son, make this resolve.
In the Buddha, the Dharma and the Saṅgha
I always take refuge.
In yoga, secret mantra and the ḍākinīs,
The embodiments who dwell within the wheel,
In all of them too I always take refuge.
In the heroes, the heroines, the empowerment goddesses,
The bodhisattvas, the great beings,
And above all in the master,
I always take refuge.
Glorious Heruka, the heroes,
The yoginīs, Vairocana and the rest,
And the measureless bodhisattvas,
All of you, please consider me.
I, so and so, from this time onward,
For as long as I abide in selflessness,
Take up well, in its pure conduct, nondual,
The vow of my own self.
Then confession and the rest,
In the words as they occur elsewhere,
The disciples are made to recite.
Then one oneself recites this verse
Melodiously, so that it flows into their ears:
The evils I have committed,
Caused to be committed and rejoiced in,
Having come before the buddhas,
All of these I confess.
Of buddhas and bodhisattvas,
Pratyekabuddhas, arhats and beings,
The accumulations of merit of meditation and recitation:
In that I rejoice.
The generation of the mind, full awakening,
The turning of the wheel of Dharma,
And the supreme assembly of bodhisattvas:
In them I take refuge.
Just as the buddhas arise,
Just as the buddhas and bodhisattvas
Generated the mind of awakening,
So here and now I generate the mind.
Then the disciple says this:
Lord, grant me the vow.
Sun of sages, all you buddhas
Without exception, consider me.
I, named such and such,
With the master abiding as witness,
Join me to the irreversible wheel
That arises from the play of the buddhas.
Into the great city whose nature is liberation,
Master, please lead me.
Then the guru says this:
Gathering your thoughts well,
Listen with a most excellent intention.
To a forgetful mind, the sugatas,
Vajrasattva and the rest, grant no blessing.
The Buddha, the Dharma and the Saṅgha,
The unsurpassed Three Jewels:
From today onward, go to them for refuge.
This is declared to be the vow and pledge
Of the pure buddha family.
The vajra, the bell and the mudrā too,
Great one, you must take up.
The mind of awakening is the vajra;
Wisdom is called the bell.
The master too shall be taken up.
This is declared to be the pledge and vow
Of the pure vajra family.
For the delightful pledge
Of the great supreme jewel family,
Material things, fearlessness, Dharma and love:
Always give these four kinds of giving.
For the great supreme lotus family,
The holy Dharma of the three vehicles,
Outer, inner and secret, you must uphold.
For the great supreme action family,
Endowed with all the vows,
You must uphold them genuinely,
And the work of offering as far as you are able.
Moreover, these fourteen
Are declared to be defeats;
They are called root downfalls,
And they become gross misdeeds.
You shall not kill living beings,
Nor take what is not given;
You shall not engage in sexual misconduct,
Nor speak falsely.

Liquor, which has become the root of all ruin,
Should be utterly abandoned.
Except for the sake of taming beings,
Abandon all that should not be done.
You should rely closely on the holy,
And honour the yogins.
The three kinds of bodily action,
The four kinds of speech,
And the three kinds of mind,
Abandon in turn as far as you are able.
Do not long for the lesser vehicle;
Do not turn your back on the welfare of beings.
Do not abandon saṃsāra either,
And never be attached to nirvāṇa.
Gods, asuras and guhyakas
You shall not despise,
And mudrās, mounts, weapons
And emblems you shall not step over.
These are declared to be the pledges;
You too must guard them.
Then the disciple says this:
Protector, please consider me.
All you buddhas who remain, without exception,
From this very time until buddhahood,
Unsurpassed ones, please consider me.
Just as the protectors of the three times
Establish beings in nirvāṇa.
This should be recited three times.
Having thus blessed the outer way,
One blesses the inner by the oral instruction.
For this, moreover, the disciple says this:
Through these virtuous actions of mine,
Having swiftly accomplished buddhahood for the world before long,
The beings sunk in the ocean of saṃsāra
I shall deliver to unsurpassed awakening.
Or else one has them say this:
Peace of all tathāgatas,
Abode of all tathāgatas,
Vajrasattva, supreme of all,
Guide, bless me.
Fully expressing all characteristics,
And pure of all that lacks characteristics,
Samantabhadra, supreme buddha,
Guide, bless me.
Perfect buddha, supreme peace,
Who purifies the conduct of wisdom,
Source of the three vajras everywhere,
Guide, bless me.
Great mind of all beings,
Naturally pure and without stain,
Arising as great Vairocana,
Guide, bless me.

With the consort appearing as himself,
Purifies the three doors of the disciple.
In order to make the disciple his son,
He then places him in the secret place,
And confers empowerment as appropriate.
Having come forth again, he bows his body before him.
Then the action vajra
Guards him with scented water and the rakṣa mantra.
With a hand anointed with scent,
Touching the crown of his head, at the three places
One recites with the three suchness syllables:
oṃ bodhicitta utpādayāmi
That is for the common one, as before.
oṃ yogacitta upādayāmi surastvaṃ yathāsukhaṃ
This is done likewise for every disciple.
Thus the first generation of the mind,
And especially the family of Vajradhara;
Just as it arose from bliss,
Buddha himself and son of the buddhas,
Father of all tathāgatas,
Son of all tathāgatas,
Eldest of the sons of the sugatas,
One who acts according to the sugatas' command, and the rest:
To those endowed with the qualities of generating the mind,
One should give flowers upon the head,
Incense and lamps in front,
And the offering of scent at the heart;
Likewise pure food and the rest
Are offered with mantras and mudrās as appropriate.
Then a pleasing tooth stick
Of good wood, such as udumvāra, aśvattha,
bhabata and the like,
Not crooked, not broken, not eaten by worms,
Not old, and free of insects,
Is made twelve finger widths in length,
With a flower tied at its tip.
On ground smeared with the five products of the cow, dung and the rest,
A maṇḍala about the size of a cowhide is made;
Before that maṇḍala
The disciple stands and then sits down,
Then rises, and having done the recitations,
Holds the stick between joined palms without hesitation.
Facing east, not low, the body not swaying,
Not throwing to either side but straight ahead,
One examines the signs from that throw.
Moreover, by the guru's instruction,
There are three: accomplishment, activity and obstacle.
Accomplishment is of three, best, middling and least.
The best accomplishment is known
From its pointing east and facing oneself.
The middling are the others; the least, too,
Is when it faces down, or the flower is stuck in mud.
Likewise the other two are to be known
From the guru's oral instruction.
Then, to remove the faults of the mouth,
Urine is given into the cupped hand.
Reciting the mantra
oṃ debiśodhana dharmā sarba pāpa niśca saṃśodhaya sarba bikalpanaya hūṃ,
They drink three sips.
After that, the disciples are filled with confidence,
And, explaining the profound and vast Dharma,
The guru says this:
In all the world systems
The omniscient ones do not arise;
Like the umbāra flower,
They arise only rarely, once in a long while.
So hard to meet is this arising
Of the nondual, profound way of conduct
That makes sentient beings buddhas.
Whatever ripening of deeds was done before
Through many countless eons,
All of it is exhausted
Through seeing the profound maṇḍala.
Likewise one recites what is taught elsewhere.
Then with oṃ āḥ hūṃ
The kuśa grass is blessed
And is used as pillow and mat.
Then, as for tying the protection cord:
With a white or a red protection cord,
Reciting the rakṣa mantra, six knots are tied,
And it is bound on the right or the left upper arm.
Then comes the offering to the fire:
The sequence of the fire offering rite
Should be looked up elsewhere, in writings
That rely on the guru's instructions.
Then the feast offering is performed as before.
The great baliṃ is likewise.
Outcastes, dogs, brahmins and the rest
Partake equally, and the leftovers are collected.
With the first portion and the mixed remains,
The outer maṇḍala of the ḍākinīs is satisfied.
Binding the right and left hands
Like a lotus or like a wheel,
Pressing down with the foot and gazing upward,
One proclaims the sound pheṃ upward,
And they are invited with melody and mantra.

By this virtue,
May all beings be free of evil,

By whatever virtue has been gained,

Delighting in the guru, the deity, the Three Supreme Ones

When the wisdom wheel departs,
One recites oṃ jñāna maṇḍal muḥ
Three times, places the left fist at the heart,
Extends the right hand to the ground,
And, snapping the fingers three times,
Requests the outer deities to depart.
Then the pledge beings are gathered into oneself:
The supporting wheel and the celestial palace
Dissolve into the four elements, and these into space;
The eight goddesses dissolve into the hūṃ at the heart,
And one remains in the pride of the deity.
Then the guru enters
Within the maṇḍala and makes offering to the deities,
And gives torma to the direction protectors.
For the disciples' preparation,
That place or else another bedroom will do.
In the lion's sleeping posture they examine their dreams;
Without any other talk,
They sleep keeping Hevajra in mind.
Then, rising early, they make the maṇḍala offering.
The guru asks and the disciple tells.
Good or bad, best, middling or least,
The guru comes to know;
If bad, means to pacify the concerns are applied;
If good, one gladdens their minds and arouses enthusiasm.
These are the rites of the preparatory consecration and preparation.

Then, on the third night, comes the entry:
The limbs of entry and the actual entry itself.
The first limb is laying out the lines.
Rising early together with the disciples,
One performs a pacifying or enriching fire offering.
Moreover, the characteristics of the master are these:
He knows correctly its very reality,
Is without anger, and endures weariness.
He, endowed with knowledge and with the mudrā,
Places the deities in the sky in front
Within moons joined face to face,
And seals them with the sixteen vowels.
Having thus joined the intermediate boundaries,
He should begin the work of laying the lines.
Take cotton thread obtained without haggling over the price,
Spun by one endowed with the marks of wisdom,
Without tangles or knots, avoiding thin ant-waist places;
Its length is twice the maṇḍala,
Its thickness a twentieth part of the door.
The stretched strands are gathered each separately
And placed within a fragrant lotus vessel.
Then, first of all, the strands
Are each meditated as without intrinsic nature.
On them, bhrūṃ, khaṃ and the rest
Become five syllables, and these
Are imagined as the cause or the essence of the five families.
Then, by the light rays of the blazing hooks
From the sun and moon eyes of the yoga,
From the heart of the wisdom deities
The wisdom threads that abide there,
The principal, Hevajra,
And the deities, nondual with the mantras, are present;
Invited and dissolved, they generate the line cord.
With the five seeds, bhrūṃ, khaṃ and the rest,

And with the mudrā in the hands,

And the strands are to be twisted together.

By placing the three, oṃ āḥ hūṃ,
One blesses it and recites this mantra:
oṃ bajra samaya sūtra matikrama hūṃ, a hundred and eight recitations.
Holding it with the two middle fingers joined, one recites.
Thus the line cord is generated.

And from his heart emanates

Hands the line cord to her,
And she too, saying jjaḥ jjaḥ, should take it.

Held from above and turned back upward,
The Brahmā lines first, the corner lines and the rest,
Are to be struck as appropriate.

The buddhas of all the world systems without remainder,
Together with their heirs and the rest,
Emanate for the sake of beings;
It is your very mind.

After that, one measures with white chalk.
As for striking the first Brahmā line:
The master faces east and the disciple faces west,
And so they strike the first Brahmā line.
With the master facing north, the second Brahmā line.
The master stands at the southeast corner,

Likewise the guru stands at the northwest,
And the disciple, at the southwest, strikes the western side.
The guru stands at that same quarter of the wind,

Likewise one strikes them in turn, going around.
The guru at the southeast and the disciple

Likewise the corner lines are struck in order.
As for dividing the parts of the lines:
One should not divide them with the cord,
For there is the danger of both increase and decrease;
With a stick or the like, straight and firm,
One marks them first with lines of khatikha chalk.
From the line units of forty-two in the four directions
Comes the grid.
Within the line of the door enclosure,
Together with a lotus of eight petals,
It is surrounded as appropriate by the band of the deities.
Each part is encircled by a vajra fence
And by the light of fire.
Within that, with the porticos,
The band of sense pleasures and the rest,
One should draw as appropriate.
When the lines are thus complete, one makes offering to the line cord,
Opens the door with the blazing gaze,
And requests the deities, together with all, to depart.
These are the rites of laying the lines.

This is the pure dharmadhātu,
Which liberates the realm of beings,
Glorious Heruka, the king himself,
The abode of all the tathāgatas.

Freed from all faults,
One properly begins the work of the colours.
The master spreads them first,
And afterward the disciples on all sides.

Thoroughly destroying all obstacles.

At the height of the thumb and the little finger,
Draw it correctly, straight and raised.

Crooked ones produce suffering,

Should know that both will die.

That very one, with a white cloth, is bound upon the head.
The ritual assistant or the temple keeper
Sprinkles water on the ground near the maṇḍala
And scatters loose flowers,
And sets out incense and all the offering waters.
Constantly one makes supplication to the guru.
Then the guru places in the mouth
The intimate substance and a nectar pill,
And, sitting on that seat, accomplishes the maṇḍala.
Together with the consort, the six limbs;
The thread of contemplation and samādhi made clear;
And by the action vajra, as before,
Beginning with the guarding by rakṣa,
Up to the offering, praise and tasting of nectar,
Oneself endowed with excellence.
The guru too, as before,
Transfers through devotion; and as for the samādhi,
Relying on the images, by seed syllables
Or by emblems alone, the deities are generated.
Having thus blended the three maṇḍalas,
From the seed syllable at one's own heart
Light rays are sent forth and the deities are gathered.
Making offering to them, summoning, causing them to enter and the rest,
Through their dissolving as before, the wisdom blazes,
Just as water and milk are mingled.

The buddhas and bodhisattvas
Are thus accomplished, and not otherwise.
Having thus blended the four maṇḍalas,
The guru should make offering and praise,
Taste the nectar and recite.
At that time, as the guru recites and meditates,
The disciple, with a maṇḍala offering first,
Makes request to the one endowed with yoga.
On the disciple's behalf, the guru himself,
In the same way as the disciple enters,
Performs it in sequence, and so too the empowerment:
The water, the crown and the rest,
Up to the final authorization, in their order.
The authorization granted by the deities is this:
"Gather sentient beings,
Benefit them, bring them into the maṇḍala,
Empower them, and make them possess the pledges.
Care for them through the rites of pacifying and the rest,
And for all sentient beings who are to be subdued,
Accomplish every aim without exception.
To the lesser and the supreme accomplishments
Join them in due order, according to their fortune."
Then the disciple, examined beforehand
And endowed with the proper qualifications,
Speaks in order to enter and obtain empowerment,
Saying, "You, great bliss, my teacher,"
And so forth, as before.
The guru, full of loving care, says, "You,
Who are you? What do you wish? In what do you delight?"
When this has been said, the disciple says,
"I am one with good fortune, and I seek liberation.
I delight in great bliss."
And further says this:
"To the master, the wisdom body of all the buddhas,
I go for refuge.
To the deities, the lord together with the goddesses,
I also bow.
Into the supreme city of great liberation,
Protector, I pray you, let me enter."

With these words one should make request to the master. Then the master should say this:
"Abode of all the tathāgatas,
Glorious Heruka, supreme city:
Into this heart maṇḍala
I shall bring the disciple to enter."

Then one should prostrate to the deities of the maṇḍala by way of body, speech and mind, saying oṃ namate hūṃ, oṃ namami hūṃ, oṃ namo nama hūṃ, and also oṃ sarba tathāgata kāya vāka citta praṇamena bajra bandhanaṃ karomi.

Having bowed respectfully with the three doors,
Then, as before, the disciple
Requests the vows from the lord,
Reciting "Sage, sun," and the rest.
Likewise the vows and the pledges
Are done as before, as in the preparation.
Then the guru says this:

"Today you enter among the heroes and the yoginīs. I shall generate in you the wisdom of glorious Heruka. Since you will obtain the accomplishments of all the tathāgatas, of the heroes and of all the yoginīs, what need is there to mention other accomplishments? Such wisdom I shall generate. You must not speak of it in front of those who have not seen the maṇḍala, lest your pledge be broken.
Glorious Heruka today
Has truly entered your heart.
Should you speak of this way,
At that very instant he will rend it and depart."

Then, giving the disciple to drink the pledge water, endowed with the five nectars, poured within a skull cup (kapāla), one radiant with the splendor of Hevajra says this:
"This is your water of hell.
If you guard the pledges, accomplishment
Is achieved by this vajra nectar water.
oṃ sarba vajra pañca amṛta udaka hūṃ.
From this day onward, I am your glorious Heruka. Whatever I command you, saying 'Do this,' you must do. Now do not act with contempt. If you do so, then without having cast off your unhappiness you will die and fall into hell."

Then this is said: "Today even the wisdom of all the tathāgatas is not difficult for you to accomplish, so what need is there to mention other accomplishments?" With this one leads the disciple inside the curtain, and the descent of wisdom should be done according to the guru's instructions.

Then, asking what was seen, and holding a garland of flowers, one says pratīccha vajra ho, and the garland is cast. Then the eyes should be opened:
oṃ Vajrasattva today
Is intent upon opening your eyes.
As the opener of every eye,
The vajra eye is unsurpassed.
he vajra paśya.
That same verse, spoken as mantra, is:
oṃ vajra satva svayaṃ dyaya cakṣādghaṭtaya tatpara udghaṭalati sarbākṣo vajra cakṣurānuttara he vajra prebeśa.

Then, removing the blindfold, one says this:
"Now give rise to intense faith,
And look upon this excellent maṇḍala.
You are born into the family of the yoginīs,
Blessed by mantra and mudrā.
All the excellences of beings
And all accomplishments have become manifest.
Give rise to all the pledges,
And be diligent in the secret mantra."

With these words one shows the face of the deity on which the flower fell, and confers it especially. One teaches the mantra and explains the suchness.

Here, the celestial palace being square is purified as the nature of the immeasurables. The four doors are the nature of the purified four applications of mindfulness. The four porticoes are the nature of the purified four right abandonments. The nets are the great afflictions purified. The half-nets are the lesser afflictions purified. The eight pillars are the eightfold noble path purified. The ornaments, such as the silk streamers, have the characteristic of purified moral discipline. The five sense pleasures are form, sound and the rest, purified. The vajra ring is the sign of turning the wheel of the profound Dharma. The ring of fire is the sign of not being overcome by outer obstructors and the like. The eight vases are the sign of being enriched with great qualities and completely filled with great compassion.

Then the suchness of the deities is taught. Here the Blessed One Hevajra is the nature of the purified aggregate of consciousness and of aversion. Gaurī is the nature of aversion and form purified. Caurī is the nature of delusion and sound purified. Vetālī is the nature of pride and smell purified. Ghasmarī is the nature of attachment and taste purified. Pukkasī is the nature of aversion and earth purified. Śavarī is the nature of delusion and water purified. Caṇḍālī is the nature of pride and fire purified. Ḍombinī is the nature of attachment and wind purified. Nairātmyā Yoginī accords with the Father.

When the suchness has thus been explained, the disciple, with a maṇḍala offering going first, should touch his head to the guru's feet, offer the supreme fee, and make request for the sake of the empowerment. If it is the common empowerment that is being received, one says this:
"Just as the great offering was made
To the Buddha Bodhivajra,
So too, in order to free me completely,
Vajra of Space, grant it to me.
I am sunk in the mire of saṃsāra
Without refuge: protect me!
Protector, by your kindness,
Grant me the knowledge empowerment."

And if it is in particular the master's empowerment, one should say this:
"Glorious Heruka, conferrer of empowerment,
Majestic one who protects beings,
Just as you did for Source of Qualities and the others,
Likewise grant it to me.
Great hero, grant me
The irreversible empowerment.
For the sake of all sentient beings
I shall always act as a master."

Then, for the common and the special disciples, there should be set out an ordinary seat, and, as the special seat, a lion throne or one marked with a lotus. A white parasol with a golden handle is raised, and the master too, having made offerings to the deities as is fitting, takes nectar, scented water and white mustard in his hand and, with the pride of the deity, says this:
"Listen, you hosts of hindering obstructors!
I am glorious Vajradhara,
Lord blazing with various weapons,
Who crushes the vicious without exception.
Depart, you hosts of hindering obstructors!
Do not transgress my command.
If you should transgress the command,
I shall reduce you to dust like atoms."

With these words, oneself abiding in the mantra and samādhi of Amṛtakuṇḍalin, one drives them out; or else, reciting the mantras of protection (rakṣā) and the like, one guards with the fence, the tent and the rest.

Then the disciple bathes,
And, wearing fine clothes, an upper garment and the rest,
In order to receive the empowerment of the sixth, the perfect Buddha,
Offers a handful of flowers to the chief deity of the maṇḍala.
This is done by reciting the mantra oṃ pratīccha vajra ho.
After that the guru takes the flower
And binds it on the disciple's head,
And in reciting oṃ dharmāḥ kuṇḍa abhiṣiñca:
"Because all phenomena are gathered and coiled together,
It is the special deity." So it is said.
The garland is the empowerment of the Victor as one's chosen deity,
Conferred for the sake of meditation, endowed with the pledges.
After that, with the action vajra
The waters of the vases are gathered
And collected into the excellent victory vase,
And after that into the conch, which is the nature of reality.
Then, with a stream of water from the victory vase,
With the sound of the bell and with melody,
The one conferring empowerment says this:
"Arisen from the abode of the three secrets,
Conferred by the Lord of the Three Realms,
The water empowerment of all the buddhas
I shall give to you here today.
In order fully to accomplish all the buddhas,
Receive this sacred water empowerment.
oṃ sarba tathāgara udaka abhiṣiñcāmi vajra svabhāva ātmako'haṃ."
This washes away conceptual thought and the stains of the afflictions.

Then a crown is made of precious materials such as gold, or carved in relief from wood, ivory and the like, or painted as a scroll painting. One imagines it as Ratnasambhava arising from trāṃ, and by summoning, causing to enter and the rest, dissolves the wisdom being into it. One makes offering and praise and recites the mantra, and says this:
"Arisen from the abode of the three secrets,
Conferred by the Lord of the Three Realms,
The crown empowerment of all the buddhas:
The precious crown, adorned with topknot and diadem,
Praised by all the buddhas,
And the empowerment of the silk streamer I shall give.
oṃ vajra parāhaka vajra aralli hoḥ."

Then again one generates the vajra from āḥ as Amitābha and says this:
"This supreme empowerment
Of all the buddhas, of glorious Heruka the king,
And of the ḍākinīs,
I confer on you today.
oṃ śrī heruka adhipati stvaṃ abhiṣiñca tiṣtha bīra samaya stvaṃ."
Then its suchness is spoken:
"The being without beginning or end,
Vajrasattva, great joy,
Samantabhadra, the self of all,
The vajra-proud one, lord of lords."
With this the vajra empowerment is given into the right hand.

Then one generates the bell from khaṃ as Amoghasiddhi and says this:
"This is declared to follow
The voice of wisdom of all the yoginīs.
You too must always hold it.
The Victors intend it for supreme awakening."
Its suchness is explained thus:
"Existence is pure by its very nature;
By its very nature it is made insubstantial.
Likewise the being who is pure
Becomes the supreme one of existence."
With vajra satva stvaṃ abhiṣiñcāmi one gives the bell into the left hand.

Then the name empowerment should be conferred. One imagines Vairocana arising from bhrūṃ, and recites "The name empowerment of all the buddhas" and so on, then says oṃ vajra śrī heruka adhibati stvaṃ abhiṣiñcami and śrī heruka nāma abhiṣekata, and gives the vajra and bell directly above the head.

Then the disciple should be made firm in the discipline:
"This is all the buddhas themselves,
Held in the hand of glorious Heruka.
You too must always hold it,
Steadfast in the discipline of glorious Heruka."
One has the disciple take hold of the vajra by its tip and recites this mantra: oṃ sarba tathāta siddha vajra samaya stvaṃ eṣa stvaṃ dharayāmi hūṃ svāhā ha ha hi hi. Having said this, one gives the crossed vajra into the hand.

Then:
"The form of glorious Heruka
Is given by the rite of one's own maṇḍala.
Attain the rank of vajra master.
The khaṭvāṅga of glorious Heruka,
The ḍamaru of the yoginī,
The vajra khaṭvāṅga together with the vajra,
The bell ringing out its tinkling sound,
The Brahmā thread borne by the lord of yogins,
The curved knife and the skull cup:
Great hero, take them up!"
Having said this, the disciple stands with the right leg extended and the left bent. The vajra and the curved knife are given on the right, the bell, the ḍamaru and the skull cup on the left, and the khaṭvāṅga is given leaning against the right side. Thus one declares him endowed with such great and noble qualities.

By this he comes to possess the authorization, the prophecy and the encouragement of the vajra master's empowerment, and his activities too become complete. The authorization is given thus: "Train so that you accomplish without hindrance pacifying, enriching, subjugating, wrathful rites, paralyzing, driving off, intoxicating, killing and the rest. These accomplishments will become complete: accomplish all the worldly accomplishments, such as wearing the boots of swiftness, bringing down rain and seizing the sun and moon, and all the accomplishments beyond the world."

These complete the vase empowerment, or the vajra master's empowerment. It purifies the stains of the body, and one comes to have the same fortune as bodhisattvas who have completed the path of accumulation. Then the second empowerment purifies the stains of speech and plants the potency of the path of joining. The third empowerment purifies the stains of mind and plants the potency of the path of seeing. The fourth empowerment purifies the afflictions and the secondary afflictions, and plants the potency to abide in the path of meditation and the distinctive path. As for the sequence of conferring these empowerments, because it must depend on the guru's own mouth, even the Tathāgata kept the intent hidden; therefore, to avoid corrupting the intended meaning, it is with this in mind that I too have not set it down here in writing.

Having thus taught the empowerment, when one wishes to perform a consecration, its meaning too is this. The summary is:
What is to be known, what is to be practiced,
And the result: these are the three aspects.

Here, what is to be known is this: consecration should be known as common and special.
Offering dominion, worshiping it as the deity,
Empowering it as one would a disciple,
And the common eye-opening with a golden probe,
Spoken with verses and mantras.
The special is the opening of the eyes.
The warming of the face is by the fire offering,
In order to make the disciple auspicious
And for the sake of satisfying the deities.

After that, what is to be practiced is this. As before, request is made to one fit to be a guru, a master endowed with the proper qualifications; the approach and the rite of the site are done as before; and the maṇḍala is laid out upon a surface level like the face of a mirror, or upon a square platform. All the preparatory articles are gathered near at hand. Here, the one fit to be a master is this:
"Steady, disciplined and intelligent,
Patient, honest and without guile,
Knowing the application of mantra and tantra,
Skilled in the work of drawing maṇḍalas,
Knowing well the ten principles:
Such a one is called a master.
The secret mantras he teaches
Will be accomplished; of this there is no doubt."
This means that he too must make the rite perfect without omission. As it is said:
"Do not let the rite fall short.
The consecration is ever reviled,
Great suffering comes in this life,
And great terror in the world beyond.
Therefore one who desires accomplishment
Should, as far as he himself is able,
Strive in every way, with full effort,
In the manner taught in the rite."

Then first the disciple, or the practice companion, makes himself endowed with excellence, with ornaments, ointments and the like, blessing the offering water, and the six-limbed yoga up to the tasting of nectar. Then the guru too, possessed of excellent devotion and its transference, like the supreme companion in yogic practice, blends the generated samādhi and the invited nature, two blended or three blended, and does everything as before up to the offering, praise and tasting of nectar. The images and the rest are cleaned and placed in the place cleaned beforehand.

Then white mustard seed is poured into fire and into water while this mantra is recited, and it is circled three times and scattered. Purification and protection should be done: oṃ he vajra adhitiṣthana barba pāpaṃ dahana he vajrāya hūṃ phaṭ svāhā. Then one anoints with sesame oil, using a brush of dūrvā grass, saying oṃ sarba tathāgata kāya biśodhani svāhā rakṣa rakṣa hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ svāhā. Then one rubs and wipes with the five barks; the bathing and the rest may be done or not done, for the support has already been purified by those earlier steps.

Holding incense, with music and song, one gathers all the buddhas in the sky before one into the form of the maṇḍala deities and says this:
"Hevajra, Blessed One,
King of vidyā-mantras, I bow to you.
Out of love for the disciple
And to make offering to you,
Compassionate one, I wish to perform
The consecration, O Protector.
Blessed One, since I am devoted,
It is fitting that you grant permission.
Stating my name as so-and-so,
This king of knowledge is called so-and-so.
With whatever articles are at hand
I wish to perform the consecration tomorrow.
Considering me together with my disciples
With loving kindness,
Please bless the entire assembly
In the consecration."
Or else one makes the request with "The buddhas abiding in the ten directions, with their heirs, and the various worldly ones" and so forth, and, scattering handfuls of flowers, says this:
"Perfect buddhas abiding in the sky,
As excellent flowers, a rain of jewels,
Fall copiously upon your bodies,
O, O, please accept them, each of you.
oṃ parama sukhā śaya sulaliti bilāsa mite namāmi bhagavān jaḥ hūṃ baṃ hoḥ hi hi hi hi pratīccha vajra kusumāñjali nātha ho.
Just as all the buddhas,
From their abode in Tuṣita,
Entered the womb of Queen Māyā,
So may you likewise dwell in this form.
Dwelling here, becoming our protector forever,
For the increase of the mind of awakening,
Please accept these flowers and the rest
For our sake."
Having said this, if the image is to enter the maṇḍala and receive empowerment, and the deity has not been established in preparation as before, it is done merely by gathering in the protectors.

Likewise, if it is an empowerment, the preparatory rite for the disciple is done as before. If it is not, the disciple is given the preparatory rite with this alone:
"Today my life has borne fruit;
My living too is fruitful.
That I shall become equal in pledge
To the deity, I have no doubt.
Of one mind with the mind of awakening,
I have become irreversible;
That today I am born
Into the family of the Tathāgatas, I have no doubt.
Today I myself am holy;
My offering and giving are unsurpassed.
By inviting all the buddhas,
Today I am supreme among those assembled."
This is what is said.

Then, if it is a preparation in which the colored powders have not been drawn, the lines, the colors and the rest are done very early, as before, and on an auspicious day the consecration is done as follows. Again the master with his companions, with the verses beginning "Do not let the rite fall short," gathers and arranges all the articles and draws all the ornaments. Again oneself with one's companions, beginning from bathing and anointing up to the tasting of nectar, makes oneself endowed with excellence as before. Beginning from the cleaning of the images and the rest, one rubs and wipes them with the five barks, namely those from the jambuka, the kata, the bilva, the mango and the picupuri. Likewise one recites the earlier verses, inserting "Today I wish to perform the consecration," up to "Today I am supreme among those assembled."

Then one bathes the image with the vase water endowed with the five products of the cow, the water empowered by reciting oṃ biśuddhe vajra grodha hūṃ āṃ bhrūṃ jṝṃ khaṃ hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ svāhā, and with music and song one should say this:
"Just as, at the very moment of birth,
The gods offered bathing
With pure divine water,
So likewise I offer bathing.
oṃ sarba tathāgata abhiṣekata samaya śriye svāhā."
One also recites other mantras, those of Locanā and the rest, oṃ ru ru sphu ru and so on, and sings any verse of auspiciousness whatsoever.

Then the body is wiped with cloth:
"Although the Sugata's form is beyond all example,
For the sake of beings it appears in every form.
Free from flaws and faults like a precious jewel,
In the manner of the world's honoring and its paintings,
With this supreme cloth, praised by the stainless Victors,
We wipe the body of the Victor.
May the afflictions and obscurations all be purified without remainder,
And may we be accomplished in the state of the three bodies.
oṃ sarba tathāgata vajra vastraye svāhā, oṃ catri gandhe svāhā, oṃ vajra abhiṣiñcani svāhā."

That is the main part of the consecration itself. It is as is said: "All the buddhas gathered into one: this is the excellent characteristic of consecration," and "They are placed in the heart of the image." It should be understood through this summarizing statement:
"Samādhi, wisdom, blessing
And empowerment: the excellent consecration."
Here, the samādhi of the maṇḍala is:
"From the image, from seed syllable or emblem,
Or instantaneously and so forth, the body of the deity
And the maṇḍala deities are generated according to the system.
With light rays from the seed syllable at the heart
They are gathered from the sky, offered to, and dissolved."
Then the blessing is:
"The six sense bases and body, speech and mind
Are blessed as before."
Then the empowerment is:
"Beginning from the water and the crown,
It is given up to the authorization.
Further, with the seven precious things and the rest,
Empowerment is conferred as is fitting.
The offering of dominion too belongs to that empowerment."

Then the rite of opening the eyes should be done. A golden probe or the like is dipped in butter mixed with honey, the eyes are opened, and one says this:
"Just as the king of physicians
Removed the world's cataracts,
So too may the Victors remove
Your cataract of unknowing."
And oṃ sarba tathāgata cakṣu cakṣu biśodhaya svāhā. The special opening of the eyes is done here in the same way as the descent of wisdom upon the disciple.

Then dominion should be offered. First the parasol and the victory banner should be offered, saying this: "By offering the parasol to this image of the Sugata's purified body, speech and mind, the purified body, speech and mind of the Sugata is gracious, of great blessing, and endowed with the power to protect beings. May the patron and all sentient beings of the three realms be relieved of the torment of all the afflictions and secondary afflictions, and come to have the fortune of protecting beings.
Just as to the buddhas of the past
A beautiful parasol with a golden handle
Was offered, so likewise I offer it.
Please accept it as you please.
oṃ sarba tathāgata chatraṃ śri samaye hūṃ."
This is recited three or up to seven times. In the same way it should be adapted and applied to the others as well. The victory banner too is done as before:
"Just as to the buddhas of the past
The sign of victory over hindering māras
Was offered, so likewise I offer it.
Please accept it as you please.
oṃ sarba tathāgata dhvaja ketu śrī samaye hūṃ."
Then streamers and pennants are offered:
"Just as to the buddhas of the past
The pennants proclaiming renown in the ten directions
Were offered, so likewise I offer them.
Please accept them as you please.
oṃ sarba tathāgata patāka śrī samaye hūṃ."
The same applies to the canopy and the rest.

Again bathing is done, and the five kinds of offering are presented. Then food is offered. Then the words of offering are applied as before: "May all sentient beings be freed from hunger and thirst, and from eons of famine and the like. oṃ sarba tathāgata ahara bruṃ ja śrī samaye hūṃ." Then garments are offered: "May all sentient beings be free from the suffering of lacking clothes and coverings and the like, and come to have the fortune of wearing divine garments of pañcālika cloth, blissful to the touch. oṃ sarba tathāgata vastra nānā śrī samaye hūṃ."

Then the warming of the face is done, and this is done by the fire offering:
"In order to make the disciple auspicious
And for the sake of satisfying the deities;
This too follows the rite as before."
Then, in particular, offerings to the inner images and the rest are made, and a feast of caru is also given.

After that comes entrusting it as an object of worship:
"The images you requested of me
Have been consecrated in particular
Just as it is taught;
I have done this and caused it to be done.
Now you should worship them
Fully and as you please."
Then the protectors are commanded:
Joining oneself to the special deity,
One summons Mahākāla,
Causes him to enter, binds him and brings him under control.
One makes offering to him and gives him torma,
And commands him with this order:
"This temple and the rest
That I have consecrated,
For as long as it remains,
You must guard and watch over well.
You must guard the commands
Of the Jewels and the noble Saṅgha,
And guard well the Teacher's word
And the orders given by the master."

Then forbearance is requested:
"Perfect buddhas and deities,
Your heirs, and all the hosts of gods, Brahmā and the rest:
Whatever has been faulty
In the rite performed, it is fitting that you forgive it."
Then, if one wishes to request the deities to depart, one should say just this:
"Granting peace and every great well-being
To the patrons,
Go to the realm of the buddhas,
And come again for the benefit of beings."
Then the stability of the blessing is proclaimed:
"May this image remain for an eon,
Or for more than a hundred eons,
And may all the gods protect it
From earth, fire, water
And wind.
May the master together with his disciples,
And their sons and grandsons, flourish,
And, overjoyed and happy in mind,
Always dwell in their own excellent abode."

Once the general consecration has been understood,
If it is a book, a painted cloth or the like,
Then, as taught elsewhere,
Transformation into syllables, bathing and the rest,
Mantras and so forth are done as is fitting.
For fear of making the text too long, they are not set out here.

After that, verses on the completion of the text are spoken:
The one who composed this: as for my name,
"Hard for adverse forces to overcome," the Unshakable,
With "vajra" at its end, is the name I was given at birth.
The name not given at birth, "Renown," whose merit pervades all,
With "banner" at its end, is a name of fair repute.
The text I composed, well established,
Is the practitioner's "Taking in Hand,"
With "into practice" at its end, complete in every meaning.
In stages adorned with simple verses,
With "called" at its end, it is of great renown.
The way that meaning is made fully complete
Is clear realization, sādhana, empowerment and consecration.
In the guru's clear realization, in harmony with the sādhana,
With accomplishment at its end, the maṇḍala rite is complete.
In the empowerment, which purifies stains and plants potencies,
With consecration at its end, the benefit of others is complete.
For the fruit of giving to be accomplished
Is activity and accomplishment for one's own and others' sake.
Know this, in a slightly reversed order:
