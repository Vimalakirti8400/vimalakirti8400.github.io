The Noble Heart of Gaṇapati (Āryagaṇapatihṛdaya)

The Noble Heart of Gaṇapati, known in Sanskrit as Āryagaṇapatihṛdaya and in Tibetan as Pakpa Tsokkyi Dakpö Nyingpo, is a short dhāraṇī text centered on Gaṇapati, the Lord of the Hosts, a figure better known in wider Indian tradition as Gaṇeśa. Set on Vulture Peak Mountain near Rājagṛha, where the Buddha is accompanied by a great assembly of monks and bodhisattvas, it records the Buddha telling the venerable Ānanda that whoever holds the heart of Gaṇapati will accomplish all their activities, achieve their aims, and find success in secret mantra practice. The Buddha then sets out the dhāraṇī itself, a long incantation of repeated Sanskrit syllables invoking Gaṇapati's power. It is Toh 1084 in the Kangyur, where it belongs among the collected dhāraṇī texts, the class of works devoted to protective and empowering spells.

In the language of India: āryagaṇabatihṛdaya. In the language of Tibet: The Noble Heart of Gaṇapati.

Homage to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas.

Thus did I hear at one time. The Blessed One was dwelling in Rājagṛha, on Vulture Peak Mountain, together with a great saṅgha of monks, one thousand two hundred and fifty monks, and with a great many bodhisattva great beings. Then the Blessed One said to the venerable Ānanda: "Ānanda, whoever holds the heart of Gaṇapati, Lord of the Hosts, will accomplish all their activities. By holding it, their aims will be achieved, and all secret mantras will also yield accomplishment.

"Ānanda, suppose a son of good family or daughter of good family, a monk or nun, a layman or laywoman, takes up this heart of Gaṇapati, Lord of the Hosts, and practices secret mantra. If they go to another land to worship the Three Jewels, or enter the circle of a king's palace, and they make offerings of flowers and incense to the buddhas, the blessed ones, and then recite this heart of Gaṇapati, all their activities will be accomplished. Have no doubt about this. In the midst of any fighting, dispute, bloody strife, or turmoil in the land, if they call it to mind, all of it will be completely pacified. If they rise early each day and recite it, they will retain whatever they have heard. No yakṣas, rākṣasas, or ḍākinīs will steal their vitality; rather, these will keep far away from them."

When the Blessed One had spoken thus, the whole assembly, and the world with its gods, humans, asuras, and gandharvas, rejoiced and praised what the Blessed One had said.

Here ends The Noble Heart of Gaṇapati.
