Homage to the Three Jewels.

Thus did I hear at one time. The Blessed One was dwelling at Śrāvastī, in the Jeta Grove, the garden of Anāthapiṇḍada, together with Prince Jeta.

Then the son of Maṇibhadra, the great yakṣa general, went to where the Blessed One was. Having arrived, he prostrated with his head at the feet of the Blessed One and sat to one side. Seated to one side, the son of the great yakṣa general Maṇibhadra addressed the Blessed One with these words:

"Venerable One, whatever monk, or nun, or layman, or laywoman recites this essence-mantra three times daily — morning, midday, and evening — I wish to follow constantly in that person's service. I shall accomplish all their needs. I shall bestow food, clothing, medicine, gold, wealth, grain, and silver. I shall bring all their aims to fulfillment. All sentient beings shall come under their sway. All their various activities I wish to accomplish without delay — except for those that involve wrongdoing and strife."

Namo ratnatrayāya. Namo Maṇibhadrāya mahāyakṣasenāpataye. Syādayathena. If one recites this seven times, it will be accomplished.

The ritual procedure for this is as follows. On the fifteenth day of the waxing moon, having purified oneself, burn agaru incense at each of the three times of day and recite this seven thousand times; one will obtain gold.
