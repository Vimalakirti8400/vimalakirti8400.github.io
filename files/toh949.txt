The Dhāraṇī Called Vajravidāraṇa (Destroying with the Vajra) (Vajravidāraṇānāmadhāraṇī)

The Dhāraṇī Called Vajravidāraṇa, or Destroying with the Vajra, known in Sanskrit as the Vajravidāraṇā Nāma Dhāraṇī and in Tibetan as Dorjé Nampar Jompa Zhejawé Zung, is a short scripture devoted to the recitation of a protective incantation. It opens with the Buddha dwelling on the vajra while Vajrapāṇi, empowered by all the buddhas and bodhisattvas, enters the vajra samādhi and proclaims a vajra heart-essence arising from the Vajra Wrathful One. This mantra is described as indestructible, firm, and unobstructed, able to overawe all beings and cut through every obstacle and harmful force. The text is Toh 949 in the Kangyur, where it belongs to the collection of dhāraṇī texts, and the same dhāraṇī is also widely used in tantric practice for purification and protection.

In the language of Tibet: The Dhāraṇī Called Vajravidāraṇa, Destroying with the Vajra.

Homage to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas.

Thus did I hear at one time. The Blessed One was dwelling on the vajra. Through the power of the Buddha, Vajrapāṇi blessed his entire body as vajra and entered into equipoise in the vajra samādhi.

Then Vajrapāṇi, through the power of all the buddhas, the blessing of all the buddhas, and the blessing of all the bodhisattvas, proclaimed the vajra heart-essence that arose from the Vajra Wrathful One. It cannot be cut and cannot be destroyed. It is true, firm and stable, unobstructed everywhere and undefeated everywhere. It terrifies all sentient beings and overawes all sentient beings. It cuts through all vidyā-mantras and suppresses all vidyā-mantras. It destroys all rites and terrifies all the rites of others. It annihilates all grahas and frees from all grahas. It summons all bhūtas and subdues all bhūtas. It sets all the rites of vidyā-mantra to work. It accomplishes what has not been accomplished and keeps what has been accomplished from going to waste. It grants every wish. It protects all sentient beings, pacifies, and increases. It paralyzes all sentient beings and stupefies them. This great power of secret mantra was proclaimed by Vajrapāṇi through the power of the Buddha:

When all wrongdoing has been purified,
All suffering will come to be no more.
This is the root of all the tantras,
Beautifully adorned with every glory.

When beings' faculties have declined,
When life is spent or life impaired,
When any kind of misfortune arises,
When the gods have turned their backs,
When friends and loved ones turn hostile,
When servants, spouse and others torment,
When there is no harmony between people,
When harm comes from dwindling wealth,
When harm comes from grief and weariness,
In fear and in destitution,
When threatened by planets, constellations, sorcery,
And by terrible grahas,
And even when one sees evil dreams
That spring from grief and weariness,
Then, thoroughly bathed and clean,
One should listen to this sublime sūtra.

Those with virtuous mind and heart,
Adorned in clean garments,
Who listen to this sūtra,
Profound, the domain of the buddhas,
Will see, by this sūtra's splendor,
That the terrible diseases
Of every living creature
Are all of them pacified.
Life and merit will increase,
And they will be freed from all wrongdoing.

Jewels, white mustard seed and dūrvā grass,
Flawless gems together with sandalwood,
Crystal, a vajra and flowers,
With water, in a vase of gold,
Or else a silver vase will do,
Wrap it in clean cloth and fill it.
Twenty-one times,
Or else a hundred and eight times,
Recite the Vajravidāraṇa,
And always bathe the king.

This completes the dhāraṇī called Vajravidāraṇa.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

"Dwelling on the vajra" renders the Tibetan literally. It may refer to the vajra seat, the site of awakening, but the text does not say so.

"Terrifies all the rites of others" follows the Tibetan spelling 'jigs; if it is read as 'jig, the phrase means "destroys all the rites of others."

The final instruction can also be read as "the king should always bathe," but the imperative is most naturally addressed to the officiant who recites the dhāraṇī.
