The Noble Root Tantra King of Avalokiteśvara Called the Lotus Net (Āryāvalokiteśvarapadmajālamūlatantrarājanāma)

Toh 681 in the Kangyur is a tantric scripture known in English as "The Noble Root Tantra King of Avalokiteśvara Called the Lotus Net," corresponding to the Sanskrit Āryāvalokiteśvara-padmajāla-mūlatantra-rāja and the Tibetan འཕགས་པ་སྤྱན་རས་གཟིགས་དབང་ཕྱུག་གི་རྩ་བའི་རྒྱུད་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་པོ་པདྨ་དྲ་བ. It is a root tantra centering on the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara, the lord of compassion, and takes the form of a teaching delivered by the Buddha to a vast maṇḍala assembly of monks and bodhisattvas gathered in a Himalayan forest grove. The text belongs to the tantra section of the Kangyur and is devoted to the practices, visualizations, and devotional frameworks associated with Avalokiteśvara, whose compassionate qualities are praised at the outset through a reverential homage. As a "root tantra," it would have served as a foundational scriptural source for related ritual and contemplative traditions centered on this beloved bodhisattva.

In the language of India: Āryāvalokiteśvara-padmajāla-mūlatantra-rāja-nāma. In the Tibetan language: The Noble Root Tantra King of Avalokiteśvara, Lord of What Is Seen, Called the Lotus Net.

I bow with reverence to the bodhisattva, the noble Avalokiteśvara, lord of what is seen, possessed of great compassion.

Thus did I hear. At one time the Blessed One was dwelling together with a great maṇḍala assembly in the vast Himalayan forest grove, resplendent with sandalwood trees and young saplings. Present there were: the venerable Mahākāśyapa, the venerable Gayākāśyapa, the venerable Nadīkāśyapa, the venerable Kuvalayadhvaja the Youth, the venerable Aniruddha, the venerable Śāriputra, the venerable Maudgalyāyana the Great, the venerable Subhūti, and others — a great Saṅgha of monks numbering two thousand two hundred and fifty. Also present were the great bodhisattvas: White Lotus, Sunlight, Dhāraṇīśvararāja, Lotus-Eyed, Jñāneśvara, Padmeśvara, Vajragarbha, Ratnagarbha, Samantabhadra, Sarvanīvaraṇaviṣkambhin, Ākāśagarbha, Maitreya, Mañjuśrī, Lokeśvara, Vajrapāṇi, Akṣayamati, Ākāśakośa, Matisāgara, Mahāvikrāmaprāpta, Candraprabha, Jālinīprabha, Pratibhānakūṭa, and Sarvaśokatamovidhvaṃsanamatī, and others — innumerable bodhisattvas, great beings. And furthermore, many monks dwelling primarily in loving-kindness, many nuns, male lay practitioners, and many female lay practitioners. And hosts of gods, hosts of nāgas, and likewise yakṣas, gandharvas (scent-eaters), asuras, garuḍas (sky-soaring birds), kinnaras (those who are human and yet not human), great serpents (mahoragas), kumbhāṇḍas, flesh-eaters, humans and non-humans — sixty million in number. And the great wrathful ones: Vajrahuṃkāra, Vajrayamāntaka, Vajraduḥprasaha, Vajrahayagrīva, Vajravighnatrāsa, Vajroṣṇīṣa, and Vajrāmṛtakunḍalīrāja, and others — seventy in all. And Locanā, Māmakī, Pāṇḍaravāsinī, Tārā, Raśmivati, Sarasvatī, Vārāhīmukhī, Bhrukuṭī, Araṇyā, Viṣaghni, Vasudhārā, Śvetā, Manobhadrā, Vijayā, Śrīmālā, Vasumatī, and Vajreśvarī, and others — six hundred thousand — all were dwelling together there.

At that time all those in that retinue made many offerings to the Blessed One out of reverence, honor, and veneration, and extolled him with many praises. Then the Blessed One, the Great Sage, entered into the meditative absorption known as "The Complete Arising of Great Compassion." The very moment he entered it, the great earth trembled in six ways and displayed eighteen auspicious signs. Seeing those auspicious signs, the king of Laṅkā, Ravaṇa, filled with great pride, came there together with his many attendants. Then the Blessed One, perceiving in his mind the mental thoughts of Ravaṇa, sent forth from all parts of his body many hundreds of millions of rays of light. Those rays of light and the trembling of the earth illuminated all the buddha-fields in the ten directions.

At that time, with a resounding proclamation full of pride, Ravaṇa together with his entire retinue fully considered in his mind the affliction of epidemic disease and other such sufferings that were ravaging Jambudvīpa. Then the great bodhisattva, the noble Avalokiteśvara, he of eleven faces, came into the presence of the Blessed One together with ten billion hundred-thousand bearers of mantra-knowledge, paid homage at his feet with the crown of his head, circumambulated him to the right, and took his place to one side.

Then, by the power of the Buddha, from within the assembly of gods a divine son named "Adorned with a Net of Precious Lotuses" rose up, pressed his palms together, and addressed the great bodhisattva, the noble Avalokiteśvara, with these words:

Ah! O Lord, O Compassionate One,
accompanied by hosts of mantra-bearers,
endowed with great blessing and power —
to vanquish the proud boasting of Ravaṇa,
to uproot afflictions at their very source,
to benefit all sentient beings,
and to be of the greatest healing for us —
please set forth the supreme essence:
the distinctive practice of a mantra
that accords perfectly with your body.

At that very moment all the assembled retinue filled the sky in every direction with a rain of flowers. Above the head of the Blessed One, the Sage, a canopy arose from precious flowers. The gods offered, with the power of pure faith and whatever was at hand, water for the feet, offering water, incense, fragrant substances, and food. The Sage consecrated these as a treasure-store of the sky. Many divine maidens made offering with melodious music, hand-held canopies, streaming pennants and victory banners swaying to and fro, and garments adorned with tinkling ornaments.

Then the Lord Avalokiteśvara, with a face like a fresh lotus in bloom, rose, smiled, pressed his palms together, and addressed the Teacher with these words:

The proud boasting of Ravaṇa's form,
his arrogance swollen with arrows and martial skill —
this has not arisen from good deeds well performed,
nor has it come from deeply wrong karma alone.
In a former time, under the teaching of the buddha Lovely-Faced,
he gave over his head in devotion,
and from that merit, countlessly accrued,
arose the confusion of his many arms,
the food and vessels, garments, wealth, and ornaments
he donated as an offering through forty aeons —
from that too come his power and strength.
As for his arrows: though he has preceded their use
with the blessing of the Bhrīṅgīriti rite,
through his innumerable violations of moral discipline
he was born into the lineage of the rākṣasas
and has brought harm to others along with his retinue.

Now, so that those who have karma beginning from the very first
may be benefited and given a foundation of benefit,
I shall speak, gathered briefly together.
Listen well and hold it in mind.

In past times, at a former age,
even before ten Ganges rivers' worth of aeons had passed,
far in the distant past before those many ages gone by,
there appeared in the world a Tathāgata, a perfectly awakened buddha,
whose name was "Fragrance of the Mandāra."
At the time that Teacher appeared in the world,
I myself had become a powerful householder.
I received from that Sage the mantra-teaching,
and immediately upon receiving it I wandered in saṃsāra
for a span of some forty thousand aeons.
Then, an aeon before ten million Ganges rivers' worth of aeons,
a perfectly awakened buddha whose name was
"King of Rays, Whose Uṣṇīṣa Blazes with Qualities,
Whose Eyes Are like a Hundred Unobstructed Lotuses"
dwelt in the world as its Teacher.
At that time I myself was a great seer and master.
By receiving and practicing this mantra-teaching
I beheld the faces of the buddhas of the ten directions
and attained the acceptance of the non-arising of phenomena.
Then, before one hundred million aeons of the past had elapsed,
there appeared in the world a perfectly awakened buddha
whose name was Amitāyus,
and I received that teaching.

At that time the buddhas of the ten directions
conferred empowerment upon me and gave prophecy.
Before you, across an uncountable span of aeons,
for the sake of this mantra-teaching,
you made great primary offerings numbering a full thousand times a thousand.
In eleven hundred million instances
you received empowerment from the buddhas of the ten directions.
The supreme karmic ripening of all that
will arise again and again
throughout the vast reaches of buddha-fields across all directions.
In the later time, when the guide known as Śākyamuni
was born into this world-realm of endurance,
even more than human existences,
some were born in the realm of hungry ghosts.
For the purpose of definitively increasing the Dharma,
on a dark night thick with cloud,
swift as a flash of lightning in that moment,
the holder of the mantra will appear in a form
possessed of eleven faces,
endowed with a thousand limbs,
with the mark of thirteen hundred eyes.
At that time, certain fortunate ones,
calling upon the name and holding faith in the body,
whoever recites this mantra
will accomplish every wish in the world,
and afterward will attain twofold primordial wisdom.

Hearing that prophecy proclaimed,
the mantra-essence of eleven faces,
spoken from the mouths of eleven hundred million buddhas,
of great power, unimpeded,
heals all beings and grants wellbeing,
clears all sickness, purifies all evil,
averts all that is inauspicious,
dispels all ill omens and bad dreams,
turns back untimely death and malicious intent,
bestows constant splendor and happiness of mind,
removes grief, suffering, and exhaustion,
pacifies all conflict and strife,
and calms māras, demons, and obstructors.
It fulfills every wish in the world.
Māras, obstructions, the disease of weapons,
blows with a staff, being bound,
being imprisoned, being attacked with arms,
being led to a place of killing, having limbs severed,
the eyes being pierced with thorns or blades,
the upheaval of water, fire, and wind,
poison, bewitchment, and reanimated corpses,
as well as thieves and non-humans —
all these are subdued, and one who is protected by this mantra,
who takes it up, holds it, inquires into it,
reads it, recites it by heart, and teaches it,
and strives in this: neither the world with its gods,
nor māras, nor Brahmā, nor renunciants (śramaṇas),
nor brahmins can cause harm.
Even heavy karma from the past
will be purified and refined until it is no more.
This is a certainty, a real thing.
Knowing this, with direct personal confidence,
take it up with a mind free of conceptual elaboration.
For this reason it is itself the refuge, the protector,
the guardian, the giver of breath and relief,
the glorious lord, the island, the very home.
To whatever extent the near and far reaches of the land extend,
by setting boundaries with this mantra,
beings will cause no harm.

Whoever pays homage and makes offerings to the buddhas,
goes for refuge and confesses misdeeds,
rejoices wholeheartedly, and recites
this dhāraṇī mantra twenty-one times —
all the buddhas of the ten directions without exception
will equally rejoice and bestow the good.
All evil without exception, the four root transgressions and the five acts of immediate retribution,
will be completely purified.
Even those who appear as yakṣas, rākṣasas, and the like,
once taken hold of and guided with a mind of loving-kindness and compassion,
will first give rise to awakening mind,
and be established on the ground of the supreme unsurpassable bodhicitta.

More than calling upon the names of buddhas
as numerous as a hundred Ganges rivers and praising them —
whatever son or daughter of good family
who recites and keeps in mind my name,
and continually recites this mantra,
will swiftly attain the ground of supreme joy,
and unsurpassable awakening will come swiftly,
as if seen in the very palm of the hand.

With single-pointed mindfulness and devoted respect,
always rising before dawn,
performing virtue in accordance with the Dharma,
and reciting this one hundred and eight times —
in this very life one will attain ten benefits:
the body will constantly be free from illness,
one will be protected by all the gods and buddhas,
one will gain wealth, grain, precious things, and garments,
enemies and hostility will be tamed, and one will be fearless,
food will be honored and a noble lineage attained,
one will be free from poison, spirits of the dead, and demonic possession,

the body will be fragrant and pleasing to all,
evil intentions and ill repute will be dispelled,
one will be free from epidemic disease and easily satisfied,
and untimely death will be cleared away.

Beyond these, four further qualities will be attained:
at the moment of death one will behold the face of a buddha,
light will arise in the sky and one will be honored by the gods,
one will never be born in a lower realm,
and one will be born in Sukhāvatī or in a higher destiny.

For one who holds this vidyā-mantra (skt. vidyāmantra) together with the wisdom-essence of the buddhas,
one will attain the awakening mind and the door to the Dharma of liberation of the buddhas.

Through accumulating virtue over many aeons,
one will come to obtain this vidyā-mantra.
Such a one is endowed with faith, has a sense of shame, and knows modesty,
is of good intelligence, diligent, and possessed of great compassion,
fair of complexion, of pleasant disposition, and speaks gently,
is soft of mind, and moves body and speech with ease.
One is deeply respectful toward the teacher and of great discipline,
has few cravings and delights in generosity,
and with reverence for the buddhas and a conscientious mind,
recites the vidyā-mantra continuously, day and night.

On the fourteenth or the fifteenth,
and especially on the eighth of the month,
one should take up the restoration ceremony before an image of the body.
From wood that is flawless — white sandalwood and the like —
crafted from a noble species, an image may be made,
or equally from earth, stone, precious gems, and such things,
a form of the Sugata may be fashioned from good materials;
or it may be painted as a picture.
Whether a single image or an expanded maṇḍala with an assembly of deities,
if one visualizes it as vividly as direct perception,
one will swiftly come to behold it in direct vision.

With a vase full of water adorned with all virtues,
by reciting the mantra over it all desired aims will be accomplished —
of this there should be no doubt.

With medicines suited to the illness,
by reciting the mantra and then eating or applying them,
or even merely fumigating with them, harm will be dispelled.
Likewise, by sealing a thread or garments with the hand-gesture (mudrā),
the same effect is produced.

For whatever desire one may have,
the fire-ritual practice should be performed.
The secret mantra, held with desire-free mental application —
I too shall enter into that.

Immediately upon these words being spoken,
the bodhisattva Mañjuśrī spoke these deliberate words
concerning the very essential nature of the mantra:

How wondrous! Phenomena that are without inherent nature
appear in manifold forms.
The instant that is free from speech and conceptual thought —
that itself is the greatness of peace.

To meditate is to take as object
the fact that the things of existence lack inherent existence.
Seeing that the ultimate is without object —
that is the fruit of meditation.

Samantabhadra spoke these deliberate words
concerning the meaning of the secret mantra:

How wondrous! The three bodies, though appearing distinct,
arise from the meaning of body, speech, and mind.
Through the yoga of their being of a single meaning,
they are to be held with certainty as a single body.

Although what appears for the benefit of whomever it may be
appears like a rainbow,
it is merely an emanation expressed in words —
in the wisdom body there is no division.

For the purification of obscurations and karma,
these deliberate words were spoken thus:

How wondrous! The essential nature of things —
unborn, unarisen, unceased —
yet the magical display that appears as arising and ceasing:
that very body appears as mudrā and mantra,
and illuminates, on the face of arising,
qualities like waves of water.
Through diligence, that very suchness
is without passage and is comprehended within.

Kṣitigarbha spoke these deliberate words concerning the cause:

How wondrous! In the space of intrinsically pure awareness,
the expanse of beginninglessly darkened mind,
disturbed by conceptual thoughts and habitual tendencies,
suddenly white and black karmic actions appear.
The white is superimposed as: buddha, Dharma, Saṅgha,
and awakening mind.
Through the enlightened activity arising from that,
the obscurations of afflictions and karma are purified.

The protector Maitreya spoke these deliberate words
concerning the path of mantra accomplishment:

How wondrous! The mind, primordially liberated and at peace,
appears clear like water,
yet having taken on the forms of beings in their variety,
it is transformed by the dense darkness of ignorance.
Because that very darkness is but the radiance of mind,
through effortless mind one is liberated.
But striving, through the defilement of mind,
will bind the stainless mind.

Ākāśagarbha (the Essence of Space) spoke these deliberate words
concerning the name of the mantra:

How wondrous! Empty, free from characteristics —

free of color and shape — pure dharmatā,
illuminating the great mind of joy,
it is not a syllable of great wisdom,
yet it is the body endowed with major and minor marks, with gnosis and bliss.
For whomever it appears with that meaning,
by the far-reaching momentum of great compassion,
this is the significance of the syllable Ta that is to arise.
The bodhisattva Padma (Lotus) spoke these deliberate words concerning this path and abode:

How wondrous! Through causes and conditions in the sky,
the continuous revolutions of sun, moon, and stars
shine out and distinguish day from night.
Through afflictions, ignorance, craving, and grasping,
the formations impel the karma of existence,
giving rise to consciousness, name-and-form, the six sense bases,
contact, feeling, birth, aging, death, and suffering.
Purification by the twofold luminous purity —
Vajrapāṇi spoke these deliberate words concerning the meaning of the fruit:

How wondrous! The profound and vast
supreme power of mantra
is not known even by buddhas or non-Buddhists (tīrthikas).
Awareness itself has no substantial existence,
so how could the object of knowledge exist as a substance?
Awareness, in the aspect of its object,
is conditioned by the conceptual duality of apprehended and apprehender.
When that is exhausted, liberation occurs.

Then the assembly of awareness goddesses
scattered perfumes and flowers,
and with a hymn of melodious mantra-song
praised the protector Avalokiteśvara:

Oṃ — O Blessed One, Protector, Compassionate Avalokiteśvara,
your faces, in their color and form, appear in countless ways —
one thousand, one hundred, eleven, six, and one —
I bow to the Protector who possesses the beautiful face of loving-kindness.
This is of Cundā.

Oṃ — Your face, in great joy, transforms into countless forms and colors.
Some faces bear loving-kindness, compassion, and joy;
some bear the face of great desire, confusion, and miserliness;
some bear the face of wrath, anger, fierce passion, and mistaken views —
to you I bow.
This is of Māmakī.

Oṃ — Your face is perfected by the empowerment of the buddhas of the ten directions,
your hand is that of a king of offerings whose teaching knows no bounds,
and you possess the mudrā of a hand that has mastered innumerable deeds —
to you, seated upon two thrones with two feet, I bow.
This is of the White-Robed One.

Oṃ — Though your acts do not touch the limit of the expanse of compassion,
you perform them in an unbroken ocean of endeavor;
you roam the ocean of buddha-fields in their entirety —
I bow to you, Protector Avalokiteśvara.
This is of Tārā.

Oṃ — Like the brilliant sun scattering its rays,
though you have no deliberate intention and are free from the darkness of all conceptuality,
across an ocean of eons you make the exceptional meaning shine clearly —
I bow to you, Protector Avalokiteśvara.
This is of Raśmivāsinī.

Oṃ — When the stream of mind ceases and passes into nirvāṇa,
attachment arises and immediately dissolves;
the root of affliction is cut without remainder —
I bow to you, Protector Avalokiteśvara.
This is of Sarasvatī.

Oṃ — Grasping afflictions as a self, one is born in saṃsāra;
examining them again and again, one is born in the lower realms;
knowing them to be without inherent nature, one becomes liberated —
I bow to you, Protector Avalokiteśvara.
This is of the Sow-Faced One.

Oṃ — Seeing phenomena as pure, one attains the higher realms;
cutting the conceptuality of dualistic grasping, one passes beyond sorrow;
possessing a mind like illusion, the benefit of others arises —
I bow to you, Protector Avalokiteśvara.
This is of the Frowning One.

Oṃ — Attachment to one's own mode of conduct rots the seed;
speaking disparagingly of others' modes of conduct burns the field of merit;
awakening is known from bodily and verbal purity alone —
I bow to you, Protector Avalokiteśvara.
This is of Dharaṇīdhārā.

Oṃ — For those overwhelmed by the poison-darkness of the view of self,
you bestow the nectar-medicine of impartial wisdom of selflessness,
and grant the practice and realization of the ten perfections —
I bow to you, Protector Avalokiteśvara.
This is of the Poison-Dispelling One.

Oṃ — In fine conduct and good deportment, free from pride and attachment;
regarding others' conduct with equanimity, acting from one's own mind;
without craving, endowed with patience, that is the natural mode of challenge —
I bow to you, Protector Avalokiteśvara.
This is of the Forest-Dwelling One.

Oṃ — Possessing conscience and modesty, delighting in giving;
endowed with faith and learning, excellent in moral discipline and wisdom;
knowing contentment and few desires, rich in this life and the next —
I bow to you, Protector Avalokiteśvara.
This is of Dhāraṇī.

Oṃ — Writing, offering, giving, listening, reading, and teaching;
reciting, holding in mind, meditating, striving — for these, the water
of ablution and such practices, the Nyungné fast, purity, and virtue are granted.
I bow to you, Protector Avalokiteśvara.
This is of the White One.

Oṃ — Taming one's own mind, acting with care in body and speech;
holding sacred offerings for the world and for what transcends the world;
through hearing, reflection, and meditation, striving — that conquers the enemy.
I bow to you, Protector Avalokiteśvara.
This is the Song of Victorious One.

Oṃ — Liberation from the bondage of duality through the non-dual path,
skillful means and wisdom, the great bliss, the path of awakening;
liberation, boundless gnosis — bestowing glorious splendor.
I bow to you, Protector Avalokiteśvara.
This is the Song of Glorious Garland One.

Oṃ — On the golden ground of excellent aspiration,
the great wish-fulfilling tree of the Dharmas conducive to awakening (bodhipakṣadharmas) arose.
You who moisten it with the stream of gnosis and compassion —
I bow to you, Protector Avalokiteśvara.
This is the Song of Goodly-Minded One.

The sound resounded, and the retinue trembled,
like grass struck by the wind.
Joining their palms at the crown of their heads,
they offered this supplication with single purpose:

Oṃ — Protector, self-arisen Blessed One,
supreme and supremely great God;
body born of manifold merit,
mind possessed of boundless gnosis;
wise one, maker of the end of existence;
virtuous one, who sets beings in ethical conduct;
pure one, great Brahmin, great Brahmā;
whose activity and conduct are as vast as the sky — to you we bow.

Then the Blessed One, the great Sage,
said to the assembly, "Excellent! Excellent!"
and with Oṃ Āḥ Hrīḥ Hūṃ Svāhā,
repeatedly spoken, he bestowed blessing.
As one with a hook draws fish from the shallows
and leads away a hundred thousand head of nāga-cattle,
so with the pure arousing of the ascetic's virtue
he established them in the precious island of awakening.

This concludes the first chapter, the chapter of the occasion, from the king of root tantras of the noble Avalokiteśvara, the Lord, called the Lotus Net.

Then the Blessed One, the great Sage,
the Protector, the perfectly complete buddha,
entered into meditative absorption (samādhi)
called Variegated Illumination.
From every limb and part of his body
he released innumerable five-colored rays of light —
sixty times sixty at their edges,
countless in number, he scattered them forth.
To the limits of incalculable buddha-fields seven times over,
throughout all ten directions without remainder,
filling everything with light — and from that light
these melodious verses appeared:

Abandon contempt for the buddhas and the Dharma.
Abandon great sloth and conduct that grasps at sufficiency.
Abandon regret and indecision regarding the Dharma.
Abandon great pride and conduct driven by attachment.
Without beings there is no path to awakening.
Relying on beings, great awakening will be attained.
The great path of awakening is great compassion.
If there were no beings, in whom could it arise?

The very instant that sound was heard,
the buddha's children dwelling in buddha-fields throughout the ten directions —
beyond telling, the supreme sovereign
Avalokiteśvara the Lord, innumerable and supreme —
all of them, together with their retinues,
arrived here in this world of endurance, Sahā.
From the eastern buddha-fields,
at the very limit of seven incalculable reaches,
from the buddha-field called Lion's Heap,
from the presence of the buddha Lion's Play,
the foremost among bodhisattvas,
Avalokiteśvara the Lord with the Lion's Roar,
white in color, like a mountain of crystal,
together with ten million of the highest awakening mind,
traveled through the sky
and arrived in this buddha-field swift as a flash of lightning.
To the Blessed One, the Protector, the great Sage,
they paid homage and circumambulated many times.
With many silver flowers bearing a thousand petals
they scattered offerings.
The Sugata, the perfectly complete buddha,
with his wondrous rays of light
and with verses of great wonder and marvel,
caused all the buddha-fields to tremble.
He who broke through the darkness of beings' delusion
and made boundless wisdom shine bright —
I too, by the power of this heart-essence,
will assist in this very enlightened activity.

Oṃ Hrīḥ Siṃhanāda Hūṃ Phaṭ.

Having thus offered that heart-essence,
to the side of the great Sage —

his retinue remained in a joyful manner,
kneeling with palms joined together.

In the supreme buddha-field of the southern direction,
at the very edge of the sevenfold immeasurable,
in the buddha-field adorned with stacked precious jewels,
where the buddha Ratnaketu dwells —
before him, the chief among bodhisattvas,
the sovereign Avalokiteśvara, the wish-fulfilling jewel,
spins the wheel.
Resembling a golden mountain in hue,
ten billion assemblies of bodhisattvas
ascended together into the sky
and, like a flash of lightning, arrived in this buddha-field.
To the Blessed One, the Protector, the Sage,
they paid homage and circumambulated many times.
With many thousand-petalled
precious golden flowers
they scattered vast and abundant offerings.
The Sugata, the perfectly complete buddha,
with his wondrous rays of light
and with verses of great wonder and marvel,
caused all the buddha-fields to tremble.
He cut the bonds of beings' miserliness
and caused the great treasury, the source of generosity, to burst open.
I too, by the power of this heart-essence,
will assist in this very enlightened activity.

Oṃ Padma Dhāra Dhake Hūṃ Phaṭ.

Having thus offered that heart-essence,
to the side of the great Sage —

Then, what is to be rightly explained: he whose very being is the gathering of the buddhas of the ten directions, the world-protector who holds dominion over the ten grounds, whose nature is the full unfolding of the ten powers, in whom the ten perfections are spontaneously accomplished, who, having completed the ten grounds as one, pervades all ten directions — such is the very defining characteristic of the Eleven-Faced One. A person endowed with fortunate merit should place the mantra upon his own body. When the body is graced with the mantra, even the son of Indra (Śakra) cannot prevail against it — what need is there to speak of other worldly forces? Sorcery, flesh-eating spirits, raised corpses, elemental beings, weapons, shadow-oppression, and all evil omens are swiftly dispelled. Proceeding stage by stage as it comes from the lama, one swiftly attains unsurpassable awakening. Without the oral transmission of a lama, nothing will ever be accomplished.

At the crown of the head, the white syllable OṂ is to be contemplated with diligence. At the forehead, the green syllable ĀḤ is likewise to be contemplated. Upon the tongue, the supreme syllable — the red and luminous syllable HRĪḤ — is to be contemplated. In the region of the heart, the blue syllable HŪṂ. At the navel, the yellow syllable HA. Likewise at the secret place, the green syllable HO. At the place of excretion, the blue syllable HO. At the two knees, the pale yellow syllable SA. At the soles of the feet, the yellow-green syllable HA. At the two palms, the white syllable HŪṂ. At the forearms, the blue syllable HRĪḤ. At the shoulders, the syllable HRāṂ is to be placed.

Having consecrated oneself through this assembly of syllables, one may enter into whatever activities and endeavors are appropriate. Those various accomplishments, too, arise from the blessing of the mantra. The body itself becomes the essential nature of the mantra; the body generated by the mantra, the body endowed with mantra, becomes the very body of mantra. Having purified through the family of the mantra-body across many buddha fields, one takes birth in the bodies of wandering beings. The conduct that liberates beings becomes equal to that of the supreme Samantabhadra, and one comes to know the meaning as inseparable from beings. The means for obtaining that meaning in its entirety — by which it is known through speech — is the very entity called the "knowledge-holder" (Skt. vidyādhara). One who abides in that is called a holder of awareness. This is not known to buddhas or to non-Buddhists (tīrthikas). To attempt it through effort tainted with something extra is sheer exhaustion, and by that very exhaustion one wanders onto the wrong path, a path difficult to traverse. Abandoning that, one should enter with diligence into this very practice of authentic accomplishment — utterly at ease and blissful — even for those of limited fortune.

If one is wearied by the full elaboration, one may instead depict assemblies of syllables, mudrās, and images numbering fifty-eight, or thirty-seven, or twenty-nine, or twenty-five, or fifteen, or nine, or five, or one — these are the magical displays of the emanation body, arranged according to one's own wish.

They become the very blessing of the mind. The Sugata's images, scripture texts, mudrās, and the like are to be consecrated. Whatever has deteriorated or become soiled by impurity is to be purified by washing and restoration, and then, with a mind free of conceptual thought, one who does so gains immeasurable merit. Even if the form and other qualities are inferior to what came before, the practitioner thereby obtains boundless merit.

The enlightened activity and the secret body,
the defining marks of the Tathāgata —
suchness itself and emptiness,
awakening's likeness to the sky —
the major marks and minor features are conventional,
like an illusion or a dream.

The thirty-two and eighty marks,
the body of syllables, symbols, and elaboration:
the saṃbhogakāya is the result consonant with merit as its cause;
the nirmāṇakāya is the result of the karmic ripening of action.
Both bear the characteristics of conventional and ultimate truth,
and for those who are fortunate, whether their perception is pure or impure,
the Sugata's enlightened activity and the two form bodies
arise from the two accumulations as the three bodies.

The dharmakāya, utterly purified, empty of all afflictions,
beyond the domain of body, speech, and mind,
realizing the objectless expanse of space —
this is the dharmakāya of the Victors.

Dependent origination, free from both eternalism and annihilation,
understood in the heart as birthless and unceasing —
through the path of mental consciousness's engagement,
it is to be known by habituating oneself in meditative concentration.

With the iron hook of faith and with wisdom,
with the virtue of a hundred thousand minds of gods and nāgas pure and white,
with the motivation of the treasury of awakening, the precious jewel —
through this, may you attain it; may the turning of the wheel arise.

Cherishing the center of the mind, and practicing that conduct accordingly,
all the tantras for making the faculties supreme, especially,
and the many taught methods for swiftly accomplishing the lotus network adorned with a thousand hands —
these are to be diligently gathered and treasured by one who is persistent and accomplished.

Those who have the scriptural transmission of the lama and who possess compassion,
the hosts of Victors and their sons all rejoicing —
as for the joy of the assemblies who protect the world, what need is there to say more?

Then, born in the Tuṣita heaven,

Supreme source of all good qualities,
vidyā-mantra endowed with bliss and blessing,
eternal, permanent, foremost of the sacred Dharma,
who dispels all conceptual elaboration and protects — to you we pay homage!

Ever compassionate Avalokiteśvara,
sovereign of the gathering assembly of bodhicitta,
who has acted and dwells throughout the eons,
assembly-lord who holds what is excellent — to you we pay homage!

Arisen from compassion and wisdom
accumulated over many eons,
the blessed garland of vidyā-mantras —
for the benefit of sentient beings,
the protector, mighty Avalokiteśvara, has proclaimed
mudrās and activities endowed with enlightened action.

Sentient beings bound with karmic stain,
fettered by afflictions and self-pride,
caught in the entanglement of sloth and miserliness,
burdened with heavy loads —
in order to swiftly liberate them,
spreading the sun of compassion
to parch the dense leaves of existence
and swiftly accomplish buddhahood,
please expound how the method of practice arises —
the taking up of the deity and the secret mantra,
the application of enlightened activity,
with an unbroken lineage of transmission.

A vidyā-mantra practice without authorization
brings no fruit though one strive; it is mere exhaustion —
a chariot without a driver:
however fine the wheels, where will it go?

To sever the root of afflictions,
the vidyā-mantra is like a sharp sword;
a lance that pierces the armor of ego-clinging;
a great sun that dries the ocean of suffering;
a fire that burns the heaps of dust of conceptual proliferation;
the moisture that gives rise to the flower of wisdom.

the supreme medicine that cures all disease;
the son of Amṛta (Skt. amṛtasya putra) who dispels anxiety and dread;
a stream of water that washes away habitual tendencies;
a great treasure that increases wealth and enjoyment;
a vessel that ferries one across saṃsāra;
and like the wind, it clears away all faults.

Kṣitigarbha then made this supplication: O Protector, noble Avalokiteśvara (the Lord who looks with eyes of compassion), sovereign lord — your blessing, like an iron hook, draws beings out of the prison of saṃsāra; as one might lead away a hundred thousand head of nāga-cattle, you rouse all virtue and set it down upon the precious ground of awakening. Then, in the Brahmā world, a god born in the manner of Prabhāsvarā — named Vast Samādhi, one of broad and profound realization — rose together with his retinue and rained down precious flowers of every kind in the direction of the Teacher, filling the sky above with a canopy of parasols. Some, through distraction of mind,

Without true instruction they deceive beings;
without attainment they destroy the teaching.
Driven by craving, they become mere entertainers for a season,
and in the next life go to the hell realms and other lower destinies.

Some, with sense-bases as doorways of karmic action,

possess instruction and the manner of the tantras,

Those over whom they hold power,
treated with contempt even by their own disciples,
see aversion grow and disputes arise,
and by this, both parties go to the hells and such.

Some, with little learning and weak wisdom,
confused, dependent on external conditions, puffed up with pride,
take up the teaching and those who hold it
as objects of fault-finding speech, and so go to the hells and such.

Some, led by spiritual companions of non-virtue
and firmly fixed on entering the wrong path,
disparage the correct path and abandon diligent effort;
those who pursue wrong practice go to hell.

Some, without contemplation, without mantra recitation,
with lamps, incense, torma offerings, and the like —
seized upon by spirits, both they themselves
and their retinues live shortened lives with diminished enjoyment.

Some, with raging and fierce craving and lust,
through jealousy of other learned ones,
exaggerate and denigrate, feign the manner of the Dharma, curse it,
and strive to establish their own wrong views —
those with entrenched darkness of mind
are born into the class of fierce spirits.

Some, through extreme miserliness,
praise gods and gurus with mere words,
yet when offering food, enjoyment, ornaments, garments,
flowers, incense, torma, and the like,
give in meagre and halfhearted dribbles —
they are certain, again and again, to be born

Therefore, having abandoned all those faults,
one who is noble and possesses good intention

and without confusion about the meaning that has been established,
recite it, hold it in mind,
and strive without doubt.
If one thereby obtains the means to liberate the assembly of beings,
what need is there to speak of one's own benefit alone?

Now, what remains to be correctly explained: by means of approach practice, having overcome the power of obstructing forces, and in order to swiftly obtain attainment, one should apply oneself to the practice with diligence.

From the first day of the white fortnight of the month, one should bathe, don fresh clothing, and sit before a fine image of the deity. Remaining in fasting retreat for one day, one recites this mantra one hundred and eight times in each of the three periods of the day and makes offerings of whatever one has.

From dawn of the eighth day, in a secluded place, on a fine seat and throne, one places the image facing west. One makes offerings of food, drink, and things enclosed by a fence. One burns agaru and durukṣa incense continuously. One eats food of white grain and milk, and for three days recites as much as one is able.

Then, for three days between, one subsists on milk, yogurt, and rice. Having greatly increased the offerings to the deity, at the foot of one thousand and eight awakening trees, one smears durukṣa butter. Piece by piece, reciting one mantra per piece, one burns them before the bodhisattva.

Then the earth itself will shake, and the image too will shake. From above, from the face thereof, a beautiful sound will come: "Excellent! Excellent! Son of good family! You who are earnest and diligent — whatever medicine you desire, whatever aim you pursue, whether one who flies through the sky, one with swift feet, or a royal sage who holds awareness — if you wish to be equal to me in fortune, this very day I shall grant it."

On the fifteenth day, that image should be placed inside a stūpa or before a stūpa that contains relics of the Sugata (the well-gone). Taking the restoration and fasting vow, in fasting retreat, one strikes the image with one hundred and eight blue flowers, reciting one mantra per strike.

At that time, from the very root of what lies before one, a sound like a great peal of thunder will arise, and the great earth will shake mightily. At that very occurrence, one should not be frightened, but should recall the mantra practice and remember: the great bodhisattva, the great being, the Protector Avalokiteśvara, the Lord endowed with great compassion — to him, I myself bow down with reverence.

May I myself, having become the protector and refuge of all sentient beings, properly and completely fulfill the hopes and wishes of all beings.

At that time, in granting what is desired, there will be no obstacle from the assembly of gods and nāgas. All desired aims of the mantra will be accomplished. This is an aspirational prayer that perfects merit.

By means of prostrations, circumambulations of supports, the building of stūpas, maṇḍalas, and the limbs of yoga — so endowed with these activities, for the sake of oneself and others, when one strives without interruption,

Those vast aspirational prayers that create virtue — they will quickly come to pass. For that reason, aspirational prayer is taught as supreme.

Whoever is negligent in virtuous practice, being lazy, is seized by māra; such a one speaks with doubt and slander, disparaging the Dharma, and will become a destroyer of the teaching. Therefore, those of corrupt caste and base conduct — keep them far away. Those possessed of miserliness, laziness, and arrogance, along with those who are heedless and distracted — they walk māra's path of bondage, the wrong path. Drive them away, expel them and cast them out.

Then it was taught that illness and harm and the like are dispelled by the mere utterance of mantra. For whatever disease or malevolent spirit, the mantrin should clearly and forcefully invoke the mantra, and recite it oneself with single-pointed mind. First, with a mantra accompanied by compassion, one is freed from sickness and possession. At that time, one should apply oneself to offerings.

For madness that arises each day, or on the second or third day, or a fever that flares every four or seven days; for a being, a ḍākinī, a flesh-eater, or a madness-causing, memory-destroying spirit causing bodily decay; for powerful demonic possession — these are dispelled by one hundred and eight recitations. Alternatively, on a five-colored thread, tie one knot per mantra, one hundred and eight knots, and hang the knotted cord around the neck or on the shoulder. All obscurations accumulated through wrongdoing will be pacified and purified — to say nothing, then, of obstacles, sickness, and the like. The waves of bliss will be inconceivable.

For swellings, growths, abscesses, burst wounds, eye sores, leprosy, boils, rashes, scabies, and the like — for all manner of malignant diseases; for injury or danger from swords, arrows, spears, and the like; for snake bite, evil scorpions, bees with sharp stingers, and the like — for all such harmful things, recite seven times, one hundred and eight times, or one thousand times. Alternatively, smear with yellow clay mixed with mud, and one will certainly be freed from illness. For illness that has flared on one side from an obstacle or a wind disorder, or for deafness or a blocked nose — recite one hundred and eight times, then anoint the body, consume, and apply to the ears and nose a preparation made from grains, butter, yogurt, and costus root, recited over seven times, and recovery is without doubt.

Then the father of all bodhisattvas, sole protector of beings and chief among guides, earnestly gave instruction on this ritual of the fire offering activity.

"Smooth a square maṇḍala using fine earth, fragrant substances, and water. Take ground rice, butter, excellent grain, tree resins or sap, and the hundred-and-eight herbs. Proclaiming them, illness and epidemic harm are pacified. For sickness, drink a small portion of the butter remainder. Any pain of illness will be overcome. Or one may take ground grain or powder, or make an effigy of earth or monkey-fat, bind it about the face in a wrathful manner with thread, recite the vidyā mantra one hundred and eight times, strike it with a sword, and perform the fire offering. With five hundred repetitions, the power of an enemy is diminished. Or one may roll together an effigy using poison, and burn it on a tree smeared with poisoned blood. With the count of sixty-four, one brings that person under control.

For a fierce spirit that clings obsessively, burn guggul and white mustard in the same manner. Smear the serika plant with sugar and honey and sour butter, and by burning it before the image of the deity, epidemic diseases of the head will be cut off. Burning twenty-one portions of white guggul summons bodhisattvas. Burn twenty-one portions of sour butter, honey, and long pepper wood before the image of the deity, and one will attain the great miraculous powers equal to all bodhisattvas of the vast earth.

If one wishes to expand one's merit, on fragrant water and pure ground, make a square maṇḍala one cubit across. Stack fragrant wood to a height of one cubit. Mañjuṣaka flowers, white mustard, honey, milk, sour butter, aloeswood, white and red sandalwood, fragrant sweet-smelling wood of the measure of one cubit's height, excellent grain in measured portions — first recite the mantra one thousand times, then proclaim it one hundred and eight times: thereby one attains long life, freedom from illness, and kingship. With one thousand, worldly aims are accomplished. With ten thousand, the gods of the desire realm come to one directly. With one hundred thousand, the form realm is directly realized. One will see all bodhisattvas of the vast earth and come to possess the six kinds of direct knowledge. With ten million repetitions, one will see all victors and move through many buddha-fields.

Furthermore, for minor activities, whatever they may be, perform the practice anywhere from twenty-one up to one hundred and eight times, keeping the mind steadfast on the mantra and the deity.

For clearing away severe epidemic illness, the primary substances to burn are three fruits. For removing great malevolent spirits, burn guggul, garlic, and the flesh of a female dog, one hundred and eight times. For bad dreams, ill omens, subtle illness, and various types of spirits, dūrvā grass and white mustard are declared supreme."

For whichever grain one desires, perform a fire offering using that very grain. One who wishes to bring down rain should burn campaka flowers, the three parts, the three sweets, nāgakeśara, and the hand of Indra — rain will fall as desired. If one wishes to cut off rain, scatter ashes and white mustard again and again in all four directions with the mantra of the dance-lord, applying oneself earnestly. White mustard, yogurt, butter, honey, and rice — by these a child is healed. For fear of spirits or epidemic illness, and for one who wishes to protect the home, perform a fire offering of red lotus exactly twenty-one times. One who desires a good mind and long life should perform a fire offering of white sandalwood. For clearing away minor epidemic illness, perform a fire offering of leaves of the campaka tree. When wishing to cure livestock disease, burn milk together with white flowers. For dispelling both illness and spirits, burn the three sweets, fine grain, camphor, the epidemic-wood of white dūrvā grass, wood that yields milk, and the wood of beautiful flowers. For one who wishes to pacify spirit-illness, burn the three fruits and similar woods. Minor spirits are pacified by burning the three sweets and white dūrvā grass. For bad dreams and whatever ill omens there may be, ground rice in butter with the mantra clears them away. For clothing, vessels, cut substances, and the body — when these are defiled by another's evil shadow — burn ground rice in butter, white incense, and white flowers. Burning tiṅglo and byid flowers clears away all eye diseases. Burning masuli together with its root clears away diseases of the ears. Burning sandalwood, musk, and butter clears away all diseases of the nose. Burning nutmeg, guggul, and chebulic myrobalan clears away diseases of the teeth and tongue. Burning sonamja and kakura destroys throat disease. Burning honey, sandalwood, and sugar clears away lung disease. Burning the three fruits together with the five sour milks clears away heart disease. Burning uśī (coriander), a type of wasp, and biyandaka clears away liver disease. Burning flowers of the five colors pacifies all internal diseases of the stomach and so forth — every last one — and one also attains a beautiful body. One who desires wealth should burn acacia in butter mixed with curd. For whatever color of cloth one desires, burn flowers of matching color together with twelve finger-lengths of thread, using fragrant woods of matching color — the acquisition is certain, do not doubt. Burning priyaṅgu, kaliṅga, lotus pollen, and the branch, while making supplication, greatly expands one's treasury of wealth. Burning rice and fruit of sprouts and so forth makes one's wealth stable. For one who wishes for offspring, burn the three fruits one hundred times — sons and daughters will increase. One who wishes retinue and troops to grow should burn jātī flowers, white cardamom, white milk, yogurt, and butter — by this, retinue and armies increase. Performing this a hundred thousand times, one obtains royal sovereignty. By burning the three sweets and the fruit of bael one hundred thousand times, one becomes a king oneself. For the sake of increasing the power of the mantra, burn neem wood with butter, white mustard, and honey one hundred and eight times. One who wishes to bring a king together with his retinue under one's control should burn white mustard together with the mustard of the Victor six times the weight of six peas. To bring the yakṣa (yakṣa) women under one's power, burn the three fruits and saffron one thousand times using the epidemic-wood of aśoka. To bring an assembly of yakṣas under one's power, burn the three fruits and guggul two thousand times using the wood of the three fruits. One who wishes to bring the gods under one's power should burn chebulic myrobalan wood, sarala resin, milk, yogurt, butter, and honey three thousand times — by this the gods come under one's power.

One who wishes to bring women under one's power should burn sesame, rice, and the like upon a ladle one hundred and eight times together with naturally occurring things — by this a lovely woman comes under one's power. One who wishes to bring children under one's power should perform a fire offering of priyaṅgu grain. Whatever ritual actions one undertakes, apply to them a mind that matches the nature of the activity. For pacification, use a mind of peace. In one of good fortune whose mind is expansive — one endowed with longing and aspiring intent — subjugation will certainly come about, without doubt. When the mind is wrathful, fierce rites ensue. A stable mind endowed with leisure and opportunity, continuously clear and present — whatever one intends through such a mind will be accomplished; otherwise it will not be so.

Just as an object in front of the body appears clearly to an eye free of defect, so when one is separated from a guru, the blessing of the mantra does not dawn. Through body, speech, and mind, in every way, one should make a committed effort to abandon disparaging the guru. Through disparagement one goes to hell and other lower destinies.

Worship the samādhi, mantra, and mudrā that have come from the guru's speech.

Water purifies and pacifies the obscuration of dust and darkness. The maṇḍala of water for pacification is white and circular alone. Earth causes grass, trees, grain, and flowers to grow and makes things flourish. The maṇḍala of earth for enrichment is yellow and square. Wind gathers and disperses, and having brought things under its sway, causes them to move. The maṇḍala of wind for subjugation is crescent-shaped and red in color. Fire ripens and burns, and by its fierce power causes things to wither away. The maṇḍala of fire for fierce rites is triangular — red within and black on the outside.

When the sun rises in the sky it pacifies the obscuration of darkness. For pacification, face east, and the auspicious garment is white. When the sun moves north, things flourish and expand — for enrichment, face north, and yellow or green garments are auspicious. After midday and through the night, things come under subjugation — for subjugation, face west, and red-orange garments are most auspicious. When the sun moves south, by means of extremely cold water one kills grass and other living things, causing scattering, uprooting, affliction, wounding, exhaustion, and violent agitation. For fierce rites, face south and wear blue or black garments.

Campaka, putikā, the hand-born white lotus, white kumuda, moonflower, and clean water together with —

these are praised for offering in rites of pacification. Mañjunāgaviśarī, sumule, asprā, great golden-hued conch, ciuleser, and yellow lotus, together with water from a great river — these are praised for enrichment rites. Mucina, red manḍu, great royal leaves, fish flesh, sindhura, red lotus, red varieties of kumuda — together with the water of a ewe — are to offered in subjugation rites. Utpala, kṣepalaka, udkhyepa, māmiśi, head of jumodsbyin, pig's ear, daṇḍotpala — together with well water — are to be offered in fierce rites.

White sālu rice and curd, with prikā incense, for pacification. Red sālu rice with milk and red sandalwood — for subjugation. Barley and masa cooked into a meal with huluka — for enrichment. Barley and millet porridge with gugul, cooked meat, and the like — know these as the food offerings and fumigants specific to fierce rites.

For the flame lamp, a wick of castor oil with an aśvattha stem is most auspicious for pacification rites, or alternatively a white lotus stem. Sesame oil or highly refined oil with a sarihaya stem is most auspicious for enrichment rites, or alternatively a portion of a cow's leg. Coconut oil, red lotus oil — these are most auspicious for subjugation rites, or alternatively bayamari. Mustard and sesame oil with a citron-tree wick, or alternatively bark of the gośma tree —

A flame whose color is pale red, glowing and clear, blazing white like the peak of a snow mountain, with the fragrance of bar-dre, smaragdus, and white incense — by these, pacification rites will be accomplished. A flame that flickers red and is swaying and thick, resembling the peak of a sun-drenched mountain, with the fragrance of thick mali-kali — subjugation rites will without doubt be accomplished. A flame whose color is yellow, with flickering clarity, blazing like a mountain at sunrise, with the heavenly fragrance of campaka — enrichment rites will without doubt be accomplished. A flame that is dark and maroon in color, writhing and flickering, emitting a rotten stench, blazing like a forested mountain — fierce rites will be accomplished.

Although there are countless seers (ṛṣis) of the world who have attained meditative absorption, the forty-four fire deities are to be excluded from the fire offering rites. The twelve who have transcended the world and possess meditative absorption are to be taken hold of: Gñānaka, Sābiki, Tukṣuseu, Myasyetro, the Wrathful of the Belly, the Wrathful Mind-Born, the Red Mṛtacomali, Bhisuyaki — these twelve are to be applied in rites such as giving benefit and so forth.

With the ash of the pacification fire offering, subtle spirits are pacified. With the ash of subjugation, spirits are bound. With the ash of enrichment, desired activities are bestowed. On the peak of a mountain it is to be scattered — thereby fierce rites dispel spirits. On the middle of a road it is to be scattered. For pacification and subjugation, scatter it in the corresponding direction, in fields, gardens, and parklands. That which purifies is like a great river; that which is enriching is like the circle of the sun; that which fills is like the ocean.

By the fire offering, the gods are gladdened; the recitation of mantras is accomplished, and mantra activities come to fruition. All assemblies of seers, all assemblies of gandharvas, and all assemblies of spirits — delighted by the fire offering, they become helpers and protectors of the yogin, warding off harm; life is lengthened, splendor increases, illness is absent, abundance of enjoyments flourishes, and all aims are accomplished just as wished.

This concludes the tenth chapter of the tantra known as the Lotus Web, the Root Tantra of the Noble Avalokiteśvara, Lord of the World — the chapter on the application of fire.

Namo ratnatrayāya. Namo āryāvalokiteśvarāya bodhisattvāya mahāsattvāya mahākāruṇikāya. Tadyathā: oṃ śuddhe mṛtaṃ balavardhani svāhā.

In the evening and at dawn, one who is a renunciant (śrāmaṇa), speaking quietly, gentle, and tamed, delighting in bathing and attending to pleasant fragrance, whose conduct is utterly peaceful and free from anger, ever reciting praises with a loving heart, deeply moved with reverence toward Avalokiteśvara himself, tears upon the face, trembling with longing, one who rejoices greatly in a guru from whom transmission has been received — such a supreme person is truly blessed. What need is there to speak of the guardian gods of the world, the elements, and the rest? At all times, within one's own mind, one should keep the essence, the secret mantra. The essence of the proximate recitation purifies the body, and the deity too draws near to one.

Reciting the root mantras many times — ten thousand, one hundred thousand, one million, three million, six million: for some, four or five such recitations complete the task. When applied to activities: seven, fourteen, twenty-one, one hundred, and likewise one thousand complete it. Immediately after, it is liquid; let it cool a little with butter. This was translated and definitively established by the great paṇḍita Soma Śrī Bhava and the Tibetan translator Kyura, the monk Tsültrim Özer, at the great temple called Amṛtodbhava in the great region of Kashgar, which is illuminated by the radiance of vidyā and in which the lotus of wisdom fully blooms.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The names of the bodhisattvas and wrathful deities in the assembly are rendered by their standard Sanskrit equivalents or by close English approximations of the Tibetan where no fixed Sanskrit equivalent is universally established. Several of the wrathful deity names (such as Vajravighnatrāsa and Vajrāmṛtakunḍalīrāja for Vajra Begse Mthar Byed and Vajra Gnod Mdzes Rgyal Po) involve some interpretive latitude; the Tibetan epithets are descriptive rather than direct transliterations, and the Sanskrit names assigned here follow the standard Vajrayāna iconographic tradition where identifiable. The "eighteen auspicious signs" is the conventional rendering of ltas bco brgyad, though some sources enumerate these differently. The total assembly figure of six hundred thousand for the female deity retinue follows the Tibetan literally.  The phrase rendered "the confusion of his many arms" translates ལག་པ་དུ་མར་འཁྲུལ་པ, which is grammatically ambiguous: it could mean either "confusion or delusion arising in many arms" or "he who is mistaken with many arms." The most contextually coherent reading, given the karmic biography being narrated, is that Ravaṇa's supernatural multi-armed form is itself a karmic by-product of those merit-generating acts intertwined with ethical failures, but the exact nuance is genuinely uncertain.

The name བྷྲིང་གི་རི་ཏི (Bhrīṅgīriti) is a Śaiva rite or mantra associated with the attendant Bhrīṅgin of Śiva; it is kept transliterated as it is a proper ritual name.  The phrase rendered "realm of hungry ghosts" for "yi dwags gling" refers to the Preta-island or continent, a cosmological region assigned to hungry ghosts in certain Buddhist cosmologies; the passage seems to say that some beings were born there rather than in human existences, emphasizing the breadth of the bodhisattva's activity across different realms and births. This is a somewhat unusual cosmological reference and may reflect a specific textual tradition within the larger dhāraṇī literature being translated.

The "twofold primordial wisdom" renders "ye shes nyis 'gyur" — literally "primordial wisdom becoming two" — which could refer to the two aspects of wisdom: knowing suchness and knowing the variety of phenomena. The exact doctrinal valence here depends on the source tradition and is worth flagging.  The phrase rendered "easily satisfied" for "དགང་སླ" literally means "easy to fill" or "easy to satiate," a standard idiom for contentment or being easily nourished; the translation is idiomatic.

The line "བདེ་བ་ཅན་ནམ་བདེ་འགྲོར་སྐྱེ" pairs the pure land Sukhāvatī with the general term "higher destiny," reflecting both the specific aspiration for Amitābha's realm and the broader meaning of a favorable rebirth.

The final line is incomplete as transmitted, ending mid-verse; the translation reproduces only what is present in the Tibetan.  The passage identifying the syllable "Ta" is part of an acrostic or name-letter series, where individual syllables of a mantra are glossed with doctrinal content. The speaker called "the bodhisattva Padma (Lotus)" is likely Padmapāṇi, an aspect or epithet of Avalokiteśvara himself.

The epithets of the goddesses — Cundā, Māmakī, White-Robed One, Raśmivāsinī, Sow-Faced One, Frowning One, Dharaṇīdhārā, Poison-Dispelling One, Forest-Dwelling One, and Dhāraṇī — are well-known iconographic forms of ḍākinī or awareness goddesses associated with Avalokiteśvara's entourage; the Tibetan rubrics identify each stanza's speaker or dedicatee.

The line rendered "two thrones and two feet" likely refers to a particular seated iconographic posture with doubled supports, though the precise iconographic referent is not entirely transparent from the text alone.  The text ends mid-sentence with "to the side of the great Sage," indicating the passage breaks off here and continues in the next chapter. This is not an error in translation but a genuine mid-sentence break in the source as given.

The line rendered "the great Brahmin, great Brahmā" reflects a deliberate parallelism in the Tibetan between gtsang ldan, "endowed with purity," and bram ze tshang pa che, which carries both the meaning of "great Brahmin" and an allusion to Brahmā (Tshang pa, a Tibetan rendering of Brahmā); both senses appear to be simultaneously intended.

"Nyungné" is the Tibetan name for a specific fasting and silent-retreat practice associated with Avalokiteśvara; it is retained here as a recognized proper term in Tibetan Buddhist practice.

The phrase rendered "nāga-cattle" translates klu phyugs, a compound that may indicate cattle belonging to or associated with nāgas, or possibly a poetic image of nāga beings themselves likened to livestock; the hook-and-fish simile suggests drawing sentient beings out of saṃsāra.  The buddha name སངས་རྒྱས་རིན་ཆེན་དཔལ is rendered Ratnaketu, a standard name in buddha-field literature for a jewel-glory buddha. སངས་རྒྱས་པདྨའི་འབྱུང་གནས is Padmayoni, literally "Source of Lotuses." སངས་རྒྱས་རྡོ་རྗེ་ཐོགས་མེད is rendered Amoghavarjra (Unobstructed Vajra); the spelling in the Tibetan is as given. སངས་རྒྱས་དོན་ཡོད་འབྱུང་གནས is Amoghasambhava, "Source of the Meaningful." The Padmanīrteśvarī mentioned in the western-direction stanza is a specific deity name preserved in the mantra. The text breaks off mid-stanza at the end of the provided passage.  The passage presents a repeating liturgical structure in which different emanations of Avalokiteśvara from different directional buddha-fields arrive to make offerings and recite praises. The rendering follows this formal repetition faithfully.

The names Padmalocana, Samayaśrī, and Vajradharmeśvara are Sanskrit equivalents of the Tibetan epithets Padma-spyan-gyi-lung, Dam-tshig-dpal, and Rdo-rje-chos-dbang-phyug respectively; these are kept in Sanskrit form as proper names of these Avalokiteśvara emanations, consistent with the treatment of similar names in the preceding passage.
