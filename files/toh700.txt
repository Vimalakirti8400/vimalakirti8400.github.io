The Thought of the Lord of the World (Lokeśvarakalpa)

Toh 700 in the Kangyur, known in Sanskrit as the Lokeśvara-kalpa and in Tibetan as the Jikten Wangchuk gi Tokpa, meaning The Thought of the Lord of the World, is a ritual tantra centered on Avalokiteśvara, the bodhisattva of compassion venerated as the Lord of the World. The text describes itself as a great tantra of all the buddhas, promising the fulfillment of every wish, and its opening instructions — on purifying a meditation seat, forming the lotus mudrā, and contemplating emptiness — are characteristic of the liturgical and sādhana literature of the tantric canon. It was translated into Tibetan by the monk Yeshe De and revised in accordance with the standardized terminology established during the great translation reforms. Sitting among the tantric works of the Kangyur, this text belongs to the broad category of Buddhist tantra concerned with deity yoga and mantra practice focused on a principal bodhisattva figure.

Homage to the Blessed One, the noble Avalokiteśvara, Lord of the World. Homage to the Buddha. Homage to the Dharma. Homage to the Saṅgha.

The thought of the Protector of the World
shall here be briefly set forth:
the great tantra of all the buddhas,
which bestows the fulfillment of every wish.

First, the one who holds the vidyā mantra should sit in a clean and secluded place that has been purified by the syllable HŪṂ, and, having formed the lotus mudrā, should recite: "Oṃ śūnyatā-jñāna-vajra-svabhāva-ātmako'ham" — and then contemplate all things as having the aspect of emptiness.

Following that, recite: "Oṃ citta-pratibodhāṃ karomi." Having recited this and then visualized a varicolored lotus with eight petals, one should imagine a moon disc upon the lotus. Above the moon disc, from the mantra-syllable HRĪḤ, one should visualize oneself as Avalokiteśvara, Lord of the World: seated in the posture of a bodhisattva, with two arms, reddish in color, at his side embracing a white-robed consort, holding an open red lotus in the dancing manner, the needle-mudrā displaying the opening. One should then recite the following:

Just as the red lotus
is unstained by the fault of attachment,
so too the faults of varied habitual tendencies
are thoroughly purified within the three realms.

In the crown ornament, one should visualize Amitābha. Then recite: "Oṃ sarva-tathāgata-kāya-vāk-citta-vajra-svabhāva-ātmako'ham."

Having thus visualized, one should then contemplate the goddess-consorts in the eight directions.

Whoever cultivates this visualization and familiarizes themselves with meditative absorption will attain stable spiritual accomplishment. That very attainment will bestow buddhahood itself and all desired enjoyments.

The perfections of the buddhas will be accomplished,
as will dhāraṇīs and the bodhisattva grounds.
It demonstrates happiness to all the world,
each individually achieving their own realization.
Buddhahood itself is the state of the bodhisattva,
and so too is the state of the solitary buddha.
The delightful rank of buddhahood
will be attained without arduous striving.

Bodhicitta combined with camphor,
blended well with red sandalwood,
and properly joined with vajra-water —
this is the essential extract of all the buddhas.
Through this excellent practice,
the extract is obtained with ease,
under the protection of the Lord of the World:
ever youthful, free from illness, in good comfort,
with life, with strength, with diligence,

all of this is accomplished in this very life.
Without remainder, without exception, all is achieved:
subjugation, magnetizing, separation,
slaying, banishment, and the like,
the application of a spike and peg,

One will attain lordship over the ten grounds
and will abide in meditative absorption.
Therefore, through the vajra-samaya of one who possesses meditative absorption,
together with the highest vidyā,
one will be fully accomplished as the Protector of the World.

Oṃ hrīḥ padma narte śvara hūṃ.

If this root mantra is recited one hundred and eight times, the non-virtues committed during the day and night will be purified. If five hundred recitations are performed, one will be freed from all illness. If one thousand recitations are performed, untimely death will not occur. If two thousand recitations are performed, one will become worthy of the offerings of all. If three thousand recitations are performed, good fortune will arise and prosperity will increase greatly. If four thousand recitations are performed, one will be free from poverty. If five thousand recitations are performed, the hearts of all enemies will be thrown into turmoil. If six thousand recitations are performed, all enemies will be eliminated. If seven thousand recitations are performed, one will be supremely victorious over the four māras. If eight thousand recitations are performed, one will become an accomplished vidyādhara. If nine thousand recitations are performed, one will achieve accomplishment of the yakṣinī. If ten thousand recitations are performed, accomplishment of the daughters of gods, nāgas, and humans will be attained. If twenty thousand recitations are performed, the yoginīs who travel through the intermediate sky will be accomplished. If thirty thousand recitations are performed, all aspirations will be fulfilled. If forty thousand recitations are performed, one will attain the vidyā of moving through space. If fifty thousand recitations are performed, one will attain the vidyā of the sword. If sixty thousand recitations are performed, one will become an accomplished vidyādhara. If seventy thousand recitations are performed, one will become master of the three. If eighty thousand recitations are performed, one will become a wheel-turning king. If one hundred thousand recitations are performed, one will become a bodhisattva. If one million recitations are performed, one will take whatever form one wishes. If one million four hundred thousand recitations are performed, one will become a buddha. If ten million recitations are performed, one will be accomplished as one like the noble Avalokiteśvara, possessed of the power to take all under one's care everywhere, and body, speech, and mind will also be blessed.

This was spoken by the Blessed One with his vajra speech.

Oṃ paṇḍari vāsinī varite hūṃ.

This is the mantra of Paṇḍaravāsinī. Recited even once, it burns away the entire mass of sins of all classes. If recited aloud before a king together with his ministers within the span of seven nights, the assembled people of all destinies and the assembled queens will be brought under one's sway. By mere recitation one will come to possess the fortune of all excellence and perfection. Following this graduated sequence, if one hundred thousand recitations of this mantra are performed, one will be equal in power to all buddhas and bodhisattvas, and the noble Avalokiteśvara himself will be directly accomplished.

Oṃ vilokini avalokāya svāhā.

The application of this mantra: if ten thousand recitations are performed, the accomplishment of clearly inducing the prā vision in a mirror, on the thumbnail, in the sky, and in similar surfaces will be achieved, revealing what has occurred in the past, what is occurring in the present, and what will come in the future. Alternatively, if a tooth-cleaning stick is praised and consecrated twenty-one times —

— and if one lies down in silence at night having first used the tooth-cleaning stick without speaking, then whatever one desires will be shown in a dream, and it will be shown without error.

Oṃ tāre tuttāre ture svāhā.

The Blessed One Tārā: if this mantra is recited one hundred thousand times, the three worlds will be accomplished. Among daughters of gods, daughters of nāgas, daughters of kinnaras (half-human celestials), sons of yakṣas, and daughters of humans — whatever one desires among these — one should visualize the source of phenomena (dharmodaya) as follows: picture it painted in detail, marked with saffron, red lac, self-arisen flowers, and bodhicitta, as a deep-red six-spoked wheel, exceedingly sharp, spinning. The practitioner should visualize this source of phenomena as situated in a secret place — a mountain ravine or riverbank. It should further be meditated upon as swaying and falling upon the practitioner. One should draw it in and consider that through it wealth and prosperity have come under one's power.

Alternatively — whether a god, a nāga, an asura, a kinnara, a vidyādhara (one who holds a knowledge mantra), a yakṣa, a wheel-turning monarch, a minister, a person, or anyone else one desires — one should visualize that being very clearly in red, and vividly picture a vajra iron hook blazing with light piercing their heart with a churning hook, and a noose binding them by the neck and drawing them in, utterly intoxicated with desire. Thereby one will draw in and bring under one's power wealth along with oneself.

Oṃ bhūrini mahācakravartini hūṃ.

The application of this mantra: make a maṇḍala with great fragrant water, vajra water, and so forth, and offer flowers of whatever kind are available as a complete offering. The practitioner dwelling there should recite while visualizing being the sovereign of an island, or another island, or a city, a town, or a district. Through this one will become the master thereof, and having enjoyed all its wealth and pleasures, one will attain birth in the world-realm of Sukhāvatī.

Oṃ bhṛkuṭī tāre tutama ta hūṃ.

This goddess called Bhṛkuṭī (the Frowning One): take dust from an enemy's footprint; blend it with poison, salt, kēśa plant, blood, soil from both banks of a river, and great fragrant vajra water; make an effigy from this; pierce all the joints and vital points with a peg made from human bone; place it inside two skulls sealed face to face; and burn it in a cremation-ground fire. If the rite is performed for seven days the enemy will die. If however the practitioner's own mind is free of evil, then in twenty-one days even a buddha would die.

Oṃ mahāśrīye maṇipadme hūṃ.

If this goddess is individually venerated with fragrance, flowers, lamps, and alms food, and the mantra is recited, one will be protected in all circumstances. One will triumph over local Dharma disputes. One will become equal to Avalokiteśvara. One will become the master of great wealth. Alternatively, if a fire-offering of ten thousand lotus flowers is performed, one will become a king.

Oṃ vajrapadmeśvarī mara padme hūṃ.

This Vajra-Lotus Sovereign Lady: if recited mentally, she performs subjugation, magnetizing, and so forth. If praised seven times and one's topknot is knotted, one will be self-protected, and epidemic disease, hidden illness, all illness, weapons, fire, thieves, lions, elephants, snakes, imprisonment, the ocean, and the cannibal disease — all of these will be pacified by one thousand recitations. Through the activity of a single recitation, the spirit of forgetfulness, the spirit of wasting away, vomiting, difficulty in excretion —

all malicious sentient beings who are difficult to tame will be defeated. Through one hundred thousand recitations, the body flourishes and good fortune arises; the body becomes youthful and free from illness, and untimely death is averted. Alternatively, one may draw this in a nāga hearth-pit and there perform a fire offering with poison, long-pepper, and heating substances, combining them with the name of the nāga. If one hundred thousand such fire offerings are performed, great rains will fall and famine will be no more.

Oṃ viśvapadme hrīḥ. The Varied Lotus: this mantra dispels dullness, arrogance, oppression, and stabbing with a peg; it takes away the wealth of others, works with the peg, creates optical illusions and wonders, brings about invisibility, enables entry into hidden caverns, enables travel through the sky, cuts iron chains, and draws things together into a ball — whatever activities of this kind are taught in the tantras, and all activities that accord with other tantras as well, it performs. It bestows accomplishments (siddhis) of body, speech, and mind. The Blessed One, noble Avalokiteśvara, is accomplished, and all activities whatsoever that one desires will be achieved. This is what is said.

Oṃ viśvapadme hūṃ. The goddess called Varied Vajra bestows the accomplishment of speech. She makes one skilled in cutting through words, and makes the speech of others falter. She will protect oneself and will protect others as well. She brings good fortune to oneself and good fortune to others, accomplishing one's own benefit and the benefit of others. Just as noble Avalokiteśvara here delights and strives for the welfare of sentient beings, so too the Blessed One herself liberates sentient beings dwelling in the realms of hell-beings, hungry ghosts, and animals. To those who have faith in the teaching of the Sugata, she protects them as a mother. Thus spoke the Tathāgata, the vajra of mind.

In this way, the royal compendium of understanding has been fully taught.

One of stable mind, free of discursive thought,
endowed with intelligence, will attain accomplishment.
Just as all the buddhas have traversed
the path of conduct that is taught here,
by applying it in the same way in this very life,
the permanent state of liberation will be achieved.

Abiding in accord with whatever one desires,
as though fulfilling every enjoyment for all,
acting with whatever compassion one wishes,
through whatever conduct one desires —
whether seated or standing,
walking, or however it may be,
laughing, speaking —
whatever it is, exactly as it is, it is as it truly is.

Even one who has not entered the maṇḍala,
or who is laden with every obscuration,

who relies devotedly on the deity —

the state of being all the buddhas,

and even the state of Vajradhara.

when one enters into the gaṇacakra assembly,

Through this excellent preparation,

the perfections of the buddhas,

Through union with all the buddhas,
