The Dhāraṇī-Mantra That Dispels Phlegm Disorders

The Dhāraṇī-Mantra That Dispels Phlegm Disorders, known in Tibetan as Bad kan gyi nad sel ba'i gzungs sngags, is a very brief healing incantation addressed in homage to the noble bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara. It consists of a short Sanskrit mantra followed by a statement of its benefits: it is said to completely dispel indigestion, bloating, tumors, dropsy, the vomiting of blood, and the stabbing pains associated with disorders of phlegm, one of the three humors of traditional Indian and Tibetan medicine. The text reflects the widespread Buddhist practice of reciting protective and therapeutic formulas to remove illness and suffering. It is Toh 1057 in the Kangyur, where it belongs to the class of dhāraṇī texts gathered in the collection of incantations and spells.

The Dhāraṇī-Mantra That Dispels Phlegm Disorders

Homage to the noble Avalokiteśvara!

oṃ śānti śānti bhiti śānti sarva viti mula myags svāhā

This completely dispels indigestion, bloating, tumors, dropsy, vomiting of blood, and the stabbing pains of phlegm disorders.

The dhāraṇī that dispels phlegm disorders is complete.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

"Phlegm" renders the Tibetan medical term for the phlegm humor (Skt. kapha), one of the three humors. The list item rendered "dropsy" is literally "water," meaning a fluid or water disorder. It could also be read together with the next item as "vomiting of water and blood." The final syllable after "complete" is a scribal flourish of auspiciousness and is not translated.
