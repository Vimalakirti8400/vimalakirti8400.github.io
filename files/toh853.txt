The Noble Mahāyāna Sūtra Called "The Twelve Buddhas" (Ārya-dvādaśabuddhaka-nāma-mahāyāna-sūtra)

The Noble Mahāyāna Sūtra Called "The Twelve Buddhas" (Sanskrit: Āryadvādaśabuddhaka-nāma-mahāyāna-sūtra; Tibetan: 'Phags pa sangs rgyas bcu gnyis pa zhes bya ba theg pa chen po'i mdo) is a short Mahāyāna sūtra concerned with twelve buddhas and the benefits of hearing and remembering their names. It is set at Rājagṛha on Vulture Peak, where the Buddha addresses the bodhisattva Maitreya and begins describing a buddha dwelling in a distant world-realm called Muktikā, Pearl-Possessing, to the east of our own. Texts of this kind belong to a familiar current of Mahāyāna literature in which the names and buddha-fields of particular buddhas are taught as objects of devotion, recollection, and protection. In the canon it is Toh 853 in the Kangyur, the collection of translated words of the Buddha, where it is classed among the sūtras.

Thus did I hear at one time. The Blessed One was dwelling at Rājagṛha, on Vulture Peak Mountain, together with a great assembly of monks, twelve hundred and fifty monks, and likewise together with a great assembly of bodhisattvas, twenty thousand bodhisattvas, headed by the bodhisattva Aparājita and others.

Then the Blessed One addressed the bodhisattva, the great being Maitreya: "Maitreya, to the east of this buddha-field, beyond buddha-fields as numerous as the atoms in ten ineffable hundred-thousand-millions of buddha-fields, there is a world-realm called Muktikā, Pearl-Possessing. There dwells the Tathāgata, arhat, perfectly complete buddha, Gaganaśrī-vimala-rajohara-surucira-guṇaketu-prabha-puṣpa-padma-vaiḍūrya-raśmi-ratnarūpa, whose body is like a jewel and who is honored with the finest incense. His body is exquisitely adorned and beautified, and from the crown of his head, the uṣṇīṣa, countless rays like the sun's pour forth. He is adorned with the moonlight of his aspirations and with vast, magnificent emanations. Manifestly arisen from the dharmadhātu, he is the noble one called Unattached King of Jewels. Even now he abides there, living and sustained, and he teaches the Dharma.

Whoever holds the name of that Victor will turn back from saṃsāra for as many eons as there are atoms in the great continent of Jambudvīpa, over great eons.

That Victor prophesied about the bodhisattva, the great being called Peerless: Immediately after that Tathāgata, arhat, perfectly complete buddha, the one described above, passes into nirvāṇa and his sacred Dharma has disappeared, Peerless will manifestly and fully awaken to unsurpassed, perfect, complete awakening. He will appear in the world as a Tathāgata, arhat, perfectly complete buddha, endowed with knowledge and conduct, Sugata, knower of the world, unsurpassed guide of beings to be tamed, teacher of gods and humans, a buddha, a Blessed One, called Jaladeva-kumāra-sūrya-pradīpa-candra-puṣpa-padma-suvarṇa-darśana-gaganasadṛśakāya, Vairocana, Raśmivyūha, Asaṅgacakra-maṇḍala, he who radiates light in the ten directions, Sarvalokadhātu-avabhāsakara-ketu-dhvaja-rāja.

One should also hold the name of the Tathāgata, arhat, perfectly complete buddha of that same eastern direction, Sarvavyūha-vimala-prabha, Light of All Adornments, Free from Stain. One should recite the words. One should pay homage.

One should also hold the name of the Tathāgata, arhat, perfectly complete buddha of the southern direction, Pratibhānavyūha-adhyāśaya, He Who Contemplates the Adornment of Eloquence. One should reflect on it.

Maitreya, if a son of good family who has faith, or a daughter of good family, recites the names of these twelve Blessed Ones, Tathāgatas, arhats, perfectly complete buddhas, then for ten days one should confess the faults of all one's misdeeds. One should rejoice in all roots of virtue. One should entreat all the buddhas to teach, and request all the buddhas to remain. And one should dedicate all roots of virtue by dedicating them to the dharmadhātu. By doing so, all misdeeds will be exhausted, and all karmic obscurations will be completely purified.

Thereafter one will also attain the perfection of the qualities and adornments of a buddha-field, the perfection of fearlessness, the perfection of the marks, the perfection of the community of bodhisattvas, and the perfection of dhāraṇīs. One will also attain the perfection of meditative absorptions. According to one's wish, one will swiftly gain the perfection of a completely pure buddha-field, and the perfection of virtuous spiritual friends. One will not fall away from unsurpassed, perfectly complete awakening, nor ever be wholly diminished in it. While wandering in saṃsāra, one's lifetimes will be altogether beautiful, and one will gain perfection of wealth, perfection of lineage, perfection of birth, perfection of family, perfection of bodily form, and perfection of a retinue of like-minded people.
