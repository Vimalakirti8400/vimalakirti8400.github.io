This short Kangyur text has no formal title of its own and is known by its opening words, "Homage to the Buddhas, the Blessed Ones," in Tibetan Sangs rgyas bcom ldan 'das rnams la phyag 'tshal. It is a brief verse prayer of auspiciousness and protection. It first recalls the seven buddhas of the past, from Vipaśyin through Śikhin, Viśvabhū, Krakucchanda, Kanakamuni, and Kāśyapa to Śākyamuni, together with the trees under which each attained awakening. It then asks the gods who have faith in these buddhas to rejoice and to bring virtue and peace. The prayer goes on to wish well-being for two-footed and four-footed beings, for travelers setting out and returning, and for all by night and by day. It is Toh 1099 in the Kangyur, where it belongs among the short dhāraṇī texts and benedictory verses recited for blessing and protection.

Homage to the buddhas, the blessed ones!

The Victor Vipaśyin dwelt at the pāṭali tree;
The Victor Śikhin at the white lotus tree;
Viśvabhū dwelt at the foot of the sal tree;
The brahmin Krakucchanda at the śirīṣa tree;
The Buddha Kanakamuni at the udumbara tree;
Kāśyapa dwelt at the foot of the nyagrodha tree;
And the supreme Śākyamuni, having gone
To the excellent place, attained awakening: Gautama.

Whatever gods abide in deep faith
Toward those buddhas of great miraculous power,
May those gods be glad and delighted at heart,
And make all things always virtuous and peaceful!

Homage to the buddhas!
May you who are two-footed be well!
May you who are four-footed be well!
May those who have set out on the road be well!
May those who return be well as well!
Well by night, and well by day,
And well also at midday,
May you be well at all times!
May no evil befall them!

All days are auspicious,
All constellations are good,
All buddhas have great miraculous power,
All arhats are free of defilements.
By these words of truth, may all be well!

The aspiration and words of truth spoken in the Queen of Vidyā-mantras, the Great Peacock (Mahāmāyūrī), are complete.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The tree names follow the parallel list in the Mahāmāyūrī. The Tibetan word given for Vipaśyin's tree is unusual and is taken here as the pāṭali tree. The place where Śākyamuni attained awakening is literally "the special or excellent abode," most likely the seat of awakening under the bodhi tree.
