The Dhāraṇī That Dispels the Wasting Disease

The Mantra That Dispels the Wasting Disease, known in Tibetan as Kṣa ya'i nad sel ba'i sngags and closing under the name of a dhāraṇī for dispelling that illness, is a very brief healing text concerned with kṣaya, the consumptive wasting disease. After an homage to the Three Jewels, it gives a short mantra and a simple ritual: water is sprinkled onto a fan, the mantra is recited sixty-one times, and the sick person's body is then fanned so that they may be freed from illness. Its Sanskrit title is not separately preserved beyond the borrowed term kṣaya, so it is best identified by its descriptive Tibetan name. It is Toh 1058 in the Kangyur, where it belongs among the dhāraṇī texts, the collection of protective and healing spells within the tantra division.

Homage to the Three Jewels!

The mantra that dispels the wasting disease (kṣaya):

namo hitu heke svāhā

If illness arises in the body, and one is struck by the consumptive wasting disease, sprinkle water onto a fan. Recite the mantra sixty-one times, then fan the sick person's body with it, and they will become free of illness.

The dhāraṇī that dispels the wasting disease is complete.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

Kṣaya, transliterated in the Tibetan, is a wasting or consumptive disease. The text also calls it "khong skems," literally an "inner drying." The final syllable after "complete" in the colophon appears to be a scribal flourish and has not been translated.
