Praise of the Fifteen Goddesses of Nairātmyā (Nairātmyādevīpañcadaśastotra)

The Praise of the Fifteen Goddesses of Nairātmyā, in Sanskrit Nairātmyādevīpañcadaśastotra and in Tibetan Dakmema Lhamo Chongalla Töpa, is a short tantric hymn in verse attributed to the Indian master Ajitacandra. It belongs to the Hevajra cycle. It praises in turn each of the fifteen goddesses of the maṇḍala of Nairātmyā, "she who is selflessness": the central goddess and Vajrā, Gaurī, Vāriyoginī, Vajraḍākinī, Pukkasī, Śabarī, Caṇḍālī, Ḍombinī, Caurī, Vetālī, Ghasmarī, Khecarī and Bhūcarī. For each it names her seed syllable, her crowning buddha, and the doctrinal qualities she embodies. The hymn closes with a dedication that all beings may cross the ocean of saṃsāra and attain unsurpassed awakening. It is preserved as Toh 1307 in the Tengyur, among the commentaries on the tantras.

In the language of India: nairātmadepipañcadaśastotra. In the language of Tibet: Praise of the Fifteen Goddesses of Nairātmyā.

Homage to the Blessed Vajraḍākinī.

To tame those hard to tame, of many different inclinations,
from the dharmadhātu, unshakable and unceasing,
you display, through skillful means, emanations of many kinds:
homage to the one of wisdom and compassion.

Embodiment of the four wheels, born from ā,
terrifying form who overwhelms the four māras,
crowned with Akṣobhya, abiding in great bliss:
homage to the host of wisdom ḍākinīs.

Lovely Vajrā, born from ā,
ever delighted, gazing upon the khaṭvāṅga,
terrifying the wicked, wearing the diadem of Vairocana,
adorned with the ornaments of the charnel ground: homage to her.

Secret Gaurī, arising from ī,
of incomparable splendor, dancing, tasting the supreme flavor,
who has realized the ultimate, her crown ornament the jewel crest,
three-eyed, holding a fresh skull: homage to her.

Yoginī in the union of vowels and consonants,
ever delighting in bliss, abiding as the mind of awakening,
crowned with Amitābha, born from ī,
rejoicing in the dance: homage to her.

Vajraḍākinī, arising from u,
purifier of the three realms, Tārā, supreme in activity,
crowned with Amoghasiddhi, with eyes full of passion,
possessed of incomparable passion: homage to her.

Pukkasī, the unborn dharmadhātu, utterly pure,
crowned with Akṣobhya, who cleanses stains,
born from u, holding a garland of fifty,
who pacifies the world: homage to her.

Śabarī, unconditioned in the three times,
free of elaboration, stainless, with Vairocana upon her head,
born from ṛ, gazing with her dance into the ten directions,
like the moon in water: homage to you.

Caṇḍālī, who travels the sacred sites, the near sites and the rest,
who burns up all formations without remainder,
majestic with the jewel crown, born from ṝ,
who makes the dripping clear: homage to her.

From the very nature of non-abiding nirvāṇa,
Ḍombinī, the moving one, who gives rise to compassion,
the graceful one crowned with Amitābha, arising from ḷ:
homage to the body stainless as the sky.

Joining the speech of the signs, which rings out loudly,
ever delighting in rudhira, blood, and antra, entrails,
crowned with the Aversion Vajra, born from ḹ,
Gaurī, gazing into the ten directions: homage to her.

Caurī, who has become the mother who makes the nature of reality clear,
striving for the benefit of beings in nonconceptual equality,
her crown ornament the Delusion Vajra, sign of the unchanging,
generated from e: homage to her.

Vetālī, who brings the whole world under her power through passion,
who produces glory and splendor,
crowned with the Pride Vajra, born from ai:
homage to her who abides in non-abiding union.

Ghasmarī, endowed with wisdom as method,
born from that very reality, adorned with the Attachment Vajra,
arising from o, born from fierce activity:
homage to her who shows the unborn as birth.

Like an illusion, like a mirage, she moves in the sky;
mistress of the intermediate space, of the nature of Heruka,
her diadem Amitābha, born from au:
homage to her who knows and sees all.

Crowned with Vairocana, moving beneath the earth,
she who liberates the whole of the world below,
arising from aṃ, adorned with the terrors of the charnel ground:
homage to the compassionate one, skilled in means.

From the purity of vowels and consonants, of moon and sun in union, beneath the earth,
beautifully adorned with the five mudrās, the curved knife, the skull cup and the rest,
dancing in the half cross-legged posture, naked, three-eyed, wearing a skirt of tiger skin,
she who emanates the three channels in the body of the Great Seal: homage to her.

Arisen from great merit and wisdom, possessing the five bodies and the five wisdoms, you of terrifying form:
having seen, by the power of my faith, merely a fraction of the qualities of the cause, I praise you with the melody of faith.
By this, may those who dwell in the ocean of saṃsāra of the three realms, scattered in the ten directions across that ocean by the winds of conceptual thought,
abandon birth, aging, sickness and death, cross over the ocean of saṃsāra, and attain unsurpassed awakening.

Composed by the master Ajitacandra.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

Two goddesses here are called Gaurī: one among the four inner goddesses, born from i, and one at the eastern gate, born from ḹ. Both are spelled Gaurī in the Tibetan, as in Sanskrit. Pukkasī's seed is written u, the same as Vajraḍākinī's; the vowel sequence would lead one to expect a long ū, but the text is translated as it stands. The author's name renders Mi thub zla ba as Ajitacandra.
