The Glorious Tantra King of Exposition of the Four Seats, Called 'The Mantra Section' (Śicatuḥpīṭhākhyāta Tantra Rāja Mantrāṃśa Nāma)

Toh 429 in the Kangyur is a tantric text known in Sanskrit as the Śicatuḥpīṭhākhyāta Tantra Rāja Mantrāṃśa Nāma and in Tibetan as the Dpal gdan bzhi pa'i bshad pa'i rgyud kyi rgyal po sngags kyi cha, rendered in English as "The Glorious Tantra King of Exposition of the Four Seats, Called 'The Mantra Section.'" It belongs to the class of Buddhist tantric literature, specifically presenting itself as a mantra section — the third part of the first tantra — associated with the tradition of the Four Seats, a cluster of sacred sites and related esoteric teachings connected with the Yoginī or Highest Yoga Tantra corpus. The text opens with homage to the Blessed One as the Glorious Yoga of Space and to the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara, along with the vajra ḍākinīs, before proceeding in verse to expound on the secret and supremely condensed suchness of mantra practice. As Toh 429 in the Kangyur, it is preserved among the canonical tantric scriptures of the Tibetan Buddhist tradition.

In the language of India: Śicatuh Pīṭhākhyāta Tantra Rāja Mantrāṃśa Nāma. In the language of Tibet: The Glorious Tantra King of Exposition of the Four Seats, Called "The Mantra Section."

At the outset, endowed throughout

Dantī mixed with insects and goat's milk —

will certainly die within seven days.

Three Ku, seven insects,

The Ram's-path and related groupings,
combined with insects and whitened,

Kami, a group of insects,
combined with human butter-fat:

The seventh grouping, Cīte,
recited over an egg-containing gem,

unbearable sensation arises in the fool.

Sāyal and related groupings,
soaked for twenty days in goat's water:
whoever eats even a small amount

Dantī's boundary insects,
combined with butter and honey,

a a a mu mu mu svāhā.

This seed of the great mantra
is the mind of the buddhas in brief.
The practitioner of faultless union —
at the very end of the night,
at the time of the first red dawn,
with a vessel of water afterward,
two seers' weights of māgadhī,
the lunar maṇḍala visualized all around,
perfectly present in the nectar's center —
by the method of contemplating nectar,
the practitioner in deepest meditative equipoise:

having praised it seven times,
one then drinks that water —
all misdeeds are destroyed,
and wisdom-nectar, the wisdom body,
a supple mind and keen discernment,
vitality, splendor, and glory increase;
from every direction, fortune, purity,
learning and wisdom, wealth,
and ailments of wind, bile, poison, and phlegm,
chest disease, lung complaints and the like, with their stabbing pains,
pain in the eyes and limbs —
all sickness is driven away by this water.

Oṃ ra ta a bṛta ni, sarva phā phaṃ ha rantu maṃ hūṃ,
bi kṛta krodha hūṃ phaṭ svāhā.

This mantra of theirs is the great mantra,
the supreme of all protections.
Having been established in former times,
it guards the body of the yogin.

Praised seven times,
and then three times at the five places,
contemplating the five buddhas,
one pours it upon the crown of the head —
kings, lions, enemies, and the like,
thieves, and fire and water,
wind, bile, and kindred sicknesses,
all terrors and fears it destroys.

Oṃ vi mo ha ni saṃ mo ha ni, sarva pri ye ti hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ svāhā.

This, it is taught, is the great mantra
that confounds all beings;
it makes those who are unloved become loved,
and all become endeared.

If one rinses the mouth in the early morning
with water held in the left hand,
the yogin who desires accomplishment —
all aims and wishes will be fulfilled.

Then is taught the supreme practice:
from the essence, the highest essence.
Vajra and the rest are to be meditated upon,
merged without duality into evenness.
Through this limb, meditate on them as distinct.
Even what is without poison is pierced by poison.
By bewilderment utterly unbearable,
the yogin who is bewilderment itself is stricken with fear.
By effort, through the method of the Dharma,
the pure body is made wholly pure.
By gazing at the lamp of purity,
the darkness of afflictions and the rest is overcome.

Sky-vajra — into the vajra,
mind and mind itself fully dissolve.
Meditating on the singular and other things —
this is the defining characteristic of this yoga.

The residue of fire and so forth arises;
at center and within center it blazes.
With its light, the expanse of space —
one gazes in perfect meditative equipoise.

From the sprout of this seed,
the vowels and consonants emerge.
Seed after seed scatters and spreads,
residing perfectly in the expanse of space.

Dwelling at the center of the solar maṇḍala,
at the center of the Mother who rises from the dead — so contemplate.
Seated perfectly on a lotus throne,
covered above with a white parasol,
with two hands in the bodhisattva's cross-legged posture,
her complexion red, her radiance even,
adorned with hook, lasso, threatening finger,
and three eyes;
her gaze graced with an alluring expression,
draped in red garments —
upon her supreme mind and thought,
contemplate the syllables of nectar.
At the tip of the hook, the syllable kṣuṃ:
reciting, one draws her in with it.
Her reflection stirs the mind,
and with one's own breath one delights her.

Oṃ rāga ta rāga ta bha ti hūṃ kṣuṃ svāhā.

With the mantra and mudrā of the hook,
the yogin draws whomever is desired.
The greatly bewildered, the utterly bewildered —
placing them in equanimity, one accomplishes fully.

From the tantra of the fourth seat,
the third of the seats of practice:
the essence of accomplishment, condensed —
here it is gathered together in brief.

Then, upon the wisdom body,
the yogin in deepest meditative equipoise,
through the intention of what is eaten and the act of eating,
should practice purification and realization.

One does not disparage actual things,
and one should not examine anything whatsoever.
High caste, low caste, and the rest —
equanimity is the fifth divinity.

Whoever — a creator and so forth —
one should not harm with the mind.

If one harbors the intention to harm,
the yogin's vow grows distant.
Whoever takes another's property —
whether great or small —
making it one's own:
the mind of theft must not arise.
To be far from the yogin's vow
is this alone.
Lies, harsh words, slander,
and idle speech — abandon all these far away.
Harming the mind of another
is itself to be far from the yogin's vow.
Through the supreme union of meaning and the seed-syllable āli,
this is the very heart of the Dharma of union.
Using confused and wrongly twisted speech and the like,
applying it in that way is itself degradation.

Through action well performed,
through a body that generates bliss,
taking on the happiness of sentient beings as one's own concern —
this is training well in the Great Vehicle.
If sentient beings are harmed or the like,
do not speak words of displeasure.
Do not engage in sinful action,
and do not disparage the sacred learning of the yogin.
The yogin is not one of sensual pleasure alone,
and must not become intoxicated with delusion.
Through the supreme union of binding the mudrā,
let it be attended to according to one's own wish.
Through the union of attending and being attended,
let the deity be satisfied and accomplishment be achieved.
By the very nature of the welfare of sentient beings,
one brings benefit through the happiness of sentient beings.
The great fruit of the Great Vehicle —
through this, it is to be attended to by the path.
In whatever and wherever one enters into union,
there and thereby one brings happiness through benefit.
The minds of sentient beings who hold vows and so on —
do not harm those minds.

At the time of birth in cyclic existence,
endowed with genuine skillful means and wisdom,
turning the existent and the non-existent
and the welfare of sentient beings into āli —
the seal endowed with the four mudrās,
bound with the secret of secrets:
within that very center, the nectar
arises and a sprout comes forth.
The supreme āli, endowed with everything
including the three petals, is without boundary.
Furthermore, the very supreme nectar
is the essential core of all that is outer.
The nectar is delightful, is fortunate,
and is praised sweetly to sentient beings.
The body of all five buddhas,
refined and blazing like gold.
A crown is bound and uplifted,
adorned completely with four hands,
with an expression of laughter and coquetry,
and ornamented with all ornaments.
In the upper right hand, a vajra hook,
and the second, a vajra lasso;
the left hand, a vajra bell,
and the third, binding with an iron chain —
the mudrā of these four hands,
binding with the secret and so forth,
the body and its color blazing:
one rests in equanimity as Amitābha, the boundless lord.

The seed of the form of ha
causes joy in the supreme syllable.
Through the form of ho, body upon body —
the deity is Vairocana, sovereign lord.
Through meditating on mind and mental factors,
one is fully renowned as the deity itself.
All bodies and embodied beings —
it is the seizing of life and mind.

The seed of the form of hrīḥ
is Vajrapāṇi, the greatly powerful.
From the form of oṃ, radiating with clarity,
it is that which realizes the minds of all.
The form of a satisfies by its very nature;
let it be fully attended to by one who possesses intelligence.
The goddess of the water of nectar —
this is the realization of what is to be known thereby.
Through the one by whom the nectar is tasted,
the fruit of the nectar is obtained.
The goddess who delights the Tathāgata
is supremely beautiful, the most attractive of forms.
Through the aspect of all the buddhas,
let the fourth mudrā be bound.

If the yogin wishes to accomplish,
let the two be meditated upon as non-dual.
The two, through the existent and the non-existent,
are made fully clear by one endowed with gnosis.
Bound by great pride,
bound together with a mind of conceptual elaboration,
through the manner of coquetry and wrath —
one dissolves into the very essence of the goddess.
On the seat of the hand of the sun,
touched by the hand of the moon,
having embraced and through that embrace,
one dissolves into the very gnosis of the goddess.
The deity of fire, the gnosis of the belly —

One should be satisfied in that abode.
Through wisdom dissolving into method,
satisfaction itself through non-dual union.

The seed of the great seed —
by this let the seed be contemplated.
The offering through this sequence
is not for the purpose of medicine.

The goddess seizes hunger and suffering,
speaks with coquetry and sweetness,
makes pride without [ordinary] pride,
and has diligence without [ordinary] diligence.
The fortunate goddess, from all sides,
bewilders, strikes with fear, and draws one in.
The unsettled mind and shame's fear —
the goddess destroys with pleasant words.
Thus endowed with qualities, the goddess,
delightful and sweet as nectar,
abides at the station of desire and liberation's rank.
Whoever follows the path of this goddess:
the water-mixture, the cuckoo's call,
coquetry and dance — these are the five she possesses.
The sixth is the diligence of the asuras (demigods),
and the seventh is she who causes mindfulness to fade.
The work of the eight messenger goddesses
is to swiftly steal away the mind.
For the nature-mind of the yogin,
she produces the form of a mind of desire.
With the characteristics as taught,
with effort let variegated thangkas be painted.

the eight messengers all around —
in the form of women, each in her manner,
colored with the color of her direction.

With the pledge mantra and mudrā,

Oṃ amṛtakuṇḍali ha ho hrīḥ a svāhā.
This is the mantra and mudrā of close engagement.

Upon the seat of the hand of the sun,
fully joining the hand of the moon,
with the heart-essence (dhāraṇī) and mudrā,
one performs according to one's own wish.
The yogin having recited one hundred thousand times,
should first perform the approach and the rest.
Afterwards, activities such as those of fire,
through the activity of the non-dual torma.

In a three-sided fire-hearth,
one should kindle the illuminating wood;
flowers fully bloomed from gods and humans,
anointed and combined with honey,
mixed thoroughly with nectar-water —
holding firmly the name of whoever it may be:
if one burns eight hundred times,
that one will certainly come under one's power.

If the ashes of the fire deity and so forth
are mixed five times with one's own seminal fluid
and offered in the midst of the nectar,
it brings under one's power whomever one desires.

The fifth, the maddening serpent,
and the wood of the ujā tree in equal measure —
if these are offered infused with nectar,
one quickly causes madness.

With the tips of neem and so forth,
the skilled one should perform the accomplishment.
With flowers, incense, fragrance and so forth,

from the root of the white argha plant

Fine lotus powder,
anointed with milk and with nectar —
after being burned eight hundred times,
the remaining ash is applied.

Washed seven times with the root-water of citraka
and dried in shadow —
at the time of the offering, the yogin,
applying that ash swiftly with nectar-water
in the snow-white kunda milk:
the body takes on a form of bewilderment,
becomes thoroughly confused, and carries the scent of confusion.
The goddess of the eight messengers
is then devoid of power and majesty.
For sentient beings confused in conceptual thought,
one engages them through this activity.

The yogin who has abandoned bewilderment
swiftly comes to comprehend all beings.
Taking the ash of this substance,
discard three parts in jātī oil.
At the time of the nectar-offering,
mix it together with the ash,
and by licking a drop of it with the tongue,
one comes to be held by the eight messengers.
The fully complete characteristics of the messenger:
she is wild, proud, and arrogant.

Take the wood that causes wildness,
and with effort kindle fire vigorously.
With flowers, honey, and nectar present,
burn it a hundred upon a hundred times.
Take that ash
and apply it in the preparation and so forth.
When that ash is applied
in the place where nectar is produced,
the color of the water changes
and its taste likewise changes.
With honey and milk present,
that form returns to its former state.

Take that ash
and mix it with three parts of the three bodies.
Seven parts of nectar
and eight parts of maruci —
at the time of offering, pour that ash
into the beer,
and whoever is merely given it
knows of their own accord what they desire from the nectar.
Whatever bewilderments belong to whomever,
let those be cleared through that method.
For the sake of clearing the bewilderment of sentient beings,
always teach the path.
Strive for the benefit of others —
this concludes one's own aims.

The applications of medicine and mantra
are lesser for those of lesser wisdom and so forth.
From the tantra of the Fourth Seat,
the third, the rite of torma —
the essence of accomplishment (siddhi) has been extracted:
this is its summary of substance.

For the purpose of accomplishing mantra,
an image is to be fashioned and employed.
By employing the supreme image,
one accomplishes it step by step through blessing.
Pacification, enrichment, magnetization,
killing, expulsion, and separation —
all are accomplished through the image.
The image is to be made with its proper characteristics.

First, the characteristics of cloth;
second, the characteristics of palm-leaf;
third, the sequence of colors;
fourth, torma and fire-offering.
Having generated the basis and one's wish,
it possesses equal, unblemished edges.
Top, bottom, and so forth are fully extensive —
on the cloth, draw the image for pacification.

A pretas's garment that has not fallen
and has not deteriorated — with blemished edges removed —
the portion untouched by dye:
that is the characteristic of enrichment.

A red cloth of a female preta,
employed without falling into ruin,
prepared untouched by dye:
this is universally known as magnetization.

Black goat hide,
and other substances such as ox and horse —
dark, fierce, and such:
that is the characteristic of the fierce activity.

In the place where it is pressed,
apply the characteristic lines.
Apportion it into three parts:
one part is to be discarded below,
two parts are placed above the fifth.
The third, the lines that are stated —
the Brahmā lines, the rod:
the fifth part is taken as seven.

The body is understood to be three parts in measure,
with limbs in peaceful proportion.
The face is twelve parts in measure.
The neck is four finger-widths.
The head and hair are the same measure.
The ear also has that measure.
The face is a third of that.
Forehead, side, and nose are equal.
The tip of the brow reaches to the top of the ear.
The eye is the measure of two finger-widths.
The socket is four finger-widths —
that is the characteristic of the eye.

From the middle of the edge of the neck,
the chest is defined by two presses.
At the place of the heart and at the navel,
there is a press, and a full press.
At the end of the breast is a press.
Two breasts pressed upon the chest,
and pressing with both hands —
both hands pressing fully.
Possessing four finger-widths,
the thigh and shin are uniformly defined.
For both knees, four finger-widths;
the measure of the foot is likewise the same.

Twelve tālas is the measure
for painting the form of a god in a diagram.
The remaining four finger-widths
serve as the seat for the eight gods.
The seat of the ninth
is said to be two finger-widths.
The maṇḍala of the central moon-disc,
together with the radiance of the garment all around,

the measure of twelve tālas:
the lower portions, the sections, and the like,

For the upper remaining portion,
the fifth family is to be shown.

one accomplishes the deity according to one's wish.
The full arrangement of the image

At this tāla measure,
both hands drawn in fully,
with two finger-widths set aside —
this is the form of the peaceful deity.

At that tāla, the face is rendered accordingly.
The belly region is large and fully so.
At every joint and joint-interval,
two finger-widths are to be removed.

Wrathful males, wrathful females, powerful goddesses,
male servants, female servants dispatched —

these are painted at ten tālas.

For one who wishes to accomplish the maṇḍala,
fully endowed with goddesses of offering —
an eighth portion of cloth

The sequence of the maṇḍala goddesses
is to be arranged in order, each in its place.
At the center, the lord vajra deity —
paint the defining marks of the principal figure distinctly.

If the neck, the knees, and the feet
are the three that are long, it is a fault.
The mantra practitioner will assuredly
be driven out, and his abode destroyed.

If the shoulders, the ears, and the fingers —
those three being short — the fault is this:
siddhis will assuredly be destroyed,
and that one's glory will be far removed.

If the cheeks, the feet, and the hips
are the three that are thick, the fault is great:
the accomplisher himself will assuredly
be afflicted by māras and obstructing spirits, and glory will recede.

If the cheeks, the chest, and the sides
are the three that are low, the fault is great:
wealth will quickly,
without long delay, be destroyed.

If the breasts, the ears, and the forehead —
those three are uneven, the fault is great:
strife and harm from enemies
will quickly befall the accomplisher.

If the chest, the body, and both thighs
are the three that are low, the fault is great:
one will be terrified by enemies,
and death too is beyond doubt.

A flattened nose, a crooked eye,
uneven ears, lips, and forehead —
one will be terrified by obstructing spirits, māras, and fire,
and attainments will be far removed.

A head that looks upward,
a seat that is uneven and irregular,
the chest, neck, and limbs —
these are said to carry faults without number.

Rays of light that are uneven and irregular,
mudrās and hand-emblems that are wrong —
afflicted by grief and sorrow,
the image will be a fault for the accomplisher.

If the backrest, the parasol, and the banner —
those three are askew, the fault is this:
death, decline and loss of glory,
and the destruction of friends and loved ones.

Thus, with great faults and flaws,

examine thoroughly and investigate carefully.

The Sanskrit title transliterated in the Tibetan opening corresponds to a Caturpīṭha tantra text, specifically a section called the "Mantra Portion" of the Tantra King of the Four Seats exposition. The Sanskrit reconstruction is approximate, as the Tibetan phonetic transcription is irregular.

The term "neuter" (ma ning) throughout this passage is a technical tantric category referring to a class of being considered neither male nor female, used here in the context of seed-syllable generation and mantra practice. It should not be taken in a purely grammatical sense.

The term "bindu" (Tibetan bandu ka, here rendered as "bindu") is left in Sanskrit as it is a technical term from tantric physiology and mantra practice referring to the luminous drop or point of concentrated essence.

The passage as a whole is highly technical tantric instruction concerning seed-syllables, their colors, their placement, and their correspondences with the four activities of pacifying, enriching, magnetizing, and wrathful action. Much of the syntax is intentionally condensed and esoteric, and any complete interpretive rendering would require access to an authorized commentary. The translation follows the literal structure as closely as possible.  The passage belongs to a tantric ritual-technical text describing the arrangement and application of seed-syllables, particularly the syllable Hūṃ and related mantra components, for the five types of ritual action: peaceful, enriching, magnetizing, wrathful or fierce, and destructive. Several terms are highly technical and their translation draws on standard Vajrayāna ritual categories rather than on unambiguous grammatical parsing alone.

The compound "སྦོམ་པོ་མ་ནིང་" rendered as "the stout one and the neuter" refers to a phonological classification of Sanskrit syllables (heavy syllable and neuter or indeterminate syllable), a usage attested in Tibetan grammatical and tantric phonological literature.

"རེ་ཕ" is a Tibetan grammatical term for the ra-vowel-sign or subscript ra, used in the placement of mantra letters; it is rendered descriptively as "ra-vowel-sign" throughout.

"སྦྲིད་པ" rendered as "intoxication" carries the sense of a bewildering, stupefying, or fascination-inducing ritual action, one of the subsidiary wrathful rites; this is the most consistent rendering with the Tibetan ritual context.  This passage is a highly technical tantric instruction on the arrangement, coloring, and application of seed-syllables and fire-offering letters for specific ritual actions, namely protection, enrichment, attraction, and wrathful practices including slaying and separation. The terminology is specialized and the syntax elliptical throughout; the translation follows the syntactic units as faithfully as possible while rendering them in readable English.

The term rendered "lung position" for གློ་རུ is literally "rib" or "side," referring to a positional placement of a letter in the syllabic arrangement; this is a technical convention of Tibetan syllabic writing used in mantra composition.

The sequence "U, la, pa, li, ta" renders the syllables ཨུ་ལ་པ་ལི་ཏ as individual seed-syllable components rather than a single word, following the tantric reading convention.

Several key interpretive choices follow.

"Repha" is the superscript vowel-carrier stroke used in Sanskrit syllabic notation; it is preserved as a technical term here, consistent with the preceding passage.

"Kunda" is a white-flowering jasmine, Jasminum sambac, used throughout Indo-Tibetan texts as a simile for whiteness; it is retained untranslated as a recognized botanical reference.
