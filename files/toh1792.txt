A Commentary Collecting the Difficult Points of the Illuminating Lamp (Pradīpoddyotanaviṣamapañjikā)

A Commentary Collecting the Difficult Points of the Illuminating Lamp, known in Sanskrit as the Pradīpoddyotana Viṣamapañjikā and in Tibetan as Drönma Selwar Jepé Kawa Tüpé Drelwa, is Toh 1792 in the Tengyur, where it belongs among the commentaries on the tantras. It is a short gloss on difficult words and passages in the Illuminating Lamp (Pradīpoddyotana), the celebrated commentary on the Guhyasamāja Tantra, and it moves through that work chapter by chapter, explaining terms, mantras, coded expressions and ritual instructions. Its notes touch on the generation and completion stages, visualization of the deities, empowerment of disciples, rites of protection and subjugation, and the yogas of the inner channels and luminosity. The colophon names the master Bhavya as its author and records that it was translated into Tibetan by an Indian preceptor together with the Tibetan translator, the monk Pal Shakya Tsöndrü.

In the language of India: pradepodyotanabisamapañcikānāma. In the language of Tibet: A Commentary Collecting the Difficult Points of the Illuminating Lamp.

Homage to the Buddha.

Here, "the two stages" are the two, the generation stage and the completion stage. "As long as" refers to the ka groups, together with ḍhad dha ya ra la, which are read twice. As for "itha," its meaning should be understood as the a groups. That itself, the holding of body and so forth, is to be grasped as having the nature of wisdom and method.

"Seen as possessing the vows of the Vinaya" means that those to be trained see him as one who holds the vows of a monk and so forth. "By those who desire the fruit" refers to the purpose of the people to be trained; it is a conventional fruit. "This hidden essence is present": by fully knowing the nature of attachment and the rest just as it is, one becomes liberated, and to teach this it says, "He fully teaches the Dharma of attachment."

"ma" means obtaining very many dharmas. "Especially" means it is to be held especially. "Like cotton seeds in a pot" means: just as, when one takes a small pot filled with cotton seeds and they swell greatly, they cannot come out again, so it cannot be taught clearly. "To those whose minds do not open" means those who are empty at heart of steadfast wisdom and so forth. "Listen" means stainless hearing.

"The one tantra": the reality of the Dharma is to be explained by way of union; there is the explanation for beginners and the explanation of the yoga free of elaboration. "Two kinds of explanation are to be given" means the explanation to the assembly and the explanation to the disciple. "By these" means that by the six ornaments the words are determined in terms of the two truths. That is the seventh ornament; by it the ultimate union is accomplished.

With "Thus have I" and so forth, the setting is shown. "Stage" here means the five stages. The four modes of explanation are the literal meaning, the general meaning, the hidden meaning, and the ultimate meaning.

One should give up the conduct of the ten, because it does not bring about bliss as it truly is, and because the ten non-virtues, the cause of suffering in the world, make one fall. Becoming wholly unattached to them, and having destroyed the ten non-virtues, one takes a genuine birth.

"The second part" is the long letter āḥ. "Expressed by the ninth" is the letter o. "And" is the contraction: the letters aṃ and āḥ. "The thirtieth and the tenth" is the letter ha. "The seed of the fifth" is the letter u. When the second part is made the seed of the fifth and the sixth, and is separated, it becomes that.

"Spread within" means fully unfolded within the source of dharmas. "e is wisdom" means the consciousness of mind and appearance. The increase of appearance itself is the great awareness. "Root" is the cause of the near-attainment of appearance; it is the initial time. These too, when reversed again and applied to the two results of appearance just as they are, the second consciousness is to be seen.

"It will always abide in going" means the life force always abides. Form and water; and from form, feeling. What possesses the aggregate of feeling is wind.

"By playing" means destroying. "Plays variously" means one should emanate. "Magically displays" means emanating. "The three vajras, the supreme samaya" means the bhaga. From "wisdom, all the Tathāgatas" up to "the pure heart of beings" are the distinctive qualities of the bhaga. "This very expanse of space" is the source of dharmas: the bhaga of wisdom, which has become the ground in which natures abide as without intrinsic nature.

In the passage "All sentient beings, entered by the presence of glorious Vajrasattva, attained the state of Vajrasattva itself," "all sentient beings" are all the bodhisattvas. "Glorious Vajrasattva is present" means by bestowing the wisdom of empowerment. "Entered" means that, by entering the nondual mind, they came to obtain bliss and mental happiness. "Because the wisdom of method arose" means because the wisdom of appearance arose.

"The culmination of the three beings" is the culmination of the pledge being, the wisdom being, and the samādhi being. "The five afflictions" are attachment, aversion, delusion, pride, and jealousy. The vajra-like samādhi is union. The subtle element is the element of wind. Drawing forth by it the indestructible drop of the wisdom of method is the first setting. Renouncing, leaving from among his queens and retinue, is the second setting. Going forth is the third setting. Entering upon the vajra seat with the vajra-like samādhi is the fourth setting. These are the divisions of the setting.

"Holding the vajra and so forth" refers to fully turning the wheel of Dharma. With "He dwelt in the bhaga" and so forth: first, the retinue, the forms and so forth of the outer maṇḍala, is drawn fully into his own body and dissolves into the eyes and the other senses; then, from abiding in the enjoyment body, the awakening mind becomes the nature of supreme joy and emerges in the birthplace of the mother. That is the meaning of "He dwelt in the bhaga."

Then comes the oblong stage of the embryo, and then the gelatinous stage and so forth. Then, in sequence through the ten months and so on, all the faculties, the eye and the rest, become fully complete. Then, going outside through the special door of the birthplace, emanating as the forms and so forth of object and subject is what becomes the excellent retinue.

The meaning of what is said in the passage beginning "Namely" is this: dwelling together with consorts is the pledge seal. The letters of the mantra are the Dharma seal. Possessing all the marks is the great seal. Acting for the benefit of beings by means of it is the action seal.

At the time of teaching the Dharma, the one endowed with those seals, through the sequence of the two-armed form and so forth, called to mind the protection wheel before him. Then, having become the embodiment of the thirty-two deities, endowed with an excellent retinue, he taught samādhi to trainees given to attachment by way of making offerings to excellent women. To show the tiered celestial palace, the abode of those deities with their respective seats, "Then" and so forth was spoken.

Then the maṇḍala deities enter into the nature of the aggregates and so forth at his heart and other places, and abide as luminosity; this is blessed with the mantra oṃ śunyatā. Immediately after that, there are the sun, the moon, the lotus, the three syllables, and the moon into which these melt, into which sentient beings enter; then, through the sequence of oṃ dharmmā dhātu svabhāva and so forth, one should contemplate up to the culmination of the three beings of Vajradhara.

Then, with "The expanse of space is all" and so forth, the lotus of wisdom endowed with the five families is filled by the emanation of the awakening mind. "All sentient beings" are the buddhas and bodhisattvas: the maṇḍala deities of Vajradhara came to obtain joy and mental happiness. With "Then" and so forth: having been sent forth and then entering again, they come to appear in the form of Vajradhara, with three faces and the like. Then, knowing the fortunes of sentient beings just as they are, he teaches according to the sequence of teaching as it has been explained. With "Then" and so forth: since disciples supplicate the guru, receive the training, and the result is then certain, in order that they abide in that training, the Tathāgata Vajradhara is shown supplicating himself.

This concludes the explanation collecting the difficult points of the first chapter of the Illuminating Lamp.

As for what is called "the designation of cause and result": the awakening mind that is the indivisible own nature of body, speech, and mind is the cause, and its enjoyment of the supreme Vajradhara is the result. "From the life force" means consciousness mounted on wind, appearing like a dream. "The abiding of the three realms" means that the threefold realm itself abides as wind, like a dream; and this very "abiding" is birth into the three realms. "Supreme" means excellent; it resembles the characteristics of the intermediate state of existence. "As one" means that wind and consciousness are gathered into one. "Swiftly" means as mind alone. "Seeing" means seeing there. "Oneself" means one regards oneself as the yogin.

These very things are the state of self-blessing: it is taught that everything from form and so forth up to mind is blessed as the six tathāgatas, and so on. With "Stable and moving" and so forth, the approach of non-Buddhists (tīrthikas) and others who meditate on emptiness alone is refuted. That is, as in "There is no meditating on what is to be meditated," and so on. Through the very mode expressed as "Meditate on whatever exists," it is examined that meditations on existence and on nonexistence are not correct.

From "Above and below" and so forth, the meditation of the śrāvaka Vaibhāṣikas is refuted; as stated elsewhere, it is the position:

"Space and the two cessations,
The three times, permanent and unconditioned."

With "Whatever is cause and result" and so forth, the Sautrāntika meditation on general meanings is refuted. With "The aggregates and so forth" and so forth, the manner of meditation of the treatises of the proponents of mere consciousness is refuted. With "In order to reverse conventional truth" and so forth, meditation on what appears conventionally as being so is refuted. With "Like the two truths" and so forth, meditation that clings to ultimate truth and conventional truth is refuted. By the ultimate meaning it is shown that the meditation is unerring: it is being fully established in natural joy and in natural luminosity.

This concludes the explanation collecting the difficult points of the second chapter of the Illuminating Lamp.

"According to the places" means the places of the center, the east, and so on. "Marked by body, speech, and mind" means that the maṇḍala deities too are marked by body, Vajra Vairocana; by this nature, taken as principal, it should be understood for mind and speech as well. This is in order to show clearly that each one of the three vajras also contains the others within it. Where it is said,

"Vairocana, of great compassion,
Or the sign of body, speech, and mind,"

oṃ śūnyatā jñāna and so forth shows the six families, the three families, and finally their culmination in a single family. "Through the union of each four" and so forth refers to each maṇḍala having, in turn, the nature of four maṇḍalas. The nature of Pāṇḍaravāsinī and the others is the deity, that is, consciousness.

This concludes the explanation collecting the difficult points of the third chapter of the work called the Illuminating Lamp.

"Rite" and so forth are the twenty rites, such as the torma and the fire offering. "This is the supreme embodiment of summoning" means the embodiment of the five aggregates and the four elements; with such a measuring cord one should correctly view the places of the maṇḍalas of the body just as they are. "Of the five kinds" and so forth shows that Akṣobhya and the others, by nature the five wisdoms, are arranged at the center and the other places. "The offering of shapes," and "three kinds," mean through the sequence of entering, rising, and abiding. "Moment" and so forth means that the momentary time of the nature of the appearances is divided into sixteen emptinesses. "The Dharma of peace" is the very subtle element of sentient beings, which has been fully understood: consciousness by nature, the embodiment of great compassion, definitively purified as peace. "It is said to be the light of fine radiance" is said with reference to those very things. "Dependent connection" means things that arise in dependence. "Scent" is applied to the body.

This concludes the explanation collecting the difficult points of the fourth chapter of the Illuminating Lamp.

"That arose from calm abiding" means it arose from having become union. "Mother, sister, daughter" means women who wish to benefit one as a mother and a sister do. "The great goddess abiding in the heart" is the inner great seal. "Even if pleased" refers to the outer seal. "Abiding" refers to this: when the bodhisattvas who abide in the samādhi of cessation abide in that samādhi of cessation, then, since the light rays of their own subtle element touch consciousness, it becomes certain for those bodhisattvas, and they abide in union.

This concludes the explanation collecting the difficult points of the fifth chapter of the Illuminating Lamp.

"By teaching the mantra" means it was taught only in outline. Then what clarifies it was spoken: "the three vajras" and so forth. The three vajras are the three kinds of arrangement that bring delight by gathering into one's own body and by emanating into the bodies of others; by these the tathāgatas are marked. And these very ones are the tathāgatas. Concerning "oṃ" and so forth, at first there is a single body. The three qualities are the three tathāgatas, endowed nondually with the mind vajra. What is said in this way also belongs to the one who embodies the six families, and it is explained that it should be applied in that way. "Explained at the time" means the great Vajradhara. "From the single one having the nature of the lord" means having become a maṇḍala deity that has become the domain of what is born together with the mind. "Delighting the wheel of both" means the wheel embodying outer and inner. "The rite just explained" is the rite of blessing the body and so forth; having performed the passion and the rest of that very rite, one abides in the second, the completion stage. The four vajras are the fourfold yoga.

In "Fully undertake the subtle yoga" and so forth, yoga is luminosity. Immediately after that luminosity, one arises as the enjoyment body through the sequence of the lotus, the moon, the three syllables, and so on. "In the opening of one's own vajra" means one should meditate on the awakening mind in the opening of the jewel. The consort is the wisdom woman; within her lotus, at the tip of the nose, one imagines a drop, a vajra the size of a barley grain, and so forth. "Conduct of merely walking, lying down, and eating" means that, apart from merely eating food, merely lying down, and merely going to explain, one abides nowhere else, focused solely on samādhi.

"The secret of the genuine mantra" is the reality of the mantra just spoken. It is the examination of the secret of yogins. By the words "moment" and so forth, natural luminosity alone is shown. "The mantrin" is Vajrasattva.

This concludes the explanation collecting the difficult points of the sixth chapter of the Illuminating Lamp.

"The six limbs" means abiding in the objects of the mind as they are, through the generation stage and the completion stage as they have been explained. "Desire" is the aspect of grasping. "The eye and so forth" means: just as the consciousnesses of the eye and the rest take up their objects and, together with mental consciousness, make the supreme objects and the others into objects of mere bliss, so too the yogin should enjoy them without abandoning them at all. "Pleasing" means supreme. "Unpleasing" means inferior. "Indeterminate" means neither. "The wrathful one is the wisdom of method" means wisdom mingled with the subtle element. "Delighting in wind" refers to the letter hūṃ. "Those palate lotuses too should be joined" means together with the uvula of the upper palate.

"The discriminating awareness of Dharma, meaning, and confidence": Dharma and meaning are to be emanated by the intellect. The discriminating awareness of confidence is to be emanated by indicating the five families and so forth, each in its own sequence, as the hundred families. "Those possessing the eighty natures" means all of them in general, without exception. "In the present life" means when the Dharma is realized.

This concludes the explanation collecting the difficult points of the seventh chapter of the Illuminating Lamp.

"The twenty-five deities" are the twenty-five deities that are naturally pure. "Other than the lord" means one should also examine birth into another family.

This concludes the explanation collecting the difficult points of the eighth chapter of the Illuminating Lamp.

The four spaces are of the nature of the four emptinesses; or else, by way of the nature of the four elements and the four seals. "Otherwise" and so forth means that if one kills and so forth at will, other than for the purpose of taming sentient beings, one will certainly fall into the Avīci hell. "Then, in that very moment" means when appearance has become clear through the separation made before. "The measure of time" means intensely generating the intention at those times.

"Locanā and the others" means: having summoned whichever of the four one wishes, completed her with the seed of one's own family, endowed her with the wisdom being and the samādhi being, and filled her with the five families, one makes the maṇḍala deities enter in the form of women such as Pāṇḍaravāsinī; first engaging in passion, one then acts, through various joys, for the benefit of various trainees.

The name of the mantra is the three syllables and so forth. The meaning of the mantra is the expression of the mantra's meaning by the three syllables. Interspersing the mantra is interspersing the mantra with the purpose, such as pacifying, that is, fully joining it with svāhā and the like and fully reciting the mantras. "Vajra words free of sound" is vajra recitation. "Order" means the five stages. The power of body, speech, and mind is the nature of wisdom and gnosis. "The lord and the others, the three beings" refers to the maṇḍala deities who embody the pledge being, the wisdom being, and the samādhi being. "King Sumbharāja" means summoned and urged even by King Sumbharāja. In "This is mine," "this is" refers to the yogin.

"Our" means of sentient beings. "Father" means I, the yogin: one should vividly imagine oneself, together with one's assembly, cherishing the many kinds of assemblies of sentient beings. "If one asks what it is" and so forth shows that the two bodies of the deity are supreme and excellent.

This concludes the explanation collecting the difficult points of the tenth chapter of the work called the Illuminating Lamp.

"From the first chapter": beginning from "the awakening mind vajra Tathāgata" and so forth, the completion stage is taught. "Then, immediately after this was spoken," and so forth teaches the generation stage. "The three signs" refers to this: the sun, the moon, and the lotus having become one, one meditates at the heart on the wisdom being, with one face and two arms, holding a wheel and a bell, and at his heart on the letter oṃ. In the same way one should also meditate on the yoga of Amitābha and the others.

"The samādhi of single mindfulness" means that the yogin of Vairocana and the others, through the sequence of the protection wheel and so forth, imagines in a mere instant the thirty-two deities who have the nature of Vajradhara. Then, through the sequence of mantras such as oṃ śūnyatā, one should imagine Vairocana and the others like a wheel. Likewise, for the sequence of the bodhisattvas, one should meditate on drawing them forth and so on through the passion of the sādhana as well.

"Likewise, generate all" means generating the sun born from hūṃ, the lotus born from a, and the three syllables born from that. "With the aggregate of form and so forth" means that the bodhisattvas enter the aggregate of form and the rest, and their becoming luminosity is the offering. "First, the cavity of the eye" means that, having emptied the eyeball and so forth, one abides in luminosity and then should imagine Kṣitigarbha and the others, born from the letter thliṃ and the rest. "Vajra utpala": the secret vajra itself is expressed by the word utpala. "One should also generate them through the arrangement of the tathāgatas" means that the yogin of Vairocana should place Vairocana, or Kṣitigarbha, at the place of his own eyes. Likewise the yogins of Akṣobhya and the others, explained below, should also imagine Akṣobhya and the others at the ears and so forth. "The three bodies, oṃ" means taking up the accomplishment of lesser, middling, and supreme forms.

This concludes the explanation collecting the difficult points of the eleventh chapter of the Illuminating Lamp.

"Thus the path" means that the activities of the faculties and so forth are purified just as they are. "Of the two stages" refers to the conventional and the ultimate of the awakening mind vajra, that is, the yogin's generation stage and completion stage. Transformation into the great seal is its result: the mundane and supramundane accomplishments.

"First, of Vairocana," and so forth means: first one engages in the yoga of Vairocana, and then, in the space before one, one meditates on a wheel of the letter oṃ for as long as it takes for heat to arise. Then, if one takes it with the hand, it will be accomplished. Likewise, the yogins of Akṣobhya and the others meditate until the vajra and the rest become manifest, and if they take it with the hand, it will be accomplished; this is connected with the results that come below, such as "wide eye." The accomplishments of pills too should be viewed in this way. "At the door of the head" means above the crown: one makes the pill manifest there, and if one meditates on it entering through that very aperture of Brahmā, it is accomplished.

"Holding a fully equal guise" means speaking in harmony with the yoga of one's own deity. "Other objects" means objects of desire. "Of body, speech, and mind" means drawing in the tathāgatas with the hooks of body and the rest and making them enter within; the meaning is summoning what is to be accomplished for persons who are like jewels among yogins. "In order to teach that very yoga" and so forth means one should bring it down from the wind maṇḍala and place it on the Mahendra maṇḍala. "Fortunate people" means that the yogin should do this for the sake of others. "In other cases" means toward vicious beings. "Uncommon" means what is not within the domain. "One should seal" means one should enter into union.

"Abiding in the hand" means: having summoned the one to be accomplished and seized him with the hand, at that letter of the recitation mantra one recites the mantras for magnetizing and the rest, and meditates. "Sound that is appropriated" means sound produced by the elements of sentient beings. Unappropriated sound is the sound of drums, wood, and the like. Both is sound made by what has mind and what lacks mind. "A part of the result" means the ordinary accomplishments. "Then the portions of ground and so forth" means the tiered celestial palace and the rest. "Cause and result" means each earlier one is the cause and each later one the result. "The lord, the great Vajradhara" is Akṣobhya. "The three secrets" are object, faculty, and consciousness: through the divisions of lesser, middling, and supreme, faculty consciousness grasps objects. When these are joined in sequence, the signs arise, and the sign faculties genuinely arise. The vajra path is from luminosity onward. "Union of method and wisdom" means the two truths having become one.

This concludes the explanation collecting the difficult points of the twelfth chapter of the Illuminating Lamp.

The two stages are the training in the incomplete stage and the completion stage. "Unpermitted fierce rites" means the subjugating and fierce rites and the like of Maheśvara and others, who have abandoned the benefit of sentient beings. "Conduct" means bringing about the performance of those activities. "Appropriating" means appropriating the body of the great seal; it is the three syllables. What is said there, "appropriating the emanation body," means that one should perform vajra recitation by applying it to the body of the great seal.

"Of the buddhas" means also the other buddhas, Akṣobhya and the rest. There, when they have been generated as buddha bodies by the three syllables, one emanates Buddhalocanā and the others, and they should honor one another with special characteristics such as union. Again, "of the buddha" means the great-seal bodies of the buddhas Akṣobhya and the rest. Honoring body, speech, and mind with the offering of the arrangement that abides within one's own body, speech, and mind becomes the unsurpassed offering. "Thus, one possessing the highest faculties" means that for one possessing the highest faculties, emanation all at once is spoken of. "Or else" shows that one should emanate gradually and make offerings. "Through the distinction of wisdom" means through wind and consciousness, which have an illusion-like nature. "Body vajra" and so forth expresses that one should do vajra recitation by way of the purification of the three syllables. "Following the meaning" and so forth is clear.

"Body" means the purification by which Yamāntaka and the others are given the characteristics of men and women. "Ignorance" means great emptiness. Seeking the aim with it and being clear means that one should set the target ablaze with hūṃ. "The rite of this very one" means that, having become a maṇḍala deity, one performs pacifying and the other rites. "Joining and separating" means placing the eyes and so forth of the target into the body and performing the rites. "From the transformation of Vairocana" means that the yogin of Vairocana performs the fierce rite endowed with the yoga of Vajrasattva born from Vairocana. "Excellence" is generation. "I" means one should take on the pride, "I am the tathāgata so-and-so." "The target's body, become one's own basis," means imagining the self of the fourth target as empty.

"The center of the expanse of space" means in the source of phenomena, the wisdom of Vajrasattva. Looking at the letter oṃ there and drawing Vajrasattva within, one meditates on oneself and should place the target at one's own heart. "In front of him" means in front of Amitābha, that is, Amoghasiddhi, who abides in the north. "To them" means that the chief of Akṣobhya and Amitābha is Amoghasiddhi, that is, the Amoghasiddhi who arises from Akṣobhya. One places the desired one at his heart. "Without depending on the moon, the vajra, and so forth" is said of generation through union, without depending on the moon, the sun, the seed, and so forth.

This concludes the explanation collecting the difficult points of the thirteenth chapter of the Illuminating Lamp.

"Of the four kinds of mantras" refers to the wrathful rites and the rites that pacify fevers and the like, by reciting the mantras of snakes and so forth. "In the region of the heart" means writing the mantra "so-and-so ākārṣaya hri," preceded by svāhā, interspersed, and placing it at the heart. "Having enclosed it between two sun maṇḍalas" means that one meditates on the target as the embodiment of the three beings and imagines washing the target with the light rays of his samādhi being. "In both palms" and so forth is clear. "Its reality is unchangeability itself" refers to accomplishing by meditation alone, without depending on substances and recitation. "The Buddha is the Teacher" refers to those who have the yoga of the two kinds of seals of Vajradhara and the others, that is, who have both kinds of stage, generation and completion. "Even those skilled in the collections of rites" means even those skilled in seeing and performing the collections of rites of other tantras.

In "Human bone" and so forth, the heap of human bones belongs to Vairocana. The peg of khadira wood belongs to Amitābha. The iron peg belongs to Akṣobhya. Pegs of sandalwood and the like belong to Amoghasiddhi and Ratnasambhava. "Falling from the place" means abandoning the place. In the same way, the pressing down of speech and the pressing down of mind are also to be performed. Or else, the secret will be accomplished.

This concludes the explanation collecting the difficult points of the fourteenth chapter of the Illuminating Lamp.

"In the center of the maṇḍala" means that the yogin of Akṣobhya abides, having joined himself with the wisdom woman and the five families. "Having embraced" means one should sport in the center of the maṇḍala. "At sunset" means when the sessions have passed and it is the time of moonrise; at midnight one should obtain empowerment. "It is accomplished" means at the time of sunrise, having become a vessel for the wisdom of outer appearance and so forth. "Preceded by natural luminosity" means that, having called luminosity to mind before one, one enters into the union of method and wisdom and should correctly contemplate appearance and the others, which are of an inner nature. The sun is the near-attainment of appearance. "Interspersing this makes it stable" refers to "so-and-so stvaṃbhaya phaṭ," preceded by svāhā.

"The wrathful one of the great pledge" is Yamāntaka. In "Taking it from another accomplisher," "accomplisher" means even from a master who knows summoning. "Of one who wishes to cut off this harm" means that if anyone, from Vairocana and Vajradhara down to any yogin of the wrathful ones, reverses it, the life span is exhausted and comes to its end. "From the beginning of that rite" means from beginning a rite for obstructing. "idaṃ dibaya" means "Give gold and so forth! hri svāhā." "Wind and so forth" means the wind maṇḍala and so on.

In "If it should occur through carelessness, then the fang seal," the seal is the seal that destroys fangs, that is, Hayagrīva. Through his yoga one should eliminate the activities of the obstructors. If one does not do this, the activities will not succeed, and then the maṇḍala and so forth will be destroyed and cut off.

"Phaṭ is killing" means "phaṭ so-and-so māraya phaṭ." This is the killing mantra. Even to the mantras for pacifying and the rest, if one adds the killing mantra at the end, then whenever there is the syllable phaṭ, it performs the action whose aim is killing.

"The sequence of wisdom" means this: having meditated on oneself as Vajra Akṣobhya through the sequence of causing the wisdom being to enter, one emanates the four goddesses, Locanā and the rest. Generating them in an instant as the nature of the five maṇḍalas, one should meditate as the yogin.
