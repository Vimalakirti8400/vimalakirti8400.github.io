The Dhāraṇī Called 'The Stūpa Accomplished by the Perfection of All Wisdom' (Sarva-prajñānta-pāramitā-siddha-caitya-nāma-dhāraṇī)

Toh 601 in the Kangyur is a dhāraṇī text known in Sanskrit as the Sarva-prajñānta-pāramitā-siddha-caitya-nāma-dhāraṇī and in Tibetan as the Dhāraṇī Called "The Stūpa Accomplished by the Perfection of All Wisdom." As its titles suggest, it is a ritual and devotional text centered on a sacred dhāraṇī formula associated with the perfection of wisdom and the consecration or empowerment of a stūpa, promising to dispel evil and ill omens through its recitation. The text opens with homages to Vajrapāṇi, the buddhas of the three times, and key bodhisattvas including Akṣobhya, Amitābha, and Avalokiteśvara, situating it within a rich Vajrayāna devotional context. It belongs to the dhāraṇī section of the Kangyur, a broad category of texts preserving powerful sacred formulas accompanied by their ritual and narrative frameworks.

Evil and evil omens are dispelled by a single recitation. Here ends the dhāraṇī called "The Blazing Ushnīsha."

In the language of India: Sarva-prajñānta-pāramitā-siddha-caitya-nāma-dhāraṇī. In the language of Tibet: The Dhāraṇī Called "The Stūpa Accomplished by the Perfection of All Wisdom."

Homage to the Three Jewels. Homage to the Blessed One, Vajrapāṇi. Homage to the Blessed One, the Tathāgata, the arhat, the perfectly and completely awakened buddha, the King of Splendor Who Manifests the Conduct and Discipline of All Phenomena.

Homage to the buddhas of the three times.
Homage to the virtuous Three Jewels.
To Akṣobhya and to Amitābha,
to Avalokiteśvara I pay homage.
To the three families I also bow.

Thus did I hear at one time. The Blessed One, the great Vajrapāṇi, dwelt together with bodhisattvas ten times ten in number — Vajrapāṇi, Mañjuśrī, Avalokiteśvara, Maitreya, and the rest — along with Brahmā, Śakra (Indra), the four great kings, and the other guardian lords of the pure realms, in a place that was the great Mount Meru composed of five precious substances, resting in its very ground as a stūpa by nature, adorned above with a palace of manifold jewels and with four boundless stūpas, and being the abode of the great natural stūpa, so that even the expanse of the summit was itself a stūpa.

Then the Blessed One, from the uṣṇīṣa upon his head and from the auspicious knot at his heart and from other such places, released rays of light of manifold colors numbering sixty trillion and one hundred thousand billions, filling without exception all the world-realms of the great trichiliocosm — inconceivable, innumerable, inexpressible — entirely and pervasively, like a sesame pod filled with seeds, with the great radiance of those light rays. Thereby all the boundless sentient beings were caused to benefit: the blind came to see forms, the deaf came to hear sounds, the mute came to speak, the lame and the weak came to walk with limbs made whole, the sick were freed from illness, and hell-beings, passing away from there, were gradually liberated from suffering. Moreover, all the beings of the various destinies were gradually caused to regain mindfulness, and the entirety of the space realm was filled and spread with the mental stūpas of the dharmakāya. Those light rays then gathered once again at the uṣṇīṣa of the Blessed One and became the reflection of the dharmakāya.

Thereupon the assembly, headed by Vajrapāṇi, was filled with wonder, and they made offerings to the Blessed One of silk streamers, victory banners, pennants, parasols, yak-tail fans, bells, silk hangings, incense, flowers, garlands, unguents, butter lamps, food offerings, music, and the like. Having made these offerings, they addressed the Blessed One with these words: "Blessed One, Protector of Beings, possessed of great compassion, please teach the dhāraṇī of the great vidyā Vimala Uṣṇīṣa — the ritual for consecrating stūpas."

The Blessed One then declared: "This dhāraṇī of the great vidyā Vimala Uṣṇīṣa — the ritual for consecrating stūpas — if any son of good family or daughter of good family holds, bears, writes, reads, or hears the name of it, all their five actions of immediate retribution (pārājika), the ten non-virtues, and every sinful action they have performed without exception will be completely purified."

This is the ritual for consecrating stūpas of the great vidyā Vimala Uṣṇīṣa:

Oṃ trayādhvē sarva-tathāgata-hṛdaya-garbhe jvala-dharmadhātu-garbhe saṃharaṇa āyuḥ saṃśodhaya pāpaṃ sarva-tathāgata-samanta-uṣṇīṣa-vimale viśuddhe svāhā.

The moment this great vidyā is proclaimed, every world-realm in its entirety becomes, by its very nature, a stūpa. If this great vidyā is written on birch bark, cloth, or tree bark and tied to the top of a victory banner, all gods, humans, and beings of every kind will offer worship, pay homage, and hold it worthy of the highest veneration. If this great vidyā is worn on the body or around the neck, gods, humans, and asuras and all other beings will regard that person as a buddha itself. Whenever one travels through mountains and crags, forests and paths, hermitages, or any region of the earth and recites this great vidyā, every mountain and every region of the earth becomes, by its very nature, a stūpa.

Oṃ trayādhvē sarva-tathāgata-hṛdaya-garbhe jvala-dharmadhātu-garbhe saṃharaṇa āyuḥ saṃśodhaya pāpaṃ sarva-tathāgata-samanta vimala-uṣṇīṣa-viśuddhe mahā-viraje-rāgambhīrasi dharmadhātu vivīsāra a alaṃ svāhā.

When this is recited, every expanse of earth likewise becomes, by its very nature, a stūpa. If one crosses a pool, a river, a lake, an ocean, or any body of water and infuses it with the mantra of this great vidyā, all those waters too become, by their very nature, stūpas.

Oṃ trayādhvē sarva-tathāgata-hṛdaya-garbhe jvala-dharmadhātu-garbhe saṃharaṇa āyuḥ saṃśodhaya pāpaṃ sarva-tathāgata-samanta vimala-uṣṇīṣa-viśuddhe mahā-viraje-rāgambhīrasi dharmadhātu vivīsāra amamakī svāhā.

When this is recited, all waters likewise become, by their very nature, stūpas. Furthermore, if one infuses the sky in every direction, filling it throughout, with the mantra of this great vidyā, all of space too becomes, by its very nature, a stūpa.

Oṃ trayādhvē sarva-tathāgata-hṛdaya-garbhe jvala-dharmadhātu-garbhe saṃharaṇa āyuḥ saṃśodhaya pāpaṃ sarva-tathāgata-samanta vimala-uṣṇīṣa-viśuddhe mahā-viraje-rāgambhīrasi dharmadhātu vivīsāra a a ekhṃya svāhā.

When this is recited, every realm of space becomes, by its very nature, a stūpa — worthy of homage, circumambulation, and offering. By reciting the dhāraṇī of Vimala Uṣṇīṣa and erecting a stūpa in any of the five great elements, all afflictions and obscurations are completely purified.

Furthermore, if the mantra of this vidyā is infused into any substance — earth, wood, stone, clay, or whatever material — and a stūpa is erected from it, that substance becomes the material for a stūpa of many precious jewels:

Oṃ ratna-caitye pañcaye vivīsāra svāhā.

When this is recited and that substance is smeared with mud or plaster and the like, everything becomes the material for a stūpa of precious jewels. If this mantra of the great vidyā has not been infused, then even if a stūpa is built from gold, silver, vaiḍūrya, and the other six precious substances, it will be no different from stone, wood, and tile. Why? Because the essential heart and the blessing of the secret mantra do not abide within it by virtue of the substance itself alone.

But when consecrated through this great vidyā and erected, whatever stūpa is built in any of the great elements will cause the builder to be thoroughly cleansed of the stains of obscuration accumulated over incalculable eons. That person will then be born into the householder's lineage like a great śāla tree and will attain the stage of non-return.

When consecrated through this great vidyā and a stūpa is erected upon stone, wooden planks, or the like, that person will without doubt be born into a brahmin lineage like a great śāla tree, will traverse the ten grounds in sequence, and will attain the eleventh, suffused entirely with light.

When consecrated through this great vidyā and a stūpa is erected from gold, silver, vaiḍūrya, or the like,

that person will without doubt be born into the royal lineage like a great śāla tree and will attain the ground of the vajra-holder.

Furthermore, when a stūpa is consecrated through this great vidyā mantra, erecting even a single stūpa becomes equivalent to erecting ten million. If this great vidyā is not applied, then even erecting ten million is equivalent to erecting one. Why is this? For the same reasons as explained above.

The mantra of this great vidyā is as follows: tadyathā — sūkṣme sūkṣme, śānte śānte, nirkule, yaśotejo, sarvabuddhe, adhiṣṭhāna, adhiṣṭhite, anumide svāhā.

The ritual for this is as follows. Recite the mantra twenty-one times over a ball of clay and then erect a single stūpa — it will be equivalent to erecting ten million. The number and merit of erecting a hundred stūpas cannot be expressed even by noble arhats among the śrāvakas. The number and merit of erecting a thousand cannot be expressed even by bodhisattvas. The number and merit of erecting ten thousand cannot be expressed even by the Tathāgatas. The number and merit of erecting a hundred thousand cannot be expressed even by the Tathāgatas of the ten directions and three times.

Therefore, for the ritual of making offerings with five or fewer offering supports, one should first proceed as follows. At the site where the stūpa is to be built, or at the site of the clay for the stūpa, or at the place from which the clay for the stūpa is to be taken — at that place one consecrates the earth-lord and cuts off the boundary against obstructors and demons dwelling in the ground, against evil intentions and harmful forces that create obstacles to the stūpa and to virtue. One applies this great vidyā mantra to a cord of thread, reciting: oṃ vajrasūtra hūṃ phaṭ. Then, visualizing that very cord as a vajra-thread, one marks off the ground to the full extent needed for the stūpa and for the clay site, cutting the boundary with the cord. By marking off the ground in this way, that very site becomes equivalent to the precious materials of all kinds used in building stūpas.

Next, one applies this great vidyā mantra to an acacia stake eight finger-breadths long, reciting seven times: oṃ vajrakīlikīlikīlaya sarvaduṣṭa na hūṃ phaṭ. Visualizing it as a vajra stake, one drives it into the center and the four corners. When this is done, one will be protected from humans and non-humans alike — with the sole exception of the ripening of previous karma. The acacia stakes should remain in place and not be pulled up until all the clay needed for the stūpa site has been taken.

Next, one recites this great vidyā mantra seven times over sesame seeds and mustard seeds: oṃ amṛte hana hana hūṃ phaṭ. By scattering them in the direction of the earth, that very site of ground becomes an immeasurable dwelling-place of the Tathāgatas. Having thus consecrated the ground as a dwelling-place of the Tathāgatas and cut off the evil intentions of obstructors, one then makes offerings and dedications with incense, flowers, torma, and the like to the gods, nāgas, asuras, and other beings dwelling in that place. When this Uṣṇīṣa dhāraṇī is read aloud over the vimba, that site of ground also becomes extremely auspicious and blessed.

Then, when the time comes to dig the clay site for the stūpa, one should dig while reciting this great vidyā mantra: oṃ vajrakhana khana hūṃ phaṭ. Reciting this while visualizing that very ground as having transformed into the five precious substances, the excavated earth becomes the cause of the perfectly pure body of the Tathāgatas.

Next, the pure earth that has been taken is set aside in a single clean place. The ritual of pounding the clay is as follows: one presses down with a mallet using the mudrā of coiled ambrosia — a vajra with three prongs —

and strikes with this great vidyā mantra: vajramuṅgara ākoṭaya ākoṭaya hūṃ phaṭ. Reciting this seven times and striking while visualizing a vajra mallet, all afflictions and habitual tendencies without exception are purified and destroyed.

Next, the kneading of the clay is performed by touching it with the mudrā of coiled ambrosia applied to the perfumed water. Within that perfumed water one visualizes the white letter A, the letter of the nature of reality, from which a lunar maṇḍala arises together with its light. The great rays of light from that lunar maṇḍala pervade the perfumed water, and one visualizes that the perfumed water itself has become of the nature of ambrosia. Then one recites this great vidyā mantra: oṃ amṛte hūṃ phaṭ. Reciting this seven times and consecrating with it, that water itself becomes like a stream of great ambrosia.

Then, for the kneading of the clay, one recites this great mantra: oṃ vajra pramardanāya hūṃ phaṭ. Reciting this seven times, that very clay becomes the body of the Tathāgatas.

Next, the consecration of that same clay as the inconceivably blessed body of the Tathāgatas is performed by binding it with the mudrā of coiled ambrosia and reciting this great vidyā dhāraṇī: namo ratnatrayāya, tadyathā — sūkṣme sūkṣme, śānte śānte, dānte dānte, nirākule, yaśo yaśo, vatite jo śuddhe viśuddhe, sarvatathāgata adhiṣṭhāna adhiṣṭhite, ānamas te hite svāhā. One personally recites this great vidyā seven times, twenty-one times, or in multiples of seven. When consecrated with this great vidyā mantra, rays of gnosis in five colors stream forth from the clay itself in all ten directions. Having streamed out in the ten directions and accomplished the benefit of all sentient beings, those rays then gather back again into the clay. The clay itself thereby becomes of the nature of the five precious substances and comes to possess the causes and conditions for the inconceivably blessed body of the Tathāgatas. One also visualizes this with the mind.

Then, at the time of forming the clay, one recites this great vidyā: oṃ vajra āyuṣe svāhā. Reciting this seven times and consecrating, clouds of rays of gnosis stream forth from the clay itself and pervade the infinite world-realms of the ten directions, accomplishing the benefit of countless beings. When those great masses of light-ray clouds gather back into the clay, that clay itself becomes utterly pure in the nature of precious gnosis, like a crystal sphere. One also visualizes this with the mind.

Then, into the interior of the clay one places the heart-essence of Vimalośṇīṣa fashioned as a pearl mudrā, written on birch bark, cloth, paper, or tree bark. Together with this, the heart-essences of the five families individually — oṃ hūṃ trāṃ hrīḥ āḥ — are woven together and fashioned as a pearl mudrā, or written out, whichever is appropriate, and placed inside. This causes the heart-mind of the Tathāgatas to be endowed with gnosis. When placing it inside, one recites this vidyā mantra: oṃ vajragarbhe svāhā. Reciting this, the heart-gnosis of all the Tathāgatas gathers and enters within. One also visualizes this with the mind.

Then, into the vessel of the stūpa one anoints with mustard-seed oil or sesame oil, reciting this great vidyā mantra: oṃ araje viraje svāhā. Reciting this three times and consecrating, that very vessel becomes a stūpa that is the dharmadhātu itself, arisen from the union of skillful means and wisdom. One also visualizes this with the mind.

Then, at the time of constructing the stūpa, one recites this great vidyā:

oṃ vajramuṅgara ākoṭaya ākoṭaya hūṃ phaṭ

Reciting this and erecting the stūpa, through the inseparable union of dharmadhātu and primordial wisdom it becomes a stūpa of the dharmakāya. Then, by means of the Vimala Uṣṇīṣa heart-essence, erecting a stūpa likewise causes it, through the inseparable union of dharmadhātu and primordial wisdom, to become an immeasurable stūpa of the dharmakāya. Then, from within the vessel of the stūpa, one recites and draws forth this great vidyā mantra:

oṃ dharmadhātaye svāhā, dharmadhātugarbhe svāhā

Reciting this seven times and drawing the stūpa forth, through the inseparable union of skillful means and wisdom there arises an immeasurable stūpa of the dharmakāya, which accomplishes the benefit of all migrating sentient beings in full excellence for as long as saṃsāra is not emptied. One also visualizes this with the mind.

Then, when the wheel is to be installed on the stūpa that has come from its vessel, one recites this great vidyā mantra:

oṃ pramaṇaye svāhā

Reciting this and installing the wheel, it becomes adorned with a precious parasol, a precious finial, a precious victory banner, precious hanging pendants, and billowing silk streamers and the like, and is adorned with the manifold qualities of nirvāṇa, thereby fulfilling the benefit of beings just as one wishes. One also visualizes this with the mind.

Then, when offering food at the stūpa, one recites this great vidyā and makes the offering:

oṃ dharmadhātu svabhāva viśuddhe dharmate svāhā

Reciting this many times and erecting it, it comes to possess the meaning of the thoroughly pure dharmadhātu.

Then, when the stūpa of the erected maṇḍala is erected as five stūpas, the maṇḍala is to be drawn in the four directions and the center, making five. Likewise, whether one has erected a hundred or a thousand stūpas, or however many, one constructs a stone-platform grid of the maṇḍala sized to hold that number of stūpas. The platform is built of stone slabs, flat stones, or wood and the like, made into a square maṇḍala. The lower throne is fashioned as a lion throne, a jewel throne, or a lotus throne as a base. On top of this, one marks the ground with footstep impressions and consecrates it with sprinklings of scented water. The entire ground where the stūpas are to stand in the center and four directions is anointed in circular patterns with saffron and sandalwood water, a cord is snapped to lay out the lines, and the maṇḍala is drawn. As for drawing the maṇḍala, when five stūpas are erected the maṇḍala is to be drawn in five parts: the four directions and the center. Likewise, the ground for however many stūpas, whether a hundred or a thousand, is divided by the vajra-net lines of a maṇḍala grid sized to hold that number. The entire ground on which the stūpas are placed is to be drawn as an eight-petaled lotus.

Then, when the stūpa is to be placed in the maṇḍala, one recites this great vidyā mantra and requests it to be seated:

oṃ supratiṣṭha stūpe svāhā

Reciting this three times and setting down the stūpa, it acts in full excellence for the benefit of oneself and others. Then the order for placing the stūpas is as follows: first, place one in the center; then to the east; then to the south; then to the west; then finally to the north, placing them in order. While placing them, one recites this great vidyā mantra:

oṃ vajrapadma samaye svāhā

Reciting this three times, one requests the stūpa to be seated upon the vajra and lotus throne. Through this, one also visualizes with the mind that all the ground, the expanse of space, and all the ocean's shores are filled with stūpas.

Furthermore, after placing each stūpa, it comes to rest upon the maṇḍala that has appeared from the powder. There one makes the fivefold offering of incense, flowers, and the like, and recites the prayer of invitation to be seated.

Regarding the anointing of the stūpa with colors and so forth: one visualizes white for pacification, yellow for enrichment, red for magnetizing, and green for protection. The vestments too are to be applied accordingly, and one likewise visualizes them so in the mind.

Next, one consecrates the ground of the stūpa maṇḍala. Above the maṇḍala, from the syllable raṃ, which is red, one recites this great vidyā mantra:

hūṃ raṃ svāhā

Reciting this three times, that very syllable blazes forth as a great mass of primordial wisdom fire, thoroughly purifying all conceptual elaborations of those places, so that the very ground of the maṇḍala is transformed into a maṇḍala made of the five precious substances. One likewise visualizes this with the mind.

Then, in the centers of however many maṇḍala sections are drawn, whether five, a hundred, or a thousand, from the syllable a, which is white, one recites this great vidyā mantra:

oṃ a candramaṇḍala hūṃ phaṭ

Reciting this as many times as is fitting, the base of each stūpa is transformed into a lunar-disc maṇḍala. One likewise visualizes this with the mind.

Next, when performing the ritual of the stūpa, one faces whichever direction is convenient, east or west. Transforming oneself through meditative absorption into the great seal body of Vajradhara, one places upon a lunar-disc maṇḍala at one's heart a five-pointed vajra radiating great rays of primordial wisdom light. From every pore of the body as well, rays of primordial wisdom light, like vast cloud-banks accompanied by buddha maṇḍalas, radiate out immeasurably to the ends of space, bestowing blessing upon all the realms of sentient beings, accomplishing their benefit, and then dissolving back into the body. One likewise visualizes this with the mind.

Next, the raising up of the palace of the stūpa: with the stūpa set in the maṇḍala, one forms the mudrā of Vajradhātvīśvarī, touches it to the top of the stūpa, and recites this great vidyā mantra:

oṃ vajradhātu adhitiṣṭha hūṃ

Reciting and consecrating with this, the stūpa comes to rest at the center of a lunar throne, upon a maṇḍala of the dharmadhātu, upon a ground of precious substances, within a boundless palace of various jewels. One likewise visualizes this with the mind.

Having thus set and placed each stūpa one by one, one then worships with the various forms of offering. After doing so, one recites this great vidyā mantra, which purifies and clarifies all the forms of offering such as the lion victory banner, clusters of offerings, various gazes and smiles, streamers, offering fans, parasols, garlands, incense, butter lamps, perfume, and all the other forms of offering:

oṃ sarva pūjā puṣpe svāhā

Reciting and consecrating with this, magnificent cloud-banks of Samantabhadra's offerings gather in abundance and descend like a continuous rain. One likewise visualizes this with the mind.

One makes the fivefold offering to the stūpa one element at a time, and as a gift one presents individually small offering scarves, offering fans and the like as streamers, and small parasols, whatever is available, one each.

Having thus set up the stūpa in accordance with the ritual, in order to make vivid the meditative absorption of the intention-continuum, one is to contemplate and visualize in the four directions and center of the stūpa — five directions in all — the buddhas of the five families (Skt. pañcakula), each with their proper body-color, ornaments, vestments, and complete retinue.

That stūpa, arisen from the five wisdoms, is by its very nature thoroughly pure and luminous. Within the vase-portion of those stūpas, one visualizes and focuses upon the heart-syllable of the Vimalā Uṣṇīṣa dhāraṇī and the single seed-syllables of each of the five families, brilliant and blazing like refined gold, each seen as one. From those syllables, rays of wisdom-light of every kind stream out boundlessly, radiating to the ten directions as the five families, whereupon clouds of Tathāgata bodies fill the entire expanse of space to its very limit and adorn and perfect it all the way to the utmost boundary of the dharmadhātu. Having thus consecrated every world-realm, they perform this benefit for all beings, fulfill every hope, and gather back again. One then visualizes that they alight upon the crown of the head and upon the heart of that very stūpa, purifying the obscurations of enlightened activity and so forth, so that the body, speech, and mind are transformed into vajra-nature.

Into a conch-shell cup one presents the arghya offering water prepared by combining fragrant water and various perfumes. One seals the arghya mudrā, places the conch cup upon it, and, focusing in meditation, one contemplates that from a white syllable A a moon-disc is formed, and the rays from that moon-disc transform the fragrant water itself into the nature of wisdom-nectar (amṛta). One then recites this great touching-formula for presenting the offering:

Oṃ amṛte arghāṃ pratīccha svāhā.

Having recited this, one wafts and raises the cup three times to the crown of the head and offers it, whereby all noble ones (āryas) are pleased. In words one also says: "May all noble ones be pleased." One then visualizes a cloud of oceans of mudrās being spread upon the crown of the head, absorbed into the wisdom-body, and all excellent empowerments being conferred.

One then reads the Vimalā Uṣṇīṣa dhāraṇī aloud and recites it as many times as possible, and thereafter extends the dedication of merit and the aspiration prayer at length.

If completing this ritual in full is not possible, one should not let the intention be lost: through the meditative absorption of one's own deity one transforms the body, and one should contemplate the stūpa, the offering articles, and everything else as being by their very nature the pure dharmadhātu. Even if a stūpa has been erected in this way, it is just as though one had performed the extensive ritual — there is no contradiction.

Likewise, even if that is not done, by reading this Vimalā Uṣṇīṣa dhāraṇī, holding it, writing it out, and keeping it, when a stūpa has been erected this itself constitutes the performance of the extensive ritual; that stūpa is transformed into a Tathāgata body and brings about excellent benefit for oneself and others.

If this Vimalā Uṣṇīṣa dhāraṇī is not recited, then even though offerings have been presented to a stūpa and homage paid, it will not count as homage truly rendered.

From the root text itself the following is stated:

When the nature of all vidyā-mantras and secret mantras is not clearly present,
and the meditative absorption of the intention is not clearly present,
then even if stūpas and the like, images and sacred forms, have been erected,
they will not come to be perfected.

And it is stated: as for the qualities of erecting a stūpa and sacred images with mantra and intention, as it is said:

The distinction of merit made through this supreme and sacred stūpa ritual —
a single stūpa erected with this dhāraṇī recited
is equal to having erected ten million stūpas.

All the five heinous acts (anantarya) accumulated over cyclic existence since time immemorial,
and every mass of wrongdoing without exception, will be completely purified.
Life will become long and excellent.

In other lives, when one takes birth,
one will be born into a supreme and noble family,
will be abundantly endowed with the finest riches,
and will be born as a wheel-turning king.

One will remember all previous and future lives.
Throughout all three times and in every world,
one will never be parted from the Three Jewels,
and obstructors and harmful forces will not disturb that one.

བསམ་

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The phrase rendered "five actions of immediate retribution" translates མཚམས་མེད་པ་ལྔ, which corresponds to the Sanskrit pañcānantarya-karma — the five heinous deeds that lead immediately to rebirth in the lowest hell without interval. Describing them as "pārājika" in the translation would be inaccurate; the Tibetan uses a distinct term. The translation above follows the standard doctrinal referent.

The passage "ten times ten in number" renders བཅུའི་, which modifies the bodhisattvas. This could also be read as "ten-fold" or simply "ten"; the exact force is slightly ambiguous, but "ten times ten" conveys a large honorific assembly, which is the intent.

The opening lines are a colophon to a preceding text (the Blazing Uṣṇīṣa dhāraṇī) followed immediately by the title and opening of a new text.
