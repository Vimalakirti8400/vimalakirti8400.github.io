The Tantra King Called the Blessed One Vajrapāṇi's Manifest Revelation of Secrets (Bhagavān Vajrapāṇi Guhyābhideśa Tantra Rāja Nāma)

Toh 464 in the Kangyur is a tantric scripture known in Sanskrit as the Bhagavān Vajrapāṇi Guhyābhideśa Tantra Rāja and in Tibetan as the Bcom ldan 'das phyag na rdo rje gsang ba mngon par bstan pa'i rgyud kyi rgyal po, rendered in English as The Tantra King Called the Blessed One Vajrapāṇi's Manifest Revelation of Secrets. It belongs to the class of tantric texts in the Kangyur and takes as its central subject the bodhisattva Vajrapāṇi, the Lord of Secrets, whose esoteric teachings and wrathful power are here revealed in the form of a royal tantra. The text opens in the familiar sūtric style, with the Buddha residing in the Willow Palace surrounded by an immense assembly of buddhas, bodhisattvas, vidyā wrathful ones, asuras, and yakṣas, before entering a meditative absorption called Subduing All Māras and setting the stage for Vajrapāṇi's revelation of secret doctrine. As a tantra king, it occupies a prominent place among the esoteric scriptures of the Kangyur, reflecting the tradition's emphasis on Vajrapāṇi as a supreme guardian and revealer of tantric teachings.

In the language of India: Bhagavān Vajrapāṇi Guhyābhideśa Tantra Rāja Nāma. In the language of Tibet: The Tantra King Called the Blessed One Vajrapāṇi's Manifest Revelation of Secrets.

Homage to the glorious Lord of Secrets, Vajrapāṇi.

Thus did I hear at one time. The Blessed One was dwelling in the Willow Palace together with a retinue of buddhas equal in number to the grains of sand along the Ganges River, together with the bodhisattva assembly including Maitreya and others, together with an immeasurable assembly of vidyā wrathful ones, together with an assembly of asuras, and together with five hundred yakṣas, all gathered in one place.

At that time the Blessed One entered into the meditative absorption called "Subduing All Māras." Then the bodhisattva Vajrapāṇi (Skt. Vajrapāṇi) draped his upper robe over one shoulder, made prostration, and spoke these words:

O holder of the vajra of secrets,
arise swiftly from the samādhi king,
the immovable, and reveal the supreme secret:
the domain of the five wisdoms,
the supreme secret of body, speech, and mind.
How is the sequence of accomplishment
and the activity of action, in brief?
By what manner of person is it accomplished,
and the supreme, who has become the king of awareness?
May the Blessed One, speak this well.

When he had made this request, the Blessed One arose from his meditative equipoise. He struck the vajra of the five inseparable wisdoms at his heart three times, causing all world-realms to tremble and shake and quake entirely; then, having sent forth rays of light called "Taming the Malevolent," which dissolved back into his heart, from the navel of the vajra arose the consort Vajrasukhā, the courageous vajra woman. She circumambulated her own teacher three times to the right, then with palms pressed together in prostration, spoke these words:

What you have well declared,
O Vajradhāra, is the supreme
sacred secret of your body, speech, and mind.
This great tantra, difficult to find,
is like a wish-fulfilling jewel,
born from immeasurable accumulations.
From the emptiness of compassion's nature,
may you, Blessed One, explain it.

When she had made this request, the Blessed One smiled, and sent forth rays of light called "Maitreya's Manifold Display," which pervaded all world-realms and then gathered back, dissolving into his own face. Then he spoke:

O Vajrasukhā, the courageous one,
just as you have asked,
the nature of selflessness and emptiness
shall be explained. Listen with devotion and retain it well.

The body, speech, and mind of Vajradhāra,
from the nature of indivisible emptiness,
are compassion's skillful means in all their variety.
Because this is most difficult to realize,
it is the domain of the buddhas
of the past, the present, and the future:
inconceivable, utterly pure,
beyond expression, immovable, unconditioned,
the dharmakāya like a stainless sky,
the supreme understanding of the three times and beyond time.
How then can this be taught?

And yet, because its nature is compassion,
know the nature of skillful means and wisdom
as the method of the non-dual pair.
This is the supreme secret:
by manifestly revealing this very reality to others,
those to be trained will be satisfied, like a king,
and the fruit of one's own aspiration will arise.
Therefore the great tantra king of the manifest revelation of secrets shall be taught.

E ma ho! The supreme secret, the indivisible vajra.
E ma ho! The great tantra of manifold jewels,
non-dual in skillful means and wisdom,
this is your own supreme secret.

This concludes the first chapter, the chapter of joyful occasion, from The Tantra King Called the Blessed One Vajrapāṇi's Manifest Revelation of Secrets.

The courageous Vajrasukhā then asked: O Blessed One, Great Vajradhāra, what manner of person is the one who accomplishes you?

The Blessed One declared:

O beautiful one, well asked is your question,
O mother who gives rise to the cause of undefiled bliss.

Supreme one, O mother of no-self wisdom,
I shall explain to you — listen well.
The person who accomplishes me
is endowed with compassion and possessed of great wisdom,
patient and replete with diligent effort,
adorned with faith and the three trainings,
courageous and versed in secret conduct,
devoted and unwavering — the three refuges his sure support.
He makes constant offering without distraction,
trusts in his chosen deity (iṣṭadevatā)
and has received the outer, inner, and secret empowerments,
holding the samaya, its signs and meanings, with certainty.
Should one practice in this way,
before long one will become learned
in my three secrets.
The fruit obtained through such effort
dwells in the heart of that fortunate one.

Thus did the Blessed One declare.

This concludes the second chapter, the chapter teaching the person who is the practitioner, from The Tantra King Called the Blessed One Vajrapāṇi's Manifest Revelation of Secrets.

The courageous Vajrasukhā then asked: O Blessed One, Great Vajradhāra, what are the places suited to accomplishing you?

The Blessed One declared:

O beautiful one, well asked is your question,
O mother who gives rise to the cause of undefiled bliss.
Supreme one, O mother of no-self wisdom,
I shall explain to you — listen well.
The supreme places for accomplishing me
are the eight charnel grounds or their vicinities,
within a charnel-ground forest,
a mountain peak or a blue-hued rocky crag
bearing the appearance of a wrathful form, facing north,
adorned with lovely blue flowers,
groves of nebu trees and utpala lotuses,
solitary, delightful, and conducive to joy,
auspicious, wealth-increasing, and commanding power,
bearing a name that accords with Indrabhūti (Śakra's realm),
and apt for practices of every kind.
Salt flats without water, a barren midlands place,
a royal palace, a marketplace,
or a dwelling place frequented by śrāvakas and the like —
in such places I am not to be accomplished.
As for those who are ripened persons,
what need is there to scrutinize location and so forth?
For them, all is the buddha-field of great bliss,
and they abide at play within it.
Therefore one should examine by means of one's family (kula).

This concludes the third chapter, the chapter called Teaching the Place of Practice, from The Tantra King Called the Blessed One Vajrapāṇi's Manifest Revelation of Secrets.

The courageous Vajrasukhā then asked: O Blessed One, Great Vajradhāra, what is the method of accomplishment?

The Blessed One declared:

O beautiful one, well asked is your question,
O mother who gives rise to the cause of undefiled bliss.
Supreme one, O mother of no-self wisdom,
I shall explain to you — listen well.
The stages of accomplishing me
are these: preceded by great compassion,
one trains in the benefit of others.
One visualizes the vajra-like sky
and makes offering with great bliss.
Purifying one's stream of being, and before the assembled buddhas,
laying regret aside, one rejoices.
One makes supplication to those who have gone to nirvāṇa,
urges upon others the meaning of what is supreme,
develops through one's family line the great offering,
and accomplishing the benefit of others, one extends it to all.
One does not regard one's own suffering
but instead places all others in happiness.
Like beings abiding as if engulfed in fire,
one cultivates compassion for all sentient beings.
One trains perfectly in the mode
of being free from conceptual elaboration and of relinquishing the two extremes.
Buddhas and sentient beings are not two;
from the outset empty and devoid of self,
all phenomena of appearance and existence
are utterly pure in their essential nature.
The meaning of the Middle Way — like space, free from conflict —
in such a dharmatā as this,
the nature of mind is non-dual.
For sentient beings who have not realized this and whose minds go astray,
in order to free them from all suffering,
one cultivates great compassion.

One's own mind itself as the syllable HŪṂ —
the mother who gives birth to all buddhas,
lotus-born, endowed with skillful means and wisdom,
free from both extremes and furnished with a seat,
non-dual, of vajra nature —
refined by compassion, the third is complete.
Single-faced, dark of body,
the right hand holds the vajra,
blazing with the vajra's pressing power,
the left hand rests upon the hip holding a bell,
poised in the sky with an alluring bearing,
adorned with jewel-bearing water ornaments.

The body adorned with silk of tiger-skin,
the right leg drawn in, the left extended, trampling the māras,
three eyes glaring, fangs bared in ferocity,
dwelling within the light of the fire of the cosmic aeon.

The three places of the three syllables —
bless the sense bases, made divine.
In the place, generate the wrathful seed syllable;
scatter immeasurable emanations in the ten directions.

By the four syllables, invite the wisdom down.
The Lord of the Family and the other buddhas —
contemplate the bestowal of empowerment and blessing as a crown.

That samādhi is the cause from which it arises.
The bodhisattva of the third rite
meditates without interruption for the benefit of others.

Alternatively, marked by the syllable of speech,
the yogin recites, performing activities without obstruction.
This is pure awareness —
ever undiminished, endowed with fruit.

This concludes the fourth chapter, on accomplishing the single hero, from the King of Tantras, the Tantra Manifestly Revealing the Secret of the Blessed One Vajrapāṇi.

Then the heroic goddess Vajra Dema (Vajradevaā) asked: Blessed One, what is the retinue of Vajradhara like?

The Blessed One declared:

O Beautiful One, well have you asked.
O mother from whom the cause of undefiled bliss arises,
O supreme mother of the wisdom of selflessness,
listen well as I explain to you.

My retinue is inconceivable.
Even were buddhas to count their number
for an entire eon, it could not be known.
Therefore it is equal in measure to the sky itself.
I shall explain it in brief summary.

The retinue of body consists of ten wrathful ones,
embodying body, speech, and mind as the essence of the five families,
immovable, they tame the vicious.
The retinue of mind consists of a hundred families,
and in brief, four emanated wrathful ones.
The retinue of speech is immeasurable,
being great garuḍas who tame the six classes of beings,
varied and utterly beyond thought.
The retinue of those to be tamed consists of the eight great nāgas,
and it is through speech that beings of that class are tamed.
The assembly of yakṣas bearing vajras
numbers five hundred, gathered by family, tamed through body.
Through mind the fierce gods and demons are tamed.
Through meditative absorption in loving-kindness they are tamed.
The multitude of emanations that tame this one through that one
has arisen through the power of aspiration.
Those who are tamed by such inconceivable means —
how could one speak of them by family?
In brief, I have shown this to you, O Beautiful One.

This concludes the seventh chapter, the chapter definitively teaching the retinue, from the King of Tantras, the Tantra of the Blessed One Vajrapāṇi Manifestly Revealing the Secret.

Then the heroic goddess Vajra Dema asked: Blessed One, what is the sequence for accomplishing the activities of Vajradhara?

The Blessed One declared:

O Beautiful One, well have you asked.
O mother from whom the cause of undefiled bliss arises,
O supreme mother of the wisdom of selflessness,
listen well as I explain to you.

For those who wish to tame the vicious nāgas,
the great vajra garuḍa of great power tames them —
an emanation of my speech,
with sharp fangs, splendid and fiercely gnashing.

From the syllable baṃ, upon a mountain of ocean,
arising from the syllable hūṃ,
one should visualize the heroic nāga-tamer.
His supreme body is black, with nine faces.
The root face is deep blue-black; the right is white,
the left is red. Above those,
the center is red, the right is green,
the left is like fresh-burnished gold.
Above those, the center is white, the right is black,
the left shines like lapis lazuli.
Every face has three eyes and vajra lips;
at the crown, a precious vajra horn.
He has eighteen arms: in the right hands
he holds a vajra, a sword, a spear-banner, a lasso,
a lance, an iron hook, an arrow,
a three-pointed action-garuḍa.
In sequence, the lord of waters, and the boundless —

Norgyé Tobgyu, Dungkyong,
Padma, Padchen, Rigden,
Jogpo, the nine nāgas, Rotö Lha —
to the lord with joined palms, Rinchen Tob.
The last ones have their hands coiled about,
each holding its own color.
Seven-headed serpents hover like clouds above.

In the left hands: a bell, a club,
a wheel, a sword, a great skull-staff,
a nāga victory-banner, a bow,
the fierce-garuḍa phurba, and the garuḍa phurba.

In sequence, to the right: the Wind-Expeller, the Joyful,
the Beautiful Heroine, the Light-Maker,
the Accomplished One, the Steadfast One, the Virtuous One,
the Illuminator — each in order resembling the father.
To the principal lord with joined palms she offers a lotus;
the Triple-Necklace One makes obeisance.
To the left, contemplate them in sequence.

The four feet rest upon a water-monster, a tortoise,
a pig, and a serpent.
The wings are vajras of the four families;
the tail-feathers are endowed with swords of the four families.
In a great clawed hide he blazes with fire,
utterly fierce, destroying all lords of the earth
and every class of nāga without exception.
Contemplate that he arrives at the highest realm, Akaniṣṭha.

Then the three places are adorned with syllables:
oṃ āḥ hūṃ, white, red, and black,
meditating on the mantra of the emanation-armor.
Oṃ hūṃ hrāṃ hrīḥ ā — contemplate these at the crown, throat, heart,
navel, and secret place.
E vaṃ ma yā — contemplate these at the shoulders and the back.
Hūṃ sā hī — contemplate these at the chest, neck, belly, and the hollows below the navel.
Jaḥ hūṃ vaṃ hoḥ — contemplate these at the four gates.
Staṃ ja ya a — the emanations and re-emanations, contemplate as inconceivable.

Then the secret mantra is revealed:
the wrathful mantra of the great karma-garuḍa,
gathering the power of all buddhas —

oṃ vajra khyuṃkara khyuṃkara vajra garuḍa tsale tsale hūṃ phaṭ

This is taught as the secret mantra of the body.

oṃ vajra garuḍa hūṃ — the vital life-word — nan rad.
lus dul nāga māra ya phaṭ.
raṃ raṃ jvala jvala śi śi dulbun rad phaṭ.

This is the noble awareness-mantra of speech.

oṃ vajra caṇḍa garuḍa hūṃ māra ya māra ya.
sphoṭa ya sphoṭa ya.
vajra vāti garuḍa vege hūṃ dze dza ri dza pri dza hūṃ phaṭ.

This is the mantra of the vajra-wrathful mind, the secret of the heart:
it lays waste to all who cause harm,
and causes the heart-blood of nāgas to drip.
This samaya of Vajradhara
is as hard to find as a precious jewel —
strive at it always.

Offer the torma of the appropriate substances,
and within it draw in the harm.

oṃ vajra śrī mamā nāga savita coga khugmaṃ liṃ dhiṃ svāhā

With this mantra, summon and offer to the deity.

If one desires to slay,
apply the fierce mantras of the body, speech, and mind awareness
together with the second phurba's wrathful mantra,
setting the slaying mantra upon a straw effigy.
Strike a likeness with the phurba:
even if it be Brahmā himself, it will certainly be destroyed.

oṃ vajra caṇḍa mahā roṣaṇa vajra kīlāya sarva vighnān muṃgara kīlāya ākoṭa ya hūṃ phaṭ.
oṃ vajra caṇḍa garuḍa kīli kīla ya sarva vighnān muṃgara kīlāya ākoṭa ya hūṃ phaṭ.

These are the mantras for striking with the fierce-garuḍa phurba.

After that, burn every last effigy
in a wrathful fire-pit,
lighting the fire with thorned wood.
With the pride of the great fierce garuḍa:

nāga bhūmi pati sarva kāya vāk citta vajra jvāla anala arka hūṃ

Burn with the appropriate mantras,
then mix the ashes with the three whites
and cast them into the waters of the ocean.

If this karma-practice is performed seven times,
one who has been seized at the heart by the poison of a great nāga
will certainly be healed.

But if the great nāga does not relent,
its body along with its dwelling
will in an instant be consumed
by fire and a rain of vajras.

Therefore, because this samaya must not be transgressed,
do not perform this practice hastily.

The accomplishment of the fierce karma-garuḍa
has been taught here to you, Beautiful One.

This is the eighth chapter, from the King of Tantras, the Tantra of the Manifest Secret of the Blessed One Vajrapāṇi, entitled "The Chapter Teaching the Accomplishment of Vajra-Fierce Garuḍa, the Deity Who Accomplishes Karma."

The heroic lady Vajrasukhā asked: Blessed One, emanation body of the Blessed One Vajradhara, what is the nature of the accomplishment of the messenger, the Black Yakṣa?

The Blessed One declared:

Beautiful One, you have asked well.
O mother who is the cause of undefiled bliss,
O supreme mother who is wisdom of no-self,
listen well, for I shall explain this to you.

This Black Yakṣa of mine
is unobstructed in all actions to be performed;
in particular, he accomplishes fierce karmic activity.

In a charnel ground, on a rocky cliff, or in a forest cave,
make the maṇḍala upon black ground,
with a lotus of eight petals and a gateway,
dark blue, triangular, blazing with firelight.

From the syllable Suṃ, visualize a Hūṃ above Mount Meru,
with rays of light radiating and gathering, transforming into a vajra.

From that arises the Black Yakṣa,
with three faces, six arms, and three legs.
The right face is terrifying, with the visage of a tiger;
the left is mighty, with the visage of a lion.

The first right hand holds a vajra;
the second holds a three-pointed trident;
the third holds a phurba dagger.
The left hands hold a great bell, an iron hook,
and a skull cup brimming with blood.

With his right he seizes a female māra in violent wrath;
with his left he presses down a male māra with a sun-like face.
His phurba-feet, red and blazing with fire,
are planted on the heads of the harmful obstructors.

He wears a garland of skulls and a robe of human skin;
he is adorned with charnel-ground ornaments of bone.
His red hair hangs downward;
his three eyes are wide, his fangs bared fiercely.
He laughs with a great ha-ha-ha and tames the malicious.

Together with a thousand emanated yakṣas,
he accomplishes all things according to the command of Vajradhara.

The vidyā mantra he offers is:
Oṃ vajra yakṣa kāla caṇḍa nīlāṃ para dhara śarīra hūṃ phaṭ.

Recite this root secret mantra one hundred thousand times. Once accomplishment is attained, target the object: strew it with grains of pepper and with a wheel of salt, insert it into the target visualization, then place it in the accomplishment vessel. Sprinkle upon it white mustard seed, cumin, and poisonous substances. Then recite this secret fierce mantra:

Oṃ vajra yakṣa kāla caṇḍa nīlāṃ vara dhara sarva śatrūṃ vidhārāya phaṭ. Hara hara phaṭ. Prahara prahara phaṭ. Chinda chinda phaṭ. Bhinda bhinda phaṭ. Visphoṭaya visphoṭaya phaṭ. Sarva śatrūṃ kāya vāk citta vināśakaraya phaṭ.

Sprinkle it until death has not yet come. Once accomplished, insert it into a skull of unknown lineage, conceal it beneath a great shrine, and press down upon it from above with a Mount Meru vajra — by this it will certainly be destroyed.

Alternatively, draw the great yakṣa upon charnel-ground cloth, and write this mantra upon his heart, enclosed within the letter E:
Oṃ vajra yakṣa kāla caṇḍa samaya manusmara siddhi hūṃ.

Write yakṣa-paṭali on his hands and Kamma-bhara on his feet. Insert this into a great torma and cover it with blue silk. Upon that, on cloth or on a leaf, draw the yakṣa's life-wheel with a hub, ten spokes, and a rim, then a further ten spokes and rim, blazing with fire. Arrange the mantra-machine around it, and write the name of the target together with a multiplier. This is the life-force of the yakṣa; it is a samaya command that must not be transgressed. Whatever tasks are assigned, he accomplishes them in body.

Alternatively, draw the yakṣa upon a skull of the royal class, anoint it with spiced butter mixed with beer, and each day apply it to whatever karmic activity is desired. Sprinkle it with flowers of matching color, and the assigned karmic activity will be accomplished.

On a ground of blue water-silk, write a wheel in gold with a hub, eight spokes, and a rim. On the outer ring of sixteen cells write the seed syllables and mantras together, and at the edges write the mantra of karmic activity. Tied around the neck, this will protect against all adverse forces — like a bee at a flame.

Write the image of an enemy together with the mantra inside a pill in which dog meat has been placed, give it at sunset to a crow, and —

— that enemy will be driven away from whatever place they inhabit, once the mantra has been recited a hundred or a thousand times over the field. Make a cord from the hair of a dog's coat, tie one hundred and eight knots in it, and with each knot recite the mantra once and place a yakṣa inside a grain of rice; thrown in this way, it will not fail to strike its mark like an arrow.

Likewise, when one is confined in a house, if humans, cattle, and the like are copulating, they will become stuck together like dogs. Place the image of a yakṣa, together with the yakṣa's heart and mantra, inside the heart of a black dog, and by merely keeping it, the yakṣa itself will become inseparable from the practitioner, like a body and its shadow, and will not depart from the practitioner even for an instant.

Such is the sequence of karmic activities, O beautiful one, that I have explained to you.

This is the ninth chapter, on the accomplishment of the Black Yakṣa, from the King of Tantras, the Secret Manifest Display of the Blessed One Vajrapāṇi.

Then the heroic woman Vajra Dema made her request: Blessed One, what is the inconceivable completion stage yoga of the Blessed One Vajradhara?

The Blessed One declared: Well have you asked, O beautiful one. O source of the cause of undefiled bliss, supreme mother of wisdom of no-self, listen well, for I shall explain it to you.

The great inconceivable completion stage: first, the activities of the deity are performed previously. Then, having attained the visualization of the generation stage, contemplate the wisdom being at the heart. Within that, at the center of the heart, adorned by a drop on a four-petaled lotus, between sun and moon, the size of a lentil seed, contemplate the vital wind dwelling within it like the tip of a lamp's flame standing upright. When the visualization of this has become stable, do not think of anything at all; yet if one contemplates the inconceivable, wisdom will arise from it. Luminous and non-conceptual, the body of awareness — that is what is called wisdom. It is free of support, utterly pure, and clear.

For one who wishes to cultivate conceptual meditation with a support, at the place of the body there is a white wheel with thirty-two spokes and petals, adorned with the marks by means of method. At the place of speech, sixteen red petals — this is wisdom — adorned with certainty by means of method. At the place of mind, the eight junctures of the seasons, with the eight principal syllables dwelling there. At the places of the karmic activities, channel-spokes of the number of a staff. Wisdom, transformed into two, is red in color. In the non-dual center thereof, the division into right and left is the separation of method and wisdom.

The wheel that awakens conceptual thought has six spokes on the side of method and six on the side of wisdom. The last is the ground that holds bliss, where the non-conceptual hero dwells, adorned by four syllables. The wheel that holds bliss has no syllable; all verbalization has been abandoned — it is the supreme unconditioned nature. Seminal fluid, blood, and lymph reside here. This is what destroys the three paths.

In an instant, the horse of the wind rouses both method and wisdom, and non-dual wisdom arises. By wisdom, conceptual thought is burned. Even the ash is not found as an object — thus the five signs arise in sequence. Then the glorious qualities must be cultivated again and again. The enjoyments of the four assemblies will truly arise in their place. Having attained the ground of non-dual bliss, the continuum of birth, death, and the rest is severed. This is the perfectly pure and excellent ground — need it be said that it will be attained?

In this way, purified thus, when all of it — the reckonings of day and night, the portions of time, staffs, water-hours, seasonal junctures, and so forth — is collected into one, non-conceptual, equal like the pure sky, wisdom arises effortlessly and spontaneously. The great wisdom of the primordial buddha — to the mother of wisdom who liberates all beings of the three realms, I bow down. Non-abiding, free of conceptual elaboration, unconditioned — to the excellent guru who has realized this perfectly, I bow down.

This yoga, difficult to express, I have explained to you, O beautiful one. If the hero is not yet stable, then through the practice of the syllable Hūṃ —

All world-realms will be destroyed.
Fire burns up firewood,
and when the wood is gone, how can the fire remain?
When what is stable and what moves have fallen to ruin,
the indestructible syllable, utterly pure —
this is the supreme secret.
This supreme, unsurpassed secret,
the realization of selflessness, I have explained to you.
Otherwise, even all the buddhas
as numerous as the grains of sand in the Ganges
would not be able to express it.

This concludes the tenth chapter of the King of Tantras, the Manifest Display of the Secret of the Blessed One Vajrapāṇi, which teaches the inconceivable stage of completion.

The ḍākinī Vajrasukhā then made her request: Blessed One, what is the nature of the empowerment into the pure lineage of Vajradhara?

The Blessed One declared:

Beautiful one, your question is excellent.
You who are the source of undefiled bliss,
supreme mother of wisdom and selflessness —
listen well, for I shall explain this to you.

First, the characteristics of the master:
beautiful in form, tamed, possessed of a loving mind,
skilled in the reality of mantra and tantra,
pleasant of speech and delighting in generosity,
steadfast and truthful, with a loving mind,
knowing others' intentions, speaking straightforwardly,
committed to sacred pledges, expert in the mantra rites,
skilled in the signs of mudrā and maṇḍala,
knowing the ten realities, endowed with compassion,
and free from worldly ways.
The wise one who knows these realities in this way —
that is the intrinsic nature of all buddhas,
and is supreme among those with awakening mind, arhats,
śrāvakas, and pratyekabuddhas.
A guru of this kind is to be relied upon as one's very life.

If one disparages the master,
accomplishment (siddhi) will not be found even in a dream.
Therefore, having pleased the guru,
one drapes the upper robe over one's shoulder,
kneels down, joins the palms, and holding flowers,
one should make supplication to draw the maṇḍala.

In a village, marketplace, charnel ground,
forest, temple, or city —
in the north-east quarter, setting aside the other two directions of accomplishment —
the belly of the constellation of the month beginning with Rohiṇī and so forth
is to be understood as the proper time.
Otherwise, fault will arise.
The wise one should act so that no fault occurs.

The raising of the ground and the examination thereof
correspond in sequence to: a temple, a forest, a flat stone, and a fine house.
The examination of all sites should be dispensed with.

Through the master's ripening, the disciples
are to be purified in their five aggregates.
Having cleared away all faults of the earth,
one visualizes the goddess arisen from the syllable Laṃ —
adorned with ornaments, holding a jeweled vase in her hand.
She is to be worshipped and supplicated:

Oṃ — come forth, come forth, O earth!
Great goddess, mother of the world,
filled full with all precious things,
adorned with excellent ornaments,
bearing ankle-rings and bracelets that resound —
for the supreme offering to Vajrasattva,
accept this offering vessel
and accomplish the maṇḍala work well.
Oṃ hi hi hi hi hūṃ svāhā.

With her permission, visualize her dissolving back into the earth.
Having again purified and fumigated the ground, for the central deity
visualize a great square fragrant seat,
circular in shape, with one-third of a wheel removed,
and upon a seat of blue flowers.
Whether of precious substance or of earth, it matters not —
avoiding the fault of a black vessel,
a striped stick of tiger and lion stripes,
an utpala lotus, and what others cannot overcome —
the syllable Ha and Sa thereby purify all illnesses.
Silver, pearl, mother-of-pearl,
and coral with gold cause wisdom to increase.
Barley, wheat, sesame, beans,
and mung beans purify the five paths.
Camphor, saffron, nutmeg,
sandalwood, and aloeswood
accomplish the five activities for the benefit of others.
Water together with the five essences
brings the yoga to its fullness.
The neck of the vase is wrapped with cloth,
the mouth is adorned with fruit-bearing branches,
and kuśa grass with its distinguishing marks
is placed in each of its respective directions.

Take the incense and make this supplication:

Blessed One, sovereign Vajradhara,
look upon me with your compassionate gaze.
For the sake of the disciples,
and for the sake of making offering to all of you,
I wish to draw this maṇḍala.
Through the power of the precious ones,
and by your gracious permission,
please come tomorrow.

This supplication is made three times.

With loving care for the disciples,
the vajra master requests the vow and its protection,
while visualizing the vajra-bearing lord —
all the deities of secret mantra,
all the world-protectors without exception,
those who have entered into perfect awakening,
the beings who delight in the Buddha's teaching,
and all who possess the vajra eye —
that the supreme maṇḍala of Vajradhara,
which I am about to enter today
together with my fortunate disciples,
may be maintained in order to bestow blessing.

Each cord is the very substance of the five wisdoms;
each one is of the five pure families.
Since all phenomena are without essential nature,
they are wound together, reciting mantra upon mantra.

The span of the maṇḍala in its general measure
is twice the diameter of the wheel,
and the twelve-cubit measure is for the width of the gates.
Reciting Hūṃ, apply the five nectars,
and reciting the syllable Jaḥ, hold it at the center.
Then, likewise, cast the great wisdom-line
into the sky.

Equally, with the second measured line,
visualizing it as pure and held by Vajradhara,
draw the great Brahma-line.
For the second set — the corner lines and the rest —
the west and south are the master's positions,
and the north and east are the places for disciples.
Apart from circumambulating to the right,
never do so to the left.

In this way, divide everything by measure.
If the measuring cord snaps, the master will die;
if it is too short or too long, illness will follow;
if the directions are confused, the disciples will be bewildered.
Therefore, let the skilled one perform this without fault.

With five precious substances or powder of grain,
or else with that which arises from the primary elements,
bless it with the ten syllables,
and from the purity of the five families,
draw with white, yellow, red, green, and black,
beginning from the direction of the lord.

If the pigments are uneven,
or if they are lopsided or low,
there will be no attainment and affliction by conflict;
if thick, illness will arise; if thin,
wealth will diminish; if cut off altogether,
death will follow; if the clockwise circuit is not drawn,
it is as though struck by a stake.

In the east draw white; in the south, west, and north
draw yellow, red, and green respectively.
The center is like beryl,
in the aspect of the lotus direction.
The borders are adorned with beautiful-colored vases,
together with the ornaments of the gates and so forth.
Draw it within a charnel-ground enclosure.
Place the fierce and wrathful ones and the rest at the corners and borders.
The center is highest; slope toward the east and north,
and draw it elevated, unmixed, and straight.

Then, visualizing Vajradhara,
with reverence and with flowers,
make this supplication:

Glorious great Vajradhara,
who shows the way of great awakening,
great protector, I supplicate you.
Grant me that very samaya.
Grant me the supreme awakening mind.
Grant me also the Three Refuges —
the Buddha, the Dharma, and the Saṅgha.
Great protector, lead me
into the supreme city of great liberation.

This is repeated three times.

Come here, my child: you are
the great vessel of the Great Vehicle's noble way.
The supreme ritual conduct of secret mantra
shall now be properly explained to you.
By the indestructible power of the vajra,
you will attain incomparable wisdom.
Therefore, wishing to attain omniscience,
let the child turn their mind to this:

Honor all the guru-buddhas;
abandon root and branch transgressions.
Toward sentient beings not yet free in cyclic existence,
arouse the great compassion,
liberate them and place them on the path of liberation,
and breathe with ease.

These things, whether in sequence or not in sequence,
examine carefully the stages for the disciple.
Give water for bathing and the tooth-stick.
Examine in order the ritual activities, the directions, and the attainments.
Bind on the protection cord and give kuśa grass.
Examine the dreams, and if they are inauspicious,
dispel them with the fire of the pacifying rite.
Bind the blindfold and invite the wisdom-being to descend.

In order to introduce the disciple into the sacred maṇḍala,
all previously accumulated non-virtues without exception
are purified by the mere sight of the maṇḍala.

"Child, who are you, and what do you desire?"
"I am of fortunate and noble family,
and I delight in you, Vajradhara."
Open their eyes, and show them even the deities of the maṇḍala.

Give the samaya water and proclaim the oath once more.
Have them cast the flower, and reveal the face of the deity.
Whichever deity the flower falls upon,
encourage them with delight in that family.

Then confer the empowerment by name
with the water, the crown, the vajra, and the bell.
Grant the identity of the five families, the five wisdoms,
the vow, the prophecy, and the permission.

Give relief, and accomplish the vajra master.
Confer the secret empowerment, the wisdom empowerment,
and the fourth, the empowerment beyond words,
in the maṇḍala of the supreme secret.

Because they have fully entered the supreme
and beheld it, declare to them:
"Today you are liberated without remainder from all your misdeeds,
for you have been born into the family.
You have been declared equal to all the buddhas,
and by this permission, you are relieved."

"Child, always guard the samaya vows
that are the cause of attainments.
The student bows to the teacher, and pledges:
'As the master has commanded,
so shall I accomplish it.
As the Blessed One has taught,
with ornaments, clothing, and jewels,
with the kingdom, sons and wife,
and with my own body as well,
I shall honor the teacher without limit or satisfaction.'"

Today my life has borne fruit.
I, the living, have obtained the great result.
Today I am born into the family of the buddhas.
Now that I have become a child of the buddhas,
I shall perform the benefit of sentient beings without equal.

The fire offering of the pacifying rite accomplishes the attainment.
The feast-wheel offering honors the Saṅgha.
All the sick, the shelterless, and the destitute
shall be satisfied with food.

Propitiate the field guardians with torma offerings,
and make supplication for the fruit of one's own wish.
Following this, the samaya conduct and generation practice
and so forth must flow without interruption.
The yogin should contemplate this.

This is the supreme empowerment of the supreme family, unsurpassed.
O Beautiful One, it is revealed to you so that the fortunate may accomplish it.
If given to those without family, it turns back upon them as poison and brings harm.
Therefore this is the arrangement of the samaya vajra.

This concludes the eleventh chapter, on the empowerment of the disciple, from the King of Tantras, the Manifest Revelation of the Secret of the Blessed One Vajrapāṇi.

Then Vajrā Devī of Joy made supplication: "Blessed One, Vajradhara, what is the method for accomplishing the nāgas of your retinue?"

The Blessed One declared: "Well asked, O Beautiful One, source of the causes of undefiled bliss, supreme mother of the wisdom of selflessness. Listen well, for I shall explain this to you.

Among those born in the family of disciples to be tamed, there are eight great fierce nāgas. Summarized by family, Varuṇa, the lord of waters, abides without fail in samaya. Because one desires to accomplish these, anoint a beautiful maṇḍala in five sections. Meditate on the lotus pavilion with its door adorned with divine banners, its gateposts and garlands most beautiful. At the center, visualize the one who holds the vajra. Before him, generate the activity-servant. On the eight petals of the lotus, visualize the eight great nāgas with their seven hoods, palms pressed together.

The deities are the lords of the earth of the four families, arrayed at the gates and corners in the order of their families. Visualize as offering substances the medicinal herbs of the nāgas. Then, having meditated on the vajra-holder, invite the great nāgas from their abodes:

ehy ehi nāgarāja saparivaura samaya jaḥ jaḥ

Let each one take their own seat.

At the center of the lotus are the lord of waters and the samaya lords without number, white and yellow, palms pressed together, with serpentine feet coiled, bowing to the principal, Vajradhara.

In the east, Ananta, blue.
In the south, Mahāpadma, the great wealth-bestower, yellow.
In the west, Takṣaka, red.
In the north, Karkoṭaka, cause of power, black.
In the southeast, Padma, resembling a white conch.
In the southwest, Mahāpadma, hailstone-colored.
In the northwest, Śaṅkhapāla, the color of madder.
In the northeast, Kulika, the color of dust.

Their tails coiled, they bow to the principal. Of varied form, the four families and the lords of the earth of varied form surround them.

The mantra sequence for these is:

oṃ vajra caṇḍa mahāroṣaṇa vajrapāṇi hṛdayaṃ nāgaya samātikrāma thaṃ udsādanaṃ bhaviṣyati svāhā

This is Vajradhara placing the nāgas in samaya.

oṃ varuṇāya hūṃ phuḥ svāhā

oṃ sphuṭa sphuṭa mahānāgarāja krodha hūṃ. siddhi siddhi sphoṭaya hūṃ phuḥ svāhā. oṃ ananta hūṃ phuḥ svāhā. oṃ vāsuki hūṃ phuḥ svāhā. oṃ takṣaka hūṃ phuḥ svāhā. oṃ karkoṭa hūṃ phuḥ svāhā. oṃ padma hūṃ phuḥ svāhā. oṃ mahāpadma hūṃ phuḥ svāhā. oṃ śaṅkhapāla hūṃ phuḥ svāhā. oṃ kulika hūṃ phuḥ svāhā.

These are the mantras of the nāga kings. One should make offerings to them in sequence and appease them. With torma like a beautiful ocean, the nāgas are satisfied and their malevolent minds are pacified. After that, one should recite the praise:

hūṃ — Great lords of water, bearers of samaya in emanated form,
Infinite, Norgye (Śaṅkhapāla), and the other lords of the nāgas —
dwelling in the vast, deep reservoirs of ocean and river waters,
adorned with varied jewels, delightful and resplendent,
you dwell in inexhaustible, abundant immeasurable palaces.
The blazing diadems of serpent heads, brilliant with jewels,
the charming wish-fulfilling gems that accomplish all desires —
your glory bestows the aims of flourishing for self and others.
Your radiance banishes all darkness beneath the earth without remainder.
Through qualities refined across many eons,
you, the nāga kings, have attained the highest prosperity and sovereignty.
With inconceivable miraculous powers you have achieved the supreme —
we praise your inconceivable qualities.

Receiving the word of the buddhas, you increase the power of faith.
You strive diligently in the practice of dwelling in the buddhas' domain.
Through the union of skillful means and wisdom you work for others' benefit.
To you who accomplish the aims of self and other, we pay homage.

Before the Sugatas (the well-gone buddhas) you made promises and swore oaths
to protect the teaching and care for all beings.
Recall your pledges, your vows, and your samaya commitments,
and through enlightened activity accomplish the aims of self and other.

Furthermore, for the worldly ones:

oṃ — Ananta and Jogpo,
Cause of Power and Kulika,
Norgye's son and Śaṅkhapāla,
Padma and the water deity,
Ocean and Great Ocean,
Śrī's Radiance and Great Radiance,
Great Form, the Coiled One, the Great Serpent —
to the great nāgas with their retinues,
with pleasing samaya substances proper to them:
the three whites and three sweets, healing medicines,
precious silks and lower garments,
the wish-fulfilling tree and the cow of plenty,
various things offered to you —
by these may all nāga diseases be dispelled.

In the future also, do not forsake the sacred Dharma.
Go each to your own abode.

This is the command of Vajradhara. If you transgress it, O nāga kings, your hearts will burn and your heads will split. Aeon-fire will blaze upon your bodies and you will come to suffering. Since that would not be good, do not transgress the samaya.

nāgarāja samaya abhiṣiñca vajracaṇḍa mahāroṣaṇa vajrapāṇi hūṃ phaṭ.

This is the fulfillment of the nāgas' samaya.

If this is done accordingly, nāga diseases will be cleansed in that very instant, and the practitioner will come to ease and happiness. Therefore strive in this practice.

O Beautiful One, as you requested, this has been definitively settled and thoroughly explained.

This concludes the twelfth chapter, the chapter on the nāga practice, from the King of Tantras called the Bhagavān Vajrapāṇi's Tantra of the Explicit Display of Secrets.

The vajra-heroine Vajrāsukhā (Dorje Dema) then asked: O Bhagavān Vajradhara, what are the signs that indicate the arising of diseases from the malevolent nāgas of your retinue?

The Bhagavān declared:

O Beautiful One, your question is excellent.
O supreme mother of wisdom of no-self,
you who are the source of undefiled bliss —
I shall explain to you; listen well.

Among the nāgas there are many classes, including the six families and others, varied in kind. Yet they have the nature of the five elements. Norgye and Śaṅkhapāla are of the royal lineage — the poison of [being] seen: yellow, ruling over earth, their bodies heavy and bloated, with four folds of flesh hanging down in a distinctive manner. Their time of movement is the fifth day at midday.

The seat of the illness descends certainly to the crown of the head. When it falls upon the wind-channels, it afflicts the flesh and bones in between. Having rubbed the hair and performed the bull-remedy, it afflicts the ears, and through connection with the lungs, putrefaction of the flesh arises in the lungs.

Gakyö of the brahmin caste — the poison of thought: red, ruling over fire, hot and tormenting. The body is red, slender, and lean, the eyes, channels, and body dry. Falling in the middle, its time of affliction is the western direction, the evening hour. Moving through the fifteen of the sky, when it falls upon the heart, the heart is joyless and mindfulness weakens. Ultimately it falls upon the head and body, and fever is great. When putrefaction reaches the heart, the tongue cannot taste. Padma Jokpo of the noble lineage — the poison of the breath: white, ruling over water, afflicting with cold and chill. The body's color is white, the skin does not acquire a sheen. Falling as the youngest, it afflicts on the eighth and the eighteenth, in the early morning at dawn — discomfort in the neck and right shoulder. Falling finally to the liver, that is its seat. The stomach does not digest food, the eyes do not perceive forms, the flesh and skin have no sensation, the skin is slightly reddish.

Padchen Tobgyu of the commoner caste — the poison of touch: black, ruled by wind, and therefore light and moving. The body trembles, the mind is agitated, the flesh leaps and shudders as if being struck. The four joints of the limbs ache, and wind disorders and phlegm arise. The upper body aches and putrefies, the lower body seizes, and nasal bleeding also occurs. It afflicts and causes bodily exhaustion in the evenings and the nights. The youngest falls after a day, on the third and the thirtieth. This one, of the gongpo class, through its roughness causes madness, derangement, and violent killing. Finally it descends to the kidneys, and the ears do not hear sound. Through the weakening of the seminal fluid, the fingers of water-sprites grow thin. The pledge of the water deity is the sky, and so it pervades everywhere. Whatever arises as described above, and knowledge of the time being absent, the spirit flags. The body's vitality diminishes and is lost, presenting varied symptoms. This is difficult to cure; it must be thoroughly ascertained. In particular, the spleen aches and moles appear on the face. The body is infested with many lice, smelling of goat, horn, and grass. The downward-clearing wind exudes the stench of decay. This one descends to the spleen, and blood also appears in the urine. A skilled physician should recognize the individual signs.

In general, whichever of the five has entered,
sequentially these manifestations occur.
Fire, caverns thick with darkness,
mountain grottos with poisonous entrances, pits,
temple precincts, swamps with ugly forms,
herds of animals, lakesides, and steep slopes —
unease of heart and burning sensation
should be known to arise first.

Singing in the evening, the body aching, the mind heavy,
the limbs uncomfortable, itching and scabbing,
pustules and small red rashes coming,
gnawing and itching, the color of flesh diminished.
Like mottled fire, cold as though cold water has been poured,
cold and damp in patches, hot in spots, the flesh mottled in large and small markings.
The flesh throbs, is numb, and seethes with pins and needles.
The eyes are round, hot, and the brows twitch.
The fingernails weaken and grow thin.
The body emits a foul smell, water blisters form,
and the smell resembles a dark lump of rotting fish or putrid water.
The upper body pierces with pain, the lower undergoes diarrhea.
The outer flesh wastes, the complexion fades, and the smell dissipates.
The orifices fluctuate, the vital essences are untimely.

Now, stated once more in brief:

The fruit of the clarity of the sense faculties
is the gateway of the liver — examine the eyes.
The queen's wheel-fortress is a good sign.
The fruit of abiding in the bones
is the beauty of the fortress of the teeth —
examine the color, the lower legs, and strength.
The fruit of strength in the marrow
is the array of copper-colored nails —
examine the mode of arising, color, and roots.
The fruit of blood and flesh
is pervading pride —
examine how it clings, the color, and the changes.
The fruit of blood dependent on warmth
is the fluid of a woman's secret place —
examine the color, the kuya vessel, and the form.
The fruit of the vital essence in the lower legs
is the root — examine the mode of abiding and the place of arising.

If all of these have their signs,
how could even I have the power to cure?
When the sequential stages of three, four, five, and so on are present,
the cure is to be effected through mantra and medicine.
By means of the seat of affliction, inner and outer, and their connection,
examine carefully and then proceed with the cure.

This concludes the thirteenth chapter, the chapter on examining the afflictions caused by nāgas (Skt. nāga), from the King of Tantras, the Tantra of Vajrapāṇi Manifestly Revealing the Secret, the Blessed One.

The heroic lady Vajra Bliss then made this request: Blessed One, what is the ritual procedure for curing nāga afflictions belonging to Vajradhara?

The Blessed One declared:

Beautiful one, well have you asked.
O mother who brings forth the cause of undefiled bliss,
supreme lady of wisdom free of self,
I shall explain — listen well.

For one seized by a wandering nāga,
there are two cases: survivable and not survivable.
When the six great definitive signs are all present,
what can even a skilled physician do?
It has arisen from karmic maturation.

For those others whom one wishes to heal,
there are two possibilities: difficult and easy.
The first proceeds by the sequence of mantra,
the second involves the connection with substances.

The practice of mantra, the first approach:
receive empowerment, accumulate the accumulations, purify the continuum,
and then perform approach practice through the threefold sequence.
For some, that alone is sufficient to pacify the affliction.
If the potency is great,
apply the fierce secret armour of protection.
For some, that alone suffices to restore life.
If the harm is severe,
recite alone the nāga's own fierce mantra —
for the sky-nāgas pierce to the very heart.
Connect the second vidyā below,
draw a vajra-wheel at the navel,
and with focus upon it, accomplish the practice.

When the type of poison becomes clearly manifest,
identify the earth-poison within the vajra.
The joyous, lineage-holding fire mantra,
and the brahmin's yantra, are auspicious.
The water yantra for fire —
with that mantra, strike it down.
Since the wind is the cause-force of great space,
apply the earth yantra to the navel.
Since the lotus-holder is water,
strike it down with the wind-wheel.
Apply each one's own colour as the fierce force.
Recite the root and heart mantras together, twined.
Combine with antidotal, opposing substances —
whatever antidote serves as the deity's fierce force,
know this in accordance with oral instruction.
If there is no ease even when the yantra is complete,
separate the royal trunk and the moon.
Understand the time-measure and the count of days in sequence
by connecting them with the signs.
When the signs reverse in succession,
the skilled practitioner should perform the appropriate fire offering.
Cut with fierce outer sequential practice,
cut with the mantras of deities and substances,
and by suppression, tame the extreme of the action.
The sequence of the peaceful and the fierce
the skilled one should examine and rely upon.

This concludes the fourteenth chapter, the chapter on dispelling nāga afflictions, from the King of Tantras, the Tantra of Vajrapāṇi Manifestly Revealing the Secret, the Blessed One.

The heroic lady Vajra Bliss then made this request: What is the procedure for performing the torma (Skt. torma, the ritual offering cake) of Vajradhara?

The Blessed One declared:

Beautiful one, well have you asked.
O mother who brings forth the cause of undefiled bliss,
supreme lady of wisdom free of self,
I shall explain — listen well.

Drawing the maṇḍala, fire offerings,
recitation, sessions, consecration,
empowerment, banishing and destroying,
honouring spirits, garuḍas, yakṣas,
nāgas, and local protectors —
whatever of these is practised without a torma,
that action either bears no fruit
or obstructions will arise nearby.
Therefore, I shall teach the ritual of the torma.

One who possesses divine yoga
should gather the most excellent provisions.
Below the torma, visualise the deity of wind:
blue in colour, arisen from the seed syllable of wind,
accompanied by a moving fan of wind.
Above that, visualise the mouth of a vulture —
conceive of red, arisen from wind.
From a triangular huṃ, three vajras —
make them firm through body, speech, and mind.
Above that, from the syllable a, a great lotus,
vast, white, and slightly red.
This is the measureless mansion, the vessel of the dharmadhātu.

Then from the syllables of purification,
visualise: bruṃ, aṃ, dzraṃ, baṃ, hūṃ.
Again from the five purified meats,
visualise: māṃ, lāṃ, pāṃ, tāṃ, bāṃ.
The perfectly pure wisdom of the five mothers,
this is the perfectly pure amṛta wisdom.
Through the practice of purifying, blessing, and connecting,
bearing the sound of the three syllables —

It blazes bright as the sun with the great taste of bliss,
the hue of the rising sun it becomes.
Born of the vajra, it is the colour of quicksilver —
this is method and wisdom, non-dual.

From the seed syllable arises a lotus throne,
vast, holding the vajra.
The guardians of the directions and the rest are invoked and dissolved.
Fingers interlaced, middle fingers joined at the tips —
the thumbs rest firm upon the vajra.
Place it at the site of the desired accomplishment,
and offer with this mantra, bearing the sound of hūṃ:

Om indra-ja ma-ja-la, ja-kla-bhu-ta-bahni, ba u-rakṣa, canda-sujja, ma-da-pa-ppa, ta-la-va-tāle, aṭṭha-sap-svāhā. Idaṃ va-li-bhuṃ-ji-gha-phulla, dhūpa, māṃsa, paṃga, ambha, kajja, sap-sāha, khaṇṭi-khuṇi-pheḍa-ga-da-svāhā. Om a-kāro mukhaṃ sarva-dharmāṇām, ādyānutpannatvād — om āḥ hūṃ phaṭ svāhā.

This is for the retinue to understand.

Particularly, the offering to the Great Garuḍa:
offer with fish, flesh, the rosary of frills,
with milk and with serpent-flesh.
For the yakṣas, fresh meat
and ale must not be absent.
Goat's blood and fruit of the tree are also offered.
For the nāgas: the three whites, the three sweets,
medicinal herbs, and various fine silks are offered.
Having presented one's own awareness,
perform whatever praise and supplication is fitting.

Hūṃ. You accomplish all benefit for sentient beings.
Grant the accomplishments that accord with our wishes.
Even after you have departed to your own abode,
when you are once more invited, at that time come again.
Om vajra-samaya muḥ — so say.

If one is not separated from these offerings and burnt offerings,
whatever the eight accomplishments — pacification and the rest —
one will attain the supreme result.

This is the fifteenth chapter, teaching the ritual of the torma offering, from the tantra king called The Blessed One Vajrapāṇi's Manifest Teaching of the Secret.

The ḍākinī Vajra Bliss requested: She addressed a petition to Vajradhara: "What is the ritual of my own sādhana?"

The Blessed One declared: "O beautiful one, well have you asked.

your excellent sādhana

Those of the present likewise teach it,
those of the future will teach it as well,

A person who possesses the awakening mind,
in a place of great seclusion,
making offerings, delighting in the teaching, drawing near —
worship the Buddha, the Vajra-Holder.
Cultivate in sequence the four divine abodes of Brahmā.

Om svabhāva-śuddhāḥ sarva-dharmāḥ svabhāva-śuddho'ham.

Meditate on the intrinsically pure nature.

Om śūnyatā-jñāna-vajra-svabhāva-ātmako'ham.

Meditate on the utterly pure, inexpressible wisdom.

Guarding within that measureless mansion,
dwelling upon the beautiful lotus,
in the pure palace of the dharmadhātu source,
the pure glory of sun and moon —

from the syllable baṃ arises a goddess.
Her body is red-green, with a single face.

She stands in dancing posture, beautiful with ornaments,

blue, with one face and two hands,

To the right, Buddha Locana, yellow,
one face, two hands — in the right
she holds a jewel, in the left a skull-cup.

To the left, Samaya Tārā, green,
right hand holding an utpala flower and a skull-cup.

To the rear, white, red, and yellow robed,
her hands holding a lotus and a skull-cup.

All of them are wrathful, standing in dancing posture,
adorned with precious bone ornaments.

They accomplish the supreme, purify illness,
protect, pacify, and enrich according to desire,
hold one's own life-force — the activities of magnetising —
and at all times also offer primordial wisdom.

The mantra for the certain teaching of the awareness:

Om vajra-sukhā buddha sarva-paśaṃ kuru suraka hūṃ phaṭ svāhā.
Om śaṃkare śānti-kare, ghuṭṭha ghuṭṭha ghuṭṭhini ghātaya ghātaya ghuṭṭhini svāhā.
Om ruru sphuru jvala tiṣṭha siddha-locanī sarva-artha-sādhani svāhā.

Om akaṭe vikaṭe nikaṭe kaṭaṃ kaṭe karoṭaṃ vīrye svāhā. Om tāre tuttāre ture mama āyuṣe svāhā.

Each and every activity connected to one's own aims
is accomplished through hundreds of thousands of recitations of the mantra.
Other accomplishments without being separately pursued —
it is you, O Goddess, who perform them.

This is a supreme secret,
higher even than what is most secret.
Therefore it is taught to you, O Beautiful One.

This concludes the sixteenth chapter, the chapter of the detailed explication of the accomplishment of the mother, from the King of Tantras, the Tantra of the Blessed One Vajrapāṇi's Manifest Display of Secrets.

The ḍākinī Vajrasukhā asks: What is the manner of accomplishing the various activities of Vajradhara?

The Blessed One declared: O Beautiful One, your question is excellent.
O source of undefiled bliss,
O supreme mother of the wisdom of selflessness,
I shall explain to you — listen well.

The practitioner who desires complete excellence
should first please me.
Then on a desirable object, draw a wheel.
Inside it, write the name of the wealth one desires.
Draw a hook and an arrow, and recite this mantra:

Om caṇḍa mahāroṣaṇa. Tadyathā: Om cale pracale lalālute ya. Deva kāmahākāla anacara ya visī svāhā.

With ten thousand recitations, whatever one desires will come. This is the hooking of a lump of wealth.

Visualising oneself as Vajradhara, make a wheel from a palm leaf in the form of Mount Meru. In the corners, between the hūṃ and the hrī, write the name of the one to be accomplished. Write the mantra at the hub. Write the mantra also on the spokes. Press it down with the left foot and recite:

Om caṇḍa mahāroṣaṇa camuṇḍi dumakeshi hrī. Hana hana hri. Paca paca hri. Deva dattā cegemo me paśiri svāhā.

With ten thousand recitations of this, one hooks a foot.

Om caṇḍa mahāroṣaṇa gaga arigrkatha tari svāhā.

Recite this mantra, rinse the mouth, then mix the bile of a horse with the juice of the dongro plant, make it into the form of a vajra, insert it into the navel wheel, and hide it at the enemy's door — they will die.

The power of accomplishment that comes from recitation —
entering fully into Vajradhara,
apply the vajra wheel-device
to the clay-smeared hands of the potter.

Om caṇḍa mahāroṣaṇa cegemo mukhate thaṃ tisagha tshānating ghanda nating laṃ ghāṃ mūtra murīṣeṇaṃ cegemo bhandhāmi svāhā.

Recite this mantra ten thousand times and press it into the house —
one will certainly be victorious over all adversaries.

Om vajra caṇḍa mahāroṣaṇa nama ni khaberāya ciricirikarta tune muśitun te bandhāsiri khali svāhā.

Write it on a vajra in blood.
Whoever conceals it in the four directions —
it binds shut the mouth of a mouse.

Om caṇḍa mahāroṣaṇa nantunate sirisiri khilikhili svāhā.

Write it on a tiger skin.
Whoever is hung with it — a wolf will guard them.

Om caṇḍa mahāroṣaṇa maṃ ra rakṣato. Nāśakarāya svāhā.

Recite over the fruit of a tree and hide it in a fence —
it will avert the harm of monkeys.

Om caṇḍa mahāroṣaṇa sarva stambhaya dumbhe svāhā.

Recite this over a mixture of honey and molasses and smear it on —
bears will flee.

Om caṇḍa mahāroṣaṇā pārāha dhīnaṃ druṃ druṃ dte svāhā.

Take poison, salt, necklace-plant, white mustard, soil from a hole, datura, kalbi, the bile of a buffalo, and the blood of a wolf. Mix them together, make eight vajras, and conceal them in the four directions — harms are pacified. This is the excellent boundary-cutting.

Om vajra caṇḍa mahāroṣaṇa bhidha hāīni kaṃ bhe svāhā.

If this mantra is recited one hundred and eight times,
this protection too will cut the boundary.

Om biti pina hī bhriṃ piṭa biṭa hūṃ hūṃ svāhā.

Recite this mantra over whatever bile there may be,
and add it to the water that a buffalo drinks —
the curds will all be ruined.

Gold untouched by fire, turquoise without a flaw, pearls without blemish — write the wrathful syllable khoṃ in gold.

Bala, muzi. Mix together the toxin of a btsan demon, gugul resin, and the fur of a monkey, and recite over them:

Om vajra caṇḍa mahāroṣaṇa garuḍa cale cale durudra māra ya. Vajra bhati garuḍa bege hūṃ rija prija nāga rāja māra ya hūṃ phaṭ.

Recite this mantra ten thousand times, and if it is bound to the body, all nāgas will flee.

Om padmākṣa padmākṣa varuṇi hūṃ phaṭ svāhā.

Recite this over clean water and wash the eyes with it — all eye diseases will be purified.

Cardamom, nutmeg, nāga kesara, and the fruit of aśoka — recite this mantra over them, and make a dot on the forehead with a needle: one will become pleasing to all.

Om vajra caṇḍa mahāroṣaṇa jaṃ bhe moha ya hrī svāhā.

Recite this one hundred and eight times.

For protecting a newborn child:
place gravel inside a bowl,
and within the cross-shaped center of the vajra
write Om vajra caṇḍa mahāroṣaṇa che ge mo la rakṣa rakṣa svāhā
along with ten hūṃ syllables, then invoke the deity into it.
Offer a burnt offering and torma as gifts,
and upon that, perform purification.
A wheel drawn with saffron water on the body
is a second protection.

Om vajra caṇḍa mahāroṣaṇa basa ha dolāyī svāhā. Me do yāsā bacina moha yi svāhā. Aes ba ji hāyi na mātayi svāhā.

If one consecrates flowers, clothing, food, and ornaments by reciting this mantra and then gives them, one will come under one's power.

Om vajra caṇḍa mahāroṣaṇa nama saranāmtadyathā. Dhara da mahādhana dhādhaḍa duḍe de svāhā.

Consecrate sand and ash by reciting this mantra and scatter them in a field — living creatures will not eat the crops.

Om vajra suṣadhe bhiśuddha allī bavara culice ra sagagandhasa pa mi habandha li sa svāhā.

Recite this mantra seven times each over a vajra-wood implement, a dali plant, and the three types of arghya offering, and insert them under the ear — no thief of any kind will be able to cause harm.

A phurba made from human bone:
the embodied form of an obstructor, with one face,
four hands holding a hammer, a lasso,
a weapon, and a three-pointed spear.
Cook these inside toxic blood, salt, black nightshade, and rock salt mixed with butter.
Wrap it in cloth inscribed with the great-power mantra.
Concealed at the door of a home, it will bring death.

Om vajra krodha mahābala hana daha paca bidhnaṃsaya. Utsādaya māra ya hūṃ phaṭ.

On the skull of a two-headed snake, write the enemy's liṅga on the tongue of a rabid dog, insert it beneath a horse's hoof, recite this mantra, and then conceal it at the person's door — they will be driven away or driven mad.

Om vajra caṇḍa mahāroṣaṇa che ge mo muptaya phaṭ.

Recite this ten thousand times.

Make the shape of a vajra from leaves and bark, and insert a karmic awareness-syllable at the center. Tie a lock of hair from a hero who died in battle at the waist and wind it around the head — obstructors will flee.

On the wing of a crane, write within the inner field of the vajra at the center the mantras of body, speech, and mind along with the name of the one to be protected. Around the outer border write the vast activity mantras. Near that, write the mantras of those who closely demonstrate the teaching. Around the outer border of those, write the wisdom mantras. Around the outermost border, draw an assemblage of fire. Roll it up and bind it to the body — no harmful spirit of any kind will find an opening.

The soil from a rat hole facing east,
the bones of a little owl, and langka grass —
pour them into the cavity of an iron door-lock, and then recite:

Om vajra caṇḍa mahāroṣaṇa kurtri sanā karti tsums dang phuṭ phuṭ svāhā.

Recite that mantra one hundred and eight times.
Stir it again and again with a bamboo stick —
there is no doubt the iron lock will spring open.

Burned with charnel-ground charcoal,
what was previously impure becomes pure again.

Inside a triangle, write the name of a military commander.
Combine it with the mantra, and drive a siṅdeṅ wood phurba into the chest —
assuredly that very one will become confused.

If the fat of a black rat,
the horn of a shacanra,
and the fur of a black dog are burned —
the various forms of grain will multiply.

The sequence of rites and so forth —
however many are inconceivable —
