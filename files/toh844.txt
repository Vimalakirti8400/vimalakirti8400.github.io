The Root Tantra of Accomplishment through Worship and Praise of the World (Lokastotrabūjatantra nāma Nopikasantakaṃ)

The Root Tantra of Accomplishment through Worship and Praise of the World, known in Tibetan as Jikten Chötö Drubpa Tsawé Gyü and given a Sanskrit title that the Tibetan transmits in an irregular form beginning Lokastotrapūja Tantra, is Toh 844 in the Kangyur, where it belongs to the collection of Ancient, or Nyingma, Tantras. It is a tantra spoken by the Blessed One Vajradhara, the great secret embodying the body, speech and mind of all the tathāgatas, who in the palace of Akaniṣṭha tames the haughty and powerful chieftains of the world, such as the māras, yamas, nāgas, yakṣas, king spirits and tsen, and binds them to protect the teaching. Across eleven chapters it sets out how these worldly spirits are empowered, entrusted with their duties, and propitiated through offerings and praise, describing the signs of successful practice in experience and dreams, the substances and supports proper to each class, and the rites they are charged to carry out. It closes by reminding its audience that these beings are not truly separate from awakened mind, and that the practice culminates in the dharmakāya beyond all dualistic grasping.

In the Indian language: lokastotrabūjatantra nāma nopikasantakaṃ
In the Tibetan language: The Root Tantra of the Practice of Worldly Offering and Praise

Homage to the Blessed One Vajradhara.

Thus did I hear at one time. The Blessed One Vajradhara was dwelling in the palace of the king of the gods in the abode of Akaniṣṭha. He is the great secret, the embodiment of the body, speech, and mind of all the tathāgatas. He perfectly accomplishes every last enlightened activity of the body, speech, and mind of each and every buddha of the three times. He strikes terror through his might, strength, fearlessness, mastery, and form, and he swiftly tames the ferocious, the violent, the arrogant, the powerful, and those who are hard to withstand. He was drawn forth from suchness, the heart essence of the mind of all the sugatas. He is the chief bodhisattva great being, who has fully entered the great ground of final culmination; the great lord of the mighty families of gods belonging to the desire realm and the form realm; and the great leader of the yakṣas of the abode of Aḍakavatī and the abode of Mount Meru.

Various light rays, the magical display of the body, speech, and mind of the buddhas who abide throughout the three times, were sent forth, and he was exhorted with these words:

O Lord of Secrets, Vajradhara!
You were empowered by all the buddhas
To guard the sacred teaching
As lord of might and strength.
Look to that great pledge (samaya),
And from the secret of body, speech, and mind,
With the supreme form that terrifies the terrifying,
Together with fierce speech,
Unleash your force with a ferocious mind;
And, to tame all who are hard to tame, without exception,
Through emanations, magical displays, and enlightened activities,
Please enact the play of your former vow!

As soon as he had been exhorted in this way, the Blessed One knew that the time had come to perform the activity of the buddhas. Although his own body, speech, and mind did not stir from the peaceful nature of reality just as it is, he looked upon the violent ones who were the objects of his taming, and throughout every world system of the ten directions, reaching as far as the limits of space, there appeared inconceivable magical displays of his body, the form of a great wrathful one, as follows.

His body is dark blue, and he has nine faces. The faces on the right are dark brown, baring their fangs; the faces on the left are dark maroon, their features drawn tight; the central faces are dark blue, wrathful and fearsome. With the blazing vajra in his right hand he stirs the skull cup of blood in his left, and drinks. In his eight lower right hands he holds the severed heads of the great gods he has liberated, and in his eight lower left hands he holds the heads of the eight great nāgas; these too are complete with their flayed hides, the bases of fortune. His nine right eyes are rolled upward, his nine left eyes gaze sideways, and his nine central eyes glare downward in anger. Fierce wrathful furrows are gathered on his brow. From his nostrils the fierce wind of existence storms forth; from his mouths blazes the fire of the eon's end. His red fangs are bared up into the realm of space. His mustache and beard gather like clouds. The dark brown locks of his hair blaze and coil up to the peak of existence. From his ears comes a great and vicious roar. His limbs are wound about with clusters of snakes. He wears as his garments the liberated māras and rudras and the hide of an elephant, and a tiger skin is wrapped around his lower body. He is adorned with dots and streaks of blood and grease, and his body is smeared with charnel ash. He is adorned with garlands of skulls, dry and fresh, dripping with flesh and blood. With his eight feet he tramples Brahmā, Viṣṇu, Maheśvara (Śiva), and Śakra (Indra), together with their consorts. He is in union with a great consort, supremely ferocious and terrifying, whose form matches his own.

Countless and beyond all telling, tens of millions of these came to be, like a full sesame pod, and all of them uttered the fierce sound of speech oṃ vajra caṇḍa sarva duṣṭāntaka hana daha paca hūṃ phaṭ. From each one of them, too, he caused inconceivable emanations, and emanations of those emanations, to appear.

The arrogant ones of the world systems, namely the hosts of gods, the māras, yamas, rākṣasas, yakṣas, planetary māras, nāgas, king spirits, ging spirits, the great mātṛkās, the hosts of ḍākinīs, the rudras, the tīrthikas, the lords of life, the lords of wealth, the lords of longevity, the lords of mantra, and the lords of existence; and furthermore the arrogant ones of the world who dwell in all the world systems, in the great charnel grounds, in the great lands, in the borderlands of Jambudvīpa, on the kings of mountains and on the small hills, in the oceans and the streams, in the pārijāta trees, in the great forests, and in inconceivably many minor places, abiding in male and female forms, chiefs and retinues and especially those who roam, together with their hosts of servants and attendants, came forth from their own abodes in haste, alarmed, with minds full of fear and dread, and with minds subdued. Like tufts of cotton tossed by the force of a fierce wind, they arrived helplessly before the Blessed One.

The arrogant ones dwelling throughout all the world systems of the ten directions, and in particular in the Sahā world system, in the four continents, in the great ocean together with the nāga abodes of the pleasure waters, on great Mount Meru, among the hosts of yakṣas of the seven mountains, in the abodes of the gods in the desire realm, together with the form realm and the formless realm, in the hells, among the hungry ghosts, in the birthplaces of animals, and on the lands of Jambudvīpa, each saw, wherever he was, a great fierce form of immeasurable splendor, which others could not approach, present before him. All of them, each separately, went for refuge to their own great chieftains. The great chieftains, together with their retinues, went for refuge to that Lord of Secrets in his form of great terror. All of them displayed, without exception, their own forms, wealth, retinues, ornaments, mounts, abodes, comforts, and particular activities, and, bringing forth the heart essences of their names, they spoke:

"mahādeva ejaḥ umadeva ejaḥ dhudra maduphrag jaḥ dunti madu jaḥ." With these words the great lord of the world, Maheśvara (Śiva), with his consort, together with the male and female māras, each offered his own name.

Then the leader of the yamas, together with his own host, offered each his name together with his life, saying: "hrīḥ ṣṭi kriṣṇa yama preta jaḥ mama yamuntri bhitri jaḥ."

Then the chieftain of the rākṣasas offered the heart essence of the names of himself and his retinue: "rakṣa raśaramati rakṣa maśuti jaḥ."

Then the chieftain of the yakṣas offered the heart essence of the names and lives of himself and his retinue: "yakṣa kuberakṣa tame yakṣi maśa jaḥ."

Then the chieftain of the worldly yakṣa gods, the Lord of the Snowy Mountains (Himavat), offered his own name: "gaṃ bhabhatra dheva taruti jaḥ."

Then the leader of the minor kings of the world, the lord of the thirteen divine the'u spirits, offered his name: "gaṃ tapaśali bartri saṃkaraśa jaḥ."

Then the chieftain of the general māras, together with his own retinue, spoke the heart essence of their names, offering: "du spraśaṃ ja akaru kaṃ ca jaḥ."

Then the mistress of the hosts accomplished from wisdom and from karma and of the samaya-bound, She Who Gathers Existence under Her Power, together with Ekajaṭī and all the hosts of ḍākinīs, offered each the heart essence of her name: "mama trag mutra raca ekajvaṭi ruru ro tiṣṭa bhatto retu mati kha muntra paca lameśa jaḥ."

Then the mighty gods of the world and the great leaders of the rudra class offered each the heart essence of his name: "deva abhiśutri rutra niji haṃ śaṃ tri jaḥ."

Then the king of the obstructors of the world, the tīrthikas, and the hosts of māras and mu spirits offered each the heart essence of his name: "vināyaka tirthi taka du mun mun paḥ jaḥ."

Then the great chieftains of the hosts of the lord of life of the world, of Vajrin, lord of the yakṣas and nāgas, of the great king of the vital soul, and of Mahākāla, lord among the gods, offered each the heart essence of his name: "giṅkara tri pale rāja bajra buru lu ja bajra la rutri ja mahākāla yakṣi jaḥ."

Then the great chieftains of the nāgas and of the planets and stars of the world brought forth the life essence of their names and offered: "nāga cita huya nāga me kṣetra hula ghuhe paśa mike hūṃ jaḥ."

Moreover, the haughty and savage lords of the world realms, together with their retinues, offered each their bodies together with their lives: "e anri jaḥ samaya srog breng breng jaḥ jaḥ."

Then the Blessed One revived those haughty ones and their retinues, granted them fearlessness, and spoke these words:

"Alas, you hosts of the world's renowned ones,
Do not be afraid of me.
I am the lord of the enlightened activity
Of the body, speech, and mind of all the buddhas.
Through the compassion and the wisdom
Of the great peace that is the nature of reality,
The three times, the conditioned and the unconditioned,
Are vividly manifest in a single instant.
For the sake of beings yet to come,
I display the secret of various bodies.
You chieftains of the haughty ones as well,
From beginningless time until now,
Have fallen under the power of affliction and delusion;
Through the habitual tendencies of your negative states,
You act unvirtuously with body, speech, and mind.
The suffering of saṃsāra flows on unbroken,
And, having fallen into the great hells,
You may never find a time of liberation.
Therefore, at this supreme present time,
As I and all the buddhas
Bring body, speech, and mind, qualities,
And the activities of our deeds to full fruition,
Listen to the words of our command.
Make your karma and your existence meaningful,
And cast off your unvirtuous habitual tendencies.
Take up the foundations of the training, give rise to the mind of awakening,
And with your might, power, ability, and ferocity,
Act in accordance with the Buddha's teaching.
Protect, as the vidyādharas command.
You who, as kings and lords,
Or bound in body in the manner of servants and attendants,
Are asked for accomplishments: carry out the activities.
If you do not act in this way,
And transgress the secret word,
The continuum of your body, mind, and life
Will be destroyed this very moment."

Having declared this, he bound them by a sacred oath and established them in it.

This is the first chapter, the introduction, on fully taming the hosts of worldly chieftains.

Then the gathered retinue of haughty ones, Maheśvara, the great lord of the gods of the desire realm, with his consort, and the others, addressed the Blessed One, the great Lord of the Secrets of body, speech, and mind of all the tathāgatas, with these words:

"Alas, Blessed One, Lord of Secrets,
Wrathful one who abides in the world,
When anyone, through attachment, aversion, craving, and grasping,
Makes offerings to us and relies upon us,
We are glad, pleased, and delighted.
In accordance with your command,
For those who hold the yogic disciplines,
So that we may be their companions in carrying out activities,
Our respective places and times,
Our own classes, retinues, servants, and armies,
The rites and enjoyments that suit us,
Our attire, our lineages, and our dispositions,
Our elements and our ways of acting,
And the particulars of joining us to samaya,
For the sake of ourselves and of the future
Holders of the way of awareness,
Please explain in full, leaving nothing out."

When they had requested this, the Blessed One, to Maheśvara and the other principal haughty ones of the world, by way of their individual rites, concealing them as one and applying them separately, the definite and the indefinite, the ordered and the mixed, the outer and the inner, the secret and the hidden, the gathered and the scattered, the concordant substances, the heart essences, the marriage bonds, the entrusting, the urging, the dispatching, the repelling, the annihilating, the tormenting, the raising, and various activities, brought forth from the great vajra samaya of body, speech, and mind, the secret of the great play, and declared:

"Listen, you haughty hosts of the world!
In later times, in the age of degeneration,
This teaching of the Great Śramaṇa
Will appear to ordinary beings.
The ways of the śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas,
The bodies of the vajra holders,
And the tantras are clear in perfecting the grounds and paths.
The unelaborated dharmakāya, without meeting or parting,
Is accomplished by training in samādhi.
Through aspirations made in former lives,
You appear as the lords of each of the six classes.
Through the arrogance of karma, you
Are terrifying and make others terrified;
Fierce, and braver even than the brave,
You can be tamed by forceful means,
And will not be tamed by peaceful ones.
The person who holds awareness
Tames all the powerful haughty hosts
And wields power over the three realms as a whole.
Through union nondual with me,
The tasks of the activities are spontaneously complete.
In a solitary place blessed
By a great and most terrifying charnel ground,
By purifying obscurations and gathering merit,
Strive in the rites of repelling hindrances and māras.
On ground of concordant substances, erect a palace;
Smear it with the substances of fierce samaya.
Whether it is furnished with a skull fortress and storehouse shelves,
Or combined with a number of e triangles and notched tallies,
As supports for the body, speech, and mind of the haughty ones,
Arrange them in accordance with the way I teach.
Outside, the gates and so forth bear terrifying ornaments,
And a great charnel ground is first laid out.
Tsara, buffalo, and water ox,
Lion, elephant, and black horse,
Bird, dog, black sheep, and camel,
Tiger, vulture, dog, and black pig,
Māra bird, makara, fish, and turtle,
White vulture, black vulture, white horse and red,
So bird, mule, and cattle:
Banners, together with flesh, blood, and hearts,
Are laid out in the places that suit each one.
Fine grain, food offerings, and samaya substances,
Amṛta mixed with flesh, blood, and hearts,
Strung together with the essence of precious things,
Are set out in equal number, or in the four directions.
Parasols, banners, and canopies,
Pendant tassels, lattices, and fringes
Are auspicious and fitting for this.
From tiger, leopard, black bear, brown bear, and wolf,
Kaṅka bird, charnel bird, and speckled vulture,
Human, horse, ox, and kele,
They are made of flesh, sinew, hide, and heads.
With bows and arrows, spears and khaṭvāṅgas,
Swords, water knives, and razors,
Tridents, ritual daggers, and many-pronged weapons,
Fierce weapons, it is ringed about and filled.
Beautiful and ugly, pleasant and unpleasant sounds,
Foul and rank odors,
Sweet, hot, sour, and rough tastes,
Hot and cold, smooth and rough, warm and cool:
With forms, sounds, smells, tastes, and touches,
Give to them just as they desire.
Then that vajra holder,
With outer, inner, and secret protection,
Annihilates whatever harms and injures,
Purifies his own continuum with the accumulation of merit,
Accomplishes with the mind of awakening that benefits others,
And meditates on phenomena as they abide.
Giving rise to compassion for those who have not seen,
Meditate on the essence, the generating cause, as hūṃ.
From it, the five bases of harm
Are imagined as the five elements of wisdom.
Above them, upon precious Mount Meru,
In the center, is a charnel ground immeasurable palace,
A skull fortress with thirty e traṃ;
Generate the samādhi like a reflection.
In its center, upon sun, moon, and lotus,
And upon four crossed corpses,
Imagine the body, speech, and mind of Vajradhara,
Myself, appearing to all.
The great consort, Tamer of the Three Realms as a whole,
From her nondual union with the principal,
From the light rays of method and wisdom,
Comes forth in the form of nine e syllables;
Emanating and gathering, the curved knife and khaṭvāṅga appear clearly.
Through training the continuum in that very clarity,
It becomes the great principal, the lord of the desire realm,
Together with the goddess, his consort.
Their feet press down a lion and an elephant.
With one face and two arms, dark blue and fearsome,
He wears a dark green garment and a draped cloak.
The great principal holds a curved knife and a heart;
The consort holds a khaṭvāṅga and a skull cup of blood.
From the heart of the nondual father and mother,
From yama dunti rakṣa yakṣa,
In the four directions, the chieftains of the māras, yamas,
Rākṣasas, and yakṣas,
Together with their consorts, are vividly emanated.
From grahe nāga,
Above and below, the chieftains of the māras, nāgas, and planets,
Fathers and mothers, are emanated and generated.
From deva trija dutri,
The god Gangzang and Namgyal
And the chieftain of the general māras are generated in the east.
From mamaṃ jati dhaki,
The Great Mother, Queen of Existence,
Ekajaṭī, and the Swift One,
The chieftains of the female hosts, are in the south.
From rutra abhi haṃ śaṃ,
The chieftains of Lhajin, the red tsen, and the ril tsen
Are generated in the western direction.
From bitra tithi rmudu,
The chieftains of the obstructors, tīrthikas, and mu māras
Are generated in the northern direction.
From triyaṃ ratri satri natri,
The lord of life of existence,
The yakṣa, kinsman of the nāgas, king of the māras,
The body god, Secret Vajrasādhu,
And Mahākāla, lord of the Dharma protectors,
Are generated at the four gates, east, south, west, and north.
In the outer courtyard, the haughty ones
And their many servants and retinues
Are generated as those who obey commands and await orders.
Each is to be indicated by his own mantra.
They are clear in the manner of sovereigns,
And from the abodes of each of the worldly ones themselves
They are invited by light
And swiftly summoned with the secret essence.
Like the manner of the samādhi,
Dissolve them into the bodies of their own images,
And the actual beings and the images merge.
oṃ āḥ hūṃ oṃ vajra caṇḍa sarva duṣṭāka hana daha paca hūṃ phaṭ
Bless them as endowed with body, speech, and mind,
And let Vajradhara empower them.
From the four gates of the charnel ground,
The eight classes, who obey commands and do the work,
Pay homage with respectful body and speech.
The hosts of outer, inner, and secret sense pleasures,
Together with those of harmful and malicious intent,
Both corporeal and incorporeal,
Are offered to the haughty hosts to delight them.
Praise them with words that accord with the meanings and symbols
Of the attire of each one's body.
Recounting the qualities of body, speech, and mind,
And their activities, urge their heart commitments.
As the body, speech, and mind of Vajradhara,
Endowed with the full measure of clarity and stability,
Pervading all reaches of space to their very limits,
Clearly visualize your own three places.
Beside each chieftain of the haughty ones
Dwell taming remedies as numerous as atoms.
Beside the abode of each ordinary one,
Imagine a great chieftain likewise.
Obeying commands and awaiting orders, they cannot stir;
They do not act to transgress in the slightest,
And dare not follow their own wishes;
Visualize them accomplishing the aim in a single instant.
Through this, when the great principal's
Approach and accomplishment, endowed with measures and signs,
Have been brought to full completion,
Clearly visualize the individual images
Of the hosts of haughty chieftains,
And recite the essence of each one's name,
Increased with the syllables of cutting and seizing.
By time, measure, number, and signs,
Having kept the measure and accomplished it without error,
Bring it down upon the signs and apply it to the rites.
Since I myself carry out the subsequent accomplishment,
The haughty ones are swiftly brought under control."

Thus he declared.

This is the second chapter, on entrusting the root activities for accomplishing the hosts of worldly leaders.

Then, what is to be fully explained:
For all these great chieftains I have explained,
Inviting them from their own abodes, seating them, pleasing them,
Praising them, and urging their heart commitments
Are to be set forth with these words of recitation:

"From the maṇḍala of the expanse of pure compassion,
Though never moving from the expanse of emptiness, the dharmakāya,
In order to tame the haughty hosts of the world
You display a greatly wrathful, heroic, fierce, and terrifying body.
Delighted, your heart commitment urged, together with your secret consort,
Please come, to tame by force those who have not realized.
Lord of the haughty, chief of the gods of the world,
Goddess of desire, source of great bliss,
Master of the retinue hosts, of fierce might, power, and armies,
Glorious one, wielding fierce, savage, and malicious prowess,
Who follow and carry out the command of Vajradhara,
Remembering the samaya, please come to this place.
bajra sarba duṣṭanta atanaya eja bhyo a gaccha samaya hriṅ hriṅ

In this maṇḍala of the charnel ground, the supreme secret,
Upon a seat of the Great God (Śiva), sun, moon, and lotus,
Blessed One, wrathful king, with majestic, blazing body,
Father and mother, sporting in delight, please be seated.
Leader and chief of all the haughty ones of appearance and existence,
Lord of the desire realm, together with the consort who delights,
Great chief of the hosts of gods and rākṣasas, with your retinue,
Please be seated on seats that suit your activities.
bajra sarba duṣṭan atanaya eja bhyo samaya stita bajra hūṃ jaḥ

Supreme heart essence of the buddhas of the three times,
Emanating from the dharmakāya the form body for the benefit of beings,
Great and mighty without rival, lord of the wrathful ones,
Together with the consort who brings under control, to you I bow and offer praise.
All the victors of the ten directions with their heirs,
Protectors of the world, who by your vows guard the ways of the teaching,
Fierce in activity, who liberate the ten fields,
To the hosts of the thirty chieftains I bow.
bajra sarba duṣṭan atanaya eja bhyo nama ārali hoḥ

To the great glorious one, tamer of all the haughty, father and mother,
These offerings that please body, speech, and mind are offered:
Parasols, victory banners, banners, and canopies,
Garlands, powders, pendant tassels, and fringes,
Incense, flowers, lamps, and cool water,
Beautiful forms, pleasant sounds, sweet smells, delicious tastes,
And smooth touches, all the sense pleasures there are:
Please accept these hosts of offerings shared by the noble ones.
In particular, the secret offerings offered are these:
Flesh, bones, and blood purified as the abode of the three times,
Samaya substances in which the five poisons are accomplished as medicine,
And the actual offering of bali, of supreme power and potency:
Please accept them in a manner in keeping with your compassion.

Following and carrying out the command of the great glorious father and mother,
Maheśvara, father and mother, sovereign lord,
Who, though beyond the world, take on the guise of saṃsāra,
To the thirty chieftains who guard the teaching by command,
These sense pleasures are offered and given:
Colors, white, red, yellow, green, and the rest,
Shapes, round, square, and the rest,
Sizes, large and small, thick and thin, long and short,
The beautiful and the ugly, the lovely and the unlovely,
The sounds of the elements, of interdependence, and of music,
Song lyrics, melodies of praise, and pleasant words,
Scents of incense, medicine, nectar, and flowers,
Smells of burnt offerings and of rot, odors and stenches,
Sweet, hot, sour, and astringent,
Salty, spiced, and bitter,
Smooth, rough, cool, light, hot, and savory,
Together with the hosts of the qualities of each,
I offer them as all you could desire: please accept them.
The five nectars and the five meats,
The five vital organs and the five elements,
The five sense faculties and the five objects,
The five aggregates and the five entrails,
Those who harm, kill, and obstruct,
Who think maliciously and hold wrong views,
Together with the five adverse factions,
I offer each of them: please accept them.
bajra sarba duṣṭan atanaya eca ja bhyo mahā balinta amṛta sukata māṃsa rakta bibata bāsuta lamata idaṃ gṛhṇantu samadu kha kha khāhi khāhi

Blessed One, great glorious tamer of all the haughty,
And great consort, Tamer of the Three Realms as a whole, with your retinue:
Your body's color is unchanging, like a reflection in a mirror,
Clear and nonconceptual, appearance unceasing;
Grant the accomplishment of the spontaneously present, complete five bodies.
Your supreme speech is unceasing, like an echo,
Pleasant and unpleasant, sound and emptiness, the voice of Brahmā;
Your melodious speech pervades space to its limits: grant that accomplishment.
Your supreme mind is free of elaboration, beyond thought, the expanse of phenomena,
Nonconceptual great bliss, method and wisdom,
Effortless and unmoving: grant the supreme accomplishment.
Your qualities, beyond all telling, are manifold;
The mundane and the supramundane, the supreme and the common,
The eight accomplishments and the ultimate: grant these accomplishments.
Pacifying the eight fears and the rest, increasing wealth,
Bringing those of wrong views under control by way of devotion,
And fierce rites that tame enemies and obstructors: grant these accomplishments.
bajra sarba duṣṭan atanaya eja kāya siddhi baka siddhi citta siddhi guṇa siddhi karmma siddhi hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ

The Blessed One, great glorious tamer of all the haughty,
Has nine heads and eighteen arms.
His body is dark blue, wrathful and fearsome.
His three right faces are dark maroon, mouths gaping;
His three left faces are dark brown, fangs bared.
His locks of hair blaze as a mass of fire.
His nine eyes, angry, roll and stare.
From his nostrils the black wind of existence scatters;
From his ears the thunder of the trichiliocosm roars.
His beard and whiskers blaze like fire.
His bared fangs cut the root of the three existences.
With the first of his nine right and nine left hands
He stirs with a vajra the blood in a skull, and drinks it.
In his lower eight right hands
He holds the fortune bases, with their heads,
Of the eight haughty gods whom he has liberated.
In his lower eight left hands,
In skull cups made from the eight great nāgas,
He fills the heart blood of the haughty ones and drinks it.
The eight charnel garments of the hero are complete on his body.
With his eight legs in stances stretched, bent, and dancing,
He presses down the four classes of the lords of the three realms.
The great consort, Tamer of the Three Realms as a whole,
Has one face and two arms and a dark blue body.
She brandishes a vajra and holds up a skull of blood.
Through the activity of your nondual mind,
The haughty ones of the world are pressed into service as slaves,
The eight classes of gods and rākṣasas are dispatched to their tasks,

Next I shall explain this thoroughly.
All these hosts of the haughty ones and their retinues
Are in essence the self-aware mind of awakening,
Never stirring from the unborn expanse.
Because they engage in accord with the ways of the world
And are pleased by offering and praise,
This is called worldly offering and praise.
It is divided into outer, inner, and secret.
All the maṇḍalas of appearance and existence
Together with the Lord of Secrets himself:
Offering and praise in the relative situation is the outer.
The assembly of one's own appearances gathered together,
Arisen from pure samādhi,
The enjoyments that are objects of the senses,
The substances that purify concepts of clean and unclean,
The four of passion and the elixir of grain essence,
Which make the other side appear as nondual,
Giving pleasure in their own state,
And the definitive display of the marks and signs of each:
This is the inner.
The assembly of mind and what is displayed to mind
Is all, from the very beginning, the great maṇḍala,
An unfabricated nature, spontaneously present.
As all the scriptures without exception teach,
There is no other dharma: the object itself
And the self of appearance that grasps it as object
Are realized and understood as nondual.
This is the utterly secret offering and praise.
Emanating and accomplishing from the body is the body;
The wheel of the sound of the ten syllables is the speech;
The self-liberated, unfabricated essence is the mind.
Gods, nāgas, yakṣas, tsen spirits, and māras,
Rākṣasas, planets, and the female classes,
Haughty and vicious, wrathful and fierce,
Swift, dull, and obscure,
Each of the classes of haughty ones and of the expanse:
With Mount Meru, pure food offerings and ornaments,
Grain sheep, heart-tip, and tsaru-shaped tormas,
White and red, rich in essence, make offerings by visualizing them.
As a father, a king, a friend, or a god,
As a mother, a lord, a servant, or a slave:
Such is the way of relying on them.
Greatness, might, and the accomplishment of power,
Increase of wealth, and being sent on errands;
Being gentle in nature, a great refuge, and obedient,
Having fierce prowess and never turning back,
And being very close: these are the good qualities.
Being harsh and harmful to oneself,
Wealth not accruing and things not agreeing,
Being tormented by sudden illness,
Their turning back and auspiciousness declining,
Not heeding instructions and being of no use,
One's own mind becoming harsh and conceited,
One's lineage not ripening and being despised by all,
Aims not being accomplished and authority declining:
These are the faults of not knowing the hidden meaning of the tantra.
The gods are like a lord of the law:
Bind them with the key points and accomplish them.
The māras are like a mighty father,
The tsen spirits like an unequaled friend,
The yakṣas like a chief of the law,
The rākṣasas an executioner who assails life,
The wild planets like a reckless youth,
The nāgas like a poisonous snake in a tree,
And the female lineages mighty lady chiefs:
Accomplish them with the remedy that matches each.
If they are not brought into the fold, they do not heed instructions.
Without the key point of compulsion, they turn into māras.
If the dagger of essence does not strike, they turn back.
If one's own skill is not sharpened, they grow lax.
If their bellies are not churned, their actions are not swift.
Without subduing their perversity, they assert themselves.
If they are not brought under control, they do not heed instructions.
By means of the eight key point instructions,
Accomplish offering and praise and entrust them with activities.
Thus it was declared.
This was the sixth chapter, on the key points of the profound offering and praise of the great chieftains of the world.

Next I shall explain something further.
To apply the chieftains of the world's haughty ones
To wrathful actions:
In charnel grounds and the haunts of vicious beings,
Having first completed the root approach,
Having refreshed the congenial substances of each,
Lay out the house of wrathful action
With thirty triangular shapes.
Outer, inner, and secret; body, speech, and mind;
Blessing the place, fulfillment, and offering,
General and individual praises:
Visualize them with the wish that they be utterly blissful.
Through the power of form, sound, and thought,
With the body and the magical device of the body,
With life and the remedy of life,
From the mind and the essence of mind,
Praise them throughout, outer and inner.
With radiating and gathering light, draw them in and summon them,
And dissolve them into the target of self and other.
When flesh, body, blood, and the rest have been eaten without remainder,
The body, speech, and mind of the chieftains of the haughty ones
Are all pleased by this.
Whatever is targeted is liberated to the full measure.
Through the discipline of samādhi,
Slowness or swiftness depends on the strength of the mind.
Have no doubt about this:
It is the secret word of the great Vajradhara.
If one performs a fierce rite with fire
And wishes to bring about a swift transference,
Build a shaped hearth with kindling of vicious wood,
Sharp, pointed, hot, rough, and foul-smelling.
Kindle it with fire from the house
Of an outcaste, a low-caste butcher, or a lowborn woman without companions,
And to the sweet poison and blood
Of which the image fully partakes,
Visualize again and offer the remainder.
With wrathful words that clearly distinguish the chief and the retinue,
Count them off thoroughly.
With the praises of each lord of the haughty ones,
Together with their key points,
Present and give to them in order,
And urge the sage to complete the activity.
Great ash and wrathful substances,
The three kinds of tainted blood and the three killers,
The wealth of the three jewels free of weapons,
And self-arisen substances that turn back good fortune:
Apply these to the inward gathering and outward radiating,
And with sound, words, and samādhi,
Cast them down and hurl them with the wheel of great power,
Visualizing the haughty ones and their retinues as carrying out the act.
Furthermore, make the offering substances into images
And dissolve them with the visualization as fully taught.
Places of movement, earth and water caves,
Battles of dry and wet, the sites of ruins,
The middle of the ocean and great highways,
Being struck by weapons and carried off by wind,
Falling heedlessly and being destroyed by fire and water:
These are the connections that arise from the effigy.
With a steadfast mind, strike with the seizing dagger,
And hand over the harm doers and their retinues as food.
Until saṃsāra is emptied,
The karmic ging will not be freed.
Those who have not realized a mind of love and compassion,
Followers with wrong views
Who have turned to fierce hatred of the teaching,
Are cut off, liberated, and tamed
By practicing fierce rites in this way.
Thus it was declared.
This was the seventh chapter, on applying the activity of the great chieftains of the world to wrathful actions.

Then, what is now to be truly explained:
When the great chiefs of the haughty ones of the world
Are thoroughly practiced in the proper way,
The signs of accomplishment and of its nearness
Are experiences, outright signs and dream signs;
These will each arise in their own way.
One is joyful and radiant, gains strength,
Bliss and realization, and enthusiasm is born;
The mind gains confidence in what will manifest,
Free of fear, anxiety and doubt,
Potent, realizing activities brought to completion:
These and others arise in sequence.
The body is supple, not troubled by the breath,
Light and stable and especially joyful;
Illness and grahas cannot overwhelm it,
The forces of the elements do not shake it,
It has a fine complexion, lovely and beautiful:
Such things as these arise in the body.
Whatever one commands, others take up;
What oneself and the buddhas have spoken
They do not discard, but follow in harmony;
Whatever one utters, pleasant or unpleasant,
Comes forth like the voice of Brahmā;
Merely hearing its sound brings others under one's power.
A graceful manner of engaging: these are the signs of speech.
Whatever one undertakes is accomplished;
Undaunted, not wavering, not giving up,
One is powerful and unshaken
And fully obtains secret might.
The signs that arise in the domain of the mind
Come before, after and immediately.
Dreams that do not come from habitual tendencies:
Kings, abbots and masters,
The great and mighty, the nobly born, the adorned,
Beasts of prey, birds and animals,
Men, women, children and the like
Coming to one's own dwelling
Is a sign of approaching accomplishment.
Taking on the manner of servants and subjects sent on errands
Is the sign that they heed commands and do the work.
Singing songs from a distant place
And gazing from afar is the sign of their slackness.
Undertaking acts of punishment against oneself
And pursuing retribution is the sign of being repelled.
Wanting food and drink and pressing for union
Is the sign that one has not made amends through confession and fulfillment.
Gathering together and lamenting with sobs
Is the sign of impurity and impaired bodily appearance.
Not heeding one's command
And wishing in turn to make one their servant
Is the sign that they have not been brought under control by the remedy.
Striking with weapons, though in proper measure,
Yet powerless, the deed not accomplished,
Is the sign that the power of the mantra has not been achieved.
Others wanting what one does not have,
And not wanting what one does have,
Is the sign that the offerings, fulfillment rites and substances are incomplete.
When one enjoys flesh, bone, blood and the like,
And one's face is lifted high in exaltation,
It is the sign that the act performed is accomplished.
The enemy host not appearing,
And oneself entering a house without doors,
Is the sign that others have turned it back with evil mantras.
In particular, for each of the eight classes:
Humans, animals, birds and the like,
A black host of males is the sign of māras.
A host of females with fine ornaments is the mātṛkās.
The great and mighty, masks and lions, temples,
Children and monkeys are the sign of king spirits.
Men, horses, hawks, wolves and wild game
Are the sign of rock tsen and mountain tsen.
Animals of watery places are the sign of nāgas.
Buffalo, goats, sheep, the many-eyed,
Darkness and blizzards are the sign of planets.
Well-dressed and wealthy men
Dwelling on mountains and lakes are the sign of yakṣas.
Fishermen, butchers, fowlers,
And the armor and weapons of war are the sign of rākṣasas.
Combine these with the good and bad explained before,
And they become the signs of the particular practice.
Thus it was declared.
This was the ninth chapter, on the signs of practicing the great chieftains of the world.

By outsiders and the like,

In order to fulfill the pledge to the teaching
