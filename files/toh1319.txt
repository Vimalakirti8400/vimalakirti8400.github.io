The Sādhana of Kurukullā (Kurukullesādhana)

The Sādhana of Kurukullā, called in Sanskrit Kurukulle Sādhana and in Tibetan Nyur Dzé Mé Druptap, "The Sādhana of the Swift-Acting Lady," is a short tantric practice manual devoted to the red goddess Kurukullā, the deity of magnetizing and enchantment. It guides the practitioner through the preliminary offerings, confession, refuge and the mind of awakening, the meditation on emptiness, and the visualization of the sixteen-year-old dancing goddess with her flower bow and arrow, her flower hook and her utpala, crowned by Amitābha, followed by self-empowerment and the recitation of her mantra. It closes with a rite for bringing a desired person under one's control, and its colophon says that the practice came from the land of Oḍḍiyāna and was translated by the Indian elder Mañjuśrījñāna and the Tibetan translator Rongzom Chökyi Zangpo. It is Toh 1319 in the Tengyur, where it belongs among the tantra commentaries as one of the many sādhanas, or deity-practice texts, preserved in that section.

In the language of India: kurukulle sādhanaṃ. In the language of Tibet: The Sādhana of the Swift-Acting Lady.

Homage to the goddess Kurukullā.

The place where she is to be meditated upon should be a secluded spot, delightful to the mind, smeared with the five kinds of fragrance. There, on a soft and ample seat, the one who keeps the observances should settle into meditative equipoise.

Then one should visualize at the heart a sun maṇḍala transformed from the syllable raṃ, massed with garlands of flame, and at its center one should imagine a red syllable hrīḥ. From that syllable hrīḥ a net of light rays comes forth and strikes the element of sentient beings dwelling in the ten directions, making them manifest in the form of glorious Kurukullā. Those garlands of light rays then gather back again, and one should meditate that they enter into that very syllable.

Then one should visualize in the sky in front the goddess Kurukullā, like a girl sixteen years old. Her blazing hair is tawny and swept upward, and her head is adorned with five skulls. She is seated upon a corpse in the half cross-legged posture, in a dancing stance. She slightly bares her fangs and curls her tongue. She is adorned with a wheel, earrings, necklace, armlets, bracelets, girdle and anklets. She wears a skirt of tiger skin, and her red eyes roll and move. Her head is adorned with the Infinite Lord, Amitābha. Of her four hands, with two she draws a bow and arrow: the arrow tipped with an unopened red utpala as its arrowhead, and the bow made of red flowers. In the remaining two hands she holds a hook of red flowers and an utpala.

Then, in order to make offering to the Blessed Lady Kurukullā, one should make offering with the five kinds of offerings, flowers, incense, lamps, perfume and food, which arise from the syllable hrīḥ resting upon the sun maṇḍala at one's own heart. This is the offering.

Having made offering, the yogin endowed with supreme compassion should bow down with reverence in body, speech and mind, and pay homage. This is the homage.

Having paid homage, one should confess wrongdoing: "Whatever wrongdoing I have committed, caused to be committed, or rejoiced in when it was done, all these and the like I confess openly in the presence of the Blessed Lady." This is the confession of wrongdoing.

Then one should rejoice in merit. The one who keeps the observances rejoices thus: "Whatever merit belongs to the buddhas and bodhisattvas, in that I rejoice." That is rejoicing in merit.

Then one should go for refuge to the Three Jewels: "To the Jewel of the Buddha, the Jewel of the Dharma and the Jewel of the Saṅgha I go for refuge, for as long as I wander from saṃsāra to saṃsāra." This is going for refuge to the Three Jewels.

Then one should generate the mind of awakening: "In order to lead forth those sentient beings who dwell in the cycle of existence, I shall make the vajra mind my principal." This is called the generation of the mind of awakening.

Then one should cultivate the four abodes of Brahmā. To meditate with a mind that cherishes all sentient beings as one would an only child: this is the meditation on equanimity.

Then, making all of them unobservable by their intrinsic nature and free of body: this is the meditation on emptiness.

Then comes the secret mantra for consecrating as mind only, oṃ śūnyatā jñāna vajra svabhāva ātmako'haṃ; with this the mind is consecrated.

Then, by the power of former aspirations, having looked with understanding to the benefit of sentient beings, the mantra practitioner arises as the body of the deity. This stage of the hidden secret mantra is called the body of the deity.

Then one should visualize in the expanse of the sky a sun maṇḍala from the syllable raṃ, massed with garlands of nets of light rays. This is the rising of the sun. Upon it, from the syllable hūṃ, black in color, fully transformed, and from a crossed vajra, one should imagine the vajra fence, the vajra tent, the vajra binding, and the vajra ground. This is the protective wheel.

In its center one should visualize the source of phenomena, the wisdom shaped like the letter e, white in color. In its center is a red eight-petaled lotus arising from a red syllable hrīḥ. Upon its pistil are a sun transformed from the syllable raṃ, and a corpse. Moreover, upon these is a white moon, full and complete, endowed with the thirty-two major marks. Upon that is a sun maṇḍala, red in color, whose nature is the eighty excellent minor marks. Between these, one should meditate that one's own mind enters into an unblossomed red utpala, transformed from a red syllable hrīḥ.

Then, from the nature of the moon, the vowels and the sun melted into one, one should meditate on the body of the goddess Kurukullā as described before.

Having visualized her, one should meditate also on a sun maṇḍala from the syllable raṃ at the heart, and on hrīḥ at its center. With its light rays the Lord Amitābha is summoned and invited, perfectly worshipped by those bearing offerings, and requested to confer empowerment. The request is abhiṣiñcatu māṃ amṛtanātha. With this, one meditates that vases completely filled with the five nectars of Amitābha's seed radiate from his heart. Then, as he confers empowerment and pours with the vases filled with the five nectars, the vases enter one's own heart; and one meditates on Amitābha at the crown of the head of the yogin into whom they have entered.

Meditating on this at the three times of day, one should recite the goddess's secret mantra. The secret mantra is oṃ kurukulle hrīḥ svāhā. In particular, one should recite it with a rosary of red sandalwood. One should diligently purify one's foods with the nectar of the five wisdoms: when one consecrates food and mouth with this triad, oṃ āḥ hūṃ, by this the secret mantra of the mouth is accomplished.

Then, if the mantra practitioner wishes to bring a man or a woman under control, beginning from the first day of the waxing fortnight he should perform ten thousand recitations. At the three times of day, focusing upon the body of the Blessed Lady, wearing a red lower wrap and a red cloth upon the body, he should imagine that from the red syllable hrīḥ resting upon the sun maṇḍala at his own heart, fully transformed, there arises a red eight-petaled lotus whose root has grown from the mud.

On those petals one should imagine, in order, eight bees transformed from the first eight vowel letters, fierce and swollen, joyful and full of lust. Then, propelled by the breath, they come out of one's nostril and enter the right nostril of the person to be won over, humming and dancing, and inflame that person with longing; and on the petals of that person's lotus too one should behold the first eight vowel letters, slightly reddish in color.

Having beheld them thus, the yogin then draws to the full, with his fist in the aiming posture, the arrow tipped with an unopened utpala as its arrowhead, and strikes the anthers of the lotus. Urged by this, by their own pledge, those bees each carry off a vowel, come out of the left nostril, and enter the yogin's right nostril; and at the heart one should make one's own consciousness and the other's become one.

Then one should imagine that person bound at the neck by the root of the utpala, seized by the womb with the hook and drawn in, unable to bear it. With the blazing one brings the person under control and sets them upon the wind wheel. With a joyful mind the yogin should recite the secret mantra and meditate in this way. The secret mantra is oṃ kurukulle hrīḥ so-and-so me basāmānaya svāhā.

In this way one should recite throughout the night until the full moon. Then, in the morning, that person will come, filled with desire, naked, with hair unbound. Urged by this king of secret mantras, one will also obtain the most abundant accomplishment.

The sādhana of the goddess Kurukullā, which came from the land of Oḍḍiyāna, is complete.

It was translated, edited and finalized by the Indian preceptor, the elder Mañjuśrījñāna, born in the land of Magadha, who went forth at glorious Mahābodhi, and the Tibetan translator Rongzom Chökyi Zangpo.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The Tibetan title, "Swift-Acting Lady," renders the name Kurukullā. "Endowed with supreme compassion" reads ལན་པ as the evident slip for ལྡན་པ. The bodhicitta formula "I shall make the vajra mind my principal" is terse and could also be understood as "I shall become the lord, Vajra Mind." Only equanimity is named among the four abodes of Brahmā, although the description given (cherishing beings as an only child) is closer to love; the text is translated as it stands. "Vajra ground" renders ས་གཞི་རྡོ་རྗེ་ར, where the final ར may stand for the vajra enclosure of the ground. The "Infinite Lord" (མཐའ་ཡས་མགོན) crowning Kurukullā is taken as Amitābha, as is usual in her iconography.  In the mantra, ཆེ་གེ་མོ is the Tibetan placeholder "so-and-so," where the name of the person to be brought under control is inserted; it is translated outside the Sanskrit. "With the blazing" (འབར་བས) is terse: it most likely refers to the blazing fire of passion or the blazing goddess, by which the person is subjugated.
