A Praise of the Blessed One Śākyamuni (Bhagavānśākyamunistotra)

A Praise of the Blessed One Śākyamuni, known in Sanskrit as Bhagavān Śākyamuni Stotra and in Tibetan as Bcom ldan 'das shākya thub pa'i bstod pa, is a short devotional hymn in verse addressed to the historical Buddha. After an homage to the Three Jewels, it extols Śākyamuni as the lamp and illuminator of the world, one who has passed beyond worldly existence yet works tirelessly for the benefit of beings. Its verses celebrate his qualities: his generosity, his ten powers, his skill in taming disciples, the excellent marks of his body, and his golden, fathom-wide radiance, closing each stanza with a refrain of bowing in reverence. This work is Toh 1152 in the Tengyur, where it belongs to the collection of praises, the class of hymns and eulogies composed by Indian masters in honor of the buddhas and other objects of veneration.

In the language of India: bhagavān śākyamuni stotra. In the language of Tibet: A Praise of the Blessed One Śākyamuni.

Homage to the Three Jewels.

You who have passed beyond the world are the lamp of the world;
You work for the benefit of the world, O you of excellent intellect.
You have found the light and cleared away the stains of affliction:
Illuminator of the world, to you I bow.

Delighting in giving, possessing the supreme ten powers,
Taming those to be tamed, you have crossed to the far shore of taming.
Blessed One, worshipped by gods, humans and nāgas,
You who have reached the end of those to be tamed, to you I bow.

Supreme in your marks, you act for the benefit of many beings;
Your fathom-wide radiance is like the color of gold.
You possess a virtuous form, excellent with the major marks:
Unequaled in the three realms of existence, to you I bow.

Having gone beyond, you bring to completion the accumulations of others;
Unmoving, you crush the hosts of opposing adversaries.
You are skilled in the minds and conduct of other beings:
Crusher of hostile hosts, to you I bow.

King of arhats, with afflictions subdued and stains removed,
Pure in livelihood, abiding in your vows and moral discipline,
Supreme god of gods, stainless and free from defilement,
Endowed with supreme compassion, to you I bow.

Having crossed the four floods, you speak the words of the four truths;
Your words of Dharma are a nectar that bestows sight.
With an impartial heart, you hold loving-kindness for all beings;
Abiding evenly in the Dharma, you guide humankind as a charioteer:
Perfected in good qualities, to you I bow.

You are endowed with a sweet and perfectly pure voice;
Your speech is pleasing like the voice of Brahmā,
Complete in its manner of speaking with all the branches of melody:
Speaker of sweet truth, to you I bow.

Empty, signless and free from wishes,
Profound, hard to see and exceedingly hard to realize:
You possess an intellect skilled in entering liberation.
Delighting in the path to liberation, to you I bow.

Wise one whose intellect relies upon causes,
Never versed in the views of the two extremes,
You teach that the fruit of action is precisely its ripening:
Free from the darkness of views, to you I bow.

You have come from nowhere and gone nowhere;
You fully teach that phenomena are always void.
Like a mirage or an illusion, you have no "I" or "mine":
Embodiment of the virtuous Dharma, to you I bow.

Arising and nonarising: you were born nowhere at all;
Arising and perishing: nowhere are they evident.
You abide in the very abode of Dharma, just as it abides:
To the Victor who abides and does not abide, I bow.

Just as things truly are, you speak them rightly;
Ever unmoving, you dwell in suchness.
Your body is like Mount Meru, like a vajra banner:
Unmoving one, equal to Meru, to you I bow.

Here ends A Praise of the Blessed One Śākyamuni, composed by the master Triratnadāsa.
