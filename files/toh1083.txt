The Noble Dhāraṇī Called the Seven Vetālas (Āryasaptavetāḍakanāmadhāraṇī)

The Noble Dhāraṇī Called the Seven Vetālas, known in Sanskrit as the Āryasaptavetāḍaka nāma dhāraṇī and in Tibetan as the Phakpa Rolang Dünpa, is a short protective scripture set on Vulture Peak Mountain near Rājagṛha, where the Buddha is staying with a great assembly of monks and his attendant Ānanda. Non-Buddhist ascetics send seven great vetālas, reanimated corpse-spirits, to harm the Buddha. Finding no way to touch him, they turn instead to Ānanda, who is struck by severe suffering when they make contact with his body. This crisis prompts the teaching of a dhāraṇī, a sacred incantation meant to ward off such malevolent forces and protect those who recite it. The work is Toh 1083 in the Kangyur, where it belongs to the class of dhāraṇī texts, the protective and ritual formulas gathered in the collection's compendium of dhāraṇīs.

In the language of India: ārya sabta petāḍaka nāma dhāraṇī
In the language of Tibet: The Noble Dhāraṇī Called the Seven Vetālas.

Homage to all buddhas and bodhisattvas.

Thus did I hear at one time. The Blessed One was staying at Rājagṛha, on Vulture Peak Mountain, together with a great Saṅgha of twelve hundred and fifty monks, and also with the venerable Ānanda, the śramaṇa who attended him.

At that time wandering non-Buddhist ascetics (tīrthikas) sent seven great vetālas to harm the Blessed One. They went into the presence of the Blessed One, but they found no opening and no hold on him. Having found no opening and no hold, they went to where the venerable Ānanda was, and when they arrived they touched the venerable Ānanda's body. The moment he was touched, Ānanda was overcome by unbearable suffering, by violent, burning feelings that brought him to the point of death.

Then the venerable Ānanda thought: "I am overcome by unbearable suffering, by violent, burning feelings that bring me to the point of death, yet the Blessed One has not thought of me. Though I have fallen into such misery, the Tathāgata has not thought of me."

At that time the venerable Śāriputra and the venerable Mahāmaudgalyāyana were sitting not far from the venerable Ānanda. The venerable Śāriputra and the venerable Mahāmaudgalyāyana heard of the venerable Ānanda's distress. The two of them then went to where the venerable Ānanda was, and when they arrived they said this: "Venerable Ānanda, now that you are experiencing unbearable, violent, burning feelings of suffering, are you saying, 'The Tathāgata has not thought of me'?"

When they had said this, the venerable Ānanda replied to them: "Venerables, the feeling in my body is like a head being split with a saw. What has touched Ānanda is like that."

"Ānanda, do not be afraid. We will lay you on a couch and carry you into the presence of the Tathāgata. The Blessed One will protect you."

Then the venerable Śāriputra and the venerable Mahāmaudgalyāyana laid the venerable Ānanda on a couch, went to where the Blessed One was, and on arriving set him down before the Blessed One.

Then the Blessed One said to the venerable Ānanda: "Ānanda, have you been struck by unbearable, violent, burning feelings of suffering?"

"Venerable Blessed One, it is so."

"Therefore, Ānanda, listen well and attentively, and keep it in mind, and I shall explain. Ānanda, the ones who touched you are seven great vetālas, mighty, hard to tame, harmful merely to behold. Who are the seven? They are the one called Mālādhara, Maṇidhara, Caṇḍa, Caṇḍāla, Peacock Heart, Life Spent, and Blanket Wearer. These seven great vetālas are mighty, hard to tame, hard to approach, of great miraculous power and great might. Ānanda, any single one of these vetālas, if it wished, could fling even Mount Meru, king of mountains, with the big toe of its left foot. Mount Meru, king of mountains, would be shattered to the size of a mustard seed. By looking upward they split things apart. If they look toward one of the directions, death follows. If they look toward the intermediate directions, they burn with fire. It is they who touched you. Even so, through the power of the Tathāgata, through your reverence in hearing the Dharma, and through the blessing of the Tathāgata, your body still lives."

"Blessed One, please remove it completely. Please turn it back. Please bind a mudrā. Please bind it with a thread."

"Therefore, Ānanda, listen well and attentively, and keep it in mind, and I shall teach it.

tadyathā hara mahāgāre ghasaravati dhara dhari dharaṇi chora chora ṇikaroti ti kotta samādhini samara saraṇi sarana kotamati sambhave bhavana nāgare bhadanī sādāniti svāhā

Ānanda, if one recites these words of the dhāraṇī-mantra fifty-two times and ties fifty-two knots, the hearts of all apasmāras and all bhūtas will be struck, and one will be freed from whatever all the grahas have done."

"Yes, Blessed One, please do so. And further, please grant that I not die."

"Therefore, Ānanda, listen, and hold this well too.

tadyathā badcale bale mahābadcale bale bhili bhili mahvabhiriṇī nimirini hana hana hatiri macathā mavicarathā stiri sthiramule mathanirmathani cālani mākanī jambhani vīdāni lhayasvakṛni bhagavati tathāgata vitathe tivighadhare barbrade haretiṣṭha nīlavasani svāhā

Ānanda, these vetālas are destructive, hostile, fierce, and murderous. Against them, one recites this vidyā-mantra twenty-one times over a kemoruka thread and ties twenty-one knots. Then the great bhūtas will be thrown into utter turmoil. Through this all of them will turn averse, will shrink back, and will be calmed; through this all bhūtas will come under control; through this all bhūtas will be completely pacified.

Ānanda, listen, and I shall teach it.

tadyathā dara dara dara kara kara kara kara mahākakkara cara cara cadacara mahācadacara hala hala hala hili hili hulu hulu hire bire hiri kiri bairaibici nikarankari sarbabhūtānāṃ prajambhani nīlabarabasini cacchu cakra cakra ayudhe svāhā

Ānanda, these words completely pacify all yakṣas, all rākṣasas, all secret mantras, pretas, sorcery, and evil. They turn back the plundering of kings and brigands. Against them, if one recites this vidyā-mantra thirty-seven times over a red thread and ties thirty-seven knots, all will be appeased."

"Ānanda, take up these words of secret mantra! Keep them! Hold them well and thoroughly in mind! Put them into practice! Therefore, Ānanda, you should hold them with mindfulness.

tadyathā dhara dhara dhadhara khara khara mahākhara mahābale palanāśanī goṭṭanī kuṭṭanī saṃhāre kuṭṭadaṃṣṭe kakhakhare makuṭṭa dhareṇi mulukka kṣenikakainīke samismaśani kini giriṇi bhogisiri sasābehāḍa kumāraka kṛtesvāsi kinanibibuddhasi bibudhyagṛhāṃbhai kṛtabhairaba bikṛtabhairaba mahābaye kṛtāna harasi kinanamārayasi kinan sarbadebanāgayakṣa gandharbha asura sarba asura sarbabhayaśaman maṃsara svāhā

Ānanda, if this vidyā-mantra is used in a fire offering, or bound with a thread, it destroys, fells, and devours everything within the range of one's wish.

Ānanda, take up the vidyā-mantra that paralyzes all vetālas:

tadyathā samele mahāhahale haradhare ghoradhare dharatti ritti birotti kunatti kutani sadgocani kacari khacari marane huhu mareṇa huhu mareṇa mahāmāladhare nayonamati svāhā

Ānanda, these words of secret mantra are equal to and like a fierce curse. In order to destroy all bhūtas, if one recites them twenty-four times over a thread of clean wool and ties twenty-four knots, no vināyaka will be able to overcome one.

Ānanda, take up these words of secret mantra! Hold them well and thoroughly! Ānanda, this secret mantra cannot be held by just anyone. Therefore, Ānanda, hold it well!

tadyathā cacchara cacchara mahācacchara ciri ciri buru buru mahāburu sambhale mahāsambhale mahābale svāhā

Ānanda, in the world with its gods, with its māras, with its Brahmās, with its śramaṇas and brahmins, and with its beings, I see no one who does not delight in this secret mantra; it is a protection and an escort. This secret mantra is fierce: it burns, it cooks, it binds, it crushes, it blazes. If one recites the vidyā-mantra sixty-one times and ties sixty-one knots, it completely pacifies all evil and cuts off vidyā-mantras."

Then the venerable Ānanda said this to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, excellent! It has been well taken up. Please tie the knots. Please set the seal."

"Ānanda, for that purpose:

tadyathā bhabhare bhare bharaṇiheraprahare harani kuthanitaṭi licchadatti

The vidyā-mantra made by Brahmā is cut off. The vidyā-mantra made by Maheśvara is cut off. The vidyā-mantra made by Viṣṇu is cut off. The vidyā-mantra made by Śakra is cut off. The vidyā-mantra made by the Four Great Kings is cut off. The vidyā-mantra made by the yakṣa general is cut off. The vidyā-mantra made by the moon and the sun is cut off. The vidyā-mantra made by a god is cut off. The vidyā-mantra made by the planets and the lunar mansions is cut off. The vidyā-mantra made from the directions is cut off. The vidyā-mantra made from the intermediate directions is cut off. The vidyā-mantra made from above and made from below is cut off. The vidyā-mantra made by a god is cut off. The vidyā-mantra made by a nāga is cut off. The vidyā-mantra made by a yakṣa and made by a garuḍa is cut off. The vidyā-mantra made by a kumbhāṇḍa, made by a perceiver, made by a śramaṇa, a brahmin, a kṣatriya, a vaiśya, a śūdra, a man, a woman, or a caṇḍāla, made by a barbarian or a mountain tribesman, made by a lion or a snake, made by the mind of creatures that roam and fly, made by an outcaste, made by a Tosalan, made by a Mudaga mountain tribesman, and made by the vidyā-mantra holders of all beings is cut off.

tadyathā gagagāraliphu mahāgagagāraliphu biśephu śaparsiddhephu caṇḍaliphu mahācaṇḍāliphu cogiphu cimiriphu cicimirephu cakrephu cakradharephu kṛṣṇephu kṛṣṇerubephu caṇḍephu sogephu pañcaṭalipu pañcaliphu bhimephu bhipāpalephu piṅgelephu piṅgala akṣephu anantephu samantiphu māśanīphu cchedaniphu bicchadvaniphu svāhā krisani bakṣani mārani pindani bindyanaṃ bakṣani nāśāni khāddyakanaṃ gālabhināśānī dāhāmasi bhasma bhasmanimasani bandhani svāhā

Ānanda, these forty-two great yakṣa generals steal splendor by night.

Ānanda, at sunrise there is a great vetāla called Harmer. In the autumn season it follows the rays of the sun, and whatever those rays touch, once touched by the net of rays, becomes streaked; that one too is cut off.

Ānanda, there is a vetāla called One-Mouthed; it too should be included within this. The one called Seized by the Nāga King should also be included within this. All nāgas, all kumbhāṇḍas, pretas, and all piśācas too are included within this. The garuḍas too are included within this. Vasudhara too is included within this. Vāyu too is included within this. Barobaṭiśirudro, the son of the Illusion-Taker, too is included within this. Heap-Holder and Spear-Holder are likewise included. Likewise, together with their ministers and together with their retinues, they too are included within this.

For a hundred leagues on every side
I have imposed my punishments.
What, then, can the bhūtas do to me?
I have destroyed all who are hostile.
Murderous ones of mighty strength,
the vetālas, what can they do?
They too, by my noose,
are encircled on every side.
By all the nooses, they too
are made dull and utterly stupefied.
Through this, may I accomplish everything,
and may the words of the secret mantra be accomplished!

namaḥ siṅṅhānāṃ siṅṅhyantu mantra bāda sva stirme sarba satvā nāñca svāhā

By whatever power all the buddhas have,
by whatever power the arhats have,
and by the splendor of the sacred Dharma,
may there be perfect well-being!

By the splendor of the noble Buddha, always, against all fears, svāhā.

The rite is this. Having recited it sixty-one times, one should tie knots in a red thread made of kemeru, clean bala, or the like. Likewise, the ritual action should also be performed. Against all bhūtas, against those that cause forgetfulness, those that distract the mind, and the like, against poison that has been given and poison that has been prepared, and against bipeds and quadrupeds, it is supreme, proven, and effective everywhere.

When the Blessed One had spoken thus, the venerable Ānanda and the world with its gods, humans, asuras, and gandharvas rejoiced and praised what the Blessed One had said.

This completes the noble dhāraṇī called The Seven Vetālas.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

Three of the seven vetāla names (Peacock Heart, Life Spent, Blanket Wearer) could not be identified with confidence and are translated literally. The first four are standard renderings of Mālādhara, Maṇidhara, Caṇḍa and Caṇḍāla. The thread type "kemoruka" is transcribed as written; its meaning is unclear. Apasmāra renders the Tibetan "those that cause forgetting."  "Perceiver" renders ཚོར་བ་པ literally ("one who feels"); the intended class in this list is unclear. Several names are translated literally because they could not be identified with confidence: Harmer, One-Mouthed, Seized by the Nāga King, the Illusion-Taker, Heap-Holder, and Spear-Holder (possibly Śaktidhara, an epithet of Skanda). "Mudaga" is a guess at an unidentified tribal name. Barobaṭiśirudro is a name given in Sanskrit transliteration and is reproduced letter for letter. སྟོན is read here as "autumn". མདུད་པ་དོར་དུ་གསོལ is read with དོར as a variant of བོར ("tie the knots"), matching the earlier མདུད་པ་བོར་ན.  Here ཆད་པ is taken in its everyday sense of "punishment," not the doctrinal sense of "annihilation," which does not fit this verse.

ཀེ་མེ་རུ and བ་ལ་གཙང་མ, the materials for the thread, could not be identified with confidence. They are kept as "kemeru" and "clean bala." The second might mean clean hair or wool.

The phrase ཀུན་དུ་འགྲོ་བ་གྲུབ་པའི་མཆོག is terse. It is rendered "supreme, proven, and effective everywhere."

The colophon spells the second Indian preceptor's name བིདྱཱ་ཀར་སིདྷ. This is taken as the well-known translator Vidyākarasiṃha, and the final syllable is assumed to be a scribal slip.
