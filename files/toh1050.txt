The Noble Dhāraṇī Called "Invulnerable to Epidemics and Creatures"

The Noble Dhāraṇī Called "Invulnerable to Epidemics and Creatures," known in Tibetan as Phakpa Rim Dang Sokchak Kyi Mi Tsukpa Zhejawé Zung, is a very brief protective incantation preserved in Tibetan translation. After a homage to the Three Jewels, it presents a single mantra that invokes Parṇaśabarī, a goddess associated with healing and protection from disease. It then gives a simple ritual: the mantra is recited seven times over a bell, and wherever that bell is rung, the place is protected from birds, beasts of prey, insects, rats, and every kind of epidemic illness. The text is Toh 1050 in the Kangyur, where it belongs to the class of dhāraṇī texts, the collected spells and incantations valued for their protective and practical power.

The Noble Dhāraṇī Called "Invulnerable to Epidemics and Creatures"

Homage to the Three Jewels.

oṃ jajare parṇaśabari svāhā namaḥ sidha byāghra rakṣuta rakṣu khathindrā mekha mekha cantiti bataye roga nidīpasataye svāhā

Recite the mantra seven times over a bell. Whoever then rings it in a place will be protected from birds, beasts of prey, insects, rats, and every kind of epidemic disease.

This completes the dhāraṇī "Invulnerable to Epidemics and Creatures."

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The phrase rendered "whoever then rings it in a place" can also be read as "in whatever place it is rung." In that reading, the place itself is what is protected. The sense of the rite is the same either way.
