Homage to the youthful Mañjuśrī.

Thus did I hear. At one time the Blessed One was dwelling in the sky above the open courtyard of a monastery, in a space adorned with an arrangement of seven kinds of precious jewels, together with an innumerable assembly of bodhisattvas.

At that time the Blessed One declared to the bodhisattvas: Sons of good family, for the benefit and happiness of all beings, take up this dhāraṇī called "The Six Gates." It is as follows.

May whatever suffering I have experienced as I wander from birth to birth in saṃsāra not become the characteristic of failing to recognize equality with all sentient beings.

May whatever worldly prosperity and happiness I have enjoyed be shared in common with all sentient beings.

May whatever sinful and non-virtuous actions I have committed be confessed individually through the supreme individual confession, and may none remain unconfessed.

May whatever deeds of māra I have performed be fully known through the supreme full knowledge, and may none remain unknown.

May all the roots of virtue I possess, whether worldly or transcendent, arising from the perfections, become the fruit of the unsurpassed primordial wisdom.

May whatever liberation I have attained bring about the complete liberation of all sentient beings.

May I not remain fixed in either saṃsāra or nirvāṇa.

Tadyathā: oṃ kṣame kṣame, kṣānte kṣānte, dame dame, dānte dānte, bhadre bhadre, subhadre subhadre, candre candre, sucandre sucandre, candrakiraṇi, candravatī, tejovatī, yaśovatī, dharmavatī, brahmavatī, sarvaklešaviśodhani, sarvārthasādhani, sarvārthaśamanī, paramārthasādhani, kāyaviśodhani, vākviśodhani, manaḥsaṃśodhani svāhā.

Sons of good family, therefore, if any son of good family or daughter of good family recites this dhāraṇī called "The Six Gates" three times by day and three times by night, all the karmic obscurations they have accumulated will be purified, and for seven lifetimes hence they will remember their former lives. They will then come to full and perfect awakening in the unsurpassed, completely perfect enlightenment.

The Blessed One, pleased, having declared these words, the assembled bodhisattvas and the sons of the gods of the pure realms rejoiced and praised what the Blessed One had declared.
