The Heart Dhāraṇī of Śākyamuni (Śākyamunihṛdayadhāraṇī)

The Heart Dhāraṇī of Śākyamuni, Toh 860 in the Kangyur, is a very short text devoted to the buddha Śākyamuni. Its Sanskrit title is probably close to Śākyamuni-hṛdaya-dhāraṇī, though the exact Sanskrit and Tibetan titles are not given in the lines available here. After a homage to the Tathāgata, the arhat, the perfectly complete buddha Śākyamuni, the text presents a brief mantra, "oṃ muni muni mahāmunaye svāhā," which plays on the epithet muni, "sage." It then promises that reciting it even once purifies every negative deed accumulated over an immense number of eons. In the Kangyur it belongs to the class of dhāraṇī texts, those compact formulas of recitation that are gathered within the Tantra division and valued for their protective and purifying power.

Homage to the Tathāgata, the arhat, the perfectly complete buddha Śākyamuni.

Tadyathā, oṃ muni muni mahāmunaye svāhā.

If one recites this even once, every negative deed one has committed over eighty thousand times a hundred koṭis of eons will be purified.

This completes the Noble Heart Dhāraṇī of Śākyamuni.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

A koṭi is ten million, so the stated span is eighty thousand times one hundred koṭis of eons. The Tibetan spelling of the mantra, ma hā mu na ye, is normalized here to the Sanskrit dative mahāmunaye.
