The Noble Great Vehicle Sūtra Called Mahābala (Āryamahābalanāmamahāyānasūtra)

The Noble Great Vehicle Sūtra Called Mahābala, known in Sanskrit as the Ārya Mahābala Nāma Mahāyāna Sūtra and in Tibetan as Pakpa Tobpoche Zhejawa Tekpa Chenpö Do, is a Mahāyāna scripture centered on Mahābala, the wrathful protective deity whose name means "Great Strength." The text opens on the great Mount Īṣādhara, where the Buddha is teaching in the presence of the bodhisattva Vajrapāṇi. It then describes the arrangement of a sacred image or maṇḍala, with the Buddha seated on a flaming lotus throne and the four-armed Mahābala at his right, holding a yak-tail whisk, a club, and a noose and making a gesture of homage. Combining sūtra framing with ritual and visualization instructions typical of early tantric practice, the work is concerned with protection and the invocation of Mahābala's power. It is Toh 947 in the Kangyur, where it belongs to the collection of dhāraṇī texts, the incantations and protective formulas gathered among the tantric scriptures.

Thus did I hear at one time. The Blessed One was teaching the Dharma on the great Mount Īṣādhara. Then the blessed bodhisattva great being Vajrapāṇi, praised, extolled, honored and delighted by all the buddhas, was also present right there.

On the left side is the Blessed One, noble Avalokiteśvara, with his retinue. To the right of the Blessed One is Pāṇḍaravāsinī. Below her are Tārā and Bhṛkuṭī. To the left is Hayagrīva, king of vidyā-mantras, together with a hundred thousand bodhisattva great beings, these and others.

Likewise, in the east are the moon, the sun, Dhṛtarāṣṭra and Indra. In the quarter of fire is Agni, surrounded by the great sages of fire. In the south are Yama and Viṣṇu. The Buddha, the Blessed One, with his retinue, is as before.

Then, at that time, the radiance of the light rays of the Tathāgata, the arhat, the perfectly complete Buddha Śākyamuni, blazing all around, passed beyond the great trichiliocosm, the three-thousandfold world-system, and all the abodes of Māra were seen to blaze all around, to blaze intensely, to spread everywhere and blaze until they became a single mass of flame. When this happened, the evil Māra, holding in his hands swords, discuses, bows, arrows and other weapons, produced many obstructive magical displays.

Then, in that very instant, the evil Māra, together with all the young gods of Māra's realm, went with a great magical display of Māra to where the Blessed One was. When this happened, a furious Wrathful One appeared: red in color, his eyes reddish-yellow like honey, fangs bared, unbearable, his tawny hair bristling upward, holding a club in his right hand and in his left a vajra, adorned with snakes, and wearing a skirt of tiger skin. He appeared everywhere toward the east, across world-systems as many as the grains of sand of twenty-one Ganges rivers, and likewise toward the south, the west and the north.

Then the evil Māra and hundreds of thousands of trillions of obstructing spirits trembled before the furious Wrathful One. The evil Māra, stupefied and tormented, was hurled down and, heedless of his life, was bound by the Lord of Secrets Vajrapāṇi with the five bonds of the noose. Then the Blessed One, the bodhisattva mahāsattva Vajrapāṇi, looking with the lion's gaze, spoke to Īśāna, the lord of the bhūtas, the maṇḍala of the Frenzied Wrathful One together with its secret:

oṃ vajra krodha mahābala hana daha paca vidhvansaya jaṭilambodhara ucchuṣma krodha hūṃ phaṭ a āṃ mahāṃ svāhā

"Friends, whoever enters the maṇḍala of this wrathful one Mahābala will accomplish all his activities. He will not meet his death by untimely death. His body will not be harmed by any fierce rites or by any illness. Humans, obstructors, and vināyakas will find no opening against him. All people will be pleased with him. All his enemies will vanish. If one enters here, all secret mantras will be accomplished. The rites of all the wrathful ones, too, will be accomplished effortlessly and spontaneously. If one is empowered here, one will be completely freed from all inauspiciousness. One will be blessed with glory.

"Then, one who has performed the preliminary practice of the great wrathful one ten thousand times; who performs the maṇḍala of the great wrathful one; who is grateful; who knows the tantra; who keeps the eight branches of the poṣadha; who has gone for refuge to the Three; who has the awakening mind; who has a mind of loving-kindness; who has become compassionate; who has seen the maṇḍala; who has been empowered; and who has been given the transmission by the gurus; one who holds and recites this king of vidyā-mantras called Mahābala, the rite that destroys all māras and all hostile minds, will not be overcome by any māras or enemies. He will be freed from all yakṣas, rākṣasas, pretas, piśācas, pūtanas, ḍākinīs, apasmāras, sorcerers, snake-holders, and all the rest, and from every fear. He will be freed from all untimely death. He will be freed from all killing and binding. He will be freed from all mutilation. He will be freed from all the fears of being dulled, paralyzed, bewildered, and the like. He will be freed from all the fears of nāgas. He will be freed from all inauspiciousness.

"Whatever poor being holds, recites, keeps in mind, or recollects this Dharma discourse at the three times of day will be freed from all the suffering of poverty. In a time of famine, if Mahābala is recited seven times, rain will fall from a great cloud. If rain does not fall, the lives of the cloud gods will wither. The mountain called རི་བཏང་བཟུང is taken as Īṣādhara, one of the golden mountains around Meru. The identification is probable but not certain. Several names in the heart-mantra list and the svāhā litany are translated by meaning because a Sanskrit form could not be identified with confidence. These are notably "the Soaring One" (possibly Garuḍa), "the hand of cattle-increase," "the bringer of rain," "the son of the god of wealth" and "the goddess who turns back austerities." For "the bringer of rain," the Tibetan reads "rain-relying," likely a slip for "rain-showing/bringing." Agni is supplied as the figure surrounded by the fire sages in the southeast; the Tibetan names only the sages, but the later litany confirms Agni. The phrase "like a tawny moon" for the arrangement of the heart mantras is rendered literally. The opening "Then the Blessed One ... Vajrapāṇi" can also be read as "Then the Blessed One and the bodhisattva Vajrapāṇi"; the draft's reading is kept.  The number "bye ba khrag khrig stong phrag brgya" is rendered loosely as "hundreds of thousands of trillions." The exact value of khrag khrig varies between Tibetan numerical lists.  The list of harmful beings includes two items, "adepts" and "snake-holders," that do not match any standard class of spirit. They are translated literally, and their intended Sanskrit equivalents are uncertain. "Cutting grahas" renders the Tibetan literally; what the grahas cut is not stated. The epithet "Frenzied Wrathful One" follows the house rendering; the Tibetan word may be a scribal variant. The epithet "Who Overcomes Adversaries with Immeasurable Power" is taken with Mahābala, as in the earlier passage. Grammatically it could also qualify Vajrapāṇi.  The wrathful deity ཁྲོ་བོ་ཆོལ་པ is rendered "the Frenzied Wrathful One." Its Sanskrit equivalent is uncertain, though the mantra's "ucchuṣma krodha" may correspond to it. The terms rendered "door bars, contraptions, and bolts" likely refer to magical binding devices destroyed by the vajra. "Sorcerers" (སྒྲུབ་པ) and "snake-holders" (སྦྲུལ་འཛིན) are given by their literal meaning because their Sanskrit equivalents are uncertain. སྲུལ་པོ is rendered pūtana and ལུས་སྲུལ་པོ kaṭapūtana, following the standard equivalences; when the two are not paired, the house list's pūtana for ལུས་སྲུལ་པོ is not applied. The nāga kings ནག་པོ and འཁོར་ལོ are translated literally as "Black One" and "Wheel," since they could not be identified with confidence. ལན་ཁྲིས is read as "ten thousand times." The list of samādhis ends without a main verb in the Tibetan. "Entering" these samādhis is implied.  The exchange on the "general fruit" (abiding in the time of the fruit, for the sake of good qualities) is terse and obscure in the Tibetan; it is rendered literally. "Free of disease" renders bro nad, read as a form of "illness". "Collection of the Dharma" renders chos kyi phung po (Skt. dharmaskandha), a body or mass of teachings, not the dharmakāya.  "Came forward" renders the unusual phrase དོང་བར་མཛད་ནས; its exact sense (approaching, assembling) is uncertain. "Like a house and like foam" follows the text as written; the "house" is an unusual item in this list of similes, and the reading may be corrupt. The phrase "the sublime attainment" interprets the bare word དམ་པ, "the sublime."  "Preceded by the Dharma Realm" renders the title-like phrase "having the dharmadhātu going before." In the verse, "stake" renders a word that in ritual contexts means the kīla dagger. Here it is used figuratively for what pins beings to existence. The Tibetan does not say who sets the dying reader on the path to nirvāṇa. "They" is read as the tathāgatas of the preceding sentence.
