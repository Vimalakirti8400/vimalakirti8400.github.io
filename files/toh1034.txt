Mantra Rite for Learning and Memory

The Dhāraṇī for a Melodious Voice is a very short ritual text, a dhāraṇī, which pairs a brief Sanskrit mantra invoking the goddess as Bhagavatī with simple instructions for its use. After an opening homage to the Three Jewels, it directs the practitioner to recite the mantra seven times over a palmful of water each morning for seven days, in the presence of the noble Avalokiteśvara, and then to drink the water. The text promises that this practice will let one learn three hundred verses of scripture in a single day, and that keeping it up for a month will make one's voice as melodious as that of a kiṃnara, a celestial musician. It is Toh 1034 in the Kangyur, where it belongs among the dhāraṇī texts, a class of short incantations and rites aimed at specific worldly and spiritual benefits, here the improvement of memory, learning, and speech.

Homage to the Three Jewels!

oṃ bhagavati śruti smriti bala va par ma siddhi svāhā

The rite is this. For seven days, each morning, in the presence of the noble Avalokiteśvara, one should empower a palmful of water by reciting the mantra over it seven times and then drink it. If one does this, one will learn three hundred ślokas of text in even a single day. If one does this for a month, one's voice will become as melodious as that of a kiṃnara. One should have no doubt about this.

The Dhāraṇī for a Melodious Voice is complete.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

Kiṃnaras are celestial musicians famed for the beauty of their singing. The final syllable of the colophon is evidently a copyist's form of the usual closing "so." It has been rendered simply as "is complete."
