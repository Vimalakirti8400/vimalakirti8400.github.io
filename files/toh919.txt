The Noble Dhāraṇī Called "Beautiful Bodily Form" (Āryarucirāṅgayaṣṭināmadhāraṇī)

The Noble Dhāraṇī Called "Beautiful Bodily Form," known in Sanskrit as the Ārya Ruciraṅgayaṣṭi nāma dhāraṇī and in Tibetan as 'Phags pa lus kyi dbyibs mdzes zhes bya ba'i gzungs, is a short dhāraṇī text. After an opening homage to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas, it consists mainly of an extended Sanskrit incantation. The incantation praises the bodhisattva Padmapāṇi, the lotus-bearing form of Avalokiteśvara, describing him as radiant with the light of countless suns and adorned with a jeweled crown and earrings. It invokes him as the great compassionate one who calms the lower realms, frees beings from suffering, and fulfills their wishes. This work is Toh 919 in the Kangyur, where it belongs to the class of dhāraṇī texts gathered in the canon's collection of incantations.

This is the dhāraṇī called "The Beautiful Bodily Form of the Blessed One Padmapāṇi." It is the supreme mansion of the bodhisattvas. Mahāvajra proclaimed it as a barrier against obstructors. Gods, nāgas, dānavas, kinnaras, and great asuras have praised it. It brings every hope to fulfillment. Whoever is veiled by many great obscurations and hears it, holds it, recites it, reads it, or sees it and understands it well will have every obscuration of karma and affliction completely purified.

If one rises in the morning and recites it once, Padmapāṇi will follow close behind. Whatever aims one desires will be accomplished simply by thinking of them.

If one stays alone in complete solitude, brings the Buddha vividly to mind, recollects the Blessed One Padmapāṇi, and recites it seven times, one will obtain whatever excellent thing one wishes. One will become pleasing to all sentient beings, and all one's lower rebirths will be stopped.

If, while walking or sitting, one recites it silently even for a moment, the karma accumulated over many hundreds of thousands of millions of eons will be purified. One will possess a great mass of merit equal to that accumulated by a thousand universal monarchs. In life after life one will be born into the great lotus family, and one will be honored by eminent people.

If one fills one's cupped hands with fragrant flowers and recites it seven times before noble Avalokiteśvara, one will obtain a thousand great qualities. The light of the nature of great compassion will also dawn in that person, and he will be endowed with great excellence.

If one gazes at his face while reciting it, one will see the Blessed One, noble Avalokiteśvara, whose face is always smiling, and one will obtain what is called "the first clarity of the light of Dharma, which makes stainless." In this very life one will attain recollection of the Buddha. One will die with the mind resting in equipoise, and in all one's lives one will remember one's past lives.

If one wishes to exhaust all obscurations of karma, just as one desires, one should observe a fast on the fifteenth day of the month, at the full moon, and put on white clothes. Before a stupa containing relics, or before an image, one should smear a maṇḍala with white sandalwood and scatter various flowers on that maṇḍala and on the Blessed One. One should make offerings of fragrant incense, lamps, and perfumes to the Blessed One and to noble Avalokiteśvara, and then, sitting right there, recite it one hundred and eight times. By this one will be freed from all wrongdoing. Even one who has committed the deeds of immediate retribution will have that wrongdoing purified. One will possess a completely purified mind stream and completely purified conduct of body, speech, and mind. The tathāgatas will always and continuously bring about for one the power of samādhi, the power of empowerment, the power of the grounds and the perfections, and the power of exceptional wisdom.

If one looks up into the sky and recites it, rain will fall. If one looks at the earth and recites it, all the crops will flourish. If one looks at the beds of rivers, ponds, and pools and recites it, water will appear. If one touches any illness with the hand and recites it, the illness will be cured. If one recites it before someone whose memory has failed, that person will regain memory. If one looks into the faces of the hungry and thirsty and recites it, they will be satisfied. If a great fear arises, one should write it on cloth and fasten it to the top of a victory banner. A boundary will then be sealed and protection established for up to a league around.

The noble dhāraṇī called "Beautiful Bodily Form" is complete.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The phrase rendered "Mahāvajra proclaimed it as a barrier against obstructors" is unclear in the Tibetan, which reads literally "spoken by the Great Vajra as obstructing." Both the identity of the "Great Vajra" and the sense of the verb are uncertain. "Dānavas" renders a Tibetan term meaning literally "sons of the giving woman," taken as the descendants of Danu. "Hundreds of thousands of millions" renders a count in koṭis (a koṭi is ten million). "League" renders yojana.
