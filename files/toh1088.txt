The Hundred and Eight Names of Śrīdevī Kālī (Śrīdevīkālīnāmāṣṭaśataka)

The Hundred and Eight Names of Śrīdevī Kālī, known in Sanskrit as Śrīdevīkālīnāma Aṣṭaśataka and in Tibetan as Dpal lha mo nag mo'i mtshan brgya rtsa brgyad pa, is a short scripture of praise that sets out a litany of epithets for the fierce goddess Śrīdevī, also called Mahākālī. It opens on Vulture Peak Mountain, where the Buddha is teaching the true names to the bodhisattvas and the goddess herself arrives to pay homage. The bodhisattva Siṃhavīrya then asks the Buddha to enumerate the names of this bodhisattva who works for the benefit of the world in the guise of a rākṣasī, a fearsome demoness, and the Buddha responds by proclaiming her many names. The text presents a wrathful protector as an enlightened being acting out of compassion, and it belongs to the genre of name-recitation texts used in devotion and protective practice. It is Toh 1088 in the Kangyur, where it is grouped among the short dhāraṇī and invocation texts of the canon.

In the language of India: śrīdevīkālīnāma āṣṭaśataka. In the language of Tibet: The Hundred and Eight Names of Śrīdevī Kālī.

Homage to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas.

Thus did I hear at one time. The Blessed One was dwelling on Vulture Peak Mountain, teaching the true names to all the bodhisattvas of both cause and result. At that time Śrīdevī Mahākālī also went to the Blessed One. She circumambulated him three times and sat down to one side in the Blessed One's presence.

Then the bodhisattva Siṃhavīrya addressed the Blessed One: "Blessed One, what is the enumeration of the names of the bodhisattva who works for the benefit of the world in the guise of a rākṣasī?" The Blessed One, with the bodhisattva Siṃhavīrya in mind, looked at Śrīdevī Kālī and spoke these words:

"Abiding deeply in hearing, reflection and meditation,
Embodying cause, result and condition,
Supreme among the wise, who benefits beings,
Glorious goddess Kālī, noble one,
Emanation of a bodhisattva,
Like the earth, you are the ground on which all rely.
Gathering good qualities, you are like water.
Ripening awakening, you are like fire.
Like the wind, you set virtuous formations in motion.
Your pure wisdom is space.
You are like the great elements,
Praised by the great hosts of bhūtas.

Having fully amassed merit and wisdom,
You are skilled in karma and its ripening.
Practiced in taming through a form of pride,
You travel the path of wisdom and compassion.
You are like an excellent vase,
Like the water of a wish-fulfilling jewel,
A wish-granting tree that benefits all.
Like the sun, clarity is yours.
Your mind is clear like the moon,
Your name is like a rabbit's horn,
Your own continuum is like a wheel.
Though you are as rare as the udumbara flower,
Like nectar you bring complete satisfaction.
You are the mistress of all beings,
And you also teach the Dharma that is without intrinsic nature.
Like a storm of water and of sky,
You tame by any means whatever in the three existences is to be tamed.
You protect the teaching of the victors of the three times.
You are truly one with the holy ones,
And you travel the path of the holy ones.

Goddess, mātṛkā, yakṣiṇī,
Rākṣasī, nāginī, piśācī,
Malevolent one, bhūtī, kumbhāṇḍī,
Fierce one, terrifying in manner, flasher of lightning,
Wind woman, black woman, terrifier,
Ḍākinī, Caṇḍālī, liberator of the field.
You tame through pacifying, enriching, magnetizing and wrathful means.
You display white, yellow, green, black and red.
You manifest in forms of eight kinds,
And show the ways of the six classes of beings.
You are able to expound the five aggregates,
And you display the five objects that satisfy the senses.
Skilled in the conduct of dependent origination,
You are skilled in slaying the subjects,
Likewise in slaying speech,
And you strive to slay the embodied.
Skilled in a mind that does not slay,
You have gone far from saṃsāra.
Foretold by the Tathāgata,
Bearing the names of a bodhisattva,
Glorious lady who protects the world,
You are loving toward those who uphold the holy Dharma.

oṃ āḥ hūṃ śrī devi kāli samaya jaḥ ḍākinī ḍāla samaya svāhā

Whoever retains these hundred and eight names
Will gain a hundred and eight good qualities as well.
They will not experience the sufferings of hell,
They will not be born in the realm of hungry ghosts,
And birth as an animal will not hold them back.
When they are born in Videha,
Lightning and the like will not strike them,
Nor in Kurava will the wind harm them.
Likewise in Godānīya too,
Water and waves will be unable to carry them off.
Whoever is born in Jambudvīpa
Will not be endangered by harms of any kind.
Even in the abodes of the gods of the desire realm,
Once born there, they will delight in the Dharma.
In the birthplaces of the four dhyānas,
The four immeasurables will increase.
In the four formless abodes as well,
They will not be born if it serves no purpose,
And never among those without perception.
In the places where the noble ones dwell,
They pursue their own aim in the manner of the three realms.
Never will they become attached to objects,
Nor will they cling to mind.
The six root afflictions,
Likewise the twenty secondary ones,
And even the five deeds of immediate retribution will be purified.
For many countless eons
They will not be born in the eight unfree states.
They will turn back the disputes of the non-Buddhists (tīrthikas),
Be endowed with perfect might and power,
And have long life, freedom from illness, and glory.
For many eons, free from suffering,
They will never fall into saṃsāra.
In order to attain nirvāṇa,
They will master the grounds of the śrāvakas,
And should come to know the twelve links of dependent origination.

The grounds of the bodhisattvas are
Very Joyous, Stainless, Luminous and Radiant,
Difficult to Master, Manifest, Gone Far,
Immovable, Good Intelligence, Cloud of Dharma,
And the great, unsurpassed ground of a buddha.
All twelve of these they will attain.
Universal Light and Nectar Light,
Sky Light and Vajra Light,
Jewel Light and Lotus Light,
Light of Action and the Incomparable Ground,
The Glorious Ground and Wisdom Light,
Omniscience and Self-Awareness:
These twelve grounds of a buddha too,
Should it come about in this life, are swiftly attained.
Even until they have attained the grounds,
They will benefit beings,
They will be endowed with wisdom and compassion,
And those who delight in the Dharma will protect them.
Gaining accomplishment, they will attain
The state of the Tathāgata Ratnasambhava.
Whoever retains the hundred and eight names
Of the glorious bodhisattva,
The glorious goddess Kālī,
Will gain a hundred and eight good qualities,
And, having accomplished body, speech and mind,
Will become a Dharma king of the three realms."

When he had said this, those assemblies of bodhisattvas and Śrīdevī Mahākālī were amazed. They rejoiced in what the Blessed One had said and praised it highly.

This completes the Hundred and Eight Names of Śrīdevī Mahākālī, together with their benefits.

The epithet rendered "liberator of the field" is uncertain. It may refer to the ritual "liberation" of a hostile object or effigy, and I have translated it literally. The line rendered "the state of the Tathāgata Ratnasambhava" reads literally "the Tathāgata arisen from a jewel," which most likely names Ratnasambhava. Although the text speaks of "twelve" bodhisattva grounds, it lists ten grounds plus the buddha ground. I have kept the number as the text gives it.
