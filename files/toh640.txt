Homage to the bodhisattva, the great being, the Remover of All Obscurations.

Oṃ amṛta bindu java mahā sukha svāhā.

Then the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Vairocana, extended his right hand and, having satisfied the bodhisattva, the great being, the Remover of All Obscurations, declared: "You who possess a treasury of precious qualities — it is excellent, excellent that you have brought forth this dhāraṇī-mantra and thereby produced great bliss for sentient beings. O son of the Victors, whoever hears this dhāraṇī-mantra shall come to possess roots of virtue in the presence of Tathāgatas as numerous as the grains of sand in ten Ganges rivers. And whoever has it resound in their ears shall likewise be freed from all obscurations. If one who resides near a stūpa containing bodily relics fasts on the eighth or fifteenth day of the waxing lunar fortnight, performs the restoration ceremony, makes offerings of incense, fumigation, butter-lamps, flowers, flower garlands, ointments, and the like, circumambulates, and recites this mantra, one shall attain whatever one desires that is excellent, and shall be liberated from all sins, all sufferings, and all fears. If one makes offerings of incense and the like to the bodhisattva Remover of All Obscurations and recites the mantra, he will appear in one's dreams, bring all one's wishes to complete fulfillment, and remove all afflictions and all sufferings."

Then Vajrapuṣpa Mahāmukuṭa, the lord of the three grounds, together with his retinue, rose from his seat in the midst of that great assembly-maṇḍala, bowed to the bodhisattva, the great being, the Remover of All Obscurations, and praised him with one hundred and eight names:

into the teaching of the vajra maṇḍala
that one shall swiftly enter, without long delay.
