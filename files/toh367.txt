Compendium of All Conceptuality: The Uttara-Uttara Tantra of the Ḍākinī-Net Union with All Buddhas Called the Compendium of All Conceptuality (Sarvakalpasamuccaya-nāma-sarvabuddhasamayoga-ḍākinījāla-saṃvara-uttarottara-tantra)

Toh 367 in the Kangyur is a tantric text known in Sanskrit as the Sarvakalpasamuccaya-nāma-sarvabuddhasamayoga-ḍākinījāla-saṃvara-uttarottara-tantra, rendered in Tibetan as the Uttara-Uttara Tantra of the Illusory Bliss-Supreme Ḍākinī-Net Union with All Buddhas, Called the Compendium of All Conceptuality. It belongs to the class of highest yoga tantras and concerns the union of all buddhas through the ḍākinī-net, centering on Vajrasattva as the embodiment of great bliss and the essence of all awakened beings. The text opens with homage to Vajrasattva and proceeds in verse, celebrating the non-conceptual mind in which suchness abides, the supreme secret of great attachment and great bliss, and the all-encompassing nature of tantric realization. As the closing colophon of the preceding chapter makes clear, this scripture is organized around the metaphor of the great wheel-turning of all conceptuality, and it sits within the tantra section of the Kangyur as a scripture of the ḍākinī-net or Saṃvara cycle.

This concludes the chapter called "The Foremost," the Great Wheel-Turning of All Conceptuality, the Great Bliss. It is complete.

In the language of India: Sarvakalpasamuccaya-nāma-sarvabuddhasamayoga-ḍākinījāla-saṃvara-uttarottara-tantra.

likewise vajra and Vajradhara.
Vajradhara itself, the great one —
what are the conduct-practices of bodhisattvas?
The māra and the victory over māra,
what is the nature of the buddhas' awakening?
The fierce vajra that liberates,
and Vajrapāṇi — what are they like?
How is the primordial family to be generated?
What are the forms of liberation through desire?
How are the three kinds of protector sought?
What is the nature of everything as suchness?
How does the heart-essence first arise?
What is the proximate heart-essence like?
Great bliss, the great vajra —
what is the nature of the meaningful samaya (sacred pledge)?
How is the maṇḍala taught?
What is its karmic ripening like?
With the measurement of lines, and likewise through conceptuality,
how are the spiritual attainments described?
The five secrets and the great suchness —
what are they, and what are they like?
The chief lords of the maṇḍalas,
and the families — what are they like?
The suchness of universal empowerment,
and the emblems of the suchness assembly,
the universal summoning and the great offering,
and the accomplishment of one's own blessing and the rest —
all of these: the great secret of suchness,
the holy and the ultimate,
for the sake of the accomplished meaning of the holy syllable —
is it not unsuitable to speak all of this?

In what way is this a tantra of the meaning of conceptuality? From non-conceptual pure phenomena, one fully conceptualizes for the sake of sentient beings. Therefore it is correctly called conceptuality, because conceptuality is thoroughly pure.

In what way is this a subsequent tantra? A tantra is explained as something thoroughly connected; the subsequent is that which was drawn forth earlier. Those very great secrets and secrets, too, are held to be the subsequent tantra.

In what way is this the wisdom of primordial gnosis? Completely abiding throughout all of space, the supreme essence of all vajras, that great bliss and great joy — the palace of the holy syllable. Thus declared the Blessed One, the glorious Vajrasattva.

In what way is Vajrasattva without beginning and without end? Beginning with the syllable ka, up to the syllable dha at the end, setting aside nga and nya, one should proceed. Beginning with the syllable dha, up to the syllable ṣa at the end, the second garland should be correctly written. In the center abides the row of syllables a, arranged in a lunar maṇḍala.

The bindu adorned with the lunar day — the heavy stroke and the lunar crescent are the two. The syllable a, unchanging, is to be meditated upon; in the center it abides as the essential nature of the vajra. To meditate always upon that as the center is great attachment and great bliss. This is what is known as the buddha-image of all buddhas.

From that, all beings, moving and unmoving, arise completely and perfectly. Since it arises from space, because it is a defining mark, it is supreme — without beginning and without end.

The great vajra, the actual mind, is what is called Vajrasattva.

Free from fault, eternal, and everlasting —

Because all attachment arises from following after desire,
that very secret Blessed One

Now, how is the Blessed One Samantabhadra? It is said:

Eternally the supreme joy of all,
the Tathāgata, primordially liberated —
Samantabhadra, lord of all,
is the supreme rite of the awakening mind.

And how is the Blessed One the chief of the glorious Vajrasattva? It is said:

The supreme among all great accomplishments,
by the mudrā of the highest great lordship,
the great vajra sovereign is turned in the heart —
the vajra essence, chief of chiefs,
the primordial first being of the sacred.

And how is the Blessed One the very mind of all? It is said:

Inconceivable, vast, and profound,

By the very stability of mind, one is a bodhisattva —
to meditate the bodhisattva of samādhi.
The bodhisattva of consciousness:
it is the very vajra, and from the vajra
arises the vajra-holder.

When one speaks of "vajra, vajra" —
listen now: what is said to be "vajra"?
With mind clear like the moon,
"vajra" is fully explained.

And how is the Blessed One the great Vajradhara? It is said:

Through the fourfold way — samādhi and the rest —
whatever is generated and cultivated,

accomplishment arises in this very life.

Though the blessing-practice of the bodhisattva,
this very self-blessing is the supreme accomplishment.
By attaining that daily,
one attains the very state of Vajrasattva.

And how is the Blessed One the conduct of Samantabhadra? It is said:

Courage, dust, and darkness too —
why should they become for the benefit of sentient beings?
Therefore this is how it goes:
the bodhisattva conduct of Samantabhadra in accordance with reality.

And how is the Blessed One the Victor over māra and over māras? It is said:

That very mind itself is māra,
that very mind itself is the Tathāgata.
For the sake of taming one's own mind,
I myself draw near to māra itself.

And how is the Blessed One the manifest awakening and the buddha? It is said:

Thoroughly piercing one's own mind —
the supreme awakening mind, the vajra itself —
from that very suchness, one becomes a buddha through that,

That inseparable, thorough piercing

The vajra, the actual body of all buddhas,
is the firm stabilization of awakening minds.
For the accomplishment of all that is firm,
I, the Victor, gave to Samantabhadra

The mother is the Perfection of Wisdom,
the father is skilled in skillful means;
for the sake of taming, Vajrasattva
is born — I myself am the family.

And how is the Blessed One the liberation of desire? It is said:

Because all accomplishments follow from desire,
the Blessed One of great desire

The secret blood-drinker —

Because the three pure worlds are boundless,
for taming them — the self of the supreme three, the three existences —
because of being everything — the Three Jewels —
the self of the three worlds, the self of the three realms.

Because merit is greatly increased in sentient beings,
the self of the utterly immovable — everything itself.
Because mind assembles and disperses, it is the moving one;
because of being the maker of all, everything itself,
it is declared: the self of the ḍākinī, the goddess.
Because of accomplishing all, the fortunate one;
conceptuality is proclaimed by sign as the unfortunate one.

The permanent establishment of existence is fully accomplished,
for the purpose of accomplishing the Great Lord (Śiva),
and for the purpose of all heroic siddhis —
the beginning of the aeon, the Blessed One declares.
Because of great attachment, the Blessed One —
the supreme, inexhaustible, supremely joyous one.
Because of its being secret, the syllable of the essence —
the noble HŪṂ, inexhaustible.

Because the maṇḍalas are inferior

The desirous one is the Blessed One, the bodhisattva,

Vajra, Vajrapāṇi —

And how is the Blessed One the great bliss and the continuum of the samaya of the vajra endowed with meaning? It is said:

The inexhaustible bliss that fulfills all things —
that is the accomplishment of the vajra.
The meaningful samaya is hard to transgress.

And how is the Blessed One the meaning of the maṇḍala? It is said:

Just as those who dwell in a place form a wheel,
so likewise do the classes of beings and destinies —
entering into the retinue in full union.

From directly beholding the maṇḍala

one should likewise depict it outwardly.

When the wise one draws the maṇḍala,

What abides at the center of the eight squares
is held to be the principal maṇḍala.

Whoever is able to truly realize
the lord of the maṇḍala as real —
by that one's union with the chosen deity,
the maṇḍala of accomplishment is fully revealed.
Through the union of drawing, having drawn a painted form,
one unites one's own mind with the painted image.
Even if something remains incomplete in the ritual,
once one has striven it does not become a fault.

The heroic one is beginningless and without end.
Vajrasattva is great bliss.
Samantabhadra is the lord of all.
Vajra-essence is the chief of chiefs.
Because he is beginningless and without end,
he is held to be the supreme primordial.
Because he is lord over all wheels,
the supreme one is the sovereign of the supreme.

The supreme of all great desires,

because the supreme syllable itself is accomplished,
because all phenomena are pure,

Because the Blessed One has great attachment,

Vajrasattva himself

And how does one know the empowerment? It is said:

Apart from the skull-garland head ornament,

nothing equals the jewel of the buddhas.
That ornament, called the jewel of all buddhas,

With the right limb pressing down in equanimity
and the left limb drawn in and piercing,
he who holds that heroic one's seated posture —
Vajra-essence is the chief of chiefs.

She who utterly pervades all supreme things

Supremely victorious and auspicious in all things,

All the accomplished ones are likewise endowed with excellence.
Joy is likewise the sovereign lady, the mistress.
The Vajra Goddess is the assembly's mudrā;
Vajrasattva's form is the supreme.

Those called the Mirror and the Blood-Drinker —
for equanimity, the supreme lord of dance;
for discriminating wisdom, the Sun;
for activity, the Supreme Steed;
and for the dharmadhātu, the buddha
is the great Sage, Vairocana.
The essential nature of the five wisdoms
is great attachment and supreme bliss.
Form and sound, and likewise smell,
taste and touch likewise —

Like Vajrasattva himself,
those suchnesses are to be served through suchness.
Arising within the dharmadhātu,
they will bestow accomplishments in abundance.

Thus spoke the Blessed One, the glorious Vajrasattva.

The Captivating One and the Holding One,
the Liberating One and the Life-Giving One,
the Sky-Farer and the Goddess —

If one displays the hair, the forehead is to be shown in return.
If one displays the eye, the navel is to be shown in return.
If one displays the navel, the lips are to be fully shown.
If one fully displays the lips, the teeth are to be fully shown.
If one displays the tongue, the belly is to be shown.
If one displays the female sex, the liṅga is to be shown in return.
If one displays the base of the thigh, the thigh is to be shown in return.
If one displays the thigh, the sole of the foot is to be shown.
If one displays the sole of the foot, the nail is to be shown in return.
If one displays the armpit, the eyebrow is to be shown in return.
If one makes a sound, the response is to be a snap of the fingers.
If one also shows the mouth, the noose is to be shown in return.
If one displays the pillar, the short lance is to be shown in return.
If one displays the nail, the breast is to be fully shown.
If one shows the fist, it is to be shown at the boundary.
If one displays the tongue, the urine and feces are to be shown.
If one makes an unattractive form, a beautiful form is to be made in return.
If one makes a hissing sound, the hand is to be shown in return.

Then, what are the marks by which ḍākinīs are recognized? It is said:

Eyes like the discharge of a diseased eye,
a trident and short lance upon the forehead,
inverted eyes and a frowning brow,
breasts that are unpleasant in appearance.
The nose is of two types —
one should know it as encircled at the crown,
surrounded entirely by three lines.
The marks of her dwelling are:
a trident, a small bell, a mirror,
a sword-form, a victory banner —
these are shown as the marks of a one-pointed dwelling.

she is to be shown by marks such as these:
sudden aversion in her conduct,
and engaging in things that are purposeless.
Upon her feet too there will appear a sword-form
and a shape resembling a trident.
Her voice is ever like that of a crow.
One who possesses these defining characteristics
is called a true ḍākinī.

Thus spoke the Blessed One, the glorious Vajrasattva.

All conceptual elaborations — the great secret —

the bell is rung in sequence.

All conceptual elaborations of the great secret —
with whatever produces delight, the offering:
the sequential offering and further still,
song, music, and dance.

Taking up the vajra bell,

Having taken up the outer interval of the fire,
equally endowed with the syllable E —
the first from the east is to be made endowed.
With a heavy tsheg endowed with the syllable U,
the last of the center is to be made endowed.
The accomplisher of all —
what is known as Blood-Drinker (Heruka) arises.
Whatever one wishes to accomplish is accomplished.
After that, whatever one desires —
by acting in whatever way one desires,
all-good, all-creating, just as it is:
whether standing or sitting,
by moving or by rising upright,
in laughing and in speaking —
wherever and in that, whatever it is, just so.
Whatever is the activity of whatever sense faculty,
in the intrinsic nature of whatever that may be,
by the application of one's own non-equipoise,
one draws the deity through one's own awareness.
By that very application,
absolutely everything is accomplished.
If one always sees all the buddhas face to face,
one is perfectly accomplished.
The union with all buddhas —
whoever is wise and reads this with faith:
that one is fully freed from wrongdoings
and becomes master of all accomplishments.

The Union with All Buddhas, the ḍākinī's illusion, the subsequent application from the supreme bliss — the accomplishing tantra of subsequent application: this is the twenty-second chapter.

Thus it is with the various subsequent attachments.

With great joy, the great pure lotus,
brilliantly and in ongoing purity — always given by supreme purity —
through the supreme blissful reality, the samaya: to me, bliss is to be accomplished in succession.
The supreme joy, supremely applied — the supremely good and very joyful.
By intrinsic nature, naturally pure — the intrinsic nature that is always accomplished in succession, actual.

This, the supreme essence of my clarity,
the highest of the highest supreme great bliss — of great power.
For the sake of what permanence of sentient beings arising from all supreme —
the very lord of all-supreme ground — is the one who applies.
Because the boundless, the unattainable, the unobstructed:
of all the great ones of the great method —
grant the growth of the supreme accomplishment equal to the sky.
Those who are without beginning, without end —
that which is without completion is incapable.
Sentient beings who are lazy, who desire little,

By whom it is accomplished — "Vajradhāra" and so forth,

by that, one's own desired accomplishment is obtained in accordance with the rite.

in this pure one, by the Victors, by this pure —
there is no obstruction, and likewise there is no māra —
even through tantra, may accomplishment grow: may it be blessed with power.

For the one who accomplishes this supreme great contemplation,
it does not descend into the nature of māra, nor is it instability itself.
The cause of stability I do not set forth here —
it is taught in order that the supreme of the supreme be swiftly accomplished.

By that which always accomplishes for me through my own essence,
supreme great accomplishment and constant great bliss,
attachment and intense joy and stability and proliferation,
and the great syllable — these I too cause to be constantly accomplished.

Then, the supreme-born Victors all arise in joy
and spread a great smile pervading everything in all directions.
With the supreme great forms of the finest particles of the space of the Lord of All,
from every side they offer to that one the sound of Excellent.

By me, with the supreme great form following through all of space,
all the buddhas who have become the highest father cry: Excellent, excellent!
You who have arisen from the supreme vajra — excellent, excellent!
You who are the sacred nature of reality, the self-nature of Dharma — excellent, excellent!
You who possess sacred action, you who are the self-nature of offering — excellent, excellent!
Grant the blessing of realization: by equal blessing,
through the great accomplishment of meaningful attainment and the constant great syllable —
when in the future there comes a time of those who are untamed,
so that they too may come to the genuine bliss of the sacred —

thus, after this great speech had been requested,
all the buddhas together with their spiritual heirs
openly praised the words spoken
by the great bodhisattva Vajrasattva.
