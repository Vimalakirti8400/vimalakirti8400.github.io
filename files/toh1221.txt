The Rite of the Hevajra Maṇḍala (Hevajramaṇḍalavidhi)

The Rite of the Hevajra Maṇḍala, known in Sanskrit as the Hevajra Maṇḍala Vidhi and in Tibetan as Gyepa Dorjé Maṇḍal gyi Choga, is a short ritual manual for the practice centered on the deity Hevajra, a principal figure of the highest class of Buddhist tantra. After paying homage to glorious Heruka, it guides the practitioner through the preliminary steps of the maṇḍala rite. These include visualizing a white syllable hūṃ at the heart whose light purifies misdeeds, meditating on emptiness, and arising as the one-faced, two-armed action Heruka. The practitioner then places the syllables oṃ, āḥ, and hūṃ at the crown, throat, and heart, recites a protective mantra, and consecrates nectar or the five products of the cow for use in the ritual. The work is Toh 1221 in the Tengyur, where it belongs among the tantric commentaries and practice texts connected with the Hevajra cycle.

In the language of India: he vajra maṇḍal vidhi. In the language of Tibet: The Rite of the Hevajra Maṇḍala.

Homage to glorious Heruka.

At the heart of one's body of ripening, upon a sun disk, imagine a syllable hūṃ, white like the radiance of the moon. White light rays stream out from it and fill the body, and one imagines that one's misdeeds are purified. Then meditate on emptiness, and by uttering hūṃ, visualize oneself as the action Heruka, with one face and two arms. On his crown, upon a moon disk, imagine a white oṃ. At the throat, upon an eight-petaled lotus, imagine a red āḥ. At the heart, upon a sun disk, imagine a blue hūṃ. Protect oneself with oṃ āḥ hūṃ rakṣa rakṣa hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ svāhā. Then recite oṃ āḥ hūṃ over nectar, or the five products of the cow, and so on, and, repeating oṃ vajra rekhe hūṃ, one should smear the maṇḍala.

Then, having washed the hands and joined the palms, one should recite its benefits:

Dung mixed with water is generosity;
Cleaning and smoothing it is moral discipline;
Removing ants and the like is patience;
Undertaking the work is diligence;
An instant of one-pointedness is meditative concentration;
Making the lines even is wisdom.
By making the maṇḍala of the Sage, one gains these six perfections.
One is born an exceptional god or human, surpassingly radiant like the moon,
Freed from every illness, with a complexion like gold;
One is born into a royal family, rich in grain, gold, and other wealth.
Therefore, in the house of the supreme Sugata, do this work with your body.

So one recites.

Then touch the ground with the left hand and recite oṃ vajra medīnī vajrī bhava vajra bandha hūṃ. Merely by saying this, imagine that the ground has become a crossed vajra.

Then the flower heaps should be arranged on the maṇḍala. In the center, say oṃ āḥ hūṃ namaḥ and cast a flower. In the east, cast a flower with gaṃ gaurī namaḥ. In the south, cast a flower with caṃ caurī namaḥ. In the west, cast one with vaṃ vetālī namaḥ. In the north, cast one with ghaṃ ghasmadī namaḥ. In the northeast, the quarter of Īśāna, cast one with paṃ pukkasī namaḥ. In the southeast, cast one with saṃ savarī namaḥ. In the southwest, the quarter of Nairṛti, with laṃ caṇḍalī namaḥ. In the northwest, with ḍaṃ ḍombinī namaḥ. These make up the maṇḍala of flower heaps.

Then light rays stream out from the hūṃ at one's own heart and enter the left nostrils of all the buddhas of the ten directions. Merely by striking the lotus at their hearts, they rouse them, and all the buddhas of the ten directions emanate as the maṇḍala of the nine deities of Hevajra. Imagine that they come into the sky before one. Then, with the intention of subduing all the obstructors who have come clinging to the tips of the light rays, one should drive out the obstructors with four hūṃ syllables.

Then recite oṃ vajra gaurī ākārṣaya ja, oṃ vajra caurī praveśaya hūṃ, oṃ vajra vettālī bandha vaṃ, oṃ vajra ghasmarī vaśaṃ kuru hoḥ. By reciting these, the nine wisdom deities are to be summoned into the maṇḍala of flower heaps, made to enter, bound, and brought under control. One should also make them stable with the hundred-syllable mantra.

Then one should pay homage:

Seeing beings oppressed by delusion,
To pacify the delusion of beings,
The Vajra Holder took the form of Gaurī;
Therefore I praise Gaurī and bow to her.
Seeing beings oppressed by aversion,
To pacify the aversion of beings,
The Vajra Holder took the form of Caurī;
Therefore I praise Caurī and bow to her.
Seeing beings oppressed by pride,
To pacify the pride of beings,
The Lord took the form of Vettālī;
Therefore I praise Vettā and bow to her.
Seeing beings bent low by attachment,
To pacify the attachment of beings,
The Lord took the form of Ghasmarī;
Therefore I praise Ghasmarī and bow to her.
To the form nondual with Nairātmyā,
I bow and pay homage, I bow and pay homage.
To the lord of the five wisdoms and three bodies,
To Vajraḍāka, I pay homage.

Then again, with oṃ deva picu vajra hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ svāhā, make offering to the Blessed One; with oṃ a ā i ī u ū ri rī li lī e ai o au aṃ, make offering to the Blessed Lady. Then make offering with the eight lines: oṃ āṣṭānanāya namaḥ, oṃ piṃgordhakeśavartmanā namaḥ, oṃ caturviṃśatinaitrāya namaḥ, oṃ ṣoḍaśabhujāya namaḥ, oṃ kriṣṇajimūtavapuṣe namaḥ, oṃ kapālamāla anekadhariṇe namaḥ, oṃ ādhmata krūra cittāya namaḥ, oṃ adhendu daṃṣṭiṇe namaḥ.

Then make perfect offering with the inner, outer, and suchness offerings, and offer as well with the offerings of outer ritual. Consecrate all the flowers and offering substances by reciting oṃ āḥ hūṃ and touching them. Purify them with scented water by means of oṃ sarva saṃśodhani hū phaṭ svāhāḥ. Offer water for the feet with oṃ vajra niriti hūṃ, and offer water for the feet with oṃ hri viśuddha sarva dharmā sarva pāpaṃ niśca saṃśodhaya sarva vikalpa apanaya hūṃ. Offer the welcoming water (arghya) with jaḥ hūṃ vaṃ hoḥ khaṃ raṃ. Then, with oṃ vajra dhūpe āḥ hūṃ, oṃ vajra puṣpe āḥ hūṃ, oṃ vajra āloke āḥ hūṃ, oṃ vajra gandhe āḥ hūṃ, oṃ vajra naivitye āḥ hūṃ, oṃ vajra śapta āḥ hūṃ phaṭ svāhā, one should present, in order, flowers, incense, lamps, food offerings, and cymbals.

Further: "I offer these to the glorious maṇḍala of Hevajra together with its circle; out of your great compassion, please accept them."

Then praise also with the Twenty-Verse Praise. Then one should confess one's misdeeds: "Whatever actions I have committed, accumulated through mistaken thinking throughout beginningless saṃsāra, and whatever I have rejoiced in when done by others, all of these I confess in the presence of the Blessed One, and henceforth I shall refrain from them. I rejoice in all the roots of virtue of all sentient beings, that is, in the merit of the buddhas, the bodhisattvas, and all ordinary beings."

"Until I reach the seat of awakening, I go for refuge to the Buddha Hevajra, who possesses great compassion and is of one taste with the three realms in great bliss; to the Dharma, that is, the mantras and mudrās he taught, the entire Dharma of the Great Vehicle, and the Dharma of the maṇḍala and the rest; to the Saṅgha of irreversible bodhisattvas; and to the host of goddesses who abide as the retinue in his circle. I shall generate the mind set on unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening. In order to accomplish benefit and happiness for all sentient beings, and to establish all of them in the awakening of a buddha, the ultimate nirvāṇa, I shall generate the mind set on unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening.

I go for refuge to the Three Jewels.
I confess each and every misdeed.
I rejoice in the virtue of beings.
I hold the awakening of a buddha in my mind."

Having thus generated the mind of awakening, one should then rely on this very path of the unsurpassed Great Vehicle. "I too offer my body to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas. You who possess great compassion, please accept it." With these words one offers one's body. Then one should make aspirations:

"Of noble birth, never falling away,
keeping the commitments, a joyful vajra-holder,
compassionate and devoted to the guru:
may I be so in birth after birth.

Vajra in hand, ringing the bell,
reciting the profound Dharma,
sharing as food the fluid of the consort:
may I be so in birth after birth.

Having become the glorious Heruka,
I shall work for the benefit of all beings.
Having attained the supreme state of Heruka,
may I place and establish every being without exception in it.

Through the offering of praise made thus, may I swiftly be born into the supreme vajra family.
For the benefit of beings, through genuine skillful means, may I be endowed with qualities like Mañjughoṣa.
Like Vajrasattva, may I bestow incomparable bliss on all the yoginīs.
Adorned with the six mudrās, dwelling in charnel grounds, may I be endowed with pure conduct."

After that, one makes good any deficiencies in the rite and makes confession with the hundred-syllable mantra:

oṃ vajra heruka samaya manupālaya heruka tvenopatiṣṭha dṛḍho me bhava sutoṣyo me bhava anurakto me bhava supoṣyo me bhava sarva siddhimme payaccha sarva karma su ca me cittaṃ śreyaḥ kuru hūṃ ha ha ha ha ho bhagavan sarva tathāgata vajra mā me muñca vajrī bhava mahā samaya satva a hūṃ phaṭaḥ

Having confessed with this, one requests the wisdom deities to depart with oṃ āḥ hūṃ vajra muḥ. The commitment deities are gathered into one's own heart, the heap of flowers is gathered into the center, and reciting oṃ āḥ hūṃ one sets it down.

The Rite of the Hevajra Maṇḍala, composed by the master Saroruha, is complete.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

"Action Heruka" renders ལས་ཀྱི་ཧེ་རུ་ཀ, the initial simple form of the deity assumed before the full generation. The meaning of སྐྱང་ནུལ in the benefits verse is uncertain. It is rendered as "cleaning and smoothing" the ground. The Tibetan list of offerings (flowers, incense, lamps, food, cymbals) does not match the accompanying mantras one for one: there are six mantras, including one for scent, but only five items. It is translated as it stands. Likewise, the text twice gives "water for the feet" for two different mantras. The meaning of དཀུགས in the attachment verse is uncertain and is rendered "bent low." In the third homage verse, the text abbreviates Vettālī as "Vettā." "The form nondual with Nairātmyā" refers to Hevajra in union with his consort.  In the first aspiration verse, the line rendered "keeping the commitments, a joyful vajra-holder" may also be read as "a Vajradhara of Hevajra," because the Tibetan word for "joyful" is the same element that forms Hevajra's name. The "six mudrās" are the six bone ornaments worn by the yogin. "Makes good any deficiencies in the rite" renders the technical term kha bskang: the hundred-syllable mantra is recited to supply whatever was lacking or faulty in the ritual.
