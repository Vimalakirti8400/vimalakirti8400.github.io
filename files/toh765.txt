The Ritual of Maṇibhadra the Yakṣa General (Maṇibhadrāya Yakṣasena Kalpa)

Toh 765 in the Kangyur is a ritual text known in Sanskrit as the Maṇibhadrāya Yakṣasena Kalpa and in Tibetan as "The Ritual of Maṇibhadra the Yakṣa of the Auspicious Jewel," with an English rendering of approximately "The Ritual of Maṇibhadra, General of the Yakṣas." It belongs to the dhāraṇī section of the Kangyur and centers on the yakṣa deity Maṇibhadra, a powerful protective figure associated with wealth and auspiciousness, presenting mantric invocations, dhāraṇīs, and practical ritual instructions for his propitiation. The text prescribes the fashioning of an image from specified woods and the performance of rites without the usual restrictions of auspicious days or fasting observances, reflecting a tantric or ritual-manual character. It was translated from Sanskrit into Tibetan by the Indian master Vidyākara Siṃha and the Tibetan translator monk Lhui Wangpo.

The dhāraṇī called "The Excellent One" is complete.

Translated by the Indian master Vidyākara Siṃha and the translator monk Lhui Wangpo.

There is no reckoning of days and no reckoning of lunar mansions; there is no fasting rite to be performed. From wood of either the arka or the nimba tree, fashion an image of Maṇibhadra the Auspicious Jewel. Place it in a position of honor before you, make offerings with flowers and all manner of other offerings, and remain in that place. At noon, anoint the belly of the Maṇibhadra image with white mustard and warm butter. If you then recite the vidyā one hundred thousand times, you will obtain one hundred gold coins, and these you should then give away.

The one made vajra from the syllable HŪṂ — enter into the charnel ground, and having learned the recitation well, in the evening the tantras, through accomplishment — here I have made supplication as a companion.

He, taking up the cloth of the charnel ground, wrathful and with reddened eyes, anointing the body with fragrant unguents and taking the charcoal of the charnel ground, should write what is to be accomplished.

Fill the limbs fully with ashes and charcoal. Bend the limbs and insert them. Place this upon the white lotus at the heart of the body of the image of the one to be accomplished. Take fresh human blood mixed with poison, beautify it with the juice of red rice husks, mix it with the body or bones, scatter it thoroughly and gather it together, roll it, cover the eyes with a thin black thread, and bind it. Smear charcoal on the outside, bury it in the charnel ground, and say, "I have killed it." Over the pit that has been filled, make the vajra sign with the hand and recite the mantra for reducing to ashes:
