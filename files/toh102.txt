The Noble Saṅghāṭī Sūtra, a Synonym of the Dharma (Ārya Saṅghāṭī Sūtra Dharma Paryāya)

The Noble Saṅghāṭī Sūtra, known in Sanskrit as the Ārya Saṅghāṭī Sūtra Dharma Paryāya and in Tibetan as Phakpa Zung gi Do chö kyi Namgrang, is a Mahāyāna sūtra belonging to the general sūtra literature of the Buddhist canon. It takes the form of a discourse delivered by the Buddha at Vulture Peak Mountain in Rājagṛha before a vast assembly of monks and bodhisattvas, and it is renowned for its emphasis on the extraordinary merit generated simply by hearing, reciting, or venerating the text itself. The sūtra is Toh 102 in the Kangyur, where it is classed among the general sūtras, and it has been particularly influential in Tibetan Buddhist practice as a text whose very recitation is held to purify negative karma and plant the seeds of future awakening.

In the language of India: Ārya Saṅghāṭī Sūtra Dharma Paryāya. In the language of Tibet: The Noble Saṅghāṭī Sūtra, a Synonym of the Dharma. First Section.

Homage to all buddhas and bodhisattvas.

Thus did I hear. At one time, the Blessed One was dwelling in Rājagṛha, on Vulture Peak Mountain, together with a great Saṅgha of thirty-two thousand monks, namely: the Venerable Kauṇḍinya Who Knows All, the Venerable Mahāmaudgalyāyana, the Venerable Śāriputra, the Venerable Mahākāśyapa, the Venerable Rāhula, the Venerable Bakula, the Venerable Surakṣita, the Venerable Subhadra, the Venerable Candanaśrī, the Venerable Viṣaghna, the Venerable Subhūti, the Venerable Nāvika, the Venerable Nandika, and others of this kind — thirty-two thousand monks in all.

Also present were sixty-two thousand bodhisattvas, great bodhisattvas, namely: the bodhisattva, great bodhisattva Maitreya; the bodhisattva, great bodhisattva Sarvadatta; the bodhisattva, great bodhisattva Kumāraśrī; the bodhisattva, great bodhisattva Kumārasthita; the bodhisattva, great bodhisattva Kumārabhadra; the bodhisattva, great bodhisattva Akṣaya; the bodhisattva, great bodhisattva Mañjuśrī; the bodhisattva, great bodhisattva Samantabhadra; the bodhisattva, great bodhisattva Priyadarśana; the bodhisattva, great bodhisattva Bhaiṣajyasena; the bodhisattva, great bodhisattva Vajrasena; and others of this kind — sixty-two thousand bodhisattvas, great bodhisattvas in all.

Also present were twelve thousand sons of the gods, namely: the son of the gods Bhavasiṃha, the son of the gods Bhadra, the son of the gods Subhadra, the son of the gods Dharmaruci, the son of the gods Candanagarbha, the son of the gods Candanasthāna, the son of the gods Candana, and others of this kind — twelve thousand sons of the gods in all.

Also present were eight thousand daughters of the gods, namely: the daughter of the gods Dundubhī, the daughter of the gods Śobhāvatī, the daughter of the gods Maheśvarā, the daughter of the gods Netraśrī, the daughter of the gods Prajāpatipratiṣṭhitā, the daughter of the gods Balādharā, the daughter of the gods Dhanaśrī, the daughter of the gods Subāhu, and others of this kind — eight thousand daughters of the gods in all.

Also present were eight thousand nāga kings, namely: the nāga king Anavalamba, the nāga king Elapatrī, the nāga king Atyuccagāmin, the nāga king Kumbhagarbha, the nāga king Kumbhaśīrṣa, the nāga king Sukṛta, the nāga king Suprīta, the nāga king Supatra, the nāga king Hastimukha, and others of this kind — eight thousand nāga kings in all.

All of them, turning toward Rājagṛha, turning toward Vulture Peak Mountain, and turning toward the Blessed One, came to that place. They arrived, bowed their heads to the feet of the Blessed One, circumambulated the Blessed One three times, and took their places in the presence of the Blessed One. The Blessed One, without saying anything, gave his consent.

Then the bodhisattva, great bodhisattva Sarvadatta rose from his seat, arranged his outer robe over one shoulder, placed his right knee on the ground, bowed with palms joined in the direction of the Blessed One, and addressed the Blessed One with these words:

Blessed One, tens of millions of gods, tens of millions of daughters of the gods,

tens of millions of bodhisattvas have assembled, Blessed One, along with tens of millions of śrāvakas and the kings of the nāgas, and they are gathered here to hear the Dharma. By what manner of entering into the way of Dharma does a Tathāgata, arhat, perfectly and fully awakened buddha cause elderly sentient beings, upon merely hearing it, to have all their karmic obscurations purified, and cause young sentient beings to strive with earnest application in wholesome qualities and attain what is supreme — so that they do not decline from those wholesome qualities, do not utterly decline, and will not fall away from them? Please teach well the manner of entering into such a way of Dharma.

When this was said, the Blessed One addressed the bodhisattva, great bodhisattva Sarvadatta with these words:

Sarvadatta, it is excellent, excellent that you wish to ask the Tathāgata about this matter. Therefore, Sarvadatta, listen well, take it to mind, and I shall explain it to you.

"So be it, Blessed One," replied the bodhisattva, great bodhisattva Sarvadatta, and he listened in the presence of the Blessed One. The Blessed One then spoke to him thus:

Sarvadatta, there is a category of Dharma (paryāya) called Zuṅ that is still in circulation in Jambudvīpa. If any person hears this Dharma category called Zuṅ, that person's five crimes of immediate retribution will be purified, and they will become irreversible from unsurpassed, perfect, and complete awakening.

Sarvadatta, what do you think? Would one say that those who hear this Zuṅ sūtra generate a mass of merit equal to the merit accumulated by a single Tathāgata? No, Sarvadatta, it should not be regarded in that way.

Sarvadatta asked: Then how should it be regarded?

The Blessed One declared: Sarvadatta, those bodhisattvas, great bodhisattvas who hear this Zuṅ sūtra will each generate a mass of merit equal to the mass of merit of as many Tathāgatas, arhats, perfectly and fully awakened buddhas as there are grains of sand in the river Gaṅgā.

Sarvadatta, all those who hear this Dharma category will become irreversible. All of them will come to behold the Tathāgata. All of them will become inseparable from beholding the Tathāgata. All of them will fully and perfectly awaken to unsurpassed, perfect, and complete awakening. As for all the wholesome qualities they will attain, the wicked Māra will not be able to impede them. Sarvadatta, all those who hear this Zuṅ sūtra will come to know arising and cessation.

Then all those bodhisattvas rose at that moment from their seats, arranged their outer robes over one shoulder, placed their right knees on the ground, and addressed the Blessed One:

Blessed One, how great is the mass of merit of a single Tathāgata?

The Blessed One declared: Sons of good family, hear the measure of the mass of merit of a single buddha. Consider, for example, all the drops of water in the great ocean, all the finest particles of dust in Jambudvīpa, and all the grains of sand in the river Gaṅgā — the mass of merit of a single buddha exceeds even the combined mass of merit of all the bodhisattvas abiding on the ten grounds who are equal in number to all of those combined.

Those sentient beings who hear this enumeration of Dharma categories will generate a mass of merit even greater than that — and the extent of that mass of merit cannot be fathomed by any process of counting. Sarvavikrāntagāmin, at that time and on that occasion, whoever hears these words and is filled with great delight will generate an immeasurable mass of merit.

Then the bodhisattva, the great being Sarvavikrāntagāmin, addressed the Blessed One with these words: Blessed One, which sentient beings are those who are utterly parched for the Dharma?

When this was asked, the Blessed One declared to the bodhisattva, the great being Sarvavikrāntagāmin: Sarvavikrāntagāmin, these two kinds of sentient beings are utterly parched for the Dharma. Which two? Sarvavikrāntagāmin, the first is a sentient being who holds an equal mind toward all sentient beings. The second is one who, having heard the Dharma, teaches it equally and correctly to all sentient beings.

The bodhisattva Sarvavikrāntagāmin asked: Blessed One, who is it that, having heard the Dharma, teaches it equally and correctly to all sentient beings?

The Blessed One declared: Sarvavikrāntagāmin, the first is one who, having heard the Dharma, dedicates it to awakening — and whenever one so dedicates it to awakening, one is thereby utterly parched for the Dharma on behalf of all sentient beings. The second is one who enters the Great Vehicle — such a one is always and constantly utterly parched for the Dharma.

Then tens of millions of gods, nāgas, humans, and goddesses rose from their seats and, pressing their palms together before the Blessed One, addressed him with these words: Blessed One, we too are utterly parched for the Dharma. Blessed One, please bring to complete fulfillment the aspirations of ourselves and of all sentient beings.

At that moment the Blessed One smiled. Then the bodhisattva, the great being Sarvavikrāntagāmin, rose from his seat and, turning to face the direction of the Blessed One, pressed his palms together and bowed, and addressed the Blessed One with these words: Blessed One, what is the cause and what is the condition for this smile?

The Blessed One then declared to the bodhisattva, the great being Sarvavikrāntagāmin: Sarvavikrāntagāmin, all those sentient beings who have gathered here and will fully awaken to unsurpassed and perfectly complete awakening — they will all come to accomplish the full range of a Tathāgata's conduct.

The bodhisattva Sarvavikrāntagāmin asked: Blessed One, by what cause and by what condition will the sentient beings who have gathered here fully awaken to unsurpassed and perfectly complete awakening?

The Blessed One declared: Sarvavikrāntagāmin, it is excellent, truly excellent, that you think to ask the Tathāgata about this matter. Sarvavikrāntagāmin, therefore listen well to this distinctive teaching on dedication.

Sarvavikrāntagāmin, long ago in the past, across incalculable eons, there appeared in the world a Tathāgata, an arhat, a perfectly and completely awakened buddha called Ratnašrī — endowed with knowledge and conduct, a Sugata, knower of the world, an unsurpassed guide and tamer of persons, teacher of gods and humans, a blessed buddha. Sarvavikrāntagāmin, at that time and on that occasion I was a young brahmin boy. All those sentient beings whom I had arranged and set within the wisdom of a buddha — at that time and on that occasion they had become deer in the wilderness,

At that time and on that occasion I made this aspiration: "May all those deer who are now utterly tormented by suffering be reborn in my buddha-field. May I, by my own hand, establish them individually in the wisdom of a buddha." The deer too, upon hearing those words, declared, "So may it be!" Through that root of virtue, Sarvavikrāntagāmin, those sentient beings have now arrived here, and they shall awaken to unsurpassed, perfect, and complete enlightenment.

Then the great bodhisattva Sarvavikrāntagāmin, having heard this great source of joy from the Blessed One, addressed him with these words: "Blessed One, for how long a lifespan will those sentient beings endure?"

The Blessed One declared: "The lifespan of those sentient beings will endure for eighty eons."

The bodhisattva Sarvavikrāntagāmin asked: "Blessed One, how long is an eon?"

The Blessed One declared: "Son of good family, listen. Suppose a certain person were to build a wall twelve leagues in breadth and three leagues in height, fill it entirely to the brim with sesame seeds, and then, after every thousand years had passed, take out a single sesame seed from that wall full of sesame. Even if that person were to exhaust all those sesame seeds in that manner, so that the wall was left without foundation or base, still the eon would not be exhausted. Sarvavikrāntagāmin, consider another example as well: suppose there were a mountain in a great wilderness fifty leagues across and twelve leagues in height, and a certain person were to build a dwelling on the face of that mountain, and every hundred years were to wipe it once with a cloth of Kāśī silk — if by doing so the mountain were eventually worn away to nothing, still the eon would not be exhausted. Sarvavikrāntagāmin, such is the measure of an eon."

Then the great bodhisattva Sarvavikrāntagāmin rose from his seat and addressed the Blessed One thus: "Blessed One, if even a single dedication can produce such a vast accumulation of merit that those beings will dwell happily for a lifespan of eighty eons, what need is there to speak of serving the teaching of the Tathāgata with the greatest frequency and devotion?"

The Blessed One declared: "Son of good family, listen. If merely hearing this Dhāraṇī teaching results in a lifespan of fourteen hundred thousand eons, what then need be said of Sarvavikrāntagāmin copying the Dhāraṇī Sūtra in writing and reading it? Sarvavikrāntagāmin will generate an exceedingly vast accumulation of merit. Whoever bows down to the Dhāraṇī Sūtra from the depths of their heart with a pure and faithful mind will remember their past lives for ninety-nine eons, will become a wheel-turning king for sixty eons, and in this very life will be beloved by all. Sarvavikrāntagāmin, such a one will not die by the sword, will not die by poison, and will not be harmed by spells. At the moment of death too, ninety-nine million buddhas will appear directly before them, and those blessed buddhas will say: 'Noble person, through hearing this great Dhāraṇī Sūtra teaching well proclaimed, you have generated this accumulation of merit.

Those ninety-nine million blessed buddhas will also each prophesy in their own respective world-realms. So take heart and do not be afraid, for how much more is this so for one who has heard in full and at great length this entire vast compendium of Dharma of the Dhāraṇī Sūtra.'

Then the bodhisattva, the great being Sarvavikrāntagāmin, addressed the Blessed One with these words: 'Blessed One, if I too were to listen to this vast compendium of Dharma of the Dhāraṇī Sūtra, how great an accumulation of merit would I generate?'

The Blessed One declared: 'Sarvavikrāntagāmin, that sentient being would generate an accumulation of merit equal to the accumulation of merit of all the blessed buddhas as numerous as the grains of sand in the Ganges.'

Sarvavikrāntagāmin said: 'Blessed One, as for my hearing this vast compendium of Dharma of the Dhāraṇī Sūtra — Blessed One, I am never sated by it.'

The Blessed One declared: 'Sarvavikrāntagāmin, excellent, excellent! It is well that you are insatiable for the Dharma. Sarvavikrāntagāmin, even I myself am insatiable for the Dharma — how much more then could ordinary childish beings ever be expected to be sated by it?

Sarvavikrāntagāmin, whatever son of good family or daughter of good family generates faith in the Great Vehicle will not fall into wrong states of existence for a thousand eons, will not be born as an animal for five thousand eons, will not be born with a dull mind for twenty-two thousand eons, will not be born among peoples of the borderlands for eighty thousand eons, will be courageous in giving generously for twenty thousand eons, will be born in the realm of the gods for twenty-five thousand eons, and will lead a life of celibacy for thirty-five thousand eons. Thereafter, for forty thousand eons they will go forth from the household life. For fifty thousand eons they will uphold the Dharma. For sixty-five thousand eons they will cultivate the meditation on mindfulness of death.

Sarvavikrāntagāmin, not even the slightest non-virtuous action will arise in such a son or daughter of good family. The evil Māra will find no opportunity to seize upon them. They will never be born again in a mother's womb.

Sarvavikrāntagāmin, those who hear this compendium of Dharma, in whatever place they may be born, will not fall into wrong states of existence for ninety-five countless eons. For eighty thousand eons they will retain what they have heard. For a thousand eons they will abandon the taking of life. For ninety-nine thousand eons they will abandon false speech. For thirteen thousand eons they will abandon divisive speech. Sarvavikrāntagāmin, sentient beings who hear this compendium of Dharma are rare indeed.'

Then the bodhisattva, the great being Sarvavikrāntagāmin, rose from his seat, arranged his upper robe over one shoulder, placed his right knee on the ground, joined his palms in homage toward the Blessed One, and addressed the Blessed One with these words: 'Blessed One, as for those who reject this compendium of Dharma — how great an accumulation of non-virtuous karma will they generate?'

The Blessed One declared: 'Sarvavikrāntagāmin, it will be vast.'

Sarvavikrāntagāmin asked: 'Blessed One, how vast an accumulation of non-virtuous karma will those sentient beings generate?'

The Blessed One declared: 'Sarvavikrāntagāmin, do not ask me all at once about the accumulation of non-virtuous karma.'

Sarvavikrāntagāmin, one who reviles even a single sūtra generates a far greater heap of non-virtue than one who arouses a thought of malice toward Tathāgatas, arhats, perfectly complete buddhas as numerous as the grains of sand in twelve Ganges rivers. And those, Sarvavikrāntagāmin, who arouse a thought of harm toward the Great Vehicle will generate an even greater heap of non-virtue than that. Sarvavikrāntagāmin, those sentient beings are utterly burned up — burned up indeed.

Sarvavikrāntagāmin asked: 'Blessed One, is it not possible to liberate those sentient beings?'

The Blessed One declared: 'Sarvavikrāntagāmin, that is so — it is not possible to liberate them. Sarvavikrāntagāmin, consider this analogy: suppose a certain person has had his head cut off, and then honey, molasses, sugar, butter, sesame oil, or any ointment suitable for anointing is applied to that head. Sarvavikrāntagāmin, what do you think — could that sentient being rise again?'

Sarvavikrāntagāmin replied: 'Blessed One, there is no possibility of that.'

The Blessed One declared: 'Sarvavikrāntagāmin, yet consider another case: there is a certain other person who strikes another sentient being with a sharp weapon, and though a single blow is not enough to sever the life, Sarvavikrāntagāmin, a wound will arise. If medicine is applied, that wound will heal. Once healed, the person, remembering the suffering, thinks: "Now I understand — henceforth I will never again engage in sinful, non-virtuous actions and will not accumulate them." In just that way, Sarvavikrāntagāmin, at the very moment that person remembers the suffering, he fully abandons all sin. At that time he will bring all phenomena directly into view, and having brought all phenomena directly into view, he will at that time fully perfect all virtuous phenomena.

Sarvavikrāntagāmin, consider this further analogy: just as a person who has died cannot be helped by parents who grieve and lament over him, in the same way, Sarvavikrāntagāmin, those sentient beings who are ordinary, childlike folk are unable to benefit either themselves or others. Just as those parents are cut off from hope, so too will those sentient beings be cut off from hope at the time of death.

Sarvavikrāntagāmin, these two things cut sentient beings off from hope. What are the two? This is to say: the first is a sentient being who commits sinful acts or induces others to commit them; the other is abandoning the sacred Dharma. These two are what cut sentient beings off from hope at the time of death.'

The bodhisattva Sarvavikrāntagāmin asked: 'Blessed One, what destiny will those sentient beings have? What will their next life be?'

The Blessed One declared: 'Sarvavikrāntagāmin, the destiny of those sentient beings who revile the sacred Dharma is without end, and their future lives too are without end. Sarvavikrāntagāmin, those sentient beings who abandon the sacred Dharma will, for an aeon, experience feelings in the great hell of Wailing. For an aeon in Crushing and Destroying; for an aeon in the Hot; for an aeon in the Intensely Hot; for an aeon in the great hell of Black Lines; for an aeon in the great hell of Avīci (the Unrelenting); for an aeon in the great hell called Hair-Raising; and for an aeon in the great hell called Kyi-hud — Sarvavikrāntagāmin, in those eight great hells they will experience suffering for eight aeons.'

Then the bodhisattva, the great being Sarvavikrāntagāmin, addressed the Blessed One with these words: 'Blessed One, this is suffering! O Sugata, this is suffering! I have no joy in hearing it.'

Then the Blessed One at that time spoke these verses:

How beings in the hells
intensely experience such suffering —
these words of great terror
you have no joy in hearing.

Whoever performs wholesome actions
will come to be in a state of good;
whoever performs unwholesome actions
will likewise come to be in a state of suffering.

One who does not know the cause of happiness,
bound by the suffering of grief
and the suffering of birth and death —
the childish are forever in suffering.

Those who keep constantly in mind the supreme buddha,
they who are wise are in a state of happiness;
those who have faith in the Great Vehicle
will not go to the lower destinies.

So too, Sarvavikrāntagāmin —
thoroughly urged on by former karma,
even a small action that is done
will yield inexhaustible fruit.

In the supremely excellent field of buddha-fields,
if a seed is planted, the fruit is great —
just as from planting a few seeds
one comes to enjoy abundant fruit.

Those who rejoice in the Victor's teaching,
they who are wise will come to be happy;
they abandon all negative deeds
and perform many wholesome ones.

Those who offer even a hair's-breadth of giving
to my teaching —
for eighty thousand aeons
they will have great enjoyment and abundant wealth;
and wherever they are reborn,
they will be ever endowed with generosity.
Such is the buddha-field of giving:
that profound one yields great fruit.

Then the bodhisattva, the great being Sarvavikrāntagāmin, addressed the Blessed One with these words: "Blessed One, how should I seek the Dharma as the Blessed One has taught it? Blessed One, how does one, upon hearing the categories of the Dharma of the Sūtra of Pairs, come to perfectly retain the roots of virtue?"

The Blessed One declared: "Sarvavikrāntagāmin, one should understand that whoever has paid homage and offered all the requisites of well-being to Tathāgatas, arhats, perfectly and completely awakened buddhas as numerous as the grains of sand of twelve Ganges rivers — the mass of merit from whoever hears these categories of the Dharma of the Sūtra of Pairs is to be understood as equal to that."

The bodhisattva Sarvavikrāntagāmin said: "Blessed One, how does one come to perfect the roots of virtue?"

When he had thus spoken, the Blessed One addressed the bodhisattva, the great being Sarvavikrāntagāmin, with these words: "Whatever those roots of virtue are, they should be understood as equal to a Tathāgata."

"Blessed One, what is the meaning of those roots of virtue being understood as equal to a Tathāgata?"

The Blessed One declared: "Sarvavikrāntagāmin, one who teaches the Dharma should be understood as equal to a Tathāgata."

Sarvavikrāntagāmin asked: "Blessed One, who is a teacher of Dharma?"

The Blessed One declared: "Whoever proclaims the Sūtra of Pairs — that one is a teacher of Dharma."

Sarvavikrāntagāmin said: "Blessed One, if those who merely hear the categories of the Dharma of the Sūtra of Pairs generate such a mass of merit, what need is there to speak of those who write it down and read it? What mass of merit will those ones generate?"

The Blessed One declared: "Sarvavikrāntagāmin, listen well. Consider this: in each of the four directions there abide, for twelve aeons, Tathāgatas, arhats, perfectly and completely awakened buddhas as numerous as the grains of sand of twelve Ganges rivers, and they teach the Dharma — yet even to attempt to describe the mass of merit from writing down this Sūtra of Pairs is beyond the power of any comprehension or verbal expression. Even all the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, as numerous as the grains of sand of forty-eight Ganges rivers, could not express that mass of merit from writing it down — so what need is there to speak of those who write it down, contemplate it,

read it — how much more so those who read it and become a treasury of Dharma!

The bodhisattva Sarvāśūra asked: Blessed One, how great will the mass of merit from reading it be?

Then at that moment the Blessed One declared these verses:

The virtue of those who read
even a single verse of four lines —
the Victors as numerous as the grains of sand
of eighty-four Ganges rivers,
who have here read the Sūtra of Pairs,
even should they speak without ceasing
of whatever merit there may be from that,
that merit would never be exhausted.
The Dharma taught by the buddhas
is rare and without limit.

And at that very moment, eighty-four hundred billion gods, turning toward the place where the teaching of the categories of Dharma of the Sūtra of Pairs was being given, pressed their palms together, bowed, and addressed the Blessed One with these words: Blessed One, how excellent, how excellent, that the Blessed One has placed such a treasury of Dharma as this here in Jambudvīpa!

Then a different group of eighteen hundred billion naked ascetics (tīrthikas) came to where the Blessed One was and addressed him thus: Victory to the ascetic Gautama! The Blessed One declared: The Tathāgata is always victorious. You naked tīrthikas — how could you possibly be victorious? They said: Be victorious. May the ascetic Gautama be victorious. The Blessed One declared: I do not see any victory in you. You abide in confusion — how could you be victorious? You naked ones, listen, and I shall speak what is of benefit to you. There is no ease in a childish mind. How could you be victorious? With the eyes of a buddha I shall also teach you the profound path.

Then those naked ascetics became angry at the Blessed One and gave rise to a faithless mind. At that moment Śakra (Indra), lord of the gods, shook his vajra, and those eighteen hundred billion naked ascetics, seized with terror and overcome with great suffering, wept and shed tears — and the Tathāgata also made his body invisible.

Then those naked ascetics, weeping, their faces filled with tears, not seeing the Blessed One, spoke these verses:

Here there is no ally, no companion.
Likewise there is no father, no mother.
It appears utterly like a desolate wilderness —
empty dwellings, no place of shelter.
Here there is no water,
no trees, no birds.
Here no person is to be seen.
Without a protector, we experience suffering;
not seeing the Tathāgata,
we suffer what is unbearable and great.

Then at that moment those eighteen hundred billion naked ascetics rose from their seats, placed both knees on the ground, and, crying out, made their appeal in song:

O Tathāgata, compassionate one,
perfectly awakened, supreme among the two-footed —
please act for our benefit!
We beseech you: protect these wretched beings!

Then the Blessed One smiled and said to the bodhisattva Sarvāśūra: Sarvāśūra, go and teach the Dharma to those other naked tīrthikas.

When the Blessed One had said this, the bodhisattva, the great being Sarvāśūra, said to the Blessed One: Blessed One, if Mount Kailāsa were to bow its peak toward the king of mountains, Mount Meru, without being pressed down upon it — in just the same way, how could I teach the Dharma while the Tathāgata himself is present?

The Blessed One declared: Kharoc, son of good family — go, and by the skillful means of the Tathāgatas, look throughout the world-realms of the ten directions at wherever Tathāgatas appear, and observe the seats upon which the Tathāgatas have settled —

"Sarvāśūra — I myself will teach the Dharma to those naked tīrthikas."

The bodhisattva Sarvāśūra said: "Blessed One, shall I go by my own miraculous power, or by the power of the Tathāgata's miraculous power, or by whose miraculous power shall I travel?"

The Blessed One declared: "Sarvāśūra, go by the blessing of your own miraculous power. Sarvāśūra, return by the power of the Tathāgata."

Then the bodhisattva Sarvāśūra rose from his seat, circumambulated the Blessed One, and vanished from that very spot.

Then the Blessed One taught the Dharma to those naked tīrthikas: "Friends, birth is suffering — birth itself is suffering. Once one is born, many terrors of suffering arise. Once one is born, the terrors of illness arise. From illness, the terrors of aging arise. From aging, the terrors of death arise."

"Blessed One, what is it that is called 'from birth, the terror of birth arises'? Once one is born as a human being, many terrors arise: the terror of kings arises, the terror of thieves arises, the terror of fire arises, the terror of poison arises, the terror of water arises, the terror of wind arises, the terror of whirlpools arises, and the terror of the karma one has created arises."

When the Blessed One had taught the Dharma in these and many other such aspects, taking birth as the topic, at that very time those naked tīrthikas were seized with great terror and said these words: "From this day forward, we shall never again delight in birth."

When the Blessed One taught this Dharma-enumeration of the Coupled Sūtras, those eighteen billion naked tīrthikas fully completed the unsurpassed, perfect, and complete awakening. And from his own retinue, eighteen thousand bodhisattvas became firmly established on the ten grounds, and all of them displayed miraculous emanations such as these: the form of a horse, the form of an elephant, the form of a tiger, the form of a garuḍa, the form of Mount Meru, the form of a swastika-pattern, and some appeared in the form of wish-granting trees — and all of them were seated cross-legged on lotus thrones.

Nine billion bodhisattvas were arrayed on the Blessed One's right side, and nine billion bodhisattvas were arrayed on the Blessed One's left side. The Tathāgata remained ever in meditative equipoise, appearing to teach the Dharma through skillful means.

On the seventh day, the Tathāgata extended the palm of his hand, and through this the Blessed One knew that the bodhisattva, the great being Sarvāśūra, was coming from the world-realm of Padmottara. The bodhisattva, the great being Sarvāśūra, had traveled to that world-realm of Padmottara by the blessing of his own miraculous power, and the journey there had taken seven days.

When the Blessed One extended his hand, at that very moment the bodhisattva Sarvāśūra was present before the Blessed One. He circumambulated the Blessed One three times, and then, with a concentrated mind, facing toward the Blessed One, he joined his palms and bowed, and spoke these words to the Blessed One:

"Blessed One, when I traveled to all the world-realms of the ten directions, with one miraculous feat I beheld nine billion nine hundred and ninety thousand buddha-fields, and with two miraculous feats I beheld ten billion Blessed Ones,

With the seventh miraculous feat I traveled to the world-realm of Padmottara, and along the way I beheld one hundred thousand billion immovable buddha-fields. Blessed One, from that point onward I beheld the miraculous feats of the Blessed Ones, the buddhas, and in ninety-two thousand billion buddha-fields I saw Tathāgatas teaching the Dharma. And under that same canopy, in eighty thousand billion buddha-fields, I beheld eighty thousand billion Tathāgatas, arhats, perfectly and fully awakened buddhas who had appeared in the world. I paid homage to all those Tathāgatas, and then returned. Blessed One, under that same canopy, beyond thirty-nine thousand billion buddha-fields, in all those thirty-nine thousand billion buddha-fields, thirty-nine thousand billion bodhisattvas had arisen, and under that very canopy each had fully and perfectly awakened to unsurpassed, perfect, complete awakening. Blessed One, I circumambulated those Tathāgatas, those arhats, those perfectly and fully awakened buddhas three times each, and then they vanished beyond the reach of miraculous power. Blessed One, in sixty billion buddha-fields as well I beheld Blessed Ones, buddhas. Blessed One, I paid homage to all those buddha-fields and all those Blessed Ones, the buddhas, and then came back. Blessed One, in another eighty billion buddha-fields I beheld Tathāgatas who were passing into complete nirvāṇa. I paid homage to those Tathāgatas as well, and then came back. Blessed One, furthermore, in ninety-five billion buddha-fields I witnessed the sacred Dharma falling into decline, and at that, Blessed One, a thought arose in me and I shed tears. I also witnessed many gods, nāgas, yakṣas, rākṣasas, and beings possessing form in the desire realm weeping and pierced by great pangs of grief. Likewise, Blessed One, I witnessed other buddha-fields — complete with their oceans, their Mount Merus, and their every region of earth — consumed entirely in flames, and having paid homage there too, I came back in a state of utter despair. Blessed One, even before I had reached the world-realm of Padmottara, along the way I beheld five hundred thousand billion thrones set out for Blessed Ones — one hundred thousand billion thrones in the southern direction, one hundred thousand billion thrones in the northern direction, one hundred thousand billion thrones in the eastern direction, one hundred thousand billion thrones in the western direction, and one hundred thousand billion thrones in the direction above. Blessed One, all those thrones were made exclusively of the seven precious substances, and upon every one of those thrones a Tathāgata sat and taught the Dharma. Blessed One, I was filled with wonder at this, and I asked those Tathāgatas: "Blessed One, what is the name of this world-realm?" Those Tathāgatas declared: "Son of good family, this world-realm is called Padmottara." Blessed One, I then circumambulated those Tathāgatas, and asked them: "What is the name of the Tathāgata of this buddha-field?" They declared: "The Tathāgata, arhat, perfectly and fully awakened buddha is called Padmagarbha, and he performs the work of a buddha in this buddha-field."

At that point I asked the following: "Even though there may be many tens of millions of Tathāgatas, arhats, perfectly and fully awakened buddhas bearing the name Padmagarbha, which one is it that I have not yet found?" That Blessed One declared: "Son of good family, I have shown you the Tathāgata, arhat, perfectly and fully awakened buddha named Padmagarbha."

Then the forms of all those Tathāgatas vanished, and there remained only the forms of bodhisattvas. A single Tathāgata remained, and I bowed my head at the feet of that Tathāgata and approached him, whereupon a throne appeared and I took my seat upon it. Then, Blessed One, many thrones appeared, and I saw that none had come to sit upon them. I said to that Tathāgata: "Blessed One, I see that no sentient being whatsoever has come to sit upon these thrones." That Blessed One declared: "Sentient beings who have not cultivated virtuous roots lack the power to sit upon these thrones."

I asked: "Blessed One, what virtuous roots must be cultivated in order for one to sit upon those thrones?" That Blessed One declared: "Son of good family, listen. Whatever sentient beings have heard the enumeration of Dharma teachings of the Paired Sūtra — by that virtuous root they will come to sit upon those thrones. And needless to say this applies to those who write it out and read it aloud. It is because you, Sarvavikrāntin, heard the enumeration of Dharma teachings of the Paired Sūtra that you came to sit upon this throne; had that not been so, how could you have entered into this buddha-field?"

When that Blessed One had spoken thus, I said to that Blessed One: "Blessed One, how great a mass of merit does one generate by hearing this enumeration of Dharma teachings of the Paired Sūtra?"

At that, the Blessed One Padmagarbha, the Tathāgata, smiled. I asked that Blessed One why he smiled: "Blessed One, for what reason and on what account does the Tathāgata smile?" That Blessed One declared: "Son of good family, listen, for the bodhisattva, the great being Sarvavikrāntin, has attained great power. Consider this analogy: suppose someone were to become a wheel-turning king (cakravartin) ruling over the four continents, and were to sow sesame across the fields of all four continents. Sarvavikrāntin, what do you think — would many seeds grow?"

The bodhisattva Sarvavikrāntin replied: "Blessed One, they would be many. Sugata, they would be many."

The Blessed One declared: "Sarvavikrāntin, suppose then that some sentient being were to gather all those sesame seeds into a single heap, and another being were to take those sesame seeds one by one from that heap and set them down elsewhere. Sarvavikrāntin, what do you think — could that being count or measure those sesame seeds?"

The bodhisattva Sarvavikrāntin replied: "Blessed One, no. Sugata, no. There would be no way to count or measure those sesame seeds."

"Sarvavikrāntin, in just the same way, the mass of merit arising from the enumeration of Dharma teachings of the Paired Sūtra cannot be measured by anyone other than a Tathāgata. Sarvavikrāntin, consider further: suppose there were as many Tathāgatas as there are sesame seeds, and all of them together were to fully proclaim the merit arising from the virtuous root of hearing the Dharma enumeration of the Paired teachings — and even then, even with the aid of such analogies, that merit would not be exhausted. How much more so, then, is this the case for one who writes it out, reads it aloud, and has others write it out!"

The bodhisattva Sarvavikrāntin asked: "Blessed One, how great a store of merit does writing it out produce?"

The Blessed One declared: "Son of good family, listen. Consider all the grasses and trees of the great trichiliocosm cut down to pieces of one finger-breadth each — that is one analogy. Now, Sarvavikrāntin, hear a second: consider all the rocks, ravines, earth, and finest dust particles of the great trichiliocosm — as many as there are of them — and suppose each became a wheel-turning king (cakravartin) reigning over the four continents. Could the merit of all those kings be measured?"

Sarvavikrāntin replied: "Blessed One, it could not — none but a Tathāgata could measure it."

"Just so, Sarvavikrāntin, the mass of merit from writing out the Dharma enumeration of the Paired Sūtra likewise cannot be measured. Indeed, one who writes down even a single syllable of this Dharma enumeration generates a mass of merit far greater than the combined merit of however many wheel-turning kings there may be — for those kings, with all their abundant merit, are not equal to it.

"In the same way, Sarvavikrāntin, the merit of a great bodhisattva who upholds and practices the sacred Dharma of the Great Vehicle cannot be surpassed by any wheel-turning king. And likewise, the mass of merit from writing out the Dharma enumeration of the Paired Sūtra cannot be measured by any such comparison.

"Sarvavikrāntin, this Paired Sūtra is a revealer of treasure-stores of merit. It pacifies all mental afflictions. It causes the lamp of all phenomena to blaze brightly. It defeats every wicked māra. It causes the abode of every bodhisattva to blaze with radiance. It fully accomplishes the accomplishment of all phenomena."

When this had been declared, the great bodhisattva Sarvavikrāntin spoke to the Blessed One thus: "Blessed One, the holy life (brahmacarya) practiced here is an exceedingly difficult practice. Why so? Because, Blessed One, the conduct of a Tathāgata is rare, and in just the same way the holy life too is rare. When one practices the holy life, one comes to behold a Tathāgata directly — one comes to see a Tathāgata day and night. When one directly beholds a Tathāgata and gazes upon a Tathāgata day and night, one sees a buddha-field. When one sees a buddha-field, one sees every treasure within it. At the moment of one's death, no fear will arise. Never again will one be born in a mother's womb. Never again will one be a being of sorrow. Never again will one be bound by the snare of craving."

When this had been said, the Blessed One spoke thus to the great bodhisattva Sarvavikrāntin: "Sarvavikrāntin, the arising of Tathāgatas is rare and hard to find."

Sarvavikrāntin replied: "Blessed One, it is rare and hard to find. O Sugata, it is rare and hard to find."

The Blessed One declared: "Sarvavikrāntin, in just the same way this Dharma enumeration of the Paired Sūtra is likewise rare and hard to find. Sarvavikrāntin, whoever hears this Dharma enumeration of the Paired teaching resounding in their ear will, for eighty aeons, remember their past lives."

For sixty thousand aeons one will attain the status of a wheel-turning king. For eight thousand aeons one will attain the status of Śakra (Indra, king of the gods). For twenty thousand aeons one will be born with a share of fortune equal to the gods of the Pure Abodes. For thirty-eight thousand aeons one will become a great Brahmā. For ninety-nine thousand aeons one will not fall into a lower rebirth. For one hundred thousand aeons one will not be born among hungry ghosts (pretas). For twenty-eight thousand aeons one will not be born among animals. For thirteen thousand aeons one will not be born in the class of asuras. One will not die by the sword. For twenty-five thousand aeons one will not come to have impaired wisdom. For seven thousand aeons one will come to practise wisdom. For nine thousand aeons one will be beautiful and lovely to behold, perfectly endowed with a form body like that of a Tathāgata. For fifteen thousand aeons one will not be born as a woman. For sixteen thousand aeons one will not be afflicted by bodily illness. For thirty-five thousand aeons one will come to possess the divine eye. For nineteen thousand aeons one will not be born in nāga realms of rebirth. For sixty thousand aeons one will not be overpowered by the force of anger. For seven thousand aeons one will not be born in a household of poverty. For eighty thousand aeons one will come to enjoy both continents, and even when poverty does befall one, one will still attain just such happiness as this. For twelve thousand aeons one will not be born in a state of blindness. For thirteen thousand aeons one will not be born in the lower realms. For eleven thousand aeons one will speak with patience. And at the time of death, when the final moment of consciousness ceases, one's perceptions will not be distorted, and one will not be overpowered by anger. At that moment one will directly behold, in the eastern direction, as many blessed buddhas as there are grains of sand in twelve Ganges rivers. In the southern direction one will directly behold as many blessed buddhas as there are grains of sand in twenty koṭis of Ganges rivers. In the western direction one will directly behold as many blessed buddhas as there are grains of sand in twenty-five Ganges rivers. In the northern direction one will directly behold as many blessed buddhas as there are grains of sand in eighty Ganges rivers. In the direction above one will directly behold ninety thousand koṭis of blessed buddhas. In the direction below one will directly behold as many blessed buddhas as there are grains of sand in one hundred koṭis of Ganges rivers. And all of those, addressing that son of good family, will offer this encouragement: "Son of good family, because you have heard this Dharma enumeration of the Paired teaching, you will come to possess in future lives qualities and benefits and happiness of just this measure — have no fear!"

"Noble son, do you see all those Tathāgatas — as many as grains of sand in countless hundreds of thousands of koṭis of Ganges rivers?"

Sarvavikrāntin replied: "Blessed One, I see them. O Sugata, I see them."

The Blessed One declared: "Noble son, those Tathāgatas have come to look upon you."

Sarvavikrāntin replied: "What wholesome action have I performed that so many Tathāgatas should come to where I am?"

The Blessed One declared: "Noble son, listen well."

"Because you obtained a human body and heard this teaching of the Paired Sūtra with your own ears, a mass of merit of just that measure has arisen for you."

Sarvavikrāntin replied: "Blessed One, if my mass of merit has grown to that measure merely from hearing it, what need is there to speak of hearing it in its entirety and with full comprehension?"

The Blessed One declared: "Excellent! Excellent! Noble son, listen now — I shall speak of the merit of a single four-line verse. Noble son, consider this example: even greater than the mass of merit accumulated by Tathāgatas — arhats, perfectly and fully awakened buddhas — as numerous as the grains of sand in thirteen Ganges rivers would be the mass of merit accumulated by one who hears even a single four-line verse from this Paired Sūtra. And if merely hearing a single four-line verse produces a mass of merit greater than making offerings to Tathāgatas, arhats, perfectly and fully awakened buddhas as numerous as the grains of sand in thirteen Ganges rivers, what need is there to speak of hearing it in its entirety and with full comprehension?

"Noble son, now listen to the merit of one who hears this Paired Sūtra in its entirety and with full comprehension. Suppose that throughout the entire world-system of a great three-thousand-fold universe every sesame seed that could be packed in were planted, and there were that many wheel-turning kings. Then suppose there were a certain man of wealth, with great possessions and abundant riches, and he gave gifts to all those wheel-turning kings. Yet the mass of merit produced by giving to a single stream-enterer (srotāpanna) surpasses even that.

"Suppose all the sentient beings of the great three-thousand-fold universe became stream-enterers, and someone gave gifts to every single one of them. Yet the mass of merit produced by giving to a single once-returner (sakṛdāgāmin) surpasses even that.

"Suppose all the sentient beings of the great three-thousand-fold universe became once-returners, and someone gave gifts to every single one of them. Yet the mass of merit produced by giving to a single non-returner (anāgāmin) surpasses even that.

"Suppose all the sentient beings of the great three-thousand-fold universe became non-returners, and someone gave gifts to every single one of them. Yet the mass of merit produced by giving to a single arhat surpasses even that.

"Suppose all the sentient beings of the great three-thousand-fold universe became arhats, and someone gave gifts to every single one of them. Yet the mass of merit produced by giving to a single pratyekabuddha surpasses even that.

"Suppose all the sentient beings of the great three-thousand-fold universe became pratyekabuddhas, and someone gave gifts to every single one of them. Yet the mass of merit produced by giving to a single bodhisattva surpasses even that.

"Suppose all the sentient beings of the great three-thousand-fold universe became bodhisattvas, and someone gave gifts to every single one of them. Yet the mass of merit produced by one who generates a mind of faith toward even a single Tathāgata — and by one who generates a mind of faith toward Tathāgatas filling that entire great three-thousand-fold world-system —"

One who merely hears this enumeration of Dharma teachings from the paired sūtra will produce a mass of merit far greater than all of that. What need is there, then, to speak of Sarvashūra who writes down, retains, recites, and masters this enumeration of Dharma teachings? And what need is there even to speak of Sarvashūra who bows down with a devoted mind toward this enumeration of Dharma teachings?

Sarvashūra, what do you think? Are all ordinary childlike beings capable of hearing this?

Even those who do hear it will not generate faith.

Sarvashūra, listen. Among sentient beings who are ordinary childlike beings, is there any who is able to fathom the depths of the great ocean?

The reply came: Blessed One, there is not.

The Blessed One declared: Sarvashūra, is there any sentient being who can drain the ocean with a single palm of the hand?

The reply came: Blessed One, there is not. Sugata, there is not.

The Blessed One declared: Sarvashūra, just as there is no sentient being capable of drying up the great ocean, so too there is no sentient being whose inclination is toward the lesser who can listen to this enumeration of Dharma teachings. Sarvashūra, those who have not seen Tathāgatas as numerous as the grains of sand of eighty Ganges rivers multiplied by ten million are not able to write down this enumeration of Dharma teachings from the paired sūtra. Those who have seen Tathāgatas as numerous as the grains of sand of ninety Ganges rivers are not able to listen to this enumeration of Dharma teachings. Those who have not seen Tathāgatas numbering one hundred thousand million will, upon hearing this enumeration of Dharma teachings, turn away from it and abandon it. Sarvashūra, those who have seen Tathāgatas as numerous as the grains of sand of one hundred million Ganges rivers — upon hearing this enumeration of Dharma teachings, they will generate a devoted mind, they will rejoice, they will come to know it just as it is in its entirety, and they will have faith in this enumeration of Dharma teachings from the paired sūtra and will not abandon it.

Sarvashūra, listen. Whoever writes down even a single four-line verse from this paired sūtra — for those beings, ninety-five thousand million world-systems will be transcended, and the buddha-field of those beings will become just like the world-system of Sukhāvatī. Sarvashūra, the lifespan of those sentient beings will be capable of enduring for eighty-four thousand aeons.

Sarvashūra, listen. Bodhisattvas, great beings, who have heard even just a single four-line verse from this enumeration of Dharma teachings of the paired sūtra — it is like this, Sarvashūra: a sentient being who has committed, caused others to commit, or rejoiced in the committing of the five actions of immediate retribution, if that being hears even a single four-line verse from this enumeration of Dharma teachings of the paired sūtra, those five sinful actions of immediate retribution will be purified.

The Second Fascicle.

Sarvashūra, listen — other qualities too shall be explained. It is like this: consider a sentient being who has destroyed a stūpa, created a schism in the Saṅgha, distracted a bodhisattva from meditative absorption, obstructed the arising of a buddha's wisdom, and taken a human life. Such a being later comes to feel remorse and grief, thinking: "By this body I have ruined myself. I am ruined in the world beyond as well. I am unworthy." A great sense of that unworthiness arises, and such a being experiences feelings of suffering, experiences feelings of unbearable pain. Sarvashūra, that sentient being will be abandoned by all sentient beings. They will be reviled. That sentient being is scorched, rendered unfit — having made themselves unfit for both worldly and transmundane Dharma.

Just as a burnt tree-stump remains as it is for many aeons, so too will that person remain in such a state. Just as a well-painted house becomes ugly when its main beam is charred, so too will that person become. Even in this world he will be without beauty. Wherever he goes, sentient beings will revile and beat him, and he will be threatened by hunger and thirst, and will find even the slightest thing to be of no avail. Because of this he will experience feelings of suffering, threatened by hunger and thirst, by the causes of being reviled, and recalling that he has destroyed a stūpa and committed the five acts of immediate retribution, he will think: "Where shall I go? Who is my protector?" A mind weary of suffering will arise in him, and he will think to himself: "Here there is no one to protect me. I shall go into the mountains and ravines and there bring about my death."

He speaks:

"Because I have committed evil acts,
I am always like a charred tree-stump.
In this world I am without beauty,
and in the next life I will not be beautiful either.
Neither within the house am I pleasing,
nor outside it either.
Through the cause of wrongdoing I have done evil,
and by that I shall go to the lower realms.
In the next life I will be in suffering —
in these lower realms, where shall I abide?
Weeping, my eyes filled with tears, I cry out aloud,
and even the gods hear my words.
Alas! In the world beyond,
going to the lower realms, my hopes are shattered."

The gods then spoke to him:

"Foolish man, let go of that
mind of suffering, and leave off.
You slew your father and slew your mother;
I committed the five acts of immediate retribution.
I have no refuge, no ally.
I will experience feelings of suffering.
Going to a mountain peak,
I shall then cast this body away."

"Foolish man, do not go!
With a mind intent on harm,
you have committed many evil acts —
do not commit such evil actions.
Those who harm me
will go in suffering to the hells.
By weeping and lamenting,
one only ends up falling to the ground.
With such diligence one will not become a buddha;
nor will one become a bodhisattva.
One will not attain even the vehicle of liberation of a śrāvaka —
therefore strive with a different kind of diligence.
Go to that mountain where the great sages dwell.
Having gone and encountered a great sage,
bow down to his feet, head to the ground, and say:
'Venerable being, grant me your protection!
I am utterly tormented by fearful suffering.
Noble sentient being, sage, please speak a living word!
Remain, and open to me the opportunity of mind.
For even a moment, speak to me of virtuous Dharma.
Tormented and afflicted by terror and suffering,
therefore I beg you: remain for just a moment.
I wish to confess the many evils I have done.
May the sage bestow his word upon me.'"

The sage spoke:

"Tormented by weeping, grief, and sorrow,
you are threatened by hunger and thirst.
Since you are in despair and are going to the three realms of existence,
eat the food that has been given.
In order to satisfy the body,
the sage gave him food.
Having eaten the delicious and pleasant food,
the sentient being became greatly joyful.
The Dharma that purifies all evil
I will explain to you afterwards."

That delicious food was eaten in a single instant. After eating, he washed his hands, circumambulated the sage, sat down cross-legged, and spoke of each and every evil he had done. He said: "I slew my father, and I slew my mother. I destroyed a stūpa. And I obstructed the attainment of awakening by a bodhisattva." Having heard those words, the sage then spoke:

"Because you have committed evil acts —
alas, O man, you are without virtue!"

"Confess the evil acts that you have done
and the evil acts you have caused others to do."

Then, in that very moment, he was pierced by the thorn of grief, and in fear and anguish he cried out:

"Who will protect me?
For I have done evil acts,
and I shall experience the feeling of suffering."

Then that man placed both his knees on the ground and said to the sage:

"All the evil I have done and caused others to do
I confess in full.
May I not meet the fruit of evil;
may I not experience suffering.
Let me draw near to you —
O sage, be my refuge.
Through remorse and through the peace it brings,
may my evil acts be calmed."

Then, in that very moment, the sage spoke to that man:

"I will be your refuge, O man. I will be your support. I will be your ally. Have no fear — listen to the Dharma directly. Have you ever heard even a little of the teaching called 'Comprehension'?" After he had breathed out these words, the man replied: "I have not heard it." The sage said: "Who but one who abides in compassion and teaches the Dharma to sentient beings would teach the Dharma to a being scorched by affliction?" Then he spoke: "O son of good family, listen further.

"Long, long ago, in the past, in a time beyond innumerable kalpas — beyond even the most innumerable — there was at that time a king named Stainless Moon, a Dharmic king, a king of Dharma. O son of good family, in the household of King Stainless Moon a son was born. The great king Stainless Moon then gathered together brahmin astrologers and asked them: 'What marks do you observe in this young boy?' The brahmin astrologers replied: 'Great King, this is inauspicious. The birth of this boy is inauspicious.' The king asked: 'What will this brahmin boy become?' The astrologers replied: 'Great King, if this boy survives to the age of seven, he will take the lives of his father and mother.' The king then said: 'Even if it means an obstruction to my own life, I will not kill this son of mine. In a hundred lives taken as a human being in this world, to harm a human body in such a way — this I will not do.' Then the boy grew with remarkable speed, such that when one month had passed he measured as much as another child two years old. King Stainless Moon recognized that this boy had been born through the accumulation of his own karma. The king then granted the boy royal authority and spoke these words: 'You shall become a king of great power and renown — rule by Dharma, and not by what is contrary to Dharma.' Having granted him royal authority, he gave him the title of king. Yet King Stainless Moon himself would no longer rule as king of his own realm. Then his ministers by the tens of millions went to where King Stainless Moon was, came before him, and spoke to him thus: 'O great king, why will you not be king of your own realm?' The king replied: 'Throughout countless innumerable kalpas, whenever I have had dominion over kingship, wealth, and power, I have never once felt satisfied.' Then, after a long time had passed, that son killed both his father and mother, and thereby he accumulated an act of immediate retribution.

"O man, at that time when that king, feeling the sensation of suffering, fell into remorse and wept with tears streaming down his face, I arose in him great compassion, went to him, and taught him the Dharma —

He too, upon hearing that Dharma teaching, I recall, was the occasion when all the remaining acts of immediate retribution were swiftly and completely purified.

Then he spoke: "Whoever hears this King of Sūtras known as the Coupled Synonyms of Dharma, through this great ascetic practice, will attain the supreme Dharma-ground (sthāna) — the purification of all misdeeds and the pacification of all afflictions."

Whoever quickly gains liberation —
hear this well, the teaching of all phenomena:
even a single verse of four lines,
spoken in an unbroken stream —
all misdeeds are thereby purified,
and the entry into the stream is attained.
Freed from every misdeed —
this is the solemn declaration, the solemn declaration.
From the bonds of fear binding mind to hell,
the suffering of sentient beings is utterly released.

Then that man rose from his seat, joined his palms together, bowed his head in homage to him, and gave praise, saying: "Excellent! Excellent, O virtuous spiritual friend! Excellent, O destroyer of misdeeds! Excellent too are those who will hear the great Coupled Sūtra that has been taught!"

Then at that very time and moment, two thousand sons of the gods descended from the sky, joined their palms together, went before that ascetic, bowed at his feet, and spoke these words: "Blessed One, engaged in great austerity — how much longer is this still needed?" The ascetic replied: "For countless innumerable myriads of hundreds of thousands of kalpas." They asked: "Through what wholesome act will these evil karmas be completely pacified in a single instant?" He replied: "By hearing the Coupled Synonyms of Dharma."

Of all those sentient beings assembled there, each and every one who heard this synonymy of Dharma and gave rise to faith received the prophecy of unsurpassed, perfect, and complete awakening.

As for any person who has committed the five acts of immediate retribution — if they hear this synonymy of Dharma called the Coupled, then in a single instant all five of those acts of immediate retribution will be completely exhausted and utterly purified, and for countless myriads of hundreds of thousands of kalpas the doors to the lower destinies will be closed. And the thirty-two doors to the heavenly worlds will be thrown open.

If even hearing just a single verse of four lines from this synonymy of Dharma called the Coupled can generate a root of virtue of such magnitude, what need is there to speak of one who honors, reveres, depicts, venerates, and performs acts of service to it with flowers, incense, perfume, garlands, ointment, powder, Dharma-robes, parasols, victory banners, and pennants — accompanied by music and instruments — and who, again and again rejoicing after each act, praises it well and says "Excellent! Excellent!"?

Then the bodhisattva, the great being Sarvasūra addressed the Blessed One thus: "Blessed One, when the Coupled Synonyms of Dharma is being expounded, do those who join their palms together and bow in homage — do they generate a mass of merit by that gesture of joining palms alone?"

The Blessed One declared: "Listen well, son of good family. Even those who have themselves committed, caused others to commit, or rejoiced in the commission of the five acts of immediate retribution — if they hear even a single verse of four lines from this synonymy of Dharma called the Coupled, and then join their palms together and bow in homage, all five of their acts of immediate retribution will be completely purified. What then need be said, Sarvasūra, of one who hears this entire synonymy of Dharma in full?"

That generates an even vaster mass of merit than all of that. Son of good family, so that you may understand the meaning of this synonymy of Dharma called the Coupled, I shall set forth a simile for you, Sarvasūra. Consider this: from the palace of Anavapta, the king of nāgas, where the sun never rises, five great rivers flow forth. Now, if someone were to count the drops of those five great rivers, Sarvasūra — could they, by counting those drops, arrive at a reckoning of their limit?

Sarvasūra replied: "Blessed One, that would be inconceivable."

The Blessed One declared: "Just so, Sarvasūra, the roots of virtue from this synonymy of Dharma called the Coupled cannot be reckoned to their limit even if one were to count for a hundred aeons or even for a thousand aeons. Sarvasūra, what do you think? Is it a difficult thing to teach even a brief portion of this synonymy of Dharma called the Coupled?"

He replied: "Blessed One, it is indeed a difficult thing."

The Blessed One declared: "Sarvasūra, one who is able to teach this synonymy of Dharma called the Coupled performs something more difficult still. For consider this simile: the drops of those five great rivers that flow from Lake Anavapta cannot be reckoned to their limit by counting."

Sarvasūra asked: "Blessed One, which are those five great rivers?"

The Blessed One declared: "They are as follows: the Gaṅgā, the Sītā, the Vakṣu, the Yamunā, and the Candrabhāgā — those are the five great rivers, and they all flow into the great ocean. Each of the great rivers, moreover, has five hundred tributary rivers. Sarvasūra, those five great rivers also have a thousand tributaries each, and they descend from the sky, and by them all living beings are satisfied. Sarvasūra, further, at various times those rivers send forth continuous streams of raindrops, and from them flowers, fruits, and harvests are born. Whenever continuous rain falls upon Jambudvīpa, water arises; and when water arises it satisfies all fields and pleasure groves and brings happiness. Sarvasūra, consider this simile: just so the lord of creatures brings happiness to all of Jambudvīpa. Sarvasūra, in the same way this synonymy of Dharma called the Coupled is also taught in Jambudvīpa for the benefit of many beings and for the happiness of many beings.

The measure of the lifespan of the gods of the Thirty-Three is not the same as the measure of the lifespan of humans. Sarvasūra, who are the gods of the Thirty-Three? Those who dwell where Śakra, the lord of the gods (Indra), resides — those are called the Thirty-Three.

Sarvasūra, there are sentient beings who speak words of good conduct, and the mass of merit of those beings defies all comparison. There are also sentient beings who speak words of harmful conduct, and the extent to which those beings shall fall into the hells and among the animals likewise defies all comparison. As for those sentient beings who are to experience the suffering of the hells, of animals, and of hungry ghosts — there is for them no refuge whatsoever.

Those who at that point are cut off from hope, lamenting and wailing as they fall into the hells — those beings are to be understood as being under the power of unwholesome companions. And those sentient beings whose mass of merit from speaking words of good conduct defies all comparison — those likewise are to be understood as being under the power of a spiritual friend. For whenever one sees a spiritual friend, one thereby sees a Tathāgata. And whenever one sees a Tathāgata, all sins are thereby purified. And whenever the Lord of Creatures rejoices in Jambudvīpa, the happiness of the sentient beings of Jambudvīpa defies all comparison.

Sarvasūra, in just the same way, this enumeration of the teachings of Zuṅ also performs the work of a buddha for the sentient beings of Jambudvīpa. Those who do not come to hear this enumeration of the teachings of Zuṅ will be unable to fully awaken to the highest, perfect, and complete awakening; unable to turn the wheel of Dharma; unable to strike the Dharma gong; unable to take their seat on the lion throne of Dharma; unable to enter into the expanse of nirvāṇa; and unable to illuminate with immeasurable rays of light. Sarvasūra, in just the same way, those who do not come to hear this enumeration of the teachings of Zuṅ will also be unable to sit at the seat of awakening.

Sarvasūra then asked: Blessed One, I wish to ask something wonderful. Sugata, I wish to ask something wonderful.

The Blessed One declared: Sarvasūra, ask whatever you wish, and I shall resolve your doubts.

He asked: Who is it that, having fully liberated those sentient beings from the five acts of immediate retribution and established them one by one on the stage of non-return, came to be the sage?

The Blessed One declared:

The words of the buddhas are profound.
Sarvasūra, listen to me.
The one who teaches the sūtra of Zuṅ
is revealed in the form of a sage.
Zuṅ also fully reveals the bodies of the buddhas
for the sake of loving affection.
Zuṅ reveals forms as numerous
as the grains of sand in the Ganges.
Revealing the form of a buddha,
and fully teaching the Dharma itself —
whoever wishes to behold a buddha:
Zuṅ is the equal of a buddha.
Wherever Zuṅ is present,
a buddha is forever present there.

The Blessed One then declared: Son of good family, listen. Sarvasūra, in the past, in an age gone by, ninety-nine immeasurable kalpas ago, one hundred and twenty billion buddhas appeared. Among them there appeared a Tathāgata called Supreme Jewel. At that time I was bold in generosity, and I paid homage and offered service to one hundred and twenty billion buddhas called Moonlike, with food and drink, clothing, incense, garlands, unguents, whatever was conducive to ease, fine food, and all articles of comfort. Having rendered that service, I recall there hearing the prophecy of the highest, perfect, and complete awakening being given at that occasion.

Sarvasūra, I also recall one hundred and eighty billion Tathāgatas called Illuminator of All Precious Things having appeared. At that time too I was bold in generosity, and I paid individual, lavish homage and offered service to one hundred and eighty billion Tathāgatas called Essence of the Multitude — with incense, garlands, unguents, ornaments, and abundance of every suitable kind — and I recall there too hearing the prophecy of the highest, perfect, and complete awakening being given.

Sarvasūra, I recall two hundred billion buddhas — among them the Tathāgata, the arhat, the fully and perfectly awakened one called Source of the Topknot, and those alone.

Sarvasūra, I recall twenty billion buddhas — among them the Tathāgata, the arhat, the fully and perfectly awakened one called Protector of Light, and those alone. There too I was bold in generosity, and I paid homage to those Tathāgatas with incense, garlands, and unguents, and I rendered them whatever service and attendance is fitting to offer Tathāgatas — and by means of such service and attendance I there too received the prophecy of the highest, perfect, and complete awakening.

Sarvasūra, sixteen billion buddhas called Stainless Light appeared in the world. At that time I was a householder, wealthy and possessing great riches and abundant resources, one who gave away all his possessions entirely. I offered to all sixteen billion of those buddhas food and drink, incense, garlands, unguents, and canopies, paying them homage in whatever manner is fitting as service and attendance to Tathāgatas — and I recall that there too I received the prophecy of the highest, perfect, and complete awakening. As for the time and measure of that prophecy, it does not fall within my reckoning.

Sarvasūra, listen well. Ninety-five billion buddhas appeared in the world, every one of them the Tathāgata, the arhat, the fully and perfectly awakened one called Śākyamuni. At that time I was a righteous king, a dharma-king, and I paid homage to all ninety-five billion of those Tathāgatas called Śākyamuni with incense, garlands, unguents, food and drink, clothing, parasols, victory banners, and pennants — and I recall that there too I received the prophecy of the highest, perfect, and complete awakening.

Sarvasūra, ninety billion Tathāgatas, arhats, fully and perfectly awakened buddhas called Destroyer of Cyclic Existence appeared in the world. At that time I was a young brahmin, wealthy, possessing great riches and abundant resources, one who gave away all his possessions entirely. I paid homage to all those Tathāgatas with incense, garlands, unguents, food and drink, and clothing, and I rendered each of those Tathāgatas individual and fitting service — and I recall that there too I received the prophecy of the highest, perfect, and complete awakening. As for the time and measure of that prophecy, it does not fall within my reckoning.

Sarvasūra, one hundred and eighty billion buddhas appeared in the world, every one of them the Tathāgata, the arhat, the fully and perfectly awakened one called Golden Sage. At that time I was bold in generosity, and I paid homage to those Tathāgatas, those arhats, those fully and perfectly awakened buddhas, with incense, garlands, unguents, food and drink, clothing, and ornaments, and I rendered them service in whatever manner is fitting as attendance to Tathāgatas — and I recall that there too I received the prophecy of the highest, perfect, and complete awakening. As for the time and measure of that prophecy, it does not fall within my reckoning.

Sarvasūra, one hundred and thirty billion buddhas appeared in the world, every one of them the Tathāgata, the arhat, the fully and perfectly awakened one called Splendor of Illumination. I paid homage to those Tathāgatas, those arhats, those fully and perfectly awakened buddhas, with food and drink, clothing, incense, garlands, unguents, canopies, and ornaments, rendering them the service and attendance that is fitting to offer Tathāgatas, and by means of such service and attendance —

those Tathāgatas as well taught me various Dharma gates (sūtra) of meaning and definitive discipline, and right there I received the prophecy of unsurpassable, perfect, complete awakening. Yet even then the time appointed for that prophecy had not arrived.

Sarvasūra, twenty-five billion Tathāgatas, arhats, fully and perfectly awakened buddhas called Victorious Star appeared in the world. At that time I had gone forth and rendered service and attendance to those Tathāgatas, just as Ānanda now renders service and attendance to me. In that very way I rendered service and attendance to those Tathāgatas, and I recall receiving there the prophecy of unsurpassable, perfect, complete awakening — yet even then the time appointed for that prophecy had not arrived.

Sarvasūra, twelve billion Tathāgatas, arhats, fully and perfectly awakened buddhas called All-Seeing appeared in the world. I rendered service and attendance to those Tathāgatas with food and drink, clothing, incense, garlands, and unguents, serving them in the way fitting to offer Tathāgatas. At that time I had gone forth, and right there I received the prophecy of unsurpassable, perfect, complete awakening — I recall this. And before long, the last of those All-Seeing Tathāgatas to appear taught this enumeration of the Dharma called Paired Categories. Upon learning of it, I then caused a rain of the seven precious substances to fall abundantly over Jambudvīpa, so that the sentient beings of Jambudvīpa were freed from poverty. Right there I received the prophecy of unsurpassable, perfect, complete awakening. Yet for a long stretch of time thereafter I still received no prophecy.

He asked: What was that time? What was its measure?

The Blessed One declared: Sarvasūra, listen well. After an incalculable number of aeons, the Tathāgata, the arhat, the fully and perfectly awakened buddha called Dīpaṃkara (the Lamp-Maker) first appeared in the world. At that time and in that age I was a young brahmin boy named Megha (Cloud). When the Tathāgata Dīpaṃkara appeared in the world, I, in the form of that young brahmin boy, was practicing the holy life. Then, upon beholding the Tathāgata Dīpaṃkara, I scattered seven blue lotus flowers before him and dedicated them to unsurpassable, perfect, complete awakening. That Tathāgata then prophesied to me: Young brahmin boy, in a future time, after incalculable aeons, you will become a Tathāgata, an arhat, a fully and perfectly awakened buddha known as Śākyamuni.

Sarvasūra, at that moment I was seated in the sky above the tops of twelve palm trees and had attained the acceptance of non-arising dharmas (anutpattikadharmakṣānti). All the roots of virtue I had cultivated through the holy life over those incalculable aeons, and all the roots of virtue endowed with the perfections — I recalled all of these as vividly as if they were yesterday and today.

Sarvasūra, had I at that moment established countless billions of sentient beings in wholesome dharmas, what need is there to speak of my desire, now that I have fully and perfectly awakened to unsurpassable, perfect, complete awakening, to bring benefit to all sentient beings?

Sarvasūra, I have taught sentient beings the Dharma in many forms. According to whatever form is fitting to tame a given being, in just such a form I have taught that being the Dharma.

In the world of the gods, I taught the Dharma in the form of a god. In the realm of the nāgas, I taught the Dharma in the form of a nāga. In the realm of the yakṣas, I taught the Dharma in the form of a yakṣa. In the realm of the hungry ghosts, I taught the Dharma in the form of a hungry ghost. In the human world, I taught the Dharma in the form of a human. To sentient beings to be tamed by a buddha, I taught the Dharma in the form of a buddha. To sentient beings to be tamed by a bodhisattva, I taught the Dharma in the form of a bodhisattva. Whatever form would tame sentient beings, in just that form I taught them the Dharma. Thus, Sarvasūra, I have taught the Dharma to sentient beings in many forms.

Why is this so? Sarvasūra, to the extent that sentient beings hear the Dharma in its many forms, those beings, in their very essence, will cultivate roots of virtue in many forms: they will practice generosity, they will accumulate merit, they will not lie down in pursuit of their own benefit alone, they will contemplate mindfulness of death, and they will perform virtuous deeds of that kind, gathered together. Through their having heard the Dharma, they will come to recall earlier roots of virtue, and those gods and humans will thereby come to have long-term welfare, benefit, and happiness.

Thus, Sarvasūra, as soon as the enumeration of the coupled Dharma is heard, its qualities and benefits become immeasurable in just that way.

Then those sentient beings said one to another: "May there be some other ripened fruit of wholesome dharmas — the maturation of the fruit of one who has fully and perfectly awakened to unsurpassable, perfect, complete awakening through what has been done and accumulated, and who wishes to bring benefit to all sentient beings!"

Then those among them who had faith in the Dharma and said truly and correctly, "The Dharma exists," — for them the great ripened fruit of well-being will be the unsurpassable happiness of the Dharma.

As for those deluded and foolish sentient beings who say, "These dharmas do not exist; there is no going to the further shore of the dharmas" — for them the great ripened fruit will be rebirth in the lower realms. Again and again they will incline toward the ground of lower rebirths. For eight aeons they will experience the feelings of suffering in the hells. For twelve aeons they will experience feelings of suffering in the realms of hungry ghosts. For sixteen aeons they will be born among the asuras. For nine thousand aeons they will be born among spirits and flesh-eaters. For forty thousand aeons they will be without a tongue. For sixty thousand aeons they will die in their mother's womb. For twenty thousand aeons they will be reduced to lumps of flesh. For eleven thousand aeons they will be born blind and experience feelings of suffering — and at that time their parents will think: "We have experienced suffering to no purpose. The birth of our child was pointless. We carried it in the womb for nine months to no purpose. We experienced the sensations of cold and heat. We fully experienced the suffering of hunger and thirst. In this life too we experienced so much suffering." When the parents see the blind child in the home, it will bring them no joy, and the parents will come to despair completely.

Sarvašūra, likewise, sentient beings who abandon the sacred Dharma are inclined toward the hell realms and the animal realm. At the time of their death they will be pierced by a great arrow of grief and sorrow.

Sarvašūra, those who say such things as "phenomena exist" and "there is a going beyond phenomena" — through that root of virtue they will, for twenty aeons, be born in the northern continent of Uttarakuru. For twenty-five aeons they will be born as equals among the gods of the Thirty-Three. After passing away from the Thirty-Three, they will again be born in Uttarakuru. They will not be born in a mother's womb. They will behold hundreds of thousands of world-systems, and every single one of them will be called Sukhāvatī. They will behold all buddha-fields, and having beheld them, they will remain right there, and right there they will awaken to complete and perfect awakening.

Sarvašūra, such is the great power of this teaching known as the Paired Dharma. Those who have faith in it will never die in a state of confusion, never departing without having relinquished bewilderment. They will also come to possess completely pure moral discipline.

Sarvašūra, there are some who think: even though the Tathāgata, day and night, liberates very many sentient beings, the realm of sentient beings is still not exhausted — many make aspirations for awakening, many are born in the higher realms of the world, and many attain nirvāṇa, yet why is it that sentient beings are never exhausted? There are indeed sentient beings who wonder about this.

Now, other practitioners who are non-Buddhists (tīrthikas), wandering ascetics, and naked ascetics think to themselves: "We wish to have a debate with the śramaṇa Gautama." Then brahmins, other tīrthika practitioners, eighty-four thousand wandering ascetics, and many hundreds of naked ascetics all came to where the Blessed One was in Rājagṛha.

At that time and moment, the Blessed One smiled. Then the bodhisattva, the great being Maitreya rose from his seat, arranged his upper robe over one shoulder, placed his right knee on the ground, and with palms joined turned toward the Blessed One and addressed him thus:

"Blessed One, Tathāgatas, arhats, fully and perfectly awakened buddhas do not smile without cause and condition — what then is the cause, what is the condition for this smile?"

The Blessed One declared: "Son of good family, listen. Today a great assembly will gather here in Rājagṛha."

"Blessed One, who will come here — gods, nāgas, yakṣas, humans, or non-humans?"

The Blessed One declared: "Maitreya, today all of these — gods, nāgas, yakṣas, humans, and non-humans — will come here. Eighty-four thousand brahmins will also come here. Nine thousand million tīrthika practitioners, wandering ascetics, and naked ascetics will come, and I will engage them in debate. In order to pacify the contentiousness of all of them, the Dharma will be taught. All those brahmins will generate the mind set on unsurpassed, complete, and perfect awakening. All nine thousand million tīrthika practitioners, wandering ascetics, and naked ascetics will attain the fruit of stream-entry."

Eighteen thousand million nāga kings will come, and they will hear the Dharma from me. Having heard it, every one of them will generate the mind set on unsurpassed, complete, and perfect awakening. Sixty thousand million sons of the gods of the Pure Abodes will come. Thirty thousand million evil māras together with their retinues will come. Twelve thousand million asura kings will come. Some five hundred kings together with their retinues will come to hear the Dharma, and all of them, having heard the Dharma from me, will generate the mind set on unsurpassed, complete, and perfect awakening.

Then the great bodhisattva Maitreya prostrated with his head at the feet of the Blessed One, circumambulated the Blessed One three times, and vanished from that very spot.

Next, the great bodhisattva Sarvashūra rose from his seat, arranged his outer robe over one shoulder, placed his right knee on the ground, and with palms joined turned toward the Blessed One and bowed. He then addressed the Blessed One in these words: "Blessed One, what are the names of those approximately five hundred kings?"

The Blessed One declared: "Sarvashūra, listen well. There is King Joyful, King Deeply Joyful, King Supreme Flock of Victors, King Host of Brahmā, King Voice of Brahmā, King Lovely to Behold, King Host of the Beloved, King Host of the Joyful, King Essence of Form, King Resplendent Victor, and King Noble Man — these and others, approximately five hundred kings in all, each attended by retinues of a hundred thousand million. All of them, with the sole exception of King Noble Man, have entered without remainder into the path to unsurpassed, complete, and perfect awakening.

From the east, thirty thousand million bodhisattvas will come. From the south, fifty thousand million bodhisattvas will come. From the west, sixty thousand million bodhisattvas will come. From the north, eighty thousand million bodhisattvas will come. From the direction below, ninety thousand million bodhisattvas will come. From the direction above, one hundred thousand million bodhisattvas will come — and every single one of them abides perfectly on the tenth ground. All of those bodhisattvas are traveling toward Rājagṛha, the great royal city, making their way to behold the Blessed One. Every one of those bodhisattvas has also entered without remainder into the path to unsurpassed, complete, and perfect awakening."

Then the Blessed One addressed the great bodhisattva Sarvashūra: "Sarvashūra, go to the world-systems of the ten directions and announce to all bodhisattvas: 'Today the Tathāgata is teaching the Dharma in the great royal city of Rājagṛha. All of you who dwell in the worlds of the ten directions, join your palms and bow — and as soon as you have heard this message and understood it, return without delay to hear the Dharma.'"

Then the great bodhisattva Sarvashūra rose from his seat, prostrated with his head at the feet of the Blessed One, circumambulated the Blessed One three times, and by the power of his miraculous abilities vanished from sight.

Then the great bodhisattva Sarvashūra traveled to the world-systems of the ten directions and announced to all the bodhisattvas: "Today the Tathāgata is teaching the Dharma in the great royal city of Rājagṛha. You should all say 'Excellent!' to this, for by doing so you will this very day obtain great benefit and great happiness." With that proclamation he traveled throughout the world-systems of the ten directions, paid homage to all the buddhas, and stirred the bodhisattvas to action with the following simile: just as a man endowed with great strength can travel a great distance with a single snap of his fingers, so the great bodhisattva Sarvashūra arrived at once at the great royal city of Rājagṛha, in the presence of the Blessed One, and took his seat before him.

There assembled in that place brahmins, other non-Buddhists (tīrthikas), wandering ascetics, and naked renouncers. There assembled also gods, nāgas, yakṣas, a great multitude of people, and a king with his retinue of about five hundred. There assembled too the evil māra with his retinue numbering thirty-three trillion.

At that moment the great royal city of Rājagṛha trembled and shook. Then from throughout the world-systems of the ten directions a rain of divine sandalwood powder fell, and a rain of divine flowers fell, and all of it came to rest in tiers, like a many-storied palace, above the crown of the Blessed One's head. At that time Śakra (Indra), lord of the gods, wielded his vajra (thunderbolt scepter) in the presence of the Tathāgata. Then four mighty wind-kings arose and stirred from the four directions, and they swept away the rubbish, the banners, and the dust from the great royal city of Rājagṛha, carrying all of it outside the city walls. Throughout the world-systems of the ten directions a rain of scented water then fell. Throughout the world-systems of the ten directions a rain of blue utpala flowers, lotuses, kumuda flowers, and white lotuses also fell, and all those flowers came to rest above the crowns of those sentient beings' heads in the form of parasols of flowers. In the sky above the crown of the Tathāgata's head, eighty-four thousand many-storied palaces came to rest, and from within each of those eighty-four thousand palaces there arose eighty-four thousand thrones adorned with the seven precious substances, and upon each and every one of those thrones the Tathāgata was seated, teaching the Dharma. Then this great trichiliocosm shook and trembled in six ways.

Then the great bodhisattva Sarvashūra turned to face the Blessed One, joined his palms, and bowed, and addressed the Blessed One with these words: "Blessed One, what is the cause and what is the condition for such miraculous displays as these appearing here in the great royal city of Rājagṛha?"

The Blessed One declared: "Consider this simile. There is a certain wild, arrogant person, beset by self-grasping and grasping at what is mine, who has fallen into poverty. The king anoints his head, and that person then goes to the gate of the king's palace and, arriving there, presses insistently, wishing to enter the inner palace. The king's ministers and retinue seize him and beat him in many ways. Now when the king hears that this poor man is pressing insistently, wishing to enter, the king thinks to himself: 'Without any doubt this person wants to kill me.' Enraged, the king says to his retinue: 'Today you will take that man out to the forest and put him to death.'"

'Kill his servants, his parents, his children, his male slaves, his female slaves, and all his workers.' With that command, all of them were put to death, and all the friends and companions of that man were pierced by the unbearable thorn of grief.

In just the same way, when a Tathāgata, an arhat, a perfectly and fully awakened buddha, teaches the Dharma, those among ordinary childish people who are arrogant in the very manner described take hold of the Tathāgata through form, color, marks, shape, and signs, thinking: 'That is the body of the Tathāgata.' Having heard many teachings, they fall into heightened pride, speak all manner of nonsense, and, overwhelmed by self-grasping and grasping at what is mine, they themselves will neither listen to nor explain such Dharma as this. If someone teaches them even a sūtra, a verse, or even so much as a single analogy, they do not hold it in attention or lend it their ear, saying: 'I already know this.' Why is that? Because they have become arrogant. They are heedless through much hearing.

Those who associate with such ordinary childish people will not come to practice such Dharma as this. They will not come to hear the words of such Dharma as this. Rather, they grow intoxicated by their much learning. People of this kind set down their own fabrications and establish their own textual positions. They deceive themselves and all the world alike, consume vast amounts of alms from villages and towns for no purpose, and even having eaten, they do not digest it well. At the time of their death, great fear will arise. The sentient beings around them will say to such a person: 'You have extensively instructed us in the crafts; how is it that you are unable to establish a self?' And he will say to them: 'Friends, I am at this moment unable to establish a self.' Then those sentient beings will break into lamentations of many kinds.

Just as in the other case, where through the conditions of his own karma that single person caused the killing of a great many kinsmen at another's hands, so too those sentient beings, at the time of their death, break into lamentations and, on account of their unwholesome companions, they see themselves falling toward rebirth in the hells or in the realm of animals.

In just the same way, brahmins and other non-Buddhists (tīrthikas) say to you: 'Do not grow intoxicated. Consider this example: just as a bird whose wings are not yet fully grown cannot fly into the sky to reach the world of the gods, so too you will not be able to attain nirvāṇa, for you do not possess supernatural powers of that kind.' Why is that? 'Because, born through the force of karma in the birth-place of a little bird, this body will not long endure before it falls apart and you die at the end. At the time of death they will be without savour and will think: why did we take up this body? We have experienced neither the pleasures of the world of the gods nor the pleasures of the human world, nor have we come to dwell in the state of nirvāṇa. If we have taken up this body to no purpose, what then will become of our destiny? Who will be our support? Where will we be born, and where will we perish?' And they will be utterly terrified.

Then the Blessed One (the Buddha) spoke to those non-Buddhist practitioners, wandering ascetics, and naked ascetics:

"Do not be disheartened here in this precious Jambudvīpa. Do not place yourselves outside the jewel of the Dharma. Friends, ask the Tathāgata whatever you wish, and I will bring all your intentions to complete fulfilment."

Then all those brahmins and other non-Buddhist practitioners, wandering ascetics, and naked ascetics rose from their seats, arranged their upper robes over one shoulder, pressed their palms together, and made this request to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, the Tathāgata, like a parasol, liberates sentient beings entirely from cyclic existence, yet among the classes of sentient beings there is no decrease or increase to be found. Blessed One, by what cause and by what condition do you demonstrate the arising and ceasing of those sentient beings?"

Then the Blessed One spoke to the bodhisattva great being Mañjuśrī's Retinue: "In order to dispel the great remorse of the non-Buddhist practitioners and to cause the great lamp of the Dharma to blaze, put on the great armour and ask the great question worthy of asking, so that in the time to come young and old sentient beings alike may come to know arising and ceasing."

Mañjuśrī's Retinue replied: "Blessed One, there are also old sentient beings who know nothing, just like the young."

"Mañjuśrī's Retinue, consider this example: one person washes his hair, puts on fresh new clothing, and steps outside his house. The other sentient beings say to him, 'You have put on your fine new clothing well.' But another person also washes his hair and puts on freshly washed old clothing, and because that old clothing is worn and faded, even though that person has washed his hair he does not appear handsome, nor does his clothing appear fine. In the same way, Mañjuśrī's Retinue, the old sentient beings there are in Jambudvīpa are not beautiful, whereas the young sentient beings demonstrate arising and ceasing."

Then all those brahmins and other non-Buddhist practitioners, wandering ascetics, rose from their seats and said this to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, among ourselves, who are the young and who are the old?"

The Blessed One declared: "You have repeatedly experienced sensations again and again in the hells, among the animals, and among the hungry ghosts, and yet you have still never had your fill of it. For that reason you are old."

Then all those brahmins and all the nāga kings said this to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, from this time forth we take no delight in experiencing sensations of suffering in cyclic existence."

And the other non-Buddhist practitioners, wandering ascetics, and naked ascetics said: "Even among the young sentient beings, there is not one who is capable of directly perceiving the nature of reality."

When they had said this, the bodhisattva great being Mañjuśrī's Retinue said this to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, pray look upon why sentient beings are so difficult to guide."

The Blessed One declared: "Mañjuśrī's Retinue, listen. The Tathāgata perceives the entire world directly."

Then ninety-four billion young sentient beings sat in the presence of the Tathāgata and neither paid homage to the Tathāgata nor spoke a word.

Then the bodhisattva great being Mañjuśrī's Retinue said this to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, what is the cause and what is the condition by which these sentient beings do not speak to the Blessed One, do not speak to him at all, do not pay homage, and make no request of the Blessed One?"

The Blessed One declared: "Mañjuśrī's Retinue, listen. These young sentient beings are incapable of directly perceiving the nature of reality." As for those sentient beings who were saying this —

"You should regard these members of the Medicinal Cohort as young sentient beings." Those sentient beings then said this: "Venerable Blessed One, we are indeed young sentient beings. Venerable Sugata, we are indeed young sentient beings."

The Blessed One declared: "These sentient beings are ones who make the world directly manifest. Now show the measure of the world from within your own bodies."

At that very time and moment, nine hundred and forty million young sentient beings, without leaving their bodies, dwelt in the sky above and attained full establishment in the tenth ground (bhūmi).

Then the bodhisattva great being Medicinal Cohort said this to the Blessed One: "Those sentient beings who apply diligent effort for the sake of utterly exhausting and utterly purifying saṃsāra — such beings are well and truly found. Blessed One, these sentient beings are born right now. Blessed One, these sentient beings are completely liberated right now, and we see that all of them have fully attained the tenth ground."

The Third Section.

Then brahmins, and other tīrthika wanderers (non-Buddhist adherents), wandering ascetics, naked practitioners, nāga kings, and Māra the Evil One together with his retinue — all of them arrived and, intending to cause mischief, said this to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, we have come here into the presence of the Tathāgata and, having heard this enumeration of Dharma, may we attain such happiness as that of a buddha. May there be in the world a Tathāgata, arhat, perfectly and fully awakened buddha such as this."

The Blessed One declared: "Good ones, it is just so. Since you have come into the presence of the Tathāgata and have heard this enumeration of Dharma of the pair, you have aroused the mind of unsurpassed, perfect, and complete awakening. Therefore all of you, before long, will fully and perfectly awaken to unsurpassed, perfect, and complete awakening."

The moment the Blessed One had spoken these words, all of those tīrthikas attained acceptance of the doctrine of non-arising, and having become bodhisattvas fully established on the tenth ground, all those bodhisattvas rose into the sky seven tāla-tree heights into the intermediate space above, and offered to the Tathāgata multi-storied mansions of seven kinds of precious substances. They all also consecrated various magical transformations and manifested displays of miraculous power.

Then, dwelling on the topmost crown of the Blessed One's head, various small flowers of many kinds were scattered upon the Blessed One, and all the Tathāgatas applied their minds to it, generating the perception of a buddha with respect to their own bodies.

Many hundreds of thousands of millions of sons of the gods also scattered offerings before the Tathāgata, saying these words: "The ascetic Gautama has found great acquisition. He has great fields. He is the protector of the world. He has found the arousal of the power of meditative absorption. He is one who is wise and who seeks wisdom. It is he who, through skillful means, gradually liberates such sentient beings as these fully from saṃsāra — and by just a single well-spoken word, that many sentient beings have been completely liberated from saṃsāra."

Then the bodhisattva great being Medicinal Cohort rose from his seat, arranged his upper robe over one shoulder, placed his right knee on the ground, joined his palms together in the direction of the Blessed One with a bow, and said this to the Blessed One:

"Blessed One, what cause and what condition is it by which these sons of good family utter such words, perform many miraculous displays, and produce many praises of the Tathāgata?"

The Blessed One declared: "Son of good family, listen. Those are not praises of me. Rather, they will praise their own bodies and place their own bodies upon the noble Dharma-throne. From their own bodies they will emit rays of Dharma-light. And in order to fully and completely awaken to unsurpassable, perfect, complete awakening, they will be embraced by all buddhas. Having fully and perfectly awakened, they will teach the Dharma."

Then the bodhisattva great being Medicinal Cohort said this to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, Sugata, although many sentient beings have been fully liberated under the canopy-sign, still sentient beings have not yet been exhausted."

The Blessed One declared: "Medicinal Cohort, it is good, it is good that you think to ask the Tathāgata about this matter. Medicinal Cohort, consider this: suppose there is a person who is wealthy, of great riches and great possessions, endowed with wealth, grain, storehouses, and many granaries, with many male servants, female servants, workers, and people who live by wages — a person who owns great heaps of treasure and many fields and pleasure gardens, and who possesses abundant grain such as barley, wheat, rice, beans, lentils, and the like. That person, having sown seeds of all those grains in the spring season, at another time sees all those seeds fully ripen. He then separates out each of those various grains, stores them away, and eats from them until the next spring season, sowing again as before. In the same way, Medicinal Cohort, when all the previously accumulated karma of sentient beings has been fully exhausted, they then seek a field of merit and cultivate roots of virtue. Having cultivated them, they diligently accomplish those virtuous qualities, cause all wholesome phenomena to increase, and give rise to joy and gladness. Medicinal Cohort, that mind of joy and gladness will not be wasted for ten million eons.

"Medicinal Cohort, in the same way, a bodhisattva who has first aroused the mind will never become one whose roots of virtue are wasted — in short, they will thoroughly understand all phenomena."

He asked: "Blessed One, what kinds of dreams does a bodhisattva who has first aroused the mind see?"

The Blessed One declared: "Medicinal Cohort, a bodhisattva who has first aroused the mind will see many frightening things in dreams. Why is that? It is in order to thoroughly purify all karma. A sentient being with evil karma cannot dispel unbearable suffering. Yet even seeing fearful dreams, that is not something to be frightened of — those are evil dreams."

He asked: "Blessed One, in what way does a bodhisattva who has first aroused the mind see frightening things in dreams?"

The Blessed One declared: "Medicinal Cohort, first, one sees a blazing fire. The bodhisattva should then understand that all one's afflictions have been burned away. Medicinal Cohort, second, even if the bodhisattva who has first aroused the mind sees thunder and great peals of thunder, one should not be frightened. Why is that? Medicinal Cohort, it is because, in the same way, all that is bound by delusion has been turned back and negativities have been purified. Medicinal Cohort, third, in the dreams of the bodhisattva who has first aroused the mind, one sees great fearful things."

He asked: "Blessed One, what are they?"

The Blessed One declared: "Even if one sees one's own head being cut off, Medicinal Cohort, one should not be frightened by that. Why is that? It is because one's attachment, aversion, and delusion have been cut off, and one has become victorious over wandering through the six classes of beings. One is not dwelling among the beings in the hells, nor among the animals, nor among the hungry ghosts, nor among the asuras, nor among the nāgas, nor among the gods. Medicinal Cohort, a bodhisattva who has first aroused the awakening mind is taken to be reborn in the utterly pure buddha-fields.

Medicinal Cohort, in the future, at a later time, should some sentient being dedicate their mind fully to awakening, they should be regarded as bearing a great commitment. Even if they remain in a state of being looked down upon and despised, Medicinal Cohort, they should not give rise to a mind of discouragement, and they should not be intimidated.

Medicinal Cohort, I have taught the Dharma abundantly. Medicinal Cohort, throughout countless tens of millions of eons I have practiced austerities, yet, Medicinal Cohort, I did not practice those austerities for the sake of material enjoyments, nor for the sake of livelihood and provisions, nor for the sake of lordly comforts. Medicinal Cohort, I practiced those austerities for the sake of penetrating and internalizing the essential nature of the Dharma — yet, for as long as that lasted, I had not yet attained the unsurpassable, perfectly complete awakening. Medicinal Cohort, at whatever time, on whatever occasion I heard this set of Dharma synonyms called the Pair, on that very day I became fully and perfectly awakened in unsurpassable, perfectly complete awakening.

Medicinal Cohort, this set of Dharma synonyms is profound. This set of Dharma synonyms is difficult to encounter through hearing. Medicinal Cohort, the arising of Tathāgatas is likewise supremely difficult to encounter. Medicinal Cohort, those who hold this set of Dharma synonyms are likewise supremely difficult to encounter. All those who hear this set of Dharma synonyms will without exception become fully and perfectly awakened in unsurpassable, perfectly complete awakening.

Medicinal Cohort, for a thousand eons they will churn through saṃsāra. They will readily encounter utterly pure buddha-fields. They will come to know cessation and the path. They will have the fortune of fully knowing the abiding place. They will have the fortune of fully knowing the virtuous abiding place. They will have the fortune of fully knowing higher knowledge and the virtuous abiding place. They will have the fortune of fully knowing the virtuous abiding place and cessation.

Medicinal Cohort, why is it called 'cessation'?"

He asked: "Blessed One, the abiding place of the Dharma is what I would call its meaning."

The Blessed One declared: "Medicinal Cohort, what is the abiding place of the Dharma?"

He asked: "Blessed One, what is called the Dharma is simply the application of diligence, the guarding of moral discipline, and being endowed with moral discipline — this I would call the treasury of the Dharma. Blessed One, this is where the treasury of the Dharma arises."

The Blessed One declared: "Medicinal Cohort, it is excellent, excellent to think of asking the Tathāgata about this matter."

He asked: "Blessed One, why do the Tathāgatas appear in the world?"

The Blessed One declared: "Medicinal Cohort, those who are endowed with extensive learning and who are wise come to fully know the arising of the Tathāgatas. Having come to know the arising of the Tathāgatas, they fully know this place of happiness that is the arising of the Tathāgatas. When the Tathāgatas arise in the world, sentient beings come to fully know all phenomena. Through skillful means, they come to fully know all phenomena."

They come to fully know worldly phenomena and transcendent phenomena. They come to fully know worldly wisdom and transcendent wisdom.

He asked: "Having realized wisdom itself, who would seek nirvāṇa?"

The Blessed One declared: "Medicinal Cohort, having fully known the Dharma, they fully know phenomena. Medicinal Cohort, having likewise fully known the gathering of the sacred Dharma, the first acquisition arises. Having grasped the Dharma just as it was heard, they come to acquire the quality of being endowed with Dharma.

"Medicinal Cohort, consider this example: A certain merchant, having set out carrying a thousand loads of gold without selling them, departed in order to make a profit. His parents said to him: 'Son, listen — carry a thousand tael of gold and bring the gold of others and your own back well.' The merchant took the gold and departed, and after about a month had passed, all that gold was secretly taken and made to disappear. In the aftermath, that man reflected, and feeling deep displeasure, the thorn of grief pierced his heart. Overcome with shame and embarrassment, he did not return to his own home. When his parents heard this, they were despondent. The thorn of grief piercing their hearts, they even tore their garments, crying: 'That wretched son was born into our household in the form of a child, has destroyed every part of the household, has left us without anything we need, has caused us suffering, and was put to work as a servant.' With such words of lamentation, his parents, in their despondency, came to the time of their death. Then, when the son heard that his parents, despondent on account of him, had come to the time of their death, he too became despondent and came to the time of death.

"In precisely the same way, Medicinal Cohort, when the Tathāgata explains this matter, those who have no faith in my teaching — they, in their hopeless despair, at the time of death will have the thorn of grief pierce their hearts as they come to the time of death. Just as those parents, becoming despondent, grieved over the gold, pierced by the thorn of grief in their hearts, and were afflicted with the thorn of grief over the gold of others and their own — so too, Medicinal Cohort, those who have no faith in my teaching will, later at the time of death, beat their chests and wail, and they will experience painful sensations. The merit they accumulated through deeds done previously will be wasted and destroyed, and afterward they will not create merit endowed with a good realm. Their hearts being afflicted by the thorn of grief, at that very time and in that very moment, they will be born in the hells, in the realms of animals, and in the realm of the Lord of Death. Seeing unbearable and fierce things, at the time of death this thought will arise in their minds: 'Who will become a refuge for me, so that I will not come to see the hells, the animals, the hungry ghosts, and the realm of the Lord of Death, and will not come to experience that painful sensation?' Then their parents spoke these words:

'O child, how can
the great terror of sickness
not come to arise? What is to be done?
Though you are frightened by sickness and death,
child, death does not yet exist for you.
From the fear and dread of sickness,
you will come to be liberated.
My consciousness is ceasing,
my body is greatly afflicted,
all my limbs are in suffering,
I have come to see death.
My eyes no longer see,
my ears no longer hear,
my nose will no longer receive,
my body's complexion is not at ease.
Limbs and digits are in suffering,
like wood, with no awareness left.
My death is not coming yet, you said —
Mother has exhausted all my consolation and cheer.'

The mother spoke: 'Child, it is not right for you to speak so.
Do not frighten me in the highest degree.
Because sickness has struck your body,
various appearances will be seen.'

The son spoke: 'Not even sickness do I see in the body;
there is no illness, there is no suffering.
I see that death is utterly unbearable —
my body has come to be devastated.'"

"When I see my entire body crushed and tormented by suffering, to whom shall I go for refuge? Who will there be to protect me?" The parents spoke: "Child, you should earnestly propitiate the gods. If you make offerings to the gods, you will live by their grace." The son spoke: "Whoever it is that will make me live — please do that. Go quickly, make haste, and ask the priest of the gods."

Then the parents went to the shrine of the god and presented incense to the god. Then the priest of the gods offered the incense to the god and spoke these words: "Propitiate the god with devotion. Make a sacrifice. Slaughter livestock for the god. Slaughter a person too. Then your son will be fully freed from his illness."

Then those parents thought: "We are poor — what shall we do? If the god is not pleased, our son will come to the time of his death. And if we are to please the god, we are too poor — from where shall we obtain a person and livestock?" Thinking this, they quickly went home, and set out to sell whatever household goods they had in order to buy livestock. Then they went to another person's house and said: "Good sir, if you lend us some gold and we are able to repay it within ten days, that is well. But if we cannot repay it, then from this very day we will bind ourselves as your servants and work." Having said this, the two of them took the gold and went to purchase a person. But the person they bought did not know that he was being bought to be killed.

Then those two deluded parents, without returning home, went to the shrine of the god and said to the priest of the gods: "Now quickly perform the sacrifice." Then the two parents themselves slaughtered the livestock and also slaughtered that person. Then the priest of the gods lit a fire intending to perform the sacrifice. And then the god descended and said: "I am taking your son away."

Then those two parents, filled with joy and gladness, said: "If our son recovers, it is worth it even if we become servants." Having made the sacrifice to the god, they returned home — and saw that their son had come to the time of his death. Then those two parents, wracked with suffering and deep despair, the thorn of grief piercing their hearts, their spirits broken, died right there in the same way.

Sāṃghika — one should not associate with bad companions. A question was put: "Blessed One, where were those sentient beings reborn?" The Blessed One declared: "Sāṃghika, do not ask me about the wretched physician. A question was put: "Blessed One, we ask. Sugata, we ask." The Blessed One declared: "Sāṃghika, listen. The mother was reborn in the great hell called Roaring Cries. The father was reborn in the great hell called Crushing. The son was reborn in the great hell of Heat. The priest of the gods was reborn in the great hell of Unrelenting Torment." A question was put: "Blessed One, where was the blameless person reborn? What became of him thereafter?" The Blessed One declared: "Sāṃghika, in this case, the one who was displeased was reborn among the gods of the Thirty-Three." A question was put: "Blessed One, through what cause and through what condition was that person reborn equal in fortune to the gods of the Thirty-Three?" The Blessed One declared: "Sāṃghika, listen. At the time of that person's death, with a mind of faith toward the Tathāgata, he said once: 'I bow to the Buddha.' Sāṃghika, through that root of virtue he will experience the happiness of the gods of the Thirty-Three for sixty aeons. For eighty aeons he will recall his past lives birth after birth. And birth after birth he will be free from every thorn of grief."

From the moment of each birth it dispels all suffering. Yet to lead all those sentient beings entirely to nirvāṇa — that lies beyond one's power.

A question was put: "Blessed One, what has obstructed the complete deliverance of all sentient beings to nirvāṇa?"

The Blessed One declared: "The host of medicine must apply diligence."

A question was put: "Blessed One, what is this diligence that must be applied?"

The Blessed One declared: "Host of medicine, listen. 'Diligence' is that which points toward the fruits. That is to say: the fruit called stream-entry is a domain of diligence. The fruit called once-returning is a domain of diligence. The fruit called non-returning is a domain of diligence. The state of arhatship and the fruit of the arhat, called cessation — these are domains of diligence. The fruit of the pratyekabuddha and the understanding that is the fruit of the pratyekabuddha — these are domains of diligence. The fruit of the bodhisattva and the ground of awakening — these too are domains of diligence. Host of medicine, all of these are called the domains of diligence."

A question was put: "Blessed One, how shall we teach stream-entry and the fruit of stream-entry?"

The Blessed One declared: "Consider this analogy. Suppose a certain person plants a tree. On that very day the tree's roots take hold, and on that very day those roots descend to a depth of a yojana beneath the earth. Another person likewise plants a tree, but on that very day the wind stirs it and the tree's roots fail to take hold. The first person then removes the tree from that place, and the other person erupts into quarrel and dispute, crying, 'Why has my ground been dug up?' At that time the king heard that these two people had fallen into quarrel and dispute. The king sent a messenger, saying: 'Go, bring those two people here. His Majesty commands it.' The messenger ran in the form of a swift man and said to those two: 'The king summons you.' At that, one of the two was afraid and frightened. The other, without fear or fright, was led to where the king was. They were brought before the king and stood in the royal presence. The king then spoke to these two men: 'Hey, you two — what are you quarreling and disputing about?' The two men rose and said to the king: 'Great King, hear us. We have not even a small plot of land of our own. On a piece of borrowed ground we planted a tree, but the wind stirred it and the roots did not take hold, and no leaves, blossoms, or fruit appeared. Great King, now this man accuses me of wrongdoing and has brought a lawsuit against me, but I have done no wrong. May you, O King, know that I have not the slightest fault in this matter.'

The king then summoned thirty million ministers, assembled them, and commanded them thus: 'You speak to it.' The ministers replied: 'What shall we say?' 'Has either of you ever seen or heard of a tree planted on the very day its roots took hold and on that same day produced leaves, blossoms, and fruit? Settle this matter between you within seven days or half a month.' The ministers then rose from their seats and said to the king:

'Great King, it is not proper for us to settle this matter. We have no standing to adjudicate it. Great King, this is astonishing. For now, let us put the question directly to this man himself: O man, tell us — is what you have said true?' The man replied: 'Great King, it is indeed true.' The king then spoke in verse:

On the very day the tree was planted,
on that same day its roots took hold,
and leaves and blossoms and fruit appeared —
to speak of this as happening on one day
is a claim most difficult to accept.
Nothing like this has ever been seen or heard.

Then that man joined his palms together and spoke these words to the king:

Please go yourself to see the tree you planted,
and look upon the roots that have taken hold.

Then the king went outside together with his thirty million ministers, and had both men placed under guard in prison. Then the king himself went to plant a tree, but that tree put forth no roots, and neither leaves nor blossoms nor fruit appeared. The king then fell into a rage and declared: 'Quickly, go now and fetch the axes for cutting down that tree.' The axes were brought. Then that man, enraged at the tree he had planted, cut away its leaves, blossoms, and fruit. When that single tree was cut down, twelve trees appeared in its place. When those twelve trees were cut down, twenty-four trees appeared, complete with roots made of the seven precious substances, with leaves, and with shoots. Then from those twenty-four trees there appeared twenty-four birds with golden crests, golden beaks, and wings of the seven precious substances. Then the king, overwhelmed by the force of his own rage, took up the axe with his own hand and struck the tree. From the place where he struck it, water of immortality flowed forth. The king then departed in a troubled state of mind and gave the command: 'Release those two men from prison.' At that very instant a god came running and said: 'As you command.' The two men were released from prison and led to the place of the tree. The king then said: 'That tree, cut down as one, multiplied and became twenty-four — was it you who caused this? The tree I planted produced no roots, no leaves, no blossoms, and no fruit.'

Then the man replied: 'Great King, merit such as I possess is not yours to command.'

Then the thirty million ministers knelt on both knees upon the ground and spoke these words to the man: 'This king is unworthy. You yourself should be king.' Then that man spoke these verses to the ministers:

I have no desire for royal enjoyment.
I have no need of wealth or grain.
By my faith in the buddhas,
may I attain the highest of the two-footed.
In the peaceful expanse of nirvāṇa,
where the Tathāgatas go —
the Dharma that leads to the home of nirvāṇa,
this I shall teach to you.
Seated in cross-legged posture,
I then made a solemn vow:
through the karma I have previously created,
I have come to be in the king's prison.
By the aspiration I have now set forth,
may my karma be exhausted.

Then twenty-four million birds rang out their melodious chimes with their vajra beaks. And at that very time and moment, thirty-two thousand multi-storied palaces arose, each one measuring twenty-five leagues in every dimension. From each of those multi-storied palaces there came twenty-five million birds with golden crests, golden beaks, and golden faces, and they spoke in the human tongue:

O King, you have not acted well.
Because you cut down that tree,
from among a hundred million trees —

come to dwell in the twenty-four.
Whatever sentient being planted this tree,
one who does not know what it is like —
through sinful actions, from now onward,
shall experience unpleasant fruit.

The king spoke:
I do not understand this matter.
O great ascetic, prophesy for me!

The birds spoke:
This one is the light of the world,
the one who shall become a perfect guide,
who shall liberate all sentient beings
from the prison of saṃsāra's existence.

The king spoke:
The second person through whom the tree did not grow —
who is that one?
What sinful action did he perform?
Let those born of two speak.

The birds spoke:
The one through whom the tree did not grow
is the fool Devadatta.
If one has done not even a little virtue,
how could his tree ever grow?

Then at that very time and moment, thirty million ministers, having heard this enumeration of the Dharma, all came to dwell fully on the ten grounds and each individually attained superknowledges. And that king too, dwelling on the ten grounds, subsequently attained manifest realization of virtuous qualities.

Then the bodhisattva, the great being Bhaiṣajyasena, addressed the Blessed One with these words: Blessed One, by what cause and by what condition did those thirty-three million ministers come to dwell on the ten grounds and individually attain superknowledges?

The Blessed One declared: Bhaiṣajyasena, listen, and I shall prophesy concerning this.

Then at that moment the Blessed One smiled. And at that moment, from the aperture of the Blessed One's mouth, rays of light of many colors, of various hues, of hundreds and thousands of colors — blue, yellow, red, white, the color of crystal, the color of silver — eighty-four thousand in number, issued forth, and with them illuminated world-realms without limit or bound. Then they returned, circled the Blessed One three times, and dissolved into the crown of the Blessed One's head.

Then the bodhisattva, the great being Bhaiṣajyasena, rose from his seat, arranged his upper robe over one shoulder, placed his right knee on the ground, turned toward the Blessed One, pressed his palms together, and addressed the Blessed One with these words: Blessed One, Tathāgatas, arhats, perfectly and fully awakened buddhas do not smile without cause or condition. What is the cause and what is the condition of this smile?

The Blessed One declared: Bhaiṣajyasena, do you see the multitudes of people coming toward me from world-realms in all four directions?

He replied: Blessed One, I do not see them.

The Blessed One declared: Bhaiṣajyasena, therefore look with discernment upon the multitude of people.

Then the bodhisattva, the great being Bhaiṣajyasena, looked out, and from the eastern direction there appeared a tree seven thousand leagues tall, and gathered on one side of it was a multitude of twenty-five billion people, who were not speaking, not saying anything, not uttering a word, not eating, not rising, not walking — they stood there in complete silence. And from the southern direction there appeared a tree seven thousand leagues tall, and gathered at it was a multitude of twenty-five billion people, and they too were not saying anything, not speaking, entirely silent, utterly wordless, not rising, not walking — they stood there in complete silence. And from the western direction there appeared a tree seven thousand leagues tall, and gathered at it was a multitude of twenty-five billion people, and they too were not saying anything, not speaking, entirely silent, utterly wordless, not rising, not walking — they stood there in complete silence.

And from the northern direction there appeared a tree seven thousand leagues tall, and gathered at it was a multitude of twenty-five billion people, and they too were not saying anything, not speaking, entirely silent, utterly wordless, not rising, not walking — they stood there in complete silence. Then the bodhisattva, the great being Sde of Medicinal Herbs addressed the Blessed One with these words: "If, having asked, I may ask so as to receive a prophecy, and if the Blessed One would grant me that opportunity, I would put certain questions to the Blessed One, the Tathāgata, the arhat, the perfectly and fully awakened buddha."

When this was said, the Blessed One spoke to the bodhisattva, the great being Sde of Medicinal Herbs in these words: "Sde of Medicinal Herbs, ask whatever you wish to ask, and by giving you a prophecy in response to each question I shall gladden your heart."

When this was spoken, the bodhisattva, the great being Sde of Medicinal Herbs addressed the Blessed One with these words: "Blessed One, what is the cause, what is the condition, by which so great a multitude of people has arrived and remains here — having come from the worlds of the four directions, and now, counting also the people of the lower and upper directions, a multitude of fifty billion have arrived and are present?"

The Blessed One declared: "Sde of Medicinal Herbs, go and ask this question yourself directly to those Tathāgatas from whose world-realms these beings have come."

Sde of Medicinal Herbs asked: "By whose miraculous power shall I travel?"

The Blessed One declared: "Sde of Medicinal Herbs, go by the power of your own miraculous ability."

Then the bodhisattva, the great being Sde of Medicinal Herbs circumambulated the Blessed One three times, vanished from that very spot, passed through ninety-six billion world-realms, and arrived in the world-realm called Moonlight, where the Tathāgata, the arhat, the perfectly and fully awakened buddha called Possessor of the Moon was teaching the Dharma, looking forward, surrounded by eighty billion bodhisattvas. There the bodhisattva, the great being Sde of Medicinal Herbs took his seat before the Blessed One Possessor of the Moon, and bowing with palms joined toward the Blessed One, addressed him with these words: "Blessed One, I see that when the multitudes gathered from the ten directions are assembled in the presence of the Tathāgata Śākyamuni in the world-realm of Endurance, they are visible there — yet when they are here, they are not visible. What is the reason for this, Blessed One?"

The Blessed One declared: "Sde of Medicinal Herbs, they are simply moving and dwelling right there."

Sde of Medicinal Herbs asked: "Blessed One, how so?"

Then the bodhisattva, the great being Sde of Medicinal Herbs went to where the Tathāgata Possessor of the Moon was, arrived there, took his seat in the Blessed One's presence, and addressed the Blessed One with these words: "Blessed One, I have come having passed through ninety-six billion world-realms, and in all of them, however many sentient beings I saw — that many I see nowhere at all here."

The Blessed One declared: "Those sentient beings arose from trees without thought."

Sde of Medicinal Herbs asked: "Blessed One, who has seen and who has heard of beings arising from trees without thought?"

The Blessed One declared: "Sde of Medicinal Herbs, have you not seen and heard of beings that do not arise from trees without thought?"

"Blessed One, I have not seen nor heard of such."

The Blessed One declared: "Sde of Medicinal Herbs, if you wish to see them, I shall show you now."

"Blessed One, I wish it. O Sugata, I wish it."

Then from the field of the Tathāgata Possessor of the Moon, with the extending of a hand, there arose from that hand a hundred billion assemblies of people, and each assembly of people extended their hundred hands and scattered incense, garlands, and various unguents toward the Tathāgata.

The Blessed One declared: "Sde of Medicinal Herbs, do you see this assembly of people scattering incense, garlands, and unguents toward the Tathāgata?"

"Blessed One, I see it. O Sugata, I see it."

The Blessed One declared: "These assemblies of people who have arisen are without thought. These people are without thought."

Then all of those hundred billion people — each of whom had a hundred hands — had all their hands fall away at once.

The bodhisattva, the great being Sde of Medicinal Herbs, seeing this, addressed the Blessed One with these words: "Blessed One, what is this? O Sugata, what is this? The hundred hands of these people fell away in an instant. Blessed One, if even one who has a hundred hands cannot be freed, what need is there to speak of a person with only two hands?"

The Blessed One declared: "Sde of Medicinal Herbs, in just the same way, sentient beings are born without thought and they cease without thought. Sde of Medicinal Herbs, know that even that body has come to be without thought."

Sde of Medicinal Herbs asked: "Blessed One, among those sentient beings, which are the young and which are the old?"

The Blessed One declared: "Sde of Medicinal Herbs, there are young sentient beings and there are old ones."

The Blessed One declared: "Those who just now fell down — those are the old. Those who were born from the trees — those are the young."

"Blessed One, I wish to see the young sentient beings."

Then the Tathāgata Possessor of the Moon extended the palm of his right hand, and from the ten directions a hundred billion assemblies of people in each direction came forth. From the directions above and below, fifty billion assemblies of people in each came. When those assemblies of people arrived, they bowed their heads to the feet of the Blessed One in homage; yet they said nothing to the Tathāgata, spoke no word at all, uttered nothing whatsoever, and stood in silence.

Then the bodhisattva, the great being Sde of Medicinal Herbs, addressed the Blessed One with these words: "Blessed One, for what reason do those sentient beings say nothing to the Tathāgata, speak no word at all, and utter nothing whatsoever?"

The Blessed One declared: "Sde of Medicinal Herbs, do you not know? The ground has no thought and does not speak; it utters no word at all and does not know even an aggregate of Dharma. Why is that? Sde of Medicinal Herbs, here, although some of the young sentient beings are seen, they do not know birth, or cessation, or aging, or illness, or sorrow, or lamentation, or separation from what is beloved,

or meeting with the unpleasant, or friendship, or separation, or death, or untimely death, or unbearable suffering — they know none of these things at all. Even seeing them, they do not become disenchanted; how much less could they come to know them? Sde of Medicinal Herbs, these things must be trained in again and again.

Then the bodhisattva, the great being Sde of Medicinal Herbs, addressed the Blessed One with these words: "Blessed One, from where do those young sentient beings who do not seek the Dharma come? From where do they pass away, and where are they reborn?"

The Blessed One declared: "Sde of Medicinal Herbs, listen well. Those who take hold of a human body — they are not made by a silversmith, nor made by a coppersmith, nor made by a carpenter, nor made by a potter, nor are they made through royal fear. Rather, they arise from the coming together of a man and a woman endowed with sinful actions. Those sentient beings, learning the various crafts again and again, thereby experience unbounded, unbearable suffering and unbearable feelings. In that, they experience the karmic maturation of the previously committed sinful and non-virtuous actions. Sde of Medicinal Herbs, these young sentient beings who appear here without standing upright will experience just such feelings of suffering. Sde of Medicinal Herbs, that is why they say nothing and say nothing at all. Sde of Medicinal Herbs, in this way those young sentient beings do not know virtue, they do not know birth, they do not know cessation, and they will not attain a human body — these, Sde of Medicinal Herbs, are called young sentient beings."

He asked: "Blessed One, how are young sentient beings born, and how do they cease?"

The Blessed One declared: "Sde of Medicinal Herbs, it is like this. Just as when someone touches fire with wood, and that wood then gradually bursts into flame — in just the same way, Sde of Medicinal Herbs, at the very beginning one is born in a human body. Having been born, one experiences feeling right away."

He asked: "Blessed One, who is born here, and who, having been born, passes fully into nirvāṇa?"

The Blessed One declared: "Sde of Medicinal Herbs, a buddha is born, and a Tathāgata passes fully into nirvāṇa. It is like this, for example: a king had someone bound and put in prison within a pitch-dark dwelling, and that person, having entered that pitch-dark dwelling, perceived the pitch-dark dwelling as nothing but pitch darkness. Then another person, touched by a previous feeling of suffering, saw him, and that person was filled with distress. Thinking, 'One who has not previously experienced suffering will have his life and livelihood cut off,' that person brought fire there and threw a small fire into the dwelling. The person who had been imprisoned saw that fire. Seeing it, he breathed in relief and felt joy arise. That fire then burned with whatever fuel fed its burning and set the entire dwelling ablaze, and that very person was consumed there. When the king heard that he had been burned, he was displeased and thought to himself: 'From now on I will not have any sentient being imprisoned in my realm.' The king then said to the sentient beings dwelling in his realm: 'O sentient beings, do not be afraid, do not be frightened, do not be in fear. From now on, no one in my realm shall be executed by punishment or put in prison. No sentient being shall be deprived of life. O sentient beings, do not be afraid.' In just this way of breathing relief, Sde of Medicinal Herbs, the Tathāgata has burned all afflictions and fully pacified all illness. Just as, when the dwelling burned, the very body of the person was also burned —

just as one who has entered upon the welfare, benefit, and happiness of sentient beings fully liberates those sentient beings who are bound from their bondage — in just that same way, the Tathāgata, having abandoned the stains of attachment, aversion, and delusion, has arisen in the world like a lamp for all sentient beings, and has fully liberated sentient beings from the bodies of hell-beings, animals, hungry ghosts, and asuras, liberating both the young among sentient beings and the old.

They asked: "Blessed One, how does one perish?"

They asked: "Blessed One, how does one live?"

The Blessed One declared: "Friends, what is called 'consciousness' is death. What is called 'one endowed with life' — that, friends, is living.

Friends, what is called 'the body' is death. It is wound about by tens of millions of sinews. It is possessed of eighty-four thousand pores. It is bound together by twenty thousand limbs. It is held firm by three hundred and sixty bones. Within the body dwell eighty-four kinds of worms, and all those living creatures too are subject to death, and subject also to the cessation of death. When a person dies, all those creatures are cut off from hope. At that moment all the creatures devour one another, and the winds surge and stir, and then those creatures experience feelings of suffering. Some grieve on account of a son. Some grieve on account of a daughter. Some grieve on account of a relative. All of them are pierced by the thorn of grief. They begin to devour one another, and having devoured one another, when two creatures remain, those two fight for seven days, and when seven days have passed, one of the creatures is destroyed and one escapes.

Now, what is called the Dharma of one endowed with life? He thinks to himself: just as living creatures, by turning against one another, come to die, so too do the sentient beings who are childish ordinary persons turn against one another. They have no fear of birth. They have no fear of aging. They have no fear of illness. They have no fear of death. Just as two creatures fight, so too do childish ordinary persons fight with one another.

Then, at the time of death, virtuous persons spoke to him: 'Man, what is it you relied upon? Did you not see even a little of the faults? Did you not see the faults of birth? Did you not see the faults of aging and illness? Did you not see the faults of death?'

He replied: 'Friends, I did indeed see the faults of birth. I did see the faults of aging and illness. And at the very end of all, I did see the faults of death.'

The four assemblies are monks, nuns, laymen, and laywomen who are devoted and faithful to the teaching. Thus, words were spoken for his benefit, and yet not even the slightest act of virtue was performed. You, O man, came to Jambudvīpa and committed non-virtue.'

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The Tibetan names of the monks, bodhisattvas, and assembly members have been rendered into reconstructed Sanskrit equivalents following standard scholarly convention for this text. A few names are straightforward translations of Tibetan epithets rather than attested Sanskrit proper nouns — for example, the nāga king "Sog ma med" (Anavalamba, "without support"), "Nya mid" (Atyuccagāmin, uncertain; the Tibetan literally means "fishless" or "without fish," which may reflect a scribal variant), and several of the divine daughters. Where the Sanskrit equivalents are uncertain, the most philologically plausible renderings have been used.

The bodhisattva "Kun tu dpa' ba" is rendered Sarvadatta following some witnesses to this text, though the name literally means "Universally Courageous"; the tradition is not entirely uniform on the Sanskrit here.  The title "Zuṅ" renders the Tibetan term for a dhāraṇī scripture, here functioning as the proper title of the text being discussed within the sūtra frame. The Tibetan word is a shortened form of "dhāraṇī" and refers specifically to the Zuṅ collection or a sūtra within it; retaining "Zuṅ" as a proper title here is appropriate given the narrative context.

The phrase rendered "five crimes of immediate retribution" translates "mtsams med pa lnga'i las," referring to the five ānantarya-karmas, the gravest actions that lead immediately to rebirth in a hell realm without any intervening state.  The name Sarvavikrāntagāmin (Tibetan Kun tu dpa' ba) literally means "one who goes forth with complete courage" or "universally courageous one." It is kept as a proper name throughout for consistency.

The phrase rendered "utterly parched for the Dharma" translates chos kyis yongs su skom pa, literally "thoroughly thirsting for the Dharma." This is a distinctive technical usage in this text describing a particular spiritual hunger or longing; the rendering "parched" preserves the thirst metaphor.

The passage ends mid-sentence as the Tibetan source breaks off at that point.  The compound "གང་ཟུང་" rendered throughout as "Dhāraṇī" refers to the specific sūtra of paired dhāraṇīs that is the subject of the discourse; context makes this consistent with the broader passage. The phrase "ཀ་ཤི་ཀའི་རས" (Kāśī cloth) refers to the extremely fine silk or muslin fabric famous from Vārāṇasī (Kāśī), used in Buddhist literature as a standard image for a fabric so soft it barely contacts a surface — hence the mountain-erosion simile.  The closing statement of the Blessed One — rendered as "do not ask me all at once about the accumulation of non-virtuous karma" — translates the Tibetan idiom "ཁ་རོག་ཁ་རོག" literally meaning something like "in one gulp" or "all at once," suggesting the matter is too great or weighty to be enumerated in one go. The idiomatic force is that the topic is so vast it cannot be summarized simply, and the translation above captures this implication. Some readers may understand the phrase as meaning "do not ask me to enumerate it exhaustively," which amounts to the same doctrinal point.  The name rendered throughout as "Sarvavikrāntagāmin" appears in the preceding passage; this passage uses the abbreviated form "Kun tu dpa' ba," which is its Tibetan rendering of that name, and is kept consistent accordingly.

The list of eight great hells follows the standard Abhidharma enumeration. The Tibetan names "sPu zhing" (Hair-Raising) and "Kyi hud" are phonetic or semantic Tibetan renderings of Sanskrit hell names — "Kyi hud" likely corresponds to Huhuva or a similar hell of extreme cold or cry, though the passage here places it among the hot hells. The sequence given does not match the standard eight hot hells list precisely; this may reflect a variant or abbreviated enumeration in this particular sūtra.  The final sentence breaks off mid-clause in the source passage; the translation preserves this as-is, since the text is clearly incomplete at this point. The passage continues in the next segment.  The phrase rendered "Mount Kailāsa" translates "ri nag po," literally "the Black Mountain." In this context it functions as a simile for a lesser mountain compared to Meru; some traditions identify it as Kailāsa, but it may simply mean a generic dark or black mountain. The doctrinal point of the image — that a lesser peak bowing without being pressed down is still less than bowing to the greatest — is clear regardless.

The name "Kharoc" is a transliteration of "kha rog," which literally means "crow" or "raven" and is used here as a personal name or epithet for the bodhisattva Sarvāśūra. This is unusual and may reflect a vernacular or regional name tradition in the source text.  The phrase rendered "Coupled Sūtras" translates a term meaning a category or collection of sūtras paired or joined together. The precise identity of this canonical collection is not entirely clear from this passage alone, though it likely refers to a specific grouping within the sūtra literature.
