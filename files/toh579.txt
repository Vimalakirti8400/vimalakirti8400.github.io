Toh 579 in the Kangyur is a dhāraṇī text known in Tibetan as Pha rol tu phyin pa drug gi snying po'i gzungs, which may be rendered in English as the Dhāraṇī of the Essence of the Six Perfections, with a likely Sanskrit title along the lines of Ṣaṭpāramitāhṛdayadhāraṇī. It belongs to the broader Perfection of Wisdom class of canonical literature and follows directly upon a dhāraṇī associated with the Perfection of Wisdom in Eight Thousand Lines, distilling the essence of all six perfections — generosity, ethical discipline, patience, diligence, meditative concentration, and wisdom — into a compact ritual formula. The text instructs that holding and continuously reciting this dhāraṇī enables the practitioner to grasp the meaning of all six perfections, realize the emptiness of all phenomena, and purify grave misdeeds including the five actions of immediate karmic retribution. As a dhāraṇī sūtra situated within the Kangyur's Perfection of Wisdom section, it represents a concise, mantrically oriented expression of prajñāpāramitā teaching intended for devotional and purificatory practice.

All obscurations and all lower rebirths will be completely purified. The dhāraṇī of the noble Perfection of Wisdom in Eight Thousand Lines is complete.

Homage to the noble mother, the Perfection of Wisdom. Homage to the Buddha. Homage to the Dharma. Homage to the Saṅgha.

Namo dharmakāya, saṃbhogakāya, nirmāṇakāya. Tadyathā: dāna-pāramitā, śīla-pāramitā, kṣānti-pāramitā, vīrya-pāramitā, dhyāna-pāramitā, prajñā-pāramitā, sarva-dharma-śūnyatā svāhā.

By holding this dhāraṇī, one will grasp all six perfections. Emptiness will be taken to heart. If it is recited continuously, one will come to know the essence and the elaboration of all phenomena. The five actions of immediate retribution will be purified. All evil omens will be rendered harmless.

The dhāraṇī of the essence of the six perfections is complete.

The phrase rendered "essence and elaboration of all phenomena" translates "mdo dang rgyas pa," a standard pair meaning the condensed and expanded forms of the teachings — the gist and the full extent — of all Dharma. The five actions of immediate retribution (mtshams med pa lnga) are the five heinous acts that, in classical Abhidharma, lead inevitably to rebirth in the lowest hell in the very next life: killing one's father, killing one's mother, killing an arhat, wounding a buddha, and causing a schism in the Saṅgha.
