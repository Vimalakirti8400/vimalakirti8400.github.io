Learning a Hundred Verses

Learning a Hundred Ślokas is a very brief text that opens with homage to the Three Jewels and to the noble bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara, the great being endowed with great compassion. It presents a short mantra and a simple ritual instruction: while washing the face, one recites the mantra three or seven times over a handful of water and then drinks it. This practice is said to let a person learn a hundred ślokas of scripture in a single day and to keep what was learned before from being forgotten. Its Sanskrit and Tibetan titles cannot be fully determined from the material at hand. The work is Toh 1043 in the Kangyur, where it belongs among the dhāraṇī and mantra texts of the tantra section, a class of short practical scriptures devoted to recitation for specific benefits, here the strengthening of learning and memory.

Homage to the Three Jewels.
Homage to the bodhisattva, the great being, endowed with great compassion, the noble Avalokiteśvara.

tadyathā khali khali balite svāhā

When washing your face, recite this mantra three or seven times over a handful of water and drink it. Then even in a single day you will learn a hundred ślokas of text, and what you learned before will not be forgotten.

Here ends Learning a Hundred Ślokas.
