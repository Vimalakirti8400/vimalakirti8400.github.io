It belongs to the class of tantric literature associated with Mañjuśrī, the bodhisattva of wisdom, and is structured as a series of chapters combining ritual instruction, wrathful invocations, and praises addressed to wisdom deities embodying fierce, transformative power. As a guhya tantra, or "secret tantra," the text concerns esoteric methods for accomplishing the four traditional activities (pacifying, increasing, controlling, and subjugating) through visualization, mantra, and ritual identification with wrathful forms of enlightened wisdom. Its closing lines mark the completion of the fourteenth chapter and the tantra's overall arrangement, situating it among the Kangyur's collection of tantric scriptures tied to the Mañjuśrī cycle.

adorned in body with a fresh, moist tiger skin,
bearing such a form, blazing in its array—

Having offered this praise, they became non-dual.
