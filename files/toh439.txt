A Fragment of the Root Tantra of the Vajra Kīla (Vajrakīlaya Mūlatantra Khaṇḍa)

The Fragment of the Root Tantra of the Vajra Kīla, known in Sanskrit as the Vajrakīlaya Mūlatantra Khaṇḍa and in Tibetan as Dorje Phurpa Tsawé Gyü Kyi Dumbu, is a tantric text belonging to the Vajrakīlaya cycle, one of the most important traditions of wrathful deity practice in Tibetan Buddhism. It concerns the fierce deity Vajrakumāra, the Vajra Youth, and presents teachings on the transformation of negative forces — particularly aversion — through the ritual and meditative power of the kīla, or ritual dagger, understood as an emanation of vajra wrath and compassion. The text weaves together verse, mantra, and doctrinal instruction, treating the kīla not as an instrument of mere destruction but as a means of liberating beings by transmuting the aggregates of existence into vajra. It is Toh 439 in the Kangyur, where it is classified among the tantric scriptures, and survives as a fragment, suggesting it represents a portion of a larger root tantra rather than a complete and self-contained work.

Homage to the Blessed One, Vajrakumāra.

The vajra wrathful one severs aversion,
the great blue weapon blazing intensely.
A bindu arises in the middle of space,
arising as the gateway of the life-force,
and is to be meditated upon in the center of the heart.
The sacred commitment of liberation through compassion
is not mere killing and suppression:
the very essence of the vajra of the aggregates —
meditate on consciousness as vajra.

May the vidyādharas of Vajrakumāra
accomplish the turning of saṃsāra into vajra.
May the deity of the Vajra Kīla, the vajra of saṃsāra,
be accomplished as the wrathful one of primordial wisdom.
The wisdom body of all buddhas,
arising from the very nature of vajra suchness,
the essential nature of the dharmadhātu —
through the unbearable blazing wrathful one,
may I be born as the treasure of the body.
Acting with skillful means for the benefit of sentient beings,
taming them with loving-kindness and compassion,
may the enlightened activity of the buddhas be perfected.
Grant here empowerment and blessing.

Those who are vicious and bear a mind of hatred —
begone! whether asuras or gods alike.

render them without power or magical ability.
