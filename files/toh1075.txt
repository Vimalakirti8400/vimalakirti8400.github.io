The Dhāraṇī of Circumambulation (Pradakṣiṇadhāraṇī)

The Dhāraṇī of Circumambulation, whose Sanskrit title is likely rendered as Pradakṣiṇadhāraṇī and whose Tibetan title refers to circumambulation, is a very brief dhāraṇī text in the Kangyur, catalogued as Toh 1075. It opens with homage to the Tathāgata Ratnaketu, the buddha whose name means "Jewel Banner," and then presents a short incantation invoking him through repeated evocations of the jewel, closing with the word svāhā. The text promises that anyone who recites this dhāraṇī while circumambulating a sacred object or site will find that even a single round becomes equal to countless circumambulations, greatly multiplying the merit of this devotional practice. It belongs to the class of dhāraṇī texts, the collection of short ritual formulas preserved in the Kangyur for recitation and practical devotional use.

Homage to the Blessed One, the Tathāgata, the arhat, the perfectly and fully awakened buddha Ratnaketu!

namo bhagavate ratnaketurājāya tathāgatāya arhate saṃmyaksaṃpuddhaya tadyathā oṃ ratne ratne mahāratne ratnavijaye svāhā

If one recites this while circumambulating, even a single circumambulation becomes equal to countless circumambulations.

This completes the Dhāraṇī of Circumambulation.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The Tibetan name Rinchen Gyaltsen, "Jewel Banner," is a standard rendering of Ratnaketu. The dhāraṇī's own wording, ratnaketurāja, adds "king," which the Tibetan homage does not.
