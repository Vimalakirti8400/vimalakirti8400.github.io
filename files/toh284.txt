The Noble Great Vehicle Sūtra Entitled 'The Three Aggregates' (Āryatriskandhaka-nāma-mahāyānasūtra)

Toh 284 in the Kangyur is a Mahāyāna sūtra known in Sanskrit as the Āryatriskandhaka-nāma-mahāyānasūtra and in Tibetan as the Phung po gsum pa, meaning "The Three Aggregates" or, as an alternate rendering suggests, "The Three Bodies." It is a relatively concise scripture in which the Buddha, dwelling at the Dharma-grove hermitage, sets forth a practice centered on paying homage to the buddhas of past, present, and future, confessing misdeeds, and rejoicing in virtue — the three "aggregates" or collections of meritorious action that give the text its name. The sūtra is notable for its devotional orientation and its vision of the buddhas as endowed with boundless wisdom, the dharmakāya, and the dharmadhātu. It belongs to the general sūtra section of the Kangyur and has been widely used in Tibetan Buddhism as a liturgical confession and purification text.

Thus is completed the Great Vehicle sūtra entitled "The Three Bodies."

Now, in the language of India: Āryatriskandhaka-nāma-mahāyānasūtra. In the language of Tibet: The noble Great Vehicle sūtra entitled "The Three Aggregates."

Homage to all buddhas and bodhisattvas.

Thus did I hear. At one time the Blessed One was dwelling in the Dharma-grove hermitage. Having paid homage to those Blessed One buddhas of the past, the future, and the present — who possess the mind that has thoroughly understood all things through great primordial wisdom, who have attained equality with all buddhas, who abide in reliance upon the dharmakāya which is without duality, who possess a wisdom vast as the expanse of space, unobstructed and boundless, who are endowed with a body fully entered into the dharmadhātu in its entirety, adorned with the crown of Dharma, free from attachment, bound by the supreme, the holy, the stainless, who with the proclamation of the great individual discriminating awarenesses speak with a voice making distinctions most precise, whose maṇḍala of wisdom is vast and luminous through the inexhaustible treasury of merit, who abide independently through their realization of the unafflicted Dharma, who are endowed with the body of wisdom utterly liberated throughout the entire dharmadhātu, whose praises of qualities, though proclaimed for incalculable tens of millions of eons, are inexhaustible and boundless, and who have attained the distinction of displaying praises of boundless qualities — to these I pay homage.

As many Tathāgatas as there are sentient beings: beginning from the full excellence of the praises of the qualities of ethical conduct of each single Tathāgata among all those Blessed One buddhas, even if one were to proclaim praises for endless eons, the full excellence of the praises of the qualities of ethical conduct of that Tathāgata would still be inexhaustible and without end. And the confidence and discriminating awareness of those Tathāgatas is likewise without end. Thus I am devoted, with the highest devotion, to those Blessed One buddhas whose ethical discipline is immeasurable.

I am devoted, with the highest devotion, to them as proclaimed by the perfectly and completely awakened buddhas — the chief, the holy, the supremely distinguished, the foremost, the best, the excellent, the highest, the unsurpassed, the unequalled, the incomparably equal, the matchless, the unrivalled, the great sovereign, the unconquered, those who gather all qualities of virtue.

Just as with ethical conduct, so likewise with meditative absorption, wisdom, liberation, and the wisdom-vision of liberation; and similarly with great loving-kindness, great compassion, great joy, and great equanimity; and similarly with powers and fearlessnesses; and so on up through all the qualities of a buddha arising perfectly — to all of these I am devoted with the highest devotion. And having been so devoted, I pay homage to those Blessed One buddhas, I praise them, I honour them, I extol them, I pay tribute to them, I bow down to them fully, I make prostration, I incline toward them, I look upon them, I contemplate them — through deeds of body in loving-kindness, deeds of speech in loving-kindness, and deeds of mind in loving-kindness, preceded by wisdom, all-good and adorned with the buddha's splendour — through deeds of body, speech, and mind I pay homage to those Blessed One buddhas, I praise them, I honour them, I extol them, I pay tribute to them, I bow down to them fully, I make prostration, I incline toward them, I look upon them, I contemplate them.

With flowers of immeasurable aspect, with incense, with garlands, with unguents, with powders, with Dharma-robes, with parasols, with victory-banners, and with

flags, with lamps, with arrangements, with canopies, with the building of stūpas, with the making of sacred images, with precious jewels, with ornaments, with vehicles, with promenades, with temples, and with all manner of furnishings — by all these vast and perfectly pure offerings I pay honour to those Tathāgatas, I revere them, I glorify them, I worship them, I venerate them, I serve them with full ceremony.

With worship that arises with certainty from the power of devoted aspiration, that utterly transcends the three realms, that arises from the phenomena of one who is without affliction, that is sealed with the mudrā of one who forms no constructs, that is perfectly accomplished from the gnosis-illusion, that is fully distinguished from the action-illusion, that is utterly purified from all abodes, that arises from the world-illusion, that is connected with the phenomena of essential nature, with the worship proclaimed by the perfectly and fully awakened buddhas — to those Tathāgatas I pay honour, I revere them, I glorify them, I worship them, I venerate them, I serve them with full ceremony.

Furthermore, those bodhisattvas who aspire to permeate the stream of the Tathāgata, who aspire to bring the stream of sentient beings to full maturation, who aspire to thoroughly purify the stream of buddha-fields, who aspire to remain in harmony with the stream of karma, who aspire to distinguish thoroughly the stream of Dharma, who aspire to fully comprehend the stream of the three times, who aspire to enter fully into the stream of the directions, who aspire to place themselves fully in the stream of non-attachment, who aspire to thoroughly purify the stream of aspiration, who aspire to survey the stream of the faculties, who aspire to transform fully into the wheel of the dharmadhātu, who aspire to gather together every wheel of beings' migrations — just as the Blessed One buddhas know and know supremely with a knowledge that is without attachment and without obscuration, and just as those bodhisattvas in that way offer worship to those Tathāgatas, so too do I, with the worship proclaimed by the perfectly and fully awakened buddhas, worship that thoroughly delights every maṇḍala assembly of bodhisattvas, that arises from the boundless conduct of the bodhisattva Samantabhadra, that enters in accordance with the domain in which the highest gnosis of the bodhisattvas abides — with such worship I pay homage to the Tathāgatas. For the sake of the act of offering to the Tathāgatas and of attending upon them, one called the supreme crown-jewel of the world dwells in all the infinite world-systems, and this offering to the Tathāgatas — arising from the perfections, arising from phenomena that are unborn, known by the wisdom-eye born in the noble lineage of the bodhisattvas, filling all buddha-domains to fullness — enters into the many world-systems pervaded by name that belong to the dharmadhātu, into the maṇḍala assemblies that fill the dharmadhātu to its fullness, and into those world-systems reaching to the limit of the expanse of space that belong to the dharmadhātu.

Beyond even that, with offerings endowed with immeasurable qualities, generated by the force of the great root of virtue, illuminating the lordship of the vajra, arising from the gnosis of the king of flowers that shines throughout all buddha-domains — with such offerings to those Tathāgatas I pay honour, I revere them, I glorify them, I worship them, I venerate them, I serve them with full ceremony. Just as I have made offering for my own sake, so too, in all the infinite and without-exception world-systems, for the sake of every sentient being there is, in every category of sentient being that exists, and for the sake of all those realms of sentient beings, I make offering to those Tathāgatas.

Thus, with a mind aroused and moistened by the power of making offering to those Tathāgatas, guided by great loving-kindness and great compassion, endowed with the aspiration to protect all beings without exception, I have entered into the treasury of the blessings of all the Tathāgatas. I have been fully embraced by the great power of wisdom and skillful means. Having utterly transcended all the paths and domains of māra, I now confess all deeds that would become obscurations.

Whatever the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, have not sanctioned with their word; whatever noble beings have wholly abandoned; whatever the Tathāgatas have censured; whatever bodhisattvas have entirely forsaken; whatever noble ones have renounced; whatever the learned have disputed; whatever the masters of knowledge have discarded — deeds arising through the compulsion of attachment, aversion, and delusion; or arising through the power of pride, concealment, and resentment; or through the power of envy, miserliness, and grief; or through the power of deceit and cunning; or arising through the force of habitual clinging — be they obscurations of karma, obscurations of phenomena, obscurations of the afflictions, obscurations of views, obscurations of ignorance, obscurations of karmic maturation, obscurations that lead to impoverishment of the Dharma, obscurations associated with the vehicles of the śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas, obscurations that become impediments to the Great Vehicle, obscurations harmful to the accumulation of all roots of virtue, obscurations born of consorting with evil companions, or obscurations and severances that obstruct the full completion of the aspirations of the conduct of the bodhisattva Samantabhadra — all such obscuring deeds, done and accumulated, all the arising of obscuration whereby sentient beings would go to the hells, would go to the realm of animal birth, would go to the domain of hungry ghosts, would be born as impoverished gods, would fail to hold the vision of the buddhas in view, would fail to receive and hold the Dharma when heard, would fail to recognise the Three Jewels — the Tathāgatas, the Dharma, and the Saṅgha — would be unable to obtain the companionship of bodhisattvas, would fail to find a true and genuine spiritual friend to fully embrace them, would come to hold only the most trifling roots of virtue, would lose the awakening mind, would fail to hear the path of the perfections, would be overcome by evil māras, would cause the buddha's Dharma to become remote, would be turned back from the engagement in cultivating all roots of virtue, would become deceitful toward spiritual friends, would fail to obtain a completely pure intention, would fail to obtain unfailing mindfulness, would fail to obtain insight into phenomena, would have no devotion toward the emanations of the buddhas, would come to have doubts about the qualities of the buddhas, would fail to penetrate the gateway of meditative absorption, would fail to attain progression from ground to ground, would fail to obtain the companionship of bodhisattvas, would fail to penetrate the import of having accomplished the work of completing the perfections, would be displaced from the great aspiration, would fail to obtain purity of conduct, would fail to obtain the illumination of the ornament of dhāraṇī, would fail to penetrate the illumination of the ornament of confident eloquence, would fail to penetrate the import of becoming fully familiar with the aspirations of the conduct of the bodhisattva Samantabhadra, would fail to realise the equality of phenomena, would fail to generate the equality of merit, would fail to penetrate the equality of gnosis —

— would be utterly destroyed by the phenomena of saṃsāra, and all those countless one million obscurations that veil one from turning toward the accomplishment of the bodhisattva's conduct and all the qualities of the Dharma — all of these, belonging to the three times, are gathered into the dharmadhātu by all the Tathāgatas who see with gnosis free of obscuration, who have obtained the domain of the eye free of attachment, who are endowed with the body that has entered into equality, who have entered the dharmadhātu free of darkness, and who have obtained the eye of gnosis. Holding firm in the conviction that one stands directly in the presence of all those Tathāgatas, and reflecting upon the immeasurable domain of the Tathāgatas, one holds the fervent conviction that in every space the size of a single fraction of a hair's tip there are Tathāgatas as numerous as all sentient beings. Just as it is in the space of a single fraction of a hair's tip, so too throughout the entire dharmadhātu one holds the fervent conviction that there are Tathāgatas as numerous as all sentient beings. Before each and every Tathāgata, one touches the soles of their feet with bodies as numerous as the minds of all migrating beings, and pays homage. For each body one holds the fervent conviction of a number of words of definitive meaning equal to the number of heads. For each head one holds the fervent conviction of a number of tongue-tips equal to the number of atoms that would fill all realms. For each tongue-tip's maṇḍala, one reflects upon the sound of words of definitive meaning — the conventional designations proclaimed by the perfectly and completely awakened buddhas, in accord with the speech conduct of all beings, in accord with the maṇḍala that is free of obscuration — and those karmic acts that would become obscurations, arising from the dispersal of the great mountain of defilements from the mind of supreme aspiration, are confessed. They are not concealed. Through confession that reaches throughout all world-realms, they are confessed, removed, shattered, utterly destroyed, abandoned, expelled — and one exercises power over karma. This is dedicated entirely for the sake of showing happiness to those who have attained the arising of strength.

Furthermore, that bodhisattva who aspires to please all the Tathāgatas, who aspires to purify entirely all bodhisattva conduct, who aspires to pass beyond all the dispositions of the bodhisattva, who aspires to fulfil all the aspirations of the bodhisattva, who aspires to obtain all the treasuries of the bodhisattva, who aspires to uphold the teachings of all the Tathāgatas rightly, who aspires to fulfil entirely all the perfections, who aspires to bring all sentient beings to full maturation, who aspires to realise all the purities of world-realms, who aspires to realise the fulfilment of the aspiration of the bodhisattva Samantabhadra's conduct — just as a buddha, a Blessed One, knows and thoroughly knows through buddha-cognition that is free of attachment and free of obscuration, so too one confesses those karmic acts that would become obscurations, and aspires that through such confession they may be well and truly confessed, become entirely pure, become luminous, become free of fault, become clear, become free of poison, become free of obscuration, become free of interruption,

— and so may all those karmic faults that would become obscurations to realising every quality of Dharma be confessed. Just as I confess for my own sake, so too, for the sake of all the sentient beings — as many as there are among the classes of sentient beings in every single one of the countless world-realms without remainder — may all those karmic faults that would become obscurations be confessed for them all.

Having thus confessed individually every one of my own karmic faults that would become obscurations, I abide in the maṇḍala of utter purity, free of obscuration, and I rejoice in the roots of virtue of all beings.

I rejoice in that excellence of a collection of roots of virtue by which every instance of being led into the misfortune of the lower realms — the misfortune of hungry ghosts (pretas) who are destitute — is entirely reversed, the mind being unharmed and luminous.

I rejoice in that excellence of a collection of roots of virtue by which every instance of being led into the misfortune of the lower-realm animal existence is entirely reversed.

I rejoice in that excellence of a collection of roots of virtue by which every instance of being led into the misfortune of the hell-beings is entirely reversed.

I rejoice in that excellence of a collection of roots of virtue arising from an auspicious station — by which every single birth among gods and humans is fully grasped and secured.

I rejoice in that excellence of a collection of roots of virtue by which all the joy, delight, and bliss of meditative concentration (dhyāna) are experienced.

I rejoice in that excellence of a collection of roots of virtue by which the mind of weariness toward saṃsāra is attained.

I rejoice in that excellence of a collection of roots of virtue by which one attains to being fully held by a true and genuine spiritual friend (Skt. kalyāṇamitra).

I rejoice in that excellence of a collection of roots of virtue by which one finds a mind of reverence toward spiritual friends.

I rejoice in that excellence of a collection of roots of virtue by which one pleases the buddhas, the Blessed Ones — and having pleased them, never again displeases them.

I rejoice in that excellence of a collection of roots of virtue by which one directly hears and listens to the Dharma as it is taught by the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, and thereby reverses all doubt and wavering of mind.

I rejoice in that excellence of a collection of roots of virtue by which one realises the arising of all three worlds from their source.

I rejoice in that excellence of a collection of roots of virtue by which one attains the arousal of the mind of a teacher toward all sentient beings.

I rejoice in that excellence of a collection of roots of virtue by which one attains the unobstructed eye that sees all beings without impediment.

I rejoice in that excellence of a collection of roots of virtue by which one purifies the great aspiration of following in the wake of the level of equality.

I rejoice in that excellence of a collection of roots of virtue by which one attains a body well adorned with the major and minor marks of excellence.

I rejoice in that excellence of a collection of roots of virtue by which one attains the utterly pure ornaments — the adornments of all qualities entirely purified.

I rejoice in that excellence of a collection of roots of virtue by which one attains the precious mind adorned with the ten powers.

I rejoice in that excellence of a collection of roots of virtue by which one attains irreversibility through the momentum of omniscience itself.

I rejoice in that excellence of a collection of roots of virtue by which one thoroughly penetrates the families of dhāraṇīs.

I rejoice in that excellence of a collection of roots of virtue by which one thoroughly penetrates the utterly pure dharmadhātu, the basic space of phenomena.

I rejoice in that excellence of a collection of roots of virtue by which one, abiding in inexhaustible karmic maturation, attains the superknowledges (abhijñā).

I rejoice in that excellence of a collection of roots of virtue by which one thoroughly penetrates the accomplishment of the ornaments of buddha-fields.

I rejoice in that excellence of a collection of roots of virtue by which one thoroughly penetrates having utterly vanquished all the assembled armies of Māra's conflict and thereby become supreme among the herd.

I rejoice in the roots of virtue consisting in the knowledge, liberation, freedom from attachment, and freedom from all defilements born from the equality of the Dharma of all śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas.

I rejoice in all the roots of virtue of all bodhisattvas — from those endowed with all bodhisattva conduct all the way to those endowed with the thorough penetration of unsurpassed, perfectly complete awakening.

For the sake of the acts of worship offered to the Tathāgatas, I rejoice in whatever pervades all the limitless world-systems called "that which is distinguished as the offering and veneration rendered to the Tathāgatas by bodhisattvas who have been empowered."

For the sake of the acts of worship offered to the Tathāgatas, I rejoice in whatever pervades all the limitless world-systems called "that which is distinguished as the offering and veneration rendered to the Tathāgatas by bodhisattvas who dwell in the supreme abode of Tuṣita."

For the sake of the acts of worship offered to the Tathāgatas, I rejoice in whatever pervades all the limitless world-systems called "Illumination, the essence of the blooming flower of merit of bodhisattvas dwelling in the womb" — which consists of offering and veneration rendered to the Tathāgatas.

For the sake of the acts of worship offered to the Tathāgatas, I rejoice in whatever pervades all the limitless world-systems called "the Crown Teacher of Samantabhadra, the bodhisattva who dwells at the supreme, most excellent seat of awakening" — which consists of offering and veneration rendered to the Tathāgatas.

I rejoice in the roots of virtue of the perfectly complete buddhas who have come in the three times — those endowed with inconceivable magical transformations through skillful means.

Moreover, previously, may the bodhisattva utterly tame all the assembled armies of Māra's conflict; may he overwhelm all the assembled forces of opposing adversaries; may he dispel the darkness of ignorance, deep darkness, and the many veils of cataracts; may he sever all paths to the lower rebirths, states of misfortune, and unfree conditions; may he thoroughly purify all the maṇḍalas of gnosis; may he attain illumination of all phenomena; may he attain the supreme body of Samantabhadra, foremost among the herd; may he attain the thoroughly pure melodious voice that fulfills the wishes of all beings exactly as they are; and for the sake of realizing all phenomena exactly as they are, may he thoroughly purify the eye of gnosis; may he reverse the wheel of saṃsāra; may he set turning the great wheel of Dharma.

Just as those who have been overwhelmed by the wisdom of the ten powers rejoice, following the example of how the blessed buddhas know and thoroughly know with a buddha's knowledge that is free of attachment and free of obscuration, so too do I rejoice with the supreme act of rejoicing, rejoicing in the entire accumulation of roots of virtue.

I rejoice, following the declaration of the perfectly and completely awakened buddhas, who are supreme, holy, supremely excellent, foremost, consummate, magnificent, the highest, beyond whom there is none higher, without equal, equal to the unequal, without peer, without compare, without rival, of great selfhood, unovercome, and who gather to themselves all qualities and virtuous phenomena.

Just as I rejoice for my own sake, so too, on behalf of all the realms of sentient beings, without remainder, throughout all the infinite world-systems, for all those classes of sentient beings in whatever number those realms of beings exist, I rejoice in all those roots of virtue.

Having thus rejoiced in all roots of virtue, I urge all the blessed buddhas. In the world-systems of the ten directions, reaching to the very limits of the ten directions, pervading all world-systems without remainder; encompassing the reaches of the lineage of those uncovered above; realizing the world-systems of the unrestricted lineage; throughout all the infinite world-systems belonging to the dharmadhātu, like a magical display of differentiation, wherever there are blessed buddhas who, having fully subdued all the armies of the host of Māra's disputes, have become completely and perfectly awakened in unsurpassed, complete, perfect awakening, who have attained non-obstruction with respect to all phenomena, who have arrived at the supreme essence of awakening, who have become of a nature that is unswayed in their manner of the Dharma, and who possess a dharmakāya that is utterly at peace — all these, standing upon the seat of urging in all the world, for the sake of taking up the Dharma, being those blessed buddhas who have not yet turned the wheel of Dharma — to these I bow with great respect, as one reverences a teacher in a painted image, and I urge them to set the great wheel of Dharma fully turning.

I urge this so that all bodhisattvas may be perfectly accomplished; so that all beings may be protected; so that all afflictions may be thoroughly purified; so that all doubts may be utterly vanquished; so that all uncertainties may be completely cut away; so that the buddha, the Dharma, and the Saṅgha may all be proclaimed; and so that the arising of buddhas in all buddha-fields may flow on without interruption. I urge all the blessed buddhas who have most thoroughly trained in all the branches of the perfections, who are irreversible, who are unovercome by the ten powers, who shatter the wheel of cyclic existence, who are of right view, who completely destroy all afflictions, who have proclaimed the four individual perfect cognitions, who are endowed with a voice of supreme discrimination, and who have examined the mode of the Four Noble Truths — to turn the great wheel of Dharma.

And I also urge all bodhisattvas, so that they may especially take as their object the power of great compassion, so that they may not interrupt the stream of their own bodhisattva conduct, and so that they may thoroughly train in all the paths of the bodhisattva.

And I also urge all śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas, so that they may especially take as their object the power of great compassion. And I also urge all sentient beings, so that they may completely turn back all non-virtuous phenomena and bring all virtuous phenomena to full completion.

Furthermore, just as those endowed with the accumulation of urging bodhisattvas urge all buddhas, all bodhisattvas, all śrāvakas, all pratyekabuddhas, and all sentient beings — in the manner that the blessed buddhas, whose knowledge of buddhahood is free from attachment and free from obscuration, know and fully know — so too do I urge with the supreme urging, the chief urging, the holy urging, the especially exalted urging, the excellent urging, the sublime urging, the excellent and distinguished urging, the highest urging, the unsurpassed urging, the unexcelled urging, the unequalled urging, the equal-to-the-unequalled urging, the incomparable urging, the unrivalled urging, the supremely great urging, the invincible urging, the urging that gathers all qualities and virtuous phenomena — the urging proclaimed by the perfectly and completely awakened buddhas. And just as I urge for my own sake, so too do I urge for the sake of all sentient beings, of every class of beings, throughout all the innumerable world-realms without remainder.

Furthermore, just as I have earnestly beseeched all Tathāgatas, so now, in all the world-realms of the ten directions extending to the very limits of the ten directions, pervading and entering every world-realm throughout its entire expanse, encompassing the entire scope of the unobstructed tantra from above, accomplishing the world-realms of the agitated tantra, and throughout all the innumerable world-realms belonging to the dharmadhātu that are like the magical display of differentiated vision — in all of these there are blessed buddhas, some of whom have turned the wheels of Dharma with great thoroughness, thoroughly tamed those who are to be tamed, thoroughly laid the bridges, thoroughly consecrated the teachings with blessing, thoroughly proclaimed the prophecies to bodhisattvas, thoroughly performed the deeds of a buddha, thoroughly cut through all quarters of opposing disputants, whose subsequent teachings are supremely exalted, who have proclaimed them throughout all buddha-fields — to these blessed buddhas I earnestly make supplication: that they may teach no-self in all objects to those beings, in those various pure abodes, those various buddha-fields, those various world-realms, those various classes of beings, who are disrespectful, whose faculties are thoroughly weary, who are not suitable vessels, whose stream of beholding the Tathāgata has been cut, who are perpetually attached, who delight in sense objects, who are seized by the obscurations of conditioned existence, who are under the power of craving for the five qualities of desire — teaching no-self correctly in all objects so as to generate disenchantment in those sentient beings who delight in cyclic existence; and that they may correctly teach that all conditioned formations are impermanent, correctly teaching that all prosperity is the gateway to ultimate ruin; and that they may correctly teach that all living faculties end in death, for the sake of correctly teaching the great nirvāṇa, the perfect peace of the fully awakened buddhas — and that, the Tathāgata having departed and completely gone beyond, they may reveal the great sorrow in the world; and that before the world of gods, humans, and asuras they may display the great nirvāṇa, with all the lords of gods weeping, all the lords of nāgas grieving, all the lords of yakṣas lamenting, and all the lords of beings deeply sorrowful — the great nirvāṇa displayed before gods, humans,

and asuras — before that world, I prostrated my body with the greatest reverence and veneration toward those teachers, and I implore those blessed buddhas to extend their lives and to remain long. May those blessed buddhas turn all beings back from the mass of suffering, establish all sentient beings in supreme happiness, cut off the path of poverty for all sentient beings, liberate all sentient beings from the great and desolate wilderness of saṃsāra, establish all beings in the unobscured domain of the buddhas, sever every path of poverty, satisfy all beings with the Jewel of the Dharma, fully purify all world-systems, bring the taming of all beings to its ultimate conclusion, and fully embrace all bodhisattvas — and to this end I beseech those blessed buddhas to remain for kalpas that reach to the limits of the dharmadhātu, that touch the very edge of the expanse of space.

To those blessed buddhas I make this supreme and earnest supplication. I implore them with the supplication that is chief, holy, supremely noble, excellent, highest, exquisite, supreme, unsurpassed, unexcelled, unequalled, equal to the unequalled, without comparison, without parallel, radiant without rival, great in self-nature, inexhaustible, gathering all qualities and virtues — the supplication proclaimed by the perfectly and fully awakened buddhas.

And further, just as bodhisattvas show such veneration, such prostration of body, such activity of speech, and such accomplishment of mental action, knowing in the way that those blessed buddhas know — a knowing free from attachment, a knowing without obscuration, knowing thoroughly and completely — and just as they thus make earnest supplication to those blessed buddhas who are oriented toward passing into nirvāṇa, imploring them to fully sustain their lives until the very uttermost end of the final kalpa, so too do I make this same supplication.

And just as I make this supplication for my own sake, I make it equally for the sake of all the classes of sentient beings — however many there are in the entire unexhausted expanse of world-systems throughout all directions, every last one of those realms of beings.

By this root of virtue generated through making such earnest supplication to the Tathāgatas, may I attain the power of roots of virtue. May I be made firm by the power of aspiration and superior aspiration. Having come to rest deeply in the power of faith and devotion, may I rightly produce the vast vessel of superior aspiration, and take up perfectly and completely every last root of virtue throughout the immeasurable expanse.

In every assembly of bodhisattvas, all bodhisattvas of the past, the future, and the present — those who strive toward omniscience without obstruction, who mindfully recall the pure maṇḍala of the dharmadhātu, who are endowed with the mind that fully protects all beings, who strive earnestly to gather the great accumulation of roots of virtue, who strive with the striving of great bodhisattvas and mahāsattvas, who have donned the great armor, who accomplish the great aspiration, who diligently engage in the vast undertaking of virtue, who are fully engaged in the maṇḍala of the great perfections, who seek to purify every ground of qualities, who seek to abide on the ground without extremes or middle, who seek to be perfected by all beings, who seek to purify the entanglements of mind, who seek to thoroughly open the doorways of Dharma, who seek to extend the great momentum of the roots of virtue — all these bodhisattvas, in the following way:

— who seek to lead all sentient beings to the ultimate culmination of the purified ten powers, who seek to make offerings and render service to all buddhas, who seek to generate omniscience — every root of virtue that has been generated, that will be generated, and that has been generated for the sake of that service, together with all those who have directed their roots of virtue solely toward every bodhisattva; and again, those roots of virtue born in the very instant of leaping from ground to ground of the bodhisattva path; and those roots of virtue arising from the completion of the aspiration prayer of bodhisattva Samantabhadra's conduct; and those roots of virtue of those who have entered, endowed with the miraculous power of the bodhisattva grounds, who have attained the subsequent realization of seeing the meaning of all phenomena, who hold the essential heart of the ground of non-attached wisdom, hard as a vajra, and who have generated an indestructible awakening mind — I gather the entire accumulation of all these roots of virtue from every door. Those roots of virtue are generated by what is called "wisdom that transcends karma from every side," in which all Tathāgatas directly manifest. Entering in each and every instant of mind into offering and rendering service to the Tathāgatas, through that very entering, and through the harmonious cause of roots of virtue, through the boundless conditions of omniscience, covering in each and every instant of mind all the realms of sentient beings and engaging in immeasurable expressions of the mode of skillful means — through all of this, perfect equality with all bodhisattvas is also fully accomplished. All those bodhisattvas transmit all those streams of the power of their roots of virtue without interruption — and to that very extent, I, having authentically generated within the vessel of superior intention an utterly pure path of devotion, do authentically press upon all those streams of the power of roots of virtue, do authentically grasp them, do claim them as my own, do take them up, do aspire to them with great devotion, do enter into accordance with them all — and I claim them as my own with what is supreme in the claiming, the foremost, the noble, the specially exalted, the excellent, the outstanding, the wondrous, the highest, the unsurpassed, the unequalled, the equal to the unequalled, the matchless, the peerless, the great in self-nature, the unvanquished, the gathering of all qualities and their Dharma — I claim them as my own as proclaimed by the perfectly and completely awakened buddhas.

And beyond that as well: by those bodhisattvas who seek to authentically gather the accumulation of merit, who seek to accomplish the accumulation of qualities, who seek to enter the domain without obscuration, who seek to abide at the extreme of non-attachment, who seek to remain in all three times — just as all the Blessed One buddhas know and thoroughly know the claiming of all roots of virtue as their own, so in the same way do I also claim them as my own. I claim them as my own with what is supreme in the claiming, the foremost, the noble, the specially exalted, the incomparable, the peerless, the unequalled, the without likeness, the purely without resemblance — and I claim them as my own by all of those endless thoughts and pathways of language and shown praises.

Just as I claim them as my own for my own benefit, so too do I claim them as my own on behalf of all sentient beings of every class who dwell in all the world-realms without exception, as many as there are sentient beings in every rank of sentient beings, on behalf of all those realms of sentient beings.

Whatever roots of virtue there are that are accompanied by my aspiration, and whatever roots of virtue there are that are accompanied by prostration to all buddhas, and whatever roots of virtue there are that are accompanied by supremely making offerings to all Tathāgatas —

whatever roots of virtue there are that are accompanied by offerings arising with certainty from the power of devotion, and whatever roots of virtue there are that are accompanied by the removal of all that would become an obscuration, and whatever roots of virtue there are that are accompanied by sympathetic rejoicing, and whatever roots of virtue there are that are accompanied by exhortation to all buddhas, and whatever roots of virtue there are that are accompanied by earnest entreaty to all Tathāgatas —

all of these roots of virtue of mine, immeasurable and boundless, having been supremely offered to all buddhas and all bodhisattvas, whatever roots of virtue there are that are accompanied by claiming them as my own — and whatever roots of virtue I have performed throughout all lifetimes since the very beginning without beginning, which have not yet been fully dedicated to omniscience — and whatever roots of virtue of mine have been accomplished by means of conditioned provisions striven after and secured — and whatever roots of virtue have been accomplished by śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas — and whatever roots of virtue of mine accomplished through action, accumulated in every time, known by the wisdom of the Tathāgatas — all of these I fully gather together. I gather them. I draw them fully together.

And just as the bodhisattvas who above make offerings to all buddhas and who wish to uphold properly the teachings of all Tathāgatas gather together all roots of virtue in the manner in which the buddha Blessed Ones know and fully know — in just that same way, I too fully gather them together.

Having gathered them, it is as follows: for the sake of performing the act of offering to the past Blessed Ones and causing their qualities to become widely renowned; for the sake of completely fulfilling the aspirations of the bodhisattva Samantabhadra of the future Blessed Ones who are yet to come; for the sake of swiftly gaining mastery of the ten powers and the aspects of awakening — I give all of this to every sentient being. I give it. I give it fully. I give it entirely. I give it definitively. To all śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas I offer it. I give it. I give it fully. I give it entirely. I give it definitively. To all bodhisattvas I offer it. I give it. I give it fully. I give it entirely. I give it definitively. To all buddhas I offer it. I give it. I give it fully. I give it entirely. I give it definitively.

For the sake of performing the act of offering to each and every Tathāgata, may all the offering arrangements adorned throughout the entire dharmadhātu, spread through the assemblies of all the presently arisen Blessed Ones, who are omniscient and who have become the very limbs of awakening, within those maṇḍalas of their retinues as vast as the extent of the dharmadhātu — may they abide everywhere throughout the dharmadhātu, adorned with all arranged offerings. May the dharmadhātu be filled throughout with adornments of coverings of every precious jewel. May the dharmadhātu be filled throughout with adornments of canopies strung above with every kind of flower. May the dharmadhātu be filled throughout with adornments of victory banners of every precious jewel raised aloft. May the dharmadhātu be filled throughout with adornments of nets strung of every precious jewel. May the dharmadhātu be filled throughout with adornments of garments woven of every precious jewel. May the dharmadhātu be filled throughout with adornments of arrangements of offerings to the buddhas in showers of clouds of immeasurable garments and of every precious jewel. May the dharmadhātu be filled throughout with adornments of the burning of every heartwood incense. May the dharmadhātu be filled throughout with adornments of the space resounding with music liberated from attachment to every object of adornment.

May the dharmadhātu be filled throughout with adornments of lotuses of every precious jewel spread in array. May the dharmadhātu be filled throughout with adornments of clouds that proclaim all things in their entirety. May the dharmadhātu be filled throughout with adornments of clouds of every precious jewel that open wide into brilliant display. May the dharmadhātu be filled throughout with adornments of clouds of various crowns of precious jewels, utterly transcending everything of the gods.

Just as the bodhisattvas Samantabhadra enter into the maṇḍalas of the retinues of the Tathāgatas, possessed of all offerings, so by this root of virtue may I too be empowered to enter in just that way into the maṇḍalas of the retinues of all the Tathāgatas, and to make offerings immeasurable beyond measure, reaching to the final limit of the furthest end.

Furthermore, just as the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, know and fully know, and just as the bodhisattvas dedicate, present, and offer all roots of virtue to all sentient beings, to all śrāvakas, to all pratyekabuddhas, to all bodhisattvas, and to all Tathāgatas, so too do I present, fully give, direct, and offer all roots of virtue to all sentient beings, to all śrāvakas, to all pratyekabuddhas, to all bodhisattvas, and to all Tathāgatas. Having offered all roots of virtue to all sentient beings, all śrāvakas, all pratyekabuddhas, all bodhisattvas, and all the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, and having gathered together a mass of merit — a mass of merit immeasurable and inconceivable, known by the wisdom of the Tathāgatas — by that may I be firmly established, and may I arouse the mind of unsurpassed, perfect, complete awakening.

Just as the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Dīpaṃkara, having made supreme offering to the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Candraprabha, first aroused the mind of awakening, so too may I arouse the mind of awakening.

Just as the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Śākyamuni, in a former life as the son of a merchant named Abhiprīta, relying upon the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Mahāśrī, first aroused the mind of awakening, so too may I arouse the mind of awakening.

Just as the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Amitāyus, when he had become a monk who was a source of the Dharma, praised the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Lokeśvararāja with the melody of verse and thereby aroused the mind of awakening, so too may I arouse the mind of awakening.

Just as the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Akṣobhya, with an unobstructed mind, an undisturbed mind, and with the aspiration to go forth and to truly renounce in all lifetimes, let out the lion's roar, and from the Tathāgata Sunetra first aroused the mind of awakening, so too may I arouse the mind of awakening.

Just as the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Śrībhadra, having generated many forms of roots of virtue in relation to the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Sāgaraśrī, aroused the mind of awakening, so too may I arouse the mind of awakening.

Just as the bodhisattva Maitreya, the King of Golden Hue, made abundant offerings and rendered homage to the Blessed One, the Tathāgata of the Radiance of the Golden Offering-Pillar, and thereby aroused the mind of awakening toward full enlightenment, so too may I arouse the mind of awakening.

Just as the bodhisattva Ocean of Intelligence, when he had become a wheel-turning king over an utterly pure realm, paid reverence to the Tathāgata of Infinite Rays of Virtue's Light, and thereby aroused the mind of awakening toward full enlightenment, so too may I arouse the mind of awakening.

Just as the bodhisattva Ākāśagarbha, having been consecrated by empowerment and become the wheel-turning king of a thousandfold universe, made an offering to the Tathāgata who had perfectly emerged from all aspirations — the King of Manifest Pre-eminent Majesty of Jewels — piling up a mass of precious gems equal in size to Mount Meru as a single canopy, and performed worship and homage for forty intermediate aeons, and thereby aroused the mind of awakening toward full enlightenment, so too may I arouse the mind of awakening.

Just as Mañjuśrī, the Ever-youthful, when he had become a wheel-turning king, made offerings and many forms of homage to the Blessed One, the Tathāgata King of Proclaimed Melodious Sound, and thereby aroused the mind of awakening toward full enlightenment, so too may I arouse the mind of awakening.

Just as the bodhisattva Samantabhadra paid reverence to the Blessed One, the Tathāgata King of the Ocean of Wisdom-Vajra, and thereby aroused the mind of awakening toward full enlightenment, so too may I arouse the mind of awakening.

There is no dharmadhātu, no basic space of phenomena, that is not pervaded by the supreme and excellent mind — a mind in which pride is stilled, a mind that is without pretension, a mind that fully protects all beings, and the awakening mind itself.

There is not even the slightest portion of the three times that is not pervaded by the accumulation of roots of virtue arising from this arousal of the mind of awakening.

There is no sentient being who is not utterly embraced by the mind that delights all buddhas, the mind that brings supreme joy to all the maṇḍalas of bodhisattvas, the mind that rejoices in all śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas, and this mind of arousal that takes complete hold of beings.

There is no Tathāgata dwelling anywhere who has not been made an object of offering.

The mass of merit produced the very instant this mind of awakening is aroused, as described above, is so vast that the entire mass of merit accumulated by all wheel-turning kings, all world-protectors, all Śakras (Indra), all Brahmās, all śrāvakas, and all pratyekabuddhas does not equal it.

Whatever mass of merit I possess that is endowed with the arousal of the mind of awakening toward full enlightenment; whatever has been given to all sentient beings; whatever has been offered to all śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas; whatever has been presented to all bodhisattvas; whatever mass of merit has been gathered by offering all roots of virtue to all buddhas; whatever roots of virtue are endowed with the devotion of those who have achieved gnosis and devotion; whatever roots of virtue are endowed with prostration to all buddhas; whatever roots of virtue are endowed with supreme offering to all Tathāgatas; whatever roots of virtue are endowed with worship arising from the power of devotion; whatever roots of virtue there are that clear away all that would cause obscuration —

whatever roots of virtue are endowed with sympathetic joy; whatever roots of virtue are endowed with urging all buddhas; whatever roots of virtue are endowed with supplication to all Tathāgatas; whatever roots of virtue of mine that are endowed with the act of calling upon the immeasurable buddhas and all bodhisattvas and making them my own; whatever roots of virtue of mine performed across beginningless lifetimes that have not been fully dedicated to omniscience; whatever roots of virtue of mine that have been accomplished through seeking the provisions of conditioned existence; whatever roots of virtue of mine that have been accomplished for the sake of the grounds of all śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas; whatever roots of virtue of mine accomplished through action, accumulated since former times, and known by the gnosis of the Tathāgatas — all of these I gather together completely. Having gathered them all, I dedicate them fully to unsurpassed, perfect, and complete awakening.

May these roots of virtue of mine bring pleasure and delight to all buddhas. May they fully purify all buddha-fields. May they fully ripen all sentient beings. May they bring satisfaction and joy to all spiritual friends and all Dharma teachers. May they properly uphold, guard, and fully protect the teaching of all Tathāgatas. May they completely purify every taint of obscuration. May they utterly tame all the contentious armies of Māra. May they accomplish complete, manifest awakening in all the qualities of a buddha. May they be endowed with the attainment of all meditative absorptions and all empowerments, adorned with all the supernatural knowledges, and transformed through all liberations.

However it may be, may these roots of virtue of mine be dedicated so that in saṃsāra there is no separation, that in other lives there is no falling into destitution, that the practice of the bodhisattva perfections is not abandoned, and that through association with childish ordinary persons there is no conformity with what tends toward obscuration.

However it may be, may these roots of virtue be dedicated so that those bodhisattvas go from this buddha-field to other world-realms where buddhas are present in person, to adorn those buddhas with the ornament of offerings arranged for them; and may these roots of virtue be dedicated to fully completing, up to the final mind of awakening attained, the ornament of offerings arranged for the buddhas.

At the time of death, may those buddhas, the Blessed Ones, extend their right hands of golden hue and place them upon the crown of my head. May I, at the time of death, be one whose mind — suffused with the Perfection of Wisdom, whose uninterrupted sequence of non-viewing of all phenomena is arrayed, and whose mind cuts the continuity of all feeling — passes away in that state.

With faith beyond counting, with reverential homage beyond counting, with joy and faith beyond counting, may I behold those buddhas, the Blessed Ones, contemplate them, speak of them on all occasions, and speak truly of them, and thus make my death. With pleasing offerings may I make offering to those Tathāgatas, and with flowers, incense, garlands, perfumes, powders, garments, parasols, victory banners, and pennants beyond number, may I make offering to those Tathāgatas, beholding and contemplating them, and thus make my death.

By this root of virtue, may all hells in every world-system be cut off without remainder. May all states of animal birth and all the worlds of the Lord of Death be cut off without remainder. May all poverty among gods, all poverty among humans, all miserliness, all corrupt moral discipline, all malicious intent, all laziness, all distraction, and all corrupt wisdom be cut off without remainder. May there be no separation from generating the awakening mind, from beholding buddhas, from hearing the sacred Dharma, and from keeping the company of spiritual friends.

By this root of virtue, may I become equal in body to all the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, equal in speech, equal in mind, and equal in gnosis. Just as the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, have known and fully known through a buddha's knowledge that is free from attachment and free from obscuration, so too may all bodhisattvas dedicate every root of virtue to unsurpassed, perfect, complete awakening, and in just that same way I too dedicate all roots of virtue, by the supreme act of dedication, to unsurpassed, perfect, complete awakening.

With a dedication that is foremost, holy, supremely exalted, excellent, sublime, agreeable, unsurpassed, without superior, without equal, unequal yet equal, without parallel, without semblance, of great majesty, highest, unassailable, encompassing all qualities and attributes — with the dedication proclaimed by the perfectly, completely awakened buddhas — I make this dedication.

Just as I have dedicated for my own sake, so too do I dedicate for the sake of all sentient beings in every class of beings there may be throughout all the immeasurable world-systems without exception, in every single one of them.

Those who wish to take up this root of virtue by practising their share of it may also say: "I rejoice in all of this."

Thereupon the bodhisattva, the great being, who abides pledged to all phenomena, spoke these words to Mañjuśrī, the ever-youthful: "Mañjuśrī, how is a bodhisattva's engagement blameless? How is rejoicing blameless, rousing blameless, requesting blameless, and dedication blameless?"

When he had spoken, Mañjuśrī, the ever-youthful, said this to the bodhisattva, the great being, who abides pledged to all phenomena: "Noble son, here the bodhisattvas, the great beings, enter into the equality of the Dharma of all the buddhas, the Blessed Ones — past, future, and present — and having done so they touch the ground with their five limbs. Then, setting the right knee upon the ground, they speak these words: 'May all sentient beings be established on the path of the right direction.' Setting the left knee upon the ground, they speak these words: 'May all those sentient beings who are established on the path of the left direction be established on the noble path and on the correct path.' Setting the right hand upon the ground, they speak these words: 'Just as the Tathāgata, seated at the supreme and sacred seat of awakening, endowed with all virtuous qualities, anointed upon his head, his body, and his cross-legged posture, pressing his right hand upon the ground, subdued Māra together with his armies —

— may these sentient beings likewise vanquish the assaults of Māra, and, seated at the heart of awakening, pressing down upon the great earth, may they attain the gnosis of a buddha.' Then, placing the left hand upon the ground, one speaks these words: 'May all those sentient beings who dwell in the ways of the childish, who hold to the left direction and are difficult to tame, be thoroughly gathered in by the four means of attraction.' Then, touching the face and forehead to the ground, one speaks these words: 'With this act of prostration free from arrogance — this act of paying homage to one's teachers that goes unseen, unrecognized as something worthy to be bowed to at the crown of the head — may all the virtuous qualities of every sentient being be exalted. And may this my prostration with five limbs dispel the five obscurations of all sentient beings; may the five faculties be perfectly complete; may the five qualities of sensory enjoyment be fully known; may the five undiminishing superknowledges be attained; may the five eyes be utterly purified; may those sentient beings born in the five destinies be exalted far above those five destinies; and may they be exalted in superior moral discipline, superior meditative absorption, superior wisdom, superior liberation, and superior knowledge of the vision of liberation.'

Then, with the five limbs perfectly purified, free from the five obscurations, established in the five powers, and inseparable from the recollection of the Tathāgata, one speaks these words:

'O buddhas, Blessed Ones (bhagavāt), possessed of the dharmakāya, without a body to be imputed, with pure speech, without speech to be imputed, with a clear mind, without a mind to be imputed, possessed of gnosis free from attachment, possessed of omniscience that has neither come nor gone, possessed of the vision of all things — look upon me with your grace.

From the very beginningless edge of saṃsāra, not having sought the ultimate boundary or my own boundary, I have conceived of what is not Dharma as Dharma, of what is not Vinaya as Vinaya, of what is not a worthy field for giving as a worthy field, of what is unwholesome as wholesome; I have been utterly bound by the perverted view of regarding my own body as a self; and whatever virtuous qualities the Buddha did not teach I have fabricated, performed, caused others to perform, and rejoiced in when performed — all of that I confess. Abiding in a focal object, and conceiving of sentient beings where there are no sentient beings, and not having recognized mind as dreamlike, I generated the awakening mind directed toward unsurpassed, perfectly complete awakening — all of that I confess. Not having recognized that there is no fabrication on my part, I have with body, speech, and mind fabricated and already fabricated both virtuous and non-virtuous qualities in the Dharma — all of that I confess. Not having recognized generosity, moral discipline, patience, diligence, meditative concentration, and wisdom as equal in nature, I have fallen into reference-points and given gifts with giving; guarded moral discipline by holding moral discipline as supreme; cultivated patience with the perception of self and other; undertaken diligence while abiding in body and mind; generated meditative concentration while abiding in perception and a focal object; and examined wisdom while taking delight in engaging in discursive thought and conceptual thinking — all of that I confess. Not having sought ultimate offering, I have made offerings to the buddha with attachment to form, ornament, and marks — all of that I confess. Not having entered the gnosis in which the dharmadhātu is undifferentiated, I have abided in differentiated phenomena, and —

— not having listened to, expounded, or thoroughly reflected on the Dharma — all of that I confess. Not having sought the Saṅgha as unconditioned, I have perceived it as a multitude and paid homage to the Saṅgha in that way — all of that I confess. Not having sought out true solitude, I have dwelt in a place of solitude and clung to having few wants and knowing contentment — all of that I confess. Not having sought to abide in the nature of reality, I have abided in arising and ceasing, and have entered upon the task of acquiring the qualities of a buddha — all of that I confess. Not having sought the state where the dharmadhātu is entirely without apprehension and having not entered that state, I have gathered sentient beings by means of a gathering that desires to bring them together — all of that I confess. Not having sought the intrinsic nature that is the defining mark of awakening, I have, through clinging to views, cultivated the thirty-seven factors conducive to awakening — all of that I confess. Not having sought the peaceful path of the contemplative life, I took going forth and full ordination and produced the substance of monkhood — all of that I confess. Whatever other roots of virtue accumulated through engagement in conventional reality, and whatever roots of virtue caused one to become happy through the conditioned, thereby becoming very far from the unconditioned — all of that too I confess.

One should declare: all of these things I confess.

Noble son, in this way, at the outset the bodhisattva should confess all appropriating phenomena that are accompanied by latent tendencies, accompanied by deliberate activity, accompanied by signs, accompanied by effort, accompanied by conceptual elaboration, accompanied by grasping, accompanied by abiding, and accompanied by reliance. As for whatever gathering and conceptual elaboration of such kinds does not arise throughout — that is the thorough purification of the confession of misdeeds. This is the aggregate of confession.

Noble son, furthermore that same bodhisattva should remain in just this way, and with these very words, should rejoice in whatever roots of virtue there are — from the moment of first generating the intention, through to their becoming fully and perfectly awakened in unsurpassed, complete, and perfect awakening — of all the blessed buddha Tathāgatas of the past, the future, and the present, and of all those who are between, that is, those who are in the interval between these.

The Buddha declared: roots of virtue without a root and without a foundation; generosity that is completely pure through being without a giver; moral discipline of intrinsic nature that is inexhaustible and unborn; patience in which there is no sentient being; diligence that is free from a referential object; meditative concentration that resolves on the essential nature of mind itself; wisdom that engages in profound dependent origination while abandoning the views of extremes on the near side and the far side; loving-kindness with the aspect of emptiness; compassion without any aspect of aspects; joy that does not arise toward phenomena; and equanimity that is not abandoned as something expendable — in all of these I rejoice.

Mindfulness fully present in mindfulness of grasping nothing whatsoever of all household things, of what is entirely ungrasped, of roots of virtue that are without a root and without a foundation, remembered in not being remembered; right abandonments that are unobstructed and unborn; bases of supernatural power endowed with body, mind, diligence, and aspiration; the five faculties as faculties without a defining characteristic; powers that, like space, cannot be overcome; factors of awakening that are the true realization of ultimate reality; the path endowed with a path of non-duality; calm abiding attained in peace; special insight that accords with knowledge and liberation; and the gnosis of direct knowledge becoming manifest — in all of these I rejoice.

From the very beginning, the thorough purity of the essential nature of body; speech completely pure in what cannot be expressed;

In all of these I rejoice: the absence of service to the buddhas without having paid homage to them; the absence of service to the Dharma without having fully embraced all phenomena; the absence of service to the Saṅgha without assembly or qualities; conduct of vows in which no form of behavior has been examined; and the Dharma practice, the pure vow, the indestructibility by all māras, the non-commingling with the entire world, the non-fixation on the ground of all śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas, the accord with the bodhisattva path, the excellent root of virtue, and whatever else exists that causes the absence of wholesome white karma to cease — of all of these, together with the luminous wholesome karma by which those bodhisattvas of past, present, and future have obtained, are obtaining, and will obtain the wisdom of buddhahood, I rejoice in all of it.

I rejoice in all of the following that belong to the Blessed One buddhas of past, present, and future who have attained awakening: their undefiled moral discipline, meditative absorption, wisdom, liberation, and the gnosis of the vision of liberation; their unconquerable powers; their irreversible fearlessness; their buddha qualities free from attachment; their great loving-kindness that does not arise from partiality; their great compassion that does not lean upon another; their usnīsa unseen from above, equal to space; the defining characteristic of the ripening of non-dual merit; and the utterly pure stream of body, speech, and mind, unadulterated like space. I rejoice in all of these, and also in the turning of the wheel of Dharma requested by all the Śakras (Indras) and Brahmās; in the teaching of the Dharma endowed with the inexpressible; and in all of the fact that the illuminating awareness appearing to all sentient beings is nothing other than consciousness.

For the benefit of all sentient beings, abiding in the domain of skillful means — whether the Blessed One buddhas of past, present, and future are residing or have fully passed into nirvāṇa — whatever offerings were made, are being made, and will be made to them until the decline of the holy Dharma, in all of these I rejoice.

I rejoice in all the following, both those that are supported by no root of virtue and those that have utterly transcended conditioned existence: the assembled śrāvakas of the Blessed One buddhas who have passed into complete nirvāṇa — those who have properly passed beyond all sentient beings; those who follow along through faith; those who have entered the dharmadhātu; those who follow the Dharma; those who are the eighth ones, having properly passed beyond the eight wrong states; those who belong to the family of non-attachment, abiding in that family; those who have entered the stream, having turned back from the stream and entered; those who are once-returners, who will not go anywhere; those who are non-returners, who, dying from here, will abide in that state; the innumerable arhats, thoroughly distinguished by birthlessness; the pratyekabuddhas who have penetrated to the depths of dependent origination; the bodhisattvas who have realized wisdom directly; the bodhisattvas who have first generated a mind like the earth; the bodhisattvas who have entered into conduct that has properly passed beyond all bodhisattva conduct; the bodhisattvas who have transcended the phenomena of saṃsāra and attained the buddha qualities, being irreversible; the bodhisattvas who have utterly transcended all births; and the bodhisattvas who are bound by one more birth — the roots of virtue of all these, and all that has utterly transcended the conditioned, in all of these I rejoice.

In the past, the future, and

the present — in all the merit of generosity, all the merit of ethical conduct, and all the merit of meditation transformed into a state where there is no object whatsoever, belonging to all sentient beings of the present time, as seen by the eye of a buddha with perfect knowledge — in all of these I rejoice. I rejoice with the supreme rejoicing, uninterrupted and without remainder, pure as the sky, realized through wisdom and primordial wisdom, possessed moreover of the Dharma of definitive meaning, unsurpassed, equal to the sky, equal to nirvāṇa, and with the rejoicing that is the sameness of the unequalled equal — thus I rejoice.

Just as the Blessed One buddhas of the past, the future, and the present, while still bodhisattvas, having united the Perfection of Wisdom with skillful means, acted without attachment, engaged in supreme conduct, and through perfect knowledge came to know — making the primordial wisdom of the buddhas directly manifest — rejoiced in those roots of virtue, caused others to rejoice in them, and will rejoice in them; and just as they trained in that sameness and that nature of reality by which rejoicing is accomplished — so likewise do I rejoice. I rejoice with the supreme rejoicing, uninterrupted and without remainder, pure as the sky, realized through wisdom and primordial wisdom, possessed moreover of the Dharma of definitive meaning, unsurpassed, and with the rejoicing that is equal to the sky — thus it should be said.

Noble child, this is the blameless mass of rejoicing as declared by the Blessed One buddhas.

Noble child, that bodhisattva should then remain in this way and speak these words: "Those Blessed One buddhas who, in world-realms beyond measure and beyond counting, have fully, perfectly, and completely awakened to the unsurpassed and perfect awakening — having realized all phenomena without grasping, having truly passed beyond the path of the four māras, having found the inexpressible Dharma that is without fault, free from all syllables, words, and sounds of every kind, having found the Dharma exactly as it is, having reflected to just the degree needed in order to hold the Dharma through skillful means, having not wasted great compassion in order to bring sentient beings to full maturation, delighting in being entreated, delighting in silence, gazing upon the tree of awakening, praised openly by all gods, nāgas, yakṣas, gandharvas, asuras, garuḍas, kinnaras, and great serpents, dwelling in joy — those Blessed One buddhas I entreat to turn the wheel of Dharma excellently. I request that the Blessed One buddhas turn the wheel of Dharma: the wheel of Dharma that does not enter into any dharma; the wheel that is not fully established; the wheel that severs both the near shore and the far shore; the wheel of the profound dependent origination; the wheel that crushes māra and the opposing forces. I request the striking of the drum that is inexpressible. I request the blowing of the conch of silence. I request the raising of the victory banner and standard of knowing the Dharma. I request the kindling of the lamp of wisdom, primordial wisdom, and liberation. I request the gathering of the clouds of great compassion.
