The Dhāraṇī of the Hundred Thousand Ornaments of the Essence of Awakening (Bodhigarbhālaṅkāralakṣadhāraṇī)

The Dhāraṇī of the Hundred Thousand Ornaments of the Essence of Awakening, known in Sanskrit as the Bodhigarbhālaṅkāralakṣadhāraṇī and in Tibetan as Byang chub kyi snying po'i rgyan 'bum gyi gzungs, is a short incantatory text consisting almost entirely of a single dhāraṇī, a sacred formula meant to be recited. After an opening homage to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas, the formula pays reverence to a tathāgata whose name evokes a radiant golden banner at the crown, and to the Buddha Śākyamuni, before invoking the power of awakening and the mind of great awakening. It calls on the blessing and empowerment of all the tathāgatas to pacify and purify every kind of wrongdoing and to establish the reciter firmly on the path to great awakening. Formulas of this kind are traditionally associated with devotional practices such as the consecration of stūpas and sacred images. The work is Toh 920 in the Kangyur, where it belongs to the class of dhāraṇī texts.

If any monk, nun, layman or laywoman, or any other son or daughter of good family, writes out this dhāraṇī, builds a stupa, and places this dhāraṇī inside that stupa, then by building a single stupa that person will have built a hundred thousand. They will also have made offerings with divine flowers, incense, garlands, ointments, powders, garments, parasols, victory banners, pennants and the like. And it is not only the stupa that is honored: with those same offerings, the Precious Buddha, the Precious Dharma and the Precious Saṅgha will be honored as well.

Nanda, I have taught this sūtra, this dhāraṇī, for the sake of such beings as these: those of weak merit, those without faith, those of wrong view, those who doubt, those who commit the deeds of immediate retribution, those whose lives are short, the beings of the hells, animals, those in the world of Yama, and beings bound for the hell of uninterrupted torment.

Nanda, in the future, in the later age, those householders, and most monks as well, who do not read this dhāraṇī and do not make offerings to it, will suffer: just as householders are said to suffer, so too will those who have gone forth.

If, on the other hand, one who in this way honors the Buddha and listens to this builds a single stupa, the eighty-four thousand adorned with the Dharma will also be honored. Moreover, that person will have an immeasurable heap of merit. It will be as though they had read and recited the sūtra Adorned with Trees. They will also have entered the worldly and the transcendent maṇḍalas.

The Dhāraṇī of the Hundred Thousand, extracted from the Hundred Thousand Ornaments of the Essence of Awakening, is complete.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The work called "Adorned with Trees" may be the Gaṇḍavyūha, but the title as written differs from that sūtra's usual Tibetan title, so it has been rendered literally. "The eighty-four thousand adorned with the Dharma" most likely refers to the eighty-four thousand stupas, or the eighty-four thousand sections of the Dharma, but the Tibetan does not specify.
