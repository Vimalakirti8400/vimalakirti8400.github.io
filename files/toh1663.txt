Commentary on the Difficult Points of the Union with All Buddhas (Sarvabuddhasamāyogapañjikā)

The Commentary on the Difficult Points of the Union with All Buddhas, known in Sanskrit as the Sarvabuddhasamāyoga Pañjikā and in Tibetan as Sangyé Tamché Dang Nyampar Jorwé Kadrel, is a commentary on the Sarvabuddhasamāyoga Tantra, an early and influential yoginī tantra centered on Vajrasattva and on the union of all buddhas with the bliss of the ḍākinīs. Opening with homage to the Blessed One Vajratīkṣṇa and a verse of praise to the union of all buddhas as supreme bliss, it promises to make clear what is hard to understand even for a keen mind, and then works through the root tantra chapter by chapter, explaining its words, its deities, its mudrās, its ritual gestures and its pledges. According to its colophon it was composed by the Indian master Praśāntamitra and translated into Tibetan by the Indian preceptors Śraddhākaravarman and Dharmaśrībhadra together with the great translator Rinchen Zangpo. It is Toh 1663 in the Tengyur, where it belongs to the section of tantra commentaries.

In the language of India: sarba buddha samāñja pañcikā
In the language of Tibet: A Commentary on the Difficult Points of the Union with All Buddhas.

Homage to the supreme lord of yogins, the Blessed One Vajratīkṣṇa!

Union with all buddhas,
Ḍākinī, illusion, supreme bliss,
Who brings forth the bliss of all sentient beings,
Self of all things: to you I bow.

What is hard to understand even for an unclouded mind,
That which is called the supreme of tantras,
Being properly requested by others,
I shall make clear, just as it is.

Here some raise an objection: "Why in this tantra did the compiler not set forth at the beginning the setting that starts 'Thus did I hear'?" Some explain it as follows. When the Blessed One was about to pass into complete nirvāṇa, he said, "Sons of good family, after I have passed into complete nirvāṇa you should compile the true Dharma; at the very beginning of all, put 'Thus did I hear at one time.'" It was omitted here in order to show clearly that the Blessed One is in the nirvāṇa that does not abide. That is why this is made evident by the very phrase "always abides." Others hold this: the great universal monarch, appearing in person to teach, spoke a tantra of twelve thousand three hundred ślokas, and this one of one thousand two hundred is the later tantra. Therefore, since the setting had already been made known where that tantra begins, it is not stated here again.

Now, a person who follows the Dharma will engage with a text when it has a subject matter, a connection, a purpose, and a purpose of the purpose, so these should be shown here. In this text the Blessed One Vajrasattva, the union with all buddhas, the supreme bliss of the ḍākinī's illusion, the self of all things, is the subject matter, and this tantra, the words that teach it, is the expression. Accordingly, the connection between them, as what is expressed and what expresses it, is the connection. Further, the Blessed One's bringing joy to the beings to be trained is the purpose. And the purpose of the purpose should be understood as this: the ability to reach completion through a sequence that lacks nothing of the practice and what it accomplishes, such as entering the maṇḍala, and the actualization of one's own essential nature.

Now, this tantra consists of ten sections. The first of them has as its nature the examination of the wisdom mudrā that enters the domain of the supreme illumination of reality. The compiler, the Blessed One Vajrapāṇi, spoke the passage beginning "The sattva" in order to begin with the teaching of the excellent place. In the passage beginning
"The sattva, the very being of all buddhas,"
he is a sattva because he is intensely devoted to the aims of sentient beings. Because he has become the essential nature of all deities, and because, as the very being of all buddhas, he is the essential nature of indivisible wisdom, he is Vajrasattva. Because he is, in the supreme sense, the essential nature of physical bliss and mental happiness, he is Supreme Bliss. He is secret because he has brought Supreme Bliss and the others together within himself, or because he is to be kept hidden from the śrāvakas and others. He is supreme because he is foremost of all. "In delight" is said because he is free of every fault, or because of the very state of delight in union with the vajra goddess. Because he has become the essential nature of all tathāgatas, or because he is equal to everything belonging to the realm of space, he is the self of all. This is to be connected with "the Blessed One Vajrasattva always abides in the celestial palace of glorious Vajrasattva."

To teach those four in order, he spoke the passage beginning "This." "This" is Vajrasattva. He is self-arisen because he arises from meditating on oneself as a perfect buddha and so on, and for that very reason he is the nature of all buddhas. He is the Blessed One because he possesses unsurpassed sovereignty, and for that very reason he is the sattva. He is alone because he has no second. For that very reason Vajrasattva is the great deity, that is, the principal one. Akṣobhya and the others are all the buddhas. Supreme Bliss and the others, having transformed into vajra goddesses, together with them, perfectly, or through complete union, subsequent union and thorough union, enter into absorption and meditate nondually, delight in one another, and unite in the character of the self of all things. Therefore it teaches the union with all buddhas. All the accomplishments of moving in the sky are the ḍāka. That itself, having the form of illusion, is illusion, and the essential nature of the unsurpassed physical bliss and mental happiness attained through it is the Blessed One. Hence it says,
"Union with all buddhas,
Ḍākinī, illusion, supreme bliss,"
and that itself is explained as supreme bliss.

If one thinks that by entering among the host of goddesses in this way one would become thoroughly afflicted, he spoke the passage beginning "Without attachment." "Without attachment" means free of attachment: the awakening of the śrāvakas and the others. "In the middle" means the nondual wisdom of the means of accomplishment. None of these is apprehended at all, because ultimately they are without intrinsic nature. To show the answer, that the goddesses bring no affliction, he spoke the passage beginning "Of all women." The illusion of all women is also that which is entered into, so this is the mudrā of the perfection of wisdom. To make that clear he spoke the passage beginning "This is nondual." The two are the apprehended and the apprehender; because these are absent, it is nondual. Because one goes and arrives by these two, it is a vehicle: the great bodhisattva together with the fruit, the ground of a buddha. It is supreme because it surpasses the vehicles of the śrāvakas and the others. The passage beginning "Is there or is there not," concerning Vairocana and the others together with their goddesses, should be understood as made meaningful by one's own perfection of wisdom. Or else, since the illusion mudrā of all women is the great mudrā of Supreme Bliss and the others, the assemblies of the families are made one.

The passage beginning "Nondual" shows its essential nature in ultimate terms: by this the Blessed One is taught to be, ultimately, of the essential nature of nondual wisdom. Conventionally, for the sake of those to be trained, he emanates as the illusion mudrā. These two aspects as well are the correct teaching of the union with all buddhas.

If one asks, "Since he teaches this, is there no accomplishing the welfare of sentient beings by other means?", for that reason the passage beginning "Illusions" is spoken. From the words "Of all illusions," it means that among all emanations the illusion of woman, the mudrā of Supreme Bliss and the others, is exceedingly distinguished. By it, those to be trained are given the higher fruits in stages, and it brings them to attain the state of Vajrasattva, whose essential nature is unsurpassed bliss and mental joy; thus it has become supremely exalted. Therefore that very one is set forth.

If one asks, "The Blessed One Vajrasattva is of the nature of nondual wisdom, free of all activity; by what cause is he distinguished in this way?", the passage beginning "By its essential nature" is spoken. Why is that? By its essential nature it is spontaneously accomplished and already accomplished. By what cause? "By the powers": that is, by the greatness of the accumulations of merit and wisdom, conceived and desired by oneself; that is, by way of relying on one's own aspiration.

Having thus shown the greatness of its cause, in order to show the greatness of its fruit, the passage beginning "The three worlds" is spoken. The three worlds are those characterized as below the earth, upon the earth, and above the earth. "By this woman" means a woman striving to accomplish Supreme Bliss and the others. "By this" means the complete Perfection of Wisdom, or the illusion mudrā. "Reveres" means makes one great in nature.

If one asks whether those who practice austerities will attain accomplishment or not, the passage beginning "Gain and" is spoken. If even while actively indulging in gain and the like one attains accomplishment, what need is there to speak of completely renouncing gain and the like? For even those of bad conduct, that is, those whose conduct of practice is incomplete, will attain accomplishment; this is to be construed as "because therefore one makes accomplishment manifest."

In order to show the meaning of ḍākinī, the passage beginning "Own nature" is spoken. "When one's own nature is transformed" means what is preceded by the transformation of one's own basis; Supreme Bliss and the others are fully perfected as accomplished in the state of a real entity. "The illusion of all women" comes about by meditating on the union of all women. "Accomplished" means perfected, and this is what is called "This wondrous illusion mudrā": the manifold wonders of the Blessed One. "The mudrā of Supreme Bliss and the others is called a ḍākinī" is the remainder of the phrase, to be supplied.

In what language? "Taught in code": that is, in the code language of all buddhas. In order to show the etymology itself, the passage beginning "Ḍā is going in the sky" is spoken: from the verbal root, the letter ḍā, she is called a ḍākinī. Therefore it says:
Because she is accomplished in moving throughout the sky,
She is renowned as a ḍākinī.
"Renowned" means comes to be well known. The mudrā everywhere without remainder is the mudrā of Vajrasattva, and that too is Supreme Bliss everywhere without remainder; because it goes throughout the sky, through union all are renowned as ḍākinīs. This also comes to teach the following: whatever great mudrā of the Blessed One Vajrasattva is accomplished in going everywhere is itself what is called "going throughout the sky."

"Buddha element" means that the element of the buddhas and the others is the buddha element. The meaning of its being called ḍākinī is shown, for Vairocana and the others together with their retinues as well, by this very word "element." Therefore, what is accomplished by them as going everywhere is to be construed as:
Of the nature of all buddhas,
She is renowned as a ḍākinī.

Here too it is explained in the oral instructions that if, with a mind steady in meditation, one first meditates on oneself exactly as the nature of one's own deity, being set in motion by a vajra garuḍa standing upon a wind maṇḍala arisen from the syllable interlaced with the heart mantra, one will go into the sky. "Explained" means renowned. "Of all buddhas" means attained from the accomplishment of Vajrasattva, and that very thing is also what is called great bliss. "Fully joined with all illusions" means: the sublime bliss that arises from fully entering into the emanation of the entire maṇḍala is called Supreme Bliss. Therefore, because he has become its very essential nature, the Blessed One is renowned as the Union with All Buddhas, the Ḍākinī Illusion, Supreme Bliss.

Now, in order to show the full application of all illusions without remainder, the passage beginning "Everywhere" is spoken: in every place and from every entity, and with all entities everywhere, that is, at all times, and in every aspect, that is, in all aspects, it becomes nothing but oneself; this is the remainder of the phrase. Why? The passage beginning "Thus, buddha" is spoken. In this way it teaches that the Blessed One is the essential nature of all entities, and it teaches the practice of meditation in meditative equipoise. Therefore, in order to explain in detail that very self of all entities, the passage beginning "Buddha, stable and moving" is spoken.

Because the mind is the basis of all thorough affliction, mind is the all-base consciousness. When that very mind, through steadily meditating on emptiness, has become isolated from apprehended and apprehender, the form of a moon disc is called "sattva." One-pointedly applying the mind to the observation of all phenomena as having the essential nature of non-entity is meditative absorption. Meditating on the moon disc as fully complete is generating the mind toward awakening, and since that very one acts for the sake of awakening, it is the conduct of awakening.

Upon that one generates the pride of meditating on the vajra, and then, from the entry of the Tathāgata, one meditates on oneself as the essential nature of one's own deity; what is explained from there up to the culmination of the offerings is the yoga of the deity. "Manifold" means having become different in aspect through application to those to be trained.

Others say: "The conduct of awakening acts for the welfare of sentient beings in the forms of Jayakara and Madhukara, and suchness is manifold because of its different aspects. For it is said:
Since through sattva, rajas and tamas as well
The welfare of sentient beings comes about,
Therefore I am Samantabhadra,
And by the conduct of awakening I go accordingly."

In order to show that very variety of aspects, the passage beginning "Likewise Māra and" is spoken. Because he harms sentient beings who enter wrong paths by slaying their minds, Māra is Vajrasattva. Because that very one has become victorious over the four māras, he is the Victor over Māra. The discerning realization of one's own mind is awakening, and that very one is the Buddha, Vairocana. The five-pronged wrathful vajra having the nature of the five wisdoms is the vajra; because he holds the vajra, he is Vajradhara himself. The essential nature of the nature of reality, pure by its intrinsic nature, having the nature of discriminating wisdom, is the lotus; because he holds the lotus, he is Padmadhara, that is, Padmanarteśvara. The eight-faceted precious gem whose essential nature is the wisdom of equality is the jewel; because he holds it, he is Vajrasūrya. Paramāśva is so explained because he abides in the essential nature of all fierce modes.

"Of them also" means that the families shown as Vajrasattva and the others, and as Māra and the others, are the retinue. Because he has become of the nature of compassion, he is attachment. Because he has become of the nature of the liberation of the śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas, and is thus free of attachment, he is free from attachment. Because he delights in all accomplishments, he is desire. Because all accomplishments possess unchanging bliss, he is liberation. For it is asked, "Blessed One, what are desire and liberation like?", and it is said:
The Blessed One, the great passion,
Delights in all accomplishments;
All accomplishments, unchanging,
Should be known as great bliss.

Because he has become the essential nature of the past and the others, he is the three times. Because he is the essential nature of the existences of desire, form and the formless, he is the three existences. Because he has the nature of the three vehicles, he is the three supreme ones. Because he is the essential nature of the three brothers, Jayakara and the others, he is the three worlds. Because he possesses the realms of desire and the others, he is the three realms. Because of different aspects, he is of manifold aspects. Mountains and the like are the stationary; humans and the like are the moving. Because he pervades everywhere, he is in all things. "Endless and supreme" means having neither beginning nor end. "Equal to the dharmadhātu" means alike with emptiness itself. "Like the element of space" means having no limit. The mention of the two terms, the dharmadhātu and the element of space, should be seen as what is renowned in the supramundane and in the mundane respectively. "All entities" are all phenomena; by these it shows that he has become self-arisen.

"Glorious one with room in all of space" means: one in whom there is a glory that has room throughout all of space is so called; it is to be construed as "one in whom there is the glory possessing the room of all space." By this it shows the one called the Blessed One. By "Vajrasattva, the Tathāgata" it shows the one called the sole, exceedingly great deity.

Now, in order to show the mudrā whose essential nature is the application of the means of attainment, the passage beginning "Ten million eons" is spoken. "All buddhas" are Vajrasattva and the others. "By the self of all buddhas" means by the one characterized as Supreme Bliss and the others; Supreme Bliss is the sublime bliss together with its cause and fruit. "Having accomplished oneself" means oneself transformed into the form of the deity. "Exceedingly" means excellently. "The self of all buddhas, Supreme Bliss" means experiencing unsurpassed bliss and mental joy through the essential nature of Vajrasattva and the others.

One whose intention and purpose are characterized by meditating on oneself is endowed with the genuine mind; to be joined, through the genuine mind, with the mind that takes suchness as its object is "applied to the genuine mind." The yoga of others, which lacks that genuine mind, has the nature of its reflection: what arises from taking the essence of a reflection as object is not genuine. If one asks what its opposite is like, the passage beginning "The genuine supreme mind" is spoken. The self meditated upon as the nature of one's own deity is "one's own supreme." Applying it as the characteristic of one's own mind is "applying the self," as it is said in the passage beginning "Whatever is the mind of sentient beings." "Unchanging" means unmoving. "Image" means something made into a form. "Cast image" means one made filled with relics and the like; by the word "and the like" are meant paintings drawn as pictures and so on.

Since one is thus diligent in the awakening mind, that is, a yogin through the stages of meditating on the awakening mind, namely a mantrin, "for that reason oneself becomes the deity" is the remainder of the phrase. The awakening mind is this self which, through the stages of meditating on emptiness, has become the fully complete moon disc, just as explained before. The vajra is the self shown there as one's own sign. "Of all buddhas" means of one's own self as it has become the form of one's own deity, and not external cast images and the like.

Therefore, applying oneself in this way to one's own samaya is "applying the self in all things": through being nondual in essence with the wisdom being, one obtains all buddhas; that is, by the power of meditation the yogin himself makes the state of the deity directly manifest. "Self" means one's own mind. The word "itself" is restrictive. "All buddhas" are all the tathāgatas. "All heroes" are the bodhisattvas who are born with certainty from the way of the sūtras.

"All vajra holders" have become the very bodhisattvas who are fully accomplished through the way of mantra; this is to be construed as "by the power of meditation they attain it in this very life." The passage beginning "The Blessed One" rules out any invention on the part of the compiler. That supreme suchness is the fivefold wisdom of omniscience, and that itself is the illumination. Suchness is the object, and entering it is the mudrā of one's own deity, which has the nature of wisdom; this is called the wisdom mudrā that enters the object, the supreme illumination of suchness. The chapter in which that is examined by the text is called by that name.

This is the commentary on the first chapter.

Thus Vajrasattva has been explained, by way of the five manifest awakenings, as the very nature of union. For it is said:
Absorption by wisdom alone
Is to be known as union.

There, in order to care for the yogin who is endowed with certainty, and to show that the Blessed One, after the wisdom being has first been dissolved within, abides with the essential nature of subsequent union, the passage beginning "This is the yoga, the Blessed One" is spoken. "This" refers to the one who has the nature of the yoga just described: "By uniting oneself with Vajrasattva, one is fully accomplished." "Uniting oneself with Vajrasattva" means that, having become nondual with the one who abides in front, oneself appears everywhere as the body, speech and mind of the deity, and so brings everything down.

The meaning of "union with all buddhas" has already been explained. Yoga is becoming habituated to meditation on it. Its lord is one who has gained mastery over freedom from all obscurations, up to being the great lord, supreme above all. The unsurpassed bliss that comes from that is the great bliss of the lord of yoga, the union with all buddhas, and that too will be fully accomplished: in this way cause and fruit are set out.

In order to show that very thing as the cause, the passage beginning "Therefore" is spoken. "Therefore" means subsequent union. "All the bliss of the buddhas" means the intrinsic nature of all deities; that bliss is attained through bliss itself. In order to show that very bliss next, the passage beginning "All buddhas" is spoken. In order to show what was meant by "through bliss itself," the passage beginning "In this" is spoken; "in that" means in subsequent union.

Bathing means cleanliness and the like; since it is not needed, one acts just as one wishes. Restraint means abstaining from salt and the like; since it is not needed, one eats everything. Austerity is turning away from all objects; since it is not needed, one enjoys everything. The practice of hardship is the observance of knowledge; since it is not needed, one indulges as one likes in the joys of desire. "Conducting oneself as one likes, one does what one wishes" means sitting and lying down as one pleases.

Having thus shown the greatness of subsequent union, in order to show that subsequent union itself, the passage beginning "Eon" is spoken. "All the supreme buddhas" means just as one's own nature is. "The rite of the mudrā" means by placing them in front as the body of the deity. "Having accomplished" means, here, dissolving them within. "Oneself" means in the form of the great mudrā that has the nature of the union explained before. "Accomplishing" means blessing, up to subsequent union. "It should be examined" means it should be done as explained before.

In order to make that very blessing clear, "He blesses all buddhas" is spoken: then, further, passing on from one to the next, all buddhas are fully accomplished. "Endowed" means that very one has become nondual together with that. "All buddhas" means just as in the yoga of one's own deity. "He also sees the truths" means obtaining the samaya. "Through the union of all yogas" means abiding in the form of the retinue. "This" and "to all buddhas" refer to this mantra practitioner who has performed subsequent union.

In "Women also," the word "also" means that men too will become liberated, having given up the male form and been transformed into the nature of a goddess. "By that" means having performed subsequent union as explained. "This" means the mantra practitioner. "Abiding everywhere as oneself" means abiding in all directions. "All offerings" are the nature of the various articles of the outer maṇḍala, pervading with great clouds of them and the like in great numbers; with these one makes offerings. "Offered to by all the tathāgatas" means worthy of offerings from all selves; this is to be construed as "one will be accomplished as glorious Vajrasattva himself, or as the very deity of all selves, just as he is."

"The self of all" means the complete meditation on the self of all entities, surrounded by all; because he is its self, the Blessed One Vajrasattva is all yogas. Its application is being applied with its pride. By that "glory becomes the basis," that is, the life, youth, health and supreme bliss of Vajrasattva.

In order to show the application of all yogins, the passage beginning "All yogas" is spoken. "The three realms" means objects such as form; while displaying them and experiencing them through the enjoyments of all yogas, one makes offerings to oneself, endowed with the pride of the body of one's own deity. And it is not only displaying: "If one fully experiences oneself the bliss of all yogas" means that one makes offerings oneself through the subsequent union explained before, and is accomplished through supreme union. "Exceedingly" means the intense delight of bliss: one will attain even up to supreme union, which is characterized by perfect union with all the goddesses.

"The self of all buddhas" means just as the nature of one's own deity is. Making offerings with that is blissful, like taking an elixir. "The rasāya of the self of all buddhas" means that uniting with Vajrasattva through that bliss is uniting with the wisdom being.

Because it brings understanding of that, it is the chapter on complete union with glorious Vajrasattva.

This is the commentary on the second chapter.

Having thus taught the essential nature of complete union with glorious Vajrasattva, the Blessed One, in order to show that a yogin who has become steady in it should then fully enter supreme union, spoke the passage beginning "This." These first two verses show the connection between the second chapter, explained before, and this one.

The passage beginning "Through the very power of union with glorious Vajrasattva" teaches supreme union. For a yogin who has performed the union and the subsequent union of glorious Vajrasattva, the supreme union is the complete union with glorious Vajrasattva. Its power is having made it steady. "Through that, great bliss": because one is sealed as having become the essential nature of Vajrasattva, it is the mudrā of great bliss. "The yogins, all who delight in bliss" is said as an exclamation of wonder. Through being united with bliss, the bliss is excellent. It should thus be understood that the lords who delight in the bliss of all accomplishments have the excellent bliss of awareness in perfect union with the two goddesses.

"The supreme women" are all the remaining goddesses of the maṇḍala. "Will be born" means each is accomplished in turn; all of these show the fruit of supreme union. "Of him" means of Vajrasattva. "That" means Supreme Bliss and the others: it should be known that they become manifest without doubt to the yogin who has performed supreme union in this way. Whatever samaya of that kind there is, is the textual tradition, and what arises from it is "born." Therefore that itself is lovely, that is, to be ardently desired. "Following the samaya of the self" means following the tradition of one's own mantras, mudrās and the rest. Supreme union is having become indistinguishable from the goddess. That itself is the great union, up to supreme union.

Because he is the essential nature of all entities, "the self of all" is Vajrasattva. His supreme samaya is not to be transgressed; the preceding word and the third are joined to that person. "Like the one" means Vajrasattva: whoever has the manner of the solitary going, the solitary conduct of Vajrasattva alone, conducts himself alone. "Those who conduct themselves together" are so called because they conduct themselves together: they are the four chief goddesses. "Those who follow in conduct" are the others besides them; this gathers them all.

"All delights without remainder" means all the mudrās, through meditating on supreme union: the mudrās of the goddesses of all the maṇḍalas without remainder. "Through the union of all the goddesses" was explained before. Through that itself comes "all good fortune without remainder," that is, having accomplished oneself. Through the bliss of union endowed with good fortune in every aspect, it arises in turn: one fully attains the bliss of union with the goddesses. "By this mudrā" means that, just as oneself is sealed with the mudrās of all the goddesses, one becomes indistinguishable from them. Whoever is Vajrasattva is himself the great deity, so he is Vajrasattva, the great deity.

"All delight in the bliss of yoga" means entering the bliss born from all yogas. "Without sin" means without misconduct. "With power" means together with power. "Openly" means directly. "Through mutual great delight" means through delight in touching one another, and through the arising of joys. "In the manner of a man" means acting as oneself, or as a man does. "Arising from all the senses" means arising from all the pores of the body hair. "Through all the bliss of yoga" means through the descent of the bliss that arises from all yogas. "Oh, arisen from bliss" means arisen with certainty from the bhaga and the liṅga. "With all the heaps of jewels" means with the lofty jewels such as the limbs of awakening, and with cymbals. "Utterly bewildered" means having entered, that is, relying on those enjoyments of all the goddesses.

"Uniting oneself with the great deity" means meditating on oneself as one's own deity: that subsequent union is entered within. The bliss that arises from all yogas is "the bliss of all yogas," and "experiencing" means experiencing it. "Life, youth, health and supreme bliss" shows in order the four goddesses, Supreme Bliss and the others. The passage beginning "The great body of all buddhas" refers to the four, the awakening of the buddhas and the rest. "The great king of all buddhas" is Vairocana. The passage beginning "Lord of all vajra holders" shows the three, Heruka and the others. "With them" means as with one's own goddesses. "That" means union through the other deities.

"He becomes accomplished as a wheel-turning monarch" means being born as one who rules over the six maṇḍalas. "Vast" means of great qualities. "Supreme" means transcending all worlds. "Delighting in the bliss of all accomplishments" means the excellent bliss of accomplishments such as going in the sky; "all the lords of bliss" are the essential nature of Vajrasattva, Vairocana and the others, and that itself is what delights. "The Ḍākinī Illusion, Supreme Bliss" means: with the excellence of the bliss of accomplishments such as moving in the sky, and being in union together with the goddesses explained before, he is fortunate; Vajrasattva in perfect union with all the goddesses is the fortunate one. That itself is the glory of the goddesses. Because it is not to be transgressed, it is the samaya. Because it is understood through those words, this chapter is so called.

This is the commentary on the third chapter.

Having thus taught the Blessed One, through three chapters, as having the nature of union, subsequent union and supreme union, then, because wisdom is accomplished without decline on all the grounds, and in order to teach the nature of the glorious chapter on samaya called "the supreme accomplishment of the good fortune of all the manifold," which is characterized by meditation on all the unions, the passage beginning "This" is spoken. This verse should be seen as the chapter of full entry, and, by way of connection, as connected with the other two chapters.

"Union with all the goddesses" means enjoying all the goddesses. Therefore, because one has accomplished oneself and is in union together with the goddess, one is fortunate; and by that very fact, having thus entered into the play of women, one desires the supreme. Whatever complete union there is that experiences the excellent bliss of perfect union with the goddesses, that is the experience of the bliss of perfect union. Through excellent attachment, the mind being attached to all the goddesses, there is passion; through all the unions the yogin is accomplished. "Melody" here is to be taken as sound. "The tangible" should be understood as touch through the touch of bliss. "Practicing their own conduct" means enjoying them by taking hold of bliss.

Having thus shown the enjoyment of the fruit of Vajrasattva, in order to show the enjoyment of the fruit of Vairocana and the others, the passage beginning "Buddha" is spoken. These are the perfection of awakening, the perfection of the jewel, the perfection of the Dharma, the perfection of action, the perfection of generosity, the perfection of moral discipline, the perfection of patience, the perfection of diligence, the perfection of meditative concentration, the perfection of wisdom, the perfection of skillful means, the perfection of aspiration, the perfection of power, and the perfection of primordial wisdom: all these perfections; and the forms of Samantabhadra and the others; and through unborn awakening, the Very Joyful, the Stainless, the Luminous, the Radiant, the Difficult to Master, the Manifest, the Gone Far, the Immovable, the Good Intelligence, the Cloud of Dharma, and the Universal Light: these twelve.

"Without māras" means free from being overpowered by the māras. "Accomplishment" means being fully accomplished. "And the difficult" is said because it must be accomplished by inferior beings through excellent diligence over three incalculable eons. "Great bliss" is so called because it is the essential nature of the unchanging bliss of all accomplishments. "The fortunate one who experiences bliss" means fortunate because of experiencing exceedingly excellent bliss; this is to be construed as "for the sake of awakening, one becomes accomplished by oneself meditating on all the yogas without error." "All accomplishments" are accomplishments such as moving in the sky, and their gradual completion is "arisen." "Buddha" is Vairocana. "Having seized" means having brought them under one's own control by the power of meditation. "Should be enjoyed" means experienced. "All the great kingdoms of the buddhas" is the state of Vajrasūrya. "The great accomplishment of all buddhas, the sublime supreme lords" is the state of Paramāśva.

Having shown the perfection of one's own aim, in order to show the perfection of the aim of others, the passage beginning "Great power" is spoken. "Accomplished and" refers to the reverse of austerity. "Memory" here is Smara, the god of desire. "All the aims of the buddhas" is to be construed as "he bestows all the aims of the buddhas and performs all the deeds of the buddhas." "Utterly destroying all the hosts" means utterly destroying all the hosts of Māra. "Wheel-turner" means fully turning the wheel of Dharma. "Displaying miracles through magical power" means playing with magical power.

In order to explain that very thing at length, the passage beginning "In some" is spoken. "The great awakening mind" is the mind of unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening. "Conduct" is that which is characterized by generosity and the rest. "Going in accordance" means as is fitting. "Excellent transference" means taking up births. "Going to great awakening" means going for the sake of awakening, up to "he went to the heart of awakening"; or else, great awakening itself is a great festival, since it is great joy and delight, and so it is "going to great awakening." "Turning the wheel" means fully engaging in turning the wheel of Dharma. "The dance of certainty" means play. "Sealing without remainder" means generating the mind toward awakening. "Manifold" means of many kinds. "All" refers to the five kinds of families; because it shows what is accomplished as both fortunate and supreme, it is called "the supreme accomplishment of the good fortune of all the manifold." "Glorious" refers to the goddesses. The samaya is what is not to be transgressed, namely Vajrasattva. Because it teaches that, it is the glorious chapter on samaya.

This is the commentary on the fourth chapter.

Having thus taught the Blessed One as having the nature of union and the rest, in order to teach the application of the rite of the maṇḍala and so forth: since the yogin endowed with the four unions as explained accomplishes his purposes through the speech of the accomplishments taught before, through the mudrā of the hand emblems, and through the manifold mudrā, and in order to teach the Blessed One as the essential nature of the wisdom of the speech mudrā, the passage beginning "This" is spoken. This verse shows the connection with the previous chapter. "Yoga" is said because he is the essential nature of the yoga as explained. Because he has gone in stages in the manner of taming sentient beings, he is the Tathāgata.

"Truly accomplished, the one alone" means in the ultimate sense. "Applied just as one wishes": in order to tame sentient beings who engage in attachment and so forth, it is in the corresponding sequence; applying it in accordance with their dispositions is meditating on Vajrasattva himself in the form of Vairocana and the rest. "In all the rites of the ritual system" means in these very rites. "Of the supreme" is because it has become the highest of the worldly accomplishments. "Supreme" means that there is nothing that surpasses even it; it has become the very nature of that accomplishment.

To show that this is not confined to worldly accomplishments alone, the passage beginning "Various" is spoken. "Through the application of various actions" means by emanating the bodies of Vajrasattva, Vairocana, and the rest. "Of the various rites that are desired" means engaging in the bliss of attachment, in aversion, in delusion, in craving for wealth, in regret, and so forth. "And taming" is because attachment and the rest are thoroughly abandoned. "Through the magical display of all the buddhas" means through the play of Vajrasattva, Vairocana, and the rest.

If one asks how this is accomplished, the passage beginning "Each day" is spoken. "Having blessed accordingly" means by oneself abiding, just as one is, in the pleasure grove of the three cravings. "Bringing about the supreme bliss of the buddhas" is through the union of the assembly maṇḍala of Vajrasattva and the rest. If one asks how, the passage beginning "Of the elements" is spoken. "Everything whose substance is of the elements" is what has the nature of gold, silver, and so forth. Then there is what is made from things whose nature is life, nourishment, and so forth. "Arising from roots" is what is made from things whose nature is wood. "Whatever maṇḍala" means whichever maṇḍala it may be, of Vajrasattva and the rest. "Relying on" means meditating. "In each of these" refers to the maṇḍala. "Having made offerings" means offering. "Moreover, beyond that" means that after this one sets forth the discourse of each separate door. "Of all the ritual systems" refers to the reassurance of Vajrasattva and the rest. "Emanated as is fitting" means playing as is fitting.

This is what is shown: just as, in one's own assembly maṇḍala, together with the disciples who are joined with their own deities, one who is endowed with one's own hand emblems and mudrās has spoken words of reassurance to oneself, so too one should speak them to the disciples. And they, in order to bring to perfection the excellence of their own devoted faith through union with their own deity, should practice in accordance with what is spoken.

With regard to sentient beings who engage in attachment, and in order to teach the reassurance of the Blessed One Vajrasattva, the passage beginning "Sattva" is spoken. Because he dwells in the nirvāṇa that does not abide, it is like something occurring in the present. Because his essential nature is unchanging, he is hard to surpass; this is intended to refer to the Dharma body. "For the sake of taming" means the taming of sentient beings by them. If one asks whether it is done, the doing is on the conventional level. "The reassurance of glorious Vajrasattva" means the reassurance given by glorious Vajrasattva: by this, sentient beings who engage in attachment and the rest are reassured.

Thus, having shown that the Blessed One, who is himself of the nature of the essence body, is on the conventional level the essential nature of all things, in order to tame those who engage in attachment; then, in order to tame sentient beings who engage in peace, and to show the way in which he is the essential nature of all because he abides in the pure sphere of reality, the passage beginning "In past times" is spoken.

"Intensely devoted to emptiness" means endowed with striving for nirvāṇa with remainder and nirvāṇa without remainder. "Through union with the nature of cessation" means connected with the two nirvāṇas just explained. "Having ceased" refers to nirvāṇa with remainder. "Fully passed into nirvāṇa" refers to nirvāṇa without remainder. "All liberations" are the complete nirvāṇa of the śrāvakas and others. Its purification is the generation of great compassion after realizing phenomena whose nature is nondual. "Generated" means made.

"Not empty" refers to the true: that which arises from the imagined exists. "Not non-empty" means that the imagined itself does not exist. "The middle way" is the wisdom that holds both of these to be nonexistent; and since even the establishing of a middle way is the domain of conceptual thought, it too is not apprehended. That itself is called the union of the perfection of wisdom. Because, being embraced by great compassion, it is of the nature of compassion and works for the benefit of beings, the words "it is the skillful means of the buddhas" are to be supplied.

"He" is Vajrasattva; it is to be construed with "becomes Vairocana, the word of the Dharma." To show the magical display, the passage beginning "Compassion" is spoken: one in whom there is compassion and skillful means, and whose perfection of wisdom is stainless, is so called. "Toward phenomena free of conceptualization" means that although he is free of all conceptualization, by the power of those to be tamed he conceives and transforms himself into all the maṇḍalas, mudrās, and so forth. "Nonconceptual" is the nondual nature; what conceives for the benefit of sentient beings is the conventional. Ultimately it is like the sound echoing from a cliff; it is just the same as an echo.

"Dharma" is the three samayas, and whatever words teach it are the "word of the Dharma." "Thereupon" means after he has become the word of the Dharma. The Great Vehicle is endowed with focus, practice, wisdom, diligence, all means, and great activity; it is the bodhisattva path together with its result, and the essential nature of the sūtras such as the Perfection of Wisdom. Because it is great in that it definitely brings about buddhahood and the rest in this very life, it is the great of the Great Vehicle: the Vajra Vehicle.

The passage beginning "Unequaled" shows the extensive qualities that have arisen from that, as comprised in the three bodies. "Song" means praise. "Of the three times" means the three kinds of time. "The three samayas" are the nature of the three bodies. If one asks what they are like, the passage beginning "Unequaled" is spoken. By the word "unmoving" the Dharma body is shown. "Sameness" is suchness, and that is the supreme dharma. One in whom that sameness is present possesses the supreme nature of sameness, and by this the enjoyment body is shown. By "Whose nature is compassion, who removes the suffering of beings," acting for the benefit of others by way of the emanation body is shown; and in these three as well he is unequaled. "Removes suffering" means that he destroys sufferings, both the visible and the invisible. "Infinite qualities" means qualities that have no end. "Bestower of all accomplishments" is said of one whose disposition is to grant all accomplishments, both worldly and transcendent.

If one asks why he removes sufferings and grants the accomplishments of the qualities, it is because "whose nature is compassion" is said. If one asks, "Even if his nature is compassion, by what does he act in this way?" the passage beginning "Stainless" is spoken. Because he is free of all obscurations, he is stainless. Because he cannot be shaken by the other, non-Buddhist (tīrthika) teachers, he is unmoving. The supreme powers, fearlessnesses, and so forth are unequaled because they are not shared, in turn, with the śrāvakas, the pratyekabuddhas, and the bodhisattvas; one in whom these are present possesses the sacred and supreme qualities.

To make clear what was meant by "unequaled," the passage beginning "Qualities" is spoken. If one asks in what he is unequaled: in qualities that are extremely subtle, that is, qualities divided into superior, middling, and inferior. "Immeasurable" means endless. If one asks with what he is unequaled: "the example is space." For if not even what serves as an example, immeasurable like space, can match him, what need is there to mention lesser examples?

For those who lack these qualities, the passage beginning "To the realms of sentient beings" is spoken: to the realms of sentient beings he definitely grants the supreme accomplishment of buddhahood. If one asks from what these very qualities arise, the passage beginning "Always" is spoken: because they are motivated by the power of great compassion, they are always stainless, that is, free of affliction. "Aspirations fulfilled" is the nirvāṇa that does not abide. "Whose nature admits of no negation" is an essential nature without cessation. "Pervading infinitely" means having become all-pervasive. "Whose nature is great compassion" is the Buddha. "Always" means without end. "Illuminating" means having become clear.

If one asks, "By whom?" the passage beginning "Immeasurable" is spoken. "The immeasurable" are the śrāvakas, the pratyekabuddhas, and the bodhisattvas: even by them he cannot be measured. "Utterly perfect" is the ultimate result. "The state of the Sugata" is complete nirvāṇa, even though he only shows himself going there. If one asks what these are, the words "Nor does he come to cessation" are spoken: because of the cause, his great compassion, he does not become subject to nirvāṇa; therefore he is of a nature that does not cease.

For that very reason, the passage beginning "How wonderful, nature of utter excellence" shows an earnest supplication. "The three samayas" are the nature of the three bodies; since he is endowed with the nature of the accomplishments that are their cause, it means: "Giver of the supreme, bestower of the most excellent accomplishment, grant it to me!" "Among the givers of the supreme, having attained the sacred and supreme state" means having become the foremost of supreme givers. "Always" means without lapse. "In the three times" means reaching to the very end of the three kinds of time. "Unobscured" means free of all obscurations.

The passage beginning "This" shows the greatness of the praise by way of a preface to the conclusion. That buddhahood is accomplished even by reciting it just once is intended to mean that this will certainly happen for those who strive and have developed the power of their habitual tendencies. "All the buddhas" are Vajrasattva, Vairocana, and the rest. Uniting oneself with them means being nondual with them. "By the three samayas" means by the three bodies. "A body one never tires of beholding" means that when one sees that body, nothing in it is disagreeable. "Granting all that is supreme" means that what one has oneself been taught is accomplished. "Then, its" refers to the king of praises. "And accomplishing" means the one by whom it is accomplished. "Of the well examined" means supremely connected: whatever attainment of the result whose nature is the three bodies one gains, that very thing is accomplished. "Accomplishment" means reaching the utmost end.

"Accompanied by snapping the vajra fingers" means snapping the fingers with the vajra of the right hand, snapping them with the left, and snapping them with both. "Clapping three times" refers to both right and left; these are the three snaps as described. "Whatever faculty comes into its range" means engaging with the domains of seeing and the rest. "The nature of each" means in the nature of the identity of each one. "United with all the buddhas" means meditating with the pride of one's own deity. "Passion" refers to songs such as ṣadajakoha. "ṣadapa and the rest" means singing songs such as cokśa pañcama. "Should recite" means chanting.

This is what is shown: endowed with the union of one's own deity through the preceding sequence, one meditates on the deities of the maṇḍala within one's own body just as they are; and, endowed with the union of meditative equipoise, in every mode of conduct one intently regards all things as the form of oneself. If one then recites songs such as ṣadapa and the king of praises, accomplishment will be attained. "The self of all" is Vairocana. "The three samayas and the rest" are Vairocana and the rest. "All the ritual systems" means by the path on which the buddhas travel.

In order to tame sentient beings of aversion and the like, and so as to teach the Blessed One as the essential nature of the emanation body, the passage beginning "In past times" is spoken to set forth the reassurance of Heruka. "Wrathful" refers to the intention. "Fierce" refers to the application.

"Vicious" means having become extremely sharp. "Through the linking of habitual tendencies" means that the result concordant with its cause has fully come about. "Of wisdom and means" means endowed with the cause, wisdom and compassion. "Wrathful one" means as wrathfulness itself. This shows the following: having realized that phenomena are like illusions, he directed his compassion toward vicious sentient beings, and in order to tame them all the tathāgatas assumed the contrived form of the wrathful Heruka. As it is said:

Great vajra, Heruka,
blazing, he utterly destroys all to ash,
the great wrathful one of all the buddhas.

If one asks by what he punishes with wrath, the passage beginning "Bearing the manner of wrath" is spoken. "Angered" refers to the anger of the sages (ṛṣis). If one asks what it is like: "the manner of wrath" means abiding; because he abides in that manner, he bears the manner. The intended meaning is this: if even a wrathful one who merely abides in the manner of wrath is able to burn up the three worlds, what need is there to mention the buddhas, who are otherwise free of all faults and have mastery over all excellent qualities?

If one asks in what way he brings forth the wrathful one, the passage beginning "Then" is spoken. The long, warm breath from the nose is wisdom, and this is the application together with its exhalation. "Union of mudrā" means the wrathful meditative union. "He" is Vajrasattva. "Brought forth" means definitely generated. "Endowed with the manner of wrath and the rest" means endowed with the moods of wrath, heroism, grace, and terror. Hence he is "of various forms," transformed so as to have four faces. "Having subdued" means having brought under control. "Shadow" means the ḍākinī. "All united in vajra equality" means united equally in all the wrathful ones. Through this, the maṇḍala of the ḍākinīs blazes. If one asks what it does, the passage beginning "Extremely vicious" is spoken. "Vajra desire-wrath" is the wrathful one of wisdom and means. "By all the unsurpassed permanent ones" means by all the emanations of the wrathful one.

Now, in order to show the Blessed One, who has become the essential nature of the wisdom of equality, as having the nature of the enjoyment body, and in order to teach the reassurance of Padmanarteśvara for the sake of taming sentient beings who engage in delusion, the passage beginning "In past times" is spoken. "Dwelling in wrong views" means having fully entered into the view of nonexistence. "He" is Vajrasattva. "In order to purify the mind of awakening" means in order to generate true wisdom in sentient beings, preceded by thoroughly abandoning wrong views. "Through union with the lotus in passion" means having become the essential nature of great compassion. "He came to abide as Vajradharma" means abiding through the purification of attachment. To show this very point, the passage beginning "Just as the lotus" is spoken. Regarding "He came to abide as Vajradharma," some say that he transformed into the form of Vajradharma and was then born as Padmanarteśvara.

"Because all attachment is pure" refers to the purified attachment of compassion; for that very reason the body color of this Blessed One is like a lotus. "Through various means of taming" means that, endowed with dance postures such as the graceful one, he transforms into various faces. "Devoted to taming the various" refers to emanations such as the Observer; because he tames, he is a tamer, and because he tames the various kinds of disciples who engage in views, he has become the cause of devotion to taming the various. "Looking on all around" means looking with the eyes of the discriminating wisdom of purified attachment. "Lord" means having gained mastery over that. "Of the union of the lotus illusion": abiding as the essence of the maṇḍala of the lotus family, through it he accomplishes movement through the sky; these are the ḍākinīs of the union of the lotus illusion. That itself is the illusion, or emanation, and through it the sacred bliss is attained. If one asks what he does through this, the passage beginning "Glorious Padmanarteśvara" is spoken. "Unsurpassed ritual system" refers to the emanations of Lokeśvara. "Purified" means that he frees beings from views.

Having shown the Blessed One as the essential nature of the maturation body, in order to teach the reassurance of Vajrasūrya for the sake of caring for sentient beings who engage in craving, the passage beginning "In past times" is spoken. "Of the pretas" means of those who belong among the hungry ghosts. "He" is Vajrasattva. "Of the lotus's purpose" means having wholly given himself over to the welfare of the whole world. "Great wealth" means that, by the force of his former impetus, he fulfills the wishes of sentient beings. "Vajrasūrya will arise" means that he transforms into Vajrasūrya himself. In the passage beginning "Ākāśagarbha, all the directions," it is because he has the essential nature of the empowerment of Ākāśagarbha, because by his nature as all treasures he fulfills the hopes of all, and because he has the form of the supreme victory banner. That he rains down all the supreme jewels, such as women, is because he has the essential nature of the great offering. Having the nature of wisdom and compassion, he is like the sun, and so he is the sun. If one asks why, it is because "intensely burning" is said.

"Acting" means hurling: striking with arrows and the like. "Repelling" means with wheels and the like, thrown by hand. "By snatching" means hurling and then seizing back again, with nooses and the like. "Gathers" means that he made the three worlds triumph over miserliness and become greatly wealthy. "Victor over the three worlds" is Vajrasūrya, and the rest. "All the ritual systems": because he emanates heaps of jewels, the meaning is "completely filled"; having purified craving, he brings beings to attain the state of Vajrasūrya.

In order to present him as Paramāśva for the sake of caring for lazy sentient beings, the passage beginning "In past times" is spoken. "Of very little diligence" means extremely lazy. "Application of violence" is the prowess of overpowering others. Its purification is removing laziness and firmly establishing beings in diligence. Therefore, as the purification of the application of violence, he arose as Paramāśva, that is, he became Hayagrīva himself. "All are truly devoted" is because, preceded by the abandoning of laziness, they wish to generate diligence. "Brazenly" means openly. "By the prowess of overpowering others" means by might. "Mouse" means one who goes to another man's wife. "By robbery" means seizing openly. "Deceiving" means devices of deceit. "Illusions" means by conjuring tricks. "The five kinds of weapons" are those hurled by machines and the like, which are explained below; because they fight with these, they are "assailed by the five weapons." "The goddess Bhīmā" is she who turns back austerities. "Mistress of Men" is called Craving and Mistress. "Vajradhara" is Vajrasattva. "Mind of awakening" means having the nature of awakening. "Accomplishes" means generating it in the mindstreams of sentient beings. "The awakening of the buddhas" is the essential nature of the realization of the peaceful nature of reality. What is generated from that is Vairocana; his "utterly vanquishing" is subduing sentient beings through the fierce application, and "his own yoga" is having the nature of the fierce application. "Sealing" is the application of diligence. "All the unsurpassed ritual systems" means those of all the families. "Through the application of discourse": this is the commentary on the ritual system of knowing the mudrās, the commentary on the fifth ritual system.

Now, to show that accomplishment comes when, by meditating as explained, one has attained the three samayas in the maṇḍala, since here it is to be accomplished through rites such as those of the hand emblems and mudrās, and to show that the Blessed One Vajrasattva abides in the manner of the assembly maṇḍala, the passage beginning "Then, furthermore" is spoken. "Various in every aspect" is exemplified by the goddesses whose forms are various in every way. That itself, being sacred, has become supreme, because it has become the cause of Vajrasattva, whose essential nature is unsurpassed bliss and mental joy. "United with all the buddhas, ḍākinī illusion, supreme bliss" is to be construed with "one should practice, abiding always in the three existences distinguished as secret and so forth." "Glory": the women who are yoginīs of Śrī are the glory, and because he has them he is glorious; the meaning is "encircled all around." "The reality of all the buddhas" is the nature of all the buddhas, up to "having become its essential nature." "Arising as Vajrasattva" means endowed with the samādhi of Vajrasattva. "Bliss" is a particular kind of activity. "Those whose nature is the tathāgatas" are the kings of knowledge (vidyārājas) and the rest; it means adorned with them. "Scent" means ointment. "The three existences" means a three-storied house. "Pleasure grove" is one's own pleasure grove, described below. "Smooth" means perfectly level; the oral instructions explain that this refers to a cushion fully stuffed inside. "Various lotuses" is any cloth on which various kinds of lotuses are painted. "Covered" means covered with it. "The seat of total purity" is the seat of the lord.

"United with all the buddhas" means joining in the maṇḍala of union. Because he has gained mastery over it, he is the lord, and he displays his magical manifestations by means of it. "The deities such as Vajrasattva" are the deities of Vajrasattva; they are called deities because they shine forth or because they play, namely Supreme Bliss and the rest. "There" means on the seat. "Performs magical displays" means that he dances and shows himself, as whatever he arises as, to whomever. "Those seats" are the seats of total purity. "For all" means for everyone. "As they are situated": placing them according to whatever place and whatever direction each has is "as they are situated"; abiding there just as they are refers to the defining arrangement of one's own place in the directions and intermediate directions. "As" means like the seat of the lord. "Arranging" means placing them there: just as that seat is covered with cloth bearing various lotuses, so too, in every maṇḍala, a smaller seat of the same kind is to be made for every goddess.

Moreover, it arises from the secret of the various lotus: because it has eight petals it is various, and it is also covered with cloth, because it is covered by the moon disc whose nature is the mind of awakening. Such is the seat of total purity, where the lords sport with the goddesses; it is outwardly conceived in this way, and for them even that is mere conception, because "glorious Vajrasattva" and the rest are names for the supreme state between the two, themselves and the goddesses. "Square" and so forth are the particulars of the outer maṇḍala. The inner one is also explained by the passage beginning "All-various, of good fortune." "It should be provided with gatekeepers" refers to the gates blessed by Vajrāṅkuśa and the rest. For that very reason the four gatekeepers, Horse-Face and the rest, are to be arranged there. "Endowed with their own deities" means adorned with the crown and other ornaments of their own deity, made of cast metal and the like. "The women are well accomplished" means that the women are adorned with their own ornaments.

This is what is shown: having drawn the maṇḍala just as described, in the center of the inner maṇḍala one should abide, intently imagining oneself endowed with the yoga of Vajrasattva, holding a vajra raised in the right hand, holding a bell in the left with a haughty air, and again drawing a bow and arrow with right and left. On the eastern petal of that same seat is a woman who is a yoginī of Supreme Bliss, holding a bow in her left hand with a haughty air and drawing an arrow with her right.

On the southern petal is the yoginī of Aho Sukhā, who abides embracing the lord with her right arm and holds the bhaga and the liṅga in her left with a haughty air. On the western petal is one endowed with the yoga of Tārā, holding a crossed vajra in her right hand and, with her left held haughtily and displayed in the manner of resting on the shoulder, bearing a kapāla together with a vajra khaṭvāṅga. On the northern petal is one endowed with the yoga of Śaśī, embracing the lord joyfully with her right hand and holding with her left, haughtily, a kapāla and an image of the Buddha; she is white in color.

In the compartments of the inner maṇḍala, beginning in the east, are, in order, the goddesses in the form of the Awakening of the Buddhas, the Turning of the Wheel of Dharma, Victory over the Three Realms, and the Vine of All Desires, each holding her own hand emblem in her left hand. Likewise, in the compartments of the southeast and the other intermediate directions are the four in the form of Horse-Face and the rest. In the corners within the vajra line of the outer squares, the figures of the flute goddess and the others are placed in the same way. Likewise, in the corners of the outer maṇḍala are the yoginīs, the Flower Goddess and the rest. In the gates, beginning in the east, are placed those who hold the hook, the noose, the chain, and the bell. The seats of the four, Supreme Bliss and the rest, are to be made of various lotuses.

In the center of the maṇḍala of Vairocana, one is to place the body of Vairocana with the mudrā of the wheel of Dharma. On the petals of that same seat, beginning in the east, are placed in order the emblems of the alms bowl, the Dharma robes, the monk's staff, and the volume of the sacred Dharma. In the compartments beginning in the east are the four, She Who Proclaims All the Buddhas and the rest, holding their own hand emblems, described below, with a haughty air, and abiding with the left fist covered by the right hand. In the intermediate directions, beginning with the southeast, are the four, the Awakening of All the Buddhas and the rest. Likewise, for the rest, one should place the bodies of the deities explained for the maṇḍala of glorious Vajrasattva, for it is said:

Through the rite of Vajrasattva
accomplish all the ritual systems.

For all those deities, too, the seat is to be made of various lotuses. Likewise, in the maṇḍalas of Heruka and the others, the fixed rules for the seats should be understood.

In the maṇḍala of Heruka, too, in the center of the inner maṇḍala, one should place the body of Heruka, four-faced and two-armed, holding on his left shoulder, in the manner of resting on the shoulder, a vajra khaṭvāṅga together with a bell. With the left hand that supports the khaṭvāṅga he bears a kapāla vessel filled with mahārakta, and with his right, in the three-flag gesture, he holds a blazing five-pronged vajra wreathed in flames; he wears a great cloth as his upper garment. On the petals, beginning in the east, are placed the blazing vajra, the vajra khaṭvāṅga, the kapāla or bowl filled with mahārakta, and what is called mahāmāṃsa, obtained by fierce means. The practitioner too should hold a blazing vajra. The rest are to be practiced according to one's inclination.

In the eastern compartment of the Blessed One is Gaurī, four-faced, holding a head of Brahmā set upon four petals, and abiding with her left leg extended. In the south is Caurī, holding a hook at her heart with a wrathful left fist and holding an eight-spoked wheel on the tip of her right middle finger; she abides with her left foot raised, in order to subdue the three realms. In the west is Pramohā, with the head of a sow, holding a vessel of liquor and abiding with her left leg extended, in order to repel. On the left is Vetālī, abiding as she confers empowerment with a white vase of nectar. In the southeast is She Who Makes Swell, holding a vine in her hand and abiding as she dances in the charnel ground. In the southwest is Caṇḍālī, holding a banner in her hand and abiding mounted on the wind maṇḍala. In the northwest is the Spotted One, holding a blazing hearth in her hand and abiding with her right leg extended. As for "utterly destroying to ash" and the rest, these, as explained for the maṇḍala of Vajrasattva, are fully present here too, so their very positions should be assigned.

In the maṇḍala of Padmeśvara, too, one should place him as follows: holding at the tips of his fingers vajras and the like marked with lotuses, on lotuses that are various, red, green, white, blue, yellow, and orange, he dances with his arms extended and turned in a wheel; resting his left hand on his hip, he holds a various lotus, and with his right hand he opens it at his heart; he is crystal in color, blazing in various ways, with a turbulent halo of light. The practitioner too, resting the left hand on the hip, holds a lotus and opens it at the heart with the right. The rest are to be practiced according to one's inclination.

In the southern compartment is She Who Observes, holding an open red lotus in the manner of turning it. In the southern compartment is Tārā, holding a green lotus in her left hand and placing her right fist raised at her heart. In the western compartment is the Ornamented One, holding a white lotus in her left hand and holding aloft an eight-spoked wheel in her right. In the northern compartment is Bhṛkuṭī, holding a blue utpala in her left hand and, in her right, dancing with the three-flag gesture and holding a spear. In the southeastern compartment is the Lotus-Dweller, holding a white lotus in her left hand and holding a makara banner in her right in the manner of dancing. In the southwestern compartment is the Vajra Lotus Mistress, holding an orange lotus in her left hand and holding a five-pronged vajra raised at her heart in her right. In the northwest is the Mistress of the Fierce Lotus, holding a lotus in her left hand with a haughty air and holding a trident in her right as she dances. In the northeast is Various Lotus, holding a various lotus in her left hand with a haughty air and an eight-spoked wheel in her right. All of them are to hold their own lotuses, as appropriate, in the left hand with a haughty air. The rest, as explained for the maṇḍala of Vajrasattva, are to be placed here with their hand emblems marked with lotuses.

In the maṇḍala of Vajrasūrya, too, one should place in the center the Blessed One, red in color, holding a bow in the left hand and fitting an arrow with the right, and again holding a disc in the left and a sword in the right. The practitioner too holds the disc of the sun.

The rest too should be accomplished according to one's inclination. In the compartments of the east and the other directions are the Goddess of Empowerment and the others, holding in their hands a jeweled crown, a treasure vase, a victory banner, and offering vessels filled with flowers and the like. In the compartments of the intermediate directions, the southeast and the others, the hand emblems are those very same. As for the rest, of those explained before, here they should hold hand emblems marked with jewels.

In the center of these same and of the other maṇḍalas is the body of glorious Paramāśva, four-armed and four-legged. With the left fist he holds a various lotus, and with the right, in the three-flag gesture (tripatāka), he raises and holds a crossed vajra (viśvavajra). With the other two hands he is to be placed holding whatever he wishes from among the hand emblems of Vajrasattva and the others. The practitioner too should hold a crossed vajra. The rest should be accomplished according to one's inclination. They are marked with their own seals.

Hence "fortunate" means fortunate through being endowed with good fortune. "The maṇḍala of the gathering" is an assembly of intimates. "Should be envisioned" means should be generated. "Accomplished" means drawn forth by mantra. It is water whose nature consists of portions in the form of a vajra arisen from the syllable hūṃ. Further, "together with the vajra" means consecrated with the vajra. "Great vermilion" is the great blood. "Prepared" refers to everything up to the words "having duly consecrated it with mantras." "By power" means accomplished as having a great nature. "Thoroughly prepared" means become even more excellent than the former: one who has strength should take the first, and one without strength the others. "Lotus vessel" is the skull cup.

To show that this very substance is thoroughly accomplished, the text says the passage beginning "Great passion." "Vajra mind" is the wisdom of emptiness. That belongs to Vajrasattva, who is the natural outflow of the essential nature of the wisdom of emptiness; his fluid, which has the nature of Vajrasattva, is blood. Therefore its being ritually formed by its mantra and so on is what is meant by "the great vermilion is prepared." Or else: making it into the syllables of the mantra, and, preceded by dissolving it inward, bringing forth just as it is what arises from wisdom and skillful means, this is its being prepared as the great vermilion itself.

That very substance, formed by the preceding sequence, is what is meant by "with vajra lips." When the great red, mixed with camphor and sandalwood, is placed in the maṇḍala of the gathering, then it is called "rousing the elixir (rasāyana)." "Like a pacifying drink" means just as in rites of pacification one does it three times and tastes it, so here too.

"The bliss of the elixir
Which is the nature of all buddhas"
means with the bliss of the elixir whose essential nature is Vajrasattva. "By this" means by what has just been explained. "Vajrasattva's life" and the rest are the accomplishments whose nature is the four goddesses. "Assembly" is the maṇḍala. Entering it through union is what is meant by "those who have entered that assembly."

In order to explain what was said, "One should envision the maṇḍala of the gathering," the text says the passage beginning "Then, having bathed." "By rousing" means by the song beginning, "Emptiness, the utmost yoga, by the power of being and nonbeing." "Slowly" means gradually. "Through the practice of covering with cloth" means in the manner of being wrapped in a garment. "One should rouse the arising by oneself" means meditating through the yoga in which everything arises as the deity's body that is oneself.

This teaches the following. Sitting cross-legged on one's seat, holding vajra and bell, one places the syllable hūṃ on a moon disc at one's heart. Having been roused by the song beginning "Yoga in emptiness," one sees oneself as like space, and makes that very space like a moon disc. Then, preceded by reciting the heart mantra, through the pride of Vajrasattva one becomes Vajrasattva and says mahāsamayasatvahaṃ. With one's own samaya seal one also says samayohaṃ, and one consecrates oneself with the heart mantra samayasatva adhitiṣṭha māṃ and the others. Then, preceded by one's own song, beginning "Vajrasattva, bliss," and by dance, one binds the great seal. This is one way of self-arising.

Again, with the secret hook joined with the syllable hūṃ one invites the Blessed One into the maṇḍala, consecrates and empowers him as appropriate, and, having performed the holding of the vajra, done with suchness and the rest preceded by the secret offering, one abides in space. One makes all the world systems empty, and below one imagines a vajra arising from the syllable hūṃ. From it arises a vajra reaching to the limits of the element of space, and when that is complete one again visualizes it as crossed. Reciting the heart mantra, one sets the vajra on the ground facing downward, and, saying anurāgaya hūṃ over all those that have become established throughout the whole of space, with that conviction one generates a seat of a various lotus. Having become steady through the proper sequence of the yoga of entities and so on, one again, in accord with the seal of speech, looks upon the sentient beings who engage in desire, and in order to take them into one's care one imagines Vajrasattva at one's heart. When he is fully roused with the song beginning "The bliss of glorious Vajrasattva," one becomes Vajrasattva.

Then, in order to rouse the one thus endowed with the first union with the song beginning "Emptiness, the utmost yoga," one recites one's own heart mantra and sends forth the assemblies of goddesses from one's own heart, and through the great seals that follow the song of their own lord they are set firmly in their own places. This is the second way.

Others, again, say this. By one's own mind alone, whose nature is the syllable hūṃ set in the midst of a blazing red syllable ho, one summons the seal who is the very nature of union. Then, from the great universal monarch (cakravartin) in whom the moon disc has melted, one draws them in with the hook of one's own mantra and they enter, and one also causes those endowed with the ten powers in all the ten directions to enter. They turn into the forms of Supreme Bliss and the others. Preceded by rousing, full exhortation and the rest, one is duly exhorted by one's own four goddesses, and so the self arises. As for this, the emanation of the deities too should be regarded as arising from the lord's ultimate awakening mind, which is like a garment, and which has become nondual with one's own awareness abiding in the hub of the vajra at the heart. So they say.

"Perfect buddhas" are Vajrasattva and the others. Because they make that very thing dawn, they are sun and moon. "The definite path" is a measure of the song, a measure of about half a mātrā; entering it definitely in fast, middle and slow tempo, as appropriate, is "transcending."

"Steady, and rousing the accomplished":
rousing what is steady and fresh, by first commencing, then accomplishing and so on, is called "steady," and the term is applied to whatever fine song one begins. For it is explained thus: "One should sing the songs called steady and so on."

To make that very thing steady, the text says the passage beginning "Emptiness." It is so called because in it there is the definite union of all the goddesses with the savor of emptiness. O you who are definitely united with it: whatever apprehended objects and apprehending subjects sentient beings imagine, because those do not exist, it is empty. "Power" is the particular kinds of undefiled wisdom that take that as their object. Through them one abides close to space itself, and because skillful means and wisdom have become nondual, one attains the awakening mind just as it is. So some hold.

"Pledge" there is so called because that very state is not to be transgressed. The Sugata who has become thus is Vajrasattva. "Sugatas" are the Constant One and the others. "Please arise" means engaging in a manner that accords with yoga. With "Vajrasattva," one's own arising is shown in brief: "O glorious Vajrasattva, fortunate one who has accomplished himself, whatever you are, that am I."

"Utterly and completely passed beyond sorrow"
is nirvāṇa without remainder. "Renewed existence" is saṃsāra. The four paths, called the displayed, the various, the changing and the right, are the four limbs of song; they should be understood respectively as measures of half a mātrā, two, four and eight. "Following the various" means extended in every various way and made manifest; in this position it is followed variously.

"Honored by the three worlds" is said because he has the essential nature of Vairocana. "Teacher of the three worlds" is said because he has the nature of Heruka. "Giving gifts to the three worlds" is said because he has the essential nature of Vajrasūrya. "Accomplish the three worlds for me" means: please make it manifest as Padmanarteśvara himself.

To show the result of emanating them, the text says the passage beginning "Vajrasattva." Vajrasattva being united throughout together with all the goddesses is "universal union." That very one is "fortunate in every various way." "All the arali" is Vajrasattva. "Will be accomplished" means will become directly manifest. Or else: that very yogin, endowed with all the goddesses, the "all arali," is Vajrasattva, and "will be accomplished" means will be born.

Having first shown the perfection of one's own aim, the text shows the perfection of others' aim with the two phrases of exhortation beginning "At once." Further, in order to show by this the very accomplishment of complete union as well, it says the passage beginning "Displayed." "Following the various" means through complete union.

"Then" means that at the time of the meditation in which the exhortation has been made, the goddesses seated in the center of the maṇḍala of the gathering should exhort the one who has the nature of Vajrasattva with the sublime suchness beginning "Passionately attached." "Passionately attached" means attached through discriminating awareness: this is Supreme Bliss. "Joy" means endowed with the bliss of embrace: this is Wondrous Bliss. "Pure lotus" means free of all the stains of the afflictions: this is Tārā. "Offering" means having fully attained all great lordship: this is Śiśi. Since they are peaceful and various, of many kinds, the words "the many forms of passionate attachment, the joy, the pure lotus, the offering" are to be construed thus: by those offerings, the pure lotus of joy of the many forms of such passionate attachment, you are honored clearly and manifestly.

If one asks what their essential nature is, it is "supremely constant yoga." "Blissful through those whose nature is the bliss of a union supremely excellent and unbroken" is the qualification. Therefore "Please accomplish" is a supplication. "The essential nature of supreme bliss" is the intrinsic nature of great bliss. "Pledge" means it is not to be transgressed. "The utmost yoga of supreme bliss" is what has become the cause of supreme bliss; because by it one has accomplished oneself and is richly endowed with supreme bliss, one is "fortunate." Because it surpasses all, it is "supreme"; it is highly praised and delighted with joy in every aim. "Enjoy!" is a supplication. The second "enjoy!" means that it is utterly delightful. ho is uttered out of passionate attachment.

If one asks with whom one is to enjoy, the text says the passage beginning "One's own pledge." "One's own pledge" is Supreme Bliss. "Faultless" is Wondrous Bliss. "Constant" is Tārā. "That very pledge" is said with reference to Śiśi. "Supremely secret" is the defining mark of union. "Lord" means having mastery over that utmost secret. "Supreme" means excellent. It is construed thus: "You who are utmost secret and supreme, enjoy yourself with these goddesses!"

With the passage beginning "The streams of beings," the exhortation is made by way of ultimate perfection. "Beings" are sentient beings. "Streams" are the emanations. "Of various kinds" means wondrous through many different forms; to tame by such streams of various kinds is the means for the welfare of sentient beings. "Clear" means appearing directly. "Knowing" means skilled in that; he is addressed thus: "You who are skilled in taming beings and clearly know how to tame." "The stable" are mountains and the like. "The moving" are humans and the like. The one who has become the essential nature of all things is addressed, "O you who have become the essence of all things, stable and moving." In the sense of having become their essential nature, he is the substance of all pledges, that is, he has become their cause.

"The yoga and bliss that is the nature of all victors" is the Union with All Buddhas, the Supreme Bliss of the Ḍākinīs' Magical Display. "Ah!" expresses wonder. "Sublime, constant, great accomplishment" is the joy of accomplishment that is utterly supreme and unbroken. "Show" means "please give delight." ja and the rest are words of exhortation by way of the four goddesses, in order to invite: through Supreme Bliss, "please discern"; through Wondrous Bliss, "please work magical display"; and through Tārā, "please dance." hūṃ means "please manifest wrath for the sake of beings." alalaho is an expression of wonder. "Please admit the disciple" means "greatly extend life." This shows the songs and the union in the sequence of the four goddesses, Supreme Bliss and the others.

Thus, after the goddesses have supplicated the Blessed One, it further shows how one supplicates with the song of one's own suchness. That is, in "Of many kinds" and so on: those who have streams of desire of many kinds, the sentient beings belonging to all world systems without exception, with none left out, are "sentient beings with streams of desire of many kinds, all without exception, with none left out." Taming the minds that engage in desire is abandoning desire through desire. Being skilled in that is "knowing." All the tathāgatas, by the characteristics of women and men, tame the sentient beings included within all world systems without exception, with none left out; taming them is producing the bliss of Vajrasattva's touch and mental bliss. "Skilled in that" is as before. "Streams of wrath of many kinds": those sentient beings who take life, all without exception and with none left out, should be understood in the same way. Gathering them in is making them blissful by drawing in their life. "Skilled in that" is as before.

rten phra rten hūṃ and the rest should be understood to be the pledge of exhortation. By these very ones, in the sequence of abiding just as in complete union, should be understood the actions of passionate attachment and the rest. "Lord of accomplishment" means having gained mastery over accomplishments. "Accomplished up to that" means the union brought to completion. "Will be accomplished" means they will come to know it. "These songs" means through the songs and seals as explained. "Of the Teacher" means the deities. "Bringing down the seal" means accomplishing through consecration with the seal. Not only that, it is also accomplished by one diligent in meditating on the women, Supreme Bliss and the others. Why is that? Because even those who are not women, that is, men, accomplish it if they bring down that seal. "Bringing down" is the consecration of the yoginī who holds the emblem together with the vajra, so that she resembles one's own chosen deities; by that she becomes beautified.

"Through the magical display
Of unprecedented music, cymbals and dance"
means with flutes and the like. "Will be accomplished" means certainly. "Deity forms and so on" means through emanation. "One's own deity" means seeing oneself as the lord of the place. "Offering" is the deities' offerings, flowers and the like; uniting equally with them is offering. Hence one is fortunate, and for that very reason one possesses supreme desire. "Fortunate in every way" means endowed with the union of Vajrasattva. "Faultless": fully accomplishing this very bliss is enjoyment. "The accomplishment of glorious Vajrasattva": by uniting with it, from that union with it one will experience the maṇḍala of bliss of all accomplishments. Why is that?
"Because it is blessed by the illusion of the buddhas":
Supreme Bliss and the rest produce the power of the buddhas' illusion.

Having thus shown the greatness of exhortation by song, in order to point out the victory banner, the text says the passage beginning "Then." "Then" means after the exhortation. "The women, the four" means the yoginīs who offer flowers and the like. "With the offering of the dance seal" means first dancing, and then binding the seals of offering, such as raising upward, which come after this. "All offerings" means the offerings of flowers and the like, as when oneself is united with all the goddesses. This teaches the following. By the preceding sequence one performs the samādhi of the supreme maṇḍala, generates the nature of the songs just as it is, performs the gathering, obtains empowerment, performs the invitation with the hook and so on, and presents the offerings; then, with the action seals, one should perform the offering and the rest with the seal of one's own deity.

Having thus completed the samādhi of the supreme king of action, one raises the wheel with the seal of glorious Great Bliss and the syllable hūṃ, and in the same way consecrates oneself. Beginning from the circumambulation, one draws the maṇḍala as prescribed. With the secret hook together with hūṃ, one dissolves the woman who is to be thoroughly accomplished into the wheel, performs the union, praises, and carries out the invitation and the rest. Having presented the welcoming water, one seals with the four seals, confers empowerment, and then carries out the rite of the five vases and so on. One should then worship the Blessed One and his retinue with the offerings of flowers and the like belonging to this very secret suchness of one's own.

"Homage to the self of supreme joy" refers to Vajrasattva, and it is construed with "grant me the supreme pledge." "Grant me accomplishment as the sublime of the tathāgatas, oṃ hūṃ" is the suchness of flowers, which has become the essential nature of mirror-like wisdom. "Sublime pledge" refers to incense, which has become the essential nature of the wisdom of equality. "Grant all the great offerings, oṃ hūṃ" refers to the lamp, which has become the essential nature of discriminating wisdom. Again, "great offering" and so on should be understood to be the unguent, which has become the essential nature of all-accomplishing wisdom.

"This seal" means by the seal of the secret tantra. "All offerings" are the offerings of flowers and the like. "With these" means with one's own secret suchness just explained: one should make offering with the goddesses of flowers and the rest and with one's own seal, as appropriate. If there is none, one should do it oneself. Then one should take on all the disciples.

The sequence there is as follows. In the maṇḍala as it is prescribed, one completes everything by the preceding sequence up to the culmination of the offering, and performs the prostrations and the rest. Having entered oneself, one empowers oneself as master of all, and meditates on the Blessed One from whom the various arises, together with his retinue, as facing one. Then, that oneself has the nature of the vajra, is pure by essential nature, and through selflessness has become one with all the deities by equality, one recites in order oṃ vajra ātmako'haṃ, oṃ sva bhā śuddho'haṃ, oṃ sarva samayo'haṃ. With heightened conviction in oneself, one sits there in the center, and while singing songs that follow the music, one should practice, placing speech, hand emblems, seals and the rest in thorough equipoise.

When one wishes to carry out the subsequent practice, one completes everything up to the culmination of one's own empowerment, and so on. One makes supplication in order to admit the disciples, performs the self-consecration and the rest, and places the disciple in union with the lord of the maṇḍala, saying śiri sukha vajra satva subhaga stvaṃ mahaṃ. Preceded by dance, with palms joined and placed on the crown of the head, one should offer the path song together with a garland of flowers, and admit the disciple by reciting the heart mantra of the Vajra Lord. One should also recite all the actions of the rite.

Likewise, in the maṇḍalas of Vairocana and the others too, it is preceded by the exhortation with the song beginning "Thoroughly united in emptiness." Again, by that same sequence one generates oneself like Vajrasattva, and then makes directly manifest the sign of one's own lord as it is. By the appropriate union one sets out the supreme king of maṇḍalas as it should be, completes everything up to the exhortation by the song of self-arising, and then admits the disciples. All the admission of disciples and the rest according to the rites of the Guhyasamāja and other tantras should also be looked to here.

"Buddha jewel": because the buddha himself is precious, he is the buddha jewel. Where it says "as prescribed," the empowerments of the garland and the rest should also be understood here. The tasting of the great red should be looked to after the admission. "And also by the hosts of the ḍākinīs' illusion" means by the Sealer, the Deluder, and the Harmer, in that order. "Then" means after the empowerment. "Through union with unobstructed dharmatā" means through the cause of nonabiding nirvāṇa.

On "And abandoning even the nature of self":
"oneself becomes utterly uncovered" is the specification of the self. Having understood that all the aggregates and so on, and form and so on, are mere designations, to abandon them completely is here to be without dependence. Not tormenting oneself, by the conduct explained elsewhere as the way to be happy through enjoying objects such as forms in a manner that accords with meditation, is not tormenting oneself with austerities.

"Enjoy happily, as you please"
means: enjoy the enjoyments of forms and the rest in a way that accords with meditative concentration, and be one-pointed.
"In all the enjoyments of yoga"
means enjoyment properly endowed with the pride of oneself as the deity. "Fearless in any way" means having nothing to fear from anything. "Delight" means "play!" Against the thought, "By enjoying objects there will be the fear of evil destinies after death," the text says the passage beginning "There is no sin." Why is that? It is what is said by the passage beginning "Pledge." "Pledge" is having become nondual with the deity. "Hard to transgress" is said because all misdeeds have turned into the nature of merit, so a transgression is hard to bring about. "Accomplishment" is the awakening of a buddha, perfected through the forms of Supreme Bliss and the others. "Companions" are the chief goddesses, Supreme Bliss and the rest. "Lovely" means those who are delightful. "By the very real" means by the pride of one's own deity. "With yoga" means endowed with the four yogas. "With the secret" means endowed with that very wisdom of the secret seal. "Seal" here means the same as mudrā. "Then" means after the seal and the rest have been given.

"Established as master of all"
means abiding as master of the maṇḍalas of Vajrasattva, Vairocana and the others. "Dharma" means in a manner not contrary to the Dharma.

"The glorious way of Vajrasattva"
is the sequence of the maṇḍala of Vajrasattva.
"Fully accomplishing all the rites"
means that the buddhas, Vajradhara and the others, grant the accomplishments of the maṇḍala.
"Fully applied in all the rites":
this shows it to be a common rite. "Because of its essential nature" means by way of the supreme great sublime suchness. If one objects, "In that case, knowing the maṇḍalas and rites of the buddhas and the rest becomes pointless," the text says the passage beginning "And the applications of various actions." By the passage beginning "That very yoga," it shows the means of Vairocana and the others.
"Of union in the nature of reflections"
means the essential nature of reflections. "Union of seals" means by holding the seal of the deity. "Dance" means "meditate!"
"By pledges of various kinds"
means with all the rest of the body. "Thus" means by the rite just explained. By the passage beginning "The buddha field," it shows the greatness of the five lords. By "those and so on," it shows them to be immeasurable. Again, by "those and so on" and what follows, it sums up.

This is the commentary on the sixth chapter, the Rite of the Maṇḍala of the Ḍākinīs' Magical Display in the Union with All Buddhas.

This maṇḍala and rite concern what is endowed with all the maṇḍalas and the wisdom of the seals. In order to show the Blessed One as the essential nature of the rite of all the maṇḍalas and of the wisdom of the seals, the text says the passage beginning "Then, further." "Vajrasattva, the goddess Śrī" means of the Buddha. "Fully emanating all the wheels" means all the maṇḍala rites. Some read "in the various caves"; there it should be applied to the various kinds of places of ascetic observance.
"Of attire of various kinds"
means of different kinds of costume. "By various means of accomplishment" means by implements of various kinds. "Red lotus" means red like a lotus. "Whatever place" means pleasure gardens and the like. "Whatever the wheels are like" means square and so on. "Whatever the arrangement" means whatever the arrangement of gateways, enclosures and the like. "Whatever beings" means whatever disciples. "Whatever the arrangement of the retinue": by completing the rite in the same way as the deities are arranged, the maṇḍala is made and emanated by the supreme Victor, and whatever merit there is in seeing that maṇḍala, the same holds for one drawn now.
"Accomplishing the equality of all directions"
means: do not cling to auspicious and inauspicious directions. "East" means placing the buddhas in the eastern direction and the others. To the question, "If ultimately maṇḍalas cannot be conceived, how does merit arise here too?" the text says the passage beginning "Maṇḍala." "The nature of one's own deity" means by the form of one's own deity. "Accomplished" means complete.
"Entirely various and fortunate"
means fully endowed in every way with all the goddesses. "Possessor of the entirely various wheel" means endowed with a retinue various in every way and complete: this is Vajrasattva.
"Because it is accomplished entirely variously"
means that, like the maṇḍala explained before, the interior of this one too is completed in every aspect, with four corners and so on.

This teaches the following. As with all the maṇḍalas, the interior too is made with four corners and so on, and one should lay out the vajra lines, to be beautified with eight pillars. Outside it, a second square with gates, drawn in a single color, is the entirely various maṇḍala: the maṇḍala of Vajrasattva. Outside all of these, the remaining vajra maṇḍala, measuring half the maṇḍala and beautified with eight pillars, should be regarded as drawn in its own single color. The interior of the maṇḍala is shown in the same way. On all the outer sides too one should make squares as before. "Maṇḍala" means that on those round ones too one lays the vajra cords and makes a square outline outside. "Of the vajra bearer" means of Heruka. "Like a half moon" is the wind maṇḍala, surrounded by a vajra fence and on the outside surrounded by a square outline. "Lotus shape" means like an eight-petaled lotus, surrounded by a garland of lotuses, and on the outside surrounded by a square outline. "Jewel shape" means like an eight-faceted jewel, surrounded by jewel tendrils, and on the outside surrounded by a square outline. "As one wishes" means as one likes. "Whatever is available" means with whatever one obtains. Among these five maṇḍalas, the maṇḍala of this one is not fixed, so glorious Paramāśva should be placed in its position. This applies up to the words "that very one becomes his maṇḍala."

"Whatever one desires" means all that one wishes in fierce rites, magnetizing and the like, and rites of increase. "Whatever the time" means that in the morning, at night, at midday and in the afternoon respectively, one enters in turn the maṇḍalas of Vajrasattva and the others. "At ease" means empowering disciples and so on without the water, the binding and the like. "Vajra" is the crossed vajra. Because he holds it in his hand, glorious Paramāśva too is so called.
"Not having entered any maṇḍala"
means never having entered maṇḍalas.
"And having abandoned the pledges"
means not guarding the pledges. "The true secret" is the fortunate master, fortunate through the very fortune explained below. Why is that? Because by him the stable yogin without beginning or end is apprehended; this is construed with "he is not to be abandoned."
"In the entirely various fortunate one"
refers to Vajrasattva; he is fortunate because he has accomplished himself. "Endowed with selfless dharmatā" means being wholly intent on all phenomena as emptiness.

If one asks what distinguishes him: he is hostile to the pledges, that is, he is one who is hostile to practicing in accordance with them. For that very reason he is ill-fortuned.

"Buddha" is Vairocana. He has thoroughly accomplished existence; he is fortunate because he has perfected nonabiding nirvāṇa. "Completely and utterly passed into nirvāṇa" means having attained nirvāṇa without remainder. "Glorious Vajrapāṇi" is Heruka.

"In order to gather the supreme and sublime wealth"
shows that by the power of his own meditative absorption he gathers great wealth. "Austerity" is thoroughly tormenting the body.

"Having gathered all the supreme kingdoms"
refers to the lord of fierce practices. "Sages" are the wondrous ones. "Master of all" is the yogin of Vajrasattva, as it is said:

"Through union with glorious Vajrasattva,
one is joined to the identity of all the buddhas.
By the sign of the buddha seal
he is called master of all."

"All bliss" is Vajrasattva; it means accomplishing his very nature as one's own. The word "disciples and so on" here means sons and the rest. "By the one entering" means by the disciple. "With the right hand" means holding the fee. Having explained the secret reality, one should have him enter.

"To the entirely various guru"
this word means the vajra master of the maṇḍala. "That" means what is hard to give away and so on. "Whatever one could wish for" means what one delights in.

"The fee for the supreme awakening of a buddha"
means attaining the state of that which is the union of Heruka, glorious Vajradhara; it is by that fee, the fee that is offered to glorious Vajradhara. "Lotus-bearer" is the fee for the master of the lotus family. "Vajrasūrya" is that for the master of the jewel family. "Richly adorned" means furnished with the supreme continents and their array. "King of horses" is that for the master of the maṇḍala of Hayagrīva.

"Empowered with the buddha-jewel"
means that the buddha itself is the jewel, and one is empowered with it. To show this, the passage beginning "the crown of the head" is spoken. Because one is empowered as master of all with the crown ornament of the five buddhas and the crown ornament of a garland of skulls (kapālas), the words should be construed as "the skull itself is the jewel," or "the buddha itself is the crown ornament." The distinction in wearing this crown ornament on the head should be seen to be just this. "Made of a single jewel" means marked with a single buddha and marked with a single skull.

"Likewise adorned with various ornaments"
means interspersed everywhere with various leaves and tendrils. To make this clear, the passage beginning "Buddha" is spoken. "Buddha empowerment" is the diadem marked with a buddha. "Lotus empowerment" is the diadem marked with a skull. With these, all the disciples are empowered by all the yoginīs, Supreme Bliss and the rest.

"Jewel vajra" is a vajra adorned with jewels. "Is a vajra" means marked with a blazing vajra. "Buddha body" means marked with the body of Amitābha. "Is a gem" means a jewel together with a vajra. "Buddha-jewel": the buddha-jewel is the buddha and jewel, the two diadems of the two, Vajrasattva and Vairocana. "Lotus" is the empowerment by Amitābha. "Vajra" is the empowerment of Heruka. "Wish-fulfilling gem" is the empowerment of Vajrasūrya.

"The supreme of all for Paramāśva"
refers to the master of Paramāśva. "Emblem seals" are the vajra and the rest. To show their divisions, the passage beginning "entirely" is spoken. The fortunate one endowed with entirely various aspects is "the entirely various fortunate one." "Of the self-arisen ones" means of Vajrasattva. The three pledges taught here should be understood to be the seal of vajra and bell, the seal of the eye, and the seal of bow and arrow.

If one asks whether there are only these, the passage beginning "space" is spoken: the nature of the goddesses, Supreme Bliss and the rest, endless like the expanse of space, abiding in the form of bow and arrow, is the emblem seal of the Blessed One himself. "Supreme" too should be understood to be that very thing. To show this, the passage beginning "bow and arrow" is spoken. The bow together with its arrow is Supreme Bliss. "The fortunate one" is the male organ. "Excellent fortune" is the seal of the bhaga; the fortunate one placed upon it is Wondrous Bliss.

The skull, the khaṭvāṅga and the crossed vajra are those of Tārā. A skull bowl, and a crossed vajra with the figure of Amitābha placed upon it, are those of Śiśi. A base pierced by an arrow, and a bow, are those of Buddha Awakening. An eight-spoked wheel, the wheel of dharmatā, is that of Māmakī. A five-pronged vajra is that of Trailokyavijayā. A wish-granting tree entwined with a creeper is that of the Creeper of All Desires. A green horse's head, raised up upon a lotus and neighing, is that of Horse-Face. A boar's tusk is that of Vajramukha. The disks of the moon and the sun are those of Vajralocanā. A crossed vajra in the middle of a skull with the figure of a buddha placed upon it is that of Vetālī, who is wholly immersed in ashes. A vajra flute is that of the One-Stringed Goddess. A lute marked with a crossed vajra is that of the Lute Goddess. The mukunda drum is that of the Lovely Goddess. The clay drum is that of the Clay-Drum Goddess. A basket of flowers, a full incense burner, a lamp stand and a bowl of perfume are, in order, those of the Flower Goddess, the Incense Goddess, the Lamp Goddess and the Perfume Goddess. The seal of the monastic robe, the begging bowl, the ringing staff and the volume of the sublime Dharma is that of the Blessed One Vairocana.

The seal of the tongue, the small cymbals, the five-pronged vajra and the jewel basket are, in order, those of She Who Proclaims All the Buddhas, the Ornamented One, Samantabhadrī and the Goddess of the Formidable Basket. The image of a buddha, the mirror, the vajra hail together with clouds, and the seal of the eye are, in order, those of She Who Possesses the Limbs of All the Buddhas, the Form Goddess Fair-Face, the Great Cloud Goddess and the Eye of Space. The blazing vajra, the skull together with the khaṭvāṅga, and the skull bowl filled with the great red should be understood. "Great compassion" is the lotus of the heart; that is the great flesh. These seals are those of glorious Heruka.

The sharp arrow, the hook, the liquor and the water are, in order, those of Gaurī, Caurī, Pramohā and Vetālī. The creeper stirred by the wind, the cloth and the blazing fire are, in order, those of She Who Makes Swell, Caṇḍālī and the Defiled One. Those of the Blessed One Padmanarteśvara are variegated, namely red, green, white, yellow and vermilion lotuses. For She Who Gazes, Tārā, the Netted One, Bhṛkuṭī, She Who Dwells on the Lotus and the Sovereign Lady of Various Lotuses, there are, in order, the pericarp of a lotus, a fist, an eight-spoked wheel, a trident, a makara banner and a five-pronged vajra. Those of the Blessed One Vajrasūrya are the bow, the arrow, the sword and the disk of the sun. The Goddess of Empowerment, the Jewel Treasure, the Supreme Banner and the Great Offering should be understood to hold, in order, a jewel diadem, a treasure vase, a banner, and vessels filled with every ornament, pendant garlands and the rest. Those of glorious Paramāśva are the variegated lotus and the crossed vajra, two kinds of seal.

The chapter on knowing the seals of all the maṇḍalas: the commentary on the seventh chapter.

Since accomplishment does not arise without offering, in order to teach, as the very nature of the chapter on the pledge, the Blessed One who is the union of all offerings, the glorious Fortunate One, the passage beginning "Then, further" is spoken. "Then, further" means after the knowledge of the seals of the maṇḍala. Since the deities of glorious Vajrasattva are Vairocana and the rest, "glorious Vajrasattva and the deities" means the deities of glorious Vajrasattva; and because in all their aspects they are entirely various and have become supreme without exception, they are "supreme, entirely various in all aspects." "All offerings are emanation" means emanating all offerings.

This teaches the following: having completed the requisites for gathering in the maṇḍala and so on, up to the offerings made by others, it is to be done with the seals of homage. Its sequence is this. Having sung the song "Glorious Vajrasattva of bliss" and performed the vajra dance, one holds the vajra and bell with the left hand in a vajra fist with a graceful air, makes the right hand into the three-banner gesture, and, with reverence for the place and in the supreme raised manner, bows the head to the left with moving eyes; reciting oṃ ādi bhū hūṃ, with a stirred mind one should oneself pay homage to Vajrasattva.

To show the other proud ones themselves, the passage beginning "Fierce" is spoken. "With Upendra" means with Viṣṇu. "With Brahmā" means with Prajāpati. "Having done well" means having done. Having performed the song and dance beginning "Of the numbered portions supremely clear," and turning the vajra palms, reciting oṃ ādi bhū oṃ, with a stirred mind one should pay homage.

"Various aspects": having performed the song and the vajra dance, one places the little fingers, from Heruka's fist, at the mouth like fangs; reciting oṃ ādi bhū ha ha ha ha ha ru lu ru lu hūṃ, with the eyebrows raised and moving, one should pay homage laughing aloud.

"The palms like a lotus with its middle open"
means that one makes the lotus by making the palms hollow. "Endowed with one's own union" means union with one's own deity. To show that lotus, the passage beginning "thumbs and little fingers" is spoken: having made the thumbs and little fingers like four petals spread open, they are then brought together by that; that is, the thumbs and little fingers, made into four petals, are to be brought together. This teaches the following: toward all the Tathāgatas and the rest, "various aspects" and so on: having performed the song and the vajra dance, one joins the palms as a budding lotus and places at the heart the seal of the two thumbs and the little fingers as a four-petaled lotus; reciting oṃ ādhi bhū hrīḥ, one should pay homage to Padmanarteśvara.

Having performed the rite with the song beginning "Of all the kingdoms of the three realms of existence," one lowers all the limbs to the ground and joins the jewel palms in order at the heart and the other places; reciting oṃ ādhi bhū hrīḥ, one should pay homage.

Having performed the song and dance beginning "Of a thousand haughty ones," holding the graceful vajra posture, reciting oṃ ādhi bhū and so on, and others, with the eyes closed one should pay homage to Paramāśva.

To teach the seals of offering, the passage beginning "The causes of dance" is spoken. "Of the action" means viskambhi and the rest. "Showing the modes" means the four called the limbed, the worded, the necklace and the sattva. "With grace" means with emptiness.

"Dancing in the manner that fits"
means engaging in actions in conformity. "With various circling dances" means with the dance of all the disciples assembled. "All offerings" means the great offering of all the maṇḍalas. To show this, the passage beginning "the right and left hands" is spoken. "Turning beautifully" means the supreme dance with a graceful air. "By this" means by what has just been explained.

"Having sealed with the seal" means: having first performed the song and dance beginning "Glorious Great Bliss, Vajrasattva," one holds the fingers of the left hand in a fist and makes the right hand into the three-banner gesture; or else, with the great seal of glorious Vajrasattva and with the song of the syllable hūṃ, one should make a perfect offering and raise it up.

Now the offering by the union of Supreme Bliss and the rest is taught. "Its" means by the seal of taking. "Having raised it" means by the practice of binding firmly. "At the crown of the head" means it should be placed at the heart, facing upward. "Should be placed" means having made the joined palms steady.

"When it is turned toward the cheeks"
means that, preceded by dance, it is circled toward the two cheeks.

"Performing the dance that brings all down"
means dancing in such a way that one is oneself fully endowed with the great seals of all the goddesses, Supreme Bliss and the rest. "Then" connects with what came above. "All offerings" means the offering of the great seals of the goddesses, Supreme Bliss and the rest. "Should be presented" means it should be made steady.

To teach the offering to Vairocana, the passage beginning "the right and left hands" is spoken. Having performed the song beginning "One could find the number of the world systems or of the atoms," and the vajra dance, and having properly turned the vajra palms from the left, one makes offering to the Blessed One Vairocana with the song of the syllable oṃ and with the great seals of She Who Proclaims All the Buddhas and the rest.

To teach the offering to Heruka, the passage beginning "the right and left hands" is spoken. With the song beginning "Various aspects," one dances, and again with Gaurī and the rest in order; then, preceded by releasing the vajra palms upward and binding them, one should make offering to Heruka with the great seals of Gaurī and the rest, letting out the vajra laughter ha ha ha ha ru lu ru lu hūṃ.

As for Padmanarteśvara: preceded by the song of gazing, beginning "All the Tathāgatas," and by dance, one claps the right and left palms with the song of that syllable, and makes offering to Padmanarteśvara with the great seals of the goddess She Who Gazes and the rest.

As for Vajrasūrya: preceded by the song beginning "The kingdom of all the three realms" and by the Goddess of Empowerment and the rest, one joins the palms and, by the practice of bending the thumbs and index fingers, binds the jewel joined palms; with the song of the syllable dhri one makes offering to Vajrasūrya with the great seals of Vajrasūrya's Goddess of Empowerment and the rest.

Paramāśva too: having danced in order with the songs of his own goddesses, beginning "A thousand who show devotion," one binds the seals of the maṇḍala offering just as one is oneself, and with any other heart mantra one wishes, one should make offering to Paramāśva and the rest with their great seals.

To teach the seal of the offering of flowers and the rest, which was spoken of in the words "having made the offering of flowers and the rest," the passage beginning "scattering upward" is spoken. This teaches the following: one should apply oneself to scattering upward and so on with the offering seals just as explained before. "Full" means furnished with the requisites of flowers and the rest; "empty" means lacking them. Or else, "whatever is available" means having found any one of these in general.

"Equal in pledge" means being endowed with it. "Accomplishing" means accomplishing the deity, preceded by the heart mantras, the seals and meditation. It means having made offering with all the supreme offerings. "Union" means perfect endowment. Because he has accomplished himself, he is the Fortunate One, namely Vajrasattva; he himself is the glorious one. "Pledge" means not transgressing. The words that teach it are called the chapter.

The chapter of glorious Fortunate One, the union of all offerings: the commentary on the eighth chapter.

Even when yogins have fulfilled their own aims and those of others by accomplishing maṇḍalas and so on, they still need to be endowed with knowledge of the seals together with music. Therefore, in order to teach the Blessed One as the very nature of the pledge of the Entirely Various One, the passage beginning "Then, further" is spoken. Here, having accomplished oneself in the Dharma maṇḍala and the rest, one should set out and meditate on the great seals of Vajrasattva and the rest, endowed with their respective colors, postures, hand emblems and seals, just as they are.

What is taught up to "the seal from which all arises" is this. "In all bliss" means unsurpassed bliss and mental happiness. Being attached to it is "red." Therefore it is "because all accomplishments are purified," that is, the awakening mind is purified. By these two causes he is like the full moon and his body's veins are red. Attachment to all who are to be trained is "all passion": by great compassion he is attached to them and delights them. For that reason he has "light blazing with various reds"; the words are to be construed as "he in whom there is light blazing in various ways with many lines of red color," up to "a red maṇḍala of light, within which red, green, blue, yellow and white blaze supremely like atoms."

"Buddha jewel" means having the diadem of the five buddhas, which is supreme over all. "Not the wealth of others" means what others do not have. "The water of the gods" is the wisdom of emptiness; one is purified by it. "With power" means by the nature of the greatness of merit and wisdom. "The entities" means by way of the aspiration that is the nature of one's own extraordinary resolve. "It is accomplished by its very nature" means it is spontaneously and completely accomplished.

For that reason, the crown ornament of a garland of skulls, the crest jewel of a buddha and the khaṭvāṅga too, because she has power over the training of beings, she is "the Lokeśvara of the wholly afflicted"; that is, she who is accomplished in bringing under control the wholly afflicted world system, she who is perfectly united with glorious Vajrasattva, is "the glorious union of Vajrasattva," abiding in union and subsequent union.

"United with all the goddesses"
teaches perfect union; for that very reason she is "the entirely various Fortunate One."

"The ḍākinī accomplishes all"
means that Vajrasattva, the identity of all yogas, is fully accomplished. "Sealed" means by the Sealer. "Deluded" means by the Deluder. "Raised" means by She Who Increases Life. "By this seal" means by the seal of Vajrasattva. "Perfectly joined in supreme joy" means joined in supreme joy through perfect union having the character of union and subsequent union. The utmost limit of bliss, which is the nature of glorious Vajrasattva, who became the Fortunate One by accomplishing himself through it, and which is Supreme Bliss, is the perfection of play.

"Through the union of right and left"
means the posture in which the right foot presses down on the left foot; by it that blissful attire is blissful just as it is.

Now, to teach the great seals of Supreme Bliss and the rest, the passage beginning "the color of the moon" is spoken. "Smiling with the color of the moon" means a smiling face the color of the moon, belonging to one whose face is like the disk of the moon.

"All accomplishments are pure"
means the awakening mind is purified. And because great compassion is purified, she is red and white in the middle of the thighs.

"The supreme hand like a blue leaf"
means: it is the hand and it is supreme, and leaf and blue are "blue leaf"; so in order, the hand and its tip resemble a leaf and blue, and that is what it is called, up to the words "the color of the hand is blue, and its tip is the color of a leaf." Why is that? Because she is called "likewise the lord of bliss": Vajrasattva has gained mastery over the lord of bliss. The touch that produces his bliss is the touch of bliss, and she has gained mastery over this. "And the thighs white and yellow" means that below the color is white. That is the various colors of divine substance, the fifth color.

If one asks whether the colors white and the rest, as taught, should be understood as belonging to each of those four goddesses respectively: these teach the colors and so on in general, by way of all the limbs.

"Blazing, with light blazing in various ways"
is said of one who is endowed with light thoroughly stirred by various blazes.

"Abiding on various lotuses"
means that her seat is placed on a various lotus.

"Firmly abiding in total purity"
means the wisdom that takes as its object the natural purity of all phenomena is total purity; because in the ultimate sense she has become its very nature, she firmly abides in total purity. Or else, "firmly abiding in total purity" is Vajrasattva, and to abide firmly in him means to be connected with him.

"Buddha" means empowered by a single buddha. "Lotus" means empowered by a single skull. The supreme jewels and the rest belonging to these and the rest are so called: one who has ornaments and garments of supreme jewels of various kinds, buddha, lotus and the rest, is called

"Supreme with buddha, lotus and the rest,
with ornaments and garments of jewel and lotus."

"Of all the buddhas assembled"
means that all the buddhas have become assembled; by this, the wholly afflicted world system, the Sahā world and the rest, becomes like an ornament. "And the action" here means holding loosely. "Repelling" means holding the bow in a manner of praise at one's own heart and repelling, with bliss, mental happiness and the like, what is opposed to equality. "Hārītī" means the right hand stretched out and the left drawn in, and so on.

"With the seal of embracing oneself"
means with the left hand in a graceful pose. "Seizing all the limbs" is the activity of all the limbs. "Accomplished" means thoroughly accomplished, up to "skilled." This teaches the following: acting skillfully in the activities and the rest, she holds the bow with the left hand in a graceful pose and abides in the manner of drawing the arrow with the right hand. Bringing the Fortunate One, glorious Vajrasattva, to the utmost limit of Supreme Bliss with the arrow and bow of Supreme Bliss, through being perfectly joined in supreme joy, is what is called play.

To teach the great seal of Wondrous Bliss, the passage beginning "various aspects" is spoken. Vajrasattva, who is marked by the pledge of various aspects, that is, by various emanations, is so called; being attached to the pledge of various aspects, she has become impassioned. By that bliss she descends into him and fully enters the continuum of the other. "The seal of embracing oneself" is the graceful left hand. Because that is supreme, she abides embracing the great supreme deity of the center, the one of whom it is spoken; this is "the great supreme deity of the center." By the distinction of the preceding words, the man in question is connected: with the left hand she gracefully holds the bhaga and the liṅga, and with the right she embraces the lord out of delight, and so on, and they sit on a single seat. "The lord of great bliss" is the one who has mastery over great bliss, namely Vajrasattva. "Supreme joy" is the embrace, by pledge, of the Blessed One Vajrasattva, whose nature is perfect union; therefore it is Supreme Bliss, and the utmost limit of that bliss is called play.

To teach the great seal of Tārā, the passage beginning "sacred thread" is spoken.

"In the manner of a sacred thread"
means in the manner of wearing a brahmin's thread. The khaṭvāṅga marked at its tip with a three-pronged vajra is the vajra khaṭvāṅga; her left hand holds it together with a skull. "The lotus kapāla and crossed vajra" means that, since it is set together with a twelve-pronged vajra, it is the supreme crossed vajra with a lotus.

That is the seal of embracing oneself, with the right hand proudly poised and the crossed vajra together with a lotus. The words are construed as "with the seal of embracing oneself": she sits holding the crossed vajra with the right hand in a graceful pose, and with the left hand holding the vajra khaṭvāṅga in the manner of a sacred thread, and so on. "Perfectly joined in supreme joy": the Fortunate One, the glorious vajra, is Vajrasattva in the form of the khaṭvāṅga; because she is endowed with him, she raises him up perfectly. Therefore it is called the play of Supreme Bliss.

To teach the great seal of Śiśi, the passage beginning "Of the perfect buddha" is spoken. "The perfect buddha" is Amitāyus. "Accomplished" means seated. "Skull bowl" means the kapāla in which the perfect buddha is seated. "The seal of embracing oneself" means with the left hand proudly poised. "The great supreme deity of the center" means that, likewise resting on the left hip, she holds the skull bowl, the kapāla on whose crossed vajra the image of a buddha is seated, and with the right she praises the lord and embraces him, and so on. "Perfectly joined in supreme joy, at the time of the Fortunate One, the vajra" means the skull bowl of the perfect buddha is accomplished; therefore she plays with that Supreme Bliss as well. "The root" is the principal, the central square. "The host of seals" means the hosts of seals that are there.

To teach the great seal of Buddha Awakening, the passage beginning "as in the treatise" is spoken.

"In conformity with the treatise"
means in conformity with the science of archery. "The glorious perfection" is the posture of ease, and with it she dances.

"When the four arrows are let fly"
means letting fly the four arrows that are the very nature of the four doors of liberation, emptiness and the rest. Because she has become victorious over the four-headed Great Brahmā, whose nature is the four māras, the māra of the afflictions and the rest, "may she be victorious over the four māras!" It should be understood that she abides having pierced with the four arrows the heads of four-faced Brahmā. Therefore glorious Vajrasattva, who pierces the heads of Great Brahmā, is so called.

To teach the great seal of the Goddess of the Dharma Wheel, the passage beginning "raising the foot" is spoken. "Raising the foot" means raising it to the outside of the foot. "Lowering" means doing the same with the left as well. "Side by side" means both bending inward. "Offering" means placing the right one above, and with it making a stance.

"When the seal of the wheel is extolled"
means that one places the wheel's hub on the tip of the middle finger of the right hand and holds it up; then, through the practice of raising the foot and so on, one raises the right foot, and it should be understood that one holds the wheel aloft and extolled in just that way. Some, however, think that raising the foot and the rest are, in order, the right-extended, the left-extended, the round posture and the level posture, the four stances of the feet.

To teach the great seal of Trailokyavijayā, the passage beginning "And the action" is spoken. The passage beginning "And the action" refers here to what is explained in the treatises on dance. "With the seal of the various" means with the seal of her own hand emblem. This teaches the following: following the sequence of the action and so on, she dances holding the seal of the trident marked with a vajra.

To teach the great seal of the Mistress of Desire, the passage beginning "creeper" is spoken. "Any creeper whatever": it should be understood that the Creeper of All Desires abides holding any creeper whatever, of mango and the like, in the right hand, with the left hand in a graceful pose.

To teach the great seal of Horse-Face, the passage beginning "tasting" is spoken.

"Having done as though drawing in and tasting"
means by saying hūṃ, preceded by the manner of tasting.

"Vajra-Face too, hūṃ at the nose"
and so on: she abides preceded by the manner of drawing in and tasting.

"Looking with fixed and longing eyes"
means that the Vajra Lamp abides holding the disks of the moon and the sun, looking in the manner of tasting everything.

"By tasting the identity of all"
means "the identity of all," that is, tasting with all things. This teaches the following: Vetālī abides holding the skull bowl, the kapāla, with the figure of a buddha set upon a crossed vajra. The great seals of the rest have been explained here already and are clear in the root text itself, so they are not explained in detail.

"Then this wisdom of the seal"
means the wisdom of the great seal just explained. "Song" refers to what will be explained below.

"By thoroughly accomplishing existence"
means attaining nonabiding nirvāṇa. By this, having danced with play, one should bind oneself in the maṇḍala of Vajrasattva as the great seal, just as it is.

To teach the song, the passage beginning "Vajrasattva" is spoken: "O glorious Vajrasattva, you who have become the very nature of uninterrupted bliss and mental happiness, since you and I have arisen from a nature that is nondual, please make it manifest for my sake, accomplish it, and let me realize and fully understand it." "The ḍākinī of various illusion" means various emanations; by them,

"Supreme one who tames in various ways the continua of beings,
please accomplish the accomplishment for me"

means through the nature of Supreme Bliss. "Make it manifest!" means "O you whose very nature is bliss." The bliss that is the very nature of all the Tathāgatas is the great bliss of all awakening. The ḍākinī of illusion whose identity is that is an emanation of Tārā. She is the various Supreme Bliss; the meaning is "accomplish the aims that are the very nature of Tārā!" The passage beginning a la la la la ho means "please perform the various dances of the Tathāgatas," and is construed with "fully display the emanation of Śiśi!"

"jaḥ hūṃ baṃ hoḥ" is the urging, in order, of Supreme Bliss, Tārā and the rest. "Emanate as a pair" likewise urges Supreme Bliss, following the order of "Ah, supreme bliss!" "Hūṃ, please make it shine!" belongs to Tārā. "Please take the disciple into your care!" belongs to Śiśi. "All the Tathāgatas, the various ḍākinīs of illusion" is spoken by the remaining goddesses.

To show the arising of Vairocana, the passage beginning "that very yoga" is spoken. "That very" means Vajrasattva. "All liberation" is both the mundane and the supramundane; its characteristic is renunciation, going forth from existence. Its purity is the realization that persons and phenomena are without self. Its completion is to abide fully in the seal of the buddhas. "The ultimate" is the realm of phenomena (dharmadhātu), because that is pure. "Free of attachment" means turned away from every object of apprehension. The words "and so forth" mean free of aversion, untouched by anger, not apprehending any clinging to things, and free of delusion, that is, having abandoned conceptual thoughts of a self and the like. Those that are free of attachment and the rest, and yet possess great attachment, are described as "because free of attachment and the rest, with great attachment." Being free of attachment and the rest, they are white; and being of great attachment, that is, possessing great compassion, they have a radiance clear with many colors.

"The jewel Dharma" is the jewel whose very nature is Dharma. "Surpassing that" means other. The awakening mind is the mind whose nature is awakening, the mind that enters into awakening. Its meaning is the Dharma whose nature is to teach the Great Vehicle, and that itself is the vajra jewel. Because this one possesses it, it is said:

"Therefore the Great Vehicle, the awakening mind,
the Victor who possesses the vajra jewel."

The great mudrā of the Blessed One is this: with the left hand clenched into a fist, one touches all its fingers with the thumb and the two index fingers of the right. The line "clear in the perfection of wisdom" shows just this, for this mudrā shows the five aggregates and the five families, without exception, to be selfless. The words beginning "the right limb" teach the manner of sitting with legs crossed.

"Having clenched the vajra fist" is the great mudrā of She Who Proclaims All the Buddhas: the thumb of the right hand is tucked inside, and the back of the tip of the thumb is pressed against the base of the ring finger, making a vajra fist; then the left hand covers and conceals it from above. Others say that She Who Proclaims All the Buddhas and the rest hold the mudrā bound, with the vajra fist covered by a stainless cloth.

Extending the little finger from that same vajra fist is the mudrā of Heruka; it is made with the right hand and should be covered. "Likewise" means that doing the same with the left hand is the mudrā of the Supremely Victorious Lady.

"The lotus woman's fist" means this: making a fist and joining the tips of the middle finger, the index finger and the thumb is the lotus fist; made with the right hand and covered with the left, it is the mudrā of Samantabhadra.

"And the beautiful jewel fist" means this: making a fist and joining the tips is the jewel fist; bound with the right hand and covered with the left, it is the mudrā of the Inexhaustible Casket.

"Having bound the buddha fist" means this: placing the tips of all the fingers on the thumb, made with the right hand and covered with the left, it is the mudrā of She Who Possesses the Limbs of All the Buddhas. Others say that here she has a buddha placed at her heart, armlets of perfect buddhas on her two upper arms, a principal buddha set at her throat as a necklace and a garland, and images of the Buddha on all her heads; and because she is blessed by holding a buddha in her right hand, she is called She Who Possesses the Limbs of All the Buddhas. So it is explained in the oral instructions.

"Having bound the woman's mudrā" means this: binding a fist with the right hand and covering it with the left is the mudrā of the Fair-Faced Lady. Some say that the woman's mudrā is the left fist held at one's own heart in the manner of praise; holding it, she holds a mirror in her left hand and remains gazing with joy at the reflection of her own nature. Therefore, they say, she is called the Fair-Faced Lady.

All the fingers gathering in the thumb is the fist of Paramāśva; covered with the left hand, it is the mudrā of the Great Cloud Goddess. Making a fist and shaping the index finger and thumb into the form of an eye is the fist of the Eye of Accomplishment; bound with the right hand and covered with the left, it is the mudrā of the Eye of Space.

All these fists should be visualized as holding their own emblems inside. Their bodily colors, in order, are crystal, gold, red lotus, variegated, saffron, the color of a mirror, beryl, and yellow.

The great mudrās of the flute and the rest are explained in the maṇḍala of Vajrasattva. So here, and in other maṇḍalas too, just as for one's remaining deities, their great mudrās should likewise be visualized there. "Knowing the mudrās" means knowing the great mudrās just explained.

"The supreme song of the buddhas' emanation" is the song of the arising of the Tathāgatas. To teach these songs in order, the passage beginning "the one who raises" is spoken. "The one who raises" and "the various followers" have already been explained by what was taught before. This, then, is what it teaches: the second song is sung in the ṣaḍja mode, and a song is sung in the devatī mode and joined with this. Here, first, preceded by a dance performed with a graceful, swaying gait, all those great mudrās should be bound.

The passage beginning "many world-realms" refers to the one who possesses multitudes of bodies that pervade world-realms as numerous as the finest particles. Because he fully engages in all the aims of the Victors there, and clearly engages in all the aims of the Victors, as numerous as the finest particles, the aims of the Victors are as numerous as the finest particles; and this is construed with "perform the magical display of these!" That which, by multitudes of bodies emanated as numerous as particles, pervades all world-realms and enters each one individually, and through which, clearly and directly, as many as there are particles, it sports: that is what is so called. It is taught without a verb of action.

"Existence" is the nirvāṇa that does not abide, and that itself is great bliss. The invocation addressed to it is "by the various emanations of all the Tathāgatas," that is, the emanations of Vairocana and the others. "Perform the magical display!" means "dance beautifully!" "Ho" means "be pleased!" "O glorious one in union with Vairocana" is likewise a word of invocation, and "today, please bring me to full accomplishment" is the remainder of the words, to be supplied. The passage beginning "then the women" has already been explained.

To show the arising of glorious Heruka, the passage beginning "those very" is spoken. "That very" means Vajrasattva. "Hostile to the sacred pledge" means those who are untruthful, vicious and fierce. "The great mudrā" here is the Blessed One, as taught before. "The great garment" is the hide of a great beast. "The great pouring" means a single diadem of skulls: the diadem with a garland of skulls, marked by a vajra blazing all around. Some, however, take "pouring" in other maṇḍalas of the assembly to be the vajra mudrā, and teach that "the great garment" means holding the ash of the charnel ground.

By the hand emblems and the hands, they conceive of him as eight-armed, as follows. With two hands he holds a human hide in the manner of a banner. With two he holds the vajra mudrā in the manner of conferring empowerment. Further, with a right hand he brandishes a blazing five-pronged vajra, and with a left he holds a vajra khaṭvāṅga. Again, with a right he holds a bowl filled with mahāraktas, and with a left he holds a skull cup filled with mahāmaṃ. So they say. Others say that he is four-armed: with two hands he holds a garment of human hide, and the other two are as before.

"Vajra khaṭvāṃ" means a khaṭvāṅga marked with a three-pronged vajra, with a bell tied to it, whose sound jingles. "With the vajra khaṭvāṅga together with the vajra, sounding with the ringing of the bell": this is how the second is joined. Because he possesses these, he is called "the one with the vajra khaṭvāṅga and the sound of a ringing bell together with a vajra."

"Displaying the various mudrās" refers to his full engagement in performing the dance of the great mudrās. What is that great mudrā in the manner of the se ral kha, with the khaṭvāṅga leaning on the left shoulder as a support for the limb? In answer, the passage beginning "of the se ral kha" is spoken. It teaches this: the hand that holds the skull cup filled with mahāraktas rests against the vajra khaṭvāṅga, and the right hand, with three pennants, holds the five-pronged vajra with its garland of flames.

"By the joining of right and left" means by placing the right foot on the left thigh. "By performing the dance of graceful descent" means that it is brought together by this posture. "Half cross-legged" means by placing the left foot on the ground. "The foot with one's own thigh" means by joining it with the left thigh. "Having bound" means that again the right foot is placed upon the left, inserted between the thighs and pressed. "By that very" means the cross-legged posture.

"Then, comfortably binding the thighs" means this: having comfortably bound the left foot with the right foot of the one to be accomplished, who abides within it, one performs tying back and the rest, just as one wishes. "Illusion" is a means of the dance of joy. "Those" means the mudrās of the cross-legged posture.

"Then by moving the thighs and shoulders" means this: as the right foot of the woman visualized within it moves and shakes the left thigh, it is the descent of glorious Heruka; and the buddha ḍākinī arising from that union is what is so called.

In this maṇḍala, the one with a peaceful face is Gaurī. The one with a deluding face is Pramohā. The one with a face of great joy is She Who Makes Swell. The one with a transformed, wrathful face is Caṇḍālī. The one whose face is in the manner of the dead rising up is Ghasmarī. Their actions, too, pacifying, fierce, deluding, bringing about death and raising the dead, and their gazes, should be known in that order.

Likewise flowers, incense, liquor and water, young shoots, cloth and butter lamps: if each of these is offered to whatever is to be accomplished, it seals it.

"By one-pointed, even union" means that one also seals with the mudrā through a meditative concentration endowed with the union of Gaurī. "The manner of seeking" means that, endowed with the union of Caurī, one draws out the heart. "By the manner of bestowing bliss" means that, endowed with the union of the Greatly Intoxicated Lady, one deludes. Endowed with the union of Vetālī, one sprinkles the dead with water and raises them. Snapping the fingers, endowed with the union of She Who Makes Swell, one dances. Slowly drawing it upward, endowed with the union of Caṇḍālī, one kills. In the manner of breathing out, through the yoga of Ghasmarī, one devours.

Further, gazing with a leftward gaze, endowed with the union of Gaurī, one seals. Placing the mind in the heart of the one to be accomplished, with the syllable ho and the union of Caurī drawing it out with the hand, one extracts the heart. This is directly devouring, or else toward the one to be accomplished. As for acting, through the union of Pramohā one deludes. Through the union of Vetālī, directly embracing with the mind, one brings about death. Having become nondual with the one to be accomplished, through the union of She Who Makes Swell, who dances, one kills.

Having become one with the one to be accomplished, through the union of Caṇḍālī, intoxicated by the utter delusion of bliss, one makes them faint. Through the union of Ghasmarī, whose manner is devouring, one devours. In the manner of planting four arrows, one seals, causing the head of four-faced Brahmā to fall. Through the union of Caurī, whose manner is to step beyond the three worlds, one draws with a hook and extracts the heart. Through the union of Pramohā, whose manner is to draw down to the ground, one deludes. The one whose manner is to confer empowerment with a vase of nectar belongs to Vetālī. Showing the manner of one's own mudrā is the union of Vetālī; endowed with it, one brings about death. Because one has become nondual with the one to be accomplished, it should be seen that in the charnel ground everything is brought to completion, out of all the supreme unions, by the power of whatever is desired.

"The seventh is the action mudrā" means that these themselves are the mudrās for the purpose of action. The colors are white, red, black, white, green, blue and black.

"The counter-mudrā" means that whatever practice is applied so that one cannot be overcome when others skillfully seal is the counter-mudrā. "Resting on the glorious left hip": the glorious right hand binds the lotus fist, and with the eyes gazing intently, there is this vajra-lotus raising and the mudrā endowed with vajra song and dance.

If one asks who it is that subdues, it is "then," that is, "the mudrās of Heruka and the rest." The glorious vajra union is Heruka. Singing his song means that, preceded by dancing, one binds the great mudrā. "Obtained by the mudrā that transforms the gaze in various ways" shows the wrathful face. "Deluding by kissing and embracing" is the second face, the utterly deluding one. "Supreme Bliss, Teacher, all, please" is the third face, whose nature is devouring. "Please show the wonder of Supreme Bliss!" is the fourth face, the playful one. The words beginning "sealed" are the urgings made in order by way of the four faces. "hūṃ a la la la ho, make it shine!" urges the shining forth. The rest is as before.

To show the songs of Gaurī and the others, the passage beginning "in the sacred pledges" is spoken. "Please keep us in the sacred pledge" means "do not let us transgress it." "Be victorious over all afflictions!" refers to the one who is victorious over all afflictions, that is, Heruka. "Please take up all without remainder" is construed as "by which one enjoys all sentient beings."

To show the arising of Padmanarteśvara, the passage beginning "that very yoga" is spoken. "That very" means Vajrasattva. "All attachment" means clinging to a self and the like. Its purification is freedom from conceptual thought through meditating on the selflessness of persons and phenomena. "Abiding gloriously in the lotus mudrā": the great mudrā that shows the purification of attachment, whose bodily color is an unchanging white, is the emptiness of all attachment. Its accomplishment is realization. "Like a perfectly pure crystal sphere" means that he is the color of crystal. "All attachment" is great compassion, which abides as manifold according to those who are to be trained. Because it is made clear by that, there is "light massed with various blazing," that is, he has a maṇḍala of light blazing and massed with many colors.

"Of the buddha of life" means Amitāyus. He himself is the jewel, and to be adorned with him means to be empowered and ornamented by him. So it is connected: "Because he is adorned with the jewel, the buddha of life, he possesses ornaments of many kinds." "By the gathering of all the buddhas" means by the Tathāgatas assembling. "He becomes supreme, like Sukhāvatī" means that he is like the world-realm Sukhāvatī.

"By the various methods of dance" means transforming and emerging through dance methods of many kinds. By that, the lotus, Various Lotus and the others marked by a lotus, as well as the vajra, conch, sword, wheel, staff, pestle and the rest, abiding together with a lotus, are "with lotus." It is construed: "by that supreme lotus, with lotus, turn the mudrās as a wheel." "Having purified by the lotus illusion" means by the lotus ḍākinīs. Placing the right foot on the ground and setting the left foot in the cross-legged manner on the lotus seat is the half cross-legged posture. "The woman's mudrā" means the fist mudrā. "Should be bound" means binding, that is, setting in place. "At the heart" is the remaining word to be supplied; it is to be held with the left hand. "It should be raised in the manner of a makara" means holding the victory banner of the stūpa, that is, gazing. Accomplished in this way, he becomes pure glory.

Likewise Tārā and the others are Supreme Awakening and the rest, and their colors, taught in order, are green, blue, white, golden and red.

"Then" means what was just explained. "Mudrā" means the mudrā of all actions. "Fully accomplish" means attaining the purified woman and the rest. It is construed with the words spoken by one who, preceded by dancing, has bound the great mudrā: "O sattva, lotuses, please show supremely the joy of illusion!" By the word "and the rest" in "all the Tathāgatas and the rest," it should be understood that he takes the forms of Vajradhara, Brahmā and others. Since training sentient beings of many kinds has the characteristic of utterly abandoning delusion, "training sentient beings of many kinds and possessing the supreme accomplishment" is how the second is joined. There, "the sattva, lotus, supreme joy, the joy of illusion" is the joy of the lotus ḍākinī. "O Lord of Dance, perform the dance of ultimate joy!" refers to the one who, delighting in liberation, acts to utterly abandon the delusion of all sentient beings. To make clear that very phrase, "holding various supreme forms of all the Tathāgatas and the rest," the passage beginning "the buddha" is spoken. "Lotus illusion" means the glory of the joy of illusion, the joy of the lotus.

The passage beginning "various forms" teaches the song of the goddesses. "Various forms": because he trains sentient beings by means of different forms, it is the form of a dance of various activities, and with it he dances. Purified by that is "Lotus, lord of the sacred pledge, buddha, please perform the dances!", that is, "O friends!" If one asks what the supreme dance of the lotus-holder is like, it is the awakening of attachment, as in the passage beginning "Just as the lotus, though deep red." There the Lord Avalokiteśvara purifies all the sentient beings in whom he causes great compassion to increase, and he dances.

To teach the setting of Vajrasūrya, the passage beginning "that very yoga" is spoken. "That very" means Vajrasattva. To make the miserly fully abandon their miserliness and to establish them in giving that is pure in its three spheres is to purify the supreme wealth completely. Therefore the one who possesses a sunlike form, conduct, bearing and great splendor is so called. With his right hand he holds an arrow, and with his left a bow. With another right hand he holds a sword, and in his other left he holds the disk of the vajra sun; graceful and smiling, he opens the left and raises it upward.

"Having bound the two vajra knees" means placing the joint of the right knee on top of the left knee. "Having sat on one's own seat" means this: pressing down firmly with the left foot in the half cross-legged manner is one's own seat, and the right foot should be placed beneath the left foot. "Showing the manners just as they are" means by showing the half diadem and the rest just as they are. The bodily colors of these, in order, are red lotus, vermilion, beryl and gold.

Some say that the goddesses of Vairocana and the rest match the bodily color of their respective lords. But that is not so, because the colors would then be incomplete, because it would follow that they are all the same, and because the ritual does not say so.

"Fully accomplishing all actions" means subduing the fierce ones and others who dwell in the three realms. "The song of the king, supreme illusion" is the song of the arising of Vajrasūrya, by whom the great mudrā is bound, preceded by dancing. "The three realms" are the three worlds. "The kingship of all" is the emanation that confers empowerment there. Therefore, in order to make those to be trained abandon miserliness, empowerment and the rest possess the supreme wealth. This is the urging by way of empowerment.

"Reconciliations, battles of many kinds, and especially the buddhas train" means by excellent reconciliation, training and punishment. Then, "victorious by waging battle" refers to the one who, having made beings fully abandon miserliness, causes them to attain many kinds of excellence. That is the king, Supreme Bliss, illusion; this is the urging by way of treasure. "Victorious in reconciliations of many kinds" means abandoning the miserliness present in the mindstreams of sentient beings. For that purpose there is the victory banner: the one who possesses the emanation of the supreme victory banner is so called, and this is the urging by way of the supreme victory banner. "The king" is Vajrasūrya. Supreme Bliss, which has become his very nature, is holy bliss. In order to obtain it there is the illusion that emanates the great offering of sentient beings. That itself is "endowed with manner"; "manner" should be seen as an emanation. This too is the urging by way of the great offering.

Further, "awakening in the clouds of beings" is by way of empowerment. The passage beginning "various offerings and gifts" is by way of treasure. "Accomplish all my hopes and show them swiftly!" is by way of the supreme victory banner. The passage beginning "a la la" is by way of the great offering.

To teach the song of the goddesses, the passage beginning "of the inexhaustible treasure" is spoken. "Of the empowerment of the inexhaustible treasure" refers to the inexhaustible, empowerment, the victorious, the victory banner and offering. "Without remainder" means everywhere, and all these distinctions are brought about by the emanations of empowerment and the rest. All of them, moreover, are for the sake of the jewel women; that is, they are the very cause of obtaining the purified woman for the sake of the empowerment and so on of sentient beings.

"Among all the various jewels,
the jewel woman alone is supreme."

That is, among all the jewels ever gathered, the jewel woman alone is supreme; she is truly the holiest. If one asks how, the pure and holy jewel woman has the nature of empowerment and the rest and is free of every fault. Therefore the jewel woman is the root of all empowerment and the rest, and of samādhi, meditation and all the rest. Hence what is obtained from her is the union of all kingship, that is, the union of Vajrasūrya.

To teach the union of glorious Paramāśva, the passage beginning "that very yoga" is spoken. "By devotion to all as pure" means intending that sentient beings, having first abandoned laziness, should generate diligence. "All the worlds" means the Supreme Lord (Śiva) and the others. "Fully conquers" means pressing down, together with the particular aspect of the hand rising upward and so forth.

"Four-armed" is taught individually: in his left hand, a fist, he holds a variegated lotus; with his right, in the manner of raising a three-pennant banner, he holds a crossed vajra. In his other two hands he holds whichever he likes of the emblems of Vajrasattva and the rest. Others say that he has four arms on each side, in agreement with other tantras: the four on the right are in the manner of raising a three-pennant banner, holding a crossed vajra, holding a sword, and holding an arrow; and with the left, a fist holds a variegated lotus, and the others hold a spear, a staff and a bow. Thus he is eight-armed, they say.

"Four-footed" means this: with the right leg extended, with a right foot he presses down She Who Accomplishes the Three and Śrī, and with a left foot the Three-Eyed One (Śiva) and Viṣṇu; and again, with the other right and left feet he presses down, in order, the Liberated Lady, Joy, Brahmā and the Lord of the Gods. Abiding thus, as before, preceded by dancing, he binds the great mudrā.

To teach the song, the passage beginning "surpassing a thousand intoxicators" is spoken. "The intoxicator" is the god of desire (Madana). By a bliss surpassing a thousand of him, he shows a form of desire, for he emanates a body that steals the heart. This teaches the first face, the playful one. The passage beginning "by the power of training" teaches the wrathful face. Further, the passage beginning "and also in a peaceful body" is the third, the peaceful one, the face of Brahmā. The passage beginning "by all purity" is the fourth, Horse-Face.

"hi hi hi hi" means utterly destroying by overpowering the adversary. "hulu hulu" means "enter!" "ruṭa maṭa" means free of joy. "smara rati" means recollecting, up to "make manifest the one endowed with the peaceful sentiment." "a la la la la ho" means that it has become wondrous. "sumbha" means "make them fall!" "nisumbhani" means to fainting. "hūṃ" means by killing. "phaṭ phaṭ" means taming. These are words of urging.

To teach the song of the goddesses, the passage beginning "friend, to a man" is spoken. It means this: the joy that arises in a person who deliberates whether a thing should be done or not done is a contrived friendship. Who would speak of him with affection, saying, "He is a friend"? Again, where an attack is begun and the deliberation arises, "should it be done or not?", that attack too is contrived. Who would say of him, "He is a hero"? Some say that the two goddesses, Various Lotus and the other, are inferred from this very song.

To teach the mudrās of the sacred pledge, by which the deities are made to abide in the maṇḍala through their pledge, and which are blessed with their own pledges when yogins accomplish themselves, the passage beginning "the index finger" is spoken. "Having pressed the thumb with the index finger" means this: joining the palms, one bends the two index fingers and presses their tips with the tips of one's own thumbs. "Press the index finger with one's own thumb" means that joining those same index fingers with the thumbs and setting them in sequence is the glorious Eye of Accomplishment. Binding the vajra palms together, one makes the two vajra eyes by that same sequence; then from the two vajra eyes one bends the two index fingers, joins them in the shape of an eye and binds them: this is glorious Vajralocanā. "Accomplished" means complete. "By this" means that through Vajralocanā one becomes accomplished as all the buddhas and the rest. "All the vajra holders" are Vajrasattva, Supreme Bliss and the others; it brings them under control. Therefore these are the mudrās of all of them. The mudrā of the vajra possessor is also that of Vajrasattva himself.

Joining the vajra palms, bending the two index fingers and inserting the two thumbs between them is the mudrā of all the uṣṇīṣa deities and of Vairocana with his retinue. "Opening upward" means this: from the vajra palms placed together, one opens the fingers upward, bends the two index fingers, and sets the two thumbs like fangs. This belongs to Heruka and his goddesses. "Deluding" means making faint. Joining the vajra palms evenly, making the two middle fingers even, and opening the thumbs and little fingers is the mudrā of the Lotus King with his retinue. "Bending the index fingers evenly outward" means making the two index fingers even and bending them from the outside. "The thumbs covering the vajra" means making the two thumbs into a vajra, joining their two tips, and binding them together. This belongs to Vajrasūrya with his goddess. "Join the little and ring fingers evenly" means that extending the little fingers together with the ring fingers belongs to the four-footed one. "Bending the index fingers with their tips even" means joining the two index fingers evenly and bending them outward like a neck.

It says that the great mudrā should be bound, preceded by holding one's own emblems, the vajra, bell and the rest, and the mudrā. To teach the knowledge of this great mudrā of the sacred pledge, the passage beginning "body, speech and mind" is spoken. Body, speech and mind themselves are vajra, through their connection with their deities. "By the union from which all arises" means in the manner from which all arises. "I, as the deity" means this: just as I myself have become my own special deity, so likewise, having completed the stages beginning with inviting the great being and continuing through blessing, empowerment and the rest, one should meditate and so on, endowed with the pride of that deity.

To teach the knowledge of the yoga mudrā, which consists of union, subsequent union and utmost union, the passage beginning "Vajrasattva" is spoken. The all-union of glorious Vajrasattva is the union from which all arises. Through it, one possesses body, speech and mind endowed with form and shape just as they are, and endowed with the mudrā of the vajra mind; this is the union of having become one's own deity. "The self of all" is Vajrasattva. "Into the self of all" means into one's own body. "Should be made to enter" is the subsequent union. The passage beginning "without attachment" refers to being endowed with the utmost union of the self just as it is. This teaches the following: having completed the union and the subsequent union in the order described, and having meditated on attachment, non-attachment and the rest as emptiness, one should meditate on oneself together with the goddesses.

Likewise the yogin of Vairocana should meditate on reality with the passage beginning "neither existent nor nonexistent." Likewise the yogin of glorious Heruka should meditate on reality with the passage beginning "all has the characteristic of space." Likewise the yogin of Padmanarteśvara meditates on reality with the passage beginning "just as this red lotus."

Likewise the yogin of Vajrasūrya should meditate on reality with the passage beginning "upon the extremely vicious and fierce." Likewise the yogin of Paramāśva should meditate on reality with the passage beginning "through the conviction that all is pure."

In order to teach all the yogins endowed with union and the rest, the text says "however many applications" and so on. "However many applications" means those found in the treatises on dance and song. "Should be fully known" means by those things that are explained in the treatises on dance and the like. The passage beginning "and actions" is the mudrā of Supreme Bliss. The passage beginning "kissing" is the mudrā of Wondrous Bliss. The passage beginning "enjoying in union" is the mudrā of Tārā. The passage beginning "of all unions" is the mudrā of Śiśi. The mudrās indicated for Vairocana and the others should be regarded in the same way.

As for "having accomplished one's own self in the maṇḍala, one should do just that, sealed with the facial mudrās and so forth of the deities": in order to show however many facial mudrās there are, the text says "of all the goddesses" and so on. "All the goddesses" are Supreme Bliss and the rest. "Great bliss" is being united with them. "Arising from that" is the transformation of the beautiful face. With this, Vajrasattva is to be construed with the words "what has turned to ash" and "brings back to life." That is, having experienced the bliss of union with those very goddesses, he dances with beautiful glances in union and lifts up his eyes; that, and the transformation of the face, is what is so called.

In order to teach the facial mudrās of Supreme Bliss and the others, the text says "skilled women" and so on. "Skilled women" are those trained in the arts. "Their great illusion" is the transformation by the coquettish play with which they transform their beautiful faces. "With lines and supreme in charm" is whatever dances with beautiful glances and has a radiant face; it is as before.

With the passage beginning "buddha awakening," the facial mudrā of the Blessed One Vairocana together with his retinue is taught. "The awakening of the buddhas" is She Who Proclaims All the Buddhas and the others. "United with her" is union. Whatever, from that, brings great delight, and gazes and smiles from them, is what is so called; it is linked up as "delighting in taking up the awakening of the buddhas, gazing, and the finger." "Fully accomplishing existence" means that the non-abiding nirvāṇa is perfected; it is for the sake of that. "Abiding in the secret view" means that whatever abides with a peaceful view is what is so called. The facial mudrā of glorious Heruka, and his own exhalation, and also those of his retinue, should be understood as they occur in their own place.

With the passage beginning "with amorousness and the like," the facial mudrā of Padmanarteśvara together with his retinue is shown.

In order to teach the mudrā of smiles, the text says "of all the goddesses" and so on. The great bliss of having entered into perfect union with all the goddesses is "the great bliss of all the goddesses." "Beautiful with that" means equal to it. "Pressed down" belongs to glorious Vajrasattva. The words from "all attachment" up to "even what has turned to ash, he brings back to life" belong, in order, to Supreme Bliss and the others together with their retinue. It is taught for the delight of Vairocana together with his retinue. That of glorious Heruka together with his retinue is what is explained as "vajra ha." That of Padmanarteśvara together with his retinue is the accompanying smile endowed with the mood of amorousness and the like. That of Vajrasūrya together with his retinue is the smiling face accompanied by wrath. That of Paramāśva is the smile with a host of delights, as explained before.

In order to teach the knowledge of the mudrā of the gaze, the text says "experiencing bliss" and so on. "Experiencing bliss" means this: union through intense union with the goddesses is thorough union through experiencing bliss. Through that, there is the languid, half-closed gaze and those transformations; this belongs to Vajrasattva. The passage beginning "skilled women" belongs to Supreme Bliss and the others. The passage beginning "by the secret of the buddhas" belongs to Vairocana together with his retinue. The passage beginning "with the brows knit in a wrathful frown" belongs to glorious Heruka together with his retinue; it should be regarded as it occurs in its own place. The passage beginning "smiling with wrathful change" belongs to Vajrasūrya together with his retinue. The passage beginning "endowed with the flavor of amorousness and the like" belongs to Padmanarteśvara together with his retinue.

In order to teach the Dharma mudrā, the text says "na mo stu" and so on. Having made manifest the meditation of an excellent vajra upon the tongue, placing it on a moon disc at the heart is the Dharma mudrā of Vajrasattva together with his retinue. The passage beginning "oṃ ru ru" fully teaches the Dharma mudrā itself of Vairocana together with his retinue. By way of the limbs indicated through signs, the passage beginning "bha ga" in the midst of the four goddesses belongs to Heruka together with his retinue.

In order to teach the heart mantra of the lord in the manner of recitation, the text says "the pitches of the voice" and so on. The high pitch of the voice and the rest are the varieties of striking stringed instruments of wood, and the body too should follow along with it. Others hold that it is the ma tra, that is, the particular variety of the ending of a note. The high pitch of the voice is a break between notes. The low is a division between notes. The tremolo is a shift between notes. Recitation is reciting in the sequence of soft and so on. "Very peaceful" means not fierce. "The supreme ones" are musical instruments. "By these three." "Of all words" means by the vajra words of great delight and the rest. "As desired" means however it pleases. "Of all songs" means by the śa ta pa and the others. The syllable hūṃ is the recitation of Vajrasattva, and it is also that of all the goddesses, because they are of the essential nature of the lord Vajrasattva. Their own heart mantras are those explained below.

"kā ka li" means that in it the śa ta is sung out twice. The syllable oṃ is the recitation of Vairocana. "With the voice of Brahmā" means with the voice of those who recite the Vedas. "The fierce vajra, the great laughter" is the loud laughter "ha ha" of glorious Heruka. Whatever song pours forth the syllable hūṃ, deep like the sound of thunder, and in which there is "ru lu ru lu," is what is so called: that is, whatever is the deep sound of the syllable hūṃ, deep like thunder, together with the sound of ru lu ru lu sent forth. This is what it teaches: "ha ha ha ha, ru lu ru lu" is the recitation of glorious Heruka. That of Padmanarteśvara is the one beginning "ste." The one beginning "hi" is that of Vajrasūrya. Those of their retinues are explained below.

"Accomplishing all destroying" means that the heart mantras of all of them, Vajrasattva and the rest, are destroying; because they accomplish and maintain fierce applications, it is linked up as "fully accomplishing all destroying." From among the heart mantras of those very ones, Vajrasattva and the rest, whichever one delights in, up to being endowed with "kā ha" and so on, is made the recitation of Paramāśva. Some say that what occurs in the tantra, and in accordance with it "hi hi hi hi hi," is the recitation of Paramāśva. As for the mantras of the lord spoken in the ritual manual (kalpa), beginning "oṃ ma hā su kha vajra satva": because those of all the goddesses are found among the heart mantras of the entire maṇḍala, they are not explained elsewhere, since through these alone all excellences will be attained.

"Knowing the mudrā of song" means knowing the mudrā of recitation. In order to indicate the knowledge of the mudrā of music, the text says "the flute and" and so on. The flute is a hollow instrument. The one-stringed is the vīṇā. The many-stringed is the ku mu da. The thick-stringed is the clay drum. The cymbals: these are the mudrās of music played by striking upon them. "Struck, glory has become the ground, accomplished" refers to the four kinds of accomplishment of the four goddesses: through binding the four, the great mudrā and the rest, and in connection with them, by means of the heart mantras and so forth that employ the syllable hūṃ and the others as appropriate, various kinds of action are accomplished together with them. And through that, those very actions, the dispelling, the play of the bull, the gha ṭa na and so on, are the mudrā of music; it fully accomplishes all accomplishments.

"jaḥ hūṃ vaṃ hoḥ" are, in order, the heart mantras of the four, Supreme Bliss and the rest. "ru ṭa ma ṭa" is that of Buddha Awakening. "sma ra ra ṭa" is that of the Wheel of Dharma. "ru lu ru lu" is that of Trailokyavijayā, Victorious over the Three Worlds. "pla srid tya ja" is that of the Creeper of All Desires. "ṭakki hūṃ ja" is that of Horse-Face. "hu lu hu lu" is that of the Vajra Mistress. "a la la ho" is that of Tārā. "śuddha siddha darśaya sarvaṃ" is that of Vetālī, the Utter Destroyer of Ashes. "ka ni" is that of the Flute Goddess. "gu ni" is that of the One-Stringed Goddess. "hūṃ" is that of the Many-Stringed Goddess. "dhuṃ" is that of the Thick-Stringed Goddess. The passage beginning "ta keṅ" is uttered as the words of the music. In the same way, for the music of Vairocana and the others too, the music of the goddesses, the heart mantras, what is uttered as the words of the music, and the syllables should be inferred as appropriate.

"Showing the clapping of the palms" is the clapping of the palms in the musical path called "Constant"; this music belongs to Vajrasattva. "The great drum of the strings" should be understood to be uninterrupted music. Those belong to Supreme Bliss and the others. The passage beginning "oṃ" belongs to Vairocana together with his retinue. The passage beginning "ko ko" belongs to glorious Heruka together with his retinue. The passage beginning "padma padma" belongs to Padmeśvara together with his retinue. The passage beginning "traṃ" belongs to Vajrasūrya together with his retinue. The passage beginning "ṭa ke hūṃ" belongs to glorious Paramāśva. The four beginning "the haughty gait" should be understood as connected with the four faces endowed with their moods.

It was said:
"With vajra cymbals attached,
All the buddhas should be gathered."
In order to show the occasion for knowing the mudrā of dance spoken of there, the text says "Vajrasattva" and so on. Striking the left cymbal is the left palm. In the passage beginning "the palms struck slowly," striking the cymbals with right and left both at once is union, and that itself is called gathering. "Following it in the manner of the path" means in accordance with the four paths, the Constant and the rest. "The fierce vajra" is glorious Heruka. Śākyamuni is the Tathāgata. All the a ra li are Vajrasattva. The Constant is Vairocana.

"Through the nature of all things" means the following. They are present and bestow blessing, endowed with the moods of these distinctions that abide in the manner of zeal and the like, moving about, displaying, delighting and so on, as appropriate; and in the same way, since glorious Vajrasattva and the others, the glory of all things, are urged on by the words beginning "one's own offering," it should be inferred that here too the maṇḍala is gathered.

This is what it teaches: draw the maṇḍala of glorious Vajrasattva as before, and in the central square draw the maṇḍala of Vajrasattva. In the eastern and the other squares are those of the four, Vairocana and the rest. In the southeastern square is that of glorious Paramāśva. In the southwestern and the other squares, place the Vajra Chief and the others. In the outer drawing, in the inner southeastern and other corners, are the Flute Goddess and the others. Likewise in the outer corners are the Flower Goddess and the others. In the eastern and the other gates, place Aṅkuśī and the others.

Then one should perform the gathering and so on. Having completed the rites of entry and the rest, one should make the offering of dance, endowed with its moods, together with the mudrās and the answering mudrās. In order to teach the offering of mudrā and dance, the text says "the application of the mudrās" and so on. "Of these" refers to what is explained below. "One's own offering" is the offering of one's own great mudrā. "With the mudrās and the answers to the mudrās" and so on means by the great mudrās connected with the challenge, the counter-challenge and the rest. If one asks how they are to be applied, the text says "entirely" and so on. "The master of all" is the master of all the maṇḍalas. "Through the application of origin and the rest" has already been explained. By dancing, that is, by beginning the dance, for as long as it lasts one becomes oneself the Entirely Various One, the fortunate one, that is, like the mudrā of Vajrasattva.

The challenge arises from the body of the master; that through it all the buddhas come forth is fully shown as Vairocana in the great mudrā. The counter-challenge is the master, as in "transformed into the great Vajradhara," or into the great mudrā of glorious Heruka. "One's own arising" is the great mudrā of Vajrasattva. In the same way it should be done, as appropriate, for the applications of all the goddesses.

These are the songs of Vajrasattva and the others. In some places, the passage beginning "stainless" is an urging by way of Vajrasattva. In some, the passage beginning "terrifying" is by way of glorious Heruka. In some, the passage beginning "of beautiful radiance" is by way of Vairocana. In some, the passage beginning "all beings" is by way of Padmanarteśvara. These words, "shows him endowed with supreme speech," are by way of Vajrasūrya. The passage beginning "in order to accomplish Vajrasattva" is by way of Paramāśva.

In order to teach the songs of the goddesses, the text says "of many kinds" and so on: in order to tame sentient beings of many kinds. "Whose ways" are the ways in which he tames beings of many kinds; by applying himself to actions such as dispelling, he dances. Among these, the pure dance is the lotus pledge: "Lord of dance, you buddhas are dancers, so dance!" This is because, through his supreme dancing of the supreme bliss of many kinds, those who are freed by it attain freedom from the afflictions.

In order to teach the knowledge of the mudrā of the offering of vajra song for each of the maṇḍalas of Vajrasattva and the others, the text says "great delight" and so on. "Great delight" is Vajrasattva. "Passion" is delight in him. That itself is the arrow and the bow; and because it is extremely subtle, it is sealed with the subtlety of those two mudrās, which is through the essential nature of Supreme Bliss. "Through joining in great delight" means perfect union. Because there is bliss through it, one becomes intoxicated by it; hence "intoxicated by the bliss of joining in great delight." This calls upon her by way of Wondrous Bliss. As for "utterly Supreme Bliss": because she is victorious through the various aspects of utterly supreme bliss, she is the one whose crest raises the banner of victory. That emanation of Tārā raises the banner of victory, victorious through the various aspects of utterly supreme bliss; it is her enjoyment. The passage beginning "of great delight" is the urging by way of Śiśi; the words beginning "of great delight" have already been explained.

The passage beginning "O" means: by the bliss of Supreme Bliss and the others, "O Supreme Bliss, bring about accomplishment!" "O wondrous bliss" is the nature of the mudrā of bha ga and liṅ ga. "Ah, wondrous bliss!" means "Goddess, grant bliss!", and "Tārā, make it shine forth and grant me bliss!" is the remainder. "Ah, Śiśi, enter, enter!" means entering into the supreme bliss of the very swift ḍākinī. The passage beginning "of Vajrasattva" belongs to Vairocana together with his retinue. Likewise the passage beginning "hūṃ, arrow of vajra action" belongs to glorious Heruka together with his retinue.

In the song that urges by way of the four faces, the passage beginning "resounding loudly" is by way of the fierce face; it belongs to Padmanarteśvara together with his retinue. The passage beginning "infinite beings" is by way of the heroic face at the back. In some places, the passage beginning "utterly certain" is by way of the peaceful face on the left. In some, the passage beginning "blazing" is by way of the first face, the amorous one. The passage beginning "empowering all" is the song urging by way of the four goddesses victorious over all māras; it belongs to Vajrasūrya. In some places, the passage beginning "of the wise" is the urging by way of the four faces of Paramāśva.

In order to indicate the knowledge of the mudrās of all the offerings, the text says "white flowers and" and so on. "Green" means green in color. "As before" means like flowers whose color is not artificial. The order in which the colors are distributed should also be seen by this same sequence. "Arisen from minerals" means that those arisen from minerals too are made fragrant with the nature of flowers. "As before" means like those that are not artificial. The two sandalwoods are the white and the red. gan da is gan da ra sa. ro dhi is rock lichen. sarja is ra sa. "Khadira and jaggery" means boiled with khadira and made into pills. "Together with ka bi ca" means together with sandalwood and tu ru ska. "To the vajra holder" means the one with Paramāśva.

In order to teach the knowledge of the mudrā of the torma, the text says "the Coquette and" and so on. The Coquette is grain liquor. The Maiden is mead. The Taste is molasses liquor. Nectar is grape wine. The Lone Hero is a drink of grape juice. The Demon of Desire is salt. The Crooked One is sour liquor that has aged for a long time. The Servant Girl is sour liquor that has aged for a short time. The Great Beast is the man who is the great beast.

"Vajra action" is Heruka. "Of the plains" means coming from the region of the plains. "The supreme flesh" means the supreme meats.

In order to indicate the knowledge of the mudrās of empowerment by mudrā, the text says "the thumb, sattva" and so on. Join the palms in the vajra añjali, and with the right thumb press the left in the manner of the sattva cross-legged posture. Bend the two index fingers, join the two middle fingers evenly, and set them at the second joint in the manner of a hook. Set the remaining fingers in the manner of the vajra añjali. With this mudrā, "Buddha Jewel," generate at the crown Vairocana, white in color, with the mudrā of supreme awakening; on the forehead, adorned by Akṣobhya, white in color, with the earth-pressing mudrā; on the right, Ratnasambhava, the color of lapis lazuli, with the mudrā of granting the supreme; at the back, Amitābha, the color of refined gold, with the mudrā of the wheel of Dharma; and on the left, Amoghasiddhi, green in color, fully granting fearlessness. Then say "oṃ a bhi ṣiñca māṃ" and confer the empowerment. Leading with a gesture like the light ends of hanging silk ribbons, move gracefully, release them from the ends, and make it firm with the palms.

"All the fingers interlaced inward" means: make the binding inward and interlace all the fingers inside. "Having joined the tips of all the fingers" means joining the tips of all the fingers on the faces where they cross one another. "The thumbs pressing the vajra" means pressing from above with the thumbs formed into a vajra. "Like the hollows of the nose and eyes" means resembling the openings of the hollows of the nose and the eyes. For this one too, one confers empowerment with "hūṃ a bhi ṣiñca māṃ," up to the culmination with the palms. Those two mudrās belong to Vajrasattva together with his retinue.

Binding the vajra palms and making the vajra jewel belongs to glorious Vairocana together with his retinue; it is joined with "a bhi ṣiñca māṃ." That of glorious Heruka together with his retinue is preceded by placing the vajra of Heruka at one's own heart; one confers empowerment with "a bhi ṣiñca māṃ." The second, moreover, is the mudrā of the diadem of a garland of skulls, connected with Vajrasattva; "glorious Vajrasattva, lotus jewel, precious crown ornament" is the form of its name. One confers empowerment with one's own heart mantra together with "a bhi ṣiñca māṃ."

"The thumbs in the hero's cross-legged posture" means: make the vajra añjali and bind the thumbs, and set the rest as before. "This" means that Padmeśvara together with his retinue confers empowerment with "a bhi ṣiñca māṃ." Join the vajra palms evenly, make the two thumbs even and extend them, make the index fingers even, bend their tips and join them with the tips of the thumbs. Forming a jewel in this way, one makes the mudrā of the glorious vajra jewel; this belongs to Vajrasūrya together with his retinue, and one confers empowerment with this and with "a bhi ṣiñca māṃ."

"Buddha jewel" is the fifth diadem, connected with Vajrasattva. For Vairocana too there is the mudrā of the vajra jewel, made into a buddha jewel. "Vajra jewel" is the vajra mudrā of glorious Heruka. "Red lotus" is the mudrā of Padmanarteśvara. "Great vajra jewel" is the mudrā of Vajrasūrya. With all those mudrās, one also confers empowerment with the heart mantra of Paramāśva.

Now, in order to teach the vajra mudrās that stir the secret of mind, the vajra and the rest that abide on the moon at the heart, the text says "the fingers mutually" and so on. Of what is called the vajra binding, with the left hand make the index finger and thumb into a womb, insert into it the right thumb as the male sign, and bind them: this stirs the secret of body of Vajrasattva together with his retinue. "The vajra of joy" is the very vajra of glorious great joy just explained. "Place the middle fingers inside the binding" means inside where they are set, twisted together, within the vajra binding. "The thumbs with the tips of the little fingers" means joining the little fingers and the thumbs. This is the vajra mudrā; it stirs the secret of mind of Vajrasattva together with his retinue. "From that" means from the vajra of bliss, great joy. From that, making the two middle fingers into hooks and praising at the heart again belongs to that very Blessed One together with his retinue.

The vajra wheels of the secret pledge belong to Vairocana together with his retinue. "The vajra mudrā" is the very one just explained. "Made perfectly" means palm pressed against palm; uttering the syllable ā and performing it, this is the mudrā of entering. "The vajra fist" is the one just explained before. "Having likewise bent the middle fingers": "the middle fingers, supreme vajra of space" means that, having likewise bent the middle fingers, it becomes entirely like space. "Likewise the tips of the thumbs even" means pressing the two thumbs against each other and joining them. "Bend the tips of the two index fingers" means bending the index fingers like a lotus; this belongs to Padmanarteśvara together with his retinue. "Vajra lotus" is the one just explained. "The little fingers evened, their tips an arrow" means joining the two little fingers well. "The vajra arrow is fully shot" means forming it in the manner of shooting an arrow; this belongs to Vajrasūrya together with his retinue. "The vajra mudrā" is the mudrā of Vairocana. "Place the middle fingers outside the binding" means joining the two middle fingers well on the outside; this is the mudrā of Paramāśva.

In order to teach the knowledge of the mudrā of the name together with praise, the text says "ā li kā li" and so on. "ā li kā li" is the garland of letters, the vowels and consonants. "Equal" means alike. "Tie a knot right at the mouth" means: just as one imagines one's own letters in their various forms, so at its mouth one should set out and write there all the letters, such as the syllable hrīḥ. All the curls should be made in the form of the leaves of a creeper. And wherever the letters are written, the letter ra should be made into a sprout. The essence that abides in the anusvāra is the sun and the moon.

The two dots of the visarga are made into two moons. Through this writing of letters, even by writing merely the names, beginning "Vajrasattva, glorious union," one becomes free from all evil and is joined with all bliss.

Some say that "ā li kā li" is the garland of letters beginning with ā. Dividing them into two equal sets by the distinctions of long and short and so on, one makes a knot with lines right at the mouth of the letter ā with the letter a; afterwards the lower ones, a and the rest, should be fully knotted with lines in the order of long and short and so on. "All the letters should be set out" means: drawing in the letters ka and the rest as well, from their radiating forth like a form of light rays, one should meditate on the awakening mind in the form of a moon maṇḍala. "Make it with curls on the creeper" means that the curl is wisdom and the creeper is compassion. The ra with the half moon, that is, with the anusvāra, is here conceived as "contemplated as the essential nature of the sphere of reality." And then, having made it melt through the fire of the letter ra, which is the nature of wisdom and means, one completes the maṇḍala resting on the Various Lotus and places here what is accompanied by the rays of ka and the rest; thus one generates the various forms and performs the emanation, the withdrawal and so on, just as they are. These explanations and the like, however, are mere conceptual constructions. This is not the occasion for writing letters within, for it is not connected with the meaning that concerns the name together with praise.

The passage beginning "Vajrasattva, god of glory" is the presentation. "Vajrasattva, glorious union" is the explanation in sequence. "Intensely attached" means marked with the word "attached." With "if the names are written" is meant the merit of writing them in accordance with the supreme of names, and likewise of reciting them, up to accomplishing them. In order to show that this applies not only to writing and reading aloud, but that also when one confers empowerment by name in the maṇḍala one should mark the name with the word "glorious," the text says "the supreme deity" and so on. "The supreme deity" is Vajrasattva and the rest. "His name" means that one should mark all of them, Vajrasattva and the rest, with the word "glorious," and confer empowerment with a vajra name.

With the names such as "glorious Vajrasattva," preceded by receiving the offering, bind the mudrā of empowerment in accordance with oneself, and say:
"oṃ Thus I prophesy:
Vajrasattva, Tathāgata,
Having turned you back entirely from the lower realms,
Accomplisher of the supreme existence,
O so-and-so,"
and then say "Vajra Tathāgata siddhi sa ma ya traṃ bhur bhu va svaḥ."

In order to indicate the knowledge of the sign mudrās, so that those devoted to silence may communicate by conventional signs at the time of recitation, the text says "rubbing the crown" and so on. Moving the right eyebrow shows "travel through the sky is accomplished." With the left, "supreme victory": the accomplishment is fully accomplished. Again, moving the left eye shows "she is a good woman," and the right, "of a good man." Touching the right ear says "meat," and the left says "liquor." Pressing the left lower lip means "touching bliss." Touching the throat is the supreme of speaking all conceptions: it shows "speaking all conceptions, the unsurpassed Great Vehicle." Drawing upward at the heart is drawing up all the vajras. "Fully gathering saṃsāra" speaks of fully embracing the non-abiding nirvāṇa. Touching the navel means "through diligently accomplishing the path of the Middle Way, it is the maṇḍala of the great wheel of Dharma." "One's own organ": raising the male organ shows "happily and blissfully one will attain all the awakening of the buddhas." The right is praising and summoning; the left is sending back. Embracing with the right arm says "all is accomplished," and with the left it says "all sovereignty is accomplished." Clenching the right hand into a fist is the gesture for "gain increase!" Opening the left is "the pine tree." Slapping the left thigh shows great zeal for great desire, and the right shows zeal for the Dharma. Striking the left kneecap is "a knot," and the right shows homage. Striking the right calf is sealing, and the left shows "be released!" Pressing the ground with the left foot is "press down!", and the right shows "utterly crush!"

Through these sign mudrās, one endowed with the application of Vajrasattva and the rest accomplishes and perfects whatever is intended by one's own sign, travel through the sky and the rest; and for one who uses those applications of mudrā as conventional signs, there is nothing that is not accomplished and not made manifest. Making the mudrās of rousing the accomplishments and so on, whatever bliss there is, such as all the awakening of the buddhas, and those paths of one's own aim and the rest, are fully accomplished, and they become nothing but complete.

In order to distinguish the meanings of the tantra, and in order to speak in secret language in the maṇḍala and elsewhere, so as to teach the code names that are synonyms of words, the text says "oṃ ā dhi bhu" and so on. "oṃ ā dhi bhu" is for paying homage. The answering sound to it is the reply, that is, uttering words of homage in return. With the passage beginning "the sattva without beginning or end," the synonyms of Vajrasattva are spoken. "Without beginning or end" means that, though present, there is no first arising at all; it is nirvāṇa without remainder. Because he is free from all that involves conceptual thought, he is Vajrasattva. Because he has become the nature of the uninterrupted stream of undefiled bliss and is thus supremely blissful, he is to be called Wondrous Bliss.

Because it is the seal in which the buddhas are gathered, it is the Buddha Seal. To show the synonyms of the goddesses, the passage beginning "Supreme Bliss" is spoken. The seals of Supreme Bliss, Wondrous Bliss, Tārā, Śiśi and the rest are taught, by reason of their actions, as respectively the Sealer, the Deluder, the Devourer, and She Who Increases Life. The other goddesses each have just one synonymous name, such as Buddha Awakening. The synonymous names from "flute" and so on up to "clay drum" are synonyms for the flute, the one-stringed lute, and so forth. The offerings, such as the Flower Goddess, are those called "musk," "enjoyment," and so on.

To explain the function of the seals that are the hand emblems of the four goddesses, the passage beginning "moving the eyebrows" is spoken. Supreme Bliss's is the bow and arrow; because it has the nature of a striking glance, it is called "moving the eyebrows." Wondrous Bliss's is what is called bhaga and liṅga, marked by the seal of the womb and by the male organ; therefore it says "of Wondrous Bliss." It says, "Tārā's skull cup is explained to be the lotus, and the khaṭvāṅga to be the victory banner." Of Śiśi it says, "This buddha seal abides in the lotus, the elixir (rasāyana) of all bliss." "All the buddhas" is expressed by the word "Śiśi." The wheel is the samaya.

"The gathering of all excellent persons" is a synonym for the maṇḍala. In the feast maṇḍala too, because all friends are gathered, it is called "the assembly of friends." So it should be understood. Elsewhere as well, apart from the feast maṇḍala, there is a maṇḍala here by way of friendship. Union with Vajrasattva that has become nondual is called the maṇḍala. Complete conjunction with all the goddesses has the character of perfect union, and it is called "the Entirely Various Fortunate One."

"Union with Vajrasattva" means meditating on oneself as having his nature. That is the line "by uniting oneself wholly with all the buddhas"; what was explained earlier is the dissolving of the wisdom being within, which has the character of subsequent union. "Because of knowing omniscience in all aspects" means being endowed with what is supreme in all aspects; the one who possesses the pride of being nondual with that is the master of all, it says. Whenever one actualizes the union whose nature is perfect conjunction with all the goddesses, then one is established as the master of all, and should also be known as the Entirely Various Fortunate One.

The accomplishment of that one established as the master of all is the subsequent accomplishment. As for accomplishment, being empowered with the empowerment of glorious Vajrasattva is the subsequent accomplishment, and this is explained to be the victory banner. The supreme is the master of all and so on, the supreme companion in the disciple's practice. The lowest is one fortunate enough to enter the maṇḍala. The middling is one empowered into the conduct of the Tathāgata.

The supreme jewel crown of all the buddhas is the crown of the five buddhas; "That is the king of all the buddhas," it says. The second is the crown of a garland of skulls, and it is explained as being in the manner of the glorious lotus. "One accomplished in the nature of a buddha" means marked with a single buddha; "accomplished" means well established. "One of the nature of the lotus" means marked with a single skull. These two are respectively the buddha empowerment and the great empowerment, as Sarvārthasiddha says.

The excellent offering fee is all one's riches and wealth, and all one's heart as well, they say. Red sandalwood mixed with camphor should be understood to be the great blood. Others say that what is called black blood is that of the vajra holder. Some hold that it is mixed with camphor. That very substance, placed in the center of the maṇḍala, is called the elixir endowed with all glory.

What is called "the great beast" in the torma rite of glorious Heruka should also be understood to be a human effigy made of dough and smeared with red sandalwood; it is not the real thing. Likewise, the meat mentioned in the torma rite of Paramāśva should be understood to be made only of dough. And the royal food, with meat and liquor, spoken of for the torma of Vajrasūrya should also be understood to be made only of flour dough. Some say that this is what is done when that is not available, like artificial fruits and the like.

"Oath" and "samaya" are synonyms. "Vow" and "Supreme Bliss" are synonyms. What is called "all the buddhas" is also the samaya: it is the essential nature of Supreme Bliss and the essential nature of Wondrous Bliss. Tārā is established as utterly clear. Śiśi is renewed existence. All these seals too are called samaya. Why? Because they are not to be transgressed.

"It is explained to be the union of the seal," it says. It is said to be the union of the seal, and likewise the union is explained to be the seal. "Union with glorious Vajrasattva" means applying oneself to meditation as the nature of all things; this is explained as "all unions." Why is that? The passage beginning "Now" is spoken: because it is the source of all the various samayas, and, being the essential nature of most excellent bliss and mental joy, it has the nature of vajra joy, the secret of mind; therefore it is called "all unions."

What that one, the embodiment of all unions, enjoys is the three realms, form and the rest. To show this clearly, the passage beginning "Its" is spoken. Form is explained as painting. "Sound is song" means singing a song. Scent and "that which delights" are synonyms. The name for taste is rasāyana. "Blissful touch" is the touch from which bliss is produced; that is the touch of bliss. Phenomena are the sense source of phenomena, and this is explained to be union. In just this way, with all the enjoyments of all unions, being endowed with the union of one's own deity, whatever one enjoys blissfully, and in whatever way, should be understood to be the samaya of one's own offering.

"If one makes offering through subsequent union" means that the wisdom being too, having become nondual, enjoys forms and the rest. As for "the bliss of all unions," whatever bliss arises from enjoying forms and the rest is explained to be great bliss. Meditating on oneself as having the nature of one's own deity is absorption in wisdom alone, and that is what is explained as yoga. Samādhi and union are synonymous names. That which has the character of perfect union, the equal union of all the goddesses, is explained to be the gathering of all the buddhas. When one abides through equal union with all and so forth, one is blessed by all the buddhas, and so one attains what is called Union with All the Buddhas. That practice is called "acting in joy." Subsequent union is called the accomplishment of the qualities explained earlier. The enjoyment of joy, as the goddesses act as men do, is explained to be, in turn, the subsequent accomplishment.

The bliss that has the character of perfect union in conjunction with the goddesses, Supreme Bliss and the rest, is called "gathering"; it is entering fully into the continuous stream. That is devotion to all unions, and it is explained that the dominion of all the buddhas is bliss. "All accomplishments" carries a word meaning "arising": whatever is accomplished in every respect is the all-pervading arising, the great accomplishment, that is, the great arising. "Supreme among all great accomplishments": being the great Vajradhara and the unsurpassed Great Lord is explained to be the unsurpassed lord. Because, being free of all the obscurations of the three realms, one's own continuum has become the Great Lord, supreme among all great accomplishments, unsurpassed bliss is the supreme bliss of the Great Lord, supreme among all great accomplishments; and that is explained as Union with All the Buddhas, the Great Lord of union.

The flower is explained as blood. Incense carries the word "radiance." The lamp is called great flesh. The synonyms of scent and of all fragrances have already been explained. What comes from that which has become milk is cream, and that is called "the full moon." The name for meat is "medicine." Liquor is called "elixir." Food is called "life." The various foods together with meat and liquor are explained to be the enjoyment of joy, the excellence of the life of the fortunate one. Ripe mango fruits and the like are explained to be the fruits of nectar. "Liberating" is explained as tasting bliss and being utterly enraptured by bliss.

Union and samādhi are synonyms; that which has the character of the goddess's union in perfect conjunction with all the goddesses is explained as the gathering of all the buddhas. "Mango," "ripe," and the rest are synonymous names.

The song, music, and dance of the union of glorious Vajrasattva are called the emanation of all the buddhas. The utterly peaceful music, song, and dance are the song of the Buddha, and that is called the supreme magical display. The song, music, and dance of the charnel ground belong to glorious Heruka, and that is called the great emanation. Passionate song, music, and dance belong to Padmanarteśvara, and that too is explained as the glorious emanation. The song, music, and dance of the lord of men are connected with Vajrasūrya, and that too is called the supreme emanation. Song, music, and dance of great delight are connected with Paramāśva, and that is called the Paramāśva emanation.

To show fully what was called "bliss," the passage on "that" beginning "ḍākinī" is spoken. This seal of the Entirely Various One is the great seal of Vajrasattva; because it moves through all of space, it is explained as the ḍākinī of union with all the buddhas. The lord of the dance of illusion is glorious Heruka, and he is given the name Aralli. "All the buddhas" and the rest are synonyms of Vajrasattva.

The passage beginning "It is explained that Ṭakki is desire" shows the marks of union with the maṇḍala; that itself, being by nature the four goddesses Supreme Bliss and the rest, is called Ṭakki and so on. "Vajra" and the rest are their seals, and should be called "the sequence of the three samayas." To show incense and the rest in order, the passage beginning "ja" is spoken: these are the heart mantras of Supreme Bliss and the rest, and they should be understood to be Vajrāṅkuśa and the rest as well.

"tiṣṭha hūṃ" means "Stand firm!" As for these, "kha kha khā hi a pi ca a pi ca da ra da ra ru ṭa ma ṭa hu lu hu lu a ra ra a ho" and "a la la ho," and also the meaning of "hi" and the rest, they run from "Buddha Awakening" up to "clay drum"; because it would contradict the oral instructions, no explanation of meaning is given for these heart mantras. Then what are they? Since they are simply heart mantras for knowing the seals of music, they do not carry a meaning. If, however, one makes conceptual thought primary, then by being made into a sādhana they come to apply in every situation.

The flower and the rest, too: since it is explained that all the offerings are presented through song of the syllable hūṃ and so on, and through mantra recitation, accordingly in the maṇḍala the heart mantra of the lord himself, and Supreme Bliss and the rest, perform the acts of sealing with the seal and so on; therefore they are called by the synonyms the Sealer and so on.

The explanations "all-showing" and so on for "Buddha" and the rest teach this: "Buddha" and the rest, the words "all-showing" and so on, are explained to be Vajrasattva; in order, they are synonyms of "thoroughly showing" and so on. To show the names of the maṇḍala of Vairocana, the passage beginning "Buddha" is spoken, following in order the meaning of "Buddha" and the rest. "oṃ" and so on are the heart mantras of the four, She Who Proclaims All the Buddhas and the rest.

The heart mantra that is the cause of both omniscience and the knowing of bliss: the syllable "oṃ" and "sarva vid" are synonyms. "oṃ ru ru tiśtha siṅdṅha lo ca ni sarva artha sā dha ni svā hā" is the heart mantra of the four who possess the limbs of all the buddhas. To show the meaning of these very syllables, the passage beginning "the thing of saṃsāra" is spoken. Knowing the accomplishment of saṃsāra is the knowledge of the accomplishment of saṃsāra: "oṃ ru ru" means "Abide in saṃsāra!" "siddha lo ca ne" means "Knowing accomplishment, sit upon the seat whose character is the undefiled, and make all the aims of beings supreme!" This is the nirvāṇa that, ultimately, does not abide. Pacification is freedom from the afflictive obscurations and the obscurations to the knowable. The ultimate together with pacification is ultimate pacification: "Having accomplished all aims, abide by way of the ultimate!" And "svā hā" is to be construed as "Abide by way of pacification!"

Through the synonyms "Buddha" and the rest, that very Blessed One whose nature is the lord is shown. Vajrasattva too is shown by the word "fortunate one," because he is the essential nature of Buddha and the rest. To show the synonyms of the maṇḍala of glorious Heruka, the passage beginning "ko ta kṣa" is spoken. As for these ten names, the passage beginning "name and" is spoken in order to show that very thing fully by way of the ten sense sources.

"Vajra weapon ba ti" is a name of that very Blazing Vajra; that Blessed One is also called a yakṣa. "Glorious Heruka's great vajra that utterly destroys to ashes" is said to show that he destroys utterly with the blazing vajra. That very one is "the great wrathful one of all the buddhas," and it is called the great destruction of his blazing vajra. That of the khaṭvāṅga is called the great victory banner. The skull bowl is explained as the lotus. Great blood is called great honey. "hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ" mean, in order, "Protect!" and "Quickly!"; they are the heart mantras of the four, Gaurī and the rest. These, "ma ṭa ru ṭa sma ra ra ṭa," mean "Play!", "Hold in mind!", and so on. They are the heart mantras of the Incense Goddess and the rest.

This Blessed One is the great king of the vajra holders, the supreme Great Lord, and he is explained as the Fortunate One. Because the Buddha, the Sun, Lokeśvara, and Paramāśva are of the essential nature of Vajrasattva, the names of the maṇḍala of Padmanarteśvara are shown by "King Avalokiteśvara" and so on. Since he has become the essential nature of the four goddesses, She Who Gazes and the rest, there are "the Blessed One, Avalokita the Lord," and so on. "śuddha" and so on are the heart mantras of the six, She Who Gazes and the rest.

"Ākāśagarbha" and so on show the names belonging to the maṇḍala of Vajrasūrya. Because he is the essential nature of the lord of the six, the one bearing the synonymous names Ākāśagarbha and the rest should be understood to be the Blessed One himself. For the empowerment, that of the treasure called "thought" is "pa pa." That of the tip of the victory banner is "ta ta." The Great Offering is the heart mantra "tra ṭa tra ṭa tra ṭa." In the maṇḍalas from Buddha Awakening onward as well, the heart mantras are those very ones of Vajrasattva and the rest. In some, they are those very ones that belong to the goddess who is the vajra chief. And for all of them, it is understood that the heart mantra of the goddesses, Supreme Bliss and the rest, is that very heart mantra of their own lord.

"Various Lotus" and so on show the names of the maṇḍala of Paramāśva; "all the world" and "Various Lotus" are synonymous names. The great awakening mind is called Various Vajra. "Through devotion to purity in all things" means intending to dispel all laziness. "Friend" means "by the friends."

Since even those especially devoted to the meaning of recitation will attain accomplishment if they recite the syllable "hūṃ" and so on, the passage beginning "passionate attachment" is spoken in order to show the knowing of the wisdom seal. Vajrasattva's recitation is the syllable "hūṃ," which means, "This is it; be attached to it," that is, "It has been made directly manifest as your own self." The syllable "oṃ" is to be recited by Vairocana, and it means, "Realize its meaning," that is, "Make manifest the awakening that is your own self." Glorious Heruka's recitation is the vajra laughter "ha ha" explained earlier, and it means, "Unite with it," that is, "Generate what has become one with your great self." Padmanarteśvara's recitation is the syllable "oṃ," and it means, "Unite with its meaning," that is, "Your own self has made manifest the nature of reality, pure by nature." Vajrasūrya's recitation is the syllable "hi," and it means, "Rejoice in its meaning," that is, "Engage, with a self that delights, in the perfection of your unsurpassed aim." Paramāśva's recitation is any one of the syllables "hūṃ" and the rest, and it means, "Teach in accordance with it," that is, having become nondual with you, one has become mingled with the deity.

To set forth the seal of consciousness, for the purpose of knowing whether accomplishments and the like have been accomplished or not, the passage beginning "the glorious union of Vajrasattva" is spoken. The union of glorious Vajrasattva is this. First one visualizes Vajrasattva from the syllable "hūṃ" and performs the outer rites, such as offerings. Then one carries out the sequence of inviting him with the great seal into a boy or a girl, and so on, and blesses the child as Vajrasattva. One visualizes in the child's eyes a vajra arising from "hūṃ" and performs one hundred and eight recitations. The blessing of truth that is to come first is taught thus:

"By whatever truth there is in the words
'Phenomena are like reflections,
primordially pure and without stain,'
may this one see without error!"

Likewise for the ear, the word "all": one makes the blessing of truth, "By the truth that phenomena are like echoes, may this one hear without error!", and first gives command to the sounds. Likewise the yogin of Vairocana does this for actions and the like. Likewise the yogin of glorious Heruka does it for all the objects of desire, form and the rest. Likewise the yogin of Lokeśvara does it for that very lotus, in the manner of the opening of the Various Lotus. Likewise the yogin of Vajrasūrya does it for flowers and the like. Likewise the yogin of Paramāśva does it, by touching and not touching, for Vajrarāja and the rest and for wood and the like; touching or not touching the vajra and so on should be understood as the hand knowing or not knowing. In everything, whether a thing is to be done or not to be done and so on, the initial union is common to all.

To set forth the seal of protection, the passage beginning "the glorious union of Vajrasattva" is spoken. The yogin of Vajrasattva makes a vajra with the syllable "hūṃ" on ash from a charnel ground or the like, and by making a forehead mark with it he protects himself and others. Likewise, the torma and so on, with water that flows to the ocean and the like, are as before. White mustard seed and the like, generated inside a human skull, are the same. Likewise, pressing the lower lip with the teeth, bowing in a stance that leans to the left, holding the bell and holding the vajra in the right hand, one should bind the directions with a terrifying air, and so on. "The bliss of all the goddesses" is "supreme" and the rest; having become nondual with them or with others, one should complete the self-blessing and so on.

The yogin of Vairocana should bind the boundaries with the vajra in his right hand. If the yogin of glorious Heruka protects everything through the practice of his song and dance, fevers and the like will be destroyed. The yogin of Padmanarteśvara, through devotion to what is pure by nature, should complete freedom from obscurations and so on by opening the Various Lotus. The yogin of Vajrasūrya should protect by accomplishing his own body as a blazing vajra. The yogin of glorious Paramāśva protects himself by completing the sequence from binding the two vajra fists and putting on the armor up to clapping the palms. Others he protects by placing them within himself and with the armor and so on. In all these applications, one should recite one's own heart mantras.

To set forth knowledge of the great science for freeing beings from harms such as fever, the passage beginning "of the Dharma being" is spoken. The yogin of glorious Vajrasattva binds the two vajra fists, opens his eyes and looks with a peaceful gaze, and strokes the one stricken by fever or the like from the crown of the head down to the feet; he imagines the Blessed One, colored like the moon, filling the body of the one he is treating. For a cold fever, one imagines the body color as red. By this, everything will be cleared away.

Likewise, one binds the two vajra fists, joins them by hooking the little fingers, joins the two index fingers, imagines the fever and the like inside, and squeezes. If that same seal is transformed through the easy application and set down at once, it will clear away suffering and the like. "The index fingers facing each other" means that if one joins the index fingers to each other, grips, and acts as though drawing out a thorn, the thorn will come out. Moreover, if this same application and the like is done in the manner of squeezing, all illnesses will be cleared away. When it is done with the Eye of Accomplishment, the blind will see. If one grips with the vajra fist, puts it in the mouth, and acts in the manner of entering, the affliction will be cleared away. For clearing away fever and the like, the procedure is the same. For a child dwelling in the womb, one imagines it in the form of the deity and performs recitation, and it will come forth.

Within the joined vajra palms, one bends the two index fingers, makes the two thumbs even, and joins them with their two tips placed inside: this is the supreme hold, the uṣṇīṣa of the sattva. If one makes it twofold and places the right-hand sattva uṣṇīṣa on a child's head, the child will be free of illness and will live, and will attain inexhaustible bliss.

One makes the right hand like a nāga's hood and accomplishes what arises from it; if one gazes, blazing, by installing it, then by the power of what one wishes it devours everything, and so forth. The yogin of Vairocana touches the middle finger and thumb of the left fist with the right thumb and middle finger, and should see the Blessed One destroying the grahas with the blazing vajras that arise from that. The yogin of glorious Heruka, having made the malevolent one the object of the rite and facing him, binds the vajra seal and, imagining blazing vajras coming forth from it, should make it shine with the ḍākinī seal. The yogin of Paramāśva should ride every horse.

The yogin of Vajrasattva visualizes his body from the syllable "hūṃ" on the right and the left, and if he performs recitation, life and the like will increase. Likewise, if one generates seeds and the like as his body and then ploughs and cultivates, everything will flourish.

When one is fully engaged in maṇḍala, practice, and the like, dangers such as hostile armies may also arise. So, to set forth knowledge of the weapon seals for turning these back, the passage beginning "by a device" is spoken. What is shot by a device is the arrow and the like.

What is not thrown is the sword and the like. What is thrown by hand is the discus and the like. What is thrown and then retrieved is the noose and the like. What is wielded by strength is the "potent," the "playful," the "upright," the "heroic," arm strength, and the like.

To show what is shot by a device, the passage beginning "posture and" is spoken, and these too are taught in order. Of these, the postures are the vaiśākha stance, the circular stance, and the right-extended stance: five kinds in all. And of these:

Where the big toes and the heels are placed well together,
know that as the posture of standing even;
so those who know reality have explained.
With the toes of the feet turned outward,
the two thighs unmoving, standing upright,
twenty-six finger-joints apart on the inner side:
this is called the vaiśākha stance.
Made even like the cry of a goose,
with the two knees bent,
and the two hands drawn within:
this is known as the circular stance.
The left lowered and bent,
the right knee stretched out along the thigh,
set in line with the left,
thigh and knee joined,
stretched five spans in breadth:
this is known as the right-extended stance.
The reverse of that
should be known as the left-extended stance.
Not slack, and firm,
supple, with the limbs displayed,
broad, upright, and wholly pure.

These last lines are the application made with ease. "The manner of drawing to one's own heart" and so on is joining completely and drawing quickly. "The left hand well straightened" and so on is standing upright. "Bringing it up to the ear" and so on is holding. These words, "the syllable oṃ" and so on, fill the interval for as long as it lasts and are not to be spoken aloud; they mean "hold it for that long." "As one has drawn in order," with its distinctions and so on, is the purpose. "Accepted by the drinker" and so on is the release. "At the square" means drawn before it. "Warm and" means to the left side. "To be circled all around" means turned back behind. "Where it faces, turning the arrow" means abiding to the right. "Lowering" means planting below the target. "Praise and" means placing above. "Pressing with the foot" means pressing the left foot with the right. That pressing, in order, pierces the target from the direction of Agni. "The heel of the foot of the eye": pressing likewise with it, one pierces the targets in the directions of Nairṛti and of Vāyu; these are the seals of the ten. "Thrown forcefully" and so on shows the four kinds of throwing. "Unsteady hand" and so on is throwing by hand. "Striking all" and so on shows taking the target in five ways. Shooting by a device, too, is treated by those skilled in it under terms such as "action."

So that the deities of the maṇḍala may be placed with their seals, and to show the seal that knows all the activities by which yogins bless the union with the deity, the passage beginning "of the two ring fingers" is spoken. "Inside the two ring fingers" means inside the roots of the ring fingers. "The thumbs should be placed well" means joining the tips of the thumbs. "The index fingers should be placed well" means bending the tips of the index fingers and pressing them with the surface of the thumbs. This is the karma seal of the Blessed One Vajrasattva with his retinue. "Placing my thumb inside" means placing the thumb inside in the manner of holding the robe; this is the Blessed One Vairocana with his retinue. From the bound fist of glorious Heruka with his retinue, the little finger is extended. The lotus fist is Padmanarteśvara with his retinue. "The vajra fist, supreme jewel" is the jewel fist explained earlier, that of Vajrasūrya with his retinue. Binding the vajra fist, placing the thumb inside, and pressing it with all the fingers is Paramāśva with his retinue. All the karma seals are bound with the two hands, and each is bound together with its own heart mantra, just as it is.

To set forth knowledge of the glorious vajra karma seal for pacifying obstructors such as desire, the passage beginning "extending the foot" is spoken. The yogin of Vajrasattva performs the practice of "extending the foot" and the rest. And with "by the union of right and left," if he presses down, with the right foot and the left foot respectively, the figure of a man and of a woman, then by doing this everything, worldly and transcending the world, will come under his power. "The yogin's own task" means being endowed with the yoga of Vajrasattva.

Having thus done all these tasks, the yogin who has pacified the obstructors trains well in the seals of Vajrasattva and the rest; and to show knowledge of the seals of all accomplishments for the sake of thorough focus, the passage beginning "the fist at the heart" is spoken. The intended meaning is that if the yogin of Vajrasattva, in the feast maṇḍala and the like, binds the woman's seal whose nature is the fist at the heart, all things will be accomplished. "All the unions of women" refers to the hosts of goddesses, Supreme Bliss and the rest. "The left should be held in a haughty manner" means holding the bell with the left in a haughty manner. "The right hand at one's own heart" means holding the five-pronged vajra with the woman's seal, in the manner of praising at the heart. "All women" is the woman's seal headed by the principal ones, Supreme Bliss and the rest; this should be understood as to be applied in the vajra swagger.

The yogin of the Blessed One Vairocana, holding the Various Lotus with the left fist and with the right in a rising manner, should accomplish all lordship. The yogin of glorious Heruka should ring the bell with the left and, with the right, whirl and brandish the blazing five-pronged vajra, and so bring forth all illusions.
