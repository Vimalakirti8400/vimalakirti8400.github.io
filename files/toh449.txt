The King of Tantras, the Later Tantra from the Ultimate Assembly, the Source of All Activities (Samājaparamārthasarvakarmodaya Uttaratantrarāja)

The King of Tantras, the Later Tantra from the Ultimate Assembly, the Source of All Activities, known in Sanskrit as the Samāja Paramārtha Sarvakarmodaya Uttaratantrarāja and in Tibetan as Phrin las thams cad 'byung ba 'dus pa don dam pa las phyi ma'i rgyud kyi rgyal po, is Toh 449 in the Kangyur, where it belongs to the Tantra section among the yoginī tantras devoted to the goddess Tārā. It is a later or supplementary tantra, opening with the great Blessed Lady Samaya Tārā, supreme consort of all the tathāgatas and sovereign of the five buddha families, proclaiming her own vajra names. The text goes on to teach the secret practices of the completion stage, including union with a consort, the channels, winds and drops, the deities of the maṇḍala and their rites, and the view of emptiness and great bliss that underlies them, while repeatedly urging reverence for women as embodiments of wisdom. Its colophon records that it was translated into Tibetan by Tāranātha, who studied it with the South Indian master Buddhagupta and later checked it against commentaries with other Indian teachers.

In the language of India: samājaparamātha sarbakarmodya uttaratantrarāja. In the language of Tibet: The King of Tantras, the Later Tantra from the Ultimate Assembly, the Source of All Activities.

Homage to Tārā.

Then the great Blessed Lady, supreme consort of all the tathāgatas, called Samaya Tārā, sovereign of all the families, the tathāgata, vajra, jewel, lotus, and karma families, maker of the moving and the unmoving, whose nature is the stable and the moving, more secret than the secret, the great secret sovereign lady, brought forth these vajra names of her own from her vajra body, speech, and mind:

Blessed Lady Tārā, Vajrā, Samaya Tārā, great Vajrayoginī, she who is adorned with the five wisdoms, she who is empowered by all the buddhas, maṇḍala of wisdom, Lady of Lunar Bliss, Lady of Wondrous Bliss, Lady Desired by Body, Speech, and Mind, mistress of the aggregates, she who has become mistress of the sense bases, she who has fully attained the elements, whose nature is free of objects, sovereign lady of all tantras, she who is awakened through the five wisdoms, dustless, stainless, great secret mantra, utterly subtle, adorned with the major and minor marks, the Purifier, the Great Purifier, she who accomplishes all rites, she who bestows all desires, she who proclaims the sound of the ten syllables, Lady Delighting in Proclaiming Sound, the Ardent Lady, the Swift Lady, completely purified consciousness, firm and blissful, essence of wisdom, Lady of Great Bliss.

"Conventionally, the world exists;

Inner bliss, aware of itself,
This luminous reality alone.
There is no existence and no nirvāṇa,
No elements and no consciousness,
No entity and no meditation;
Suchness is like the ocean.
It transcends evil and merit and things to be done;
It is not an entity that is one or many.
All entities have become one;
The one wisdom itself emanates.
Knowing suffering, happiness, and objects,
It is the great bliss beyond them all.
There is no action and no agent;
It is unstirred, its nature pure.
There is no desire and no freedom from desire;
One does not fix upon a middle.
Through this wisdom of great desire,
Awakening is given to the passionate.

Saṃsāra is mind itself; there are no outer things.
Awakening will not come from anywhere other than mind.
Existence and peace are tasteless and without essence;
One's own mind, whose nature is compassion, the protector, is here."

In the past, from the union of the goddess Blissful One
And the buddha Akṣobhya,
I was born together with my brother Acala.
The Dharma taught by the arhats
Was being damaged by māras and asuras.
When the gods and asuras waged war,
Lord Acala hacked to pieces
Twelve hundred billion asura lords,
And enjoyed all of their wives.
Humans, gandharvas, kinnaras,
Yakṣas and nāgas he slew,
And cast them all into the field of Akṣobhya,
And the name of all of them became Akṣobhya the Victor.
Acala, having become one of great passion,
Relied on me, his sister, and on his mother too.
Through that supreme bliss of bliss,
Acala became the buddha, the Protector.
Acala said to the arhats:
"Through the sin of abandoning women,
You have abandoned wisdom and abandoned bliss,
And so have made your compassion meager indeed.
Whoever abandons wisdom and compassion
Thereby utterly abandons the Buddha,
The true Dharma and the Saṅgha as well.
Therefore monks who revile women
Are deficient in their vows and laden with sin.
True striving in restraint comes from union with women;
Since others have no pure conduct,
These shaven heads deceive the world with illusion."
Through the samaya of your sisters,
The chief monks of the four continents at that time,
Through the bliss of the samaya of women,
Attained unsurpassed awakening,
Renowned as Vairocana and the rest.
In this age my brother is Gautama,
And Gopā, his sister, is I, Tārā.
Through the bliss of eighty thousand women,
In a household of enjoyments he attained the ten grounds.
Then, when in the sacred fields he fully practiced
The conduct of the avadhūta,
He was known as "one who roams the whole world."
With Nandā and Nandabalā,
Through the bliss of food and of union,
He was empowered and became a perfect buddha.
Acala, in wrath, conquered Māra,
And the daughters of Māra, who displayed themselves,
He took into his care by practicing great desire.
Although apart from fierce conduct and sexual union
There is no other path to awakening,
For the sake of Maitreya, the great-minded, free of passion,
Because of his inclination, the Victor displayed an illusion.
Therefore women who practice me
Should please their brothers and others.
By the samaya of Acala the king,
Any yogin should honor
His sister, his daughter and his mother too.
If he delights all widows with bliss,
Acala is pleased.
Whatever man makes offerings to me
Is blessed by the wrathful Caṇḍa.
This recitation is the consolation of Acala.

oṃ mahā gambhi ryai mahā kā ru ṇi ke bha ga vāṃ bā le bar da a bha ya ha sti tāre tutta re ture svāhā This is for protection from piśācas.

A green wheel diagram with eight spokes:
In the center place the one to be accomplished;
On the eight spokes, the eight syllables,
Surrounded by the citadel of the mantra.
Green, it will protect the land;
White, it cheats death.
One is freed from illness and from prison,
And life, wealth, diligence,
Power and intelligence increase.
Salt, bezoar, urine,
Earth, citraka and the hero plant,
Musk and rotsen made into pills
Protect against poisons;
Practice the yoga of recitation and meditation.
With the root of piṇḍataka
And calf dung one paralyzes poison.
A threefold wheel diagram of sixteen spokes:
Write the mantra of Kurukullā,
And prepare an image of a couple in union.
Pierce the secret place with a copper needle,
Fill its mouth with urine and vermilion,
And heat it over a fire of juniper.
By striking with flowers, and with food, incense
And preparations of eye ointment, one brings under control.
Having imagined Tārā as white,
One recites a hundred thousand times in silence.

Arising from the union of the eternal Buddha,
Endowed with the four beginning with the Clever-Minded One:
Hastyali, dongra, cumin,
Drokmen, costus and lalaphü,
Drunk with goat's milk and butter;
If one eats calamus and haltya, recited over,
This will make the intellect increase.
Having offered the maṇḍala, the mudrā of fearlessness
Touched to the crown of the head protects from bandits and thieves.
Guggulu, the leaves of dāli,
Costus, musk and the hero plant combined,
Mixed with calamus and tree resin:
Pills made from these, recited over a hundred thousand times, should be eaten.
Protected at the five places with urine and lawa,
This is supreme for curing leprosy.
Flowers of bhriṅgarāja mixed in,
If inhaled through the nose, remove old age.
Through three years of union with women,
By retaining the semen one is made youthful.
Duga and nalesham in the ear
Remove deafness once a year has passed.
Sea foam and sea fruit
Mixed with goat urine destroy diseases of the eye.
oṃ sumbhi ro bha na ye sva ha
Koṣṭa bone and crow's eye
With a golden peg protect against the bride's illness.
Make a maṇḍala with the Hook Lady;
If one worships it, one will be freed from prison.
Haltī, bezoar and musk:
Hang the root mantra on a vase,
And at midnight bathe the pregnant woman;
She will give birth easily and without illness.
Mix the fruit of sutotsi with butter,
Recite over it and give it to the woman;
When she has bathed, even one who has the form of a barren woman
Will certainly come to have a son.
Hang up a protection wheel diagram,
Offer the maṇḍala and do the recitation,
And bathe the child every single day:
This is the best way to nurture children.

Again it was said:
At the navel, eight hrīḥ in the form of bees
Are sent forth, and one should draw in her essence.
jaḥ ho hūṃ a paṃ hi a karṣa ya aṅku śa jaḥ
If one makes a mark on the forehead,
One brings all women under one's control.

Again it was said:
Abiding at the center of the expanse of space,
Imagine a maṇḍala ten million leagues wide.
One who knows the rite confers empowerment there.
Arisen from the three vajra secrets
Of all the buddhas,
The empowerment of the abode to which all the three realms bow
Should be bestowed.
The man is made the wisdom of the Tathāgata,
The woman the goddess of knowledge.
Embracing a girl who has reached sixteen years,
The one who meditates on bliss is the master.
This secret must not be told to anyone.
If one should speak of it, at that time
The extremely sharp stake
Rests in the hand of the yoginī;
Whoever destroys the samaya
She pierces through with it supremely.
Kissing the bhaga, eat the caru:
This is the secret empowerment, the second.
The teacher, having given her to his son,
Through absorption in union one meditates on their equal taste.
When the bliss of the ten grounds is attained,
It is called the wisdom-gnosis.
Transcending passion and freedom from passion,
Unobjectified in the middle, luminous,
Unchanging, permanent and supremely subtle:
The fourth gnosis has no coming or going.

The one of the great discipline of uniting the two,
Accomplished in great bliss, is the master;
One full of passion, who with deceit reviles women
And fills his begging bowl, is no master.
The rite of the ground, the master's activities,
Empowerment, samaya, fire offering,
Consecration, the rite of the water offering,
The vajra, the bell, the rosary,
Filling and pouring, and likewise the six mudrās,
The measures of images and the final rites,
Averting, protection and recitation,
Destroying illness, grahas and fevers,
Increasing life, wealth and lineage,
Bringing under control, killing and expelling,
Dividing and suppressing, and the rites of temples:
These rites come from the master.
Likewise elixirs, the vidyādharas,
Various actions and accomplishments,
And many kinds of medicinal preparations
Are set forth in tantra after tantra:
The Saṃpuṭa, the Vajraḍāka,
The Catuṣpīṭha, likewise the Vajrapañjara,
The Saṃvarodaya and the Candraguhyatilaka,
Acala, Red Yamāri,
The great tantra of the Manifold Tārā,
Kurukullā, Ekajaṭī,
And the tantra of the eleven yoginīs:
These are the expanded applications of all.
Whatever others there are,
Know them as various tantras.
All that was taught in the age of strife
Is gathered together here.
Moreover, what is hidden in the others
Is here taught openly.
Even for ignorant fools,
The great bliss that abides forever,
The fourth gnosis, unchanging,
Is called the self-arisen dharmakāya.
Because it arises from conditions and is impermanent,
The wisdom-gnosis is not the fourth.
The action seal and the wisdom seal
Are not the meaning in the ultimate sense.
The ultimate is free of all designations;
Therefore it cannot be spoken.
The distraction of the world by desire,
The solitude of those free of passion,
The mental delight in the forms of faces and arms,
The accomplishment of bliss in the subtle drop,
The effort of meditating on the rega of the navel,
The supreme joy of seeing emptiness,
The mindless bliss when the winds dissolve,
The supreme joy of coming to work for beings,
The supreme bliss of union with women,
And the bliss of the mind and of the image:
These are all accompanied by conditions.
The innate great bliss is something else.
Body, speech and mind, with examples,
Passion and intermediate passion,
And what is called freedom from passion, together with letters,
Joy and supreme joy,
The joy-free, moving and unstable,
The knower of pleasure and pain,

Clear and unclear are mere signs;
In that, they have no essence.
In passion there is no real passion,
In freedom from passion no freedom from passion,
And even in the middle there is no middle.
All that arises from conditions is overcome.
Free of all things and entities,
It is the entity that is the self of all entities.
Its nature is unchanging bliss,
Without example and without letters,
Without movement and without change.
By the nature of great bliss,
Nirvāṇa alone is true.
In this maker of all there is no maker.
Self, sentient being and what is called the life principle,
The Principal, the Lord, atoms and the rest
Are all imagined within this gnosis.
Vajrasattva, the supreme deity,
He of the great truths of cessation and the path,
Whose stains were exhausted from the very first,
For whom all dharmas are awakened from the beginning,
Teaches this way of the inseparability of the two truths.
Just as a lamp placed in a house
Appears outside through five windows,
So the light of the lamp of the ground of all
Is emanated as what are called the five sense consciousnesses.
The mind conceives of them as five lamps.
Thinking, "The light and the flame are different,"
The fool, his mind divided, grasps at an "I."
Look at it as waves arising from water.
Whatever is the Dharma of the perfect buddhas
Abides in each and every being's element,
Undivided and unseen,
Like gold ore within the rock.
Union in body, speech and mind,
The four vajras made manifest,
Image, echo, the reality of space:
This is called the body of great bliss.
Navel, throat, the lotus of the heart,
And again the head are proclaimed;
Beings of the three existences arise
And dissolve into this very thing.

Thus spoke Vajrajñāna, the one who confers empowerment.

Then Vajravārāhī and the others, the mātṛkās, the īśvarīs, and the women of the gods, humans, nāgas, gandharvas, yakṣas, rākṣasas, kinnaras and the rest, as many as the atoms of thirty-two countless Mount Merus, fell into doubt. Being in two minds, they addressed the Blessed One Vajrasattva with these words:

"How was the consolation
Of the buddha Vajradhara taught?
Why was so little spoken in this age?
What was taught definitively about great passion?
How do those who recite vidyā-mantras
Perform the approach to the deity?
The body purified by the yoga of the winds,
And likewise, what are joy and the rest?
The supreme samaya of women,
And what is the nature of liberation
Through all bad eating and bad conduct?
O deity, Protector, please explain!
What here is the discipline of knowledge?
And how does the practice of forceful means take place?
Please explain as well the accomplishment
Of women and of men."

"Listen, O vajra one, listen well!
Two kinds of consolation are taught.
Here the first is Vajrasattva,
And afterward the Tathāgata.
In time past, long ago,
Vajrasattva, the supreme deity,
Gathering those whose nature is the buddhas,
Sported with the knowledge women, his consorts.
Abiding in the source of phenomena, the realm of space,
Sporting in yoga with an ocean of gnosis,
He gave birth to buddhas and bodhisattvas,
All of them, with none left out.
He whose self is the nature of great bliss
Entered after the realm of beings,
And so all attained the awakening of great bliss.
To those who clung to bliss,
The buddhas taught emptiness,
And by that, countless beings became fully awakened.
Later, those who clung to emptiness
All fell into the hells,
So the eternal Victor sent forth an emanation
And established them in the Middle Way.
By greatly accomplishing his own and others' aims,
He liberated immeasurable beings supremely.
Those following the conventional, holding to an I,
Thrown into turmoil by many conceptions,
He urged on to the supreme activity
That fulfills all the wishes of others.
Through emanating as the Jewel Sun,
They, with supreme joy,
Became inclined toward unsurpassed awakening.
Others, who were possessed of attachment
And at that time were bound by desire,
He emanated as Padmanarteśvara,
And by the path of the bliss of desire
Inexpressible numbers of beings became fully awakened.
Later, those attached to bliss
Degenerated through laziness,
And the evil-minded world fell into turmoil.
He emanated himself as Paramāśva, the king,
And established beings in diligence.
Adulterers, thieves, enemies and the rest:
Through manifest emanations, each in its own way,
He established them all in the vajra state.
When the diligent, through their various conducts,
Became exceedingly wrathful, fierce and vicious,
He emanated as the furious Heruka,
And came to delight in fierce killing.
Rākṣasas, yakṣas, bhūtas, grahas
And mātṛkās he destroyed, and with vidyā-mantras,
Substances, weapons, mechanical devices
And medicines too he destroyed them all.
In order to pacify the vicious ones of hatred,
Once again, into the Protector of great bliss
They will enter in time to come.
This is the consolation, the sacred maṇḍala.
Here there are the ultimate and the conventional,
Called the enjoyment body and the emanation body;
Others are cause and result:
So the consolation is taught as twofold.
Here, too, the ultimate is of two kinds:
Great bliss and suchness.

The two conventional ones, moreover,
Are provisionally posited as "the world" and "the maṇḍala."
Because lives are short, beings cling to striving,
Delight in killing living creatures,
And are distracted by indulging in desires;
So now the tantras are taught only briefly.
For thirty-two countless eons
One may strive and still have no awakening;
Since through bliss it is accomplished in this very life,
Therefore great passion is taught.
Through bliss, bliss will be attained;
The bliss of the buddhas is great bliss.
One accomplishes a cause that resembles the result;
Through an incomplete emptiness one passes beyond sorrow.
Being without sin, having faith,
Generating the mind supremely toward awakening,
Without avarice, arrogance or pride:
By this one will certainly accomplish it.
If even one who has committed the five acts of immediate retribution
Can become a practitioner of this,
What need is there to speak of others?
First recite the mantra of Amṛtakuṇḍalin,
Destroyer of Obstructors, a hundred thousand times,
Then build stūpas and the like,
And perform offerings, circumambulation and so on.
A woman of excellent limbs, endowed with faith,
Attractive, or else another,
One who practices mantra, compassionate and intelligent,
Possessed of courage: she is called the companion.
Where there are no brahmins, tīrthikas or śrāvakas,
There the beginner should dwell.
With a painted image and with offerings,
First meditate on the deity of one's family,
And strive at recitation, ten million upon ten million times.
Afterward, having meditated on the maṇḍala,
One should do a hundred thousand recitations,
And apply oneself to fire offerings and worship.
Having drawn a beautiful maṇḍala,
One should worship it for six months.
The mantrin, the mantra and likewise the companion,
The place, the time, the substances and diligence,
And the deity is said to be the seventh.
With samaya, diligence, patience,
Faith, compassion and unimpaired mantra,
If there is no laziness, one will quickly attain it.
When the deity and the vidyā-mantra are powerful,
And one has strength of mind and has obtained the signs,
By this one should accomplish the siddhis;
Otherwise there will be affliction.
Filling by gazing from the left,
And likewise drawing in from the right;
The gaze at the forehead is the recaka,
At the tip of the nose it is the vase.
From the navel the winds are produced:
Fire and wind,
And likewise water and earth;
Through the mind's activity in the cakras,
They abide above, at the sides, in front and below.
The life wind and the downward-clearing wind,
The upward-moving, the pervading, and the fire-equalizing abide,
Moving through all the cakras.
The nāga, the tortoise and the rest likewise,
The snyema and the others abide at ease.
In the eyes, the ears and the nose,
The tongue and the body is the mind of awakening.
Likewise, although there are nine doors,
There are four cities and four doors:
Two are the sacred doors,
And the inferior doors are of two kinds.
By binding their activity, the doors are opened,
And just as water flows down from a canal,
So by the yoga of the winds
The body is purified.

A foremost practitioner, whether a layman, a novice or a monk, dwelling on a very solitary mountain, in a forest, in a pleasure garden or at home, takes a young woman endowed with youth, or one who has come of age,

Of beautiful form, with lovely eyes and fine hair.
Having found one of steady mind on this path,
He fully undertakes the supreme joy.
Unattached to the enjoyment of desires,
But passionately devoted to bliss,
The one endowed with yoga, delighted at the sight of her,
From that obtains the siddhi of union.
Her body full and well developed,
With sweet words of delight and with music,
Embracing, kissing and rubbing the breasts,
Arousing the stamen with its petals,
Moving and still, the delights are hastened.
For oneself, whose eyes come to close,
Since there is much activity and little bliss as its fruit,
This partial bliss is the first joy.
Having looked above and below,
Into the moist lotus, fully opened,
The swollen vajra is fully inserted:
Trembling and change, and the sound of sīt,
The syllable u with warmth, the sound failing,
And the girl will attain gnosis.
The effortless, pervasive bliss
Is rightly called supreme joy.
From the moving of the two who fully move,
Crying or laughing or behaving like a child,
Unable to bear the desire, mindfulness lost,
The gnosis of bliss and clarity in oneself,
Free of intrinsic nature: this is the innate joy.
Having abandoned the joy that is free of its savor,
One should hold back with supreme gnosis,
Or else by the practice of forceful means.
The vajra contracting and the lotus closing,
Recollecting the bliss: this is the joy of joy.
The supreme bindings are of seven kinds:
Placing both feet on the shoulders,
Above, at the side, facing, from behind,
Fully extended, and crossed.
With substances one should honor the drop.
All the practices of the sacred, hot place of birth
Should be performed.
By this gnosis will increase.
The women who dwell in the sacred fields
Should be subdued through the practice of great desire;
They are not pleased by worship with flowers and the like.
Whatever practice awakens the vajra and the lotus,
That should be relied upon.
Releasing the mind of awakening into the center of the lotus,
And making it the token of her gnosis,
Drink it, together with the flower, through the liṅga:
This is the bliss of rasāyana.
Having caused supreme joy and the rest to abide,
Though it is unchanging, it shows itself as change.
With half the semen, in the cakra
Of the wisdom woman, coming and going are exhausted.
Gazing on beautiful form, the sounds of delight,
Her scent, drinking the honey of her lips,
Touch, and the joining of each pair,
The vajra raised up by the bhaga,
Squeezing, and moistening with dew,
Warmth and movement, the three consciousnesses:
These are proclaimed as the grounds of supreme joy and the rest.
Conceal the aggregates in the jewel vessel.
For beginners
There is no gnosis in abandoning women.
Through the union of the two at the beginning and the end,
The bliss of the buddhas is fully accomplished.
A master who lacks the capacity, when conferring empowerment,
Should cause the mind of awakening to descend into the lotus,
And thus make offering to the sugatas;
If it is emitted elsewhere, siddhi will be far away.
Women too are liberated by this:
With devices of finger, wood or leather
One should awaken the channels.
In order fully to liberate women of great desire,
The yoginī tantras have been extensively opened.
The seventy-two producers of bliss
Are the special channels of women.
Women quickly attain awakening;
Men are not so.
With other perceptions, incompleteness
And non-objectification, there is no downfall from this;
But those who, with desire, emit while waking or in dreams
Incur a fault.
Enjoying desire with an excellent woman at the four times
Is the chief of chiefs among the samayas.
If one does not worship the lotus
Every month, or every single year,
Then through the impairment of the samaya
One falls into the Great Heat hell.
Those who desire siddhi should honor all women,
And bow to them with respect.

If one should show respect even to female animals,
Why would one not respect human women?
Wisdom is woman, in the form of a human woman,
Who has become mother, sister and daughter,
Deity, guru and companion,
Most loving, gentle and kind.
The ignorant, who do not repay what has been done for them,
And revile her, saying, "Hers is an inferior birth,"
Will meet with disease, epidemics and a short life,
And will surely be cooked in hell.
The ignorant mantrin who torments a woman
Breaks every samaya, and so
In that very instant falls into Avīci.
If one has become the enemy of a beloved brother,
Even honoring his brother will not please him.
Just so, with one who reviles women, the goddesses
Are displeased and do not accept his offerings.
Though for countless tens of millions of ages
The unfortunate practice only what is good,
They do not attain precious awakening;
Through this union with the mudrā
It is attained in this very life.
Meditating on the sameness of intrinsic nature
And fully accomplishing the bliss of the mudrā,
If from union with a woman one makes manifest
The Great Seal (mahāmudrā),
Then bathing and purity are not needed,
And even without austerities and vows
It is accomplished through bliss itself and joy.
Therefore one enjoys as one wishes,
Eats everything, and likewise engages in everything;
Even in one's movements one does as one pleases.
In whatever way one acts, one will be liberated.
From images, cast statues and the like
Yoga does not arise;
But if one strives earnestly at the mind of awakening,
From that, yoga becomes the deity.
All moving, speaking and conduct
Become mudrā, mantra and rite.
Having properly taken up a lamp,
One retrieves a jewel lying amid the darkness;
So in the darkness of the obscurations
One should seek the dharmadhātu through the bliss of woman.
Knowing that those who dwell very close to it
Have no respect for that very dharma of theirs,
The Victor taught freedom from desire;
In the ultimate sense it is not so.

It was further said:
First, practice in secret, at night.
If the mind is not stable in that,
Gather it in and take pleasure in the ordinary way.
Otherwise, with the observance of the madman,
Wander everywhere like a piśāca.
Having accomplished the miraculous powers and the five superknowledges,
Practice openly in the world.
If one has attained the noble grounds,
One then practices the conduct victorious in all directions,
Roaming through all the realms;
Or else, in a border land or in one's own home,
One is liberated through hidden conduct.
Or else there is the maṇḍala of the gathering:
Twelve women at play,
Adorned with ornaments, their faces and the rest made beautiful.
The woman of the east comes to the center,
And the other takes her pleasure in the east, and so on,
Playing in many and various ways.
Practice this in pleasure groves and similar places.
Performing every kind of song and dance,
Perform the empowerments and all the rites.

It was further said:
Open the door of the vajra,
And likewise open the lotus wide.
With a device of wood or clay,
One should play in the anal passage.
Restraining the life wind and likewise the downward-clearing wind,
Make them into a single ball.
Having entered into play with joy,
Unite slowly, slowly,
Opening the door and pressing with the finger.
By the probe of the maiden's channel
The dhūtī is opened.
In its place the wind expands,
And all the wheels of the channels awaken;
Caṇḍālī blazes, and there is melting and dripping.
The fire that blazes through forceful union
Courses through the rasanā;
The dripping is from the lalanā;
The fluids mingle in the central channel (avadhūtī).
Blazing, it goes from wheel to wheel;
Turned back many times and blazing again,
It pierces the egg of Brahmā.
By its falling from there,
The seal of Dharma is fully accomplished.
This is called manifest awakening.
Contemplate the bliss that arises as the goddess.
Take a caṇḍāla woman, a brahmin's daughter,
A merchant woman, a singer, a woman arrow-maker,
A dancer, and other women of the families;
Or else contemplate a goddess, a kinnarī,
A nāginī, a gandharvī, and the like.
The melted drop, the form of the moon,
Together with the flower, the sun, one should partake of.
Method and wisdom having become one,
This assemblage of semen, blood and wind
Here dissolves.
What is called body and mind is transcended;
This is the very transformation of the ground of all (ālaya).
From this comes perfect enjoyment (saṃbhoga).
Or else, like Siddhārtha, as an illusion,
One unites fully with Māmakī and the others.
The Buddha himself, in embodied form,
Unites with the goddess in great passion;
Yet because inclinations differ,
He conjures emanations and magical displays.

It was further said:
oṃ kartika sarva māraṇaṃ maṃsa kartaya kartaya hūṃ phaṭ
oṃ vajra kāpāla sarva śatrūṃrakta dhāraya dhāraya hūṃ phaṭ
oṃ hana hana māraya māraya sarva śatru triśūla hūṃ phaṭ
This is the observance.
Images, letters and emblems
Are fixed in the three maṇḍalas;
Having emerged from the heart, they are placed in the heart.
Then the weapons and mudrās are set out,
And by command the rites and siddhis are authorized.
Embracing the maidens
And attaining bliss from caressing their breasts:
This is the bestowal of the master's empowerment.

The Tārās of pacifying and the other rites are white, yellow, red and black. They abide on water, on the great power of earth, on fire and on wind, and ride a king of geese, an elephant, a horse and a buffalo. The goddess of pacifying holds the gesture of fearlessness and a white utpala. The goddess of enriching holds the gesture of supreme giving, a vase and an ear of grain. The subjugating goddess holds a hook and a noose. The slaying goddess holds a sword and the noose of time. These are the goddesses of the directions.

Those placed in the four corners:
Buddha, Jewel and Dharma,
And Vajra Tārā are to be imagined,
White, yellow and red,
And not white; they are four-armed,
Holding the boon-granting gesture and an utpala,
A ḍamaru, and likewise a trident.
Six-faced, six-armed, holding a hammer
And a noose, six-legged,
Yamāntaka, crusher of Brahmā;
His hands busy with sword and skull cup,
Crushing crooked-eyed Indra and Īśāna;
The supreme one who holds a staff, a lotus,
A wheel and a noose;
The king of wrathful ones, Padmāntaka,
Blazing with splendor like the fire at the end of the eon,
Pressing down nāgas and Yama with his feet;
Kuṇḍalin, with a vajra kīla,
With a crossed vajra and a hammer,
Holding a bell and a kīla,
Intent on striking the vināyakas together with Māra.
These three heroes are the color of clouds.
The others, whoever they are, are messenger women;
Make each accord with its own rite.

This is the fourth chapter, on all the secrets.

Then the retinue offered praise:
You who are the union of the two organs,
Whose very nature is without intrinsic nature,
Who wield the weapons of wisdom,
Cutter of the various fierce rites,

Lady of mantra, encircled by hosts of mātṛkās,
Net of Indra, with rays of light streaming out and swirling,
You who embrace the lovely coemergent one,
O you of great intelligence, grant me empowerment!

When they had made this supplication, the glorious Vajrasattva, through union in equipoise with Samaya Tārā, entered completely into the bodies of all the tathāgatas, and the vajra let fall the awakening mind, which completely filled every realm of space. By this, all sentient beings attained supreme bliss.

In places of utter solitude,
Destroy the māras with the syllable hūṃ.
oṃ āḥ āli kāli:
The two channels, moon and sun.
These are the mirror-like wisdom.
That which has become seed and sign
I have explained as the discriminating wisdom.
Having become one is the wisdom of equality.
Working for the benefit of beings is the accomplishment of activities.
The perfected image is the dharmadhātu.
The union of the two organs, the three vajras,
The deity of all perfect mantras;
Secret, great secret, and supremely secret;
Yoga and great yoga,
Anuyoga and atiyoga;
The three kinds of wisdom of emptiness:
This is the principal of principals of all the tantras.
The indestructible, the mantra and the substance
I have explained as the three drops.
Blessing, full imagining,
And full perfection: these are the three yogas.
The unclear, the one of small wisdom,
The one of wisdom, and the one of great wisdom:
