The Torma Ritual for Bringing Relief to the Pretī Jvālāmukhī

The Torma Ritual for Bringing Relief to the Pretī Jvālāmukhī, known in Tibetan as Yi dags mo kha 'bar ma dbugs dbyung ba'i gtor ma'i cho ga, is a short ritual scripture concerning offerings made to hungry ghosts, or pretas. It opens with the Buddha teaching at the Nyagrodha Park near Kapilavastu, where Ānanda, meditating alone late at night, is visited by the pretī Jvālāmukhī, whose name means "Flaming Mouth." She warns him that he will die in two days and be reborn among the pretas unless he makes generous offerings of food and drink to countless hungry spirits. The text goes on to set out a torma offering ritual, using sacred food offerings and recitation, through which such beings can be relieved of their hunger and suffering and the practitioner's own life and merit protected. It is Toh 1079 in the Kangyur, where it belongs among the dhāraṇī and ritual texts associated with the tantra section, and its story is closely related to the widely practiced East Asian rites for feeding hungry ghosts.

The Torma Ritual for Bringing Relief to the Pretī Jvālāmukhī

Homage to the Three Jewels.

At that time the Blessed One was staying at Kapilavastu, in the Nyagrodha Park, together with a great saṅgha of monks, a great assembly of bodhisattvas, and a great assembly of sentient beings, and he was teaching the Dharma.

Then Ānanda was sitting in solitude, settled inwardly and attending one-pointedly, and so was bringing the Dharma to direct perception. In the last part of the night, the pretī Jvālāmukhī appeared before him and said, "O Ānanda, the day after tomorrow you will die, and you will be born here in the realm of the pretas."

Ānanda said, "What means is there to rely on?"

The pretī said, "Ānanda, if tomorrow you give food and drink, seven great Magadhan measures each, to every one of the pretas, who are as numerous as the sands of a hundred thousand koṭis of niyutas of Ganges rivers; if you also satisfy a hundred thousand brahmin sages; and if you make offerings to the Three Jewels in my name, then your life will be extended, and I too will pass from the world of the pretas and be born in the world of the higher realms."

Then Ānanda saw the pretī Jvālāmukhī: gaunt, ugly in color, her body withered, flames blazing from her mouth, her belly and mouth parched, her mass of matted hair disheveled, her body hair and nails long. Having heard those harsh and unpleasant words, he was frightened and terrified, and his hair stood on end. He rose from his seat and, with great haste and the force of great speed, went to where the Blessed One was. Having arrived, he bowed at the Blessed One's feet and, trembling, spoke these words:

"Blessed One, please be my refuge! Sugata, please be my refuge! I am going to lose my life the day after tomorrow. Blessed One, I saw the pretī Jvālāmukhī, and she said, 'You will die the day after tomorrow.' When I asked, 'What means do I have?' she replied, 'If you satisfy the pretas, who are as numerous as the sands of a hundred thousand koṭis of niyutas of Ganges rivers, and also satisfy a hundred thousand brahmin sages, then your life will be extended.' Blessed One, please tell me how I should accomplish this."

Then the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Śākyamuni, spoke these words to the venerable Ānanda: "Ānanda, there is a means by which you will satisfy the pretas and the brahmin sages, so do not be afraid. Ānanda, there is a dhāraṇī called Radiant Everywhere, Great Victor, Mighty Light. Merely by giving with it, the pretas, as numerous as the sands of a hundred thousand koṭis of niyutas of Ganges rivers, will be satisfied, and each preta and each brahmin sage will be given seven Magadhan measures of food and drink. Long ago, when I was a brahmin, I received it from the bodhisattva, the great being Lokeśvara, and from the Tathāgata Lokeśvaraprabha. With it I satisfied with food and drink pretas and brahmin sages beyond counting, a great many indeed, and all of them passed from the world of the pretas and were born in the world of the gods. Ānanda, take it up and master it:

namaḥ sarba tathāgata abalokite oṃ sambhara sambhara hūṃ

Ānanda, one should recite this seven times over the torma for the pretas, then stretch out one's hand and give it at the doorway. One should also snap one's fingers. When this is done, the moment it is given, the pretas of the four directions, as numerous as the sands of a hundred thousand koṭis of niyutas of Ganges rivers, will be satisfied, and each preta will be given seven Magadhan measures. Merely by eating it they will pass from the world of the pretas and be born among the gods.

Ānanda, you should teach this constantly, continuously and without interruption to monks, nuns, laymen and laywomen. When this is done, one will possess a mass of merit, and one's life will be long. One will possess the mass of merit of a hundred thousand koṭis of tathāgatas. One will never be seen by the roaming nonhumans, bhūtas, piśācas and the like, yakṣas, rākṣasas, grahas or pretas. One will also possess strength and diligence. One will also be endowed with beauty, with splendor, and with mindfulness.

One who wishes to satisfy the brahmin sages should recite it seven times over a vessel filled with water for offering and with things to be presented, and give it at a place where there is flowing water. When this is done, brahmin sages as numerous as the sands of many hundreds of thousands of koṭis of niyutas of Ganges rivers will be satisfied with the food and drink of the gods. Those brahmin sages will also be satisfied in their faculties, and all of them will utter the sound, 'oṃ, may there be well-being!' Those beings, too, will become completely pure in their intentions and will possess the splendor of a brahmin. They will also always live the holy life. They will also come to possess roots of virtue planted with tathāgatas as numerous as the sands of a hundred thousand koṭis of niyutas of Ganges rivers. All their enemies will always be crushed.

One should recite it twenty-one times over flowers, incense, perfume, or provisions of food and drink, and present them to the Buddha, the Dharma and the Saṅgha. When this is done, that son of good family or daughter of good family, that monk or nun, that layman or laywoman, will have made offerings, with offerings of the gods and the supreme offerings of the tathāgatas, to the Buddha, the Dharma and the Saṅgha of the buddhafields of all the tathāgatas of the ten directions. They will have honored them and served them. They will also be held in mind and given prophecy by all the tathāgatas, and be protected on all sides by all the gods."

"Ānanda, go and become thoroughly familiar with this. Teach it correctly to all sentient beings as well. And gather all the accumulations of the roots of virtue too. This I declare."

The Torma Ritual for Bringing Relief to the Pretī Jvālāmukhī is complete.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The name of the tathāgata is rendered Lokeśvaraprabha from the Tibetan "Lord of the World Light"; the Sanskrit form is a reconstruction. The dhāraṇī's name, "Radiant Everywhere, Great Victor, Mighty Light," is translated from the Tibetan because its Sanskrit form cannot be securely identified. The Tibetan term for the day of Ānanda's death means "the day after tomorrow"; some parallel versions speak of three days.  The phrase rendered "teach it correctly to all sentient beings as well" can also be read as "teach it to all sentient beings again and again, correctly." The Tibetan permits both readings.
