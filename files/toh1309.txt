An Extensive Exposition of the Means of Accomplishment of Nairātmyā (Nairātmasādhanapauṣṭikanirdeśa)

An Extensive Exposition of the Means of Accomplishment of Nairātmyā, given in its Sanskrit title line as Nairātmasādhanapauṣṭikanirdeśa and known in Tibetan as Dakmemé Drubtap Gyepar Tenpa, is a tantric meditation manual, or sādhana, devoted to Nairātmyā, the goddess of selflessness who is the consort of Hevajra. Opening with homage to the Blessed Mother, the Perfection of Wisdom, it guides the practitioner through a full day of practice: the morning preliminaries of offering, confession, refuge, the awakening mind and the four immeasurables, meditation on emptiness, the visualization of Nairātmyā and her maṇḍala of fifteen yoginīs, the purification of the aggregates, elements and sense bases as goddesses, empowerment, recitation of the vowel mantra, and the midday and evening sessions with torma offering, dismissal of the deities and dedication. Its colophon attributes it to the glorious Ratnākara. It is Toh 1309 in the Tengyur, where it belongs to the tantra commentaries section among the practice texts of the Hevajra cycle.

In the language of India: nairātmasādhanapauṣṭikanirdeśa. In the language of Tibet: An Extensive Exposition of the Means of Accomplishment of Nairātmyā.

Homage to the Blessed Mother, the Perfection of Wisdom.

Although in general there are many methods for seeking reality, here, in this context, the mantra practitioner who has been made a fit vessel for meditation should rise early in the morning. Visualizing at his own heart, upon a moon disk, the seed syllable of Nairātmyā, with a garland of light, he should wash his face and so forth, carry out the protection of the yoga, of the place and of himself, and then, having fully entered the place of meditative concentration, sit on a comfortable seat and recite his own mantra over the offering water.

He should visualize the maṇḍala of Nairātmyā, the embodiment in which all the tathāgatas of the three times are perfectly gathered, as abiding in the space of the sky before him, and make offerings to it with the eight offering goddesses sent forth from the seed syllable at his own heart:

Gaurī, holding the hare-marked moon;
Caurī, holding the sun;
Vetālī, water vessel in hand;
Ghasmarī, holding a vessel of medicine;
Pukkasī, holding a vajra in her hand;
Śavarī, holding the supreme essence;
likewise Caṇḍālī, with her small drum;
and Ḍombī, embracing and holding the neck.

Having made perfect offering with these, he should perform the confession of wrongdoing and the rest before them, as follows:

"I confess each and every one of my wrongdoings. I rejoice in all the virtue of ordinary beings and of noble ones. Until I reach awakening, I go for refuge to the Buddha, I go for refuge to the Dharma, I go for refuge to the Saṅgha. In order to bring happiness and benefit to all sentient beings, and so that those who have the fortune for the three kinds of awakening may attain those three awakenings, I give rise to the mind set on supreme, unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening."

Then, in order to bring excellent happiness to all sentient beings, one should cultivate loving-kindness. Then compassion, which takes the form of removing the suffering of all sentient beings. Then joy, which takes the form of wishing that all sentient beings never be parted from happiness. Then equanimity, which takes the form of joining all sentient beings to worldly and world-transcending virtues.

Then: all phenomena arise from the mind, and as appearances they are not real. Thus the mind's appearing as objects, as apprehended and apprehender, is an error to be dispelled; like a dream, it is not fixed. In order to dispel the error completely, one should completely dispel what appears as objects, such as blue and yellow. For this reason, the various appearances are one's own mind; and one should view that mind as like the sky at midday in autumn, stainless, without appearances, mere boundless clarity. This is the ultimate awakening mind. This is the primordial Perfection of Wisdom; such is the supreme wisdom of emptiness. Here one should bless it with the mantra and meditate on it. The mantra is: oṃ śūnyatājñānavajrasvabhāva ātmako'haṃ.

After that:

From the letter repha, visualize a sun in front;
on that sun, hūṃ, the very substance of a crossed vajra.
With that vajra one should visualize
the fence and the net being bound.

Then one should visualize the source of phenomena (dharmodaya), white like the autumn moon, an upward-pointing triangle whose interior abides as the sphere of great emptiness. In its center is the yellow square maṇḍala of Mahendra. Above that is the round white maṇḍala of water. Above that is the red triangle of fire. Above that is the blue bow-shaped wind. These transform completely into a great multicolored lotus with anthers, marked with a crossed vajra, upon which one should visualize a palace constructed in tiers. It is made of the nature of five kinds of jewels, supremely blazing; square, with four doors, and adorned with four archways. On its ledge, two sets of eight offering goddesses present offerings. It is beautified with garlands and half-garlands, adorned with vajra threads, and filled with the sound of bells on crest ornaments that move about its various upper sides as the wind stirs them.

In its central tier one should visualize an eight-petaled lotus. On its anthers and in the four directions, one should imagine five corpse seats. In the corners of the outer ring there are four, at the doors four, one below and one above.

Then, at the heart of the central corpse seat, the meditator's mind, meditated on before as boundless and clear like crystal, is gathered in the form of a garland of the fifteen vowel letters; and that melts completely, and one should visualize it as a full moon. Above that, the garland of the thirty-four consonant classes melts completely and becomes a sun disk. Above that, the letter ā becomes a curved knife. At its center is the letter a. From that letter a all the tathāgatas are emanated in the form of fifteen yoginīs; and when they are perfectly gathered back into it, the moon, the sun, the curved knife and the seed syllable melt completely, and by this one should meditate on the yoga of the Blessed Lady Nairātmyā.

She is black in color, greatly fierce,
adorned with the five mudrās:
the wheel, the earrings, the necklace,
the bracelets and the girdle;
her lower garment of tiger skin blazes with light;
her reddish-yellow hair stands upright;
she has two arms and a single face,
her brow knit in wrath, her eyes red.
In her left she holds a skull cup and a khaṭvāṅga,
in her right she holds a curved knife.
She abides in a dance in the half-cross-legged posture,
on a moon seat, radiant with the hare-marked one's light,
upon the lotus of the four wheels,
adorned with four syllables.
Between her breasts, on a moon, a curved knife,
where the seed syllable abides, beautiful with light.

These are the first yoga, the first meditative absorption, and the essential nature body.

Then, after that, from the seed syllable abiding on the emblem at the heart one should send forth fourteen moon seats and place them on the heart of each of the corpse seats. Then again from that same seed syllable one should send forth fourteen sun disks and set them upon those very moons. From the even union of moon and sun the seed syllables arise upon the suns. Here, on the suns in the eastern and other directions of the inner ring, there are, in order, ā i ī u.

On the suns in the corners of the outer ring, beginning with the northeast, there are, in order, ū ṛ ṝ ḷ. On the suns of the doors, beginning with the east, there are ḹ e ai o. On the sun below is au. On the sun above is aṃ. From each seed syllable comes a curved knife, and the curved knives and seed syllables melt completely and become fourteen goddesses, all of them just like the yoginī Nairātmyā.

On the petals of the directions are Vajrā, Gaurī, Vāriyoginī and Vajraḍākinī. In the corners are Pukkasī, Śavarī, Caṇḍālī and Ḍombī. At the doors are Gaurī, Caurī, Vetālī and Ghasmarī. These twelve goddesses face toward Nairātmyā. Below is Bhūcarī, who gazes downward. Above is Khecarī, who gazes upward. At the heart of each of them, on a moon, rests a curved knife, at whose center is her own seed syllable.

After that, one summons the wisdom circle with the light rays of the mantra, expels the obstructors with the syllable hūṃ, and, in order to please those who dwell in the sky, offers with one's own mantra the offering water, the water for the feet and the water for the mouth, over which the mantra was previously recited. Then, saying jaḥ hūṃ vaṃ hoḥ, one summons the wisdom maṇḍala into the pledge maṇḍala, binds it and brings it under control, and should imagine that the two have become of one taste, like water mixed with milk.

Then one should see Vajrā abiding as the very nature of one's own aggregate of form. In the aggregate of feeling is Gaurī. In the aggregate of perception is Vāriyoginī. In the aggregate of formations is Vajraḍākinī. In the aggregate of consciousness is Nairātmyā. In the earth element is Pukkasī. In the water element is Śavarī. In the fire element is Caṇḍālī. In the wind element is Ḍombī. In the sense base of form is Gaurī. In the sense base of sound is Caurī. In the sense base of smell is Vetālī. In the sense base of taste is Ghasmarī. In the sense base of touch is Bhūcarī. In the sense base of phenomena is Khecarī. Again, in the sense base of the eye is Vajrā. In the sense base of the ear is Nairātmyā. In the sense base of the nose is Gaurī. In the sense base of the tongue is Vāriyoginī. In the sense base of the body is Vajraḍākinī. In the sense base of the mind is Nairātmyā. In aversion is Nairātmyā. In delusion is Vajrā. In attachment is Vāriyoginī. In pride is Gaurī. In jealousy is Vajraḍākinī.

At the head of each of them one should bless the body with the syllable oṃ. At the throat one blesses speech with the syllable āḥ. At the heart one should bless the mind with the syllable hūṃ. In the same way, one should firmly resolve that the remaining aggregates, elements, sense bases, afflictions and secondary afflictions are completely pure.

Then, with the light rays of the seed syllable at one's own heart, one summons all the tathāgatas and sets them in the sky. One should make perfect, manifest offerings to them with the eight mothers (mātṛkās) emanated from the seed syllable at one's own heart, and request empowerment. Then one should imagine that they empower the lord of the maṇḍala and the deities of that same maṇḍala with vases of precious jewels filled with the nature of the five wisdoms. One should seal them with the five families that arise from the water of empowerment:

By the Heart, the Eternal, the Jewel,
Lokeśvara and the Accomplisher of Aims.
By the Heart, the Eternal, the Jewel and Lokeśvara,
those who dwell in the intermediate directions.
For the door guardians it is likewise.
By Delusion and Passion, those below and above.

Then one should send forth the eight offering goddesses from the mantra at one's own heart, emanate them, gather them back perfectly, and make offerings. Of these, the first six, with the vessels in their hands, do a little: the offering substances appear, each distinct and very clear, in the skull cups held in the hands of those to whom offering is made, and they accept them. One should see that they enjoy these fully and are satisfied. The eighth, Ḍombī, embraces the neck of the Lady of the Wheel. Apart from her, the other seven yoginīs of the maṇḍala enter into that. Therefore all of them enjoy fully through the vajra born from abiding in the lotus of the Blessed Mother herself.

Black, red, and then yellow,
green, blue and white, in order:
as mere natural joy
one should imagine them, on the Chief Lady of the Wheel.

These are what is called the Supreme King of the Maṇḍala, the second meditative absorption, and the enjoyment body.

After that, urged by the Lord of the Wheel, all the goddesses in turn emanate from their own bodies mere clouds of corresponding forms, and so devote themselves to the welfare of beings, their own bodies being like mere clouds. These are gathered back into the root bodies. Here the door guardians and Bhūcarī establish sentient beings, respectively, in the faculties of faith, diligence, mindfulness, meditative absorption and wisdom. Khecarī establishes them in the powers of faith, diligence, mindfulness, meditative absorption and wisdom. Pukkasī establishes them in the grounds called Very Joyful, Stainless and Luminous. Śavarī establishes them in Radiant, Difficult to Conquer and Manifest. Caṇḍālī establishes them in Far-Reaching and Immovable. Ḍombī establishes them in Good Intelligence and Cloud of Dharma. The remaining ones establish them in the grounds of the five buddhas: the four goddesses of the directions in the four wisdoms, beginning with the mirror-like wisdom, and the Blessed Mother Nairātmyā herself in the utterly pure wisdom of the sphere of reality. These are the third meditative absorption, called the Supreme King of Action, and the emanation body.

When one grows weary, one rests for a moment, and then recites in order the blazing mantra syllables, mentally written as a garland of letters upon the moon at the heart, reciting slowly for as long as one does not grow weary. The mantra is the fifteen vowels with svāhā placed at the end.

Then one dedicates the merit completely with two verses, saying:

"The riches of all the buddhas, every one,
the state of great Vajradhara:
through this virtue may I attain it,
and may beings too attain it there.

The conduct practiced for the sake of perfect awakening,
and what the Vajra of Awakening has proclaimed
of the perfectly complete buddhas:
may I practice both of these conducts."

Drawing the deities of the maṇḍala into the drop of the seed syllable at one's own heart, one rises and, as is comfortable, maintaining the pride of one's own deity and never parting from it, one should carry out all one's activities.

At the midday session, one enters the shrine room, sits on a comfortable seat, and sees oneself as the yoginī Nairātmyā. Sending forth the fourteen goddesses from the mantra at one's own heart, one should see them seated in their respective places. One should resolve for a moment that the aggregates and so on of oneself and of them are completely pure and are blessed by the three syllables, give the empowerment as before, and continue through to the conclusion with the dedication. In the same way, one should gather in the maṇḍala, rise properly, and do everything while maintaining the pride of one's own deity. The torma too should be offered with this: oṃ akāro mukhaṃ sarvadharmāṇāṃ ādyanutpannatvāt oṃ āḥ hūṃ phaṭ svāhā.

At the evening session, at the end, one presents the offering water and requests the wisdom maṇḍala to depart, saying:

"oṃ You have accomplished every aim of sentient beings;
grant the accomplishments that accord with them.
I request you to depart to the realm of the buddhas,
and I ask you to come again."

After saying this, one urges them also with oṃ muḥ. Then one dissolves the pledge maṇḍala within, and should protect the place and oneself through yoga. At the time of going to sleep, one should resolve upon mere natural joy and then lie down. One should do this continually, again and again.

By the power of whatever virtue there is in teaching
this means of accomplishment, delightful as a garland of pearls,
may all sentient beings who cling to "I" and "self"
spontaneously become the very nature of Nairātmyā.

The Extensive Exposition of the Means of Accomplishment of Nairātmyā, composed by the glorious Ratnākara, is complete.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The text reads "as appearances they are not bliss" (bde ba); this has been taken as a slip for "not real" (bden pa), which the context of dispelling error plainly requires. "Gtu ma mo" in the verse on the offering goddesses is read as gtum mo, Caṇḍālī, which the seven-syllable meter and the standard Hevajra list of eight goddesses confirm; it is not the class of mātṛkā goddesses. "Corpse seat" renders zhing, the tantric term for a human corpse used as a seat, as the later phrase "corpse seats" confirms. The "lotus of the four wheels, adorned with four syllables" is given literally; its precise referent is not explained here.  Drag 'dzin ma, assigned to the fire element and to two of the grounds, stands in the position Caṇḍālī holds elsewhere in this text and in the Hevajra system, and is rendered as Caṇḍālī. In the list of afflictions the text reads ser sna ma for pride; this has been taken as a slip for ser mo, Gaurī, who completes the series of the five inner goddesses. The text assigns Nairātmyā to both the ear and the mind sense bases; this is translated as it stands. The opening "skyon ni" ("as for fault") of the final paragraph is read as skyo na, "when one grows weary", which the following clause requires. "Rnam sa" before the first six offering goddesses is read as rnams. The names in the sealing verse (Heart, Eternal, Jewel, Lokeśvara, Accomplisher of Aims, Delusion, Passion) are coded designations of the buddha families and are given by meaning.  "Thig le lo" is read as thig le la, "into the drop". "Sems cad" in the departure verse is read as sems can, "sentient beings". "The Vajra of Awakening" (byang chub rdo rje, Bodhivajra) is an epithet of the Buddha or Vajradhara as the proclaimer of tantric conduct. The colophon gives the author as dPal dKon mchog 'byung gnas, Śrī Ratnākara; whether he is to be identified with Ratnākaraśānti is not stated here.
