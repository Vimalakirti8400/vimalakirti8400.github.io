The Rite of the Sequence of Activities for the Hevajra Maṇḍala (Hevajramaṇḍalakarmakramavidhi)

The Rite of the Sequence of Activities for the Hevajra Maṇḍala, known in Sanskrit as the Hevajra Maṇḍala Karma Krama Vidhi and in Tibetan as Kye rdo rje'i dkyil 'khor gyi las kyi rim pa'i cho ga, is a ritual manual belonging to the Hevajra cycle of tantric practice. After paying homage to the glorious Hevajra, it explains how a qualified master, one who has received empowerment from his own guru, keeps the tantric commitments, and has all the required qualities, should draw the maṇḍala in order to confer empowerment on a disciple. The text begins by describing the qualities of a suitable disciple: gentle and pleasant in speech, loving and compassionate toward all beings, earnest in study and reflection, and full of faith in the secret Dharma of method and wisdom. It then sets out the ordered sequence of ritual activities for the maṇḍala. The work is Toh 1219 in the Tengyur, where it is placed among the tantric treatises and ritual texts associated with the Hevajra Tantra.

In the language of India: he vajra maṇḍal karma krama vidhi. In the language of Tibet: The Rite of the Sequence of Activities for the Hevajra Maṇḍala.

Homage to the glorious Hevajra.

First, here is how the master draws the maṇḍala in order to confer empowerment on a disciple. The master must have received empowerment from his guru, abide by the commitments, and possess all the required characteristics.

First, the characteristics of the disciple should be examined. One should accept as a disciple someone who is gentle, mild, and pleasant in speech, and who has loving-kindness toward all sentient beings and a mind of compassion. The disciple should apply himself earnestly to study, reflection, and reading, and have great faith in the secret Dharma, the profound way of method and wisdom. He should be respectful toward the guru, unafraid of the treatises, and greatly generous.

After the disciple has been examined in this way, one should first perform the approach practice of the deity. In this, the recitation of the principal deity should be done one hundred thousand times, and the recitation of the goddesses a thousand times. On this point some masters say: "The recitation of the goddesses need not be done. Why? Because they are included as limbs of the Hero, one should do the Hero alone."

If signs appear from doing the recitation in this way, one should begin drawing the maṇḍala. First, purify the mind. Endowed with the pride of Hevajra, please the deities, and then thoroughly examine the ground on which the maṇḍala is to be drawn. It may be a very secluded forest, the house of a bodhisattva, the shore of a great ocean, a charnel ground that is a place of accomplishment, or a place where a maṇḍala has been made before. Or else it may be a pleasing site to which one's own mind is drawn, beautiful and delightful. Sprinkle it with fragrant water and strew various flowers over it.

Whatever the place, if it slopes toward the east and the north, it will bring the yogins accomplishment. If it is high in the center, it will be attained by the vidyādharas of the Great Seal (mahāmudrā). If it is high in the north, one will be followed by the ḍākinīs. If it is high in the east, conceptual thoughts about the four and five families and so forth will be exhausted. If the ground is low in the center, the mantra practitioner will be beset by fear. If one practices in a place that has the right characteristics, one will gain whatever accomplishment one desires. This is the stage of examining the ground.

Sit in the middle of the ground examined in this way, in the identity of Hevajra, and rouse and supplicate the earth goddess Pṛthivī. Strike the ground three times with the fingers of both hands. Imagine that, at the striking, she emerges from within the earth, summoned, and stands before the yogin. Imagine that this goddess is adorned with every ornament, yellow in color, lovely and beautiful, holding a vase in her hand, and that she says: "What shall I do? Please give me your command." To the goddess so imagined, offer water for welcome, refreshing water, offerings, and torma, and speak as follows:

"Over the way of conduct and its special features
of all the buddhas, the protectors,
over the grounds and the perfections,
you, O goddess, hold sway.
Just as Śākyasiṃha, the protector,
destroyed the hosts of Māra,
so shall I, having triumphed over Māra,
draw the maṇḍala."

After saying this three times, imagine that the earth goddess, with a joyful countenance, says, "You are the master, so draw the maṇḍala!" and then dissolves into the ground. This is the stage of requesting the ground.

Then sprinkle that site with fragrant water and anoint it with the five nectars. Then, knowing the position of the mahoraga, dig down to the depth of the neck, the navel, or the knees, for the sake of supreme, middling, or lesser accomplishment. Then clear away whatever spoils the soil, such as stones, bits of wood, charcoal, bones, potsherds, gravel, and clods. Fill the pit with other good, rich earth mixed with fragrances such as sandalwood, saffron, camphor, and musk. Alternatively, mix fragrances with water over which the syllable hūṃ has been recited, fill the pit with this together with dung and urine, and tamp it down firmly. Place in it treasures of jewels and the like.

On top of the ground, made level as the palm of the hand, sit in the yoga endowed with the six limbs. By saying oṃ rakṣa rakṣa, make the whole ground vajra in nature, and make the sides and the space above into a vajra tent and a vajra canopy. Then, with oṃ vajri bhava vajra bandha hūṃ, make everything radiant with light.

"For the offering to wrathful Hevajra
you, O goddess, have become the witness.
With powder ground from various jewels
I shall draw the meaningful maṇḍala."

This is the stage of blessing the ground and remaining in preparation.

Then the master, again endowed with the six limbs and wearing the five ornaments, namely the crossed ornament on the crown, the necklace, the earrings, the girdle, and the bracelets, holds vajra and bell. Four practitioners in the meditative absorption of the four families, the goddesses Gaurī and the rest, seated in front and in the other positions as described above, sit in the four directions. Holding vajra and bell, they should perform the exhortation. Before the exhortation, offerings should be made. The one in the meditative absorption of Gaurī makes the five offerings, saying oṃ vajra puṣpe āḥ hūṃ, oṃ vajra dhūpe āḥ hūṃ, oṃ vajra āloke āḥ hūṃ, oṃ vajra gandhe āḥ hūṃ, oṃ vajra naivedye āḥ hūṃ. She praises with the verses beginning "You are surrounded by yoginīs," and then exhorts as follows:

"Peace of all the tathāgatas,
abode of all the tathāgatas,
of Vajrasattva, the source of all,

O Guide, please teach the maṇḍala.
Perfectly complete in every characteristic,
having fully abandoned all that is not a mark,
Samantabhadra, supreme in body,
O Guide, please teach the maṇḍala.
Truly awakened, supreme and peaceful,
engaging in wisdom, engaging in manifold ways,
Samantabhadra, supreme in speech,
O Guide, please teach the maṇḍala.
Great mind of all sentient beings,
pure by nature and without stain,
source from which the great Vairocana arises,
O Guide, please teach the maṇḍala."

Then the one meditating as Hevajra, endowed with the mudrā mentioned above, should dispel the obstructors. On both feet he visualizes crossed vajras arising from the syllable hūṃ. In the right eye he visualizes a sun disk arising from ma, and in the left a moon disk arising from ṭa, and he looks in every direction with the vajra gaze. As it is said:

"Eyes moving very swiftly,
drawing in the eyelashes:
this is called the blazing gaze.
It is for summoning all beings."

Gazing in this way, he holds the bell in the left hand, and with the right hand firmly grasps the golden vajra above and below. With the following he should expel those dwelling in that place: "In this place I will undertake the benefit of sentient beings. Gods, nāgas, yakṣas, gandharvas, bhūtas, piśācīs, rākṣasas and the rest, whatever lords of the ground dwell here, go away quickly! Having heard the command of glorious Heruka, depart quickly! As for those who do not go, by this vajra of my wisdom and this khaṭvāṅga their crowns will split into a hundred pieces." This is said three times. Then, starting from the northeast, the quarter of Īśāna, uttering many hūṃ, he should circumambulate three times. He imagines that many rays of light like the fire at the end of the eon issue from beneath his feet, so that the malevolent ones, afraid of being burned, scatter in every direction.

Moreover, he dances with the eight playful steps:

Moving the feet, then moving the hands,
raising the brows, and raising them high in turn,
slightly bashful, with sidelong glances,
seated on a seat with thighs spread wide,
the wide eyes performing the dance,
the lotus of the mouth with a smile,
bearing fangs that are slightly red,
a row of teeth white like the kunda flower:
glorious Heruka, supreme yogin,
wondrous in liberating sentient beings.
With such dances as these
obstructors and the rest should be expelled.

Then, with the rakṣa mantra taught earlier, one should make the vajra ground, the tent, the canopy and so forth. By doing so, even a maṇḍala site that has faults becomes free of faults. This is the stage of dispelling obstructors.

Then the site is smeared with fragrant water containing the five products of the cow, and with the five nectars, and on it a dot of perfume is made. On that dot a flower is placed and visualized as a variegated lotus. On the lotus one visualizes a sun disk arising from a, and upon it, by applying each one's own mantra, hūṃ gaṃ caṃ baṃ ghaṃ baṃ saṃ laṃ ḍaṃ, one generates the maṇḍala of deities and imagines each seated on its own seat.

Then the wisdom being together with the retinue should be invited. When one exhorts with gaurī jaḥ, many hook-bearing Gaurīs emanated from Gaurī radiate forth and invite Hevajra together with the retinue from the natural maṇḍala. When they are seated above the vajra canopy, one worships them with prokṣaṇaṃ, arghaṃ, añcamanaṃ, vidhi, incense, flowers and the rest, and also worships them with the offerings of the eight goddesses.

Then, with caurī hūṃ, Caurī holding a noose places them inside the palace. With bettālī baṃ they are bound in the same way. With ghasmarī hoḥ they are delighted, and the wisdom wheel is made one.

Then again one worships with the outer offerings and also with the inner offerings, and ringing the bell, one praises with the words of vajra song, such as:

"Because existence is pure by nature,
it is made insubstantial in its very essence.
May the beings pure by nature
bring about the sublime existence."

and:

"Supreme nature of all things,
abiding from the very beginning as the self of all,
nonduality of method and wisdom,
I bow to Hevajra."

Then the nectar offering too is to be tasted by glorious Hevajra together with the retinue. Then the following should be said:

"Arisen from nondual wisdom,
all-pervading lord, embodiment of accomplishment,
chief of teachers, glorious Heruka,
perfectly united with Nairātmyā,
ḍākinīs of the directions and intermediate directions,
knowledge goddesses possessed of miraculous power,
bodhisattvas and all the rest,
every one of you, please attend to me.
To benefit the disciples
and also to make offerings to you,
I, so-and-so, the vajra holder,
will draw the maṇḍala tomorrow.
So, regarding me with loving kindness,
please bestow your blessings."

Then one should offer garlands of fragrant flowers, the five nectars, incense and the like. This is the stage of supplicating the deities.

Then the chief disciple together with the others should be prepared and honored, and the following is recited:

Come here, child, into the Great Vehicle;
you are a vessel for the great way.
This rite, the conduct of secret mantra,
I shall explain to you correctly.

All the perfect buddhas who have passed,
likewise all those who have not yet come,
and the perfect buddhas who have now appeared,
who remain for the benefit of beings,
all of them came to know
this excellent rite of secret mantra,
and so, heroes at the foot of the tree of awakening,
they won the mark of omniscience.

The practice of secret mantra is unequalled.
The glorious protector Heruka
vanquished with it the hosts of Māra,
fierce beyond bearing and great in their armies.
Therefore, in order to attain omniscience,
child, form this resolve.
In the Buddha, the Dharma and the Saṅgha
you are always to go for refuge.
In the yoginīs, the secret mantras, the ḍākinīs,
and all whose nature is to dwell in the wheel,
in all of them always go for refuge.
In the heroes, the heroines, the goddesses of empowerment,
in the great bodhisattvas,
and above all in the master,
I always go for refuge.

Glorious Heruka and the other heroes,
the yoginīs, Vairocana and the others,
and all you countless bodhisattvas,
consider me with your minds.
From this time onward, you, so-and-so,
until you abide at the ground of selflessness,
in its nondual pure conduct
you are to take up the vow well.

Then one should have them confess their wrongdoing and so on with the following verses:

Whatever wrongs I have done,
caused others to do, or rejoiced in,
I come before the buddhas
and confess them all.

In the merit gathered through meditation and recitation
by the buddhas and bodhisattvas,
the pratyekabuddhas, the arhats and all beings,
in that I rejoice.

In the arising of the mind of awakening, in full awakening,
in the turning of the wheel of Dharma,
and in the conduct of the host of bodhisattvas,
in that I go for refuge.

Just as the buddhas came forth,
just as the fortunate bodhisattvas
generated the mind of awakening,
so this very day I generate that mind.

Protector, consider me.
All you buddhas who remain, without exception,
from this very moment until buddhahood unsurpassed,
please look upon me.

The Buddha, the Dharma and the Saṅgha,
the unsurpassed Three Jewels,
and the vow of the Buddha yoga
I shall take up correctly.

For the great supreme vajra family,
the vajra, the bell and the mudrā
I shall take up correctly,
and the master too I shall take up.

For the pleasing pledge
of the great supreme jewel family,
six times every day
I shall always give the four kinds of giving.

For the pure supreme lotus family,
arisen from great awakening,
the outer, the secret and the three vehicles,
the holy Dharma, I shall take up entirely.

For the great supreme action family,
to be endowed with all the vows
I shall take up correctly,
and acts of offering I shall perform as I am able.

The discipline of moral training,
the gathering of virtuous qualities,
and working for the good of beings, the three kinds of discipline,
these too I shall hold firmly.

Through these virtuous deeds of mine,
may I swiftly attain buddhahood before long in this world,
and the beings sunk in the ocean of saṃsāra
I shall carry across to unsurpassed awakening.

When they have made this request, the master should protect the disciples. In the body, speech and mind of the disciples, he visualizes a wheel at the crown, a lotus at the throat and a vajra at the heart, and in the center of those three the three syllables oṃ āḥ hūṃ. Then the master takes a vajra in his hand, touches it to the disciple's three places, saying oṃ āḥ hūṃ, and anoints the three places with scented water. Then one by one he places flowers on the disciples' heads, offers incense before them, and gives a lamp and scent at the heart.

Next, the rite of the tooth stick is this:

Of udumbara or aśvattha,
clean, anointed with flowers and scent,
it should be made twelve finger-widths long,
not crooked, not split, not eaten by insects.
On the ground, with cow dung, a maṇḍala
the size of a cowhide is made,
and placed upon it for the disciples,
it is worshipped with flowers and other offerings
and given to the assembly of disciples.
Then, facing east,
they chew the tooth stick in silence.
When the chewing is done, without breaking it
and without tossing it this way or that,
they throw the tooth stick straight in front.
The signs at that time are these:
if it then points toward oneself
and its tip stands upright,
that is declared the supreme accomplishment.
Likewise, if it points elsewhere,
that is explained as the middling accomplishment.
If it points to an intermediate direction or to the north,
one attains worldly accomplishment.
If, among a hundred throws of the tooth stick,
once in a while it points downward,
that certainly signifies
the accomplishment of the underworld; there is no doubt of it.

Then scented water is poured into the hand, and the rinsing and sipping are done. Reciting the mantra oṃ hrīḥ viśuddha dharmā sarva pāpaṃ niścā saṃśodhaya sarva vikalpa apanyā hūṃ, one has them drink three sips of water.

After that, to gladden the minds of the disciples, one should teach the Dharma:

An omniscient one does not arise
in every world realm;
like the udumbara flower,
only once in a hundred times, rarely, does one appear.
Therefore, through this nondual, profound way of conduct,
which arises rarely and is hard to meet,
beings are made buddhas.
Whatever ripening of deeds done before
through many countless eons,
all of it is exhausted
by seeing the profound maṇḍala.

Then kuśa grass is consecrated with oṃ āḥ hūṃ and is to be placed as a pillow and also spread as a seat. Then the protection cord is tied: six knots are made in a red protection cord, and it is bound on the left upper arm for protection. Then offerings are made and the torma is sent out. The master enters inside and duly worships the deities of the maṇḍala with outer and inner offerings. The disciples, in that very place of preparation, lie down on the kuśa-grass seat in the lion's sleeping posture, and in order to observe the signs in their dreams, they should sleep in silence, their minds in the meditative concentration of Hevajra. Then, at the end of the night, one should ask what dreams each has seen. Having heard the question, they should tell their dreams, good or bad, without holding anything back. Then, when good dreams have been seen, one should rejoice with the disciple and point out the supreme, middling or lesser accomplishment.

If a bad dream is seen, one should have the disciple drink the five products of the cow and perform a wrathful fire offering. By that the disturbing thoughts will be pacified. This is the stage of the preliminary consecration of the disciple.

Then one makes the victory vase of gold, silver or copper; if these are not available, it should be made of clay. The other vases as well should be made of clay if precious materials cannot be found. All of them should be smeared with fragrant scent and white earth, and set down with the hand emblem placed on the center of a lotus.

Then imagine the vases to be without intrinsic nature. From that arises the syllable khaṃ, white. The white khaṃ melts into light, and from it arises a vase: meditate on it as white, without black streaks, with a long neck, a large belly and a drooping lip. Upon it, from a red paṃ, a lotus; upon that, from a, a sun disc. Upon that are hūṃ gaṃ caṃ vaṃ rghaṃ vaṃ saṃ laṃ ḍaṃ. From their complete transformation the wheel of deities is fully generated.

Then one performs the inviting of the wisdom wheel and the rest. With the four mudrās, accompanied by gaurī jaḥ and the other syllables, one summons them, makes them enter, binds them and pleases them, and dissolves them so that they are inseparable. Then one offers to them prokṣaṇaṃdaṅ, arghaṃ, añcamanaṃ and vidhi, the five kinds of offerings, the eight goddesses, and the praises. Then one should recite the mantra one hundred and eight times or one thousand and eight times, and set it down. Then one performs the fumigation with incense and so forth, and the scattering of white flowers all about and so forth.

Then imagine those deities melting into light, so that the vase is filled with white and red bodhicitta. Then fill it by pouring in excellent scented water, and add medicines and the like as well. These are made from the roots of śvetabrehati, apacitadanta, utpala, kaṇḍakari, hasadeva and the like; they serve as the sign of the bodhicitta of the maṇḍala. Rice, peas, barley, sesame and wheat are the nature of the minds of the deities of the maṇḍala. Crystal, silver, pearl, conch and coral serve as the sign of the bodies of the deities of the maṇḍala. Further, one puts in moon and sun, water, medicine, scent, molasses, sugar and foods.

The vase should be furnished with the five kinds of signs of the lord, and its neck should be tied with cloths of blue, white, yellow, red and green. If these are not available, a single length of cloth may be used for all. Its mouth should be adorned with a good kind of milky tree, together with fruit, leaves and twigs. In the other vases one does not put medicines and the like; rather, each deity is made to melt into light. Praise, offering and mantra recitation are performed for all of them.

Then the vases are invited to be seated upon white flowers scattered all about on a spread cloth. At that time dance offerings are also presented. The vase for all activities is marked with a crossed vajra. Having made offerings to it, one invites it to be seated in the outer northeast corner and charges it with its task, saying: "Please protect against the vicious obstructors until the rite has been accomplished." Nine tormas, nine lamps, nine food offerings for the deities, and nine incenses: these make up the stage of the preliminary consecration of the vases.

After the preparation of the site, during that day, one should perform the fire offering for increase. The master, endowed with excellent characteristics, knowing the suchness of true wisdom, free of anger, and accompanied by a wisdom consort adorned with the five mudrās, should invite the deities of the maṇḍala to come into the sky in front, and then begin the work of laying out the lines.

For this, a wisdom woman endowed with the proper marks spins cotton into thread without tangles, knots and the like, twice as long as needed, with a thickness one twentieth of the door. Each thread is tied separately and placed separately in a lotus vessel smeared with fragrance. Then meditate on the threads as without intrinsic nature. They become their respective syllables, and from the complete transformation of the syllables imagine that the threads arise. Then, from the heart of the wisdom being there is a wisdom thread. With the hook of the yogin's eyes, blazing as sun and moon, one invites the chief, Hevajra, the ḍākinīs of all the buddhas, and those who are nondual with the chief's mantra, and dissolves them into it. Having thus generated the wisdom line-cord, one recites the chief's mantra over the five threads and twists them into one. One should recite oṃ vajra samaya sūtraṃ māti krama hūṃ one hundred and eight times. Placing the three syllables oṃ āḥ hūṃ at the three points, the two ends and the middle, one blesses it and presents offerings to it. Then from the heart of the chief one emanates a companion to hold the thread, and saying jjaḥ jjaḥ, one hands it over.

First one should lay out the lines in the sky, exhorting with these words:

When the line-cords are laid out,
O buddhas and the rest of all world-realms without exception,
for the benefit of beings,
now is your time to act.

In the same way the lines on the ground should be laid out. The master sits at the place of Varuṇa and the disciple at the place of Śakra, and they lay out the Brahmā line. The master sits in the direction of Yama and the disciple at the place of the yakṣa, and they lay out the second Brahmā line. The master sits at the place of Agni and the disciple at Īśāna, and they lay out the eastern line. The master sits in the direction of Vāyu and the disciple at Nairṛti, and they lay out the western line. The master stays in that same place and the disciple sits at Īśāna, and they lay out the northern line. The master sits at Agni and the disciple at Nairṛti, and they should lay out the southern line.

Lay out the lines completely with the thread,
but do not measure with the thread,
for there is the danger of its stretching and shrinking;
measure carefully with something firm.

Take bamboo or the like, straight and thin, make it into a khatika, a measuring rod, and mark forty-two line-units on each side. Having thus marked out forty-two, with the lines inward from the door enclosure one should draw the circle of the lotus center. The circle of the lotus filaments should be drawn as far as fits within the band of the deities. At the edge of the maṇḍala, the vajra fence and the fire mountains are each encircled with one unit. Above the four bands of the portals, of the four units, on one portion of a half-unit is the dharmācakra; on one is the central spoke of the crossed vajra, and to its tip the tip of the lotus stem should be joined.

Then the master sits in the center of the maṇḍala and visualizes disks of the sun and moon in his two eyes. Turning round, he gazes, and saying hi hi he imagines that the door opens. Then, reciting vajra baga akrama, he comes out. When the sequence of laying down the lines has been completed in this way, he worships the wisdom cord with offerings and, reciting sva artā citta parama artha ñca ahita gaccha, requests the deities to depart. This is the sequence of laying down the wisdom lines.

Then the colored powders, made of rice well dyed in their colors, are set out in order. One meditates on them as being without intrinsic nature and imagines that they become the five syllables bhrūṃ āṃ jṝṃ khaṃ hūṃ. One imagines that these are transformed, and that from them arise colored powders of every kind. Then the yogin, with the hook of his eyes blazing with sun and moon, invites Hevajra and the ḍākinīs together with their retinue, and imagines that they dissolve into the colored powders. Reciting oṃ vajra cītra samaya hūṃ many times, he presents offerings to the colored powders.

Then, beginning from the northeast corner, the master draws in order five lines, black, white, yellow, red and green, even, one span in length. He presents offerings and scatters loose flowers over the lines. While drawing the lines in this way, the master says:

This is the pure dharmadhātu,
which liberates the realm of beings,
the glorious king of blood-drinkers himself,
the abode of all the Tathāgatas.

So it is said. And it is also stated:

Being entirely free of every fault,
the work of the colors is done correctly.
The master lays them down straight;
afterward the disciple, all around.

And:

Avoiding the little finger and the thumb,
draw the lines properly, straight and raised.
If the lines are uneven,
crooked, or low,
by low lines there is no accomplishment,
a crooked one brings torment,
and by a broken one, master and disciple,
both of them, should be known to die.

Since this is stated, one must be extremely careful. Then one should draw the maṇḍala properly, in accordance with the scripture. That is the sequence of laying down the colors.

Having drawn the maṇḍala in this way, one should now set out the symbols. On the hub of the central lotus, on top of the four māras lying crossed and a sun disk, draw a white human skull (narakapāla) together with a crossed vajra. In the east, a black curved knife; in the south, a pale red ḍamaru drum; in the west, a reddish-yellow tortoise; in the north, a green snake; in the northeast, a purplish lion; in the southeast, a white monk; in the southwest, a blue-green wheel; and in the northwest, set out wrathful vajras. Moreover, when setting out the symbols in this way, set each one out while reciting the heart mantra of that deity. The eight vases too should be drawn or set out in their places, the east and the rest. This is the sequence of setting out the symbols.

Then one should draw a fine canopy, and also draw and arrange all the other ornaments. Outside, plant the markers of the guardians of the directions, and generate each guardian of the directions individually. Invite the deities into them, dissolve them in, and set them in place. Then, taking up the vases, led by incense and carrying musical instruments of every kind, circumambulate the maṇḍala three times and set the vases properly in place. Set the vase for all activities in the northeast corner.

Then, in the evening, the master puts on his ornaments, bathes, anoints his body with fragrances, and sprinkles the ground at the edge of the maṇḍala. Then, seated on a comfortable seat, he should practice together with a caṇḍālī consort; if there is none, he should do it by meditative absorption. Then the master, endowed with the six limbs, properly generates the complete wheel of deities. Then he commands Gaurī and invites the deities. To those invited he presents the five kinds of offerings. Then he performs summoning, drawing in, binding and delighting, and dissolves them in so that they are non-dual.
