The Dhāraṇī Called Increasing Wisdom (Prajñāvardhanī-nāma-dhāraṇī)

The Dhāraṇī Called Generating Wisdom, known in Sanskrit as the Prajñāvardhanī nāma Dhāraṇī and carried in Tibetan under a title of the same meaning, is a brief ritual text centered on a spell for increasing wisdom and mental capacity. It opens on the slopes of Mount Meru, where the bodhisattva Matimān, addressed here as the Blessed One, is teaching the Dharma to the gods who dwell on the mountain. The bodhisattva Youthful Form asks him, for the sake of beings whose wisdom is dull, whose intellect is weak, and whose memory fails to retain what they learn, to explain this dhāraṇī. Matimān then recites a short incantation invoking the growth of wisdom, intelligence, and understanding, and goes on to describe the benefits for anyone who holds it. The work is Toh 1037 in the Kangyur, where it belongs among the dhāraṇī texts, the class of scriptures devoted to protective and empowering spells.

In the language of India: prajñā vardhani nāma dhāraṇī
In the language of Tibet: The Dhāraṇī Called Generating Wisdom.

Homage to the Omniscient One.

Thus did I hear at one time. The Blessed One, the bodhisattva called Matimān, was dwelling on the slope of Mount Meru. Surrounded by all the sons of the gods who dwell on Meru, and with them gathered before him, he was teaching the Dharma.

Then the bodhisattva, the great being Youthful Form, joined his palms and said to the bodhisattva Matimān, "Blessed One, for the sake of sentient beings whose wisdom is dull, whose intellect is weak, and whose minds do not retain, please explain the dhāraṇī called Generating Wisdom."

He explained:

hūṃ picu picu prajñā vardhani jvala jvala medhā vardhani dhiri dhiri buddhi vardhani svāhā

Whoever holds this dhāraṇī, reads it, writes it, or has it written, or, at the very least, hears even a single syllable of it, will come to have great wisdom in this very life.

If someone, every day for six months, recites this incantation seven times over a handful of roots and drinks it at sunrise, he will retain a thousand verses each time. If he drinks it even once, he will retain five hundred, and his wisdom will increase day by day. Day by day, merely by reciting it, he will gain mindfulness and a retentive mind.

The Dhāraṇī Called Generating Wisdom is complete.

This was translated by the Indian preceptor Jñānagarbha and the translator, the monk Lui Wangpo. It was revised and finalized by the Indian preceptor Vidyākarasiṃha and the chief editor and translator, the monk Devacandra.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The text unusually calls the bodhisattva Matimān (Tibetan Blo ldan, "Possessing Intelligence") "the Blessed One." This is translated as it stands. The name of his interlocutor, Tibetan gZhon nu'i gzugs can, could not be identified with confidence in Sanskrit. It is translated by its meaning, "Youthful Form." The translator Lui Wangpo (Klu'i dbang po) is a Tibetan, and his name is given in its Tibetan pronunciation. The editor Devacandra is a Tibetan whose name the colophon gives in Sanskrit form, and that form has been kept.
