Homage to the Three Jewels (Ratnatrayanamaskāra)

The Dhāraṇī for Making Prostrations is a very brief ritual text that opens with homage to the Three Jewels and then teaches a short mantra to be recited before a representation of the Buddha, the Dharma, and the Saṅgha, with a full-length prostration made at each recitation. It promises that three such prostrations bring merit equal to hearing, contemplating, and meditating on the entire Tripiṭaka, and that performing three prostrations every day leads in this very life to the meditative absorption of the wisdom of the path of seeing, while protecting the practitioner from harm by humans, nonhumans, spirits, and epidemic disease until that attainment is reached. The mantra itself invokes Mañjuśrī and auspicious glory. The text is Toh 1049 in the Kangyur, where it belongs to the collection of dhāraṇī texts, the short incantations valued for their protective power and merit-generating practice.

Homage to the Three Jewels.

Before a representation of the Three Jewels, recite this mantra once and make one full-length prostration with each recitation. If one makes three prostrations in this way, the merit will equal that of hearing, contemplating, and meditating on the three collections of scripture (the Tripiṭaka). If one makes three prostrations each and every day, then in this very life one will attain the meditative absorption of the wisdom of the path of seeing. Until one attains it, no harm will come from humans, nonhumans, bhūtas, or epidemic disease.

oṃ namo mañju śriye namaḥ su śriye nama utta ma śriye svāhā

The dhāraṇī for making prostrations is complete.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

"Each and every day... three prostrations": the Tibetan repeats the number ("three three"), which as a distributive most naturally means three each day, though it could also be read as three sets of three. The final syllable after "complete" seems to be a scribal variant of the usual closing particle and has no separate meaning.
