The Noble Heart-Essence of the Queen of Vidyā Mantras, the Peacock (Āryamāyūravidyāgarbhe nāma)

Toh 560 in the Kangyur is a dhāraṇī text known in Sanskrit as the Āryamāyūravidyāgarbhe and in Tibetan as the Noble Heart-Essence of the Queen of Vidyā Mantras, the Peacock, or in English roughly as the Noble Heart-Essence of the Peacock Queen of Knowledge Mantras. It belongs to the genre of protective dhāraṇī literature, presenting a series of invocations and sacred formulas intended to guard practitioners against a wide range of dangers — including fear of enemies, wild beasts, poison, fire, and water — as well as illnesses arising from imbalances of wind, bile, and phlegm. The Peacock (Mayūra) is a figure of ancient Indian religious tradition associated with the neutralization of poison and the warding off of harm, and this text draws on that symbolism to offer comprehensive spiritual and physical protection. Sitting within the Kangyur's collection of dhāraṇī and mantra literature, it represents an important example of the ritual-protective scriptures that formed a significant part of Buddhist practice across South and Central Asia.

Homage to the Blessed One, the Great Peacock. Homage to the Buddha. Homage to the Dharma. Homage to the Saṅgha.

If one finds oneself in the midst of any fear — fear of enemies, fear of conflict, fear of obstructing spirits (vighnakārakas), fear of untimely death, fear of snakes, fear of wild beasts, fear of poison, fear of fire, fear of water, fear of wind, or any fear whatsoever — one should call this to mind.

If one finds oneself in the midst of any illness — illness arising from wind, illness arising from bile, illness arising from phlegm, illness arising from their combination, being seized by malevolent spirits, struck by epidemic disease, having drunk poison, or any illness whatsoever — one should call this to mind.

Nārāyaṇi, pārāyaṇi, harita, tāli, kuntāli, ilimisti, kilitirimisti svāhā. May the foundations of the vidyā mantras be accomplished.

Ha ha, hi hi, hu hu, kharā, marā, khahi, khahi, hūṃ hūṃ, phaṭ phaṭ svāhā. Protect me! Protect me! May I live for a hundred years! May I behold a hundred autumns!
