The title of this dhāraṇī in Sanskrit, Āryamahādaṇḍanāmadhāraṇī, means "The Noble Dhāraṇī Called the Great Staff." The Tibetan rendering "be con chen po" (great staff or great cudgel) faithfully reflects "mahādaṇḍa."

The figure named in Tibetan as "sgra gcan 'dzin" (literally "the one who holds the seizer of sound," an epithet of Rāhula) is here rendered as "Rāhula," the well-known son and disciple of the Buddha. This is a standard Tibetan rendering of the Sanskrit name Rāhula.

The phrase "four circuits" (Tibetan: 'khor bzhi pa) in the context of the mantra likely refers to four groups or rounds of the vidyā-mantra. The precise structural meaning of this term within the ritual context is not entirely transparent from the text alone.

The mantra syllables are transliterated as closely as possible from the Tibetan phonetic rendering of Sanskrit; standard critical editions may show variants.  The names rendered as "King Vecon" and "King Without-Vecon" translate Tibetan རྒྱལ་པོ་བེ་ཅོན and རྒྱལ་པོ་བེ་ཅོན་མེད, which appear to be transliterations of Sanskrit divine names not yet identified with certainty in the surviving parallel texts; they are rendered phonetically here rather than guessed at.
