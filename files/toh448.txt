The King of Yoginī Tantras called 'The Ultimate Assembly of Tārā, Accomplishing All Activities' (Samāja-parasārtha-sarva-karmodyāna-nāma-hārāloginī-tantra-rāja)

Toh 448 in the Kangyur is a yoginī tantra known in Sanskrit as the Samāja-parasārtha-sarva-karmodyāna-nāma-hārāloginī-tantra-rāja and in Tibetan as the Phrin las thams cad 'byung ba'i sgrol ma 'dus pa don dam pa, rendered in English as "The King of Yoginī Tantras called 'The Ultimate Assembly of Tārā, Accomplishing All Activities.'" It is a tantric scripture centered on the goddess Tārā, presenting her as a supreme deity whose power encompasses the full range of enlightened activities, including the attainment of siddhis, the subjugation of gods, demons, and humans, and the command of nāgas and other non-human beings. The text belongs to the class of yoginī tantras, a category of highest yoga tantra literature associated with feminine deity cycles, and sits within the tantra section of the Kangyur.

In the language of India: Samāja-parasārtha-sarva-karmodyāna-nāma-hārāloginī-tantra-rāja. In the language of Tibet: The King of Yoginī Tantras called "The Ultimate Assembly of Tārā, Accomplishing All Activities."

Homage to Tārā.

Then, all siddhis (accomplishments) shall be set forth as that which truly achieves them. Even a hundred million women shall bow to me with faith. Gods, asuras (demigods), vidyādharas (knowledge-holders), and the foremost among human beings shall be brought under one's power. Equal to the sun and the moon, the King of Garuḍas bows down in veneration. The nāgas are made to tremble, and all daughters of human beings, and those of every non-human kind — all of them made to flow — and the women and demonesses who subjugate by the hundred thousands, by the hundreds, and every day: by the great desire of a single man, tens of thousands of human women, and daily goddesses, demonesses, and hundreds of thousands of women are subdued. From the activity of one who swells with semen, one who enjoys all sensual pleasures — all of this is fully taught and made into vajra. Liberation from every fear, and abiding in life for a great aeon. Sky-travelers, the invisible, ṛilus and similar vidyādharas, rasāyana (essence extraction), the gifts of the suppressed, the great mantra — these fully accomplish. The twelve cakra ḍākinīs, the ten million mighty elemental spirits, the gods of the triple city including Brahmā and the rest — all of these shall be made to do one's bidding. In the past age, Vajradhara manifested the maṇḍala of the buddhas' breathing, Supreme Bliss, Supreme Bliss, the vajra of desire, the mental bliss, and the drop, and so forth, were taught. The king of mantras of Tārā — in a hundred times a hundred thousand — was taught before. In the age of perfection (Kṛtayuga), human beings practiced it. Then the sixth hundred thousand was taught; from the west, twenty thousand practiced it. This was taught for a later time. Its essence in this age of strife, practiced by the left-handed practitioners — that which follows in the wake of a definite hundred thousand — this tantra shall again become manifest. This certainty, through this great passionate desire, bestows awakening upon those who are desirous. All the activities of the buddhas arise, the mantra called "Samaya Tārā," from the assembly of all ḍākinīs — the sacred ultimate meaning is also accomplished.

Then the Blessed One, Lady Samaya Dīpā, spoke this queen of knowledge mantras:

Oṃ kurukullā hrīḥ svāhā. This is Kurukullā.

Oṃ tāre tuttāre ture hūṃ phaṭ. This is the essence.

Oṃ tāre svāhā. This one requires no urging in speech.

Oṃ āḥ taṃ hūṃ. This activates the mind-emanation.

Oṃ marīcye ma svāhā. This is Marīcī (the Lady of Light Rays).

Svāhā laṃ laṃ laṃ hrīḥ jaḥ phaṭ phaṭ. These accomplish the eight activities.

Jaḥ hūṃ baṃ hoḥ. This is for summoning.

Oṃ vajra-rakṣa rakṣa hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ svāhā. This is for protection.

Paraṇa phaṭ. This is for dispersing.

Oṃ akrodhe kayamāntāka hūṃ. This belongs to Yamāntaka (the Destroyer of the Lord of Death).

Oṃ namo acala kāna ceṭṭā ceṭṭā ceṭṭā ceṭṭā tuṭṭha tuṭṭha gha gha ghātaya hūṃ phaṭ. This belongs to the Wrathful One (Acala).

Oṃ padmānda kṛta vajra krodha bhahaya grīva hūṃ hulu hulu hūṃ phaṭ. This belongs to Hayagrīva (the Horse-Necked One).

Namaḥ samanta buddhebhyaḥ. Namo mahā vajra krodhāya. Mahā bhrotāṭa bhaira dābāya. Vajra sandotad ṅghaya. Oṃ amṛta kuṇḍali hūṃ phaṭ. This belongs to Amṛtakuṇḍalin (the Coiled Nectar Wrathful One).

Oṃ hūṃ śrī ya devī kālī kālī mahā kālī hūṃ bhyo. Oṃ mahā kālī ka ra lī vikranda kāla rātī caṇḍa rūpiṇī hūṃ phaṭ. This is the mantra of the Black Goddess (Mahākālī).

Oṃ vajra kīli kīlāya sarva vighnān tāṃ hūṃ phaṭ. This belongs to the ritual stabbing with the kīla (the sacred dagger). This completes the first chapter on mantras.

Then, from the heart of the Blessed Lady, a host of lassos and iron hooks radiated forth, and this mantra was uttered: Namaḥ santa kāya pākṣiṭṭha vajrāṇāṃ oṃ ṭakki hūṃ jaḥ.

Having gathered all malevolent forces
and led them into the expanse of space,
the Blessed Lady emanated a host of vajra kīlas
and struck them with the kīla at the five places.

Amṛtakuṇḍalin, the great wrathful one,
urge on the vajra kīla!
Throughout the entire expanse of the three-thousandfold world,
bind fast every obstacle-maker!

Oṃ laṃ stambhaya laṃ.

Having looked down at an anthill with one's gaze
and pressed it down with the right foot,
a kīla made of human bone
upon being driven in, brings death that very instant.

Drive it into serpents, lions, and elephants,
into the limbs of thieves.
Those who are malicious and bear hostile intent,
those who lead astray — drive it into them.

Having opened the wisdom lotus,
and joined it with the mouth of the vajra kīla,
together with the heads of the respective families,
drive it into Vajrasattva and the other mantra-deities.

By this, accomplishment of the vajra will come about.
When struck with the vajra kīla,
the aggregates are utterly destroyed,
and the vajra state is attained.

Thus it was declared. This is the samaya (the sacred bond) of the great yoga.

In the center of the wind between moon and sun,
meditate on the seed of one's own mantra.
When the suchness of the deity is thus visualized,
accomplishment will come at the sixteenth stage.

When the mind has become like space,
and the moon and sun have set,
and the wisdom that has not yet ripened dissolves,
the vajra deity will be accomplished.

Or else, through the ascetic discipline of the vajra,
having thoroughly purified the impurities of the body,
the great wisdom, one's own mantra deity,
will be accomplished at the moon-fish stage.

Through the rays of light of one's own mantra seed,
examine the vajra tent and the rest.
One is protected from the eight fears,
and even the bodiless ones cannot see one.

From the hair-pores, illnesses are expelled
entirely, like monkeys or dogs driven away.
By this samaya of the wisdom being,
pierce them through with the vajra weapon.

In the center of the expanse of space,
visualize a blazing white fire of wisdom.
Accomplish the blazing of fire
throughout the entirety of the realm of sentient beings.

Even those who commit the five acts of immediate retribution
are led to the pure realm.
Through this vajra wisdom of evil,
in an instant they become glorious.

At the center of the navel, touch it with the sense of contact —
the syllable A is supremely subtle,
the unchanging place of blissful joy and happiness.
It becomes the accomplishment of the indestructible Brahmā.

Contemplate one's own mantra hūṃ and the syllable oṃ,
and the vajra syllable āḥ.
Having perfectly accomplished the suchness of the wind,
it becomes a powerful irresistible speech.

Five rays of light abide at the heart —
the lotus becomes the vajra,
and one becomes the all-pervading king of wisdom.

Then Vajrasattva and the other lords of yoga made this request. Blessed Lady, what is this?

From the navel, the wind element arises.
Why does it become a property of the body?
By meditating on the path that transcends Māra,
how does one become Brahmā and so forth?

What does the lotus do to open the vajra? Why do these not become contradictory? The one who knows the reality of the sacred commitment has spoken. Listen — listen as it truly is, like a vajra.

There is no other body that exists outside. The intrinsic nature of mind is its agitation. From that there arises the deluded body. The navel is what is called the fine hair — these arise from it.

By practicing desire with the brahmin woman, one transforms into the vajra body. Because wind causes speech to arise, this is the vajra speech — declare that very suchness. Through the sacred commitment of the girl who sings with melody, one becomes the vajra speech.

Because the mind that conceives of duality through delusion is transformed from this drop, through the sacred commitment of the beautiful-eyed female servant, one becomes the vajra mind.

I am not known by beings. Brahmā, Viṣṇu, the Great God (Śiva), the god endowed with magical power — the world conceives of these. But body, speech, and mind are the vajra — all are emanations of my own form. The vajra of body becomes Brahmā; the vajra of speech is the Great Lord; the dispelling of emergence is the vajra of mind.

This is the perfectly complete buddha, inseparable from the realm of sentient beings. Their body, speech, and mind, being equalized, appear as Brahmā and the rest. The body abides at the emanation wheel; the throat likewise is the dream state. The yoga of sleep is placed at the heart. Bliss is explicitly declared at the crown. Furthermore, one speaks of the unobstructed channels, of hares, tigers, rivers, day and night, and all things — in all of these the buddha acts like a skilled dancer and mime.

By the vajra's full contemplation in sleep, the sacred commitment of Brahmā dispels delusion. It is difficult for others to abandon sleep. Without a woman, there is no striving in vow. Desire with the great Brahmā practice — this is the sacred commitment of peace that abandons attachment. If one fully meditates on aversion as the vajra, it becomes the sacred commitment of Viṣṇu that dispels confusion. It is difficult for others to abandon it through loving-kindness. These three aspects constitute the great secret.

The happiness of the gods together with miserliness — diligence and envy are the sacred commitment; by deception and cunning, the māras are tamed. This sacred commitment is extremely difficult to transgress.

The essence of the lotus, the blissful one — so long as the fruit is not obtained, there is nothing. By this root, the lord of the vajra realm opens and separates the two vajras. Wisdom accomplishes realization — the method; without wisdom, there is no skillful means. The vajra causes the lotus to open, and the lotus opens the vajra. The emanation wheel of great bliss as well distinguishes Dharma from enjoyment.

Therefore this is the great secret, hidden with effort in other tantras. Regarding this, then: this is the secret of the vajra of the three bodies.

The abiding without mind, the production of delusion, the illumination of former abiding — in this world the three bodies abide: the dharmakāya, saṃbhogakāya, and nirmāṇakāya.

Self-arisen essence, beyond words — all phenomena have attained the intrinsic nature of peace. The knowing of conditions appearing in their variety — this is the dharmakāya of the vajra-holder.

This mind is like a reflection in a mirror, subtle with a body without perspiration. Speech too is like an echo. The appearance of the pure maṇḍala.

According to the intentions of sentient beings, various emanations of different kinds arise. The saṃbhogakāya and nirmāṇakāya, becoming one, lead beings to great bliss.

The network of channels is the goddesses — likewise, the maṇḍala of the yoginīs, women endowed with the elements and their auxiliaries. Who would abandon this and merely take a painting? Regarding this: this is the sacred commitment of subsequent yoga.

When the mind is thoroughly pervaded by stain, consciousness does not itself become gnosis. Do not seek to listen, O queen of gnosis, at the place of travel of the supreme vehicle. Listen — listen truly, like a vajra. When one examines things as real or unreal, one sees the vajra of the unreal correctly, the genuine suchness of pure intrinsic nature.

The syllable a is the gateway of the dhāraṇī of the unborn. The syllable hūṃ is the gateway of the dhāraṇī endowed with gnosis. The act of phaṭ is the gateway of the dhāraṇī that designates.

The syllable tṛṃ is the gateway of the dhāraṇī of the sun. The syllable āḥ is the gateway of the dhāraṇī of all speech. The syllable ha is the gateway of the dhāraṇī of deathlessness. The syllable hrīḥ is the gateway of the dhāraṇī of attachment. The syllable oṃ is the gateway of the dhāraṇī that causes terror. The syllable khaṃ is the gateway of the dhāraṇī that is like space. The syllable oṃ is the gateway of the dhāraṇī of great bliss. The syllable kṣa is the gateway of the dhāraṇī of utter non-abiding. The syllable aḥ is the gateway of the dhāraṇī of non-duality. The syllable ya is vajra-wind, and is the gateway of the dhāraṇī. The syllable ra is vajra-fire, and is the gateway of the dhāraṇī. The syllable va is vajra-water, and is the gateway of the dhāraṇī. The syllable la is vajra-earth, and is the gateway of the dhāraṇī. The syllable taṃ is vajra-mind, and is the gateway of the dhāraṇī. The syllable iṃ is the vajra-heroes, and is the gateway of the dhāraṇī. The syllable vi is that which acts within the element of space, and is the gateway of the dhāraṇī. The syllable a is the gateway of the dhāraṇī of all phenomena.

Consciousness and gnosis are the yoga.
By their supreme union as vajra,
meditate on yoga within yoga —
this is called subsequent yoga.

And this is the sacred commitment of supreme yoga.

The mind, which does not abide, like a monkey or lightning,
which is so difficult to hold firmly —
listen, O queen of gnosis, to the examination of the vajra of desire.
Listen — listen truly, like a vajra.

Gold ore in stone contains oil,
yet there is no peace to be found there.
By the method of burning and separation,
it becomes clear like the sun.
If iron can endure fire,
why should others not endure it?

Therefore, do not discriminate
between the two aspects of gnosis and consciousness.
From discriminating those two aspects, wrongdoing arises,
and through wrongdoing, merit is exhausted.
Suffering is called wrongdoing;
supreme bliss is merit.
When bliss and suffering have become equal,
there is liberation — beyond merit and wrongdoing.
Through suffering the mind is scattered,
and virtue and non-virtue are distinguished.
By the bliss of a woman's enjoyment,
non-conceptuality and non-duality are achieved.

In a delightful mountain retreat,
in a solitary forest place,
take hold of an intelligent partner with fine eyes and a stable disposition.
First gather together,
then accomplish the vajra ritual perfectly.
The supreme consort of the Tathāgata —
let her be consecrated with the great sacred commitment.
Perform the characteristics of pressing in and lifting,
likewise of thrusting strongly,
and the characteristic of the needle and the boat.
With a sound like a buzzing bee,
rouse her with certainty and intelligence.
Having cleansed her with graceful movements and gestures,
take hold of her hair upon the crown and press her flank.
Taste the honey of her lips.
Embrace with the vajra embrace,
call out with the vajra cry,
and press with teeth and nails.
Enter into the vajra and the lotus,
make the sounds of śvita,
suckling the breast and performing the manner of the man —
all engagements are to be performed.
The breast, fingers, waist, lips,
ears, eyes, crown, and the lotus as well —
moaning, ears, armpits, waist, and signs —
apply the fingernails and stroke the breasts and armpits;
press with the thighs on the cheeks and the palms of hands and feet,
and perform the eight modes of embrace.
Move the fingers, the liṅga, the tongue, and with the tongue,
arouse the lotus and awaken the supreme channel.

Avoid a lotus that is too deep, too small, too loose, too rough, constantly dripping, or devoid of warmth and moisture. A lotus that is elevated, soft, level at the top, firm and solid, endowed with blood and fluid, with little hair, that does not drip at improper times, and is long with a rounded inner core — such is the mark of a woman of good fortune. Having aroused her thoroughly in this way, as long as the sound of sīta arises from within, that is the measure. With the syllable haḥ one is to bind the life-force at the time of supreme joy.

Alternatively, it is the sound of hūṃ dwelling at the heart center. Or again, it is the sound of hrīṃ ha dwelling at the throat. By performing the actions of hūṃ and phaṭ, and carrying out the purification of the great arrow, one binds the seminal essence.

Furthermore, at the interval of the half of the fourth part, one is to meditate on the innate. If the drop dwelling in the seed-node of the lotus is to be drawn, it is drunk up by the vajra. By the application of circling and related movements, and by equalizing through the application of breath, one brings the two into balance. Having purified this through the applications of filling, pressing, engorging, and the vase-breathing technique, one enters into practice with women, and through this very body one is to attain the siddhi.

From that, through the fourfold distinction of joy, there arise in the practitioner eight strengths of the heart: erection, perspiration, horripilation, alteration of breath, trembling, change of complexion, arising of tears, and absorption.

The woman's attainment of gnosis is as follows:

The heart trembles upon attainment,
trembling and loss of mindfulness arise,
hair loosens, the body is naked and flooded with moisture,
the eyes grow crimson-red and extremely fine —
the manifestation of the five wisdoms (pañcajñāna).
Through the woman's knowing awareness, which is gnosis,
the five wisdoms are thereby attained.

With the vajra united with the lotus and water-born,
the vajra of the body is to be praised in pleasing words.
When the breath becomes equal
and the sound of sīta arises with a change of voice,
the speech becomes the nearby accomplisher.
The activity of the Protector melts completely.
The sixteen portions of the jewel descend,
and through the bliss of mind the duality ceases —
thus accomplish the reality of the mind.

Through the most excellent application of the bow-shaped one,
the drop, like a conch and a jasmine flower,
is to be held fast with the bliss of composed diligence.
The supreme gnosis of one's own awareness,
that very reality of the Great Siddha,
purifies consciousness into gnosis.
By this, the great vajra-nature,
the inseparable arising from the three bodies,
comes to be accomplished as an ocean of gnosis.

Through the most excellent application of the great gnosis,
one who is supremely skilled in yoga is to be praised.
This woman, the illusory mudrā,
is the supreme of all pledges.
By all manner of evil consumption and evil conduct,
the obscurations will by no means be completely purified.

Thus it was declared.

Now this is the pledge of the first yoga. One bearing the lotus, fearless, bearing the mudrā, having meditated on yoga with two hands, abiding in the center of the expanse of space, the maṇḍala is four-cornered with four gates. At the center it is entirely round, endowed with five activity-carpets. Red utpala lotuses are scattered densely. From the union of moon and sun, the pledge Tārā is to be visualized. Blue-green in color with spiraling red rays, fangs bared, a tongue of flame blazing — offerings, a curved knife, a ḍamaru drum, an arrow, a lotus stalk, a skull filled with blood, the three roots, and the fourth, holding a flower-bow. Adorned with charnel-ground ornaments and lotus ornaments, right leg extended in playful royal-ease posture, the assemblies of goddesses arising from their respective mantras are to be emanated from the center of the heart.

Oṃ tāre svāhā — for the eye.
Oṃ tuttāre svāhā — for the ear.
Oṃ tu svāhā — for the tongue.
Oṃ re svāhā — for the heart center.
Oṃ tāreṇi svāhā — for the crown.

The master of the mantra, having bestowed blessing, is to equalize gnosis and pledge. Having meditated on the meaning of empowerment, one stabilizes the mind as the deity.

This is the second chapter, the chapter of great yoga.

Then Tārā declared:

Oṃ lāṃ māṃ pāṃ tāṃ. Hūṃ vajra abhiṣiñca hūṃ. Ratna mukuṭa abhiṣiñca hūṃ. Oṃ hūṃ vajra abhiṣiñca hūṃ. Oṃ hūṃ tāṃ hūṃ vajra aṅghipati stāṃ abhiṣiñca hūṃ. Oṃ jaḥ hūṃ baḥ hoḥ vajrasattva staṃ vajranāma abhiṣiñca hūṃ.

These are the mantra-syllables of empowerment.

The vajra maṇḍala is to be drawn, beautifully adorned with eight pillars, properly complete with four lines, with an outer fence and a ring of fire. The charnel grounds too are to be drawn.

In the center, place a blue lotus. To the east, draw a pacifying lotus. To the north, draw a vase. To the west, a blazing iron hook. Place a sword to the south. These are the emblems of pacifying, enriching, magnetizing, and destroying. In the four corners, draw the goddesses of the families, each adorned with a lotus. At the gates, place the iron hook and the rest. The mighty wrathful ones who harm enemies are also to be placed in the corners. The host of goddesses who perform the activities is to be drawn on the outer portion. Then, establishing oneself at the center and emanating the host of vajra emblems, one purifies the elements of sentient beings. Fill the expanse of space to the fullest with clouds of offerings and rejoice. Taking flowers that are entirely fresh from the supreme bhaga, offer them to the cakra of the yoginī.

She spoke again:

Having sealed all nine gates, from the five-offering stūpa that is spread wide, transfer your own mind to the vital point at the apex of the breath through the joining of the arrow-base method. Even one who has committed the five without-interval acts will undoubtedly be liberated by this. One should practice the discipline of awareness: wearing old human-skin cloth or practicing the discipline of nakedness, having abandoned all anxious thought, gathering just as it appears in its way into the bliss of desire and enjoyment, and through all manner of inferior food and inferior conduct, one accomplishes unsurpassed awakening.

This, in relation to that, is the vajra samaya. The ācārya from whom one has received empowerment, who has become one's single ācārya, is to be examined as equal to the sister Tārā. That vajra ācārya likewise, and also one's vidyā or one's own consort, are to be proclaimed entirely as the supreme ācārya. Because the lama is equal to all buddhas, one must not even pass through his shadow. However, the willing or the delighted sovereign lady, she from whose nine gates of both has come forth — one partakes from a single vessel without conceptualization, and with a mind of purity one offers the tsumha. Having spread out a bed together and displayed the chamber, grasping the vajra with the hand and turning it, inserting the finger into the lotus, embracing firmly, arousing delight through words and syllables, drawing with teeth and nails, offering food through the aperture and the gate — all this, done with reverence and desire and with tenderness, there is no fault in this passing. From the ācārya's delight all accomplishments are permanently attained by that noble one. If on worshipping with the yuja with respect, and if the mind of mudrā becomes absorbed, one should take and eat what is drawn from the petaled one, offer the lotus as a gift, and the device and machine of sexual union — these are without fault, for they are what bring satisfaction to that reality.

Then she spoke on the manifold activities:

In the four directions and four corners, make the form of a lotus, each one marked with three tāṃ syllables. In the center, make it fully round. Oṃ prasanna tāre amṛtamukhī ta locanī sarva artha svaṃghani sarva arthasāṃghani sarva sattva vāśaṃkari — sī bā puruṣo sārvani bāba śyikuru tāṃ svāhā. This mantra is set at the center, and the wheel has ten spokes, with ten syllables on the lotus. If the name is inserted within two hrīḥ syllables, it brings the person under one's power. Alternatively, an eight-spoked wheel has eight syllables, with the syllable tāṃ on a moon disc at the center. If the wheels are joined face to face, within seven days the person will certainly come under one's power. Or with a six-spoked wheel there are six syllables. At the center: hrīḥ vāśaṃkuru hrīḥ svāhā. Write this on a bhoja leaf with gorocana, red sandalwood, lac, and blood. This brings all three states of existence under one's power.

The wheel made with saffron and the rest, with eight spokes together with butter and honey, through the practice of joining, brings under one's power. Oṃ sarva mahānītāre tuttāre duṣṭāṃ mohāya bhagavati sarvaduṣṭānāṃ bandha bandha hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ phaṭ phaṭ svāhā.

Having folded down the edge of a garment, as the supreme means of binding a thief, through openly praising the lotus — upon whomever it is given, that one comes under power. From the lotus arises the emblem-wheel.

At the navel of the variegated vajra, write Hrīḥ at the end of the name after the initial stroke, and when placed in the center of a drinking bowl, it arouses. Within the interior of an eight-petaled red lotus, enclosed within it, write eight ka syllables at the navel. With mustard seed upon flat stones, with the lid closed and placed down, it arouses intensely.

That wheel is free from poison. In water to which human bone has been attached, write hūṃ phaṭ upon a skull. By this one surely kills.

On wheat flour, write with saffron, offer with yellow flowers together with oṃ, or else write enclosed within svāhā. This is the supreme means for increasing and protecting.
