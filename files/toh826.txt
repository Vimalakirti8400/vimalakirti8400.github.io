Auspicious Verses (Maṅgalagāthā)

This short text, entitled in Sanskrit Maṅgalagāthā and in Tibetan བཀྲ་ཤིས་ཀྱི་ཚིགས་སུ་བཅད་པ (Bkra shis kyi tshigs su bcad pa), translates as "Verses of Auspiciousness" and is catalogued as Toh 826 in the Kangyur, placing it among the collection's short dhāraṇī and ritual verse texts. Opening with homage to the Three Jewels, the composition consists of a series of auspicious verses invoking the blessings of the Buddha's deeds and presence, celebrating his descent from the higher realms surrounded by divine hosts, his honoring by gods and celestial beings, and his passage into supreme peace. Each verse follows a refrain-like structure, naming a source of auspiciousness—such as the veneration offered by Indra, nāgas, garuḍas, yakṣas, and gandharvas—and dedicating that merit to bring peace to all beings. As a devotional and liturgical work, it belongs to the genre of auspicious verses (maṅgala-gāthā) recited for blessing and benediction, reflecting a tradition of invoking the Buddha's qualities and the reverence of both human and divine beings to generate well-being for all migrators.

Whatever auspiciousness belongs to the Victor who draws near to teach,
by that auspiciousness may all beings be brought to peace.

For the benefit of migrating beings he came here from the higher realms,
surrounded by Brahmā and the hosts of gods holding in their hands yak-tail whisks, parasols,
and various other emblems—whatever auspiciousness there is in that,
by that auspiciousness may all beings be brought to peace.

The Tathāgata who passed into supreme peace,
openly honored with many mandārava flowers,
openly praised by the supreme gods—whatever auspiciousness there is in that,
by that auspiciousness may all beings be brought to peace.

Honored by the lord of gods (Indra), by nāgas, and by the lords of men,
worshipped by garuḍas, yakṣas, and the lords of gandharvas,
the Tathāgata endowed with the ten powers—whatever auspiciousness there is in that,
by that auspiciousness may all beings be brought to peace.

This completes the auspicious verses of the Three Jewels and of the Twelve Deeds.
