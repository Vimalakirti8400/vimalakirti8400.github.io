Homage to the Blessed One, the Gentle Thunderbolt (Mañjuśrī).

Thus did I hear. At one time, the Blessed One, Destroyer of Māra (Skt. māravijit), together with the hosts of māra, was dwelling at ease in the palace of Other-Wielding Emanations in Akaniṣṭha.

The Blessed One declared: "Let Vajrapāṇi, the great commander of the yakṣas, the Blue-Clad One, take up this teaching, which has been authorized by buddhas as numerous as the grains of sand in the river Gaṅgā."

Thereupon, the bodhisattva Vajrapāṇi offered this praise, saying, "I request you to speak." He praised as follows:

Homage to you who are surrounded by assemblies of countless tens of millions of billions of yakṣas; who grind and gnash your fangs in ferocity; whose eyes are like masses of firebrands; whose breath blazes forth like tongues of flame; whose voice resounds with terror; whose arms and shoulders are adorned with lotus flowers and water deities; whose head is crowned by the wealth-bestower and the conch-bearer; who wear a blazing crown of precious serpents; who are adorned with ear-ornaments of the blazing nāgas Nandaka and Upanandaka; whose feet crush and devour blazing venomous serpents; who are girdled with Vāsuki, king of the nāgas, as a belt; who bear a black serpent as a topknot; whose full face shows brows drawn together in a frown; whose upper lip presses down; whose tongue flickers like lightning; who bear the likeness of the Lord of Death; whose great belly devours serpents; who stand so vast that the fathomless depths of the ocean — eighty-four thousand leagues — reach only to your knees; who are enthroned above the senses and the sun; who strike terror with the sound of your clapping hands; who cause trembling with the cry "A-la-la!"; who destroy all nāgas; who set everything in turmoil with great laughter; who, bearing the form of the Lord of Death, terrify the three realms; and who tame all nāgas — to you I bow.

Then, in that very sky, a great fierce one possessed of a vast palace and the meditative absorption of the great wheel gathered the retinue, and proclaimed:

Vajrapāṇi the supremely fierce,
the vajra wrathful one who vanquishes the greatly fierce,
the vajra splendor, lord of mantra,
the invincible vajra, greatly terrifying,
the vajra whose activities accomplish all purposes —
in the body of the great wheel of skillful taming,
the great self-nature of the five elements,
beautiful, radiant with rays of light,
lustrous, sending forth melodious sound and breath,
generating and accomplishing all actions —
he hooks, gathers, binds, and pacifies;
accomplishing actions that subdue the extremes,
of the indestructible nature of the bindus,
through the union, the flow and convergence of sun and moon,
the destroyer of thousands of classes of obstructors —
to you, Lord of the Wheel, I offer praise!

Through the blazing of the seed-syllable,
he nurtures and sustains skillful means and wisdom with fire;
having bestowed the drink of great bliss,
he destroys suffering and afflictions — to him I bow.

Oṃ a-kāro mukhaṃ sarva-dharmāṇāṃ ādyānutpannatvāt oṃ āḥ hūṃ.

Having thus praised the deities of the maṇḍala, the Blessed One smiled.

He said "Excellent!" to the bodhisattva Vajrapāṇi, and dismissed the assembly.

Then Vajrapāṇi said to the Blessed One: "Excellent! Excellent! Therefore, I request you to speak this tantra — which accomplishes all things through its secret mantra, destroys all sins, and tames the three fierce ones." Having circumambulated, he sat together with the assembly in the presence of the Blessed One.

Then the Buddha, the Blessed One, declared: "For the purpose of taming the malevolent ones, this vidyā-mantra, spoken and blessed by buddhas as numerous as the grains of sand in the river Gaṅgā, is to be recited. It is as follows:

Tadyathā — bho bho pānnāga adhipataye, idaṃ nīlāmbara-dhara-śarīram."

At that time, for one seized by the possession of a yakṣa, the mantra is to be cast over water and the water then sprinkled. To turn back nāgas, one should use a whip of smag-shad wood or a whip of Nepalese cane. For hungry ghosts and those afflicted with forgetfulness, one should use a cord. To eliminate epidemic disease, for hemiplegia, and for those bitten by leeches, one should use a blue cord. For one who desires wealth, one should remain in fasting abstinence for a single day and be fumigated with incense, and in a dream all matters of virtue and non-virtue will be revealed. In the presence of a stūpa enshrining bodily relics, having worshipped with blue flowers and the like and recited the mantra one thousand times, one should make supplication, and it will bestow seven thousand gold coins. For one who wishes to break a seal, one should use ashes. For one who wishes to open an iron door, one should use mustard seeds. To guard against all fears, one should use old butter, ashes, and mustard seeds. Against all fears of illness, tie a protective knot on a blue cord and wear it around the neck, and it will become the supreme protection. For children threatened by all manner of spirit possession, tie a cord of five colors around the neck and it will become the supreme protection. To drive away thieves, one should use salt. For poison, one should apply kuśa grass. To protect oneself, one should recite it mentally or with water. To protect others, one should use a blue cord. For all these activities, the mantra is to be recited — whether once, or three times, or fourteen times, or twenty-one times, or one hundred and eight times — and it will be accomplished.

When the Blessed One had thus spoken, the bodhisattva Vajrapāṇi, together with the host of māras, rejoiced and praised the teaching.

The noble tantra called Vajrapāṇi of the Blue Garment Taming the Three Fierce Ones is complete.
