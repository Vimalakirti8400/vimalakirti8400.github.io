The Conduct of the Yoginī (Yoginīsañcāryam)

Toh 375 in the Kangyur is a Buddhist tantric text known in English as The Conduct of the Yoginī, in Sanskrit as the Yoginīsañcārya, and in Tibetan as the Rnal 'byor ma'i kun tu spyod pa. It belongs to the class of Yoginī tantras within the Kangyur's collection of tantric scriptures, and it concerns the esoteric rites and modes of conduct associated with advanced Vajrayāna practice, including the sequential and reversed orders of arising, the reading of signs, and the cultivation of great bliss through the path of yoga. The text opens with a homage to the glorious Vajrasattva and a verse salutation to the guru as vajra-holder, through whose grace the state of great bliss is said to be attained in a single instant. Its content is presented as an explication of teachings that remain concealed within the broader tantric corpus, laid out as a practical guide for the accomplished yogic practitioner.

In the language of India: Yoginīsañcāryam. In the language of Tibet: The Conduct of the Yoginī.

Homage to the Blessed One, the glorious Vajrasattva.

Through whose grace the state of great bliss
Is attained in a single instant,
To the feet of the vajra-holder (Skt. vajradhara), precious as a jewel,
That guru — I bow down.

Now what is to be rightly taught
Is the supreme rite of conduct.
To cultivate this, the master of three wheels,
Is to abide on the path of yoga.

What remains concealed in the tantra
Regarding the forward and reversed order of arising,
Just as it was taught, that is spoken of;
The explanation too is given in the same way.

Every last teaching given separately
Is the detailed rite of signs.
Through mere knowledge of this,
The practitioner attains accomplishment.
By the mode of thirty-seven,
One abides on the side of awakening.
This is taught as the object of meditation in the world:
The glorious Heruka, supreme among beings.

Now first, the sovereign of yoga should generate divine pride toward the five aggregates. Toward the aggregate of form: Vairocana. Toward the aggregate of feeling: Vajra Sun. Toward the aggregate of perception: Padma Lord of Dance. Toward the aggregate of formations: Vajra King. Toward the aggregate of consciousness: Vajrasattva. The essential identity of all Tathāgatas is Śrī Heruka Vajra. Toward the two eyes: Vajra of Delusion. Toward the two ears: Vajra of Aversion. Toward the two nostrils: Vajra of Envy. Toward the tongue: Vajra of Desire. Toward the body: Vajra of Miserliness. Toward all the sense bases together: the expanse of the sovereign vajra. Toward the earth element: she who causes downfall. Toward the water element: she who causes death. Toward the fire element: she who summons. Toward the wind element: she who commands dance. Toward the space element: she who is the lotus-web. In this way, divine pride is to be generated toward the aggregates, elements, and sense bases. From the Conduct of the Yoginī, the purification of the abodes of the deities: the first abiding.

Now the thirty-seven factors of awakening (Skt. bodhipakṣadharma) are to be applied through divine yoga. What are all of these? The close placement of mindfulness on the body; the close placement of mindfulness on feeling; the close placement of mindfulness on phenomena; the close placement of mindfulness on mind; the miraculous foot of aspiration; the miraculous foot of diligence; the miraculous foot of analysis; the miraculous foot of mind; the faculty of faith; the faculty of diligence; the faculty of mindfulness; the faculty of meditative absorption; the faculty of wisdom; the power of faith; the power of diligence; the power of mindfulness; the power of meditative absorption; the power of wisdom; perfect meditative absorption as a factor of awakening; perfect diligence as a factor of awakening; perfect joy as a factor of awakening; perfect pliancy as a factor of awakening; perfect discrimination of phenomena as a factor of awakening; perfect mindfulness as a factor of awakening; perfect equanimity as a factor of awakening; right view; right intention; right speech; right action as its fullest scope; right livelihood; right effort; right mindfulness; right meditative absorption; generating virtuous phenomena not yet arisen; fully guarding virtuous phenomena already arisen; abandoning non-virtuous phenomena already arisen; and not generating non-virtuous phenomena not yet arisen. Thus: the close placements of mindfulness, the right abandonings, the miraculous feet, the faculties, the powers, the factors of awakening,

and the noble eightfold path in its fullest scope — these are the thirty-seven factors conducive to awakening. This concludes the second part: the teaching of the thirty-seven factors conducive to awakening from the conduct of the yoginī.

Now, to summarize briefly the enumeration of the divine correspondences of these, as they stand: the close placement of mindfulness of the body is the ḍākinī. The close placement of mindfulness of feeling is Lāmā. The close placement of mindfulness of phenomena is Khaṇḍarohā. The close placement of mindfulness of mind is the Corporeal One. The miraculous foot of intention is the Supremely Fierce One. The miraculous foot of diligence is the Fierce-Eyed One. The miraculous foot of investigation is the Luminous One. The miraculous foot of mind is the Great-Nosed One. The faculty of faith is Vīramatī. The faculty of diligence is the Dwarf Woman. The faculty of mindfulness is the Sovereign of Laṅkā. The faculty of meditative absorption is the Shade of the Tree. The faculty of wisdom is the Earth Protectress. The power of faith is the Great Terror-Bringer. The power of diligence is the Wind-Force One. The power of mindfulness is the Beer-Drinker. The power of meditative absorption is the Blue-Cleansed One. The power of wisdom is the Supremely Good One. Perfect meditative absorption as a factor of awakening is the Horse-Eared One. Perfect diligence as a factor of awakening is the Bird-Faced One. Perfect joy as a factor of awakening is the Wheel-Force One. Perfect pliancy as a factor of awakening is Khaṇḍarohā. Perfect discrimination of phenomena as a factor of awakening is the Beer-Seller. Perfect mindfulness as a factor of awakening is Cakravartinī. Perfect equanimity as a factor of awakening is the Supremely Courageous One. Right view is the Great-Powered One. Right intention is the Wheel-Turner. Right speech is the Great Diligent One. Right action in its fullest scope is the Crow-Faced One. Right livelihood is the Owl-Faced One. Right effort is the Dog-Faced One. Right mindfulness is the Sow-Faced One. Right meditative absorption is the Blessed Glorious Heruka. Generating virtuous phenomena not yet arisen is Steadfast Yamī. Fully guarding virtuous phenomena already arisen is the Messenger of Yama. Abandoning non-virtuous phenomena already arisen is Tusk-Bearing Yamī. Not generating non-virtuous phenomena not yet arisen is Yama the Destroyer.

This concludes the third part from the conduct of the yoginī: the teaching on the pure divine forms of the thirty-seven factors conducive to awakening.

Then the body of the glorious Heruka, the supreme hero of non-duality, is to be rightly expounded: each limb and member of that body abides as follows. The nails and teeth are half-skulls. The body and its hairs are great bones. The skin and its impurities are bones. The flesh is the one who gnashes fangs. The sinews are the enemy of the gods. The bones are Amitābha. The kidneys are Vajra-Radiance. The heart is the Vajra Body. The two eyes are the One with Sprouts. The bile is the Vajra-Haired One. The lungs are the Great Hero. The intestines are Vajra Hamzad. The spleen is the Supremely Good. The belly is Vajra the Excellent. The excrement is the Great Terror-Bringer. The hollow of the navel is the Ugly-Eyed One. The phlegm is the Supremely Powerful One. The pus is Precious Vajra. The blood is Hayagrīva. The dust is Ākāśagarbha. The fat is the Glorious Heruka. The tears are Padma, Lord of Dance.

The saliva is Vairocana. The mucus is Vajrasattva. This concludes the fourth chapter, which presents the pure display of the hero drawn from the conduct of the yoginī.

Then, further, what is to be explained:
The accomplisher of all attainments (Skt. siddhi),
by merely knowing which
the practitioner will obtain all attainments —
just as one knows the inner nature of oneself,
so is the sacred outer maṇḍala.
The manner in which the arrangement is applied
is the synthesis of all essences.
Through the mode of support and supported,
Bhairava and the rest abide truly in their places.
As transmitted from the guru in due lineage,
whoever knows this ritual —
upon the mountain, on the soft, variegated petals
of a lotus in full bloom,
the hero who dwells in its center,
accomplishing all desired aims —
the ḍākinī, the Lāmā, and she who bears the form of Khaṇḍarohā:
place them in the lotus directions.
They are the accomplishers of all attainments.
Two-armed, with one face,
of varied form, the Lady of Display.
In the four intermediate directions
place the skull-cups filled with the fifth.
Pūllīra and the rest are joined in their proper order,
abiding as domain and dweller in domain.
Invite them; they remain in the center of the wheel.
The yogin with awareness should always offer worship.

The syllable Ku refers to Kulūtā: the hero is Vajrasattva and the heroine is Mahāvīryā. These are the adjacent charnel grounds. The wheel of body — moving beneath the earth.

Kākāsyā, Ulūkāsyā, Śvānāsyā, and Sūkarāsyā are in the directions. Yamādādhī, Yamādūtī, Yamādaṃṣṭriṇī, and Yamāmathanī are in the intermediate directions.

In this way, so long as the outer and inner nature have not been definitively comprehended as one's field of experience, one should continue to meditate upon them as objects of contemplation.

From the Conduct of the Yoginī, the fifth chapter, which teaches through the abodes of the deities.

Then the emblems of all, beginning with the sovereign,
the assemblies of mudrā,
one's own color, hand-implements, and manner of abiding —
these are to be definitively concealed within the tantra.

The great king Śrī Heruka,
who utterly vanquishes all that is malevolent —
with two hands he perfectly embraces,
and with two he holds the elephant hide.
In the remaining right hands: an enemy-stake, a trident, a curved knife, and a ḍamaru drum.
In the remaining left hands: he raises the khaṭvāṅga,
a great red skull-cup, a lasso,
and the heads of Brahmā — these are described.
He is adorned with twelve of these.
He holds a garland of skulls as an offering vessel —
this is to be known as numbering fifty.

The Blessed One's consort stands before him:
one face, massively blazing.
In the embrace she holds skull-cups
filled with the blood of malevolent māras.
With the right hand she constantly makes the threatening gesture,
and in the fierce hand she holds a curved knife.
Her body color is red, blazing greatly,
her form substantial, adorned with skull fragments.
Her hair flows loose; her body has three eyes.
Her bared fangs are awesome and terrifying.
She is well embraced about the hips by his legs.
The two abide in the mode of kissing.

The four ḍākinīs and the rest
hold khaṭvāṅgas, vessels, and curved knives.
On their bodies they have three eyes, the five mudrā-ornaments, and the like,
and are adorned with flowing loose hair.

The twenty-four heroes
are adorned with the five mudrā-ornaments and the like.
Two hands, one face, three eyes —
their crown of matted hair is bound with silk.
They well embrace vajra and bell,
and enact great passion and subsequent desire.

The twenty-four yoginīs
are adorned with flowing loose hair,
with the five mudrā-ornaments and the like,
and their crowns are adorned with jewels.
They embrace with a vessel and the rest,
and hold curved knives with the threatening gesture.

Śrī Heruka and all the rest
abide in the posture with the right leg extended.
They are adorned with necklace, armlets, earrings,
and a garland of skulls.
The offering vessel, the skull-cup held aloft, and ashes —
these are explained as the sixth mudrā.
Through the purity of the six perfections,
they are adorned with the six mudrā-ornaments.

The vajras of body, speech, and mind
are adorned with pure ornaments below the three eyes.
Through the distinctions of body, speech, and mind,
one should meditate on oneself as the essential nature
of the accomplishments of the three syllables,
and those who desire accomplishment should do nothing else.

Whatever merit arises from that —
the vast qualities of accomplishment —
could not be expressed in their merit
even by hundreds and thousands of tongues.

The four, beginning with Crow-Face,
are all gatekeepers.
They hold khaṭvāṅga and skull-cup,
and on their bodies they bear the five mudrā-ornaments and the like.

Yamādhṛta and the rest are at the four intermediate directions.
They have both legs extended,
possessing two forms, captivating the mind —
just as the ḍākinīs arise,
those of Crow-Face and the rest are distinguished accordingly.
Thus, without exception, they abide in the wheel,
in accordance with the sequential rite of color-distinctions.

From the Conduct of the Yoginī, the sixth chapter, which teaches one's own emblems, faces, mudrā-ornaments, body colors, and weapons.

Then the two armors —
by whoever knows them, swiftly
will the practitioner attain accomplishment.
This is to be definitively explained as concealed within the tantra.

Oṃ ha. Nama hi. Svāhā hu. Bauṣaṭ he. Hūṃ hūṃ ho. Phaṭ haṃ.

Apply the armor to: the heart, the crown, the top of the head,
the shoulders, and the two eyes.
The first is Vajrasattva,
the second abides as Vairocana,
the third is Padmanartteśvara, the lord of dance,
the fourth is described as Śrī Heruka,
the fifth is Vajrasūrya,
and the sixth is the supreme horse, Hayagrīva.
Thus, by these six armors,
one is most excellently protected.

Oṃ baṃ. Haṃ yoṃ. Hrīṃ moṃ. Hrīṃ hrīṃ. Hūṃ hūṃ. Phaṭ phaṭ.

The navel, the heart, likewise the mouth,
the crown, the top of the head — these are the armor-points.
The first is Vajravārāhī.
The second is to be called Yāminī.

The third is Mohinī herself.
The fourth is described as Dākinī.
The fifth is known as the Universal Terror.
The sixth is described as Caṇḍālī.
These six armors together are the Sow and the non-dual yoginī.

From the General Practice of the Yoginī, the Two Armors Explained — the seventh chapter.

Then, having understood the two armor-points,
one meditates fully on the wisdom wheel,
and through the application of mantra and mudrā,
swiftly draws the samaya wheel in.
Press the foot down and gaze upward;
above, proclaim the sound Pheṃ.
The heroes and yoginīs dwelling
in the world-realms of the ten directions — summon them.
With mantra, rouse them truly,
and in that very instant invite them in.
Curve the finger; the middle finger is firmly joined.
The thumb is precisely applied as a vajra hook.
This is placed at the center of the forehead,
and by rolling and turning it round and round,
the mantrin bestows blessing upon the heroes and yoginīs.
The syllable dyā is precisely applied at the tip of the nose.
The syllable byā is properly placed at the hub of the sex organ.
At the navel, the heart, the mouth, and the forehead,
consecration is bestowed with the full assemblage of mantras.
At the navel and at the heart, the yoginī —
by the mantras of the buddha-ḍākinīs —
through the two channels, the avadyūtī, is heroically blessed.
The yoginī and the hero are non-dual.
The first joy is the hero;
the supreme joy is the yoginī;
the joy of total delight is the entirety.
The means of bliss — all of this is to be known.
In the realm of the gods, on the earth's surface, and beneath the earth,
in an instant they become a single form.
What I have explained as meditation,
and what is described as the gathering —
with the two arms, right and left,
the turning is the bliss of the ḍākinī.

From the General Practice of the Yoginī, the Inviting and Gathering of the Wisdom Wheel — the eighth chapter.

Then, listen — those who wish to know
what is to be understood as the emanation body
and what is desired as the perfect enjoyment body.
The great mudrā of the Great Vehicle
is said to grant the attainment of the yoginī.
Above the great mudrā
there is no other yoga more supreme.
Śrī Heruka, the great king,
is the lord sovereign over all yogas.
The net of the ḍākinīs, bound,
taken from that which is equal to space —
omniscient wisdom is its intrinsic nature.
Śrī Heruka, the supreme hero,
in whom all noble beings are gathered,
is accomplished through the drink of peace.
The supreme station is eternal,
and the flawless abiding is permanent.
Join properly to sons and so forth;
dwelling as object in each and every domain,
establish oneself as the identity of Śrī Heruka.
The twenty-four syllables
are to be understood as the perfect enjoyment body,
abiding within the great yoga.
In brief, one who enters with thoroughness —
let the diligent, discerning mind know this.
Now listen, practitioners — I shall explain
how the reversal is taught.
Śrī signifies non-dual wisdom.
He signifies the emptiness of cause and so forth.
Ru signifies freedom from arrangement.
Ka signifies non-abiding anywhere.
That is empty of intrinsic nature,
all-pervading and indestructible.
Meditating on this in brief,
one will attain the desired accomplishment.
Meditating fully on the three bodies —
the dharmakāya, the saṃbhogakāya, and the nirmāṇakāya —
and the thirty-seven factors of awakening:
these one must certainly meditate upon.

From the General Practice of the Yoginī, the Explanation of Entry — the ninth chapter.

Then, if one grows weary from meditation,
employ the special mode of condensed conduct.
Do not be separated from the practice —
this is the yoga of conduct.
As described previously,
elaborately perform the dhyāna ritual,
and to whatever activity one desires,
offer the torma especially.
On the tenth day of the waning moon
and likewise on the tenth of the waxing moon,
diligently make excellent offerings of
beer and so forth to the heroes and to the Vajra Goddess.
Join the mantras described earlier with the seal,
and guard them properly in accordance with the ritual.

Oṃ ā ra llī hoḥ jaḥ hūṃ baṃ hoḥ. Vajra-ḍākinī samaya stvaṃ dṛśya hoḥ.

Recite this thus three, four, or five times.

With the samaya substances and the rest well in place,
open the vajra-joined palms with the fingers pointing upward.
Let the heroes and yoginīs recite it openly.

Oṃ kha kha khāhi khāhi. Sarva-yakṣa-rākṣasa bhūta-preta-piśāca-unmāda-pasmāra-vajra-ḍāka-ḍākinī-adaya imaṃ baliṃ gṛhṇantu samayaṃ rakṣantu mama sarva-siddhim me prayacchantu yathābhaṃ. Yathéṣṭaṃ bhuñjatha jighrata pibata mātikramatha mama sarva-kārtayā. Satsukhāṃ viśuddhaye sahāyikā bhavantu hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ phaṭ svāhā.

The mantra of the hero's wheel and the others,
adorned also with charnel grounds and the like —
the yogī, facing south,
makes excellent offerings at a lofty site.
As indicated by what is proximate,
one should offer according to one's ability.
On the tenth day of the middle winter month and the rest,
make offerings with faith.
To the noble person, the tamer of all,
offer every kind of happiness.
There, the food offerings are to be eaten,
and the remainder is to be left in a vessel.
The glory that comes to one's hand through this
nothing whatsoever can destroy.
This is to be worshipped by those who are devoted.
When the glorious Heruka becomes fully delighted,
he will bestow the supreme of all joys,
for the supreme abides in his very hand.

At night, with hair unbound,
if one makes torma-offerings naked in the vow-conduct,
the glorious Heruka, the lord of yoga,
will be gladdened by the mothers through this.

This is the tenth chapter of the Conduct of the Yoginī, which teaches the protection of making torma and offerings on the tenth day.

Then, the six perfections,
the thirty-seven factors of awakening,
the grounds, the ten stages and the rest —
these are of the nature of the Great God (Śiva),
the supreme divine one.
Whatever things by whatever means
one properly engages the minds of people —
by that one is transformed into that very thing,
like a jewel possessed of various forms.
Likewise, whether dwelling with fear
or dwelling without fear, however one dwells,
through pure meditative concentration or through meditation,
all activities are excellently accomplished.
The supreme fruit of conduct and liberation
is to be attained in this very life.
The meditation I shall explain
is an essence gathered from all.

One who possesses the understanding of an erroneous mind —
you must not teach it to any such person.
As the guru has taught in due order,
that by which one quickly comes to understand:
in ten million thousands of lives,
urged on by illusory wisdom,
with genuine faith aroused
and abiding in the yogic mode of conduct —
such a one is fit for it.

One who is mute and dull, possessed of bad methods,
one who has the understanding of a childish mind,
sentient beings who are not yet fully matured,
those who have gone astray through external treatises —
all of these do not know it.
Covered by the darkness of non-knowing,
delighting in relying on arrogance,
possessed of a mind with pride and the other seven —
all of these do not know
the secret domain of purification of the buddhas.

The great master who is the glorious Heruka's true entry,
who is the great self,
swiftly generates the accomplishments
and is one who instills immediate confidence.
One who knows the yoga of the rite of total purification —
to that one the activities of the ritual are to be taught.
Within the three existences without remainder,
that one knows the teaching of the omniscient.

By seeing, by touching,
by hearing, by remembering —
from all misdeeds one is liberated,
thus, this is beyond doubt.

Turning back the gods and making sport,
turning back the distinctions of mantra,
turning back the secrets of meditation —
these accomplish every essence.

The minds fully ripened
that are the common ground of all the tantras —
here the samayas of the mantras of preparation and the rest
are taught in that way.

The relief spoken of by the Sugata (the Buddha, the well-gone one) —
a pure body, free of fault —

Even a mantra-practitioner of little merit
shall obtain it through my delight.

From the All-Pure Conduct of the Yoginī — the eleventh chapter, teaching the purification of all things.

Then: neither guarded nor unguarded,
neither one who holds a maṇḍala nor one who does not,
without recitation, asceticism, or fire-offering —
in brief, one who has gathered the mind in proper form.

The consciousness to be apprehended and the primordial wisdom —
these are to be striven for through what the guru has taught.
The way of external treatises
is an exceedingly long path.

For yogins, through skillful means,
striving in service and veneration of the guru —
this is the teaching of the defining mark of the Teacher,
and it is the hero who teaches that very suchness.

The self of the yoga of momentariness,
possessed of the mudrā of momentariness —
in all respects, everything is instantaneous;
in an instant the secret mantra is recited.

Speaking, seeing, embracing with the hand,
clasping, the pair of two, and so forth —
the Tathāgata has declared these as relief;
they are to be relied upon to an ever higher degree.

All is taught here.
Other varieties are like grass.
That very suchness is the life of living creatures;
that very suchness is the immutable, the supreme.
That very suchness pervades everything
and abides distinctly throughout all bodies.
That very suchness is the wisdom of the buddhas;
it is declared to be glorious Heruka.

Men, those of indeterminate sex, and the rest —
even the subtle essence is grasped therein.

In that which has arisen as the self of wisdom —
those who abide on the grounds,
all the treatises without exception,
those who abide at the ultimate end of the Vedas:
in this world and in others,
without that, nothing is accomplished.

From among the ultimate ends accomplished through the Vedas,
this is proclaimed as the supreme essence.

All these beings appear as the very nature of the five buddhas,
appearing like skilled dancers and beautiful paintings.
That which is called great bliss is one alone,
and the experience of that one dances in many forms.

Sitting and rising, speech itself,
entering and completing, purity itself,
blessing, empowerment,
and meditative absorption — these are the condensed worship.

Then: the other name of the ritual,
the non-duality of skillful means and wisdom —
in that very suchness, everything arises;
it performs the sending forth and the gathering.
