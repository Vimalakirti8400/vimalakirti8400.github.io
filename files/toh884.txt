The Dhāraṇī Called "The Stūpa Accomplished as Bringing Everything to Perfection through Knowledge"

The Dhāraṇī Called "The Stūpa Accomplished as Bringing Everything to Perfection through Knowledge," known in Tibetan as Shepé Tamché Tartar Chinpar Druppé Chörten Zhejawé Zung, is a short scriptural incantation whose opening gives only a Tibetan title, with no Sanskrit title preserved. It begins with homages to the Three Jewels, to the Blessed One Vajradhara, to the buddhas of the three times, to Akṣobhya, Amitābha, and Avalokiteśvara, and to the three buddha families. It then presents a teaching set on a great Mount Meru made of five substances, whose very base is described as having the nature of a stūpa and on which stands a jeweled mansion. The text centers on the stūpa as a sacred embodiment of awakened knowledge and on a dhāraṇī connected with it. It is Toh 884 in the Kangyur, where it belongs to the class of dhāraṇī texts, the collected spells and incantations of the canon.

In the Tibetan language: The Dhāraṇī Called "The Stūpa Accomplished as Bringing Everything to Perfection through Knowledge."

Homage to the Three Jewels. Homage to the Blessed One Vajradhara. Homage to the Blessed One, the Tathāgata, the arhat, the perfectly complete Buddha, the King of Splendor of the way of all dharmas, of discipline, and of miraculous display.

Homage to the buddhas of the three times;
Homage to the virtuous Three Jewels;
To Akṣobhya and Amitābha
And Avalokiteśvara I pay homage.
Homage also to the three families.

Thus did I hear at one time. The Blessed One, the great Vajradhara, was dwelling on the great Mount Meru, made of five kinds of substance, whose very base abides as the intrinsic nature of a stūpa. Upon it was a mansion of various jewels and four immeasurable stūpas, and because it is the abode of the great stūpa of intrinsic nature, even the shape of its summit had become a stūpa. There he dwelt together with the bodhisattvas of the ten grounds, such as Vajrapāṇi, Mañjuśrī, Avalokiteśvara, and Maitreya, and with the lords of the pure realms, such as Brahmā, Śakra (Indra), and the Four Great Kings.

Then the Blessed One sent forth from the uṣṇīṣa on the crown of his head, from the śrīvatsa at his heart, and from other places, some sixty hundred thousand koṭis of niyutas of light rays of many different colors. With the great illumination of these light rays he filled and pervaded all the countless, inconceivable, and inexpressible world systems of the great trichiliocosm, as a pod is filled with sesame seeds. Through this, among all the infinite sentient beings, the blind saw forms, the deaf heard sounds, the mute were able to speak, the lame and the crippled walked and their limbs were made whole, the sick were freed from illness, and the hell beings had their lives shortened and were gradually freed from suffering. Moreover, the beings of the destinies gradually regained their mindfulness. Having done this, he filled and expanded the whole element of space with the dharmakāya, the stūpa of the mind. Those light rays then gathered back into the crown of the Blessed One's head, whereupon they became an image of the dharmakāya.

Then the retinue, Vajrapāṇi and the others, were filled with wonder, and they made offerings to the Blessed One of canopies, victory banners, pennants, parasols, yak-tail whisks, bells, hanging silk streamers, incense, flowers, garlands, ointments, lamps, food offerings, music, and so on. Having made these offerings, they said this to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, protector of beings, endowed with great compassion, please explain the rite for consecrating a stūpa, the great vidyā, the Vimaloṣṇīṣa dhāraṇī."

The Blessed One declared: "If any son of good family or daughter of good family holds, keeps, writes, reads, or listens to the name of this rite for consecrating a stūpa, the great vidyā, the Vimaloṣṇīṣa dhāraṇī, then the five acts of immediate retribution, the ten nonvirtues, and whatever evil deeds that person has done will all be purified without remainder. This is the rite for consecrating a stūpa with the great vidyā of Vimaloṣṇīṣa:

oṃ traiyadhā sarva tathāgata hṛdaya garbhe jvala dharmadhātu garbhe saṃharaṇa āyuḥ saṃśodhaya pābaṃ sarva tathāgatasamanta uṣṇīṣa vimale viśuddhe svāhā"

As soon as this great vidyā was spoken, all the world systems became of the intrinsic nature of a stūpa.

If one writes this great vidyā on paper, cloth, or birch bark and fastens it to the top of a victory banner, it becomes worthy of the offerings, prostrations, and honor of all gods, humans, and others. If one wears this great vidyā on the body or at the neck, gods, humans, non-humans, and others will regard that person as the Buddha himself.

When one goes to a mountain crag, a grove, a hermitage, or any place at all, if one recites this great vidyā, all the mountains and places will become like stūpas. If one recites

oṃ traiyadhva sarva tathāgata hṛdya garbhe jvala dharmadhātu garbhe saṃharaṇa āyuḥ saṃśodhaya pāpaṃ sarva tathāgata samanta uṣṇīṣa vimale viśuddhe mahāvirajeragaṃ virasi dharmadhātu garbhe vivisāra a alaṃ svāhā

then all the ground as well will become of the intrinsic nature of a stūpa.

When one crosses any water, river, lake, ocean, or the like, if one consecrates it with this great vidyā mantra, all that water too will become of the intrinsic nature of a stūpa. If one recites

oṃ traiyadhe sarva tathāgatā hṛdaya garbhe jvala dharmadhātu garbhe saṃharaṇa āyuḥ saṃśodhaya pāpaṃ sarva tathāgata samanta vimala uṣṇīṣa viśuddhe mahāvirajeragaṃ virasi dharmadhātu vivisāra amamaki svāhā

then all water too will become of the intrinsic nature of a stūpa.

Furthermore, if one applies this great vidyā mantra so that it pervades space as far as space extends, all of space too will become of the intrinsic nature of a stūpa. If one recites

oṃ traiyadhve sarva tathāgatā hṛdaya garbhe jvala dharmadhātu garbhe saṃharaṇa ayuḥ saṃśodhaya pāpaṃ sarva tathāgatā samanta vimala uṣṇīṣa viśuddhe mahāvirajeragaṃ virasi dharmadhātu vivisāra a a ekhaṃya svāhā

then the whole element of space will become of the intrinsic nature of a stūpa, and it will become worthy of prostration, circumambulation, and offering.

If, reciting this Vimaloṣṇīṣa dhāraṇī, one establishes a stūpa upon any of the five great elements, all afflictions and obscurations will be purified.

Furthermore, if one consecrates with this vidyā mantra any material at all, whether earth, wood, stone, clay, or rubble, and builds a stūpa, it will become the cause of a stūpa of various jewels. If, having recited oṃ ratna caitye bañcaye vivisāra svāhā, one smears it with cow dung and the like, all of it will become the cause of a jewel stūpa.

If this great vidyā mantra is not set in, then even if one has a stūpa built of the seven precious substances, such as gold, silver, and beryl, it will be no different from stone, wood, and clods of earth. Why is that? Because the essence and the blessing of the secret mantra do not reside in the intrinsic nature of these materials themselves.

If one sets it in after consecrating it with this great vidyā, then on whatever great element one establishes the stūpa, the stains of obscuration of all countless eons will be purified. One will be born into a householder family that is like a great sāla tree, and will attain the irreversible ground.

If one consecrates with this great vidyā and establishes a stūpa on stone, wooden planks, and the like, one will without doubt be born into a brahmin family that is like a great sāla tree. One will traverse the ten grounds in succession and attain the eleventh, Samantaprabhā, Universal Light.

If one consecrates with this great vidyā and establishes a stūpa on gold, silver, beryl, and the like, one will without doubt be born into a royal family that is like a great sāla tree, and will attain the ground of Vajradhara.

Furthermore, if a stūpa is set up with this great vidyā mantra, then even setting up a single stūpa will be the same as setting up ten million. If it is not set up with this great vidyā, then even setting up ten million will be the same as setting up one. Why is that? It is as explained above.

The mantra of that great vidyā is this:
tadyathā sūkṣme same samaye śānte dānte nirkule yaśo tejo sarva buddha adhiṣṇāna adhiṣṭhite anumide svāhā

Its rite is this: one recites it twenty-one times over a ball of clay. If one then sets up a single stūpa, it will be the same as setting up ten million. The number and the benefits of setting up a hundred cannot be told even by the noble śrāvaka arhats. The number and the benefits of setting up a thousand cannot be told even by the bodhisattvas. The number and the benefits of setting up ten thousand cannot be told even by the tathāgatas. The number and the benefits of setting up a hundred thousand cannot be told even by the tathāgatas of the ten directions and the three times.

Therefore, the offering rite to be performed for setting up five or fewer supports for offering is as follows. One consecrates the marking cord with this great vidyā mantra, reciting oṃ vajra sūtra hūṃ phaṭ. Then, visualizing that very cord as a vajra cord, one marks out with the line as much ground as is suitable for the stūpa, and as much ground as is suitable for the clay. When the ground has been marked out in this way, that site itself will become like the cause of a stūpa of many kinds of precious substances.

Then, on that same ground, one should set this great vidyā mantra into pegs of khadira wood eight finger-widths long. Reciting oṃ vajra kīli kīlaya sarva duṣṭaṃ hūṃ phaṭ seven times and visualizing them as vajra pegs, one plants them at the center and at the four corners. When they are planted in this way, one will be protected from humans and nonhumans, except for the karmic maturation of former actions. The khadira pegs are to be left in place and not pulled out until as much earth for the stūpa's clay as is needed has been taken.

Then one should recite this great vidyā mantra over sesame and white mustard seed, reciting oṃ amṛte hana hana hūṃ phaṭ seven times. By scattering them over the area, that very area will become countless abodes of the tathāgatas. If one then recites this Vimaloṣṇīṣa dhāraṇī, that area will become exceedingly auspicious and filled with blessing.

Then, when digging the earth for the stūpa's clay, one should dig while reciting this great vidyā mantra. Reciting oṃ vajra khana khana hūṃ phaṭ and visualizing that very ground as having turned into the five precious substances, if one digs, it will become the cause of the completely pure body of the Tathāgata.

Then the clean earth that has been taken is placed in a single clean spot. Reciting vajra muṅgara ākoṭaya ākoṭaya hūṃ phaṭ seven times, visualizing a vajra mallet, if one pounds, all afflictions and habitual tendencies without exception will be purified and destroyed.

The great rays of light of that moon disc pervade the scented water, and one visualizes the scented water itself as having become the nature of amṛta. Then one should recite this great vidyā mantra, oṃ amṛte hūṃ phaṭ, seven times. Through this consecration, that water will become like a stream of great amṛta.

Then one should recite this great mantra for kneading the clay, oṃ vajra bramardanāya hūṃ phaṭ, seven times, and that very clay will become the body of the Tathāgata.

The scripture, too, states this:

Because the very nature of the mantra
Of all vidyā mantras and secret mantras is not clear,
And the samādhi of contemplation is not clear,
Stūpas and other sacred images,
Though erected, will not come to completion.

So it is stated. As for the qualities of erecting stūpas and images by one endowed with secret mantra and contemplation, it is said:

This sublime rite of the stūpa:
The distinction of the merit it brings
Is that, with a stūpa in which this dhāraṇī is recited,
To erect one is like erecting ten million.
Of saṃsāra from ages long past,
The five deeds of immediate retribution and the heaps of evil,
However many there are, are cleansed without remainder.
One comes to have a long and perfect life.
When born in other lives,
One is born into a supreme and noble family,
Fully endowed with the finest wealth,
And is born a wheel-turning king.
One comes to remember former and later lives.
In all the worlds of the three times
One is never separated from the Three Jewels,
And obstructors and harms cannot shake one.
One's wishes are completely fulfilled.
In the end, one attains manifest awakening.

The Vimaloṣṇīṣa dhāraṇī is complete.

These translations are released into the public domain (CC0) in the spirit of spreading the Dharma to all corners of the earth.

Just as the Bodhi tree now standing at Bodh Gayā neither is nor is not the same tree beneath which the Buddha attained awakening, so too this stupa is intended as the next generation of stupa — dependently arising according to the conditions of its environment, and ceaselessly proclaiming the Dharma for the benefit of all sentient beings.

May whoever encounters these words derive great benefit.
