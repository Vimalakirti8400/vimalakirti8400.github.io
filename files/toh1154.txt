A Praise of the Buddha in Ten Verses (Buddhastavadaśa)

A Praise of the Buddha in Ten Verses, whose Sanskrit title appears in the translation as Buddhastavadaśa, is a short devotional hymn addressed to the Buddha Śākyamuni. In a series of verses, each ending with a refrain of homage, it celebrates his first arousing of the aspiration for supreme awakening, his attainment of deathless liberation for the benefit of beings, his victory over Māra, and his steadfast pure conduct. It also praises his lion-like confidence before rival disputants and the reverence shown to him by kings, by Brahmā, and by the gods, recalling as well his long lineage of practice under Dīpaṃkara and other earlier sages. The work is Toh 1154 in the Tengyur, where it belongs to the collection of praises, or stotras, a class of devotional poetry composed in honor of the buddhas and other awakened beings.

In the language of Tibet: A Praise of the Buddha in Ten Verses

Homage to the Buddha.

Having first given rise to the cause of supreme awakening,
For the sake of benefiting beings you won deathless liberation;
Shining with perfectly blazing light, victorious over Māra:
To the Victor Śākyamuni I bow down in homage.

King of sages, holy one, supreme protector of others,
Through single-minded diligence firmly abiding in the supreme pure life,
A lion, lord of beasts, before the mighty disputants of divergent minds:
To Śākyamuni I bow in homage.

Having won the supreme from Dīpaṃkara and the other sages,
Praised by lords of the earth who wear blazing, precious jewels in their topknots,
By Brahmā and by the ageless gods,
To Śākyamuni, who has abandoned evil, I bow down in homage.

The Sage is encircled by an ornament of vast rays of light;
His brilliant light, spreading through the broad expanses of fire and water,
Delights to pervade the realms of Brahmā's world:
To Śākyamuni I now bow down in homage.

Sage, supreme being, teacher of the supreme truth,
Skilled, as the rite requires, in providing the means
By which the world's gods and asuras pass into nirvāṇa:
To Śākyamuni, worthy of reverence, I bow down in homage.

Dwelling amid the sages with joy and compassion,
By supreme samādhi bewildering desire and fierce wrath,
Endowed with the finest speech from lips like the supreme bimba fruit:
To the holy Śākyamuni I bow down in homage.

Through holding the Sage's Dharma, disease and plague are carried off;
His two feet are adorned by the crown of Hari (Viṣṇu);
He ever abides in the samādhi of union:
To Śākyamuni, granter of the supreme, I bow down in homage.

Sage, holy lion, supreme lion among men,
For whom Brahmā, lord of the Sahā world, raised the supreme parasol,
To whom the chief, Vasundharā the Earth, bowed with hands of longing:
To Śākyamuni, worthy of homage, I bow down in homage.

The Sage is the Sugata, crown of all who bring peace,
Who has abandoned every fault, an ocean, a treasury of qualities,
Like a treasure that dispels all the sufferings of beings:
To Śākyamuni, who dispels evil, I bow down in homage.

The Sage, who with diligence accomplishes the aims of beings,
A protector like the lord of garuḍas, rightly guides
Beings who have fallen and brings them to bliss on the far shore:
To the Teacher Śākyamuni I bow down in homage.

This completes the Praise of Glorious Śākyamuni in Ten Verses.

This was composed by Śrī Vanaratnapāda. It was translated by the poor Gyatso Dé, who lives by the kindness of that great paṇḍita himself. The scribe was the intelligent Jampa Kunga.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

Several verses are compressed and admit other parsings. In verse two, "the mighty disputants of divergent minds" is an interpretive rendering of an obscure line. In verse three, the jewels worn in topknots are taken as describing the praising lords of the earth. In verse ten, the phrase rendered "a protector like the lord of garuḍas" might also be read as "protector of the lord of garuḍas." The translator's self-description "poor" renders the Sanskrit loanword dāridra, an expression of humility.
