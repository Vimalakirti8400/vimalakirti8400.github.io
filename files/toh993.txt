The Queen of Vidyās Called the Blessed Lady, the Noble Aṅgulī, Accomplished by Recitation (Pāṭhasiddha Bhagavatī Āryāṅgulī Nāma Vidyārājñī)

The Queen of Vidyās Called the Blessed Lady, the Noble Aṅgulī, Accomplished by Recitation, known in Tibetan as Bklags pas 'grub pa bcom ldan 'das ma 'phags ma sor mo can, is a short protective scripture centered on a powerful female deity of mantra named Aṅgulī. Set on a mountain called King of Fragrance, where a community of monks is staying, it presents the noble Avalokiteśvara teaching a set of fierce mantra words. These are said to remove the poison of nāga lords and snakes, and to subdue a wide array of harmful beings, including rākṣasas, bhūtas, piśācas, kumbhāṇḍas, and dangerous animals. As its title suggests, the text's power is meant to be realized simply through recitation, making it a practical incantation for protection against poison and malevolent forces. It is Toh 993 in the Kangyur, where it belongs among the dhāraṇī and vidyā texts, the class of scriptures devoted to protective spells and mantras.

The Queen of Vidyās Called the Blessed Lady, the Noble Aṅgulī, Accomplished by Recitation

Homage to the buddhas!

At that time, on a certain day, the monks were staying on the mountain called King of Fragrance. Noble Avalokiteśvara was also dwelling there at that time, and he said to me: "Son, listen to these words of mantra!

They remove the poison of the lords of the nāgas. They remove the poison of snakes. When their servants, the rākṣasas, the bhūtas, the piśācas, the powerful beings, the kumbhāṇḍas, the tiny creatures, the male phramen spirits and the female phramen spirits, the makaras, the tigers, the kumbhāṇḍas, the horned creatures and the fanged creatures, properly hear these great and fierce mantra words, Aṅgulī, then this chief of mantras crushes the vicious beings. Whatever mantra adept knows it completely does away with the viciousness of the gods."

oṃ namo bhagavati ārya aṃguli tadyathā oṃ
Whether it be a nāga or a nāginī, a snake or a female snake, a yakṣa or a yakṣiṇī, a bhūta or a female bhūta, a male phramen or a female phramen, one seized by a vicious being or one not seized by a vicious being:
pha pha pha hare hare hare reja re beśa be nādare sarba rere hara hara mara mara dhara dhara bidhara bidhara mara mara māraya māraya maṭa maṭa motaya motaya śīghraṃ śīghraṃ laghu laghu ānaya ānaya ṅhara dhara bandha bandha bandha ghurṇa ghurṇa ghurṇaya ghurṇaya
Rid of their viciousness the nāgas who dwell within eighty thousand yojanas! Bind all the nāgas! Slay them! Set them free!
oṃ hulu hulu hala hala hala biśaṃ

The Blessed Lady Aṅgulī, Queen of Accomplishment, declared:
takke ṭakke takkeni ṭakkeni śira śira candrakeśarohiṇe
You who clear away all obstructors, you of a thousand activities, accomplished Blessed Lady Aṅgulī,
tathābigata
worshipped by thirty assemblies of nāginīs,
jaya jaya siddhe
you who bring victory, you who wear a garland of human skulls,
hūṃ kṣa
you who remove poison for an instant, a moment, a short while, and until daytime,
asura debita
and you who destroy the power of the wicked, you who destroy obstructors: Mother of the gods, to you I bow and offer praise!
svāhā

These fierce mantra words should not be recited in front of a snake, for it would die, and its brain would split into a hundred pieces. If a snake hears these great and fierce mantra words, they burn it up entirely.

oṃ kili kili mahākili kālarūpini
Turn back all obstructors! Defeat the enemy, defeat the adversaries!
svāhā

This is the Aṅgulī Accomplished by Recitation, together with its heart mantra.

tadyathā oṃ dhune dhune dhuni dhuni satyatāṭṭahāsini dhudhuni bandha bandha
The many vajra dhāraṇīs declared: It causes the poison of Takṣaka to be cast out, and:
jaya jaya muja muja muñja muñja
Release it! You of swift force, called Lightning and Wind, great vetālī, Blessed Lady who came forth from the mouth of Śākyamuni!
muya muñu muñca svāhā

Here ends the Great Queen of Vidyās Called the Blessed Lady, the Noble Aṅgulī, Accomplished by Recitation.

The opening "at that time, on a certain day" renders an unusual introductory phrase that seems to stand in for the standard sūtra opening. The mountain name "King of Fragrance" is translated literally because no Sanskrit name could be identified with confidence. The phramen (male and female) are a class of sorcerous spirits known chiefly from Tibetan sources. No secure Sanskrit equivalent is given for them here. "The many vajra dhāraṇīs declared" could also be read as the name of a deity, something like "Vajra Many-Dhāraṇī". "Lightning" and "Wind" are kept literal, as in the drafts of this text.
