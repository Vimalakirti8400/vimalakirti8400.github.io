The Vidyā-Mantra of Attaining the Supreme (Agravidyāmantra)

The Vidyā-Mantra of Attaining the Supreme, known in Sanskrit as the Agravidyāmantra, is a brief ritual text centered on a single incantation addressed to the goddess Śaṅkhinī. After paying homage to her, it lists the mantra's benefits: completing the preliminary approach practice, increasing splendor, wealth, power, retinue, and lifespan, and pacifying enemies and obstructing forces. It instructs the practitioner to make offerings to the Three Jewels and, with a mind of loving-kindness, recite the mantra one hundred and eight times in a single session, and it then gives the mantra itself. This work is Toh 1036 in the Kangyur, where it belongs among the dhāraṇī and mantra texts, the collections of short incantations used for protection, prosperity, and spiritual accomplishment.

In the language of India: agravidyāmantra. In the language of Tibet: The Vidyā-Mantra of Attaining the Supreme.

Homage to the Blessed Lady, the goddess Śaṅkhinī.

It brings the approach practice to completion, increases splendor, increases wealth, increases power, increases one's retinue, increases life, pacifies enemies, and pacifies obstructors. Having presented offerings to the Three Jewels, with one's mind abiding in loving-kindness, one should recite it one hundred and eight times in a single session. It is as follows:

namaḥ sarva tathāgatānāṃ tadyathā oṃ śadkhini devi āgaccha āgaccha tiṣṣa dhane dhana jaye vrī vrī vṛddhri kari dhri dhri dhṛti karī nāna vi vidyā veśa vasta āyurdharaṇi yu yu āyuṣpālani tathāgatāsmrar bodhicitta matī laṃ vodara dehi me varana śadkhini svāhā

By this very virtue, may I see her in this very emanation body. May I attain patience with regard to emptiness. May I see the meaning of emptiness. May I accomplish the aim of emptiness.

By reciting this, all obstacles will be dispelled.

The Noble Attainment of the Supreme is complete.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The goddess's Tibetan name means "She Who Bears the Conch." It is given here as Śaṅkhinī, which matches the name invoked in the dhāraṇī.

The opening list of benefits has no stated subject. It is taken to describe the effects of the spell.

"Approach practice" renders bsnyen po, read as bsnyen pa, the approach or recitation phase of deity practice. That reading is not certain.

In the first aspiration the Tibetan states no object for "see." "Her," meaning the goddess, is supplied from context. The phrase could also be read as "may I see [it] as this very emanation body."
