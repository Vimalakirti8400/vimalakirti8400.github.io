Homage to all buddhas and bodhisattvas.

Thus did I hear. At one time, the Blessed One was dwelling in the Palace of the Sun and Moon, the Abode of Complete Victory, together with a great Saṅgha of monks and ninety-nine million billion hundred-thousand arhats.

At that time, the Blessed One spoke to the bodhisattva Heap of Complete Victory: "Heap of Complete Victory, to the north of this buddha-field, beyond a number of world-systems equal to the finest dust-particles of sixty Ganges rivers, there is a world-system called Manifest Joy. There the Tathāgata, arhat, and perfectly complete buddha named Dharma Eminence presently dwells, lives, and teaches the Dharma. Go there to behold that Tathāgata, to pay homage, to honor, and to make offerings to him."

Thereupon the bodhisattva Heap of Complete Victory went to that world-system and, at the feet of the Blessed One Dharma Eminence, paid homage with his head, circumambulated him seven times, and sat down to one side.

When the bodhisattva Heap of Complete Victory was thus seated to one side, the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Dharma Eminence, spoke to that bodhisattva and said: "Noble son, from where have you come here?"

Then the bodhisattva Heap of Complete Victory said nothing at all in reply to what the Blessed One had said, and sat in silence.

At that time, all those assembled in the retinue — gods, nāgas, yakṣas, asuras, garuḍas, kinnaras, great serpents, humans, and non-humans — thought to themselves: "Why does this bodhisattva Heap of Complete Victory not respond and sit in silence when addressed by the teacher of the three worlds?"

Then the Blessed One, gazing out in all ten directions with a look like that of a lion surveying his domain, perceived in his mind the doubt that had arisen among the entire assembly, and he smiled. As the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Dharma Eminence, smiled, rays of light with a radiance the color of gold illuminated the ten directions with many hundreds of thousands of beams. In that very instant the great earth trembled. From the ten directions, bodhisattvas bearing various forms, colors, and aspects, upon witnessing this great miraculous display of the Blessed One, all gathered together in that world-system. They bowed their heads at the feet of the Blessed One and, having established themselves through their respective accumulations of virtue, they touched their right knees to the ground and, facing in the direction of the Blessed One, joined their palms in reverence. Overcome with wonder and amazement, they smiled joyfully and addressed the Blessed One with these words:

"Blessed One, we have witnessed the many miraculous displays of light-rays. Blessed One, we have never before witnessed such a manifestation of brilliant light. Blessed One, this matter is excellent — and so, out of compassionate care for us, please declare to us the purpose of this smile that the Blessed One has made."

Then the bodhisattva Garland of Light addressed the Blessed One in verse:

You who are the supreme guide and most excellent of persons, compassionate one,
You do not smile without reason —
For me and for the world, O luminous compassionate one,
Declare the purpose of your smile and what you will do.

Then the Blessed One, the Tathāgata, whose complexion shone like the gold of the Jambu River and whose physical form blazed with splendor and radiance, sending forth hundreds of thousands of billions of rays of light — to him, shining thus, the bodhisattva Garland of Light spoke these words: "Blessed One, I have witnessed many miraculous displays, and never before have I witnessed such a manifestation of brilliant light-rays."

The Tathāgata then declared: "Garland of Light, this small display of light-rays from the Tathāgata that you have witnessed here in this direction — these signs and manifestations are not without cause, and they are not without something to be done. Garland of Light, therefore listen well and hold it in mind, for I shall explain it to you. Upon hearing the purpose for which I smiled, you, son of good family, must not be frightened, must not be startled, must not become alarmed, must not be bewildered, must not be doubtful. Why is that? The domain of the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, is inconceivable. Their aspirational prayers are inconceivable. Their miraculous transformations are inconceivable. Seeing these matters rightly, do not be doubtful.

"Garland of Light, did you see how the Tathāgata Śākyamuni sent the bodhisattva Mass of Victories here to my presence?"

He replied: "Blessed One, I saw it. Well-gone One, I saw it."

"Son of good family, when I asked this bodhisattva Mass of Victories, 'Where have you come from?' he remained entirely silent and said nothing at all. Son of good family, it was upon seeing this significance that I smiled here.

"When the Tathāgata asked that question, all those in the retinue wondered to themselves why this noble being had not answered the question. Son of good family, as to why this noble being did not answer the Tathāgata's question: he realized that among phenomena that have no coming and no going, the Blessed One was causing him to seek a coming that does not exist, by asking 'Where have you come from?' He further considered: since all phenomena are beyond expression, and if one penetrates that, then there is no delight in asking the Blessed One about the place of origination, 'Where have you come from?' Son of good family, in brief, through this he taught all phenomena — he was teaching the authentic Dharma.

"Son of good family, the bodhisattva Mass of Victories, having understood that when it comes to phenomena that are without letters, without words, beyond expression, free of letters, without signs, free of signs, without anything to be taken up, free of anything to be taken up, without coming, without seeing, without abiding, free of mind and mentation and consciousness, free of cause and condition, without words, without distinction, without views, without permanence, inexpressible in words, without convention — phenomena such that even in a single letter there is no such thing as the incorrect, no abiding in accumulation, no letter, no one to tame — knowing all of this, son of good family, he gave teachings in this teaching that has no teaching."

Then, by the power of the Blessed One, the bodhisattva Luminous Body addressed the Blessed One with these words: "Blessed One, if all phenomena are beyond being taught, then all those phenomena would have to be taught by a mute."

The Blessed One declared:

"Son of good family, it is just as you have said. It is not only mutes — all sentient beings teach the Dharma while not knowing it."

Then came the request: "Blessed One, please explain how it is that all sentient beings teach the Dharma without seeking to do so. Sugata, please explain this."

When this request had been made, the Blessed One spoke to the bodhisattva Luminous Body with these words: "Son of good family, consider this example. The blind cannot see forms even when the sun has risen, and they do not know what the light of the sun is like; yet when others show them what the sun's characteristics are, they become convinced, through the words of others, that it is the sun.

"Son of good family, in just the same way all phenomena are gathered inwardly, gathered into the basic space (dharmadhātu) — a space without dimension, without letters, free from letters — and though all phenomena are inexpressible, sound arises through the power of conditions.

"Son of good family, consider this further example. When an echo arises in dependence upon a mountain cliff, that echo of the mountain cliff is empty. Son of good family, in just the same way sounds of letters arise in dependence upon conditions, and those sounds of letters are empty.

"Son of good family, you should regard everything that any sentient being expresses as gathered within perfect individual knowledge of phenomena. Whatever is expressed in words should be regarded as the perfect individual knowledge of phenomena, and whatever is expressed in words should also be regarded as the perfect individual knowledge of meaning.

"Therefore, son of good family, you should regard all the slightest movements of any sentient being whatsoever as gathered within the four summaries of the Dharma. Elemental forces, not knowing, do not keep the ground still. And just as a sighted person is convinced of the very nature of the Dharma in the same way that the non-blind person understands — son of good family, one who wishes to thoroughly seek the meaning should seek it within their own mental stream. Son of good family, one who wishes to thoroughly seek awakening should seek it within the five appropriated aggregates.

"When the meaning of this enumeration of the Dharma was taught just as it is, the entire world-system of the great trichiliocosm trembled in six ways, and it was illuminated all over with a great brilliant light. The Blessed One, the Tathāgata, who is the Dharma's noble tongue, extended the organ of his tongue and covered the entire world-system of the great trichiliocosm with that tongue. From that tongue-organ there issued many hundreds of thousands of billions of rays of light, and those rays illuminated all the world-systems from the great hells and so forth all the way up to the summit of existence. Then the Blessed One withdrew that same tongue-organ and spoke to the entire assembled retinue:

'Friends, one who possesses such a tongue does not speak falsehood. Friends, examine whatever I have just said exactly as it was spoken, have faith in it accordingly, and place your trust in it accordingly.'

"Then those dwelling on the ten grounds, along with gods, nāgas, yakṣas, asuras, garuḍas (Skt. garuḍa), kinnaras (Skt. kinnara), great serpents (Skt. mahoraga), and the entire retinue of humans and non-humans — all rose from their seats, joined their palms together, and with one voice said these words: 'Blessed One, please speak the teaching of the true meaning. Sugata, please speak it. By the power of the Buddha we shall have no hesitation and no doubt.'

"The Blessed One then looked over the entire assembled retinue two and three times, and spoke these words: 'Sons of good family, for the benefit of the many and for the happiness of the many, let this be taught with the roar of a lion.'"

"Sons of good family, in the world-realm called Endurance, the Tathāgata, arhat, perfectly complete buddha known as Śākyamuni is present even now, living and dwelling, and he too teaches the Dharma. Sons of good family, that Tathāgata is none other than myself, the noble one of the Dharma, and in that world-realm of Endurance I teach the Dharma to sentient beings by means of many and various practices."

Then the entire assembled retinue, filled with wonder and amazement, offered their praise to the Blessed One, saying: "Excellent! Blessed One, how well you have roared the lion's roar to all sentient beings. Excellent! Excellent! Blessed One, those sentient beings who will hear this lion's roar of the Blessed One roared here in this assembly, and having heard it will come to trust in it — if they are not of inferior roots of virtue, how much more so those who hold it, retain it, recite it, teach it extensively and correctly to others, and make offerings to this sūtra with flowers, incense, garlands, perfumes, powders, garments, parasols, victory banners, and pennants! Any sentient being in whose ear this sūtra of the lion's roar is heard is one who has been blessed by all the buddhas."

Then the Blessed One bestowed his approval on those bodhisattvas: "Sons of good family, it is just as you have said. Excellent! Excellent! Anyone who, upon hearing this true lion's roar of the Tathāgata, offers the word 'Excellent' with a mind of pure faith — that son of good family or daughter of good family is not one of inferior roots of virtue. Such persons are embraced by me. The great bodhisattva Maitreya (loving-kindness) also acts as their patron. They carry my awakening upon their shoulders. Those sentient beings who have faith in this sūtra when the five degenerations have descended — these I have fully ripened, and in other lifetimes as well, the undefeated one will embrace them. The ocean of their births has been dried up. Māra has been defeated. The afflictions have been dispelled. The drum of the Dharma has been beaten resoundingly. The three states of existence have been thoroughly reversed. Victory over opponents has been secured. The host of afflictions has been vanquished.

"If some son of good family or daughter of good family were to serve and honor all the Tathāgatas with every kind of pleasant offering for as many aeons as there are atoms in ten hundred million trillion great trichiliocosms, and yet upon hearing this account of the Tathāgata's former miraculous deeds were not to place faith in it — that would be a great fault. By the word of the Tathāgata, that son of good family ought to pay homage to the Tathāgata by paying homage. And if any son or daughter of good family hears this teaching correctly set forth by the Tathāgata, and with faith and a thoroughly delighted mind offers the word 'Excellent' — the merit of this surpasses the merit of one who made offerings and rendered service to the Tathāgata by hundreds and thousands of times and more. Therefore, sons of good family, all those who have gone for refuge to me should write out and keep this sūtra."

Then the bodhisattvas Vikitaviṣṭhāpita, Prabhāmāla, Vidyutprabhā, Viśuddhalocana, Maitreya, Abhayaṃkara, Avalokiteśvara, Mahāsthāmaprāpta, Mañjuśrī, Vāgīśvara,

Sarvaśīvaraṇaviṣkambhin, Avalokiteśvara, Samantabhadra, and eighty-four hundred thousand billion other bodhisattvas spoke thus to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, in the final age, at the final time, when the five degeneracies (Skt. kaṣāya) have descended, we will propagate this sūtra widely. Blessed One, know that any sentient being who has faith in this sūtra has fully passed beyond sorrow. Blessed One, those sentient beings who have not generated roots of virtue will not come to hear this sūtra even once. As for the merits of those who enter into it broadly, even ten trillion aeons would not suffice to comprehend the limit of those merits."

Then the Blessed One granted his approval to those bodhisattvas with the word "Excellent," and said: "Sons of good family, excellent, excellent! Do so — hold what you have said as if it were a support for the Tathāgata's teaching."

When the Blessed One had spoken these words, the bodhisattva Vicitaviṣṭhāpita and all the assemblies gathered there — gods, humans, asuras, and gandharvas — rejoiced at what the Blessed One had proclaimed and offered him great praise.

The noble Mahāyāna sūtra called "The Proclamation of the Lion's Roar" is complete.

The name "ནམ་མཁའ་ལྡིང" refers to garuḍas, the great eagle-like beings of Indian cosmology, rendered here as garuḍas. "མིའམ་ཅི" are kinnaras, semi-divine beings, and "ལྟོ་འཕྱེ་ཆེན་པོ" are mahoragas, great serpents — the standard eightfold assembly of supernatural beings appears here. The verse by the bodhisattva Garland of Light follows in the Tibetan but is not included in this passage, so the translation ends at the point where that verse begins.  The epithet ཆོས་འཕགས — literally "Dharma-noble" or "Dharma-exalted" — applied here to the Tathāgata appears to function as an honorific descriptor of his golden complexion and the Jambu River comparison that follows; it is rendered implicitly through the surrounding description rather than as a separate epithet to avoid interrupting the flow.

The bodhisattva ལུས་འོད་གསལ, "Luminous Body" or "Body of Clear Light," is a new figure entering the dialogue here and has been rendered accordingly.  The phrase rendered "gathered within the four summaries of the Dharma" translates ཆོས་ཀྱི་སྡོམ་བཞིར་འདུས་པར, which could also be read as "the four collections of the Dharma" or "the four seals of the Dharma." The precise referent is somewhat ambiguous in this context; the translation uses "summaries" as a neutral reading.

The passage ཆོས་འཕགས་ཀྱི་ལྗགས་ཀྱི་དབང་པོ could mean "the tongue-organ of the noble Dharma" or function as an epithet; it is rendered as "who is the Dharma's noble tongue" to convey both the miraculous physical gesture and the implicit praise.  The world-realm name rendered "Endurance" is Skt. Sahā, the standard epithet for our world-system, meaning the world that must be endured. The Tibetan mi mjed is the conventional rendering.

The phrase translated "the noble one of the Dharma, myself" renders chos 'phags nga nyid, which is somewhat unusual; it may be read as "I am indeed the Tathāgata who is supreme in Dharma." The rendering given is the most natural doctrinal reading.

The sequence of bodhisattva names at the close resumes a list from the sūtra's frame narrative. Several names, such as Vikitaviṣṭhāpita (rnam par rgyal ba'i phung pa) and Vāgīśvara (spobs pa dpa' bo), are reconstructed from the Tibetan epithets rather than attested Sanskrit; this is noted for scholarly awareness but does not affect the translation.
