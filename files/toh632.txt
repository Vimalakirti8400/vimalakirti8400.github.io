The Noble Abridged Ritual Tantra of the Wrathful Vajra King (Ārya-vajra-krodha-rāja-kalpa-laghu-tantra-nāma)

Toh 632 in the Kangyur is a tantric scripture known in Sanskrit as the Ārya-vajra-krodha-rāja-kalpa-laghu-tantra and in Tibetan as the Phakpa Dorje Khrowö Gyalpö Tokpa Düpé Gyud, rendered in English as "The Noble Abridged Ritual Tantra of the Wrathful Vajra King." It belongs to the class of Buddhist tantric texts and concerns the wrathful vajra deity known as the Wrathful Vajra King, presenting an abridged or condensed ritual tantra associated with this powerful figure. The text opens in the traditional sūtra style, with the Buddha residing in a magnificent celestial palace in the Heaven of Mastery over Others' Emanations, situated upon a vajra essence maṇḍala, suggesting a revelatory setting appropriate to tantric scripture. As part of the Kangyur, it is counted among the translated word of the Buddha and is placed within the broader collection of tantric texts preserved in the Tibetan Buddhist canon.

In the language of India: Ārya-vajra-krodha-rāja-kalpa-laghu-tantra-nāma. In the language of Tibet: The Noble Abridged Ritual Tantra of the Wrathful Vajra King.

Homage to the glorious Mañjuśrī.

Thus did I hear. At one time, the Blessed One dwelled upon the vajra essence maṇḍala of the wind circle of the sky in the Heaven of Mastery over Others' Emanations (Paranirmitavaśavartin), consecrated by blessing, at the center of a palace of stacked stories, exceedingly tall, extending for a hundred thousand leagues, adorned with divine, immeasurable mansions numbering eight hundred million, themselves constructed from the seven precious substances — with thrones of many jewels, lotus-seats and lion-supports; with reliquary shrines of multi-faceted precious stone at the crowning pinnacles, adorned with lotuses, wheels, and parasols raised on high, from which silken pennants streamed and billowed; with shadows cast upon the ground below, whereupon the assemblies of bodhisattvas descended from their divine vehicles and entered within. Beautiful with manifold ornaments: varied bells, victory banners, garlands of jeweled flowers, nets, half-nets, fly whisks, half-moons, half-vajras, hanging festoons and tassels, and much else besides; pervaded by porticos, vestibules, door panels, jeweled mirrors, parasols, and yet more; beautified by many layered tiers of precious stones — amid all of this, the Blessed One dwelled together with the great maṇḍala of his retinue.

That assembly was as follows. The gathering of bodhisattvas included noble Avalokiteśvara, Mañjuśrī, Samantabhadra, Maitreya, Sarvanīvaraṇaviṣkambhin, Śāntamati, Vicitravikīrṇa, Gaganalocanadhara, Ākāśagarbha, Rūpaśuddha, Kṣitigarbha, Vajrapāṇi, and others — seventy million great bodhisattvas (mahāsattvas) in all. Śāriputra, Maudgalyāyana, Aśvajit, Kāśyapa, and others — forty million śrāvakas. The great and famed Śakra (Indra), Brahmā, Viṣṇu, Maheśvara, Kubera, Yama, Nandīśvara, and others — three hundred classes of deities. Together with this multifaceted retinue of bodhisattvas, śrāvakas, and world-protectors, the Blessed One generated a great, majestic display in harmony with the diverse intentions and aspirations of those present, performing many miraculous displays and emanating many rays of light, and thus remained in their midst.

Then the Blessed One, gazing upon the retinue with a gentle smile, extended the faculty of his tongue, and spoke to the great bodhisattva Vajrapāṇi:

Through the gateway of letters, names, words, and speech,
blessed by the Dharma of mantra-form —
whether a mere syllable, a half or a whole,
direct perception and inference, long and short,
indicated by the marks of vowels and consonants —
I, who embody the ten grounds, the ten perfections,
the ten masteries, the ten aims, the ten aspects,
the four fearlessnesses and the unshared qualities,
loving-kindness and great compassion:
through the mantra of a single vowel,
may all manner of suffering be perfectly stilled,
may captivating wealth and lordship be granted,
may many forms of might and power be produced,
may stable abiding be attained, may definitive meaning be attained,
and may the supreme vajra awakening mind be aroused.

In brief, so that the minds of whoever desires whatever
may be fulfilled according to their wish —
Sharpen the unsurpassed vajra,
Proclaim the unsurpassed sound of Dharma,
Turn the unsurpassed wheel,
Take up the unsurpassed sword,
Grasp the unsurpassed lotus,
Make the unsurpassed jewel shine bright.
Having elaborated in the manner of a maṇḍala,
then condense it in the manner of a reliquary shrine.
Gods, nāgas, yakṣas, gandharvas (scent-eaters),
asuras, garuḍas, kiṃnaras,
rākṣasas, flesh-eaters, the hosts of forgetfulness —

Transform these many emanations
into something that strikes terror even in the terrifying.
The moment that supreme voice is heard,
Vajrapāṇi, the greatly powerful,
joyfully joins his palms together
and makes this supplication in pleasing words:

From gold and the five precious substances
arises the supremely excellent jewel-mountain,
which is the support and dwelling place of all beings
and is held as their true sign and symbol.
By fully purifying consciousness
and the four aggregates and the four elements,
those who know mantras, having taken up
the deities, mudrās, and enlightened activities,
will come to possess the sequential path of gnosis
of discipline and awakening, distinguished and supreme.

Then the Blessed One, the great Sage,
supreme among the most excellent, the lord of omniscient gnosis,
sent forth from his body many rays of light,
brilliant and radiant in various hues.
When those rays pervaded all the fields
adorned with their foundations, their cores, and their flowers,
the earth trembled in six ways,
the world-protectors and the hosts of māras
all fell into a swoon without exception,
the śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas trembled with fear,
and the assembled hosts of wise bodhisattvas
were utterly filled with awe.

At that moment, from the ten directions' fields,
from the hair pore-reliquaries (Skt. ūrṇā-koṣas) of all the buddhas,
the hosts of the great wrathful ones arose,
drawn by the tip of the light-ray,
and gathered here in this maṇḍala at the center of the field.
The assembled mass of light rays then converged
and dissolved into the auspicious vase.
At that moment, from the blessing of that light,
this sound rang out through the sky:

"All the sons of the Victors who have arisen
from the dharmadhātu — come every one
to where the Sage, the perfected buddha, protector of the Śākyas,
dwells in his great splendor.
Having heard the supreme, stainless, sacred Dharma
and the virtuous way of practice in secret mantra,
may you reach the unconditioned awakening
and attain the Protector of the World."

Then the entire assembly of Vajrapāṇi and his retinue,
the whole of the assembled maṇḍala,
cupped their hands, filled them with flowers,
scattered them in offering, made prostrations, and circumambulated.
With clear and reverent manner,
kneeling with palms joined, they made this supplication:

"O supreme great gnosis that does not abide at any extreme,
O chief source from which an ocean of qualities arises,
O great Sage fully adorned with all defining marks,
who has relinquished all that bears no mark —
what is that stainless virtue?
What is the mode of practice in secret mantra?
What is that unconditioned awakening?
How does one become the Protector of the World?
As the supreme medicine for us
and in your great compassion for all beings,
O Protector, O Blessed One, O great Sage,
please explain this again and again."

Then the perfectly and fully awakened one,
supreme among the two-legged, declared:
"O Vajradhara, lord of secrets,
you who are endowed with the eye of great wisdom,
in order to strive for the benefit of self and other —
listen well and hold it in your mind.
The way of being perfectly and truly at the ultimate —
I shall now explain this.

Taking life, taking what is not given, lying, sexual misconduct,
harsh speech, divisiveness, idle chatter,
covetous intent, harmful intent, wrong view, and intoxicants,
foul-smelling and unclean food and drink,
the flesh and blood of living creatures, and wrong livelihood —
the guru, the Saṅgha, stūpas, and so forth,
and in particular the possessions and implements of worthy recipients,
stealing from those who are lowly and impoverished,
selling texts, exploiting the Dharma for profit,
weapons such as swords, poisons, and the like —
seeing these as you would a pit of fire, abandon them,
and practice every form of whiteness of conduct.

Writing, making offerings and gifts,
listening, retaining, reading,
making offerings, reciting,
and with the mind reflecting and meditating upon these —
strive in the ten white paths for a long time
with ability, without interruption.

The awakening mind that lacks all fixation
is pure and stainless as crystal.
By practicing this supreme path, one severs
the mass of suffering swiftly — it is virtue.

The selflessness of phenomena, equality,
and the original primordial purity of one's own mind,
the absence of cause, result, and conditioning factors,
and freedom from defiled happiness —
from these arise, without exception,
all the qualities that are wings of awakening."

The conduct of the selflessness of phenomena is this: by making the wisdom of the dharmadhātu manifest, the unmixed phenomena are perfectly and clearly present — this is the unconditioned awakening, the very ground and body of all phenomena.

In the desire realm and the other realms, one engages in enjoyment, delighting thoroughly in their qualities. This is the secret, hidden from buddhas and non-Buddhists alike. For immeasurable, inconceivable eons, the vast, profound, and extensive trainings difficult to practice — mantras, mudrās, and meditative absorptions dwelling in body, speech, and mind — because they protect in an inseparable manner, all the buddhas declare this to be mantra.

The form endowed with the major and minor marks, perfect in all qualities, dwelling in the two truths — this is the protector of the bodhisattvas. For some it appears as the form of a wheel-turning king, for some as the form of a great ascetic, for some as the form of a rākṣasa or rākṣasī, for some as the form of a virtuous being. Turning the wheel of Dharma, gazing like a lion, and performing many diverse acts — for the two-footed ones, asuras, and the rest of the two divisions and so on, one has become the protector of all beings.

Those conceived by a mind of a Lord who is permanent, similar, and without certainty — such are the protectors of ordinary worldly people. Distinguished by form, appearance, and color, varied in land, time, and manifestation, they are worthy of worship beyond number.

The moment the Sage had spoken, Vajrapāṇi rose up, made prostrations and circumambulation, and then, seated before him, spoke these words: "O Blessed One, foremost guide, what exactly is what is called mantra? What is the nature of what is called mudrā? How is meditative absorption explained? How do they dwell in body, speech, and mind? How does one protect in an inseparable manner? For the sake of benefit to myself and out of love for others, please explain."

Then the foremost Victor, supreme among victors, extended the power of his tongue, and with the melodious voice of the great Brahmā, in order to exhaust conceptual thought, declared this:

"Essence-mantra, proximate essence, and root mantra — these are the activity mantras. Essence is what is to be recited, and seed. Proximate essence is known as supreme wisdom, and also as the secret essence. Activity mantras are two: the activity mantra that stabilizes one's own nature, and that which accomplishes the assemblage of preparatory practices.

Mudrā is the movement of the formations of the hands, and the change of stance of the feet. Because it symbolizes, seals, and renders unchangeable, it is explained as mudrā.

When one gathers the mind to single-pointedness, and through this brings superior phenomena down upon the mind — that is called meditative absorption.

Thus, relying upon these, as one's former condition is wholly transformed, this body — like a fine-winged bird freed from its egg — soars greatly into the sky. In all the buddha-fields of the Victors, hearing the true Dharma from the Victors, one then expounds it to all the sons of the Victors. And through a knowledge that cognizes in a single instant, knowing objects filling every direction without remainder — this abides in body, speech, and mind.

When mind and mentation merge, wisdom arises in a blissful manner. This is what all the buddhas of the three times have declared: protect in an inseparable manner."

This concludes the first chapter, the chapter setting forth the meaning of superior abiding, extracted from the tantra known as the Compendium of Awareness of the noble Vajrakrodharāja, King of the Wrathful Vajra.

In order to tame great obstructing forces difficult to subdue through peaceful means, and to tame those who lead beings astray, having understood the distinctions of their minds from within the heart, and in order to overpower those by assuming the same form — remaining on the conceptual-thought-free path of space, the form that tames those difficult to tame — the fully awakened one, supremely noble, wrathful, of great power and glory, son of the sons of all the buddhas: to that one whose name is famed as Immovable, victors and sons of victors here constantly make prostrations. Brahmā and all the gods and every embodied being constantly make offerings to that one.

Performed by the victors, sustained by the sons of the victors, the fulfillment of aspirations, inconceivable —

Having perfected gnosis, one attains the ground of the Immovable;
by that attainment one is called Immovable.
A hero who vanquishes adversarial opponents —
the wrathful one is the one who cuts them down completely.
Through the unfailing true words of mantra,
the hosts of māras and śrāvakas cannot move him.
Victorious through wrath, free of conceptual thought, toward māra —
through miraculous power he is Immovable, beyond all motion.

The supreme essence of suchness,
the body of the Victor arisen from emptiness:
abiding in the very nature of dharmadhātu,
this emanation purifies the realms of conditioned existence.

Like space, stainless and free of conceptual elaboration,
all the buddhas of the three times arise from it.
In the nature of space, free of elaboration,
what is called secret mantra is only a conventional designation —
yet it transcends the domain of speech.
The Protector taught it for the sake of understanding through convention.

Mind-free, nature-free, emptiness —
one meditates upon it with the mind as though upon an illusion.
The goals one desires — beautifully equal, without substantiality —
are granted by the proclamation of this lord of secret mantra.

The non-Buddhists hold to self, life-force, person,
a primal cause, and a supreme being pervading all existence —
deluded and arrogant, clinging to cyclic existence as permanent,
bound by the snare of error, they generate suffering.

There is no self, no life-force, no sentient being,
no agent, no primal cause,
no all-pervading ground, no substantial thing,
no perfect inherent nature in matter.

In order to cut the snare of erroneous views that bind to existence,
one meditates on forms of various kinds, without substantiality.
Through release from the substantial, one attains the level of emptiness,
cultivating familiarity with that distinction between outer and inner.

Meditate on the head as a water-bubble, as though an illusion.
Arising from emptiness through meditative absorption,
freed from the binding of conceptual thoughts and misdeeds,
one attains the level of deathless gnosis.

No aggregates, no elements, no sense bases,
no all-base consciousness, and no person,
no apprehended and apprehender, no bondage —
so the Victors constantly teach.

Without anything, parted from everything, abandoning everything:
if with diligence one constantly meditates on emptiness, bodhicitta, and the likeness of an illusion,
the sprout of the awakening mind will arise.

Like a wish-fulfilling jewel, of great miraculous power,
a body like space, unstained and unobstructed —
a bodhisattva who engages in mantra practice
thereby attains meditation upon the awakening mind.

What is proclaimed as wondrous, and upon hearing it straightaway,
one offers praise to the chief of the wrathful ones:

Constantly pure and stainless,
the stream of conceptual thought cut off, free of all mental proliferation —
to the great hero who has entered beyond examination and thought,
I bow down and offer praise.

You, for whom all phenomena are unmoved,
who has perfectly refined every domain of activity —
the omniscient one, ever unmoved,
whose nature is free of elaboration: to you I bow down.

For the sake of benefiting the many various sentient beings,
various forms of conduct and activity have been taught.
The paths of purity arise perfectly and completely
from the pure mind: to that I bow down and offer praise.

Like clear sky, pure and parted from clouds,
having abandoned the finest particle of darkness —
you, the Protector, whose body is like a crystal sphere,
free of all conceptual proliferations: to you I bow down and offer praise.

Like space free of elaboration,
utterly parted from all substantial things —
the aggregates, elements, and sense bases utterly purified:
to the ocean of gnosis I bow down and offer praise.

Like a rainbow appearing in manifold colors in the sky,
you, the chief, behold beings with a pure mind.
The great hero arisen to liberate all beings from the lower realms,
arrayed in great armor: to you I bow down and offer praise.

The essence arisen from the nature of reality,
the single syllable of great splendor —
dispelling the dense darkness upon the ground of beings' suffering:
to that I bow down and offer praise.

By this secret teaching of the supreme mantra's lord,
it is said that the sons of the Victor increase Dharma and give rise to wisdom,
and swiftly approach the unsurpassable level.
To you, the lord, I bow down and offer praise.

Liberated from karma and birth — from all that is conditioned and subject to change —
the Victor, arisen as a self-cause without any cause:
to you, supreme lord, I bow down and offer praise.

With the flame of gnosis, without substantiality,
having burned all the mountains of conditioned phenomena,
victorious in the single nature of dharmadhātu —
to the unsurpassable body I bow down and offer praise.

Having understood the meaning of the praise, he taught the ritual for the benefit of beings.

From the wrong practice of secret mantra and from habituating oneself wrongly to miserliness, from mental ideation overpowered by desire, mantra practitioners are reborn in the realm of hungry ghosts.

At the first stage, such a being interrupts offerings of butter lamps, flowers, food, torma cakes, garlands, and the like — the substances and implements of worship, human bodies, and food. It causes harm and gives rise to unpleasant illness, and brings harm to the crops.

At the second stage, having become a fierce yakṣa, it roams about craving flesh, blood, and alcohol; it interrupts hearing, reflection, and practice; it gives rise to illness, steals life-force, and produces wrong views.

In order to vanquish the spirit-possession that bears the knowledge mantra, the practitioner should exert himself and recite the mantra. By the suppression-rite, with the mantra's power generated through torma, fire offering, mudrā, and mantra — like a forest fire — they are vanquished without obstacle.

The gods are what stabilize those, and the maṇḍala diagrams are what illuminate them. What purifies all of them utterly — the supreme, peaceful bodhicitta — to the churning mass of wrong-conceptualized saṃsāra is like the blade of a sharp sword.

This is the second chapter, the chapter on suchness, from the tantra known as The Condensed Points of the Noble Vajra Wrathful King.

Now the mudrās are as follows.

Right hand: extend the middle finger flat, then straighten it. Press the ring finger and little finger down with the thumb. Draw force inward, release in the gesture of drawing out, and cast it upward through space. The sword mudrā destroys the hosts of māras.

Left hand: make a fist, raise the index finger, and rotate it — this is the lasso mudrā. Bring the middle finger and little finger together separately. Bend the ring finger inward. Press the thumb flat against the side of the middle finger. Placed at the heart center: the armor mudrā. Clasp the fingers in a fist, closed inward. Turn the two thumbs slightly: the skull mudrā.

Place the thumb of the left fist inside the right fist, then raise the right thumb upright — this is the hammer mudrā. Strike as if pounding in the ten directions.

Right fist: raise the middle finger. Place this mudrā at the crown of the head — the crown mudrā. Left fist: raise the middle finger. Always brandish this — the command-seal mudrā.

Press both fists flat together at the belly — this is the stone mudrā. Place the right fist at the heart center — this is the heart-adornment mudrā.

Extend both hands with fingers spread out individually — this is the mudrā of binding the ten directions.

Press the palms together with both middle fingers placed inside — this is described as the incense mudrā.

Fists back to back, middle finger and thumb touching, ring finger hooking around — this is the torma-offering mudrā.

Cup the hollow of the palms like a sheath and present them — this is the incense-offering mudrā.

Both hands separately, middle and ring fingers bent, thumbs pressing down, placed, extended — the flower mudrā. Palms joined, middle and ring fingers bent, little finger and index finger extended — the food mudrā.

Sit in the cross-legged posture, hold the grip, bend both thumbs inward, cross and interlock the fingers, then fan outward — this is the mudrā for inviting the chief hero.

Press the palms together, bend the fingers, press with the thumbs — this is the mudrā for summoning the assembly of bodhisattvas.

Form the grip, press the little fingers flat and extend them, then bend each of the remaining eight fingers separately — this is the mudrā for summoning without exception all śrāvakas, pratyekabuddhas, and those with miraculous powers.

Place the right fist on top of the left, extend the two index fingers separately and curve them slightly — this is the mudrā for summoning male and female messengers.

Form fists with the backs outward, then extend the middle fingers separately and straighten them; bring the two thumbs slightly together and draw inward — this is the mudrā for summoning worldly beings. Do the same but with the right hand rather than the left — this is the mudrā for summoning ascetics and sages who hold vows. Raise the middle finger of the left fist — this is the mudrā for summoning the sisters of accomplishment (the ḍākinīs of siddhi).

Extend the right hand flat and open; press the three fingers with the thumb and fan them — this will summon the seven mothers and all the mātṛkās at the time of ritual activity.

Raise the knees alternately in a straight posture and press the soles of the feet to the knees; join the palms with flowers at the crown of the head, fan them toward that direction, and gaze with a piercing stare. With this circular posture, all these subdue the hosts of obstructing spirits and protect the yogin.

Extend the right hand upward; point the index finger of the left hand at the heart; press the two feet together and strike the heel; recite Hūṃ aloud in a fierce voice and gaze forward. In the crescent-moon posture that shakes the three levels of the earth, and in the posture with the left leg extended, recite Ha Ha, seal the step-mudrā, cross the hands upon one another, and drive them against the obstructing spirits. Then open the wheel-mudrā.

In the posture with the right leg extended, thrust the vajra-joined palms upward into space; with wrathful furrowed brows, press the lips, and extend each pair of limbs like fire. In the posture of even feet with the sound of Hūṃ, the mudrā of the two index fingers is the seal. From the posture of both feet placed evenly, turn the right around the left at the navel; to the front with the right, to the rear with the left — pressing three and three at the joints is the triple-point, and that is the triple-point mudrā.

Extend the hand upward and raise the club; drive it against the obstructing spirits and gaze with a piercing stare. Strike with the mallet on the right while reciting Hūṃ; point the index finger of the left hand at the heart. Both together, spread wide — perform the wheel. Face west, feet placed evenly; right to the front, rear and left direction — with the left hand press the three obstructing spirits. In sequence: the mudrā of fire, the sword, the index-finger snare, binding in all four directions; recite Ha Hūṃ loudly — the mudrā of overpowering majesty.

Embrace both knees and turn both fists; pressed with a line, this subdues. To expel the obstructing spirits, gaze with a piercing stare. Cut off in the single-pointed vajra posture. Rotate the right and left feet alternately: south and north with the left, east and west with the right — extend and strike hard. From behind: straight on, the mallet, the vajra, and the lasso-mudrā — perform these in the four directions. With the wheel posture, the mudrā, and the dance posture, even the guardians of the directions are terrified — how much more so the hosts of elemental spirits! All are brought under control.

Now the ritual procedure for a supreme painted cloth image shall be explained.

For one who desires a painted image, first obtain good, clean cloth appropriate for the purpose. Wash it, protect it with mantras, and dye it saffron yellow.

The painter must be of good lineage, be calm and disciplined, have heard the Dharma, be skilled in drawing, meticulous in work, have few desires, a steady mind, and be free of anger. Apply to the cloth a sizing of lac-resin, bark extract, and alum solution.

For the colors: for green, use bronze-flower and malachite; for upper-yellow, use orpiment; for red pigment, use lapis-red, vermilion shell, and red ochre; for blue, use indigo, blue-bark, and gypsum blue; for black, use soot and añjana; for white, use śvetā chalk, which renders white. Prepare the ground of the cloth with sizing and priming. Write with the brush — finest, medium, or coarse — and choose an auspicious day: a victory day, a Saturday, a square-constellation day, a full-moon day, a sixth-lunar-day, or a day on the divine boundary. Then what is to be correctly explained at greater length:

For the virtuous painted cloth image of the Sage, at the center, beside the supreme tree of awakening, is the form of a wheel-turning king arrayed in gold, wearing saffron and adorned with the mudrā of teaching the Dharma. On either side stand two kings of beautiful lotus form, their hands cupped and filled with flowers. To the right stands the white lord of the world, robed in saffron, with palms joined in reverence.

At the lord's feet is the Immovable One with three eyes, blazing in wrath, adorned with nāgas, his upper body wrapped in elephant hide, his lower wrapped in tiger skin, bearing precious ornaments and a sacred cord, enthroned upon a red stone seat. In his hands he holds a sword and a lasso, and he stands with his left arm slightly extended.

To the left side of the principal figure stands Vajrapāṇi, blue and saffron-clad, holding a vajra and a lasso. Beneath him is the sādhaka, kneeling and holding an incense house. At the feet of the principal figure is the Great Wrathful One — the black one who stabilizes the three realms — with both hands raised upon a jeweled throne.

The principal figure is of varied lotus form. For the purposes of worldly lordship and for the wrathful, for Vajrapāṇi and the blue of the sādhaka — the fifth limb, a single trunk — two beautiful nāga maidens are depicted embraced in his arms, cradling them close.

This is for the purpose of peace, increase, and power.

Then the correctly explained painted image: at the center is Śākyamuni, to the right is the noble Mañjuśrī, and to the left is Vajrapāṇi.

For the cloth image intended for close practice: extremely fearsome and massive in form, with one face and four arms, adorned with nāgas, blood dripping from the mouth, tongue rolling, fangs bared, uttering the great Hrīḥ sound, with three eyes blazing like the rays of the newly risen sun, his saffron below like dark rain clouds, his body garlanded with a fresh-skull necklace, his head adorned with a crown of four skulls, on the left a jeweled side-lock hanging down. With the blazing light of the fire of the final aeon, he burns the assembled obstructing spirits of the directions.

His first right hand holds a vajra, his second holds a sword blazing with light. His first left hand holds a vajra-lasso, his second extends the threatening finger. Enthroned upon a jeweled stone seat, he presses down the king of obstructers, and abides in the dance posture of the left leg extended. Before him paint the peacemaking sādhaka holding the incense house. Make continuous offerings of whatever is available.

Now the painted cloth image for accomplishing fierce activity shall be correctly explained.

On a charnel-ground cloth, the Immovable One — dark in color, with three eyes gazing sideways, wearing saffron robes, with wrathful furrowed brows, holding a sword and a lasso. Once the image is painted, open its eyes with one's own blood and then make supplication.

For this image, even a vidyādhara need not speak of accomplishing fierce rites. Or alternatively, for the purpose of varied activities, depicted with blue complexion, adorned with elephant hide, in the posture of extension and contraction, holding a vajra and bell, seated upon a lotus moon — paint this image.

For the painted cloth image used in routing armies: the wrathful one of golden color, the Immovable One, his form fearsome with fangs bared, adorned with a garland of blazing flames, with four faces and four arms —

holding a sword and lasso in the first pair of hands, with a jewel and threatening gesture below, four feet pressing upon the hearts of four māras. Following this, the painted cloth image for the accomplishment of a vidyādhara is now correctly described.

The Immovable One, wrathful, with a single face, of fair and glowing complexion, the color of a zarma flower, bearing a sword, lasso, and threatening gesture, the right knee raised and the left set just slightly into the ground — in the sky above, two divine sons fan him with relief, a form delightful to behold. To his right, paint Vairocana; to his left, Amitābha. Before him, paint the practitioner as well.

Then make offerings to all the vidyā consorts, strew flowers, and upon opening the eyes of the image, present food. Consecrate it with the auspicious-connection mantra. After that, set down the aspiration:

Om. The merit I have gathered, immeasurable and beyond reckoning — by its power, may all beings without exception attain the unsurpassed state. May whatever hell-beings, animals, and hungry ghosts there are in countless numbers — by the power of my merit — all come to possess happiness. May whatever gods and humans suffer poverty, sickness, famine, harm from enemies, and the like — by the power of my merit — be freed from all of that. As many as are all sentient beings, that many are the oceans of suffering of every kind; may all of those be stilled, and may not one of them know suffering. May all sentient beings who engage in non-virtue of body, speech, and mind be separated from it, come to engage in virtue in abundance, and all attain the state of happiness.

Then, with all the sons of the Victors assembled in prostration, taking in hand incense, flowers, and all the other articles of offering, in order to distinguish the upper and lower seats, recite these words inviting the Sugatas:

Om. Blessed Ones who are Sugatas, you who actually dwell as protectors — for the sake of compassion toward me once more, you who have power over the Victors and their sons — go forth to accomplish your aims, and out of compassion, come again.

Then, so as not to harm the accomplishment and the samaya, do not touch with the hand the torma, the flowers, the incense, the maṇḍala ornaments, and the colored powders. Instead, using a cloth whisk and the like, sweep them up comfortably, recite the mantra of unborn and unceasing reality, and cast everything into a river. Then cook rice, milk, meat, sugar, and butter together, and together with the rice-dish, hold a feast for the Saṅgha and for brahmins so as to generate joy.

Thereafter, keep the painted image in a secret place and worship it there. In particular, worship it on the eighth day of each month. Present offerings to the lama. Always please every mantra-holder without exception and do not cause them displeasure. Turn away from sentient beings who lack faith. Generate certainty in the Three Jewels, and abandon doubt and laziness.

To attend to one's own aggregates: bathe and keep oneself clean; wear unsoiled, spotless garments. Eat foods such as rice — those that are pure and without impurity — in small measure, drinking likewise. Eating too much produces sleep, laziness, and bad smell. Rise at the very first light of dawn and strive until late into the night. When sleeping at the middle of the night, lie down with the intention to benefit beings.

Then, the sequence of deity meditation to be correctly explained: having made one's own form empty, from the garland of letters of the syllable circle, as sound resonates, the great vidyā appears like the reflection of the moon. Abiding therein are the vajra and so forth — these are precisely one's own mudrā. From that arises the supreme body, just as a rainbow appears, perfectly endowed with the major marks, minor marks, radiance, and light.

Namaḥ sarvatathāgatebhyaḥ, saparivarākebhyaḥ, sarvathā, oṃ sarvasaṃskārapariśuddhe, dharmate gaganasamudgate svabhāvapariśuddhe, mahānayaparivāre svāhā.

Having directed the mind to one's own body,
all wrongdoing is utterly pacified.
Having arisen perfectly in excellent conduct,
dwelling in mind that has become knowing and aware,

who has not generated the awakening mind (bodhicitta),

The bodhisattva without beginning or end,

A — the vajra lasso, full of compassion:
the trunks of affliction and cyclic existence —
having bound them with the lasso of skillful means,

Oṃ sarva-tathāgata-vajra-pāśena-abhiṣiñca hūṃ.

One who has not obtained empowerment or permission,
who does not possess the samaya vows,
and who does not please the guru —
their practice yields no fruit, and they proceed to the lower realms.

Whoever holds wrong views and harms the guru
is certain to proceed to the hell realms.
One who transgresses these in this life
will perpetually be afflicted by disease, plague,
enemies, foes, and famine.

The maṇḍala ground should be delightful:
a riverbank, a lakeside, a mountain valley,
a grove of fruit trees and flowers,

the southern gate to Vajra.

"Come, son" — for you are a vessel

Through supreme meditation on the unmoving Protector,
the source of buddhas of the three times,
incomparable primordial wisdom is obtained.
Even the Protector, Śākyamuni — the Lion of the Śākyas —
through this unrivaled practice of secret mantra
heroically vanquished the armies of Māra,
utterly terrible, with their great hosts.
To tame the world, he turned the wheel of Dharma
over all phenomena, and then passed into nirvāṇa.

So that you likewise may attain omniscience,
son, apply yourself to this with your intelligence.

Like the flower of the udumbara,
appearing only once in a hundred aeons,
or perhaps not appearing at all —
the Protector, the great Guardian of the World,
is exceedingly difficult to encounter.
And even when encountered,
the arising of the manner of a protector of secret mantra
is supremely rare — a thing of wonder.

From the terrifying great ocean of saṃsāra,
with its utterly unbearable suffering,
nothing on this earth liberates swiftly like secret mantra —
it is like a wish-fulfilling jewel.

Abandoning the fear of old age, sickness, and death;
finding in companions the fulfillment of harmonious desires;
freedom from famine, enemies, and robbers, and bliss;
the constant enjoyment of food, drink, clothing, ornaments, and wealth;
the attainment of happiness, prosperity, and abundance;
and swift awakening — for one who desires buddhahood,
strive earnestly and swiftly in the methods of secret mantra.

Just as the protectors of the three times
have determined upon awakening without fail —
that unsurpassed awakening mind, the supreme one,
I shall now generate.

The three ethical codes — the moral discipline of vows,
the moral discipline of gathering virtuous qualities,
and the moral discipline of acting for the benefit of sentient beings —
from this day forth, hold these steadfastly, supreme among all.

Generate an elevated mind toward all beings,
and present offerings to the lama with a request.
Like a wish-fulfilling jewel, the unsurpassed form —
the peaceful state of buddhahood — shall be attained.

Taking life, stealing, lying, unchaste conduct,
drinking intoxicants, singing and dancing,
wearing ornaments and perfumes, sleeping on high and great beds,
and eating in the afternoon —
on the eighth, eleventh, and fifteenth days,
and especially throughout the open sky of empty moments,
leisures and endowments are supremely rare,
the Buddha's teaching is supremely rare.
Having taken the vow, guard it without distraction,
as you would guard your very life.

Always take refuge in the Three Jewels.
Having obtained a body endowed with the happiness of gods and humans,
the states of hell-beings, animals, and hungry ghosts —
once obtained, supremely hard to escape —
never attain those again.

I, the hero who wields the sword,
who practices the secret wheel,
shall reduce to dust and fine powder
those malicious ones who transgress the command.

Now, what is to be explained next is this: the chief among minor karmic applications is the rite of the planetary lords. It dispels harm and generates benefit.

In the center of the maṇḍala smeared with the five kinds of cow-substances, place a lotus of many colors in a double tier. In the center is the buddha; likewise in the south, the Dharma — a white vajra-dharma form; in the east, the Saṅgha — green; in the west, Īśvara (the Lord of the World) — red; in the north, Vajrapāṇi; in the northeast, Mañjuśrī — yellow; in the southeast, the face of Yamāntaka, the destroyer of death, is black. He holds a staff and rides a buffalo. In the direction of the immovable protector stands the direction of the rākṣasas. In the direction of wind stands Hayagrīva. The Three Jewels display the Dharma-teaching gesture; the bodhisattvas and wrathful ones bear their own emblems. All are seated upon precious thrones.

Behind these, the planetary lords: in the east, the Sun, white-yellow, rides a horse and holds a lotus; in the west, the Moon, white, holds a white lotus flower and rides a goose; in the south, Mars of red color with red eyes, sits upon a sphere and holds a torch; his hands are the color of gold and he holds a blue lotus; in the north, Mercury, seated on a lotus, is blue in color and holds a book; in the southeast, he rides a nāga — Rāhu, white, holds a wooden staff; in the southwest, seated on a golden throne; in the northeast, Saturn, dark in color, stands above Brahmā and holds a staff; in the northwest, Ketu — dark and smoky, upon a serpent, holds a wheel and bears a sharp green sword. He is seated on a lotus in the presence of the chief figure.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The list of bodhisattvas includes several whose Tibetan epithets are rendered by meaning rather than attested Sanskrit equivalents. "Sarvanīvaraṇaviṣkambhin" (སྒྲིབ་པ་ཐམས་ཅད་རྣམ་པར་སེལ་བ) is the standard identification. "Śāntamati" (ཞི་བའི་བློ་གྲོས), "Vicitravikīrṇa" (སྣ་ཚོགས་བཀྲམ་པ), "Gaganalocanadhara" (མཁའ་འོད་མེ་ཏོག་འཆང), "Ākāśagarbha" (ནམ་མཁའི་སྙིང་པོ), and "Rūpaśuddha" (གཟུགས་མཛེས་དག་པ) are reconstructed Sanskrit equivalents; the last three are well attested; the middle two are less commonly documented by name and the Sanskrit is reconstructed here.

The passage ends mid-sentence in the source text, listing beings before the verse is complete; the translation follows the Tibetan precisely and does not supply the missing conclusion.

The term "སྟོབས་མིའམ་ཅི" (kiṃnara) is rendered as "kiṃnaras," the mythological half-human beings, which is standard.  The phrase rendered "hair pore-reliquaries of all the buddhas" translates a compound that literally means "reliquary" or "treasury" at the hair-pore (ūrṇā or more generally from the body-pores). In tantric iconographic context this refers to light-rays and hosts of deities emanating from the pores or hair-follicles of buddha-bodies; the rendering preserves this technical sense while remaining accessible.

The line "does not abide at any extreme" renders "mi gnas ye shes," the gnosis of non-abiding, a reference to non-abiding nirvāṇa (apratiṣṭhitanirvāṇa), the awakening state that abides neither in saṃsāra nor in a one-sided nirvāṇa. This is a key Mahāyāna doctrinal term.

The final list of ten virtuous practices (writing, offering, giving, listening, retaining, reading, making offerings, reciting, reflecting, meditating) corresponds to a standard tenfold enumeration of Dharma practice found across Mahāyāna sūtras and here adapted to the tantric context.  The phrase rendered "the form of a great ascetic" translates dge sbyong chen po, literally "great śramaṇa," referring to a great renunciant; in context this likely alludes to the Buddha's own appearance as a wandering ascetic, or to a monastic form.

The verse "for some as the form of a rākṣasa or rākṣasī" renders srin po srin mo, the male and female flesh-eating demons of Indian mythology, here listed among the wrathful emanation forms of the bodhisattva-protector.

The final two lines of the passage are syntactically incomplete in the source — the text breaks off mid-sentence at the colophon transition into the next chapter, hence the translation ends at the last grammatically complete unit.  The phrase rendered "the Victor arisen as a self-cause without any cause" translates "rgyal ba rgyu med bdag nyid 'khrungs," which is a paradoxical doctrinal claim: the awakened one is beyond both cause and the causal process of conditioned existence, yet arises as his own ground. This reflects the tathāgatagarbha-inflected tantric view of the text, not a theistic creator-god claim.

The verse on "single syllable of great splendor" likely refers to a root or seed-syllable of the deity being praised, consistent with tantric dhāraṇī literature; the specific syllable is not named in this passage.  The mantra syllables throughout this passage are reproduced in transliteration as faithfully as possible from the Tibetan phonetic rendering. Many syllables are corrupt or variant forms typical of tantric transmission; no attempt has been made to normalize or correct them, as their liturgical form is intentional.

The phrase rendered "interrupts offerings" and "interrupts hearing, reflection, and practice" translates bar du gcod pa, literally "to cut in between," a standard idiom for obstacle-spirits interfering with spiritual activity.

The colophon verse ending "like the blade of a sharp sword" closes the second chapter and functions as a summary verse contrasting the ultimate remedy (bodhicitta) with the gross interference of spirit obstacles — a doctrinal juxtaposition typical of this genre of kriyā-tantra and mahāyoga ritual literature.  The Tibetan term མ་མོ (mamo) denotes a class of fierce feminine spirits in the Indo-Tibetan tantric tradition; "offering spirits" is used here for the sake of intelligibility, though "mamos" would be the more precise designation. Similarly, སྲིང་མོ ("sisters") in this ritual context refers to a class of female attendant or guardian figures associated with the maṇḍala assembly.

The term རྐང་སྟབས་ཚམ་ངམ (literally "the offering fitting as a footstool" or "footrest-level offering") is a technical ritual term for an offering placed at the lowest level, typically at the feet of the invited guests; "footstool offering" renders this functionally.

The phrase དཔའ་བོ་སྤྱན་དྲང་བ refers to the invitation of "heroes" (vīras), the male tantric practitioners or deity-figures of that class; "heroes" is retained as the most transparent rendering.  The passage lists the twenty-seven classical Sanskrit lunar mansions plus Abhijit, together with the nine grahas (planets in Indian astronomy), naming them in their Sanskrit forms transliterated into Tibetan. These have been restored to standard Sanskrit here. "Abhijit" appears between Uttarāṣāḍhā and Śravaṇā, its traditional position as the intercalary nakṣatra.

The mudrā descriptions are highly technical and prescribe precise finger configurations for ritual use. The terminology follows standard Tibetan tantric mudrā instruction texts. Several compound gesture terms are translated according to the named mudrā conventions of such ritual manuals rather than purely by their literal meaning, which would be opaque or misleading in isolation.  The term rendered "sisters of accomplishment" for དངོས་གྲུབ་སྲིང་མོ is a somewhat unusual compound; it likely refers to a class of ḍākinī or female attendant spirits associated with siddhi, and the rendering preserves both elements of the compound. The precise ritual choreography described throughout is highly technical and some ambiguity in the sequence of limb movements is unavoidable given the compressed verse form; the translation follows the most grammatically natural reading in each case.  The passage contains extensive lists of pigment names and materials in Tibetan, many of which are technical color-preparation terms drawn from Indo-Tibetan painting traditions.
