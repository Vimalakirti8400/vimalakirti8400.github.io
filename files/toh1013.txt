Dhāraṇī of the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Who Completely Clears Away All Lower Realms, the Arhat, the Perfectly Complete Buddha (Sarvadurgatipariśodhanī-nāma-dhāraṇī)

The Dhāraṇī of Purifying All Lower Realms, whose Sanskrit title likely corresponds to Sarvadurgatipariśodhana, is a very brief scripture centered on the Tathāgata whose name in Tibetan, Ngensong Tamché Yongsu Selwa, means "He Who Completely Clears Away All Lower Realms." After paying homage to this buddha, the text gives a short mantra-like incantation, or dhāraṇī, that calls for the purification of all negative deeds and karmic obscurations. It then describes the benefits of memorizing and retaining even a portion of the dhāraṇī: the ability to remember past lives across vast stretches of time, the purification of karmic obscurations, swift attainment of irreversibility on the path, and merit that remains inexhaustible until one reaches awakening. This work is Toh 1013 in the Kangyur, where it belongs among the dhāraṇī texts, the class of short incantation scriptures valued for their protective and purifying power.

Homage to the Blessed One, the Tathāgata Who Completely Clears Away All Lower Realms, the arhat, the perfectly complete Buddha.

tadyathā oṃ śodhanaṃ viśodhanaṃ viśodhane mama sarva pāpi piśodhane śuddhe viśuddhe sarva karma ābarṇa viṣuddhe svāhā

If any son or daughter of good family thoroughly masters and retains the letters, the words, the names, or the whole of this dhāraṇī, that person will remember their past lives for one million four hundred thousand eons. All their karmic obscurations will be purified, and they will swiftly attain irreversibility. Until they reach the seat of awakening, their merit will never be exhausted.

The dhāraṇī called Purifying All Lower Realms is complete.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The Tathāgata's name in the homage, "Who Completely Clears Away All Lower Realms," is translated from the Tibetan. It probably corresponds to the Sanskrit Sarvadurgatipariśodhana, but that form is not certain, so the name is given in English. The title in the colophon, "Purifying All Lower Realms," is also given in English for the same reason.

The phrase "thoroughly masters and retains" renders a Tibetan expression that could also be read as "retains until awakening." The translation follows the sense of the verb byang, "to be well trained in" or "to master."
