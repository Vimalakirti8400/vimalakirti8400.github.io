Praise of the Glorious Venerable Mahābodhi (Śrīmanmahābodhibhaṭṭārakastotra)

The Praise of the Glorious Venerable Mahābodhi, known in Tibetan as Dpal ldan rje btsun byang chub chen po'i bstod pa, carries a Sanskrit title preserved in a somewhat irregular form that approximates Śrīmanmahābodhibhaṭṭāraka Stotra. It is a devotional hymn addressed to the Buddha Śākyamuni, honored here as the "Great Awakening," and it begins with a homage to the Buddha. Its verses praise him under many epithets, including the lotus-eyed perfect Buddha, the omniscient lion of the Śākyas, and the Tathāgata free of conceptual thought and mental elaboration, and each verse closes with an act of bowing down. The work is Toh 1153 in the Tengyur, where it belongs to the collection of praises, or stotras, that open that portion of the canon.

In the language of India: śrīmānmahābodhibhantūraksya stotraṃ
In the language of Tibet: Praise of the Glorious Venerable Mahābodhi.

Homage to the Buddha.

Perfect Buddha with eyes like the white lotus,
Omniscient one who has reached the state of compassion,
Śākyasiṃha, wholly excellent,
To the Teacher I bow down.

Śrīghana, glorious lord,
Bringer of peace, possessed of the aggregate of moral discipline,
Peaceful, glorious, maker of glory,
To you of peaceful form I bow down.

Accomplished one, who teaches selflessness,
In whom there is no ground for blame,
Knower of the ways, stainless lord,
To the one free of all turbidity I bow down.

Without rival and without pride,
Without conceptual thought, Tathāgata,
Unmoved by any of the afflictions,
To the one free of elaboration I bow down.

Lord of all, knower of liberation,
Guide who takes on manifold forms,
In whom the manifold marks are perfectly complete,
To the one free of attachment I bow down.

Tamer of those hard to tame, supreme among beings,
Pure sage, son of Śuddhodana,
Sugata, of excellent realization, bringer of peace,
To the one renowned as virtuous I bow down.

Freed from turbidity, foe of desire,
Holder of the lotus, untouched by turbidity,
Possessed of a beautiful form, vessel of loving care,
To you of lotus light I bow down.

Great in intelligence, great in diligence,
Great in power, subduer of opponents,
Great in offerings, great in teaching,
To the great Rāhu I bow down.

Perfect in knowledge and in conduct,
Lord of all, stainless light,
Disciplined in bearing, ample and vast,
To the one free of faults I bow down.

Noble one, purifier, healer of all,
Unconquerable by others, free of decline,
Protector who benefits self and others,
To you of boundless light I bow down.

God of gods, the Great God,
Saluted by the gods, inexhaustible,
Become a valid authority, lord of the gods,
To you whose form is of divine substance I bow down.

Supreme illuminator of the ultimate,
Supreme and holy lord,
Supreme benefactor of others,
To that Blessed One I bow down.

Fully victorious over the foe, the four māras,
Peaceful, bringer of peace, knower of suchness,
Ever practicing the essence of suchness,
To the caravan leader I bow down.

Senses subdued, afflictions subdued,
Lord of the Victors, supreme among persons,
Holy Brahmā become supreme,
To the field of merit I bow down.

Blessed One, free of attachment,
By this praise of your very form,
May all who dwell within the fires of hell
Attain a place of coolness.

The Praise of the Glorious Venerable Mahābodhi, composed by the master Choksé.

This too, into the very language of the Land of Snows,
At the command of the supreme venerable emanation body,
Was translated by Shalu Lotsāwa,
The monk Chökyong Zangpo.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

"Mahābodhi" (literally "Great Awakening") in the title refers to the Buddha, most likely as the image enshrined at the Mahābodhi temple in Bodhgaya. Many of the epithets correspond to the traditional Sanskrit lists of names of the Buddha, such as Śrīghana, son of Śuddhodana, "accomplished one" (siddha), and "knower of the ways." "The Great God" and "lord of the gods" are titles normally belonging to Śiva and Indra, here applied to the Buddha, so no Śiva gloss is given. "Great Rāhu" is kept literally as a name in the list; its sense is presumably the one who eclipses all rivals. The phrase rendered "ample and vast" uses the word otherwise used for the city of Vaiśālī, but here it describes the Buddha's bearing. The author's name, rendered phonetically as Choksé (literally "Longing for the Supreme"), could not be identified with confidence.
