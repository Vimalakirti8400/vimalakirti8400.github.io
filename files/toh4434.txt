The Medical Treatise Called the Accomplished Essence (Siddhasāra)

The Siddhasāra, known in Tibetan as Menchö Chepa Drubpa and in English as The Medical Treatise Called the Accomplished Essence, is a compact handbook of Indian medicine composed by the physician Ravigupta, who says he wrote it for his elder brother when he fell ill with pallor disease. Drawing on the great classical treatises of the science of life, it sets out in thirty-one chapters the theory of the three humors of wind, bile, and phlegm, the groups of medicinal substances, diet, and the signs of death, followed by the diagnosis and treatment of illnesses such as fever, diarrhea, wasting disease, tumors, leprosy, and hemorrhoids, and closes with purgation and enema therapies and rejuvenating remedies. Translated into Tibetan by the Indian preceptors Jinamitra and his colleague together with a Tibetan translator, it became one of the foundational sources for Tibetan medicine. It is Toh 4434 in the Tengyur, where it belongs among the treatises on medicine, one of the classical sciences.

Here is the medical treatise called Siddhasāra, in thirty-one chapters.

In the language of India: siddha sara. In the language of Tibet: The Medical Treatise Called the Accomplished Essence. The first section.

This medical treatise has thirty-one chapters in all. They are: the chapter on the system; the chapter on the groups of drugs; the chapter on food; the chapter on the signs of death; the chapter on fever; the chapter on diarrhea; the chapter on the bleeding disorder; the chapter on the great wasting disease; the chapter on abdominal tumors; the chapter on dropsy; the chapter on urinary disorders; the chapter on leprosy; hemorrhoids, and the chapter on fistula of the private parts; the chapter on pallor; hiccup, and the chapter on difficult breathing; the chapter on cough; vomiting, and the chapter on thirst; the chapter on retention of urine; the chapter on constipation; insanity, and the chapter on epilepsy; the chapter on wind diseases; the chapter on diseases from drink; the chapter on erysipelas; the chapter on swellings; the chapter on healing wounds; the chapter of the system of eye medicine; the chapter of the system of curing poisons; the taking of elixirs; the chapter of the system of aphrodisiacs; the chapter of the system for children; the chapter on the five therapeutic procedures; and the chapter on procedures.

I, Ravigupta, son of Durgagupta,
Having bowed to the all-benefiting Omniscient One,
Shall explain this Siddhasāra,
Which is of true benefit.

This has been composed as an exposition of the texts of the system, so that those of small intelligence, who cannot cross the ocean of the science of life, may understand it. This science that sustains life was taught by Brahmā in the heavens. In due course it was taught to the people of the king of the land of Kāśī.

Now I shall explain the chapter on the system. Its branches are: eye medicine; healing the body; dispelling seizing spirits; extracting weapons and splinters; protection from poison; protection of life; protection of children; and increasing the semen.

A person, whose nature is made of the qualities of the great elements, is the basis of disease. Diseases are held to be of the body, of the mind, adventitious, and innate. Among these, diseases of the body are fever, leprosy and the like. Diseases of the mind are anger and the like. Adventitious diseases are those that arise from injury. Innate diseases are hunger, thirst, aging and the like.

Time is without beginning or end, and is marked by units such as the instant of a blink of the eye. Its continuum is divided into six seasons. Of these, the last month of summer and the first month of autumn are the rainy season. The middle month of autumn and the last month of autumn are autumn. The first month of winter and the middle month of winter are winter. The last month of winter and the first month of spring are late winter. The middle month of spring and the last month of spring are spring. The first month of summer and the middle month of summer are the hot season.

Because the sun goes to the north and goes to the south, moving along two paths, there come to be the season of rains, the cold season and the hot season. In the seasons of rain, cold and heat, bile, phlegm and wind accumulate, and in autumn, spring and the rainy season they are aroused. The number of diseases and the number of seasons are to be matched with each other in order.

Wind diseases arise in the afternoon and at the end of the digestion of food. Bile diseases arise at midday, at midnight, and while food is being digested. Phlegm arises right after eating, at dusk, and in the early morning. In the same way, the disturbance of the humors should be worked out according to the particular combinations of two of them and of all three.

The body that is the seat of the humors, the bodily constituents and the impurities is called the body. When these are in balance there is no disease. When they become excessive or deficient, there is disease. The humors are wind, bile and phlegm. The bodily constituents are seven: chyle, blood, flesh, fat, bone, marrow and semen. The impurities are feces, urine, sweat and the like.

Wind has these particular qualities: it is cold, light, subtle, hard, rough, unstable and powerful; it makes the breath move, makes speech come forth, keeps things well in place, sets things in motion, and expels the impurities. Bile is sour, pungent in taste, and burning with heat; it makes things flow, produces luster, and is the cause of a ruddy complexion. Phlegm is sweet and salty in taste, smooth, heavy, and very sticky.

Wind dwells within the region of the anus, the hips and the thighs. Bile dwells within the intestines. Phlegm dwells within the stomach, the throat, the chest, the head and the joints. When the humors go wrong, one should proceed according to the procedure for that very site. When the illness becomes severe, one should distinguish its particulars and proceed by altering the procedure.

As for the six tastes, sweet and the rest, one should always eat whichever of them is a cause of freedom from disease, according to the particular state of the humors. If one does not do so, disease results. Pungent, bitter and astringent tastes disturb wind. Pungent, sour and salty tastes disturb bile. Sweet, sour and salty tastes disturb phlegm. To pacify these humors, if one eats what is opposite to them, those who have a disease will recover, and for those without disease it becomes a cause of well-being.

The sweet taste benefits the eyes and increases the bodily constituents. Sour purges the feces, benefits the heart, digests food, ripens, and produces heat. Salty purges, ripens, digests, and softens the flesh. Pungent removes corpulence, sluggishness and poisons, produces heat, and ripens. Bitter removes fever and the disease of thirst, produces heat, purges, and stimulates the appetite.

Astringent binds, reduces the flesh, makes the stools dry, stops diarrhea, and heals wounds.

A drug is the chief thing, because it is the support of taste, potency and post-digestive taste. As for taste and the other factors, since each earlier one is present in each later one, each later one is the chief in relation to the one before it. Here potency refers to what operates in the interval before the taste has undergone digestion; it resides in and depends upon the substance, and is characterized as cold or hot. Alternatively, the term may also be used for the very power of the substance. After digestion the tastes become of two kinds, sweet and pungent. Of these two, the former is heavy and the latter light.

The physician, the medicine, the patient and the attendant: being endowed with these four is the set of limbs of healing. Without them, healing does not succeed. Of these, the qualities of the physician are: having learned the medical texts by heart and understood their meaning, knowing the practice of medicine, being skillful with the hands, and being clean and free of self-seeking. The qualities of the patient are: having wealth, a life span not yet exhausted, fortitude, and awareness of his own condition; such a one is curable. The qualities of the medicine are: grown in good ground, rich in taste, of the finest quality, and properly gathered at the right time. The qualities of the attendant are: being clean and free of self-seeking, devoted, conscientious, and strong.

When one undertakes the work of giving medicine, one should act only after examining these: the place, the time, the age, the digestive fire, habituation, the constitution, the medicine, the body, fortitude, strength, and the disease.

Among these, as to place: a region with much water, forests and mountains is called a wet region, and it gives rise to diseases of phlegm and wind. A dry region is one with little water and few trees, and it gives rise to diseases arising from blood and from bile. In a region where dry and wet are mixed, the diseases too are mixed.

Age is of three kinds: childhood, middle age and old age. Living on milk up to one year and then on food, up to sixteen years one is called a child. From then up to seventy years one is called middle-aged. Beyond that one is called old. In these, in order, phlegm, bile and wind predominate; they are to be matched one by one according to that sequence. For children and the old, one should not use harsh treatments such as hot caustics, cautery and bloodletting, but should use gentle treatments.

Bodies too are held to be of three kinds. For an emaciated body one should use medicine that builds flesh. For the corpulent one should use medicine that reduces flesh. For one whose flesh is moderate one should use the procedure of maintaining it unchanged.

As for strength, some have much flesh but little strength, and some are lean yet strong; therefore strength should be carefully ascertained from endurance, capacity for exertion, and being hard to sway and firm.

Fortitude is what keeps one unshaken. A person who, when suffering comes or something unheard-of happens, is neither terrified to death nor carried away with joy, because he possesses it, is a person of fortitude.

Habituation is understood as follows: when food, drink and so on, though by nature unsuited to someone, become comfortable for that very person, this is called habituation.

A person of wind constitution has a lean and rough body, thin hair, a restless mind and a restless body, talks much, and in dreams soars through the sky. A person of bile constitution has hair that whitens before its time, a pale complexion, sweats readily, is quick-tempered and sharp-minded, and in dreams sees blazing fire. A person of phlegm constitution has a steady mind, a compact body and many children, glossy hair, and dreams of water and white things. A person whose constitution combines two humors or all three is to be recognized by mixed signs; and though the other humors are present, the constitution is said to be that of whichever predominates.

As for the heat of the fire within: from these four, namely phlegm, bile and wind each predominating in turn, and their being equal, the heat of the fire too becomes of four kinds, weak, excessive, irregular and balanced; these are to be matched one by one in order. Of these, when the heat of the fire is balanced, one should use the procedure for keeping it from changing. For irregular heat, one should dispel wind. For excessive heat of the fire, one should use the procedure for removing bile. For weak heat, one should purge phlegm.

The diminishing of the heat of the fire through food remaining undigested is the basis from which all diseases arise. Its signs are of four kinds: a heavy, overfull belly; sour saliva; nutrient and chyle remaining undigested; and feces and urine not passing downward.

Of these, where the belly sits heavy and overfull, there is much mucus and saliva, the phlegm causes nausea, and it lies coiled, neither rising nor moving down. For this, give sweet flag and salt boiled in water to drink, and induce vomiting. With sour saliva there come sour belching, dizziness, vomiting and great thirst. For this, give water that has been boiled and then cooled, and have the patient stay in a breezy place. When nutrient and chyle remain undigested, the body becomes sluggish, there is yawning, the head feels unsteady, and food is repugnant to look at, and so on. For this, one should fast, and if one sleeps for a few periods during the day, it is digested. One should fast only moderately, so that wind disease does not arise from too severe a fast. From the failure to pass downward come stabbing pains in the belly, frothing wind, and blockage of feces and urine. For this, apply steam fomentation and give hot water to drink.

Since all diseases thus arise from eating unwholesome food, one should therefore avoid what is unwholesome and eat what is beneficial.

Among these, the unwholesome foods are as follows. Peacock, partridge, the animal called godhā, and francolin: these should not be eaten smeared with castor oil and cooked over a fire of castor wood. Ghee and honey should not be eaten in equal parts. Ghee that has been kept in a bronze vessel for ten days or more should not be eaten. Honey should not be taken washed down with warm water, nor should it be eaten mixed with rainwater. Likewise, honey should not be eaten together with pork and radish. The meat of the heron should not be eaten together with liquor. Curd and chicken should not be eaten together. The vegetable kacamaca and molasses should not be eaten together. Fish and the vegetable upodaka should not be eaten together. Oily cakes of flour mixed with sesame oil should not be eaten together with fried food. Fish and molasses should not be eaten together. Vegetables, dried meat, flour of sour fruits, sesame oil cake, horse gram, salt, bamboo seed, curd and fish: these are mostly incompatible with milk and should not be eaten together with it. While the body is anointed with a paste of priyaṃgu, one should not drink rice cooked in milk. Thibi meat cooked with mustard oil should not be eaten. Fish fat and long pepper should not be eaten together. The mixed porridge, a porridge of rice, sesame and lentils mixed together, should not be taken together with liquor and with rice cooked in milk. Fish, parched grain, ghee and buttermilk should not be eaten mixed together.

If a person unknowingly eats such foods, incompatible in taste and potency, or others like them, he will be stricken with disease, or else he will die. When disease has arisen in this way from eating incompatible food, it should be removed with purgatives and emetics. For those who are habituated, who have a strong digestive fire and great strength, it does no harm.

The chapter on the system, the first, is complete.

Next I shall explain the chapter on the groups of drugs.

Smantari, baspruba, castor, jhaśa, zangtsiwa, jīvaka, caltrop, nyeushing, laṅguli, śiravidari, hānasabādika, pṛhati, dri, cikali, dvepahepa, and the bean ātmakupta: this group of drugs removes wasting disease, abdominal tumors, wind disease, difficult breathing, cough, and diseases arising from bile.

The trees nyagrodha, udumbara, plakṣa, maduka, aśvattha, tintuka, pilaya, juniper, padara, pāratha, naṇṭa, bṛkṣa, mango, willow, palāśa, gochey, śreta, sengtrom and jambu: this group of drugs removes bile, the bleeding disorder, burning, and the disease of intoxication. It removes wounds and diseases of the womb. It removes urinary disease, and removes wounds, heat of the body, and diseases of the womb.

Long pepper, krutrugtre, sweet flag, kuṭaja, juniper, bora, long pepper root, nutgrass, ginger, cardamom, white aconite, haraṇyaka, clematis, black pepper, anise, gabra root, murā, the fruit of mahānimba, caraway, white mustard seed, asafoetida, putsesel, and viḍaṅga: this group of drugs removes wind and phlegm.

Cardamom, gyapö, nutgrass, hareṇyaka, shingtsa, gandhapatra, white sandalwood, saffron, tsawa, sandalwood, yetsal, nāgapuṣpa, pine, gucchārasa, guggulu, satsipok, nyachi, dadhi, jema, white resin, byeghranakha, tiger's claw, spikenard and costus: this group of drugs removes pustules, itching, poison, wind and phlegm.

Bauna, artagala, nyeushing, ginger, caraway, biśanika, saireya, the two kinds of bṛhatī, dūrvā grass, sputika, śigruka, chebulic myrobalan, agnananta, balba, krutrugtre, nagtamala and samorta: this group of drugs removes internal tumors, the disease of excess phlegm and fat, abdominal tumors, and headache.

The two kinds of sengtrom, lawa, aśoka, plantain, pala, elabaluka, kadamba, jigini, śṛparṇi and guḍūcī: this group of drugs dries up phlegm and the disease of excess fat, removes diseases of the womb, heals wounds, stops diarrhea, and removes all poisons.

Ambhaṣṭha, dhataki, sengtrom, samabaga, lotus stamens, licorice, aralu and bilva: this group of drugs removes diarrhea with fever of digestion.

Emblic myrobalan, chebulic myrobalan, belleric myrobalan, long pepper and krutrugtre: this group of drugs removes all the harm of phlegm fever, and greatly increases virility and the digestive fire; yet it neither makes one excessively virile nor makes the digestive fire excessive.

What are called the three fruits, chebulic, belleric and emblic myrobalan, remove recurrent fever with exhaustion, benefit the eyes, produce heat, and remove urinary disease, leprosy, bile and phlegm.

Bṛhatī, dhaṅaki, pāthā, licorice and kuṭaja: this group of drugs is ripening, and removes urinary disease arising from the three humors.

Patoli, white sandalwood, murpa, putsesel, pātha and guḍūcī: this group of drugs removes bile, phlegm, loss of appetite, vomiting, fever, itching and poison.

Guḍūcī, nimpa, coriander fruit, licorice and white sandalwood: this group of drugs removes the disease of thirst, heat of the body, loss of appetite, vomiting, and all fevers.

Butsangto, ramnyewa, shingkar, budrenshing, med, mahāme, jīvaka, zangtsiwa, prapuṇḍarīka, grapes, riddha, bṛhatī, nyikkar, the two kinds of saha, payassya, juniper and guḍūcī: this group of drugs greatly builds flesh, increases breast milk, lengthens life and gives virility, and it removes the bleeding disorder and wind disease.

Taltre, juniper, putsesel, madhuga, the two kinds of sandalwood, tipala and licorice: this group of drugs swiftly removes the bleeding disorder, calms the terrible disease of thirst, overcomes violent bile fever, and removes intense heat of the body.

The eye medicine barberry extract, tsashyam, nāgakesara stamens, lotus stamens and licorice: this group of drugs removes the bleeding disorder, poison, and the disease of heat in the body.

Black pepper, rock salt, the two kinds of nagtsur, asafoetida, shilajit and pangma: this group of drugs removes the disease of excess fat, and removes retention of urine and obstruction by stones.

Ramnyewa, ignamandha, the gaśa tree, bṛkṣadani, kuśa grass, morata, nyeushing, mallow, tuntuka, caltrop, basuka, basira, dharaba grass, seiraya, and the root of sinsin: this group of drugs removes urinary gravel, retention of urine, obstruction by stones, and affliction by wind disease.

Śaba, danda root, drabanati, guhi, tsaśyama, guḍūcī, the nyerong tree, the seeds of jarṇu, śaṃgini, śaste, dongka, tilvaka, kimbalaka, karañja and jarṇu: this group of drugs removes constipation, dropsy, distension of the belly, poison and abdominal tumors.

Bilva, agnimantha, tunṭyaka, śiriparṇi and patala: this group of drugs is called the greater five roots. It produces heat and removes phlegm and wind.

Pṛiṣṇaparṇi, pir, eraṇja and the two kinds of bṛhatī: this group of drugs is called the lesser five roots. It builds flesh and removes wind and bile.

Bidari, taltre, tsaga śrigri, and basadana yü: these five roots of climbing plants remove retention of urine, bile and wind.

Gṛdhya, hala, caltrop, seireya and karamidhaka: these five roots of thorny plants remove phlegm and wind.

Kuśa grass, the two kinds of kaśa, bha, and reed: these five roots of grasses remove bile and retention of urine, and cleanse the urinary passage.

The best of physicians should make the distinctions: these groups of drugs may be cooked in sesame oil, cooked in ghee, used to anoint the body, or drunk, and each should be done according to its procedure.

To extract and boil a decoction of the drugs, use water sixteen times the amount of the drugs, and boil it until one quarter remains. To boil a medicated ghee, take the decoction reduced to one quarter, four times the amount of the ghee, boil them together, and let only the ghee remain. To prepare it with milk, the milk is made equal to the ghee, and boiled until the milk is used up. To boil powdered drugs, take ghee four times the amount of the powder, and a decoction four times the amount of the ghee, and boil them; when the decoction is used up and the powder is well blended, it is cooked.

Of these, what is to be drunk and what is to be given as an enema are alike: they should be cooked to a moderate degree. What is for anointing the body should be cooked thoroughly. What is for pouring into the nose should not be cooked through. All three are strained before use. This is taught as the general procedure.

The chapter on the groups of drugs, the second.

Since beings have no means of sustaining life other than food and drink, the particular benefits and harms of these will now be set out with certainty.

Among them, red-husked śāli rice, great śāli rice, and kalama śāli rice are sweet. They increase semen, are unctuous, produce little flatus, and make the feces scant. Of these, red-husked śāli rice overcomes the three humors and cures thirst disease and the disease of excess fat. Great śāli rice is supremely aphrodisiac. Kalama śāli rice cures phlegm and bile.

White rice that ripens in sixty days is sweet in taste, cool and heavy, and overcomes the three humors. Sixty-day rice with a black husk is slightly inferior to the former in taste and in its power once digested.

Wild millet is rough. It dries, produces wind, and cures phlegm and bile. Foxtail millet, common millet and kodo millet are said to be similar to it.

Barley is cool. It produces much flatus and much feces, and cures bile and phlegm. Wheat is cool, heavy and sweet. It is aphrodisiac and cures wind.

Mung beans are astringent and sweet. They are light, and cure phlegm, bile and diseases of the blood. Black gram is unctuous, heavy and very hot. It increases the impurities of the nine orifices, is aphrodisiac, and cures wind. White beans are not aphrodisiac. They cure phlegm and bile, and cause distress through wind.

Horse gram cures difficulty in breathing, hiccups, hemorrhoids, phlegm, semen and wind. Lentils are cool. They cure the fever of the bleeding disorder, and afterwards are binding. Chickpeas arouse desire for intercourse, cure diseases of the blood, phlegm and bile, and produce wind.

Small peas are cool and sweet in taste. They bind the feces and cure phlegm and bile. The other small pea is said to be like the small pea. Chickling peas are also like these, except that they produce wind all the more.

Sesame has an ashen taste and is sweet, bitter and very hot. It produces vigor and bile. In general, the various pod-bearing seeds are cool and rough, and diminish vigor.

Now the particular kinds of meat will be explained. Goat meat is neither too cool, nor too heavy, nor too unctuous. It does not produce the humors, but cures them. Mutton is sweet, cool and heavy. It binds the feces and builds flesh. Buffalo meat is unctuous and heavy. It induces sleep, increases semen and builds flesh. Pork is heavy. It is aphrodisiac, cures wind, and induces sweating.

Hare's meat kindles the digestive heat and cures phlegm and bile. Wind it neither increases nor diminishes: it is neutral. The meat of the khargosol is cool. It cures the three humors and binds the feces and urine.

Peacock meat cures wind, and makes the hearing clear, the eyes keen, the complexion good and the voice pleasant, and it increases semen. Chicken meat is unctuous, heavy and very hot. It cures wind and is aphrodisiac. Partridge meat is neither too heavy, nor too hot, nor too sweet, and cures all the humors. The meat of the ur bird, the lark and the quail produces heat and cures combined disorders. Sparrow meat cures combined disorders and increases phlegm and semen. The meat of the gray partridge is cool and light, and cures phlegm and the bleeding disorder. Pigeon meat is cool and heavy, and cures the bleeding disorder. The meat of the blue pigeon and the dove is slightly lighter than that.

The meat of water birds is unctuous, hot and heavy. It is aphrodisiac and cures wind. Among them, goose meat is aphrodisiac and especially cures wind. Fish meat is unctuous, hot and heavy. It cures wind but produces bile disease and the bleeding disorder. The meat of the water creatures, the bulukī, the turtle and the crab, cures wind and bile and is aphrodisiac.

Now the particular kinds of greens and roots will be explained. The green kākamācī cures the three humors. Kalam increases breast milk and is aphrodisiac. Tsigeri cures phlegm and wind. White mustard leaves produce all the humors. Chinese neu, tsulli neu, wild neu and red Mön neu: these cure wind and phlegm, make the feces pass easily, and rid one of the bleeding disorder.

Raw radish produces the humors; cooked, it cures wind and phlegm. Young radish cures all the humors and benefits the heart and throat. Karkoṭaka, vārtāka, paṭola and karvelaka: these cure leprosy, urinary disease, difficulty in breathing, cough, bile and phlegm. The ash gourd cures all the humors, benefits the heart, and cleanses the urinary passage. Kaliṅga fruit and the bottle gourd cure bile and produce wind. Cucumbers and melons produce wind and phlegm and cure bile. Bamboo shoots, nyeushing, paitorto and kebuka: these cure phlegm and bile. Lotus root, śālūka, śṛṅgāṭaka, māluka and kaśeruka: these are heavy and cool. They cure the bleeding disorder, are aphrodisiac, and increase breast milk.

Now the particular kinds of fruit will be explained. Pomegranate binds the feces, stops diarrhea and cures wind, and it is not unwholesome for phlegm and bile either. Emblic myrobalan is like it, and after it is eaten its taste becomes sweet. It destroys the humors, is laxative, and is aphrodisiac. Khalu cures tumors, wind, phlegm, difficulty in breathing and cough. Ripe wood apple fruit is heavy. It binds the feces and cures the humors and poison. Mango fruit cures wind, builds flesh, increases semen, improves the complexion, and produces vigor.

Rose apple fruit produces wind and cures phlegm and bile. It binds the feces and causes distension. Tinduka fruit cures phlegm and bile. Juniper fruit cures wind and bile. Bilva fruit produces wind and keeps things from passing downward. Priyāla fruit cures wind. The fruits of the palmyra, the rājādana, the plantain, the jackfruit and the coconut are sweet, unctuous and heavy, and produce semen and flesh. Grapes, madhūka, drago, tambal and parūṣaka: the fruits of these cure wind, bile and diseases of the blood, and make the feces pass easily. The fruit of the śamī makes the hair fall out. Kośāmra, nyoti, dantaśaṭha and karmarda: these fruits should be known to produce the bleeding disorder. Pīlu fruit cures tumors.

Now the particular kinds of spices will be explained. Dried ginger, black pepper and long pepper cure phlegm and wind. Of these, black pepper is not aphrodisiac, while dried ginger and long pepper are aphrodisiac. Asafoetida cures tumors, colic and constipation, and overcomes wind and phlegm. Lalaphü, coriander fruit and cumin are supreme for curing wind and phlegm.

Now the particular kinds of salt will be explained. Rock salt benefits the eyes, is aphrodisiac, and pacifies the three humors. Black salt is very hot, and cures constipation and pain in the heart. Biḍa salt is very hot and sharp. It cures pain in the heart and agrees with wind. Tsokhada salt is like these but slightly weaker in power. Sea salt is heavy and makes food break down. Pond water cures wind and phlegm. Water from a pool produces wind. Spring water is rough and produces digestive heat. Water that falls from the lip of a high cliff is light and cures phlegm. The chapter explaining the rules for food and drink, the third.

I shall explain the chapter on curing fever. Fever is said to be the king of all diseases. Therefore the procedure for curing it is explained here first of all. Fever first arose when the sage called Endowed with Learning insulted the Great God called Rudra (Śiva), and the Great God Rudra, enraged, sent forth his breath. From that it became eight kinds of fever: the separate fevers of wind, of bile and of phlegm; the fever in which wind and bile are combined; the fever in which the two, bile and phlegm, are combined; the fever in which the three, wind, bile and phlegm, are combined; and adventitious fever.

As for the way fever first arises: through faults of diet, and through faults of conduct such as sitting and lying, the humors enter the stomach and drive the inner heat outward; joining with the chyle and remaining there, they produce fever.

Of these, the signs of fever arising from wind are: the body is cold and trembles; there is dizziness and delirium; the hair of the body stands on end; there is stretching and yawning; the head, the waist, the thighs and the ribs ache, and there is sourness at the heart; there is great thirst; the eyes, the color of the skin, the nails, the feces and the urine are blackish, and there is an astringent taste in the mouth; the cheeks ache; breathing is difficult; and the cough cannot be stopped.

The signs of fever arising from bile are: the body is very hot and hot within; there is great thirst; there is fainting; sweat breaks out; the mouth tastes pungent; or there is dizziness and delirium; there are diseases of the nose and diseases of the mouth, and eruptions break out on the lips and in the throat; one is irritable and very angry; one longs for coolness; the eyes, the nails, the color of the skin, the feces and the urine are yellow; belching has a bitter taste; and there is diarrhea.

The signs of fever arising from phlegm are: difficulty in breathing, coughing, colds, much saliva, loss of appetite, vomiting, much sleep, heaviness of the body, sluggishness, lassitude, a sweet taste in the mouth, gooseflesh, a pale color in the eyes, the nails, the skin, the feces and the urine, and a longing for warmth.

The signs of fever arising from the combination of the two, wind and bile, are said to be: great thirst, heat of the body, dryness of the throat, dryness of the mouth, the hair standing on end, sleeplessness, vomiting, and severe pain in the joints and the head.

The signs of fever arising from the combination of the two, wind and phlegm, are to be recognized from these: torpor, lassitude, heat of the body, pain in the joints and the head, heaviness of the body, coldness of the body, cough, loss of appetite, and sweating of the body.

The manner of fever arising from the combination of the two, bile and phlegm, is: being half cold and half hot, loss of appetite, sweating, cough, torpor, a bitter taste in the mouth, confusion, lassitude, and great thirst.

The manner in which a fever arises from the combination of the three, wind, bile and phlegm, is: pain in the joints, the bones and the head; being half cold and half hot; torpor; loss of appetite; dizziness; a rasping in the throat; stabbing pain in the ears; the eyes red and squinting; bile and blood coming from the mouth; vomiting; great thirst; sleeplessness at night; the tongue turning black as if burnt and rough to the touch; the color of the body turning bluish; and red pimples breaking out. If all these features are present in full, it cannot be cured. If they are present only in part, it is said to be hard to cure.

As for adventitious fever: from the torment of a disturbed mind, the fever of bile becomes hot. That is to say, desire, grief, fear and anger disturb the mind; and the fevers that arise from whichever of the three, wind, bile and phlegm, has arisen are shown by their respective signs.

The method of curing fever is this. As soon as a fever has first arisen, one should carry out the procedure of fasting without harming the patient's strength. But for fevers that have arisen in those with wind, exhaustion, anger, grief, coughing, or a ruptured lung, one should not carry out the procedure of fasting. The measure of fasting is this: when the patient has become hungry and thirsty, the feces and urine pass in the proper way, and the body has become light, the fasting has been done well. If the fasting is too severe, it turns into a wasting disease, torpor, dizziness, difficulty in breathing, and the disease of exhaustion.

Of these, for a fever of phlegm and wind, one should give boiled water, warm, to drink for thirst. For a fever arising from bile, heat or poison, one should give a cold decoction of bitter substances to drink for thirst. If one gives a cold decoction of dried ginger, bālaka, parpaṭa, vetiver, nutgrass and sandalwood, that too cures thirst, vomiting, fever and heat of the body.

After the fasting has been carried out, as food for digestion one should give, in a way that is beneficial and agreeable, a gruel based on the decoction of whichever medicine digests wind, bile or phlegm respectively. Or one should give a thin gruel of roasted and ground rice with dried ginger added. Or, for a fever of wind, one should give boiled śāli rice with a broth of the meat of game animals and wild birds. If it does not subside with that, then once seven days have passed one should give digestive medicines and pacifying medicines. If medicine is given before seven days, while the fever is newly arisen, that very medicine greatly increases the humors.

Then, for a fever arising from wind, one should give the decoction of the digestive medicine, the five roots beginning with bilva. Or one should give the decoction of long pepper root, guḍūcī and dried ginger boiled together. Or one should give the decoction of guḍūcī, nutgrass, jitser and dried ginger boiled together. Or one should give the decoction of emblic myrobalan, nutgrass, the fivefold roots, guḍūcī and coriander fruit boiled together. Or one should give the decoction of tsakhri, nutgrass, guḍūcī, pālaka, the two kinds of bṛhatī, and caltrop boiled together. Or, if one gives the decoction of sthirā, kaleśī and dried ginger boiled together, it benefits wind disease. For a fever arising from phlegm, one should give the decoction of the digestive medicines khalung root, dried ginger, guḍūcī and long pepper root boiled together, mixed with the ash of scorched barley; or else the decoction of the group beginning with long pepper, taught earlier, boiled and mixed with the ash of scorched barley. The method of curing a fever that has arisen from the combination of all three humors, wind, bile and phlegm, is this. If two are predominant and one is slight, one should increase the strength of whichever is slight. If one is predominant and two are slight, one should reduce the strength of whichever is predominant; or else one should increase the strength of whichever is slight. Thus, where the shares are unequal, greater and lesser, in brief one reduces the strength of whichever is predominant and increases the strength of whichever is slight, so that by every means their strengths are made equal and in harmony. Where all three are disturbed in equal shares, one should first perform the procedure for pacifying phlegm. After that one should perform the procedure for pacifying bile. Then one should perform the procedure for pacifying wind. In this way one should overcome the fever that has arisen from the three humors.

For the fever in which the three are combined, give to drink the decoction of the digestive medicines dhamaka, the three fruits, deodar, juniper, sandalwood, vetiver, parpaṭa and paruṣaka wood boiled together. Or else the decoction of the three roots, nutgrass, the three fruits, parpaṭa, paṭola, harista, kuṭaja, kirātatikta, guḍūcī and pāṭhā boiled together also clears the fever that has arisen from the three humors.

If at the end of that fever in which the three are combined a swelling arises at the base of the ear, it is hard to cure. One should pacify it by bleeding to let out blood, by anointing with medicines, and by drinking ghee.

Through the combination of the three humors, the activities of body, speech and mind are drawn in, so that, like a corpse, they do not do what they should, and this gives rise to the life-threatening disease called abhinyāsa. If that disease of abhinyāsa strikes a person, one counts him among the dead, and having pledged to make the dedication as for one already dead, one should nonetheless, as one grasps after a vessel sinking in water, set to work in all haste and treat him.

The method of treating it is this. The juice of khalung fruit is mixed with asafoetida, dried ginger, and medicines of pungent, bitter and sharp taste, and the strained juice is poured into the mouth until he returns to awareness. If the heartwood of madhuka, rock salt, sweet flag, black pepper and long pepper, in equal parts, are ground in water, strained, and poured into the nose, he will also return to awareness. If the fruit of śirīṣa, long pepper, black pepper, rock salt, barberry extract, jiku, realgar and sweet flag, in equal parts, are ground in cow's urine, strained, and applied to the eyes, he will also come to awareness. When the procedure has been done in this way, or else to bring him back to awareness, one cauterizes him on the forehead or on the heels of both feet with an iron probe.

If one drinks the decoction of vyāghrī, durālabhā, bhārgī, lizurwa, budrenshing and puṣkaramūla boiled together, it also pacifies phlegm and the fever of abhinyāsa down to its root. If the decoction of khalung, gambhārī, bilva, vyāghrī, pāṭhā and castor root boiled together is mixed with salt and cow's urine and given to drink, it also clears the disease of abhinyāsa and the disease in which the belly is swollen and sluggish.

Karavī, puṣkaramūla, castor root, khyichewa, vāsaka and guḍūcī; the group called the great five roots, explained earlier, bilva and the rest, and the group called the lesser five roots, pṛśniparṇī and the rest, the name for the two groups of five, great and lesser, taken together being the ten roots, those ten; and lizurwa, budrenshing, durālabhā, bhārgī and punarnavā: if these, in equal parts, are boiled in cow's urine and drunk, awareness is also restored, and the grave fever called abhinyāsa and the grave disease of exhaustion are swiftly cleared. If one drinks the decoction of karaṇḍa, krutruktré, madder, khyichewa, bilva, kulakaja, the two kinds of bṛhatī, karavī and the three pungents boiled together, it also clears the blocking of the gullet and the rattling sound in the throat that come about on account of the disease of abhinyāsa.

Now the different times of the remittent fevers are explained. When the three humors, wind and the rest, have entered the seats of the seven bodily constituents, chyle and the rest, remittent fevers arise, and these become of five kinds: the continuous fever, the constant fever, the daily fever, the fever that rises once in two days, and the fever that rises once in three days. The method of curing them is as follows. The digestive medicines nimba wood, chebulic myrobalan, guḍūcī, nutgrass, paṭola and the fruit of kuṭaja, in equal parts, combined together; khyichewa, parpaṭa, pāṭhā and the two kinds of taltré, these in equal parts combined together; paṭola, ariṣṭa, grapes, dongka, the three fruits and pāṭhā, shaka and shama, in equal parts combined together; white sandalwood, vetiver, coriander fruit, nutgrass, guḍūcī and dried ginger, in equal parts combined together; and deodar, sthirā, dried ginger, vāsaka, emblic myrobalan and aloeswood, in equal parts combined together. If each of these five medicinal compounds is boiled separately, the decoction cooled and sweetened with honey and sugar, and each is drunk separately, each compound clears one of the five kinds of fever mentioned above. They are applied one to one, following the order of the numbering of the fevers and of the compounds. Or else it is also taught that each of the five fevers is cleared by all five of the groups of medicine, each on its own.

Having explained in this way the procedure for bringing the humors to ripeness: if, once the humors within the fever patient have ripened, they stay where they are and do not go out, this too becomes a great harm. Therefore, whether the humors within have ripened or have not ripened, one should expel them with purgatives as is fitting, judging by the patient's strength, the season and the disease. The purgative is this: the decoction of licorice, dongka, grapes, parpaṭa, pāṭhā, the three fruits and paṭola boiled together, with powdered chebulic myrobalan added, purges and clears fever.

The decoction of paṭola, dongka, parpaṭa, viśālā, the three fruits and trivṛt boiled together, mixed with the ash of roasted barley, purges. It cleanses all fevers. The decoction of parpaṭa, chebulic myrobalan, trivṛt, the root of dantī, khyichewa, dongka, the ash of roasted barley, ghee and rock salt boiled together also purges and clears fever. The three fruits, long pepper, trivṛt and śyāmā grass, finely ground, kneaded with sugar and honey and made into pills, also purge; they clear combined fever, the swelling of dropsy, and the disease of coughing up blood.

For chronic fever, and where phlegm has been exhausted, milk is like nectar. For new fever it is like poison and kills a person. The method of giving milk is this: milk boiled with the five roots and then cooled; or milk boiled with four times its amount of water until the water is gone; or milk boiled with the heartwood of shapa wood; or else, if one drinks milk warm, fresh from the milking, it clears fever. If milk is boiled together with long pepper, licorice, grapes, the herb pala, white sandalwood and taltré and drunk, it swiftly clears fever. If one drinks milk boiled with young bilva wood, or else boiled with castor root, it at once clears virulent fever. If milk is boiled together with jaggery, dried ginger, balā, vyāghrī and caltrop, cooled, and drunk, it pacifies swelling, fever, and blockage of feces and urine.

If the patient has no wish even for the milk preparations, and has been left without luster by the heat of the fever, another method of cure should be used: he should drink ghee alone, or else ghee boiled with medicines, judging by the heat of the digestive fire. Ghee boiled with medicines is this: if one drinks ghee boiled with grapes, or boiled with balā, or boiled with licorice, or boiled with the three fruits, or boiled with guḍūcī, it at once clears fever.

The ghee called the great vāsaka ghee is this. Vāsaka wood, ariṣṭa wood, guḍūcī, bhārgī, the five roots, the three fruits, durālabhā, licorice, grapes and tangpala are ground to powder, two zho of each, and boiled with thirty-two srang of ghee and with water four times the amount of the ghee until the water is gone. The name of this ghee is the great vāsaka ghee. If one drinks it in the proper measure, it clears all fevers.

The making of the lesser vāsaka ghee is this. Ghee is boiled with two things, the decoction of boiled vāsaka wood and vāsaka itself ground to powder, until the medicinal decoction is gone; it is cooled and mixed with honey, and its name is the lesser vāsaka ghee. If one drinks of it in the proper measure, it clears fever, the great wasting disease of the lungs, difficulty in breathing, phlegm, pallor, bile, and the disease of coughing up blood.

The ghee called the wholesome ghee (kalyāṇaka) is this. The chapter on the signs of death is explained. If one carelessly performs medical treatment without knowing that the patient is finished, it ends only in added weariness and disgrace. Therefore one should judge the length of life from these three: messengers, portents of death and signs. Portents of death are whatever changes come over the dying person's disposition, the color of his body, his senses and his manner of behavior; in short, the best of physicians should recognize these as portents of death. Whoever grasps sense objects wrongly with his senses, and comes to hate his physician, his friends and his teacher and to delight in his enemies, will die. Whoever sees clouds, rainbows and lightning in a clear sky, and sees the sky covered with celestial palaces and vehicles, will not live. One who does not see the star called the goddess Arundhatī, the star gongchuk, or the Nine Joints, and who sees the great earth as small as a circle eight paces round, his life faculty has already passed. Whoever sees stars by day like blazing fire, sees the color of the sun as of many hues, and sees the wind as having form, is taught to be certain to die. One who sees a hole bored in the face of the rising sun, and does not perceive with his nose the smell of a lamp that has gone out, will die.

If one sees one's own reflection in a mirror or the like with its limbs incomplete, or does not see it at all, or sees in it the appearance of various sentient beings, one is already dwelling in the house of Yama. Whoever's left eye has sunk into its socket, or whose tongue has turned black, or the shape of whose nose has changed, or whose lips have turned black and lost their shape, or whose mouth has come to stink, is to be given up. When the eyes have turned red, have become bloodshot, do not move, are filled with tears and are askew, and the eyebrows have drooped and gone askew, know that he has become a corpse. When the color of the face has turned red, black, yellow or a pale blue, when shame is gone, when the complexion has become unsightly and mindfulness has failed, know at that time that he has already passed away. If the head looks as though thickly covered with the powder of cow dung, the hair falls loose, and the nails and teeth become unsightly in color and as if coated with curd, know that his life is exhausted. Whoever's forehead veins appear pale yellow, smoky, red, pale blue, black or white will go to the house of Yama. When lice crawl on the forehead, when crows do not want the torma, when he sleeps too much or becomes wholly sleepless, at that time he cannot last long. Whoever's flesh on the ankles, the kneecaps, the forehead, the shoulders, the cheekbones, the cheeks and the jaw grows slack and hangs down will give up his life before long. Whoever's flesh has wasted while the pitch of his voice has grown loud, whose voice has grown weak though he still has strength, and whose hairline parts, know that he has been caught by the noose of the time of death. Whoever's flesh wastes though he eats, whose strength grows though he does not eat, and the color of whose face has become oily or changed to another color, his life faculty has already passed. If he suddenly smells foul, or has turned into a foul smell, or blue flies circle around him, he will die.

Now the particulars of signs are explained. In a dream, having one's body smeared with oil, going toward the south, and riding a pig, a buffalo, a beast of prey, a donkey or a camel are not good dreams. If one dreams of being bound and led toward the south, with laughter, by a woman with loosened hair, dressed in black and red clothes, it is undesirable. If one dreams of meeting corpse carriers, non-Buddhist (tīrthika) renunciants and the like, of keeping company with them, of drinking honey and sesame oil, and of dancing with one's body smeared with filth, it is not good. Dreaming of falling from mountains, cliffs and the like, of being bound, of being overpowered by others, of being pecked by crows and the like, and of falling, is not a good sign. If one dreams of climbing a lhaté juniper, a lac tree, an anthill or a pāribhadra tree, or of finding sesame oil, cotton, the residue of pressed oilseed, or iron, it will turn out ill. Dreaming of taking a bride, of wearing a garland of red flowers and red clothes, of being carried away by water, and of eating cooked meat is undesirable. If one sees things of this kind in dreams, and other various things like them, one who is not sick will be struck by disease, and one who is sick will die.

Now the particulars of good signs are explained. If one sees or dreams of gods, brahmins, victory banners, parasols, a bull, lotus flowers, a king, white flowers, white clothes, clear water, a blazing fire, living friends, virtuous and gentle people, and women adorned with ornaments, it is good. If in a dream one dreams of a bull, of riding a horse, of climbing a mountain, of walking among trees with milky sap and trees with fruit, of finding a mirror, raw meat or a garland of flowers, or of crossing a great river, one who is not sick will find wealth, and if one is a sick person, the disease will quickly be healed.

Now the particulars of messengers are explained. A messenger of low caste, or with incomplete limbs, or a non-Buddhist renunciant and the like, or one holding a noose and a staff in his hand and behaving wildly, one wearing red, black or worn-out clothes, and one with a staring look: these are said to be undesirable. A messenger wringing his hands, shedding tears, with head and body smeared with butter, or cutting grass and wood; a woman or a eunuch coming as messenger; one touching with his hand the parts of the body whose names are feminine, such as the nose, or whose names are neuter, such as the eye; one touching with his hand toward the male organ; one who comes riding a donkey, a camel or a buffalo; and one who sits and speaks of suffering: these are unfit. The opposite of these is positive.

When the physician sets out to go to the patient, it is good if he hears the trumpeting of an elephant, thunder, the sound of drums and the like, and if he sees jewels, a garland of flowers, raw meat, a parasol, a full vase and the like. When the physician sets out and goes, if creatures with masculine names, such as the goose and the pigeon, are seen staying on his left, and those with feminine names, such as the chough and the rikek, are seen staying on his right, and when he enters the patient's house he is well received, he should know that his task will achieve its aim. Seeing a gateway, a victory banner, a tree with milky sap, or a tree with fruit and flowers, and birds flying or perching on the right or the left and singing sweetly, are all positive too. If a dog and a fox go off to the right, it is positive. Seeing the creature godhā, snakes and nāga serpents is undesirable at all times. Thus whatever physician, having carefully examined and drawn distinctions in this way, undertakes the treatment with certainty, his fame becomes beautiful like a garland of flowers in a gathering of many virtuous people. The sixth chapter, on the signs of death, is complete.

Now I shall explain the chapter on curing diarrhea.

Diarrhea arises as follows. Through unsuitable food, and through eating and drinking what is excessively heavy, excessively oily, excessively rough or excessively hot, through eating far too much or far too little, and so on, the digestive fire is destroyed; the humors then gain strength, and from this diarrhea results. It is of six kinds: that arising from each of the three humors singly, that arising from all of them combined, that arising from grief, and that arising from fear. I shall now explain their signs.

The signs of diarrhea arising from wind are these: the stool is dusky red in color, frothy and rough; it comes in small amounts, again and again, mixed with mucus, with griping, and with noise.

The signs of diarrhea arising from bile are these: the stool is yellow, red, black, blue or green; it is very foul-smelling and loose; the anus becomes hot and burning and pustules appear on it; and there is great thirst.

The signs of diarrhea arising from phlegm are these: the stool is white in color, slimy, astringent, oily and cold; there is little griping; there is loss of appetite; and the chest is hot and water comes from the mouth.

The signs of diarrhea arising from all three humors combined are these: the stool resembles pork fat, butter or sesame oil, or the water in which meat has been washed. Know that this is hard to cure. When the stool resembles honey, or butter and sesame oil, or pieces of liver come out, or it resembles milk or meat that has been pounded and pounded, or it is of many colors, very foul-smelling, and with seared fat in it, the patient will not live. Pustules appearing at the anus, prolapse of the anus, wasting of the flesh, being struck by severe complications such as difficulty in breathing, failure of the digestive fire, constant fullness of the belly, and pain in the kidneys and loins are also signs that the patient will not live.

The signs of diarrhea arising from fear and from grief should be known to be like the signs of diarrhea arising from wind. For these two as well, one should carry out the procedure for dispelling wind, give the patient joy, and set his heart at rest.

All six kinds of diarrhea are further of two kinds, distinguished as ripe and unripe. For ripe diarrhea, one should carry out the procedure of fasting and the procedure of emesis, in that order. Its drink and food are ginger, a decoction of bāleka, and light food, which are wholesome. If unripe diarrhea is stopped too early, it turns into diseases such as tumors and leprosy. Unripe diarrhea is this: if the stool is put into water, it sinks to the bottom. Ripe diarrhea is this: if it is put into water, it comes to the top and spreads out.

For unripe diarrhea, one should carry out the procedure of fasting and the procedure of emesis, in that order. Its drink and food are ginger, a decoction of bāleka, and light food, which are wholesome. If unripe diarrhea is stopped too early, it turns into diseases such as tumors and leprosy. Therefore it is good first to purge with chebulic myrobalan and then carry out the procedure for stopping the stool.

The method for stopping it is this: take as a base the decoction of the group of medicines beginning with long pepper explained earlier, and make a rice gruel, a broth, or a buttermilk gruel, and give it to drink. How to prepare the buttermilk gruel will be shown in detail below. Or else take as a base the decoction of the group beginning with turmeric, or of the group beginning with sweet flag, make a rice gruel, and give it to drink; unripe diarrhea will then subside.

Or else combine ginger, ativiṣā, asafoetida, nutgrass, kuṭaja and trutruk tré; or combine nutgrass, tejovatī, pāṭhā, long pepper and kuṭaja; or combine rock salt, kuṭaja seeds, sweet flag and putsé shel; or combine sauvarcala salt, sweet flag, chebulic myrobalan, pāṭhā, viḍaṅga and ginger; or combine cardamom, kuṭaja seeds, śābara lodhra, turmeric and barberry; or combine kuṭaja bark, ativiṣā, ginger and nutgrass. Of these six ripening formulas, give a decoction of any one to drink. Or else take any one of these formulas ground to a fine powder, boil it in clean water, or put it in beer or sour gruel, and give it to drink. Or else take the three hot spices, ativiṣā, asafoetida, sweet flag, black salt and chebulic myrobalan, ground to a fine powder, put it in boiled water and give it to drink; this dispels even severe unripe diarrhea. Or else take sweet flag, bilva, long pepper, ginger, costus, caraway, kulaka and viḍaṅga, ground to a fine powder, put it in boiled water and drink it; the diarrhea will stop.

Now I shall explain the procedure for ripe diarrhea. When the heat of the stomach is weak and ripe diarrhea comes again and again, one should quickly carry out the procedure of binding medicines. For one afflicted by diarrhea arising from phlegm, one should eat food with a broth well prepared with pūtika, the three hot spices, bilva, trutruk tré, buttermilk, pomegranate and asafoetida. Combine clematis, ativiṣā, costus, pāṭhā and putsé shel; or combine chebulic myrobalan, nutgrass, ginger, bilva and karkaṭikā; or combine trutruk tré, long pepper, long pepper root and great long pepper; or combine viḍaṅga, sweet flag, shangshing, coriander seeds and the three hot spices. To give a decoction of any one of these four formulas boiled together is also good; it produces digestive fire and strength. Or else a warm decoction of chebulic myrobalan, trutruk tré, pāṭhā, sweet flag, long pepper root, kuṭaja and ginger, or else a powder made of them as medicine, dispels diarrhea arising from phlegm.

Grind one srang each of the root of the aṅkoṭha tree and of barberry, knead it with rainwater, make it into pills of about two zho each, and administer them as an enema; this dispels every kind of diarrhea. Take the powders of bilva, nutgrass, dhātaka flowers, pāṭhā and ginger, and the juice of plantain fruit, in equal parts, put them in molasses mixed with buttermilk, and drink it; this too stops diarrhea.

Now I shall explain the procedure for diarrhea arising from wind. After blood and stool have come out, the stool is held back by the force of wind, and mucus like froth comes out with griping; the name of this disease is "passing mucus with straining" (pravāhikā). For it, if the digestive fire is strong, let the patient drink a decoction of trutruk tré and bilva boiled in milk, mixed with molasses and sesame oil; the obstruction of the stool will then be eased. If one puts powdered long pepper, or else powdered black pepper, into milk that has been boiled and taken off the fire, and drinks it for three days, even long-standing passing of mucus with straining is dispelled. If one combines sesame oil, butter, honey, sugar, ginger and molasses water, stirs them and drinks this, passing of mucus with straining is likewise dispelled at once. If one drinks the powders of dhātaka flowers, gyashuk leaves, the kapittha tree, drupmar chang and lodhra mixed with curd, this too dispels the distress of passing mucus with straining. If one combines the pulp of the bilva fruit, molasses, lodhra, sesame oil and black pepper and eats them, the exhaustion caused by passing mucus with straining is quickly dispelled, and one becomes well.

If even this does not calm it, the procedure for an enema of licorice and sesame oil is this. Cook and wrap the leaves of the śālmali tree, cover them with clay, and place them in smoldering embers. When the clay has dried and the leaves are cooked, pound the leaves finely in a mortar into a powder, and, together with butter and powdered licorice, knead it into thirty-two srang of milk that has been boiled and taken off the fire. This is called the slimy enema medicine (picchābasti). If it is given as an enema, it dispels fever and diarrhea arising from bile, destroys tumors and chronic fever, and removes disease of the stomach and swelling.

Now I shall explain the procedure for curing impaired stomach fire. When the digestive fire has dwindled, the humors disturb the stomach fire, and from this comes the disease of disturbed stomach fire (grahaṇī). It is of four kinds: arising from wind, bile and phlegm individually, and arising from all of them combined. Their signs and the methods of curing them are like those of diarrhea. Thus the signs of disturbed stomach fire arising from wind, and the methods of curing it, are like the signs of diarrhea arising from wind and the methods of curing it. Those arising from bile, from phlegm and from all combined should likewise each be matched accordingly.

The method for producing digestive fire is this: if one makes the medicine ajamodā, trutruk tré, clematis, the three hot spices, rock salt, the two alkalis explained earlier, long pepper root and asafoetida into pills with the juice of the citron fruit and eats them, this produces digestive fire. If one grinds triphalā, gajé fruit, the three hot spices, rock salt, black salt and sauvarcala salt, puts them into dzeu khajar and drinks it, this dispels the disease of pale swelling, stomach disease, tumors, and stabbing pains. If one kneads the powders of the medicine lalaphü, the three hot spices, rock salt, the two kinds of caraway and asafoetida with clarified butter and eats it before meals, it dispels wind and produces digestive fire. Take the powders of śaṭhī, coriander seeds, pāṭhā, bilva, trutruk tré, ginger, lalaphü, long pepper root and gyashuk fruit, together with butter, curd four times the amount of the butter, and juice of the herb cāṅgerī four times the amount of the butter; boil these until only the butter remains, and drink as much as is suitable. It dispels stomach disease, hemorrhoids, prolapse of the anus, retention of urine, swelling of the belly, passing of mucus with straining, difficulty in breathing, the disease of pale swelling, the disease of thirst, cough, and violent vomiting that cannot be stopped, and it whets the appetite.

For the disease of disturbed stomach fire arising from bile: if one puts the powders of barberry extract, ativiṣā, bilva, jitser bark, jitser fruit, nutgrass, pāṭhā, dhātakī, putsé shel and ginger into rainwater mixed with honey and drinks it, this dispels hemorrhoids, passing of mucus with straining, blood disease, pain in the kidneys and loins, and diseases of the anus. Take kuṭaja wood, the three hot spices, nutgrass, tsatri and putsé shel in equal parts, trutruk tré at twice that portion, and kuṭaja bark at sixteen times that portion. If one mixes the powder of these with rice-washing water and drinks it, this dispels stomach disease, jaundice, the disease of pale swelling, urinary disease, loss of appetite, diarrhea, tumors, wasting disease, and fever.

For the disease of disturbed stomach fire arising from phlegm: if one puts the powders of the medicine śaṭhī, the three hot spices, chebulic myrobalan, the two alkalis, long pepper root, citron fruit and rock salt into water that has been boiled and taken off the fire and drinks it, the disease will subside. Boil madhūka flowers, strain the decoction and let it cool, and add to it a quarter as much honey. Pour this into a jar whose inside has been smeared with a mixture of lotus root, aguru and sesame oil, and keep it in barley for one month. If one then drinks it in suitable amounts, it produces digestive fire. Smear the inside of an earthen pot with a mixture of long pepper root, trutruk tré, chebulic myrobalan, long pepper and viḍaṅga, pour buttermilk into it, and keep it in barley for one month. If one then drinks it in suitable amounts, it dispels stomach disease, hemorrhoids, cough, tumors and worms.

Thus, for one struck by the disease of disturbed stomach fire, in brief, one should judge by its severity and the like, draw very careful distinctions, and then give, as appropriate, food and drink that produce digestive fire, medicinal powders, medicinal decoctions to drink, medicinal butters and so on.

Because worm disease arises from diarrhea and from the disease of disturbed stomach fire, I shall next, at this point, explain the kinds of worm disease, their signs, and the methods of curing them. The signs of worm disease are these: falling ill with fever, a poor complexion, pain in the ribs, pain in the heart, heaviness of the body, dizziness, finding food unappetizing when one sees it, and diarrhea. These are signs that worms have arisen in the belly.

The methods of curing it are these. If one takes the medicine viḍaṅga, rock salt, the alkali of burnt barley, kampillaka and chebulic myrobalan, ground to a fine powder, mixes it with buttermilk and drinks it, this dispels all worms. Boil śigru, barberry, mūṣaparṇī, nutgrass and triphalā; if one puts powdered long pepper and viḍaṅga into the decoction, mixes it and drinks it, this too dispels worms. If one eats powdered mūṣaparṇī with a broth of roasted rice and afterward drinks sour gruel, this too purges worm disease. If one eats powdered viḍaṅga mixed with honey, this too dispels worms. If one drinks the juice of the leaves of the pāribhadraka tree mixed with honey, this too dispels worms. Or else boil the powders of triphalā, sweet flag, dantī root and kampillaka in butter and in cow's urine four times the amount of the butter; when the cow's urine has boiled away, strain off the dregs and drink the butter in suitable amounts; this too dispels worms. The seventh chapter, on curing diarrhea, is complete.

The disease of hemorrhage (raktapitta) arises as follows. Through exposure to the heat of the sun, fire and the like, and through eating food and the like that is too hot, sour things, salt, medicines with sharp alkalis, and things of pungent taste, bile becomes disturbed; from this, the blood residing in the liver and spleen is expelled, and it comes out both upward and downward.

The signs of hemorrhage arising from wind are these: the blood is bluish gray, red, thin and frothy. The signs of hemorrhage arising from bile are these: the blood is like the color of cloth dyed black, smoky, like the color of cow's urine, or like the color of barberry extract. The signs of hemorrhage arising from phlegm are these: the blood is oily, slightly pale, sticky and thick. The signs of hemorrhage arising from two humors combined are mixed. The signs of hemorrhage arising from all three humors combined include all the signs of each.

Hemorrhage arising from a single humor that comes out upward is easy to cure. Hemorrhage arising from a single humor or from two humors combined that comes out downward can only be managed by the procedures of food and medicine so that it does not flare up. Hemorrhage arising from all three humors combined, whether it comes out upward or downward, is very hard to cure. When, as a result of hemorrhage, there is vomiting, fainting, fever, difficulty in breathing, cough, loss of voice and heat of the body, and when the blood is like the fruit of the jambu tree, like a rainbow, and stinks like a corpse, there is no means of curing it.

In one who has strength and is able to eat, if the disease of bad blood is stopped from the very start, it turns into diseases such as throat disease and goiter, tumors, hemorrhoids, fever and leprosy. Therefore one should not stop it early. For hemorrhage, first carry out the procedure of fasting as far as one is able. For drink, give a decoction of white sandalwood, putsé shel, solo and nutgrass boiled together.

For hemorrhage that comes out upward, one should first strengthen the body and give a purgative. For that which comes out downward, in accordance with the patient's strength, one should first carry out the procedure of rice gruel and the procedure of emesis. To strengthen the body, combine butter, honey, roasted rice flour and sugar, add emblic myrobalan and pomegranate to make it sour, and give it to drink. As a purgative, put the powder of any one of these, the pulp of cassia fistula, emblic myrobalan, nyerong wood or chebulic myrobalan, into water that has been boiled and cooled, mix it with sugar and honey, and administer it. As an emetic, a cooled decoction of nutgrass, kuṭaja and licorice mixed with the pulp of posöncha, milk and honey is supreme for dispelling the disease of hemorrhage; it should be drunk.

For those whose flesh is wasted, whose strength is exhausted, for children, for the old, and for those with wasting disease, emesis and purgatives are not suitable; one should carry out the procedure for stopping the flow of blood. The procedure for stopping the flow of blood is this: combine paṭola, jasmine flowers, nimba, the two kinds of sandalwood and juniper; or combine red Mön lentils, vāsaka, lodhra, black earth and madayantikā; or combine kakkola, ramnyé root, the two kinds of taltré, licorice and nyeushing root; or combine drégang, emblic myrobalan, dhātaka flowers, kuṭaja bark and solo. A decoction of any one of these four formulas, in equal parts, cooled and mixed with sugar and honey, is supreme for quickly dispelling the disease of hemorrhage; it should be drunk very cold. If one puts the finely ground powder of priyaṅgu, barberry extract, baked earth and lodhra into either the pounded juice or the cooled decoction of the herb vāsaka, mixed with sugar and honey, and drinks it, this too dispels the disease of hemorrhage. If one puts the powder of any one of crocodile claw, juniper, gandhapriyaṅgu, lodhra and red ochre into rainwater mixed with sugar and drinks it, this too stops the flow of blood.

If one eats the powdered flowers of khadira, saptaparṇa, pārtha, śālmali and kovidāra mixed with honey, this too dispels hemorrhage. If one eats any one of the ripe fruits of udumbara, tampala, chebulic myrobalan, drago and grapes mixed with honey, this too dispels the disease of hemorrhage.

Take a fresh kuṣmāṇḍa fruit, peel off its skin, throw away the seeds inside as well, and cut the flesh in between into pieces. Roast it, and boil eight srang of it together with thirty-two srang of good clarified butter. Into this put the finely ground powder of the three called the three fragrances, namely cinnamon, gandhapatra and cardamom, together with coriander seeds and black pepper, half a srang of each of these, and two srang each of long pepper, ginger and caraway. Let it cool, mix it with honey half the amount of the clarified butter, and eat it; this too dispels the disease of hemorrhage. It also destroys rupture of the lungs, consumptive wasting disease of the lungs, the disease of difficult breathing, palpitation of the heart, the one called "the smotherer," fever, the disease of thirst, cough and vomiting. It benefits the lungs, builds flesh, produces virility and strength, gives a good complexion, and makes the voice pleasant.

If one boils any one of these in milk and drinks it, grapes, the four called "the milky ones," namely śālaparṇī, pṛśniparṇī, mudgaparṇī and māṣaparṇī, bālā, ginger, gokṣura and nyeushing root, this too dispels blood disease. Take the powders of sugar, elavāluka, dūrvā grass, the two kinds of sandalwood, nutgrass, juniper, the filaments of the utpala flower, madder and putsé shel in equal parts, and boil them in goat's butter, milk four times the amount of the butter, and rainwater, until half the butter remains. If one uses this in any way, whether drinking it, giving it as an enema, rubbing it on the body or pouring it into the nose, this too dispels the disease of hemorrhage. Take vidārī, licorice, the two medās, the fruit of the dinatali tree, pomegranate, nyeushing, the herb kakkola and the pulp of the citron, in equal parts, ground to a fine powder, and boil it in butter, milk four times the amount of the butter, and water, until half the butter remains. If one eats as much of this as is suitable, it dispels fever, destroys obstruction of the stool, swelling of the belly and stabbing pain, and removes cough and the disease of hemorrhage.

For bleeding from the nose: if one takes the strained powder of any one of conch shell, red ochre, dhātaka flowers and licorice, mixes it with woman's milk and pours it into the nose, the bleeding will be stopped. If one pours into the nose any one of these, pomegranate juice, the juice of dūrvā grass, the pulp of the logkok tree, or the juice of kündong, this too stops the flow of blood from the nose.

For hemorrhage that comes out downward, one should act according to the procedure for blood fever explained earlier. For hemorrhage that comes from the male organ, one should act according to the procedure for urinary disease arising from bile, which will be given in detail below. For hemorrhage that comes from a woman's female organ, giving milk mixed with sugar and honey to drink is beneficial, and it will subside. Or else, if one puts the powder of any one of red ochre, the leaves of the jambu tree and utpala root into rice-washing water mixed with honey and drinks it, the blood that comes from the female organ will also be stopped. Or else, if one mixes the powder of any one of the medicine ananda, gandhapriyaṅgu, sandalwood and nāgakesara with beer and drinks it, the blood that comes from the female organ will also be stopped. Or else, if one mixes the powdered root of red Mön lentils with honey and barberry extract in rice-washing water, stirs it and drinks it, this too dispels every kind of bleeding from the female organ. Or else, if one boils any one of the herb bunnawa, śāluka, small pulses and alambuṣā, and fumigates the female organ with it, this too dispels the illness. Again, grind mudgaparṇī and cook it together with sesame oil; if one soaks silk or cotton in this and places it inside the female organ, this too stops the bleeding and makes it soft and comfortable.

In general, for one who has the disease of hemorrhage, entering cold water, pouring cold water over the body and the like are wholesome. As for food, śāli rice, Mön lentils and the like, and cooling meats of wild game and birds, are good. The seventh chapter, on curing the disease of excess blood, is complete.

Next I shall explain the chapter on curing the great wasting disease. The great wasting disease (rājayakṣmā) arises from four bases, that is, from these four causes: overexertion, the disease of depletion, holding back feces and urine, and eating at improper times. Its signs are these eleven: loss of voice, cough, difficulty in breathing, loss of appetite, fever, headache, pain in the shoulders, pain in the sides, pain in the heart, pain in the kidneys and loins, and vomiting of blood and phlegm. One who is distressed by these signs, whose flesh is wasted and who has no strength, should be given up. Any other, if medicines are available, should be treated by the procedures quickly. One from whose mouth come pus-like matter and red, bluish gray, green, blue or yellow phlegm, who has difficulty in breathing, who is exhausted and distressed by cough, and whose voice has failed, will not live. When nearly all food turns into nothing but feces and urine, the flesh is dried up and the body's vital essence is exhausted, so that the life force is sustained only by the feces present inside, one should not purge what is inside, but always preserve it.

For one who has strength and suffers from the great wasting disease, the curative actions to be carried out are these five: emesis, purgation, pouring medicine into the nose, a strong purgative enema, and an unctuous enema. If these five actions are carried out on one whose bodily flesh is exhausted, they become like poison and must not be done. As food for one suffering from the great wasting disease, śāli rice, ripened sixty-day rice, wheat, barley, Mön lentils and the like are wholesome.

As drink, good beer is beneficial. As for meat, the flesh of birds and of wild game that agrees with that disease is suitable.

Its medicine is this: if one eats a powder of long pepper, grapes and sugar mixed with sesame oil, ghee and honey, or else a powder of aśvagandhā, long pepper and sugar mixed with honey and clarified butter, it dispels the great wasting disease.

Or else take these combinations: bubran wood, grapes, long pepper, chebulic myrobalan, drago and jitser combined together; or molasses, emblic myrobalan, parched rice, truktruk tré, long pepper and dried ginger combined together; or elecampane, lizurwa, ramnyewa, sugar and jiu rukpa combined together. If one eats the powder of any one of these medicinal combinations, mixed with ghee and honey in increased proportion, it dispels cough and difficult breathing, and a blocked voice is cleared.

Take tālīśa leaves, black pepper, dried ginger and long pepper in ascending order, each increased by one part over the first; cinnamon and cardamom, each half the amount of the tālīśa; and sugar, eight times the long pepper. A powder medicine made by grinding these and combining them together dispels cough, difficult breathing, loss of appetite, disease of the spleen, fever, wasting disease and weakness of the stomach's digestive heat, and it benefits the heart; it also dispels diarrhea, tumors, hemorrhoids and vomiting.

Take dried ginger, long pepper, black pepper, nāgakesara flowers, cinnamon and cardamom, in descending order, each increased by one part over the last; and sugar equal to the combined amount of all of them. A powder made by grinding these and combining them benefits a blocked throat, and it dispels pain in the heart, tumors, and the misery of hemorrhoids.

Take cinnamon, cardamom, long pepper, bamboo manna and sugar in ascending order, each doubling the part before it, starting from the first, and grind them. This powder, mixed with honey and ghee, dispels pain in the sides of the ribs, difficult breathing and cough, and it whets the appetite.

If one eats a powder of nyeushing, vidārī, aśvagandhā, chebulic myrobalan, punarnavā, balā, atibalā, nāgabalā and caltrop, mixed with ghee and honey, it brings to an end the consuming great wasting disease.

Whoever eats shilajit, honey, the three pungents, drubmar chang and iron powder mixed together, and takes as food cooked rice with milk, will quickly be cured of the great wasting disease.

Eating honey, drubmar chang, vidaṅga, shilajit, iron powder, ghee and chebulic myrobalan mixed together, while eating food that is suitable and wholesome, dispels even the terrible great wasting disease.

Or else, one who has the great wasting disease should eat sugar, honey and fresh butter mixed together, or else ghee and honey mixed in unequal parts, and take as food cooked rice with milk; if one does so, the flesh becomes firm.

Boil the juice of the root of the medicinal plant nirguṇḍī, pounded together with its leaves, with a quarter of that amount of ghee. If, once only the ghee remains, one drinks it in the proper measure, it restores vigor and strength to one whose bodily vital essences have been exhausted by the great wasting disease, makes the body firm, and dispels every disease.

Take elecampane, lizurwa, guna, balā, utpala flowers, long pepper, jhaṭa, jīvantī, licorice, vyāghrī, dog's tongue, jitser, the bark of kuṭaja and caltrop in equal parts, and grind them. Boil the powder together with four times as much ghee and with water four times the ghee, until only the ghee remains, and strain it. If one drinks it in the proper measure, mixed with a nourishing broth, or with meat soup, or with boiled milk, or with any decoction such as one of asafoetida, it dispels the great wasting disease with its eleven signs.

Boil goat's ghee, goat's milk, goat's curd, goat's urine and the liquid of goat droppings with the five kinds of salt. If, once only the ghee remains, one drinks it in the proper measure, and afterward drinks goat's milk, it dispels the great wasting disease. Eating food made of barley, sleeping in a goat pen, drinking goat's milk, rubbing the body with goat droppings and urine, and bathing will also dispel the consuming great wasting disease.

Boil thirty-two sang of sesame oil with four times that amount of milk and one sang of licorice powder. When the milk is used up, strain the sesame oil and make it clear and free of impurities. Again boil it with four times the milk and one sang of licorice powder, and when the milk is used up, strain the sesame oil. Adding milk and licorice as before, boil it in this way with great care a hundred times. If this sesame oil is used as a drink, poured into the nose, anointed on the body and given as an enema, it dispels the great wasting disease, heart pain, wind disorders and pale swelling, and it does away with the diseases above the collarbones, madness, the disease of coughing blood, and erysipelas.

Take one sang each of the following: the five roots beginning with bilva; nutgrass; balā; the four leaves, namely śālaparṇī, pṛśniparṇī, mudgaparṇī and māṣaparṇī; the herb ṛddhi; long pepper; lizurwa; chebulic myrobalan; jīvaka; zangtsiwa; guḍūcī; grapes; bunnawa; medā; jīvantī; kākanāsikā; utpala flowers; cardamom; jada; bubran wood; butsha to; jitser; sandalwood; vidārī; caltrop; vyāghrī; and elecampane; together with five hundred emblic myrobalan fruits. Boil these in sixty-four measures of water, each measure reckoned at eight sang. When one quarter of the water remains, strain it. Into the liquid pour twelve sang of sesame oil and clarified butter in equal halves, and fifty sang of sugar, and boil it again until it becomes just thick enough to be stirred. Take it off the fire and let it cool. Into it put two sang of long pepper powder, four sang of bamboo manna, six sang of honey, and one sang of what are called the four fragrances, namely cinnamon, gandhapatra, cardamom and nāgakesara, combined in equal parts. The name of the medicinal paste made by combining these is the Medicine of the Sage Cyavana. If one eats it in the proper measure, it dispels the misery of heart pain, difficult breathing, the disease of thirst, cough, dreg and the consuming great wasting disease; it makes the mind keen, the voice pleasing, and gives virility, and it becomes a rejuvenating elixir.

Take fifteen sang each of wood-apple fruit and jishal, thirty-two sang of fresh emblic myrobalan, or sixteen sang if dried, half of that of chebulic myrobalan, and two sang each of long pepper, elavāluka, sengtrom, vidaṅga and black pepper, and boil them in sixty-four measures of water. When one quarter remains, strain the liquid and let it cool, and into it pour two hundred sang of molasses. If one drinks this mixture, it dispels hemorrhoids, the great wasting disease and leprosy; it does away with swelling, pale swelling, fever, disease of the spleen, heart pain, pain in the kidneys and lower back, and tumors; and it dispels disease of the stomach, jaundice, blotches of the skin, worms and loss of appetite.

If on top of the great wasting disease another serious illness such as fever arises, then for whichever of them is curable one should perform its own method of treatment. In short, for one who has the drying disease, the great wasting disease, one should make the body firm through bathing the body, applying fragrances and the like, and one should build up vigor and strength.

This is the eighth chapter, on treating the great wasting disease.

Tumors arise when wind and the other humors are greatly disturbed through faults of diet and faults of conduct such as sitting and lying. They lie within the abdomen like lumps and are of five kinds. The sites of these five are the right and left sides, the heart, and the bladder. Further, I shall teach their signs together with the methods of treatment.

All tumors are to be recognized by these signs: loss of appetite, retention of feces and urine, a preponderance of wind, disturbance of the intestines, distension of the belly, and frequent belching.

The signs of a tumor arising from wind are taught to be these: stabbing and piercing pain, distension of the belly, obstruction of feces and urine, the tumor itself throbbing, shifting its place and changing in size, stabbing pain in the nape of the neck, the head and the jaws, and aching of the frame.

The signs of a tumor arising from bile are taught to be these: the body is hot, there is great thirst, dizziness, sweating, sour belching and a bitter taste in the mouth, and the tumor itself cannot bear to be touched.

The signs of a tumor arising from phlegm are these: much saliva, loss of appetite, heat in the chest with water coming from the mouth, difficult breathing, cough, inability to digest food, the tumor itself hard and protruding outward, and vomiting.

The signs of a tumor arising from the combined humors are these: the tumor itself is unbearably painful and burning hot, hard like stone and protruding outward, and it impairs the mind, the body and the digestive heat. It is incurable.

A woman's blood tumor arises from miscarriage, from childbirth and from the flow of menstrual blood. The tumor itself is painful and burning hot, and she comes to resemble a pregnant woman.

When one is afflicted by a tumor that protrudes outward like a tortoise, with the veins standing out, and is also burdened by many other serious diseases, and the region of the heart, the area around the navel, and the hands and feet are swollen and it has become unbearable, such a case is to be abandoned.

For a tumor arising from wind, one should first give medicated ghee to drink. After that one should treat it with powder medicines, anointing the body with oil, fomentation, a strong purgative enema and an unctuous enema.

The medicated ghee is this: boil the ghee of vidaṅga, the three fruits, the three pungents, yimo leaves, coriander fruit and truktruk tré in milk and water, and if, once only the ghee remains, one drinks it in the proper measure, it dispels a tumor arising from wind.

Take two zho each of the powders of black salt, the ash of scorched barley, sweet flag, putsé shel, chebulic myrobalan, truktruk tré, vidaṅga and the three pungents, and boil them in thirty-two sang of ghee, an equal amount of milk, and water four times the ghee. If, once only the ghee remains, one drinks it in the proper measure, this too forcefully dispels the pain of a tumor arising from wind, and dispels hiccups, difficult breathing, worms, disease of the spleen and cough.

Take one sang each of the powders of dried ginger, yimong, truktruk tré, long pepper, the ash of scorched barley and rock salt, and boil them in the decoction of the medicine tsiri and of bilva, with thirty-two sang of ghee, until only the ghee remains. This is supreme for dispelling tumors, dry constipation, pale swelling, difficult breathing, disease of the stomach and cough, and for pacifying fever, severe head colds, disease of the spleen and hemorrhoids.

Take the powders of asafoetida, starbu, the three pungents, jiu rukpa, cardamom, sweet flag, tsapru salt, black salt, coriander fruit, caraway, the ash of scorched barley, Nepalese pomegranate, elecampane, ajagandhā, lizurwa, truktruk tré and lalaphu, and boil them in ghee and four times as much curd, until only the ghee remains. This dispels tumors arising from wind, stabbing pain, and distension of the belly.

Take the powders of spama fruit, cardamom, truktruk tré, the three pungents, yimong, lalaphu, rock salt, caraway and long pepper root, with a decoction of juniper berries and radish boiled together that has turned sour, and boil them in curd, and in ghee together with milk. When only the ghee remains, it dispels tumors, stabbing pain and obstruction of the feces, and it does away with disorders of the womb, inability to digest food, hemorrhoids, difficult breathing, heart pain and pain in the sides of the ribs.

If these very medicines that were taught for combining with ghee are finely ground and drunk mixed with any one of the following, namely boiled water, whey, beer, sour gruel or salts, they likewise dispel tumors, stabbing pain and the like.

Take asafoetida, sweet flag, tsapru salt, dried ginger, lalaphu, chebulic myrobalan, truktruk tré and costus in ascending order, each increased by one part over the first, and grind them. If one drinks the powder mixed with any of the liquids mentioned above, such as beer, it is said to dispel tumors, distension of the belly and dropsy, to overcome stabbing pain, hemorrhoids, difficult breathing and cough, and to kindle the heat of the stomach.

If one drinks the powders of asafoetida, khalu, starbu, Nepalese pomegranate, rock salt and tsapru salt mixed with good beer, this too dispels tumors arising from wind.

If one drinks the powders of tsapru salt, black salt, starbu, the ash of scorched barley and asafoetida mixed with any of the liquids such as whey, it relieves those afflicted by tumors, stabbing pain and obstruction of the feces.

Take two zho each of the powders of vidaṅga, bṛhatī, dantī root, the milky latex of snuhī, the three fruits and rock salt, and boil them in eight sang of ghee and water four times the ghee. When only the ghee remains, drink half a sang of it each day, mixed with meat soup, and purge downward. If one then eats food that includes meat, tumors, dry constipation, leprosy, hemorrhoids, impaired digestive heat and dropsy will disappear.

Take one sang each of nyerong wood, the milky latex of snuhī, emblic myrobalan, bālaka and kampillaka, and half a sang of rock salt, and boil the powders of these in eight sang of ghee and water four times the ghee. What remains when only the ghee is left is called Dhibindu Ghee. If one drinks two zho of it each day, followed by warm boiled water, and purges downward, it overcomes every kind of tumor and all dropsy.

For one who has a tumor arising from bile, one should first give any one of the medicated ghees, namely the medicated ghee of butsha to and the like, the medicated ghee called Mahātikta, or the medicated ghee of vāsaka and the like. After the belly has been softened, one should give a purgative, and after that a nourishing enema.

Boil a decoction of any one of the groups taught earlier, the group of nyagrodha and the rest or the group of utpala and the rest, with ghee, putting into it the powders of the ten medicines called the life-giving ones, taught earlier. If, once only the ghee remains, one drinks it in the proper measure, it dispels a tumor of blood and cough arising from bile.

Boil powdered chebulic myrobalan with ghee four times the chebulic myrobalan and a decoction of emblic myrobalan four times the ghee. If, once only the ghee remains, one drinks it in the proper measure, this too quickly dispels a tumor arising from bile.

Or else, for one who has a tumor of bile, one should give to drink a decoction of the three fruits into which powdered nyerong wood has been put, or else give sugar and kampillaka mixed with honey, and purge downward.

The remedies for a tumor arising from phlegm are these: one should treat it with fomentation, fomentation by binding, drinking medicated ghee, sharp purgatives, a sharp unctuous enema and a sharp strong purgative enema, and also with the measures taught earlier in the procedure for tumors of wind.

Take one sang each of the powders of the medicines called the heat-kindling ones, namely dried ginger, long pepper, long pepper root, truktruk tré, yimong and the ash of scorched barley, and boil them in thirty-two sang each of ghee and milk and water four times the ghee. What remains when only the ghee is left is called Six-Sang Ghee. If one drinks it in the proper measure, this too dispels a tumor arising from phlegm. It overcomes disease of the stomach, the disease of pale swelling, disease of the spleen, cough, difficult breathing and fever, and it does away with swelling and dry constipation.

If one drinks in the proper measure the ghee that remains when the ground powder of the six heat-kindling medicines mentioned above is boiled in a decoction of the group of dongka and the rest with ghee, or else the ghee combined with long pepper and the rest, explained earlier, this too dispels a tumor arising from phlegm.

If one drinks the powders of black salt, truktruk tré and asafoetida mixed with buttermilk, this too kindles the digestive heat. If one drinks the powders of tsapru salt and lalaphu mixed with buttermilk, this too agrees with phlegm and wind.

When blood in a woman's womb has turned into a tumor, and ten months have passed so that it has proved to be not a child but a tumor, one should soften the belly by giving medicated ghee to drink and by fomentation, then perform the procedure of oleation and give a purgative.

If one drinks the powders of śaṭī, the bark of tsiri bilwa, pine, gi and long pepper mixed with a decoction of sesame, this too dispels a tumor arising from the blood of the womb. If one drinks a decoction of sesame into which have been put molasses, the three pungents, clarified butter and the powder of gi, this too dispels a blood tumor.

For a woman whose menses do not come, put the ash of kiṃśuka wood into a decoction of emblic myrobalan, boil it, and give it to her to drink. Or else, if she drinks the liquid of scorched barley ash and the powder of the three pungents mixed with beer, this too dispels a blood tumor.

When a blood tumor has accumulated inside and the blood of the womb flows heavily and does not stop, one should stop it by the procedure for coughing blood taught earlier and the procedure for dispelling wind disease taught earlier, and also calm the pain.

For diseases arising from wind and the other humors that cannot be cured by these means, a physician who has seen this done before and is practiced in it should let blood from the vein of the arm, and should apply cautery to tumors arising from phlegm and wind.

In short, one with a tumor should avoid heavy food and whatever gives rise to phlegm, and should always protect the digestive heat and his vigor and strength. Food and drink should be eaten in amounts judged by whether the condition is severe or mild.

This completes the ninth chapter, on treating tumors.

Next I shall explain the chapter on treating dropsy. Dropsy arises when, because the digestive heat is weak and unwholesome food is eaten, the bases of disease are disturbed, and when one drinks cold water while the body is sweating, so that the channels and the outlets of the body are blocked. From this the terrible disease of dropsy arises in eight kinds.

The signs of dropsy arising from wind are said to be these: stabbing pain in the belly, pain as if it would burst, sounds arising within, swelling of the hands, feet and testicles, the appearance of dark wrinkles, and veins standing out.

The signs of dropsy arising from bile are said to be these: the flesh of the belly is soft to the touch, one is struck by fever, there is sweating, great thirst, heat of the body and dizziness, and the belly appears covered with blue and yellow veins.

The signs of dropsy arising from phlegm are to be known as these: much saliva, heaviness of the body, difficult breathing, heavy sleep, swelling, loss of appetite, being struck by fever, difficulty in curing it, hardness of the belly, thickness of the skin, and the belly covered with white veins.

Dropsy arising from the combined humors comes from eating hair, nails and the like that have been mixed into food in order to bring a woman under one's control, and also from poisons that bring about miscarriage or that ruin the body. It is to be known as having all the signs.

Dropsy that arises on the right side is called dropsy of the liver; that which arises on the left side is called dropsy of the spleen. Both of these arise from a disturbance of phlegm and blood, and they come about from the combination of bile and phlegm.

When one eats food mixed with bird feathers and hair, the intestines become blocked and feces do not come out. The dropsy that arises from this, forming inside between the heart and the navel, is hard to evacuate, and it is called dropsy of the obstructed anus.

If one eats food containing sharp splinters, the intestines are pierced from within, and the juice of the food leaks out and collects inside, so that stabbing pain arises below the navel. This is called dropsy of the perforated intestines, and its signs resemble those explained earlier, such as the sign of stabbing pain in the bladder.

What is called water dropsy is this: when, after one has drunk oil or been given an emetic or a purgative, one drinks cold water and it turns into disease, the belly becomes round and large, glossy in color, and like a full leather water bag.

Among these eight kinds of dropsy, two, dropsy of the perforated intestines and dropsy of the obstructed anus, are incurable. The rest are said to be difficult to cure, and among them too, a case that has turned into water dropsy is to be abandoned.

For dropsy arising from wind, one should do the following in order. Give the ground powder of the group of medicines beginning with sthirā, and medicated ghee boiled with it, to drink; apply fomentation; and give an oily purgative. When the belly has subsided, bind it with cloth so that it does not swell again. Anoint the belly with medicines combined to suit that disease, and over this apply a binding fomentation using a covering made of a warm, soft-haired skin that is not rotten, or if there is none, a covering of silk floss, or a sheepskin. Give an unctuous enema of powdered truktruk tré boiled together with sesame oil, and a strong purgative enema combined with a decoction of the group of medicines beginning with sthirā. Let the patient eat food together with milk, a nourishing broth or meat soup, whichever is suitable.

For dropsy arising from bile, first have the patient drink an unctuous preparation of the powder of sweet-tasting medicines boiled with ghee. After that give a purgative of the powders of nyerong wood and the three fruits boiled in ghee. Or else boil a decoction of the group beginning with the nyagrodha tree with ghee, and when only the ghee remains, combine it with honey and sugar and give it as a strong purgative enema. That too should be accompanied by a prior unctuous enema. The unctuous enema is this: into a decoction of the group beginning with nyagrodha, put the powder of that same group of medicines and boil it together with sesame oil, and when only the sesame oil remains, strain it and give it. Then apply a binding fomentation with thick rice-milk porridge. As food, too, boil a decoction of the group of medicines beginning with sthirā with milk, and let the patient eat with what remains when only the milk is left.

For dropsy arising from phlegm, first give the unctuous medicated ghee combined with long pepper and the rest, explained earlier, and after fomentation has been applied, give a purgative of ghee boiled with the milky latex of the snuhī tree. Then give a strong purgative enema of a decoction of the group of medicines beginning with muṣkaka combined with sesame oil, cow's urine and the powder of the three pungents, and also carry out the procedure of the unctuous enema. The unctuous enema is this: boil together a decoction of that same group of medicines, sesame oil and the powder of the three pungents, and when only the sesame oil remains, strain it and give it. Then apply a binding fomentation made of yeast, radish seed and white mustard seed combined. As food, let the patient eat, in measured amounts, a broth of peas or milk with the three pungents.

For dropsy arising from the combined humors, one should apply all the procedures taught earlier for the kinds mentioned above, beginning with dropsy arising from wind. Or else, for this one, one may also give a purgative of the powders of bok and śaṅkhinī boiled together with ghee.

For one with dropsy of the spleen as well, one should carry out the procedures of the unctuous enema, fomentation, purgatives and so forth, and should also let blood from the vein on the inner side of the left elbow.

Pound vidaṅga, truktruk tré, rock salt, flour and sweet flag coarsely in equal parts, smear them with ghee and roast them in a sealed vessel. If one grinds this finely and drinks it mixed with milk, it dispels tumors and dropsy of the spleen. Or else, give to drink a decoction of white pepper mixed with the powders of rock salt, truktruk tré and long pepper, or else a decoction of white pepper mixed with the ash of palāśa wood and the ash of scorched barley.

If one drinks the powders of rohitaka and chebulic myrobalan steeped in cow's urine or in water, this too dispels all dropsy, disease of the spleen, urinary disease, hemorrhoids, worms and tumors. Or else, one should drink rock salt, or the ash of burnt nyachip, mixed with milk in the proper amount. Or else, for dropsy of the spleen, give powdered long pepper mixed with milk to drink.

All that was taught in the procedure for treating dropsy of the spleen should also be given for dropsy of the liver. Or else, for dropsy of the liver, one should let blood from the vein on the inner side of the right elbow.

Since in the dropsies the impurities of the belly, such as feces, are predominant, purgatives are taught to be given many times.

Next, after that, I shall also teach what is called the general procedure for dropsy. For dropsy, one should drink castor oil many times, mixed with either milk or cow's urine. Or else give sesame oil mixed with asafoetida and the ash of svarjikā, or the powder of the medicine jyotiṣka mixed with milk.

Boil one sang of the milky latex of snuhī and six sang of powdered nyerong wood in thirty-two sang of ghee and milk eight times the ghee, until only the ghee remains. If one strains it and drinks it in the proper measure, this too dispels dropsy and tumors.

If one drinks the powders of the three fruits, janu, pok, shidrö, sweet flag, dog's tongue, spama fruit, putsé shel, nyerong wood, rock salt and long pepper, mixed with any one of cow's urine, warm boiled water or meat soup, this too dispels all the afflictions of tumors, dropsy, disease of the spleen, leprosy, hemorrhoids, blood fever and puffy swelling.

If one drinks the powders of the medicine viśālā, khini wood, dantī root, nyerong wood, dong wood, the three fruits, turmeric, vidaṅga and kampillaka mixed with cow's urine, this too dispels dropsy.

If for seven days one eats no other food and drinks no other drink, but drinks only buffalo's urine mixed with milk, or else if for one month one eats no other food and drinks no other drink, but drinks only camel's milk, this too dispels puffy swelling and dropsy.

Or else, one with dropsy should drink, in whatever amount is proper, powdered long pepper steeped in the milky latex that flows from the snuhī tree.

Or else, to calm dropsy, one should drink the powders of yimong, dantī root, truktruk tré, vidaṅga and the three pungents mixed with milk; or else the powders of pine and truktruk tré mixed with either milk or a decoction of ginger; or else the powders of yimong and dried ginger mixed with either milk or a decoction of ginger.

If one drinks the powders of the ash of scorched barley, the ash of svarjikā, truktruk tré, the three pungents, shidong and the five kinds of salt mixed with liquid butter, it dispels tumors and all dropsy.

To dispel every kind of dropsy, one should drink the powders of the medicine viśālā, kini wood, dantī root, shidong and jitser mixed with cow's urine.

In short, since every kind of dropsy mostly arises from a combination of many bases of disease, for every kind of dropsy one should carry out the procedures that calm the bases of disease, wind and the rest.

For these three, dropsy of the perforated intestines, water dropsy and dropsy of the obstructed anus, a physician who knows the procedure, after an agreement has been made that the outcome may be life or death, should also carry out the procedures of puncturing the belly and of incision.

As food for those afflicted by dropsy, śāli rice, barley, Mön lentils, milk, and the meat soups of creatures that live on dry land should be taken slowly, in amounts judged by the patient's vigor and strength. One with dropsy should not drink water, sleep during the day, eat heavy food, or engage in what produces phlegm or in strenuous exertion. Riding on mounts should be entirely avoided.

This completes the tenth chapter, on treating dropsy.

Next I shall explain the treatment of urinary disease. From eating unwholesome food and from faults of conduct such as sitting and lying, urinary disease is said to arise in twenty kinds. The sages, the munis, have said that what has thus turned into urinary disease is urinary disease.

The signs with which urinary disease first strikes are these: the teeth and the like become very dirty, the hands and feet are hot, the color of the body turns red, there is great thirst, and the mouth has a sweet taste.

The names of the urinary diseases arising from phlegm are these ten: the one called like water, like sugarcane juice, like beer, like rice flour, like powder, thick, sluggish, like salt, like foam, and the one called like semen.

The names of the urinary diseases arising from bile are these six: the one called greenish yellow, blue, madder red, ash colored, like blood, and the one called sour and ripened.

The names of the urinary diseases arising from wind are these four: the one called like marrow broth, like honey, like elephant urine, and the one called like fat. The signs of all these urinary diseases are to be seen according to the meaning expressed in each one's name. Of these, those arising from phlegm are said to be easy to cure. Those arising from wind are regarded as incurable. Those arising from bile can at least be held in check by diet and medicine.

Now, for one who has a urinary disease arising from phlegm, there are these medicines: lalaphü, lotus root, guḍūcī, and chebulic myrobalan, combined together; pāṭhā, barberry, embelia, turmeric, arjuna, and jitser, combined together; embelia, turmeric, barberry, chebulic myrobalan, and gyapö, combined together; sāla wood, arjuna, kadamba, barberry, and lalaphü, combined together; white sandalwood, aloeswood, costus, and deodar, combined together; kaṭphala, nutgrass, pāṭhā, tirita, and chebulic myrobalan, combined together; and ambaṣṭhā, khadira, barberry, agnimantha, and the three fruits, combined together. Of these seven medicinal compounds, take any one, boil it, let the decoction cool, mix it with a generous amount of honey, and give it to drink.

For one who has a urinary disease arising from bile, there are these: paṭola, neem leaves, guḍūcī, emblic myrobalan, and nutgrass, combined together; chebulic myrobalan, emblic myrobalan, pushel tsé, nīlavarī, and nutgrass, combined together; lotus root, juniper, nutgrass, chebulic myrobalan, and kuṭaja, combined together; udīcya, dhātakī flowers, sengtrom, and kāliyaka, combined together; and utpala, pushel tsé, sengtrom, arjuna, and white sandalwood, combined together. Of these five medicinal compounds, take any one, boil it, let the decoction cool, mix it with honey, and give it to drink.

In a urinary disease where wind is predominant and one of the two, phlegm or bile, is its companion, one should determine which has become the companion. Where phlegm is the companion, boil the powder of suitable medicines of hot taste and strongly warming potency together with sesame oil, in a medicinal decoction four times the quantity of the sesame oil, until only the sesame oil remains, and give of this to drink in a suitable amount. Where bile is the companion, boil the powder of suitable medicines of sweet taste and cooling potency together with ghee, in a medicinal decoction four times the quantity of the ghee, until only the ghee remains, and give it to drink in a suitable amount.

If one drinks the pressed juice of fresh guḍūcī, or the cooled decoction of the dried plant, mixed with honey, this too clears away all urinary diseases. The pressed juice of fresh emblic myrobalan, or the cooled decoction of the dried fruit, mixed with powdered turmeric; or else the cooled decoction of the three fruits, deodar, barberry, and nutgrass, mixed with honey; or else the cooled decoction of kuṭaja wood, saptaparṇa, barberry, trutruktré, and the three fruits, mixed with honey: if one drinks any of these, it clears away urinary disease. If one drinks the powder of sāla wood, pukar, and kambika, two zho each, mixed with emblic myrobalan juice and honey, this too is supreme for clearing away all urinary diseases. Or else the powder of the three fruits mixed with honey, or shilajit mixed with honey, or iron powder mixed with honey, or chebulic myrobalan powder mixed with honey: these medicinal confections should be eaten in order to turn back urinary disease.

Take sengtrom, cardamom, trutruktré, dantī root, pāṭhā, gandhapatra, cinnamon, nāgakesara, lawa, elecampane, the three pungents, tsatri, deodar, kuṭaja, viśālā, white aconite, gi, costus, mūrvā, gandhapriyaṅgu, embelia, the three fruits, pushel tsé, asafoetida, long pepper, and its root, and lalaphü. Boil the powder of these, two zho each, in sixty four measures of water, reckoning one measure as eight sang. When a quarter of the water remains, let it cool and strain it. Mix honey with the reduced medicinal decoction in half its amount, pour it into a jar that has served as a vessel for ghee, seal the mouth, and keep it inside barley for half a month. If one drinks two sang of this mead every day, this too clears away urinary disease, and it destroys stomach diseases, pallor disease, hemorrhoids, leprosy, and loss of appetite.

As for the diet of one who has urinary disease, bitter vegetables, the meat of spotted deer that live on dry land, the meat of birds, foods made from barley, Mön lentils, sālu rice, and sixty day rice are beneficial. As drinks, molasses, grape wine, decoctions of khadira heartwood and the like, mead, a decoction of kuśa grass, and a decoction of the three fruits are praised for urinary disease.

As a consequence of urinary disease, many boils arise. When they first appear, blood should be let from the vein. Boils that have ripened should be lanced and treated according to the procedure for healing wounds.

This completes the eleventh chapter, on the treatment of urinary disease. This is the fourth section of the Siddhasāra.

Next, I shall explain the chapter on the treatment of leprosy.

Leprosy arises when, through sinful conduct and the eating of bad food, the three humors are disturbed, and the skin, flesh, blood, and lymph become corrupted. It takes eighteen forms. They are: the leprosy called udumbara, which is like the fruit of the udumbara; the one called shazigu, which is like the color of a conch; the one called kākaṇa, which is like the ripe fruit of the guñjā; the one called thickened skin, which is like the skin of an elephant; the one called white lotus, which is like the color of its own name; the one called like the tongue of a deer, which is rough; the one called hundred wounds, which has many wounds; the one called pervasive and dustless; the one called rotted skin, in which the skin wastes away; and the one called split feet, in which the feet crack with many fissures. These ten forms of leprosy are incurable.

The remaining eight are curable. They are: the leprosy called like a pot, which is black in color and rough; the one called flame, which has large wounds; the one called fine pimples, which are like moles and are bluish pale in color; the one called eruption, in which many pimples break out from one place; the one called spotted, which is like the kuba flower; the one called drop, which is glossy in color and hard to remove; the one called itch, whose pimples are white and red in color; and the one called foot eruption, whose pimples ooze.

Of these, leprosy arising from wind is hard, bluish pale and red in color, rough, and unbearably painful. Leprosy arising from bile is said to make the body hot, to rot greatly, and to ooze blood. Leprosy arising from phlegm makes the body putrefy; the skin is thick and itchy, glossy and white in color, and heavy. Of these, a leprosy that has the signs of arising from all three humors, having every feature, or one that is struck by many grave complications, should be abandoned.

Since it is taught that all leprosy for the most part arises from the coming together of all three humors and from an increase of the blood, for a severe leprosy, in one who has vigor and strength and has previously been given oily treatments, one should open a vein and let blood. For a mild leprosy, one should apply scarification, rub with cuttlefish bone, draw out blood with a gourd, and set leeches to suck. Emetics and purgatives are also given, according to the nature of the humor.

For a leprosy on which bloodletting may not be performed, and for one that is numb, without sensation, one should apply the ash of muka, the ash of sesame stalks, and the like. For a leprosy that has become numb and without sensation, where the skin is hard and cannot be removed, one should endow arrow poison and the like with mantras and anoint the body with it.

Grind paṭola, the three fruits, ariṣṭa, guḍūcī, dhavani, vāsaka, and karañja into a powder, and boil it with ghee in water four times the quantity of the ghee. When only the ghee remains, strain it. Its name is Vajraka. If one drinks a suitable amount of it, it clears away leprosy.

The ghee called the Great Bitter is this. Take tsatri, the three fruits, pushel tsé, pāṭhā, ariṣṭa, nutgrass, jitser, licorice, the two kinds of thaltré, putsé shel, khyichewa, white sandalwood, guḍūcī, long pepper, juniper, sweet flag, viśālā, the fruit of kuṭaja, vāsaka, mūrvā, paṭola, cassia, trolo, white aconite, turmeric, bakhal, and the root of nyeushing. Take the powder of all these, altogether a quarter of the quantity of the ghee, emblic myrobalan juice twice the quantity of the ghee, and water eight times the quantity of the ghee. Boil these with the ghee until only the ghee remains, and then strain it. If one eats a suitable amount of it, this too clears away severe diseases arising from wind and bile, leprosy, tumors, and pallor disease, and it does away with fever, madness, and scrofula.

For leprosy in which bile and blood predominate, give ghee boiled with powdered paṭola, or boiled with powdered barberry, or boiled with powdered khadira, or boiled with powdered neem. Or else boil eight sang of the powder of the medicine abartaka with thirty two sang of ghee in a hundred sang of water. When only the ghee remains, drink a suitable amount of it mixed with warm tsap, and after the medicine has been digested, one should eat a meal of kodrava rice. One performs the procedure in this way every day for seven days; after that, for three days, without taking the medicine, one eats agreeable and suitable food; and then for seven days one again performs the medicinal procedure as before. If one does this until one is cured, one will be freed from the diseases of leprosy, tumors, dropsy, and fistula of the private parts.

Or else grind the fruit of jātī, the flower of jasmine, and the three pungents, and mix them with vegetable oil; or else, if one anoints the body with realgar and black pepper ground in sesame oil, and with the leaves of the arka tree, this too clears away every leprosy disease. If one grinds together chebulic myrobalan, karañja, white mustard seed, turmeric, balguñja, rock salt, and embelia, mixes them with cow urine, and anoints the body, this too clears away leprosy. If one anoints the body with costus, castor, and eḍagaja, together with sesame oil or cow urine, this too overcomes leprosy. Sengtrom, barberry extract, rock salt, and trapuṇḍa are also taught as a class of ointments. If one grinds together costus, rock salt, white mustard seed, embelia, and eḍagaja, mixes them with tsap, and anoints, this too clears away eruption and the leprosy called drop. If one grinds sulphur, bezoar, realgar, costus, the medicine kāli, pushel tsé, and gandhapatra, mixes them with water, and anoints, this too clears away every leprosy disease, such as the spotted one.

If one mixes the powder of gandhapatra, black pepper, black vitriol, costus, and realgar with sesame oil, keeps it in a bronze vessel for seven days, and anoints the body, this too removes the spotted leprosy and shazigu. If one mixes sulphur with any one of these, the ash of roasted barley husks, the ash of the plantain, or the ash of the flower of the khara tree, with sesame oil, and anoints the body, this too clears away the spotted leprosy.

If one anoints the body with any one of these, the medicine dakunani mixed with peacock bile, or the ash of the medicine pālaka mixed with peacock bile, or jasmine flowers mixed with elephant dust, this too removes shazigu.

If one boils the powder of costus, the medicine aśvamāra, the bark of the arka tree, rock salt, pushel tsé, and trutruktré in cow urine and sesame oil, and anoints the body with it when only the sesame oil remains, this too clears away leprosy.

Boil the powder of costus, aśvamāra, bhṛṅgarāja, arka, and rock salt in cow urine, with the leaves of the snuhī tree and sesame oil. When only the sesame oil remains, put a very little arrow poison into it. If one anoints the body with this, it too clears away eruption, foot eruption, and the like.

If one mixes a finely ground powder of madder, the three fruits, lac, turmeric, realgar, bezoar, and sulphur with sesame oil, warms it in the heat of the sun, and anoints the body, this too clears away itch.

If one mixes the powder of the medicine kākanāsikā, udumbara, neem, nutgrass, the three pungents, and embelia with a decoction of neem and drinks it, this too clears away every skin disorder.

If one drinks any one of these, a decoction of guḍūcī, a decoction of the three fruits, or warm boiled cow urine, mixed with guggulu, for one month, this too clears away skin disorders, wounds, and emaciation of the body.

If one drinks any one of these, shilajit, turmeric, or barberry extract, mixed with honey and cow urine, for one month, this too clears away leprosy and pallor disease.

If for three weeks one stays in a warm house or in the hot sun, drinks a warm decoction of hot medicines mixed with the juice of the medicine bākucī, and eats food with milk, this too brings freedom from leprosy.

If one eats the powder of bākucī mixed with fresh butter and honey, afterward drinks buttermilk, and also eats one's food mixed with buttermilk, there is no fear of leprosy.

Sesame, ghee, the three fruits, honey, the three pungents, gojé, and sugar: these seven are combined in equal parts, and the name of this compound is Seven Equal Parts. If one eats a suitable amount of it, it makes food tasty and gladdens the mind, and it clears away leprosy; and for this there is no regimen to keep.

If one eats the powder of embelia, trutruktré, sugar, emblic myrobalan, iron rust, and long pepper mixed with sesame oil, it clears away all leprosy diseases, even very severe ones. If one eats the powder of chebulic myrobalan, sesame, and gojé kneaded with molasses, this too clears away leprosy. If one eats the powder of gojé, embelia, and somarājikā kneaded with molasses, or the powder of embelia, the three fruits, and long pepper kneaded with honey, this too clears away leprosy, worms, urinary disease, spreading wounds, and fistula of the private parts.

To pacify skin disorders, one should also drink the powder of kuṭaja mixed with melted butter. Or else one should eat sesame and the powder of somarājaka, combined in equal parts, for one year, while keeping the regimen.

If one eats khadira, shilajit, honey, clarified butter, and embelia combined, and keeps the regimen as prescribed, swelling leprosy and long-standing swelling diseases will be removed before long.

If one takes the liquid of burnt khadira root, collected as it drips into a jar, mixes it with emblic myrobalan juice, ghee, and honey, and eats it, this too clears away leprosy, and it becomes an elixir by the essence of the medicine.

If one eats the cooled decoction of khadira mixed with honey and barberry extract, or else the powder of the three fruits and embelia mixed with ghee and honey, this too clears away leprosy.

If one uses a decoction of khadira for anointing the body, rubbing the body, bathing, drinking, as water for preparing food, and so on, and drinks it constantly, it clears away all leprosy and skin disorders. Just as khadira is used in the procedure for repelling leprosy, so too should śiṃśapā, cassia, ariṣṭa, barberry, the rohitaka tree, and asana be used in the same way.

For one with leprosy, as food, sālu rice, kodrava, wheat, barley, old legumes such as Mön lentils, bitter vegetables, and preparations with the meat of game that lives on dry land are beneficial and suitable.

This completes the twelfth chapter, on the treatment of leprosy.

Next, I shall explain the chapter on hemorrhoids and fistula of the private parts.

Now, hemorrhoids are of six kinds: those arising from each of the three humors, those arising from all of them combined, those arising from blood disease, and those that are congenital from the very beginning. It should be understood that they arise in the place where the three folds of the anus are.

Of these, the signs of hemorrhoids arising from wind are these: the pimples are small and undeveloped, dry, with red veins and bluish pale, without oiliness and rough; the pimples are high and low and uneven; they are painful and cause obstruction; and they have all the features of wind. The signs of hemorrhoids arising from bile are these: they are red, yellow, and black in color, unbearable to touch, soft, oozing blood and having lymph, with dizziness, heat of the body, and so on; they have the features of bile. The signs of hemorrhoids arising from phlegm are these: they are white in color and pale yellow, hard to remove, oily and sticky, the pimples are large and without sensation, and they have the features of phlegm. The nature of hemorrhoids arising from all combined, and of hemorrhoids congenital from the very beginning, is to have the signs of all of them. The signs of hemorrhoids arising from blood are taught to be a red color and the oozing of blood.

Of these, these three should be abandoned: those that arise in the place of the inner fold, those arising from the combination of the three humors, and those congenital from the very beginning.

The rest are said to be hard to cure. In one with hemorrhoids, the following complications should be abandoned: vomiting, stupor, pain in the flanks, great thirst, fever, heart pain, pain in the urinary passage, pimples breaking out in the mouth, rows of pimples breaking out at the anus, and wasting of the flesh.

Or else hemorrhoids may be grouped into two kinds. Dry hemorrhoids arise from phlegm and wind combined. Those that arise from bile and blood disease combined should be known as moist and oozing.

Of these, I shall first explain the procedure for dry hemorrhoids. To pacify hemorrhoids, apply any one of these fomentations: a fomentation of cow dung, a fomentation of warm porridge, a fomentation of radish, or a fomentation of dill. Also pour on a decoction of hemp; fumigate with the smoke of the burned fat of any one of these: black snake, cat, camel, pig, or the animal jaduka; or else anoint with turmeric powder mixed with the latex of the snuhī tree; or else one should anoint with the powder of long pepper and turmeric mixed with cow bile.

Or else there are these: the powder of sarjikā ash, dantī root, lāṅgalī root, and trutruktré, with cow urine; long pepper, the seed of the śirīṣa tree, the latex of the arka tree, costus, and rock salt, combined together; and the powder of turmeric, rooster dung, guñjā fruit, and long pepper, mixed with cow urine. These three kinds of ointment are beyond doubt: if one anoints with any one of them, it does away with hemorrhoids.

If one boils the powder of dantī root, aśvamāra, black vitriol, embelia, bezoar, trutruktré, and rock salt in the latex of the arka tree and sesame oil, strains it when only the sesame oil remains, and anoints with it, this too clears away hemorrhoids.

Or else one should eat chebulic myrobalan mixed with molasses; or chebulic myrobalan steeped for one day in cow urine; or chebulic myrobalan and long pepper roasted in ghee; or chebulic myrobalan, nyerong wood, and dantī root combined.

Or else one should diligently drink buttermilk mixed with the ground powder of gojé; or buttermilk mixed with the ground powder of trutruktré and lalaphü; or buttermilk mixed with the powder of dry ginger and bilva. Or else set curd in an earthen pot smeared with trutruktré, and when it has set, drink either the curd or buttermilk. Again, following the same procedure, set curd in an earthen pot washed with pirga, pota, and guḍūcī, and drink either the curd or buttermilk. Or else, if one constantly drinks the powder of asafoetida and trutruktré mixed with beer, it clears away hemorrhoids. Or else one should drink the powder of dry ginger and trutruktré mixed with molasses and beer.

Or else, for one with hemorrhoids, roast dantī root, gojé, trutruktré, and the three fruits in equal parts, with a double portion of rock salt, in a sealed vessel. The powder, once charred, should be mixed with other food and eaten.

If one eats two zho of black sesame every morning and follows it with cold water, this too pacifies hemorrhoids, makes the teeth firm, and makes the body put on flesh.

Boil the powder of the three pungents with ghee in the liquid of palāśa ash, three times the quantity of the ghee. If one drinks a suitable amount of it when only the ghee remains, hemorrhoids will without doubt be removed.

Boil one sang each of the powder of the three fruits, the ten roots, trutruktré, and dantī root in sixty four measures of water, reckoning one measure as eight sang. When a quarter of the water remains, strain it, mix it with eight sang of molasses, pour it into a ghee vessel, and let it ferment for one month. Its name is Dantī Root Wine. If one drinks a suitable amount of it, it clears away hemorrhoids, loss of appetite, constipation, stomach disease, and pallor disease.

Take two sang each of viśālā, kapittha, trutruktré, and pāṭhā, and thirty two sang each of chebulic myrobalan and emblic myrobalan. Boil the powder of these in a hundred and twenty eight measures of water. When a quarter of the water remains, strain it, mix it with a hundred sang of molasses, pour it into an earthen jar that has served as a ghee vessel, and let it ferment for half a month. Its name is Fruit Wine. If one drinks a suitable amount of it, it clears away stomach disease, hemorrhoids, and tumors.

For hemorrhoids with pain and diarrhea, one should follow the procedure for diarrhea arising from wind taught earlier. For hemorrhoids with pain, where the feces are dry and cannot be passed, one should repeatedly follow the procedure for constipation that comes below.

Boil the ground powder of śaṭī, bilva, long pepper, trutruktré, sweet flag, licorice, deodar, elecampane, lizurwa, costus, and posöncha in sesame oil and in milk four times the quantity of the oil, until only the sesame oil remains. If one strains it and gives a suitable amount as an oily enema, it agrees with wind, lets the feces come out easily, and clears away hemorrhoids, anal pain, and retention of urine.

Take either of these two, the group beginning with putsé shel or the ten roots explained earlier, together with the powder of posöncha, licorice, śaṭī, costus, gandhapriyaṅgu, nutgrass, and rock salt. According to whether the disease arises from bile or from wind, boil it with whichever of the two, ghee or sesame oil, is fitting, in milk and water. When only the ghee or sesame oil remains, it should be given from below as a strong purging enema.

When it is determined that painful hemorrhoids are oozing blood, one should act according to the procedure for the disease of bleeding. First, if one prepares and eats medicines, food, and the like made from bitter things, it kindles the warmth of the digestive fire and ripens the humor.

For one with blood disease hemorrhoids, give as a ripening medicine the cooled decoction of pushel tsé, yarita, and barberry bark boiled together; or else the cooled decoction of dry ginger, sandalwood, tsatri, and jitser. Or else, in order to pacify thirst and blood disease, give the powder of neem bark, kuṭaja fruit, barberry extract, and white aconite, mixed with honey and the water in which rice has been washed.

If one drinks the powder of samaṅgā, utpala, plantain fruit, tirita, sesame, and sandalwood mixed with goat's milk, this too clears away bleeding hemorrhoids.

Or else, for one with blood disease hemorrhoids, let the food be eaten with goat's milk. If one eats as medicine kuṭaja bark, honey, and the froth of molasses combined; or else the powder of the medicine mayūraka mixed with honey and the water in which rice has been washed; or else lotus stamens mixed with honey and fresh butter; or else lotus stamens mixed with sugar, bleeding hemorrhoids will be eased.

Take one sang each of yimo leaves, balbu leaves, and black pepper; two sang each of long pepper and long pepper root; two zho each of the four called the four fragrances, namely cinnamon, cardamom, gandhapatra, and nāgakesara, and of pushel tsé; three sang of dry ginger; and molasses three times the total of all the medicines. The name of this, combined and made into pills, is the Balbu Pill. If one eats a suitable amount of it, and washes it down with any one of these, beer, nourishing broth, meat broth, milk, whey, or tsap, according to whichever humor predominates, it kindles the warmth of the digestive fire and clears away all hemorrhoids, vomiting, heart pain, cough, tumors, and fever.

If even these procedures do not bring relief, a physician who is skilled and has seen it done before should bend back and bind the patient's legs and so forth, and by whatever means make the anus visible. When he has examined where the pimples are, he should make a smooth tube of horn or of iron, shaped like a cow's teat, with a bore of four finger widths at the mouth and five finger widths in length, and make about three large holes in its side. He inserts it into the rectum and aligns the side opening directly with the place where the pimple is. When the pimple has entered the tube, if it is a hemorrhoid arising from wind or phlegm, he should cut the pimple with a blade and cauterize what remains with iron. Or else he should cauterize it without cutting. Or else, whichever of the six kinds of hemorrhoid it may be, the pimple that has entered the tube should be anointed with the ash of the medicine muṣkaka mixed with tradar.

For painful hemorrhoids, in short, whatever builds up a person's strength, and whatever medicine, food, and drink agree with wind, only these should be given, according to whether the illness is severe or mild. One with hemorrhoids should abandon foods that disturb the respective humors, sitting on a hard seat, squatting, holding back feces and urine when they are coming, and riding horses and the like.

Now I shall explain the varieties of fistula of the private parts. Within two finger widths of the anal opening, on the side, there arises a debilitating boil. When it bursts, its name is fistula of the private parts, and it is said to be of five kinds. Of these, the name of the boil arising from wind is Hundred Wounds: it is red in color and has unbearable stabbing pain. The name of the one arising from bile is Camel Neck: it is said to be red in color and hot like a blazing fire. The name of the one arising from phlegm is Constant Oozing: it is said to be white in color and itchy. The name of the one arising from the three humors combined is Conch Spiral: it has the signs of all of them. The name of the one that arises when food containing a sharp splinter has been eaten and it pierces the passage through which urine comes is Gone Astray. Of these, the two that arise from the three humors combined and from the piercing of a splinter should be abandoned. The rest are hard to cure.

Of these, when the boil first appears and is not yet ripe, one should let blood from around it or from a vein, anoint it with a plaster, pour over it a decoction of medicines suited to the humor from which it arose, cold or warm as is fitting, anoint and massage it with sesame oil mixed with medicines suited to the disease, and carry out the procedures in order up to the giving of a purgative.

I shall also explain the procedure once it has burst. First, one should examine with a smooth iron probe how deep the burst wound is and in which direction its track runs; slit it open along the course of the wound's track; apply the ash of the medicine muṣkaka and the like; cauterize it, and so forth, step by step in proper order; and then, following the procedure for healing wounds, give suitable medicines according to the humor from which it arose.

Of these, the procedure for healing the wound is as follows. If one mixes the powder of cassia, turmeric, and gyashuk fruit with ghee and honey and anoints the wound of a fistula of the private parts from which urine drips, the wound will close and not spread. If one anoints with nyerong wood, tejovatī, dantī root, madder, turmeric, barberry, barberry extract, and neem leaves combined, this too clears away a spreading wound.

For the disease of fistula of the private parts, boil the powder of these same medicines in a decoction of karavīra, turmeric, dantī root, laṅkā li, rock salt, trutruktré, khalung, arka, and kuṭaja, and in sesame oil. When only the sesame oil remains, strain it, and it should be packed into the wound.

A person who has the disease of fistula of the private parts, even after the wound has healed, should for one year very strictly avoid riding animals, sexual intercourse, exerting strength, strenuous effort, and heavy food.

This completes the thirteenth chapter, on the treatment of hemorrhoids and fistula of the private parts.

Next, I shall explain the chapter on the treatment of pallor disease (pāṇḍuroga). Pallor disease comes about from eating far too much of foods and the like that are pungent, sour or salty, of food one is not already accustomed to, and of earth and fats. It is of four kinds, distinguished as arising from each of the three humors separately and as arising from all of them combined.

The sign of pallor arising from wind is that the person's body is dark in color, and it is also accompanied by the signs of wind and the complications of wind disease. The sign of pallor arising from bile is that the actions of bile appear, and the eyes, skin, feces and urine are yellow. The sign of pallor arising from phlegm is that the color is white, and it is accompanied by the diseases arising from phlegm. The sign of pallor arising from the three humors combined should be known as having the signs of them all.

If someone has pallor disease together with complications such as wasting of the blood, coughing, wasting of the flesh, vomiting and thirst, and is yellow in color and sees everything as white, he will die.

In all pallor diseases, first make the inner body soft by drinking medicated ghee, and after that, in keeping with the humor involved, treat with strong medicines that purge upward by vomiting and downward by purging.

For the medicated ghee: two zho each of the powder of murwa, putséshel, turmeric, jitsher, long pepper, sandalwood, solo, khyiché, kuṭaja, tsakhri, paṭola, nutgrass and devadāru are boiled with thirty-two srang of ghee and with milk four times the amount of the ghee. When only the ghee remains, it is strained; if this is drunk in the proper amount, it removes pallor, fever, pustules, swelling, hemorrhoids, and diseases of discharging blood.

If one boils the three fruits, guḍūcī, vāsaka, putséshel, tsakhri and nimba, reduces the decoction, mixes it with honey and drinks it, this too removes pallor disease and jaundice.

Or else, one who has pallor disease should drink chebulic myrobalan mixed with cow's urine, and eat food containing milk. Or else, one should eat iron powder that has long been purified in cow's urine, and follow it with milk.

If one eats the powder of the three fruits, trutruktré, nutgrass, vidaṅga and the three hot spices, together with iron powder equal in amount to all those medicines combined, mixed with honey and ghee, this too removes pallor, jaundice, swelling and urinary disease.

If one takes the powder of sesame, the three hot spices, juniper berries and drubmar chang, together with iron powder equal in amount to all those medicines combined, mixes these with honey, and eats it in the proper amount without kneading it, this too removes severe pallor disease and swelling.

As for jaundice: when pallor disease is neglected, the bile becomes disturbed and it turns into jaundice, so it too is explained here. Its sign is that the eyes and the color of the body are yellow. What is called great jaundice arises when jaundice becomes very severe from the start. For both of these, give an oily purgative.

If bala, turmeric, nimba, the three fruits and licorice are ground in equal parts, boiled with milk and buffalo ghee, strained when only the ghee remains, and drunk in the proper amount, this too removes jaundice.

If one regularly drinks, every morning, a decoction of any one of guḍūcī, the three fruits, barberry or nimba, mixed with honey, this too removes jaundice.

If one eats the powder of iron, turmeric, barberry, the three fruits and putséshel, mixed with honey and ghee, this too removes jaundice.

If one eats the powder of emblic, iron, the three hot spices and turmeric, mixed with honey, ghee and sugar, it quickly removes even severe jaundice.

One srang each of the powder of long pepper, great long pepper, long pepper root, trutruktré, lalaphü, black pepper, rock salt, vidaṅga, the three fruits, coriander seed, juniper berries, gonyö and the fruit of nushing, together with the powder of shing nyerong, eight hundred srang of sesame oil, ninety-six srang of emblic juice, and fifty srang of jaggery: the name of what is made by boiling these together is the Wholesome Medicinal Confection. If one eats this in the proper amount, it removes pallor, jaundice, hemorrhoids and compounded poisons, does away with urinary disease, leprosy, fever, breathing difficulty and stomach diseases, and also becomes an elixir of rejuvenation.

For one afflicted by jaundice, the juice of dronpu shiwa is a good eye medicine. Or else, one may also anoint the eyes with a fine powder of turmeric, red ochre and emblic mixed together. Again, the powder of the root of karkoṭaka, or the powder of the fruit of jālinī, is put into the nose. Or else, for one afflicted by jaundice, the powder of castor and long pepper mixed together is put into the nose.

The disease called the greenish disease arises from wind and bile. Its signs are that the color of the body is greenish, bluish gray and yellow, that one is struck by fever, has great thirst, weak digestive fire, lassitude and torpor; it arises in someone who has pallor disease. It is overcome by sweetish food and drink and by medicines that remove wind and bile. Just as taught in the procedures for jaundice and pallor disease, they should be applied to this greenish disease as well.

This completes the fourteenth chapter, on the treatment of pallor disease.

Next, I shall explain the chapter on the treatment of hiccup disease and breathing difficulty. Hiccups and breathing difficulty: when diseases of wind and bile go beyond their bounds and come to follow phlegm, severe hiccups and breathing difficulty arise. Each of these two is also of five kinds.

The five kinds of hiccup disease are these. The one called the great hiccup is loud and forceful and harms the vital points. The one called the deep hiccup disease rises from the region of the navel and comes with a deep sound. The one called the paired hiccup disease comes forcefully with hiccups in pairs, is unbearably painful, and makes the head shake. The one called the small hiccup is gentle in force and rises from the chest and the collarbone. And the one called the hiccup arising from food comes about when, from eating and drinking far too much, the seat of wind becomes constricted. Of these diseases, the two called the small hiccup and the one arising from food are easy to cure. The rest are said to cut off life.

Of the five kinds of breathing difficulty, the one called the great sound has heavy, labored breathing, and the heart and the gaze are turned awry. The one called the upward kind looks upward, the breath comes forcefully, it stupefies, and there is stabbing pain in the heart and the sides of the ribs. The one called the intermittent is the one in which the breath breaks off from time to time and comes forcefully. The one called the stupefying strikes with a severe cold, stupefies the heart, and grows especially severe at daybreak. The one called the small breath arises from unhappiness, is mild, and does no harm. Of these diseases, the one called the small breath is easy to cure. The one called the stupefying can only be kept at the point of recovery by food and medicine. The rest are fatal and should be given up.

For one struck by hiccup disease or breathing difficulty, it is good first to anoint the body with sesame oil and to apply sweating. One who has strength should be cleansed by vomiting upward and purging downward. For one who is thin and weak, there are pacifying medicines: the kernel of juniper, barberry extract and parched rice combined together; putséshel and red ochre combined together; long pepper, emblic, sugar and dried ginger combined together; green vitriol and white resin combined together; the fruit and flower of the paṭala tree combined together; and long pepper combined with the tips of the branches of drago. If any one of these six medicinal combinations is mixed with honey, made into a confection and eaten, it removes hiccup disease.

Licorice mixed with honey, long pepper mixed with sugar, or dried ginger mixed with jaggery: if any one of these three is put into the nose, this too removes hiccup disease.

Fly droppings mixed with either a woman's breast milk or the juice of legentsi; or else white sandalwood rubbed down in a woman's breast milk: if put into the nose, this too removes hiccup disease.

Or else, to pacify hiccup disease, take any one of realgar, cow's horn, costus, saratsi resin or kuśa grass, mix it with ghee, burn it inside a sealed vessel, draw off the smoke through a tube, and inhale it by mouth and nose; this too removes hiccup disease.

Melt two srang of good ghee, heat it, and mix it with one srang of rock salt; or else put the ash of parched barley husks into good ghee that has been melted and heated: drinking this also removes the affliction of hiccup disease.

The procedure for treating breathing difficulty: if jitsher, long pepper, grapes, buthrenshing and chebulic myrobalan are finely ground, mixed with honey and ghee and eaten, this removes breathing difficulty, cough, wind disease and the stupefying kind.

If jaggery, black pepper, turmeric, lana, grapes and long pepper are ground, mixed with sesame oil and eaten, this too removes severe breathing difficulty.

Or else, one should eat honey, ghee, the root of gabra and the powder of licorice mixed together.

If the powder of chebulic myrobalan, tsakhri, long pepper and jitsher is mixed with honey and ghee and eaten, this too removes breathing difficulty.

If the flowers of the rambha tree, of kunda and of śirīṣa, together with long pepper, are ground, mixed with the water in which rice has been washed and drunk, this too removes breathing difficulty.

Or else, for hiccup disease and breathing difficulty, one should drink the powder of gabra root and dried ginger mixed with warm boiled water. Or else, one should drink the powder of dried ginger, sugar, gabra root and black salt mixed with warm boiled water.

Or else, cinnamon, cardamom, zalaka, lizurwa, dried ginger, jīvantī, manu, jhaṭa, chawa, aloeswood, long pepper, nutgrass, jiurukpa and spikenard are ground in equal parts, and the powder is mixed with sugar eight times the total of all those medicines. If this powdered medicine is eaten, it removes hiccup disease and breathing difficulty, and does away with cough, fever, heart pain and stabbing pain in the sides of the ribs.

The ash of either peacock's feet or the roots of its feathers, burnt and mixed with ghee and honey; or else the burnt meat of a porcupine mixed with ghee and honey: if eaten, this too removes hiccup disease and breathing difficulty.

As a drink for one severely afflicted by hiccup disease or breathing difficulty, one should drink in the proper amount a decoction of the ten roots, a decoction of devadāru, or some of the liquors.

When someone has hiccup disease or breathing difficulty, the physician should distinguish very carefully the particulars such as how severe or mild it is, and should give a regimen of food and drink that removes phlegm and wind.

This completes the fifteenth chapter, on the treatment of hiccup disease and breathing difficulty.

Next, I shall explain the chapter on the treatment of cough. Cough is of five kinds: arising from each of the three humors, arising from injury of the chest, and arising from consumption. These arise from the disturbance of the five winds, called the one that makes the breath move, the one that produces speech, the one that settles well, the one that moves, and the one that expels impurities. The disease gathers in the heart, the chest, the shoulders, the throat and so on, and scrapes them: this is called cough.

The signs of cough arising from wind are said to be pain in the heart, pain in the head, stabbing pain in the sides of the ribs, a dry cough that cannot be stopped, and loss of voice. The signs of the kind arising from bile are great thirst, heat in the body, a salty taste in the mouth, and vomit that tastes hot and is yellow in color and hot. The signs of cough arising from phlegm are heaviness of the body, much saliva, being struck by colds, and loss of appetite. The signs of cough arising from injury of the chest are coughing up blood, pain in the ribs, breathing difficulty, and pus coming from the lungs. The sign of cough arising from consumption is that blood and pus come up with the cough; it arises from the three humors combined. Among these, cough arising from injury of the chest can only be kept at the point of recovery by food, medicine and so forth. Cough arising from consumption is incurable. The remaining three are curable.

The procedure for treating them is as follows. If the powder of dried ginger, jitsher, buthrenshing, grapes, lizurwa and sugar, ground together, is mixed with sesame oil and eaten, it removes even unbearable cough arising from wind.

The powder of lizurwa, buthrenshing, long pepper, gabra root, nutgrass and jitsher, mixed with jaggery and sesame oil: the name of this medicinal confection is the Invincible, and it removes cough arising from wind.

The powder of konti, rock salt, the three hot spices, vidaṅga, costus and asafoetida, mixed with ghee and honey: this medicinal confection is praised for cough disease and hiccup disease.

For one who has cough disease arising from bile: the powder of long pepper, sugar, bamboo manna, parched rice, emblic and grapes combined together; the powder of licorice, long pepper root, mūrvā, grapes and dried ginger combined together; and the powder of long pepper, drago, bamboo manna and caltrops combined together: any one of these three medicinal combinations should be mixed with ghee and honey, made into a medicinal confection, and eaten.

For one who has cough disease arising from phlegm: the powder of great nutgrass, chebulic myrobalan, emblic, long pepper and tamālaka combined together; the powder of chebulic myrobalan, long pepper, nutgrass, devadāru and dried ginger combined together; and the powder of trutruktré, long pepper, long pepper root and great long pepper combined together: it should be understood that if any one of these three kinds of medicinal combination is mixed with honey, made into a medicinal confection and eaten, it removes cough disease arising from phlegm.

If the powder of madder, barberry extract, mūrvā, trutruktré, pāṭhā, long pepper and turmeric is mixed with a generous amount of honey and eaten, it removes cough disease arising from injury of the chest and from consumption.

The powder of devadāru, bala, rāsnā, the three fruits, the three roots, juniper and vidaṅga, combined with sugar equal in amount to the total of all those medicines: this powdered medicine removes all five kinds of cough disease.

The method of preparing the medicinal confection called Agastya is this. Take two srang each of śaṅkhapuṣpī, ātmaguptā, trutruktré, the ten roots, lizurwa, bala, juniper fruit, gabra root, apāmārga, long pepper root and manu, together with a hundred chebulic myrobalans and sixteen handfuls of barley, and boil all these together in water five times the amount of the barley. When the barley is well cooked, take it out and strain it. Into this liquid put those myrobalans, with a hundred srang of jaggery, eight srang of ghee, eight srang of sesame oil and eight srang of long pepper powder, and boil them together again over a low fire. When it has become just thick enough to be drawn up with a ladle, take it out and let it cool, then pour in eight srang of honey and mix. If, from this elixir of rejuvenation, one eats two myrobalans every morning together with some of the medicinal confection, it removes all cough diseases, breathing difficulty and consumption, and removes stomach disease, loss of appetite, hiccup disease, hemorrhoids, fever, heart pain and swelling.

Next, I shall explain the treatment of vomiting disease and thirst disease. Vomiting disease is of five kinds: arising from the disturbance of each of the three humors, arising from all three combined, and arising from seeing, touching, smelling, hearing talk of, or thinking about things that are ugly and repugnant, such as what is rotten and decayed. The signs of vomiting arising from wind are that the vomit is astringent in taste, frothy and dark in color, and comes forcefully and painfully. The signs of the kind arising from phlegm are that the vomit is sticky, sweet in taste, cold and astringent. The kind arising from all combined should be known to have all the signs.

The signs of the kind arising from what is ugly and repugnant are to be understood from those very things mentioned at the outset as its causes: dejection, ugliness, distress of mind and so on.

If the vomit comes with blood, contains charred fat, and the person is wasted in flesh and weak, and is also struck by other complications, he is incurable and should be given up.

Since all vomiting diseases arise from the stomach, the seat of indigestion, then, except for vomiting arising from wind, first carry out the procedure of fasting, and if that does not pacify it, afterwards give a purgative. For the kind arising from wind, first pacify it with medicines suited to it. As the purgative, give the powder of chebulic myrobalan mixed with honey and made into a confection, or else give some other purgative that is pleasant and appetizing. For one who has strength, give emetics and purgatives. For one who is thin and weak, give pacifying medicines.

The procedure for pacifying vomiting disease arising from wind: drink rock salt mixed with ghee; or else one should drink the powder of rock salt, black salt, tsabru salt and the three hot spices mixed with ghee.

To pacify vomiting disease arising from bile, one should drink the powder of bala and red ochre mixed with rice-washing water; or the powder of putséshel mixed with rice-washing water; or else the powder of putséshel mixed with a cold decoction of emblic.

For one who has vomiting disease arising from phlegm, one should eat the powder of vidaṅga, the three fruits and nimba mixed with honey; or else the powder of vidaṅga, musk and dried ginger mixed with honey.

If one drinks a reduced decoction of any one of śiraphala, guḍūcī or mūrvā mixed with honey, or else the powder of mūrvā mixed with rice-washing water, it removes vomiting disease arising from wind, from phlegm and from bile.

If the powder of long pepper, black pepper and realgar, the powder of parched rice equal in amount to all of those together, and honey are put into a cold decoction of the fruit of kapittha and the fruit of khalung, and drunk, this too removes vomiting disease.

The powder of the kernel of juniper, long pepper and emblic combined together; the powder of parched rice, the three fruits and dried ginger combined together; the powder of śyāmāka grass, barberry extract, nutgrass and juniper stones combined together; the powder of fly droppings, sugar and long pepper combined together; the powder of long pepper, black pepper, kapittha and bala combined together; and the powder of cinnamon, cardamom and gandhapatra combined together: if any one of these six kinds of medicinal combination is mixed with honey, made into a medicinal confection and eaten, this too removes vomiting disease.

For vomiting disease that has lasted a long time, one should carry out the procedures that remove wind, such as the medicated ghee called the Wholesome, milk medicines, aphrodisiac medicines and meat broths.

Thirst disease, too, is of five kinds: arising from each of the three humors separately, arising from indigestion of food, and arising when injury of the chest has turned into consumption. Now I shall explain the chapter on treating constipation (udāvarta). This is said to produce the disease of constipation. Its signs are pain in the heart, the kidneys and lower back, and the flanks; swelling of the bladder and groin; distension of the belly; obstruction of the voice and throat disease turning into a swelling; attacks of fever and vomiting; the eyes growing blind and the ears deaf; disease of thirst; and so on. The feces will then come out, and distension and pain are also dispelled. Alternatively, boil powders of house soot, long pepper, posoncha, white mustard seed and molasses in cow urine until it thickens like candied sugar, and make suppositories as before; this too is good.

Alternatively, take asafoetida, sweet flag, costus, the ash of natron, and tsapru salt, increasing them in order by two parts over the first, grind them, and mix with warm boiled water. If this is drunk, it too dispels distension of the belly, stabbing pain, heart pain and abdominal tumors. Take two parts of nyerong wood, four parts of long pepper, and five parts of chebulic myrobalan, grind them, and boil them in molasses equal in quantity to the whole mixture until it thickens like candied sugar; pills made from this dispel the disease of fecal obstruction. As food, one should eat a gruel of raw barley coarsely ground, with milk or meat broth, or any other foods suited to the condition.

That arising from phlegm causes heart pain, a dull appetite, and water coming from the mouth. That arising from a combination of all three humors has all the signs, and should be abandoned as incurable.

For fecal obstruction arising from wind, powders of ajwain, asafoetida, rock salt, the ash of roasted barley, black salt, and chebulic myrobalan, mixed with good beer and drunk, dispel it. Next I shall explain the chapter on treating the diseases of insanity and epilepsy. It is taught that there are five kinds of it. The signs of insanity arising from wind are to be known as making threatening gestures at oneself and striking oneself, weeping, laughing, and singing. The signs of that arising from bile are anger, desire for cool things, threatening, chasing after people, and so forth. The signs of that arising from phlegm are much sleep, little speech, delight in women and pursuing them, and lack of desire for food. That arising from a combination of all has all the signs; it is intractable and should be abandoned. Insanity arising from an adventitious cause comes from harm done by the disturbance of grahas such as gods. It is to be recognized by the patient's possessing superhuman knowledge, insight, strength, speech, courage, and the like.

For the disease of insanity arising from wind, first one should drink medicated ghee. For that arising from bile, first give a purgative. For that arising from phlegm, first give an emetic. If a suitable amount of it is eaten, memory becomes clear and the mind becomes keen, and by its power deafness, stammering, and muteness are dispelled as well. Alternatively, one should grind licorice, asafoetida, sweet flag, gyapö, śirīṣa, white garlic, and costus in goat urine, strain it, and pour the juice into the nose and apply it to the eyes.

For one struck by the disease of insanity arising from an adventitious cause, a physician who is pure and free of expectation should dispel it according to the proper procedures: offerings, tormas, food offerings, oblations, fire offerings, mantras, applications to the eyes, pouring medicine into the nose, and so forth.

For that arising from a combination of all, one should give whichever of those procedures is fitting, matching it to whichever humor is predominant. The wind disease called swollen knee is when an increase of wind and blood makes the knee joint swell.

Gout arises when people who sit idle and have never known hardship engage in strenuous behavior such as exhausting exertion, so that wind and blood are disturbed. At first it is located in the hands and feet, and later it enters the body. The signs of gout in which wind predominates are said to be pain, the bones feeling crushed and broken, dryness and roughness of the body, and black color. The signs of gout arising from blood and bile are red color and swelling, throbbing pain, oozing of serous fluid and breaking down of the tissue, pain, heat of the body, and smooth color. The signs of gout arising from phlegm are little pain, itching and stiffness of the body, and great swelling. The signs of gout arising from a combination of two humors, or of all three, are to be recognized from a mixture of the signs taught above. Gout that arises from a single humor and is recent is easy to cure. That which arises from a combination of two humors and has lasted up to a year can be held in check with food and medicine. That which arises from a combination of all three humors, in which wounds burst open and ooze and the bones break, is incurable and should be abandoned.

The method of treating gout is this. First administer medicated ghee to drink, and after the patient's strength has been built up, perform venesection and let blood in all cases except that arising from wind alone. After that, matching it to whichever humor is involved, one should give whichever is fitting of the five procedures taught before: a strong enema from below, a mild oily enema from below, an emetic, a purgative, and pouring medicine into the nose. For that arising from wind, carry out the procedure of an enema. For that arising from bile, give a purgative. For that arising from phlegm, give an emetic. Alternatively, gout arising from a predominance of wind will be dispelled if it is anointed with flour mixed with ghee and goat's milk. Alternatively, one should anoint it with any one of these: powder of roasted sesame mixed with milk, powder of flax mixed with milk, powder of dill mixed with milk, or powder of castor seeds mixed with milk. Alternatively, finely pound the flesh of any creature that lives in water, such as fish, add various spices, cook it well, and anoint with it. For gout arising from a predominance of bile, anoint with a paste of licorice powder mixed with ghee, milk, and flour. Alternatively, anoint with the ground powder of the ten medicines called life-giving, mixed with ghee. For gout arising from a predominance of phlegm, one should anoint with any one of these mixed with cow urine: powder of aśvagandhā and sesame, or powder of white mustard seed, or powder of punarnavā and śigru.

For every kind of gout, one should eat powder of chebulic myrobalan mixed with molasses. Alternatively, one should drink a decoction of boiled guḍūcī. Alternatively, one should drink the rejuvenating medicine of long pepper boiled in milk: increase it by five long peppers each day until the number of long peppers reaches a hundred, then reduce it by five long peppers each day until it comes down to five, and then increase it again as before. With such a procedure of boiling, one should guard oneself well and drink it constantly.

The twenty-first chapter, on treating wind diseases, is complete.

Next I shall explain the chapter on treating the diseases of alcohol. The ten qualities that were taught as benefiting or harming in the case of poison are also present in alcohol. From drinking it wrongly, the humors of wind, bile, and phlegm are disturbed, and severe diseases of alcohol arise.

The signs of a change of behavior caused by wind are halting speech, speaking a great deal, speaking with dryness, unsteady and confused words, a rough body whose color turns bluish or turns reddish, and intoxication. The signs of a change of behavior caused by bile are a red and yellow color, anger, and delight in fighting. The signs of a change of behavior caused by phlegm are little speech, coherent words, pensiveness, a pale complexion, and withdrawn attachment. A change of behavior arising from a combination of all the humors has all of these.

The signs of the alcohol disease arising from wind are pain in the heart, the brain, and the flanks, stiffness of the body, attacks of hiccups, and coughing up phlegm. The signs of the alcohol disease arising from bile are great thirst, heat of the body, sweating, the color turning yellow, loss of awareness, and stupor. The signs of the alcohol disease arising from phlegm are sluggishness, water coming from the mouth, loss of appetite, vomiting, and stiffness of the body. The alcohol disease arising from a combination of the three humors is to be known as having all the signs.

For the alcohol disease arising from wind, after the alcohol drunk earlier has been digested, if one pours powder of black salt and the three hot spices, with a little water, into alcohol and drinks it, it dispels the disease. Alternatively, if one pours powders of salt, black salt, black pepper, zimtiklé, and ajwain into alcohol and drinks it, this too dispels severe alcohol disease arising from wind. Alternatively, in order to dispel alcohol disease arising from wind, one should pour powders of juniper berries, pomegranate, tintiḍīka fruit, ajwain, and rock salt into a gruel made of alcohol and olkön, and drink it. As a drink, one should take sour rice water and pomegranate kneaded into sour water. It is also good to drink the oily meat broth of wild animals that live on dry land, or broth mixed with rock salt and pomegranate, and to take food with a sauce.

For alcohol disease arising from bile, one should make alcohol half water, mix it with honey and sugar, and drink it. Alternatively, one should drink any one of these: a decoction of boiled sweet medicines mixed with sugar, or the broth of Mön lentils mixed with sugar, or the reduced broth of meat cooked in a decoction of boiled sweet medicines; and all of them are to be taken cold.

For alcohol disease arising from phlegm, one should mix an emetic with alcohol and make the patient vomit, and also carry out the procedure of fasting in keeping with the patient's strength. Alternatively, one should drink powder of heat-producing medicines mixed with alcohol, or a decoction of the three fruits mixed with powder of the three hot spices, and one should keep to the regimen. As food, one should eat food made of barley, prepared with either the broth of dried radish or the broth of peas, made hot with spices and with meat added, together with the meat of wild animals that live on dry land, with little fat.

Any secondary diseases caused by alcohol disease, such as wasting, fever, phlegm, pain in the flanks, and diarrhea, should be pacified by giving the medicines for each. For one whose bodily flesh has been wasted by alcohol, one should restore it by a strong enema from below, a mild oily enema from below, anointing and massaging the body with oil, bathing, and administering medicated ghee and milk. The medicated ghee for this is made by boiling powders of nyeushing, punarnavā, and licorice in ghee with water four times the quantity of the ghee; when only the ghee remains, a suitable amount should be drunk. Alternatively, if a decoction of boiled bala and atibalā mixed with milk is drunk, it dispels the wasting disease. Pour ghee, milk, and powder of licorice into a decoction of boiled punarnavā, boil it, and when only the ghee remains, squeeze it out; if a suitable amount of it is drunk, this too restores and brings back to health one whose radiance has been destroyed by drinking alcohol.

The twenty-second chapter, on treating the diseases of alcohol, is complete.

Next I shall explain the chapter on treating erysipelas. Erysipelas is to be known as being of seven kinds, arising when the humors are disturbed by constantly eating food that is salty, sour, pungent, strongly heating, sharp, and the like. It is called erysipelas because it spreads and moves through the whole body. The signs of erysipelas arising from wind are pustules that are soft and black in color, swelling, attacks of fever, and shooting pain. The signs of erysipelas arising from bile are pustules yellow and red in color, heat of the body, and attacks of fever. The signs of erysipelas arising from phlegm are pustules white and pale in color, itching, and attacks of phlegm fever. Erysipelas arising from a combination of all the humors has all the signs. The bursting erysipelas arises from blood and bile together; its signs are said to be pustules that are black, bluish pale, or red in color, swelling, ripening and then bursting, heat of the body, and increase of fever. What is called fire erysipelas arises from wind and bile; it is to be recognized by signs such as pustules colored as if burnt by fire, their surroundings red like glowing embers, heat of the body, great thirst, and diarrhea. What is called mud-like erysipelas arises from phlegm and bile; its signs are pustules that are pale and red, or black and glistening, covered with filth, hot, and with their surroundings made muddy by serous fluid.

Of these, erysipelas arising from each of the three humors separately is easy to cure. The remaining kinds are said to be incurable. When it occurs both outside and inside the body, or occurs over the joints, all seven are especially incurable.

For erysipelas arising from wind, from among the five groups of five roots taught earlier, use any one of the four groups of five roots other than the five grass roots, to anoint the body, to pour over the affected part, to prepare a medicated ghee, and so forth. Alternatively, boil powders of rice, barley, and licorice in ghee and milk until cooked into a porridge, and anoint the painful part with it. Alternatively, pour milk over the painful part. Alternatively, pour the essence of ghee over it.

For erysipelas arising from bile, one should anoint with powders of prapauṇḍarīka, madder, juniper, putse shel, sandalwood, licorice, and utpala mixed with milk. Alternatively, it is good to use the group beginning with the nyagrodha tree, taught earlier, pouring its decoction over the affected part, applying compresses, and preparing a medicated ghee. Alternatively, melt ghee and let it solidify, doing this up to a hundred times; when it has matured, pour in a large amount of licorice powder, mix it, and anoint with it. Alternatively, if one anoints with powders of likhlumik, putse shel, sandalwood, nut grass, pearl, precious gems, and red ochre mixed with ghee and milk, it dispels erysipelas arising from bile.

For erysipelas arising from phlegm, if one anoints with a paste of khadira, saptaparṇa, nut grass, dhava wood, dongga, deodar, and kuraṇṭa ground together, it will subside.

Alternatively, one anoints with a paste of licorice, the three fruits, butsé to, and śirīṣa ground together. Alternatively, for erysipelas arising from phlegm, it is good to anoint with the group of medicines beginning with varuṇa, to pour decoctions over it, to prepare medicated ghee, and so forth. Drinking a decoction of paṭola, nimba, the bark of barberry root, putse shel, khyichewa, and licorice boiled together also dispels every kind of erysipelas. Drinking a decoction of nut grass, nimba, and paṭola boiled together also dispels all erysipelas. Alternatively, one should drink a decoction of emblic myrobalan, paṭola, and mung beans boiled together, mixed with ghee. In short, for erysipelas one should first purify with emetics and purgatives, and should also open a vein and let blood. If, even after the procedure has been carried out in this way, the pustules fester and burst, one should carry out everything taught in the procedure for healing wounds.

The twenty-third chapter, on dispelling erysipelas, is complete.

Next I shall explain the chapter on treating swelling disorders. As for swelling disorders: when someone who is enfeebled eats heavy foods, sour things, and foods that give rise to phlegm, the humors become disturbed, and from this six kinds of severe swelling disorder arise. The signs of all swellings are easy to treat.

Among them, the signs of a swelling disorder arising from wind are these: the swelling itself shifts from place to place; wherever it is pressed, the hollow stays there; its color is black and red; and it is unbearably painful. The signs of a swelling arising from bile are these: it ripens and bursts quickly; it is soft in color; it is yellow and red; it is hot; and it makes the body hot. The signs of a swelling arising from phlegm are these: its color is glossy and white; the swelling is pale; it is hard; and it itches. The signs of swelling arising from blood and of swelling from external injury are like the signs of bile. The signs of a swelling arising from all the humors combined include all of these.

If the humor dwells within the stomach, the upper body swells. If the humor dwells within the intestines, the lower body swells. If the humor dwells in between, the middle swells. If the humor pervades everywhere, the whole body swells. If the swelling arises from a single humor, is not compounded by some other serious disease, and occurs in a person who is not bedridden and has great strength, it is easy to treat. Swelling of the whole body, and swelling that spreads upward from below, are grave.

For swelling disorders, the physician should match the treatment to the humor and, using the medicine groups for each humor, prepare medicated ghee and medicines in sesame oil, and apply them as ointments, as pourings over the affected part, and as drinks. The five actions, namely a strong evacuating enema given from below, an oily enema given from below, emetics, purgatives, and medicine poured into the nose, should also be administered according to the procedure, matched to the greater or lesser degree of heat.

Besides these, I shall also explain other general procedures for calming swelling disorders. Powders of deodar, chebulic myrobalan, dry ginger, and punarnavā mixed with warm boiled water; or else powders of viḍaṅga, white aconite, the bark of dukmo nyung, dry ginger, deodar, and black pepper mixed with warm boiled water; or else powders of the three hot spices, iron filings, and the ash of burnt barley, mixed with a decoction of the three fruits: drinking these also dispels swelling disorders. Alternatively, one should drink powders of putse shel, iron filings, the three hot spices, and shing nyerong mixed with a decoction of the three fruits; or else white guggulu mixed with cow's urine; or else powder of long pepper mixed with milk. Alternatively, one should eat jaggery mixed in equal parts with powder of chebulic myrobalan, or else jaggery mixed in equal parts with dry ginger. Alternatively, one eats jaggery and fresh ginger in a suitable amount. For the kind arising from wind, one eats these together with meat broth as food. For the kind arising from bile, one eats them together with milk as food. For the kind arising from phlegm, one should eat them together with coarse broths as food. If the procedure is carried out in this way, swelling disorders, wasting disease, dropsy, insanity, tumors, hemorrhoids, and difficulty in breathing will be cured. Drinking powders of deodar, punarnavā, and dry ginger mixed with milk, or else a decoction of krutruk tré, the three hot spices, shing nyerong, and deodar boiled together, also dispels swelling disorders.

What is called elephantiasis arises from a disorder of fat and flesh, and is a swelling of both legs so that they look like pots. It is of three kinds, distinguished by which of the three humors gives rise to it, with phlegm predominating, and these are shown by the signs of each humor. It arises in places that are cold at all times, very damp, and never able to dry out. Elephantiasis that has lasted more than a year, and elephantiasis like an anthill, are not survived. The procedure for treating it is said to be anointing with medicine suited to the humor, fomentation, opening a vein and letting blood, and similar procedures, together with all that was taught in the procedure for treating swelling disorders, as appropriate.

Goiter is of three kinds, distinguished as arising from wind, arising from fat, and arising from phlegm. The signs of goiter arising from wind are that it is black and red in color, the breath smells foul, and it dries the palate. The signs of goiter arising from fat are that it is soft and oily. The signs of goiter arising from phlegm are that it is very large and hard. If it occurs in someone who is emaciated, or has lasted more than a year, it cannot be cured. What is called a garland of tumors (gaṇḍamālā) is the name for when, on the neck, phlegm and fat together form many nodules like the stones of emblic myrobalan encircling it; it is called a garland of tumors, and it persists for a long time without healing.

The procedure for treating goiter is this: the physician should match the treatment to the humor and, using the medicine groups for each humor, get rid of the goiter by fomentation, anointing, medicated steam baths, opening a vein and letting blood, giving emetics and purgatives, and having the patient drink medicines in sesame oil.

For a garland of tumors, powder of the bark of śākoṭaka is boiled in sesame oil and water until only the oil remains, and this is strained; or else powders of nimba, aśvamāra, and nirguṇḍī are boiled in sesame oil and water, and when only the oil remains it is strained and poured into the nose. Alternatively, when a garland of tumors has not yet ripened, one incises it, removes the nodules, and cauterizes the mouth of the wound. When it has ripened and burst, one anoints it with a preparation of muṣkaka ash and cleanses it; and both of these should then be treated according to the procedure for wounds. Alternatively, one makes an incision at a point twelve finger-breadths above the heel, removes the nodules of fat like fish eggs that lie within the calf, cauterizes the mouth of the wound, and proceeds according to the procedure for healing wounds.

Fleshy tumors arise from each of the three humors separately, and are nodules of blood, flesh, and fat. They are to be recognized from the signs of each humor. Among them, fleshy tumors arising from blood and flesh cannot be cured. The method for treating fleshy tumors should follow what was explained in the procedure for treating goiter. Alternatively, one should also incise, cauterize, and apply medicinal ash, and cover the place with cloth or leaves.

The twenty-fourth chapter, on treating swelling disorders, is complete.

Next I shall explain the chapter on treating wounds. It should be understood that wounds are of two kinds, distinguished as those arising from the body itself and those arising from outside. Of these, the former arise from the humors; the latter are injuries from weapons and the like.

The signs of a wound arising from wind are these: the area around the wound is rough, red in color and bluish-pale; there is little discharge; it throbs; and it is not very painful. The signs of a wound arising from bile are these: the wound and its surroundings are yellow, red, and blue in color; there is much discharge; and the body is hot. The signs of a wound arising from phlegm are these: it is hard and its swelling is pale; it oozes a pale serum; and it is cold and viscous. The signs of a wound arising from a blood disorder are that it is red in color and bleeds. The signs of wounds arising from two humors combined, or from three combined, are shown by a mixture of the signs of whichever humors are combined.

At first, when it has not yet ripened, one anoints it with a poultice that calms it. When it is turning to pus, one carries out the procedure for ripening it. When the pus has formed, one opens it, clears out the pus and serum cleanly with drawing medicines, and then applies medicines that grow new flesh in the wound.

As for the medicine for bringing it to pus: powders of sesame, yeast, linseed, and costus, with flour and rock salt, mixed with sour curd and kneaded into a poultice, are held to be the best means of ripening. The signs of its becoming hot with pus are said to be a burning heat, aching and stabbing pain, red color, and swelling. The signs that pus has formed are that the earlier signs of active ripening subside, the skin gathers into wrinkles, and it rises up into a point on one side.

As for the medicine for making it burst: powders of shing nyerong, snuhī latex, asmari, and the group beginning with cirabilva, together with krutruk tré and the droppings of pigeons, jagak birds, and sparrows, mixed together; or else anointing it with a preparation of muṣkaka ash, which is the best of the medicines for making it burst.

The method for clearing out pus and serum with drawing medicines is this: one should anoint with powders of sesame, rock salt, licorice, nimba leaves, turmeric, barberry, and shing nyerong mixed with ghee. Alternatively, anointing with powder of nimba leaves and sesame mixed with honey also cleanses the wound.

As for the medicine that grows new flesh in a wound: if one anoints with flour ground from raw barley mixed with ghee and honey, the wound will close. The signs of a healing wound are that it is without swelling, soft and glossy, red like the color of the tongue, without pain, with the discharge stopped, and without puffiness; these are said to be the signs that the wound is becoming clean.

For a wound arising from wind, one should use the greater and lesser five-root groups in every procedure. For a wound arising from bile, one should use the group beginning with nyagrodha in every procedure. For a wound arising from phlegm, one should use the group beginning with dongga in every procedure. Further, anointing with powders of blue utpala, balā, barberry, medā, licorice, sandalwood, samaṅgā, and dhātakī mixed with ghee also grows new flesh in the wound. Alternatively, if one anoints with powder of the young shoots of the milky-sapped trees mixed with ghee, or with powders of the three fruits, the hanging shoots from the branches of the banyan, khadira, and sengtrom mixed with ghee, the mouth of the wound will close. Alternatively, sprinkling it with powders of arjuna, udumbara, aśvattha, sengtrom, the bark of jambū, licorice, kaṭphala, and lac will also grow new flesh in the wound. Powders of putse shel, beeswax, turmeric, licorice, the fruit and leaves of naktamāla, mālatī, and nimba leaves are boiled in ghee and water, and the ghee that remains on its own is also beneficial for wounds. Alternatively, powders of prapauṇḍarīka, licorice, butsé to, ranyé, and sandalwood, together with a decoction of the milky-sapped trees, are boiled in sesame oil, and when only the oil remains it is strained; this too heals wounds.

On top of the medicine anointed on the mouth of the wound, it is good to lay leaves of karañja, plakṣa, jambū, and the like, and over that to bind it with cloth or the like; this cleanses the wound and heals it. When worms have got into a wound, it helps to anoint the mouth and the inside of the wound with a preparation of the group beginning with jiu dukpa.

When a healed wound has left a hollow, if one anoints it with powder of the leaves of beans, mung beans, and other kinds of legumes mixed with water, or else with powder of ground kośa fruit or ground jambang ko mixed with water, it will fill back up.

For a wound that has become crooked, one who knows the procedure should examine where the wound has formed a pocket, cut it open with a blade, clear it out cleanly, anoint it with medicines that draw out pus and serum, and let them soak in; then one should carry out, in due order, the whole procedure for wounds, including the medicines that grow new flesh at the mouth of the wound and close it.

For a wound with a splinter lodged inside it, one draws the splinter out with tongs and removes it, and then carries out, in due order, the procedure of medicines taught for healing wounds.

When, because an old wound has lasted a long time, the flesh has wasted away and dried up, one should carry out the procedure with medicines that build up the flesh, and should also heal the wound.

For a wound that keeps breaking open, one lets blood from the area around the wound and gives emetics and purgatives. For a wound from external injury, immediately after the wound occurs, one anoints it with a mixture of ghee and honey and binds it, and carries out procedures that reduce the heat of bile and blood and cool it.

When a bone is broken or a joint is dislocated, one who knows the procedure should straighten it, set the joint, anoint it with ghee and licorice, bind it, wrap it tightly with cloth, and keep it from moving by means of bark from the milky-sapped trees; and one should guard it carefully so that it does not fester in any way.

The diet for wounds, fractures, and bruises: one eats śāli rice, mung beans, food made from barley, and the meat of wild animals that live in dry country. One should avoid curd, milk, sour things, heavy foods, and sleep.

The twenty-fifth chapter, on treating wounds, is complete.

Next I shall explain the chapter on the eye-medicine tantra. Since what the King of Videha taught, called the Eye-Medicine Tantra, is very extensive, I shall not explain all of it here, but only a portion.

The nature of the eye is that it is a ball, and it is red, white, and black. It is said to possess the great elements of earth, water, fire, and wind; and because its location is a cavity, it also possesses space.

Through the distinctions among the faults of wind, bile, phlegm, and blood, there arise four kinds of a severe eye disease called "tearing," and this is the basis from which nearly all eye diseases arise.

The signs of tearing eye disease arising from wind are said to be cold tears, dry discharge, and pain with a dazed feeling. The signs of tearing eye disease arising from bile are hot tears, yellow discharge, a burning heat, and red vessels. The signs of tearing eye disease arising from phlegm are that the eye is covered with white discharge, the tears are sticky, and it itches and swells. The signs of tearing eye disease arising from a fault of the blood are that the eye is red with a burning heat, a vivid redness appears, and the tears are streaked with red vessels.

When, through eating unsuitable food and not eating at the proper times, the eye disease called tearing has grown great with heat, it becomes the eye disease called "pounding," and the eye and half of the head ache unbearably. Its signs too should be recognized, as described above, by matching them with the signs of each humor.

The procedure for treating eye disease arising from wind is this: one should pour into the eye a strained liquid made from powders of sugar, sengtrom, and rock salt mixed with lukewarm water, or else sugar, dry ginger, and śābara sengtrom prepared by the same procedure as before. Alternatively, one pours into the eye powders of kustara flowers, licorice, sugar, and dry ginger mixed with lukewarm whey and strained, or else the liquid of powders of dry ginger, rock salt, licorice, and sengtrom roasted in ghee. Alternatively, powders of licorice, sandalwood, madder, sengtrom, and tsak yuknam mixed with lukewarm water and strained, or else the group beginning with bilva boiled, also dispel unbearable pain from eye disease when poured into the eye.

Alternatively, the root of eraṇḍa is boiled in goat's milk and reduced until lukewarm; then, with the eye held open and covered with a clean white cloth, it is poured over it in an unbroken stream from the side of the eye. This is the best of what is called eye medicine given through a strainer. Alternatively, long pepper, balka, licorice, and rock salt boiled in goat's milk and reduced until lukewarm, made into a medicine given through a strainer as before, also dispel unbearable pain from eye disease.

Alternatively, rock salt and curd are mixed in an iron or copper vessel; or else, in a copper or iron mortar, turmeric and long pepper are pounded fine with a pestle of brass and mixed with curd, and left in that same vessel overnight. If this is taken up with a copper or iron eye-medicine probe and anointed on the eye, it dispels unbearable pain in the eye.

For eye disease arising from bile, a decoction of prapauṇḍarīka, licorice, turmeric, emblic myrobalan, and juniper is boiled down, mixed with honey and sugar, and, as applied before, given through a strainer and poured continuously; this dispels the eye disease. Alternatively, powders of sandalwood, ariṣṭa leaves, licorice, barberry, and chenyangtsa are mixed with cold water, combined with honey and sugar, and given through a strainer, poured continuously.

For eye disease arising from phlegm, powders of soot and white mustard seed, one part each, and turmeric and barberry, two parts each, are mixed with lukewarm water and given through a strainer, poured continuously; this dispels tearing eye disease. Alternatively, in a closed vessel smeared with nimba decoction, powders of four parts sengtrom and one part each of soot and white mustard seed are roasted until scorched and ground, and then mixed with lukewarm water; this too should be given through a strainer and poured continuously.

For eye disease arising from blood, powders of tirita, the three fruits, licorice, sugar, and great nut grass are mixed with cold water, given through a strainer, and poured continuously; this dispels tearing eye disease.

If one takes up with a probe powders of sengtrom, licorice, realgar, barberry, and rasāñjana mixed with goat's milk, or else a decoction of barberry boiled down and mixed with honey, and anoints the eye with it, it dispels every kind of tearing eye disease.

When eye disease first strikes and the pain is swelling up, sengtrom and emblic myrobalan, combined, are roasted in ghee, ground fine, mixed with realgar, kneaded with water, and anointed on the skin outside the eye. Alternatively, the eye medicine cakṣu is wrapped in clean cloth and boiled in cow's urine; when it is thoroughly cooked, it is stripped of its husk, dried, and ground fine, and merely pouring this powder once into the eye at bedtime at once removes even an unbearably swelling eye disease. Alternatively, for an eye that is painful and swollen, powders of one part śigru seed, eight parts sengtrom, and three parts realgar, all ground fine and mixed together, are spread as a plaster on clean cloth, and the eye is covered with it. Alternatively, if rasāñjana, chebulic myrobalan, barberry, red ochre, and rock salt are made together into a liquid paste and anointed on the skin outside the eye, this too dispels all eye diseases.

For every case of pounding eye disease, one opens the vein of the forehead and lets blood, and should also carry out, as appropriate to each condition, the medicinal procedures taught in the section on tearing eye disease.

When a white spot appears on the dark part of the eye, it is called lingtok, and it should be understood to arise from phlegm. When something red appears on the film of the eye, it is called red lingtok, and it arises from a fault of the blood.

The method for calming white lingtok is this: if one anoints the eye with powder of any one of the following, beer dregs, the heartwood of the madhūka flower tree, the kernel of beleric myrobalan, or rock salt, mixed with honey, the lingtok will disappear. Alternatively, anointing with powders of camphor, black pepper, licorice, conch, cow's tooth, rock salt, realgar, and sandalwood mixed with śigru decoction and made into a collyrium also dispels white lingtok. Anointing with powders of cuttlefish bone, hen's eggshell, rock salt, conch, and the kernel of śigru fruit mixed with water and made into a collyrium also removes lingtok and the like as though it were pared away with a knife. Anointing the eye by dipping an eye-medicine probe into equal parts of powdered hen's eggshell, realgar, conch, chingbu, sandalwood, and rock salt also removes lingtok, pterygium, and the like. Anointing the eye by dipping an eye-medicine probe into powders of sandalwood, rock salt, chebulic myrobalan, and lac, mixed in increasing order, each one part more than the one before, also removes lingtok, pterygium, and the like. Anointing the eye with a collyrium of equal parts of powdered unopened jasmine buds, lac, tsak yuknam, and sandalwood mixed with water also dispels damage and sores in the eye and lingtok, and clears an eye engorged with blood.

For red lingtok, one should anoint the eye with any one of these: conch powder mixed with honey, kataka fruit mixed with rock salt, or cuttlefish bone mixed with sugar.

Anointing the eye with an eye medicine of jasmine-leaf juice, honey, powdered turmeric and barberry, and rasāñjana, or else with powder of long pepper wrapped in clean cloth, boiled in cow's urine, and dried, dispels the eye disease of being unable to see at night.

Realgar, bamboo manna, tangzil, and the three hot spices are kneaded with cow's bile and rolled into thin sticks the size of a bean, which are rubbed down in water; or else deodar powder is soaked in urine and dried, and this is done many times over. Anointing the eye with these dispels eyes filled with tears. Alternatively, anointing the eye with powders of babla, deodar, and sweet flag mixed with the juice of jiurukpa leaves, made into a collyrium and dried in the shade, also dispels chronic bleariness of the eye. Anointing the eye by dipping an eye-medicine probe into a powder of babla and tangzil, ground fine together with copper amounting to twice the two combined, or else anointing with powder of the three hot spices and rasāñjana mixed with water, also dispels bleary eyes.

It should be understood that the general sign of every eye disease that has become dim vision is that sight becomes very blurred. The particular kinds are also explained. The signs of dim vision arising from wind are that whatever one sees has red veins, wavers, and appears hazy. The signs of dim vision arising from bile are that things appear blue and yellow in color, and one sees blazing fire, fireflies, and lightning. The signs of dim vision arising from phlegm are that whatever one sees appears soft and pale in color and seems fixed. The signs of dim vision arising from the humors combined are that one sees all of these signs together. The signs of dim vision arising from blood are that whatever one sees appears very red.

When the eye disease called dim vision is neglected and grows grave, it becomes the eye disease called "covered by a film." Its signs at first are like the signs of whichever humor it arose from, as explained in the section on dim vision; but it should be understood that the confusion then grows ever more severe until one cannot see. Among these, the film-covering eye disease arising from phlegm is easy to treat: it is good to open a vein and let blood.

The procedure for treating dim vision is this: anointing with a collyrium of the kernel of chebulic myrobalan, licorice, emblic myrobalan, black pepper, and pangma ground in water dispels dim-vision eye diseases. A collyrium made by grinding the three hot spices, iron filings, rock salt, the three fruits, and tangzil in water and mixing them well is called the black collyrium; anointing with this too dispels dim-vision eye disease.

Anointing with a collyrium made of powders of turmeric, emblic myrobalan, long pepper, kataka fruit, and white mustard seed mixed with rainwater also dispels all eye diseases. Anointing with a collyrium made of powders of vyāghrī, nut grass, mango fruit, licorice, long pepper, and rock salt soaked with goat's milk in a copper vessel also dispels all eye diseases.

A collyrium made by grinding in water equal parts of the tooth of any four-footed animal such as cattle, lac, the fruit of karañja, the fruit of bṛhatī, musk, camel bone, fish bone, viḍaṅga, the three hot spices, and costus is called the tooth collyrium. Anointing with it dispels dim vision, film covering, pterygium, the outbreak of pustules, the outbreak of sores, lingtok, and the like. Anointing with a liquid eye medicine of powders of realgar, rock salt, green vitriol, conch, the three hot spices, and rasāñjana mixed with honey also dispels film covering, lingtok, pterygium, and dim vision.

Anointing the eye by dipping an eye-medicine probe into the juice of kapittha mixed with twice as much sulfur juice, or else into rasāñjana and tangzil mixed with four times as much powdered nut grass, also dispels dim vision. Alternatively, powders of one part cloves, two parts copper, three parts tangzil, five parts sulfur, and three parts nāgakesara are roasted until scorched in a closed vessel and ground fine; one should coat an eye-medicine probe with this and anoint the eye regularly.

Powder of either tangzil or emblic myrobalan is mixed with ghee and honey and put inside the husk of a chebulic myrobalan from which the kernel has been removed; it is closed up again, covered with dough, and burned in a fire of juniper. When the dough is charred and the smoke has stopped, it is taken out again and allowed to cool. Coating an eye-medicine probe with the powder made by grinding the medicine that is inside the chebulic myrobalan, and anointing the eye with it, is the best remedy for dispelling dim vision.

The powder of turmeric, barberry, chebulic myrobalan, spikenard, costus, and long pepper ground fine is called the Buddhist eye medicine. If, as before, one coats an eye-medicine probe with it and anoints the eye, it dispels all eye diseases. Alternatively, powders of tangzil, spikenard, and gandhapatra are mixed with ghee and put into the mouth of a dead black snake whose head has been cut off; this is covered with clay and burned, and when the clay is fired and turns red it is taken out and allowed to cool. Coating an eye-medicine probe with the powder made by grinding fine the medicine inside it, and anointing the eye, also dispels dim vision.

Seeds of dukmo nyung are mixed with ghee, wrapped up in leaves of paṭola, and boiled in water; when they are cooked, the liquid inside is cooled and poured into the eye. Or else, if one pours into the eye the liquid from boiling, as before, the eggs found in the dried husks of dukmo nyung seeds, the eye will be able to see.

Alternatively, if one regularly eats at bedtime powders of the three fruits, iron filings, and licorice mixed with ghee and honey, it dispels all eye diseases and also becomes an aphrodisiac. If one boils powder of the three fruits in a decoction of the three fruits, milk, and ghee, and drinks a suitable amount of the ghee that remains each night, it also dispels dim vision before long. In a decoction of rāsnā, the three fruits, and the ten roots, together with ghee, one boils powder of the ten medicines called the jīvaka group; drinking a suitable amount of the ghee that remains also dispels dim vision.

Now I shall explain the procedure for ear diseases. The signs of an ear disease arising from wind are stabbing pain and aching in both ears, deafness, noises, and discharge. That arising from bile is a burning heat. That arising from phlegm is that the inside of the ear is dry and smells foul.

The medicines for treating them are these: if one fills the ear with the warm juice of any one of muraṅga, chushing, śigru, radish, or fresh ginger mixed with rock salt, moves it about so that it soaks in, and then pours it out, it dispels ear disease. Ground rock salt is wrapped in a yellow arka leaf, and on the outside this is further covered with seven layers of arka leaves and placed in embers; it is roasted until the inner leaves are almost scorched, and then the outer leaves are thrown away and the rock salt inside is thrown away too. Pouring into the ear the juice squeezed from the still-moist inner yellow leaf also dispels ear disease. The tips of twigs from any of the bilva group, deodar, or torchwood are wrapped in cloth, dipped in sesame oil, and set alight; pouring into the ear, while warm, the liquid that drips down also dispels ear disease. If powders of costus, budranshing, sweet flag, deodar, shuti, asafoetida, and rock salt are boiled in urine and sesame oil, and the sesame oil that remains is poured warm into the ear, it also dispels the torment of ear disease. In a decoction of costus, chebulic myrobalan, and the like, one boils licorice, butsé to, mung beans, coriander, and pig fat; pouring into the ear the pig's grease that remains also dispels earache and noises in the ear. When the ear aches unbearably, has noises, and oozes serum, one pours into the ear something warmed and mixed with chenyangtsa. Alternatively, pouring into the ear powders of the leaves of varuṇa, kapittha, mango, and jambū boiled in sesame oil and water, the sesame oil being what remains, or else the juice of jasmine leaves, dispels the ailment of pus discharging from the ear. If, even after this method has been applied, dried pus remains inside, one should first pour in an oily medicine prepared with sesame oil or medicated ghee to make it able to come out, and apply fumigation; after that one should clean out the putrid matter with a soft scraper.

Now I shall explain the varieties of nose disease. The nose disease called catarrh arises from the three kinds of humor. That arising from wind is a thin discharge from the nose.

That arising from bile is a discharge yellow in color. That arising from phlegm is a discharge that comes with difficulty. That arising from all the humors combined has all the signs. The treatment for catarrh is this: the physician should, according to procedure, anoint the head with oil, apply fomentation, pour medicine into the nose, give emetics, have the patient drink medicated ghee, and have him eat foods that are hot and sour, and so forth. Or else, for catarrh, all kinds of fragrant medicines should be burned inside two vessels joined mouth to mouth, and the smoke, drawn off through a tube, should be inhaled through the mouth and nose. Or else the so-called four fragrances, namely cinnamon, cardamom, gandhapatra and nāgakeśara, compounded together, or else black thaphé ground into a fine powder, should be snuffed into the nose. If one eats a preparation of the powder of lizirwa, tamāla and the three hot spices boiled in ghee and molasses, it too dispels catarrh, cough, pain in the sides of the ribs, pain in the heart, and pain in the kidneys and loins. The powder of vyāghrī, danti root, sweet flag, śigru, chiurukpa, the three hot spices and rock salt should be boiled in sesame oil and water; if what remains, the sesame oil alone, is poured into the nose, it dispels the disorder of pus discharging from the nose that comes from neglected catarrh.

Now I shall explain the procedure for diseases of the tongue. Diseases of the tongue are of three kinds. The tongue disease arising from wind is this: the taste of food is not perceived, the tongue becomes cracked and stiff, and it turns red like the leaves of the śāka tree. The signs of that arising from bile are that the tongue is rough, hot and as if blistered, with a stinging burning. The signs of that arising from phlegm are that the tongue is swollen thick all over and numb. When the tongue has become numb in this way, one should scrape the tongue and let out the diseased bad blood, and then, according to whichever humor it arose from, carry out the procedures from the group of medicines for that particular humor: pouring medicine into the nose, filling the cheeks and spitting it out, and anointing the tongue.

Now I shall explain the procedure for aching teeth. The two kinds of tooth disease, shooting pain and the teeth being set on edge, arise from wind. For both of these, one should rinse the mouth and spit out with ghee or sesame oil combined with wind medicines and warmed, or with a warm decoction of boiled wind medicines. Or else, if the teeth are rubbed with the powder of putseshel, nutgrass, tejovatī, pāṭhā, turmeric, barberry, sengtrom, costus and samaṅgā, it dispels itching of the gums, bleeding of the teeth, and shooting pain. Heat with eruptions breaking out in the mouth arises from a disturbance of blood and bile. For this one should let blood, give a purgative, and fill the mouth with any one of ghee, sesame oil, honey, milk or cow's urine, hold it there for a long time, and then spit it out. Or else, if one fills the cheeks with a cooled decoction of boiled jasmine leaves, guḍūcī, grapes, chitsher, barberry and the three fruits, mixed with honey, and spits it out, it dispels heat and eruptions in the mouth. The disease in which a small uvula sits or swells in the hollow of the palate arises from phlegm and a disturbance of blood. It should be lanced, and then a compound of the three hot spices, rock salt, sweet flag and honey should be placed in the mouth and the liquid spat out, so as to draw out the phlegm.

The throat disease called śāluka, in which round eruptions like juniper berries break out inside the throat, arises from phlegm. Its procedure is to lance it with the bloodletting instrument, the finger lancet bound on with a thread; then, as before, the medicine suited to it should be rubbed on and the liquid spat out. The throat disease called rohiṇī, in which a long fleshy outgrowth like a sprout arises inside the throat, arises from each of the three humors singly, from all of them combined, and from faults of the blood; it should be recognized by means of the respective signs. There is a treatment for it, but since, if it grows large, it can even endanger life, the treatment should be carried out swiftly. The procedure for all of these is to perform bloodletting and let out blood, then place in the mouth again and again a compound of the medicines for whichever particular humor it arose from, and draw out the phlegm. For that arising from wind, rock salt, black salt and tsabru salt combined with honey should be placed in the mouth and the liquid spat out. For that arising from bile and from blood, the powder of sugar and mashing combined with honey should be placed in the mouth; and for that arising from phlegm, the powder of house soot and the three hot spices combined with honey should be placed in the mouth and the liquid spat out. If the powder of the medicines rāsnā, black salt, the three hot spices, house soot, nutgrass and the ash of burnt barley is combined with honey, placed in the mouth, and the liquid spat out, this too cures every disease of the throat. Or else, placing in the mouth the powder of rock salt, orpiment, realgar, lac and barberry combined with honey, and drawing out the phlegm, is supreme for dispelling diseases of the throat and diseases of the mouth. The powder of cinnamon, cardamom, gandhapatra, black pepper, the ash of burnt barley, the ash of the pata tree, the ash of kuśa, and the five hot ones, namely long pepper, long pepper root, cavya, citraka and dry ginger, should be combined with molasses and water that have been boiled until cooked and then taken off; if this is made into pills, placed in the mouth, and the liquid spat out, it too dispels diseases of the throat. Likewise, if the bark of barberry root, tejovatī, long pepper and barberry extract are combined, as before, with boiled molasses and made into pills, placed in the mouth, and the liquid spat out, they too dispel diseases of the throat and diseases with eruptions breaking out in the mouth. If kuṭaja, white aconite, pine, pāṭhā, putseshel and nutgrass are boiled in cow's urine until a quarter remains, then cooled, strained, mixed with honey and drunk, this too dispels diseases of the throat.

Now I shall explain the varieties of head disease. Head disease arising from wind is a feeling as though the brain were empty, with shooting pain. That arising from bile is extremely hot. That arising from phlegm is heavy and numb.

That arising from all three combined has all the signs. The disease in which there are worms inside the head is itching and shooting pain. The head disease called "turning like the sun," which comes on when the sun rises and subsides when the sun sets, and pain in half the head: these two should be known to arise from wind and bile. The head disease with shooting, unbearable pain in the temples arises from wind, blood and phlegm; it is incurable and should be left untreated.

For head disease arising from wind, one should carry out the medicinal procedures that cure wind disease. Or else, taking as the base a decoction of medicines that dispel wind, one should anoint and foment the head with what is called a compound broth, a broth of rice, sesame and Mön beans mixed together, or with rice-milk porridge and the like. Or else the powder of long pepper, costus, zedoary, utpala and sandalwood is boiled in the juice of the karkoṭa tree and sesame oil, and when only the sesame oil remains it is strained and poured into the nose. Or else the powders of medicines and the decoctions of medicines should be combined with ghee and anointed on the head. Or else, in a decoction of boiled licorice, orpiment, rāsnā and the ten roots, together with ghee, the powders of sweet-tasting medicines, such as the ten medicines called "the life-giving," are boiled; when only the ghee remains it is strained, and if one drinks it in the proper measure it dispels all diseases above the collarbones. If the powder of jīvaka, zangtsiwa, grapes, sugar, licorice, balā and utpala is boiled in sesame oil and milk mixed half and half, and when only the sesame oil remains it is strained and poured into the nose in the proper measure, it dispels head disease arising from wind and bile.

For head disease arising from bile, the powder of balā, tiger's claw, putseshel, licorice, utpala and sandalwood is ground together with milk and anointed on the head. Or else milk, or a decoction of cooling medicines and the like, is poured over the head. Or else the powder of cinnamon, gandhapatra and sugar is mixed with the water in which rice has been washed, strained, and poured into the nose. Or else the powder of grapes, sugar and licorice is boiled in ghee and milk, and when only the ghee remains it is strained and poured into the nose.

For one afflicted by head disease arising from phlegm, first the heartwood of the madhuka tree is pounded coarsely and boiled, and the head is fumigated with its rising steam. Or else the head is anointed with the powder of pine, rohiṣa, śaraṃgata, rock salt and drönshing ground together with water, and after that medicine should be poured into the nose to draw yellow fluid out through the nose. The medicine for drawing yellow fluid out of the nose is this: if the powder of zedoary, eraṇḍa root, sweet flag, gyapö and vyāghrī fruit is boiled in sesame oil and water, and what remains, the sesame oil alone, is poured into the nose, it dispels the eye disease of dim vision arising from wind and phlegm, and all diseases above the collarbones.

For one who has head disease arising from all three humors, old ghee should be drunk. Or else one should also carry out whatever medicinal procedures benefit the three humors. For one who has head disease arising from worms, if a decoction of boiled three hot spices, gyapö and śigru seeds is poured into the nose, it dispels the worms. Or else, if the powder of the fruit of the apāmārga tree, the three hot spices, turmeric, shawa, asafoetida and viḍaṅga is boiled in cow's urine and sesame oil, and when only the sesame oil remains it is strained and poured into the nose, it dispels the worms.

For the two, the disease that aches from sunrise until sunset and pain in half the head, the powder of taltré, licorice and costus is mixed with sour gruel and smeared on the head. Or else one should pour over the head a decoction of boiled medicines that benefit wind and bile. As food, one should eat foods that benefit wind and bile, such as shitiu. If a decoction of boiled jasmine ash, nutgrass, cinnamon and cotton fruit, or else the powder of licorice boiled with ghee and strained, is poured into the nose, it dispels all diseases of the head. Or else, if the powder of dry ginger, viḍaṅga and licorice is boiled in a decoction of boiled bhṛṅgarāja together with ghee, and six drops of it are poured into the nose, it dispels all diseases above the collarbones.

Now I shall explain the remedies for white hair. If the powder of prapuṇḍarīka, licorice, long pepper, utpala and sandalwood is boiled in a decoction of boiled emblic myrobalan and sesame oil, and what remains, the sesame oil alone, is poured into the nose, it makes white hair and tawny hair disappear. Or else, if the oils of menram, śelu, sesame and beleric myrobalan are boiled with the juices of bhṛṅgarāja, nīlī, kuranda and śirīṣa, and what remains, the oil alone, is constantly poured into the nose, white hair disappears. The powder of peacock bile, lotus root, shingla ko, matayanti, tongzil, utpala, nīlī, bhṛṅgarāja and green vitriol is boiled in beleric myrobalan oil and water; when only the beleric myrobalan oil remains it is strained and kept in an iron vessel for one month. If it is then anointed on the head and poured into the nose, it at once makes disappear both white hair that has come before its time and white hair that has come in its time. If iron powder, rock salt and rice are boiled in any one of sour gruel, salt water or whey, smeared on the head at night, and washed off in the morning with a decoction of the three fruits, the hair becomes black.

For hair falling out in round patches, if the juice of the medicine sarahata is mixed with honey and anointed on, the hair will grow. Or else one should smear on the root or the fruit of the ölmasé, either one, mixed with cow's urine, or else the juice of gojé. If the sprouts of the pata tree and the powder of bhṛṅgarāja are mixed with a decoction of guḍūcī, warmed in the heat of the sun, and anointed on, hair that does not grow will also grow.

One who wishes to benefit eye disease should constantly drink ghee, use preparations of the three fruits, perform bloodletting, and give purgatives; and as food one should eat rice, Mön beans, barley, and the meat of wild animals that live in dry country.

This completes the twenty-sixth chapter, the Eye-Medicine Tantra.

The Siddhasāra: the seventh fascicle, which is the last.

Next I shall explain the chapter of the tantra on curing poison. Poison is of two kinds: plant poison, and the bite poison of scorpions and snakes. Of these, plant poison has the nature of roots and the like; the latter arises from snakes and the like. Poison is said to have ten properties for good and ill: lightness, unmanifest taste, subtlety so that it enters the channels, roughness, great heat, swiftness, pervasiveness, gathering and drawing phlegm, carrying odor, and sharpness.

Snakes are said to be of three kinds, distinguished as the hooded type, the coiling type and the striped type, which have respectively the nature of wind, the nature of bile and the nature of phlegm; they are matched one by one in that order. The mixed types have the signs of two mixed together. For one bitten by a snake of the hooded type, the wound is black, and the bite produces all the varieties and signs of wind disease. For one bitten by a snake of the coiling type, the wound is large, yellow in color and swollen, and the bite produces all the varieties and signs of bile disease. For one bitten by a snake of the striped type, the edge of the wound is pale, the swelling quickly becomes hard and will not yield to pressure, it is sticky and oily, and the blood that oozes out is very thick; the bite produces all the varieties and signs of phlegm disease.

Of those bitten by a snake on the day of the lunar mansions Maghā, Hasta, Kṛttikā, Āśleṣā, Bharaṇī, Pūrvaphalgunī, Pūrvāṣāḍhā and Uttarabhādrapadā, some will die even if they are treated with the greatest care. A snakebite on the fourth, fifth, sixth, ninth and twenty-ninth days of the month, and at the two junctures of time, daybreak and dusk, is also said to be severe. Among those bitten by a snake, one whose hair comes out when pulled, on whose body no mark appears when it is rubbed or struck, and whose body hair does not stand up and on whom no gooseflesh appears even in extreme cold, should be given up.

As soon as the person has been bitten, ghee should first be given quickly, in order to protect the heart so that the poison does not seize it. After that, it is taught that one should bind the place that was bitten, press and draw out the poison with a suction horn and the like, surround the bitten place with water, cauterize the wound, and let blood from the wound and the area around it.

For one bitten by a snake of the hooded type, if one drinks the juice of nirguṇḍī and śvetā, the poison is dispelled. Or else the powder of the root of sinbu varaja should be moistened with its own juice and dried, moistened and dried again, many times in this way, and then boiled and drunk. For one bitten by a snake of the coiling type, the powder of the tips of the branches of the banyan tree, madder, jīvaka, zangtsiwa, sugar, tambala and licorice should be mixed with the water in which rice has been washed, or with water boiled and cooled, and drunk. For one bitten by a snake of the striped type, if the powder of hareṇukā, costus, gyapö, the three hot spices, putseshel, white aconite, licorice and house soot is mixed with cold boiled water and drunk, it dispels the poison.

If the powder of spikenard, sandalwood, rock salt, long pepper, licorice, black pepper, utpala and cow's bile is mixed with water, strained, and applied to the eyes, one who has been struck by poison and has fainted will rise up again. Or else, if these, naktamāla, pösoncha, the three hot spices, bilva root, turmeric, barberry and chiurukpa flowers, are powdered, mixed with goat's urine, strained, and applied to the eyes, consciousness will also return. If the powder of mayūraka seeds, śirīṣa seeds and rock salt, or else the powder of barley, pösoncha seeds and pata, is mixed with water, strained, and poured into the nose, consciousness will also return. If the powder of the root of the medicine karkoṭa is steeped many times in goat's urine, ground together with gruel, strained, and poured into the nose, one whose mind has been impaired by poison will also regain consciousness.

The powder of the fruits of śirīṣa, ariṣṭa, naktamāla, balā and dodaśataka, ground together with cow's urine and strained, is the supreme antidote to poison: if it is drunk or otherwise applied, it dispels the poison. A compound of aṅkoṭa, ariṣṭa, datura, snuhakṣīrī, karañja, aśomara, punarnavā, citraka, pali, jasmine flowers, kuṭaja bark and arka is also a great antidote to poison. A compound of gyapö, black pepper, realgar, pine, naktamāla, arka, turmeric, barberry, śirīṣa and long pepper is also an antidote to poison and dispels poison. The seeds of the bitter gourd ground together with cow's bile are called Brahmā's antidote: if this is drunk or otherwise applied, it dispels all poisons. This medicine too, the five parts of the śirīṣa, its root, bark, leaves, flowers and seeds, ground together with cow's urine, dispels poison. The powder of madder, cardamom, turmeric, barberry, lac, spikenard, licorice and hareṇukā mixed with honey is also an antidote to poison: it dispels poison, restores consciousness after fainting, and performs every such function. The antidote of the powder of the five salts, shingnyerong, dantī root, vaśira, the three hot spices, turmeric, barberry and madder, mixed with honey and kept inside a horn, also performs every function.

The antidote compounded of the powders of sandalwood, orpiment, realgar, costus, cinnamon, gandhapatra, cardamom, nutgrass, white mustard seed, spikenard, juniper, gyapö, saffron, chiurukpa, ekahā, bamboo, spṛkkā, asafoetida, bālaka, lāmajjaka, zedoary and gandhapriyaṅgu is called Moonrise, and it destroys all poisons. This antidote, the powder of shingnyerong, nāgakeśara, pāṭalī, long pepper, madder, kiṇihī, realgar, gobitar, black pepper, gyapö, turmeric, barberry, white pok, aparājitā, bṛhatī and licorice ground together with cow's urine, is called Sunrise, and it overcomes poison and grahas. A compound of īśvarī, plantain, nagi, candrā, śvetā, red Mön nyeu and nirguṇḍī, or else any one of them singly, is supreme for destroying poison.

Ash of the lac tree is mixed with water and strained, and the impurities and dregs are thrown away. Into this the powder of hareṇukā, costus, gyapö, the three hot spices, chiurukpa, taltré, nutgrass, spikenard, asafoetida, turmeric, barberry, licorice, viḍaṅga and rock salt is boiled, cooked until it is just thick enough to cling to a ladle, and kept in a horn. If every morning a dose of it the size of a juniper berry is mixed with any one of rice-washing water, milk or rainwater and drunk, it dispels even poison of great potency, and it does away with chronic illness, abdominal tumors, dropsy, hemorrhoids, urinary disorders, weak digestive heat and fevers.

Or else, for one who has drunk poison, one should give an emetic of the juice of cow dung mixed with honey and make him vomit. Whichever of the many antidote compounds one gives, one should first quickly give ghee to drink, in order to protect the heart so that the poison does not seize it. For root poison and for wounds made by a poisoned arrow, ghee compounded with turmeric, rock salt and honey is supremely good; it should be drunk. If one eats a paste of copper and gold powder mixed with sugar and honey, it pacifies even all the most violent compounded poisons.

For one stung by a scorpion, one should fumigate with the smoke of palm leaves, nimba leaves, hair and old cloth burned with ghee, or else with the smoke of peacock feathers burned with milk. Or else, if the wound is anointed with a paste of palāśa seeds ground together with the milky sap of the arka tree, or with a paste compounded of long pepper and śirīṣa fruit, it relieves one afflicted by scorpion poison. Or else pills made of the powder of realgar, rock salt, asafoetida, jasmine leaves and dry ginger mixed with the juice of cow dung also relieve one afflicted by scorpion poison.

For the poison of a rat's bite, if the powder of the roots of sesame, karkoṭa, tilaka and gikarṇikā is mixed with sugar, honey and ghee and drunk, it dispels the poison of the rat bite. Or else, for the poison of a rat bite, one should drink the powder of hacara root mixed with honey and rice-washing water, or else either the flowers or the leaves of the tilaka ground together with milk. Or else, if one drinks the broth of either cat meat or mongoose meat, cooked, it quickly dispels the poison of a rat bite together with its virulent complications.

For one bitten by a mad dog, the tips of the branches and the roots of the aṅkoṭa tree are boiled down; if one srang of clarified butter is added to three srang of this decoction and drunk, it dispels the poison of the mad dog's bite. Or else, if fresh butter and ground sesame powder are mixed with the milky sap of the arka tree and sesame oil and drunk, it quickly dispels even the hard-to-cure poison of a vicious dog's tooth.

The antidote for the bites of all small creatures is this: the powder of the ash of burnt barley, the ash of svarjikā, the three hot spices, sweet flag, asafoetida, viḍaṅga, rock salt, gyapö, ambarta, white aconite and costus should be put into rice-washing water and anointed on. If the powder of shingnyerong root and red Mön nyeu, in equal parts, is mixed with ghee and drunk, this too quickly dispels even the most potent poison of every small creature. Again, a compound of pigeon droppings, dantī root, cow's tooth, rock salt, shingnyerong, the seeds of légentsi, snuhakṣīrī, citraka and karṇikāra is also supreme for dispelling the poison of small creatures. A compound of sandalwood, juniper, costus, gyapö, putseshel, pāṭalā, nirguṇḍī, taltré and śelu is a medicine that dispels the poison of every small creature with venomous mouths. A compound of these, kapittha, pāṭalī, śelu, śirīṣa, the two kinds of punarnavā and the two kinds of śvetā, is also a medicine that dispels all the poison of every small creature with venomous mouths.

For all others afflicted by poison, apart from the poison of a scorpion's sting, one should carry out cooling procedures in food, medicine and so forth. No one struck by any poison should give way to anger, sit in the sun, sleep in the daytime, or have sexual intercourse. Those who, after being struck by poison, are seized by other virulent diseases such as fever should be cured swiftly with whatever medicine is proper to each. Those afflicted by poison should eat and drink foods that do not produce heat.

This completes the twenty-seventh chapter, the tantra on curing poison.

Next I shall explain the chapter of the tantra on rejuvenation and on aphrodisiacs. A medicine that does away with white hair and illness is called rejuvenation. It should be undertaken in youth, in young manhood, or else in middle age. One should begin only after first cleansing the body inside and out with emetics, purgatives, bathing and the like.

The method of preparing it is this. A vessel is filled with a compound of ghee, honey, iron powder and the powder of emblic myrobalan fruit and set in the middle of a heap of barley for half a year; if one then eats of it continually in the proper measure, it does away with the whitening of the hair. Or else, if the powder of chebulic myrobalan, long pepper, viḍaṅga, emblic myrobalan and sugar, together with iron powder, is mixed with ghee and sesame oil and eaten, one is not overcome by old age. If a man eats viḍaṅga, saptaparṇa, emblic myrobalan and iron powder mixed with honey, ghee and sesame oil, and becomes youthful, that is no great wonder. If the powder of viḍaṅga, the three fruits and long pepper, together with iron powder, is mixed with ghee, sugar and honey and eaten continually, it too dispels aging and the whitening of the hair. Iron powder, the powder of sugar, dry ginger and long pepper, ghee and sesame oil, poured into a decoction of the three fruits and boiled until just thick enough to cling to a ladle, also becomes a rejuvenating medicine.

Iron is beaten flat, heated red-hot, and plunged into a cooled decoction of the three fruits until it stops boiling, and this is done many times. If that iron is filed with a file, and the powder is mixed with honey and ghee and eaten, it too brings long life. Or else a decoction of boiled emblic myrobalan, ghee and honey, in equal parts of a hundred srang each, and two hundred srang of iron powder are compounded, poured into an iron vessel and kept; this too is supreme for bringing long life. If seed oil, beer and the powder of the three fruits are mixed with honey and eaten, it too does away with white hair, the harbinger of Yama. If the powder of viḍaṅga, ghee, honey and iron powder, in equal parts, are compounded, poured into a vessel, and set in the middle of a heap of Mön beans, and one eats of it in the proper measure, the hair turns dark blue and one becomes strong. A hundred srang of tambala powder is compounded with a hundred srang of ghee and honey and kept in a vessel for one month; if one eats of it continually and takes food with milk, it does away with white hair and one becomes beautiful like the moon. A hundred srang of the powder of the root of the medicine vārāhī is mixed with honey; if one eats it little by little, follows it with milk, takes food with milk and ghee, and does this diligently, one will be endowed with youthful vigor. Or else, for one month, one eats a paste of finely ground powder of the medicine vārāhī mixed with honey; if after that one drinks vārāhī powder boiled with milk, cooled and strained, this too becomes a rejuvenating medicine. If half a srang of the finely ground powder of punarnavā root is put into milk that has been boiled and taken off the fire, and drunk continually for two months or a year, one will also be free of aging. If nyeushing, saptaparṇa, putseshel, pāṭhā, balā, nāgabalā, vidārī, taltré and vyāghrī are each taken singly, following the procedure for punarnavā explained above, the benefits will be the same.

For one who wishes to put on flesh: for half a month one should drink the powder of the medicine aśvagandhā mixed with any one of sesame oil, ghee, milk or warm boiled water, and eat suitable food. Those who wish to practice rejuvenation should always eat, as their food, rice that ripens in sixty days cooked with milk, mixed with any one of the broth of Mön beans, trotter broth, or the meat broth of wild animals that live in dry country.

The procedure for making virile is this. If one constantly eats two zho of licorice powder mixed with ghee and honey, and drinks milk after it, it makes even an old man virile. If one srang of Mön beans at a time is taken following the procedure above, it will have the same effect. If one drinks the powder of ātmaguptā seeds, tribulus root and sugar mixed with milk, or else the powder of the medicine uccaṭā mixed with sugar and milk, one too becomes virile. A paste of vidārī powder steeped many times in its own juice, ground, and mixed with ghee and honey is said to be extremely aphrodisiac. Whoever eats sesame steeped many times in milk in which the testicles of a he-goat have been well cooked and dissolved, and afterwards drinks milk, will have his member always erect. Or else Mön beans are husked and finely ground, and the powder is boiled in milk and ghee until it becomes a very thick porridge; it is taken off, cooled, mixed with honey, and made into pills the size of a bilva fruit. If one eats one of these every day, the member becomes erect and one becomes capable of intercourse. Rice flour is mixed with the powder of sesame, ātmaguptā and Mön beans, kneaded with milk, and cooked in ghee; eating this too is said to be extremely aphrodisiac. Foods and drinks of every kind, pleasing music and voices, incense, unguents and various fragrant things, flowers, garlands, clothes, fine ornaments and the like are also causes that make a man virile.

This completes the twenty-eighth chapter, the tantra on rejuvenation and aphrodisiacs.

Next I shall explain the chapter of the tantra on children. Women should be cherished, for they are a sacred field and give birth to priceless children. If disease arises in their genitals, both joy and the birth of children are lost; therefore I shall explain the procedure for curing it.

The signs of a disease of the female genitals arising from wind are pain in the genitals, a rough color, twisting, hardness, and frothing. That arising from a disturbance of bile is to be recognized by burning heat in the genitals, dripping of blood, and the onset of fever. Disease of the female genitals arising from a disturbance of phlegm is itching, coldness, numbness, and the oozing of a sticky discharge, white like rice water.

The procedure for curing a disease of the female genitals arising from wind is this: apply a fomentation of rice cooked in milk, or a fomentation of boiled Mön beans; or else soak a soft cotton cloth in warm sesame oil, heat it, and apply it as a fomentation. In short, for a disease of the female genitals arising from wind, drinking medicated ghee, fomentation, and oil enemas are good.

Alternatively, for a disease of the genitals, administer the powdered root of long pepper and the root of zintiklé mixed with beer; or else administer the powder of long pepper, the seeds of upakuñcikā, and black salt mixed with beer.

Take two zho each of the powders of rock exudate, the three fruits, bhārgī, rāsnā, guḍūcī, nyeushing, turmeric, barberry, punarnavā, and med; add thirty-two srang of ghee and milk in the same measure as the ghee, and boil them until only the ghee remains. If this, strained, is drunk in a suitable amount, it too cures disease of the female genitals arising from wind; it is also good for disease of the female genitals arising from bile, and it is supreme for bringing about conception.

For disease of the female genitals arising from a disturbance of bile, follow the procedure for removing the disease of bleeding explained earlier. For disease of the female genitals arising from phlegm, do everything with the group beginning with āragvadha: drinks, washes, douches, ointments, and so on.

Having thus rid the genitals of faults and disease, the method for bringing about conception is this. The man should first strengthen his body with medicines and good food and stay without sleeping with a woman. If the woman bathes after four days have passed since her menstruation began, and they then lie together, a child will be conceived. If they lie together when four, six, eight, ten, or twelve days have passed since menstruation began, the child will be a boy. If they lie together when five, seven, nine, or eleven days have passed since menstruation began, it will be a girl. If the man's semen is abundant and the woman's blood scanty, it will be a boy; if the man's semen is scanty and the woman's blood abundant, it will be a girl. From the thirteenth day after menstruation began onward, no child will come.

If one wishes for a boy, grind with milk the powder of rock salt, or of the branches or tips of the banyan tree, whichever is at hand, and strain it; if four drops of that liquid are poured into the woman's left nostril before they lie together, it will be a boy. When the woman is menstruating, if she drinks the powder of khalung seed or of nāgakeśara, whichever is at hand, mixed with melted butter, and lies with a man, then too she will conceive.

The sign of conception is this: if the nipples are a bluish gray color, know that there is a child. Once she has thus become pregnant, she should be guarded against violent work, grief, exhaustion, and anger, and she should be given whatever her mind desires and whatever suits her. If she is frightened and alarmed, struck and injured, or eats and drinks food and drink that are hot and very pungent, the fetus will slip away. This is to be recognized by the signs of bleeding and pain, and then the procedure for restoring it should be carried out. To cure such a case, pouring cold medicinal decoctions over the body, immersing her in cold medicinal decoctions, and anointing the body with cooling medicinal pastes are good.

Further, the medicines for restoring a fetus that is slipping away are these. Boil the ten life-giving medicines with milk, strain, mix with sugar, and administer. Again: the powders of white sandalwood, butsangto, grapes, bamboo manna, and sugar combined together; the powders of licorice, juniper berries, cardamom, madder, dhātakī flowers, and sugar combined together; and the powders of sugar, utpala, licorice, sengtrom, white sandalwood, and taltré combined together: administer any one of these three medicinal formulas mixed with rice-washing water. Or else grind the members of the group beginning with utpala, among the groups of medicines explained earlier, and administer them mixed with sugar and milk. Or else administer the young leaves of the group beginning with the banyan (nyagrodha), or the powder of their bark, whichever is at hand, mixed with sugar and milk. Or else administer the strained decoction of root medicines that grow in water, such as the lotus, mixed with rice flour and sugar; or else boil those root medicines with milk, strain, and administer mixed with sugar.

As for the pregnant woman's food: from the beginning of pregnancy until the first, second, and third months have passed, give her sweet and cooling foods. When the fourth month arrives, give her food with butter. When the fifth month arrives, give her food with clarified butter and milk. When the sixth and seventh months arrive, administer good broths and medicated ghee of gokṣura and the like, or ghee prepared as taught earlier. In the eighth month, give her food with milk, and give a nourishing enema of sesame oil and licorice boiled in milk, strained when only the sesame oil remains. From then on, as food, broths, trotters, and rich meat broths of wild animals that live on dry land, moistened and eaten, are good.

Then, when the child is born, cut its navel cord, leaving about eight finger-widths, wash the infant with gently warm water, and put into its mouth just a little ghee and honey mixed together in unequal proportions. Then make an electuary of gold powder and fine powders of sweet flag, brāhmī, harītakī, and costus mixed with ghee and honey; or else mix gold powder with a decoction of brāhmī. If a very little of either is given continually, the child will become intelligent and of good complexion.

As for the woman who has given birth: for three or five days after the birth, administer sesame oil or melted butter in a suitable amount, and give her somewhat oily food. When the afterbirth will not come out, administer the powders of the group beginning with long pepper explained earlier, mixed with beer; or else about two zho of the powder of rice root mixed with urine or a sour liquid, whichever is at hand, and it will come out.

When the child will not come out even though the months are complete, mix with ghee the powder of any one of the following, the root of the vaduśaka tree, lāṅgalī, or vāsaka, and smear it on the genitals and the navel; then it will come out.

After the child is born, a disease called alaka arises in the woman. Its signs are pain in the heart, the head, and the bladder, with stabbing pains. For it, administer the ash of roasted barley mixed with either ghee or warm boiled water; or else the decoction of the group beginning with long pepper mixed with rock salt; or else the decoction of coriander fruit mixed with butter, the three pungents, and the powders of what are called the three aromatics, namely cinnamon, cardamom, and gandhapatra.

Or else, for a disease arising from childbirth, quench red-hot iron in a decoction of the five roots, doing this many times; or else quench heated iron in beer as before; if this is administered, the disease will subside.

If the child's mother or the wet nurse who suckles it eats and drinks heavy food and drink, eats and drinks at irregular times, early and late, and in amounts too large or too small, the three humors, wind and the rest, become disturbed and the breast milk turns unwholesome, and from that various kinds of disease arise in the child.

The sign that breast milk has been spoiled by wind is to be recognized from this: the milk itself tastes astringent, and when poured into water it floats on top. The sign that breast milk has been spoiled by bile is this: the milk tastes hot and sour, and when it is poured into water, yellow streaks appear on the surface. The sign that breast milk has been spoiled by phlegm is this: the milk is thick, very heavy, and sticky, and when poured into water it sinks to the bottom. Breast milk that has not been spoiled by the humors, by contrast, mixes with the water when poured into it and stays a pale color.

The method for removing the fault of breast milk spoiled by the humors and restoring it is this. First give the child's mother or the wet nurse who suckles it melted butter to drink. After that, administer an emetic made of long pepper powder and honey mixed with a decoction of nimba, and make her vomit. Give her food with the essence of Mön bean soup and meat broth. Or else, for spoiled breast milk, she should be purified by drinking digestive medicines: a decoction of bhārgī, devadāru, sweet flag, pāṭhā, and white aconite; or else a decoction of the group beginning with āragvadha; or else a decoction of the group beginning with musta.

For a child struck by fever, take equal parts of the powders of parched rice, khaṇḍa sugar, sugar, bamboo manna, and licorice; if an electuary of these mixed with honey is given, it cures all fevers. Or else, if one gives the powder of long pepper, white aconite, and bubrenshing ground together and mixed with honey, or else an electuary of the powder of white aconite alone mixed with honey, it cures fever, cough, and the diseases of vomiting. If an electuary of the powders of parched rice, rock salt, and longkok wood mixed with honey is given, it cures the disease of vomiting. If an electuary of bamboo manna mixed with honey is given, it also cures a child's cough and the disease of difficult breathing. If an electuary of the powders of parched rice and rock salt mixed with the powder of khalung fruit is given, or else an electuary of parched rice, sugar, rock salt, ghee, and honey mixed together, it also cures vomiting and the disease of hiccups.

If an electuary is given of the ground powders of great long pepper, dhātakī, sengtrom, bilva, and bālaka mixed with honey, or else the decoction of those same medicines, cooled and mixed with honey, it cures children's dropsy and swelling of the belly. If a decoction of samaṅgā, dhātakī flowers, taltré, and sengtrom is cooled, mixed with honey, and given, it too cures dropsy.

For children's eye diseases, for what is called kukūṇaka, and for what is called bothak, one should apply an eye salve of the powders of lalapü, vidaṅga, bali, orpiment, barberry, lac, and chakyuk cloth. Or else, if the outside of the eyes is smeared with a paste of the powders of devadāru, red ochre, and musta, strained together with goat's milk, it cures eye disease.

When sores break out in a child's mouth and thrush arises, one should smear on an electuary of the bark of the aśvattha tree and powdered costus mixed with honey, or else the powders of barberry, licorice, harītakī, and jasmine leaves mixed with honey.

Take the powders of the medicine gaurī, licorice, the root of nyeushing, sengtrom, śālaparṇī, pṛśniparṇī, rājādana, sugar, juniper, sandalwood, grapes, lotus flowers, kumuda, utpala, jīvaka, zangtsiwa, medā, butsangto, marnyewa, and the two kinds of taltré; thirty-two srang of milk twice over; the five barks, as they are called, that is, the bark of the nyagrodha tree, the bark of the udumbara, the bark of the aśvattha, the bark of the plakṣa, and the bark of the vetasa; and the five roots. Boil the decoction of these in four times the measure of ghee, and strain it when only the ghee remains. Its name is the medicated ghee of gaurī and the rest. It is good for the disease called jyotika, for erysipelas arising from bile, for thrush with sores breaking out in the mouth, and for those seized by grahas, the seizing spirits. If it is administered in a suitable amount, it cures every disease of children.

When a child cannot sleep at night, cries and cannot be soothed, gives off a bad smell, and behaves in various ways, flailing its body and limbs and so on, grinding its teeth and scratching with its nails, these come from a disease caused by grahas, the seizing spirits. For this, there are three fumigation formulas: garlic, nimba leaves, lac, and bamboo bark combined together; white mustard seed, nimba leaves, bamboo bark, and lac combined together; and a shed snake skin, old flowers that have been offered to the gods with sound, and white mustard seed combined together. If the child is bathed with the cold decoction of the leaves of madhūka flowers, the aśvattha tree, saptaparṇa, and veluka boiled together, that too dispels grahas.

Or else, in order to pacify the grahas, one should offer torma with this mantra that accomplishes all actions, perform the actions of the peaceful fire offering, tie on a mantra amulet, and so on. The mantra is this: namo spagapaste garuḍāya namastamabakaya satyasatyatanute svāhā

Here ends the twenty-ninth chapter, on the tantra of children.

Next I shall explain the chapter on the five actions. The five actions are to be known as emetics, purgatives, medicine poured into the nose, the strong purgative enema, and the oily enema. I shall explain their procedures.

The method for administering an emetic is this. First the physician gives the patient an oleation of ghee, sesame oil, or any other oily substance. After that come what are called the five kinds of sweating: warming the body by a fire in a warm house; steaming with the vapor of water poured onto heated stones so as to draw out sweat; heating in the sun; fomenting with bundles of heated sweating medicines; and sweating with heated cloths, stones, and the like. When the body has been well warmed with any one of these five kinds of sweating, clothe the patient in warm clothes and give the emetic. Have the patient sit on a seat reaching about to the knees, stir the throat with a castor stalk or something soft such as a bird's feather, and make the patient vomit.

As for the emetic: the powders of long pepper, madana, and rock salt mixed with honey and a decoction of licorice; this is the best of emetics. Keep it warm, administer it warm, and make the patient vomit. Or else there is what is called the compound gruel: a gruel of roasted rice, sesame, and Mön beans, well cooked with madana; or else madana seeds boiled with milk; or else the emetics mentioned above, long pepper and the rest with honey, mixed with the decoction of five madana fruits. If this is drunk warm, the patient will vomit to the heart's satisfaction. Bitter kuba, devadāru, the fruit of kuṭaja, kṛtavedhana, and dhāmārgava are also emetics; each of them is prepared in the same way as madana.

Then, after vomiting, if bile has come up and the belly, the chest, and the head have become light, the emetic was good. If it has not turned out like this, the emetic is said to have been poor. If one does not know the measure of vomiting and it is excessive, it turns into a disease. Its signs are to be recognized from these: pain in the heart, aching in the lungs, injury to the throat, loss of mindfulness and stupor, trembling of the body, and vomiting of blood.

The emetic should be given, in keeping with the patient's strength, for diseases arising from phlegm, severe colds, urinary disease, leprosy, throat swelling, the drinking of poison, and indigestion. It must not be given as an emetic to those who have eye disease with dimness of sight, those with dropsy, tumors, the disease of thirst, those worn down by a wasting disease, pregnant women, those with wind disease, and those who have pallor disease.

The method for administering a purgative is this. First give an oleation of ghee, sesame oil, or any other oily substance. After that, heat the body by sweating as explained before and give an emetic, and after that give the purgative. It is said that if the method for administering the purgative is not followed in this way and the procedure is reversed, it gives rise to disease of the stomach.

A person of bilious nature has soft bowels. A person of the nature of wind or phlegm has hard bowels. Bowels in which the humors are equal in strength are moderate. For these, the dose of the medicine should be estimated as appropriate and then given.

The method for giving a purgative to a person afflicted by a disease arising from wind is this: the powders of nyerong, rock salt, and dry ginger should be drunk mixed with either sour gruel, trotter broth, or the broth of the meat of wild animals that live on dry land. For one who has a disease arising from bile, give a purgative of nyerong powder mixed with the decoction of sweet-tasting medicines, or of any medicine beneficial for bile disease, such as khawa. For one harmed by a disease arising from phlegm, give a purgative of the decoction of the three fruits mixed with the powder of the three pungents and cow's urine. The electuary of the powders of long pepper, dry ginger, nyerong, and the ash of roasted barley mixed with honey is the best purgative for every disease arising from phlegm; it should be eaten. The purgative called the iron arrow, made from the powders of harītakī, rock salt, and long pepper mixed with a warm decoction boiled in water, is the best purgative for curing every disease; it should be drunk. One srang of hwak, one srang of nyerong powder, and two zho of long pepper powder: if these are eaten in a suitable amount as an electuary purgative mixed with honey, it too cures every disease. Add the powders of the three pungents, dantī root, nyerong, harītakī, and shingdong, together with the powders of what are called the three aromatics, namely cinnamon, cardamom, and gandhapatra; mix with butter and roll into pills. This purgative too cures every disease.

For dropsy and the like, steep the powders of nyerong and shingdö many times in the leaves of the snuhī tree, mix with butter and honey, add the three aromatics, and give this electuary as a purgative.

Then, after the purging, if phlegm has come out, the patient has become hungry, the body is light, and the complexion has become clear, the purgative was good. Itching of the body, variegated spots appearing on the body, and heaviness of the body are to be known as signs that the purgative was poor. The signs of an excessive purgative are pain, fainting, the onset of hemorrhoids, the entry of wind, loss of mindfulness, and a discharge from below like the water in which raw meat has been washed.

The purgative should be given to those who have leprosy, hemorrhoids, worm disease, erysipelas, or the dreg disease and pallor disease. It must not be given to those who have a wasting disease, to the emaciated, or to pregnant women.

When, after an emetic has been given, the patient vomits far too much and the vomiting cannot be stopped, if the physician gives just a little of a purgative that is tasty and pleasing to the mind, the vomiting will stop. When, after a purgative has been given, the patient is purged far too much and it cannot be stopped, if a little emetic is given, it will stop. For a purgative that has been far too strong, one should anoint the body with juniper, bamboo manna, nāgakeśara, and sandalwood, pour them over the body, and give them as drinks.

What is called medicine poured into the nose is ghee, sesame oil, or medicine poured into the nose. It is to be known as being of five kinds: pouring ghee or sesame oil alone into the nose; pouring in medicated ghee or medicated sesame oil to draw phlegm and serum out through the nose; smearing the inside of the nose with oil; squeezing a bundle of medicines at the nostrils and pouring it in; and putting medicinal powder into a tube and blowing it into the nose.

The method for administering these kinds of nasal medicine is this. On a day when the patient is free of illness, before eating food, rub the two hands together hard, and with their warmth massage and warm the mouth, the nose, and the whole face thoroughly. Clear away completely all the impurities of the mouth, the nose, and the throat, lower the head, and administer it. The one undergoing the procedure of nasal medicine should avoid what is unsuitable, such as washing the head, anger, sexual intercourse, and sleeping during the day.

The measure of ghee or sesame oil poured into the nose is eight drops; or else dip a finger once beyond its second joint and pour into the nose whatever clings to it. Another measure is to pour half a srang into the nose. Another measure is to pour four, six, or eight drops, estimated from whether the patient's strength is great or small. All these many measures that have been taught are to be given in keeping with whether the patient's strength is great or small. Pouring medicated ghee or sesame oil into the nose to draw phlegm and serum out through the nose is given for diseases from the collarbone upward.

Now I shall explain the procedure of the oily enema of medicated ghee and the nourishing enema. The equipment for the medicinal enema is this. Make a nozzle twelve finger-widths long out of iron, copper, or any such material, tapering and round like the shape of a cow's tail. Make the thick base wide enough for a thumb to fit, and make the thin tip wide enough for the ring finger to fit, and smooth. About three finger-widths back from the thin tip, make a ridge about the size of a spindle whorl. At the mouth of the thick base, tie on firmly a good cow's bladder as the vessel for the medicine. Cut two ridges of suitable size so that the bladder does not slip off, and tie the mouth of the bladder between them.

Then, when the medicine is given, the patient should first eat food and then lie on the left side, with the left leg stretched out and the right one drawn up. Smear the anus and the nozzle with oil. Boil the powders of śuṭī and rock salt with ghee or sesame oil and water, and strain when only the ghee remains; or another medicine may also be used. Taking a measure of two, three, or four srang, matched to the person's own strength and suitably dosed, give it warm as an enema. Insert the tip of the nozzle until it reaches the ridge, hold the nozzle firmly at its middle with the left hand so that no air goes into the bladder, and with the right hand squeeze the bladder, neither too forcefully nor too slowly, without pausing or stopping in between, continuously, finishing it at one time. In order that no air go inside, leave just a little of the medicine remaining.

When the medicine has been given, lay the patient on the back, raise all four limbs upward, and stretch and bend them vigorously three times. Then turn the patient face down and have him strike his buttocks with his own heels two or three times. After that another person should strike the soles of his feet two or three times with the fist, and then he should be made to lie with the lower body raised. With this procedure of medicated ghee, it is not harmful even if it stays coiled within and does not come out until a day has passed. If it cannot come out for even longer than that, then, following the procedure for retention of feces taught earlier, insert a medicinal suppository, or else expel it quickly with a mild purgative and purify. If, although it has not come out, there is no sign of discomfort or heaviness and the patient remains normal, there is no need to give a purgative or the like; drinking warm boiled water will also make it come out.

Ghee coming out mixed with feces is the sign that the medicine was good. Ghee alone coming out, not mixed with feces, and the body becoming hot, are the signs that the medicine was poor. If the ghee has not come out, but the feces have come out normally and there is no sign of discomfort in the belly, then the medicine has been digested inside because the heat of the digestive fire is great, and there is no harm.

As for food, when the patient becomes hungry after the medicine has been given, only then should he eat something light and suitable. If, after the medicinal enema has been given, it comes out before long, the medicine has not taken effect; it should then be repeated and given again by the same procedure as before. Both the nourishing enema of oily medicated ghee and the strong purgative enema are to be given to those who show signs of wind disease.

It must not be given to those who have a wasting disease, leprosy, hemorrhoids, the disease of vomiting, urinary disease, dropsy, and the like.

The procedure for the strong purgative enema is this. Two or three days beforehand, give an oily enema of medicated ghee. On the day the strong purgative enema itself is given, when the sun is warm, from early morning anoint the body with oil and massage it, and with various kinds of sweating make the body thoroughly warm and the flesh relaxed. From the morning on, without eating any food or drinking, give the medicinal enema when it is just past noon.

The method for giving it is this. Lay the patient on the back, and, just as for the nourishing enema of medicated ghee, fit the nozzle to the bladder. Make the medicine warm, neither too hot nor too cold, squeeze the bladder with the hand, and give it neither too forcefully nor too slowly, without pausing or stopping in between, continuously, finishing it at one time. Have a vessel or a pit ready nearby beforehand for the waste that comes out, and the patient should keep his mind from wandering elsewhere and fix his attention on the thought, "The medicine in the belly is about to come out."

As for the time for expelling it: after the medicinal enema has been given, it should come out when about thirty syllables have been recited at a moderate pace, neither fast nor slow. The patient should squat normally and expel it. Then, if the disease has not gone or has not subsided, estimate from the person's strength whether he can bear more or not. If he seems able to bear it, add more as before, up to two or three times, and purge. After that, bathe him with warm water and let him eat good food with meat broth. If after that one sees wind entering the body, immediately give the nourishing enema of oily ghee, following the procedure explained earlier. If there is no fear of wind entering right away, wait two or three days and only then give the nourishing enema of oily ghee.

If the strong purgative given inside does not come out, it turns into many diseases, such as retention of feces, stabbing pains, and distension of the belly. Since the danger is great, in that case it is good, as soon as the proper time for it to come out has passed, to give a further oily enema, to give a purgative to drink, to frighten the patient, and to carry out similar procedures. Because this procedure of the strong purgative enema has great benefits but also great danger, it must not be given hastily; many physicians skilled in the procedure should gather, examine the case well, and then give it.

For the strong purgative enema: six srang of medicinal decoction, one part of ghee or sesame oil, three srang of medicinal powder, and rock salt and honey as appropriate. This is the general procedure. On this basis, depending on whether it is given as a medicine for wind, bile, or phlegm, add the medicines specific to each as set out below, making up the total of dry and wet ingredients to twenty-four srang altogether, and compound it as set out in detail below.

The strong purgative enema that removes wind disease is this: the decoction of whatever medicines remove wind, the powder of nyerong, just enough rock salt to give taste, and sour gruel, combined together and given warm. Take two srang each of the group beginning with juniper, the ten roots, rgyasen, and dried radish together, and boil them in sixty-four phul of water, reckoning eight srang to one phul of water; strain when one eighth of the water remains. A strong purgative enema of this combined with the powders of sweet flag, upakuñcikā, long pepper, shunyi, rock salt, and musta, together with honey, sour gruel, and cow's urine, also cures wind disease.

The medicine for the strong purgative enema that removes bile is this: if one gives the decoction of the group beginning with the nyagrodha tree, the ground powders of the group beginning with butsangto, ghee, milk, and sugar combined together, it removes bile. The medicine for the strong purgative enema that removes phlegm is this: if one gives the decoction of the group beginning with āragvadha, the ground powders of the group beginning with long pepper, cow's urine, and honey combined together, it removes phlegm.

Take one srang each of putsé shel, musta, pushel tsé, madder, balā, rāsnā, punarnavā, bṛhatī, guḍūcī, śālaparṇī, pṛśniparṇī, āragvadha, vibhītaka, gokṣura, and madana together; boil them in sixty-four phul of water, and strain when one eighth of the decoction remains. Boil this with eight phul of milk, and into what remains, the milk alone, add two zho each of the powders of gandhapriyaṅgu, licorice, śaṭī, kuṭaja bark, and vibhītaka, and rock salt, ghee, honey, and the meat broth of creatures that live on dry land, as appropriate. This strong purgative enema of these combined removes wind and bile. It increases virility and greatly kindles the digestive heat, and it also cures pain, tumors, lung disease, the great consuming chronic disease, retention of urine, and retention of feces.

Take honey and sesame oil in equal parts, a decoction of krutruktré equal in measure to those two combined, one srang of śuṭī powder, and two zho of rock salt. This strong purgative enema of these combined needs no precautions in its procedure. It is wholesome for increasing virility, kindling the digestive heat, making flesh grow, and removing retention of feces, worms, and tumors, and it carries no danger of harm.

Two srang each of honey, ghee, milk, and sesame oil, and two zho each of pama fruit and rock salt: this strong purgative enema of these combined is the best for strengthening.

The measure of the nourishing enema of oily ghee is generally three srang. The measure of the strong purgative enema is eight times that. For both the digestive medicine and the purgative, give one srang each of medicinal powder. Further, match it to whatever the patient's strength allows.

Just as, when the root of a tree is moistened with water, its leaves grow green and glossy, so too with a person: if the nourishing enema of oily ghee and the strong purgative enema are given from below according to these procedures, the complexion becomes glossy and one becomes endowed with strength. This completes the thirtieth chapter, the chapter on the five actions.

Now I shall explain the chapter on procedures.

Garlic has, among the six tastes, all except sour: these five tastes, sweet, bitter, pungent, astringent, and salty. That is why it is called "that which leaves no satiety." Combined with other medicines it removes all three humors. A healthy person who wishes to gain strength should eat it during the fortnight of the middle month of spring. One who is ill may eat it at any time. Take garlic that is mature, whose bulb has grown firm, flawless and full of good qualities; while keeping the regimen, its juice should be drunk, increasing the dose by two zho at a time. It is taught that it is given in amounts of two, four, or up to six srang, as the measures of best, middling, and least, matched to the patient's strength.

For wind and phlegm, the physician should give it together with sour gruel, salt, beer, and the like. For bile, give it together with milk or sweet broths. As food, one should eat bread made of wheat, with meat broth from birds that dwell in dry country, and one should avoid anger, sexual intercourse, grief and the like, and medicines and foods that are hard to digest. Its regimen is gauged from the illness, and should be understood as three measures, best, middling, and least: up to seven days, twice seven days, or three times seven days.

Garlic juice combined with ghee removes bile. Combined with sesame oil it removes phlegm and wind. Combined with fat or marrow it restores those with a ruptured chest or wasting disease. If garlic is finely pounded, boiled with milk, mixed with sugar and drunk, and after the medicine is digested one eats food with milk or meat broth, it removes exhaustion from coughing up blood and phlegm, wasting disease, wind disease, difficulty in breathing, madness, and epilepsy, and it is good for fractures, stabbing pains, worm disease, depletion of semen, and barren women. Those with hemorrhoids, those afflicted by a heat disorder that discharges like a calf's slaver, those with strings of swellings, the extremely emaciated, pregnant women, and women nursing a child should not drink it.

Onion is only slightly less potent than garlic, and it too should be eaten according to the procedure.

For wind disease alone, castor oil should be combined with any one of these: beer, sour gruel made with roots, a decoction of the five roots, or meat broth of animals that dwell in dry country; warmed, matched to strength, it should be drunk in the proper amount. Castor oil given with a decoction of the three fruits removes phlegm and bile. Given with a decoction of the ten roots, it removes diseases arising from wind and bile. Drunk with goat's milk or with grape wine, it removes retention of urine and obstruction by stones. Combined with milk it removes wind tumors. Those with jaundice, pallor disease, feverish diarrhea, vomiting, leprosy, fever, phlegm disorder alone, or the coughing up of blood and phlegm alone should not drink it.

The procedure of the marking nut: according to the procedure, boil five marking nuts in cow's urine until a quarter remains, knead them, and discard the dregs. Smear the lips and palate with butter and drink the cold liquid. By this method, increase the dose by five marking nuts each day, and drink as before until reaching seventy marking nuts. Then decrease by five each day, and on reaching five fruits, again increase and decrease in the same way. After the medicine drunk has been digested, one should eat cold boiled rice with ghee and milk that has been boiled and cooled. This rejuvenating procedure makes the mind clear, does away with wrinkles and white hair, and removes leprosy, hemorrhoids, blood fever, and the ills of worms. It also purifies semen that has become diseased. If one drinks marking nut oil, matched to strength and in the proper amount, for one month, one is freed from all the harms of disease and lives with a firm body for a hundred years. One should avoid warming oneself at fire and in the sun, sleeping during the day, seed oil, and heavy foods. Even after the medicine is finished, one should keep the regimen for twice as long as the time the medicine was given. In a person in whom bile predominates, during this treatment, pustules may break out in the mouth, the body may swell, and fever and the like may strike. For that, one should anoint the body with a paste of cooling medicines or with a cold decoction, pour it over the body, and drink it.

The procedure of increasing long pepper: boil milk and reduce it to half, grind five long pepper fruits into it and drink it. In this way, for ten days, increase the dose by five long peppers each day and drink it. Then decrease by five long peppers each day, and on reaching five long pepper fruits, again increase and decrease as before. If one eats food with milk and keeps to the regimen, it removes grime, pallor disease, tumors, hemorrhoids, difficulty in breathing, swellings, and dropsy, and it does away with fever, its relapses and exhaustion, and irregular fevers. It also becomes an aphrodisiac.

Shilajit comes from gold, iron, and the like. These are potent and resemble the color of their source. That which smells of cow's urine, is oily and heavy, and is free of stones is good. Wash it thoroughly with water, knead it in cow's urine, and temper it according to the procedure. Then, for whichever of the three humors its powder is given as a medicine, soak it many times in a decoction of the group of medicines for that humor and dry it. If it is drunk mixed with a decoction of the group of medicines for whichever humor it is given against, it removes every disease. In the same way, if the powder of drubmarchang is soaked in a decoction of medicines, mixed, and drunk, it removes every disease. Those undergoing the procedure of these two medicines should not eat pigeon meat or horse gram.

The procedure of chebulic myrobalan: if powdered chebulic myrobalan is combined with rock salt, it removes wind. Combined with ghee, it removes bile. Combined with dry ginger, it removes phlegm. Combined with molasses, it removes every disease.

The procedure of emblic myrobalan: strain sixteen measures of emblic juice, reckoning each measure as eight srang, pour it into a vessel smeared with honey, seal its mouth, and leave it for a long time. It should be drunk in winter, in late winter, or in summer. If it is combined with barberry extract and milk, it removes bleeding that rises upward and bile. Combined with sugar, it removes pallor disease and stops menstrual bleeding that will not stop. Combined with honey, it removes phlegm diseases. Combined with the three hot spices, it kindles heat where the digestive heat is weak. Combined with the medicine somarāja, it removes leprosy. Combined with the bark of kuṭaja, it removes hemorrhoids.

Thus I, having studied the treatises of the ocean of the science of life, those of the son of the sage Born of the Treatise, the son of the sage Knower of the Lineage, and others, have set down and composed in order these thirty-one chapters of a tantra that is no more than a ray of the science of healing. My elder brother Devagupta, struck by pallor disease, had me undertake it, and for his sake this medical treatise was made by Ravigupta. This is the thirty-first chapter, the chapter on procedures.

The Siddhasāra, made by Ravigupta, son of Durgagupta, a westerner and a horse master, is complete. It was translated and finalized by the Indian preceptors Jinamitra and Atigyavarma and by the translator, the monk Candra.

By my writing out this supreme science, the Siddhasāra,
may all beings without exception, limitless in number,
tormented without respite by the humors of wind, bile, and phlegm, by the three poisons,
and by the afflictions, the three and the eight, that become passing conditions,
through medicines turned to nectar, forever abandon
the wind, bile, three poisons and the rest that afflict them,
clear away the faults of the path through the supreme grounds so hard to traverse,
