The Tathāgata Candraprabha (Candraprabhatathāgata)

The Recollection of the Name of Candraprabha, whose Tibetan title begins with the words for the Tathāgata Moonlight, the arhat, is a very short scripture centered on the buddha Candraprabha, whose name means "Moonlight." It is Toh 868 in the Kangyur, where it belongs to the class of dhāraṇī and protective-formula texts, though I cannot confirm the full Sanskrit and Tibetan titles from the lines given here. The text opens with a homage to the Tathāgata Candraprabha and then offers a brief mantra, which invokes the buddha, dharma, and saṅgha as sources of blessing. It concludes with a promise of benefit: anyone who rises early each morning and calls the buddha's name to mind will remember their past lives over forty thousand eons and will not fall into the lower realms. In this way the text presents the simple practice of recollecting a buddha's name as a means of protection and spiritual merit.

Homage to the Tathāgata Candraprabha, the arhat, the perfectly and completely awakened buddha!

Tadyathā, candre candre, sucandra, maticandra, candrakiraṇa, siri siri, buddha adhiṣṭhite, kili kili, dharma adhiṣṭhite, kili kili, saṃgha adhiṣṭhite svāhā.

Any son or daughter of good family who always rises early in the morning and calls to mind the name of the Tathāgata Candraprabha will remember their past lives over forty thousand eons, and will not fall into the lower realms. Through recollecting the name of Candraprabha, one will obtain these qualities.

Here ends the Recollection of the Name of Candraprabha.
