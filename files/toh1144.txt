A Praise of the Three Jewels (Triratnastotra)

A Praise of the Three Jewels, known in Sanskrit as the Triratnastotra and in Tibetan as Dkon mchog gsum gyi bstod pa, is a short devotional hymn that pays homage in turn to the Buddha, the Dharma, and the Saṅgha. It opens with a simple verse of salutation to all three and then praises each more fully. The Buddha is honored as one who has perfected the two great accumulations, attained the four wisdoms and the three bodies, and is likened to a wish-fulfilling tree. The Dharma is celebrated both as the twelve branches of scripture and as the unborn, unceasing reality free of elaboration. The work is Toh 1144 in the Tengyur, where it belongs among the collected praises, the class of verse hymns composed by Indian masters in honor of the Buddha and the objects of refuge.

In the language of India: triratna stotraṃ. In the language of Tibet: A Praise of the Three Jewels.

I pay homage to the Three Jewels.

I pay homage to the Buddha, the foremost.
I pay homage to the Dharma that protects.
I pay homage to the great Saṅgha.
To these three I always pay homage.

You have perfected the two great accumulations and attained the four wisdoms and the three bodies.
Free of conceptual thought, you know all things with perfect clarity.
You possess the dharmakāya, which is like space, and the form body, a body of beauty.
I pay homage to the Buddha, who is like a wish-fulfilling tree.

The twelve branches of scripture, the outflow concordant with the nature of reality,
And the Dharma that has no arising or ceasing and is free of elaboration:
Focusing on these, one accomplishes every good quality.
I pay homage to the sublime Dharma, the cause of all that is good.

Having removed, step by step through their antidotes,
The afflictive obscurations and the obscurations to the knowable, they abide on the grounds.
They work for the benefit of sentient beings and purify buddha fields.
I pay homage to the great Saṅgha of noble ones.

This completes A Praise of the Three Jewels, composed by the great master Mātṛceṭa.
