Commentary on the Wrathful Chapter of the Tantra of Glorious Mahākāla, Called the Great Charnel Ground (Śrīmahākālatantrarudrakalpamahāśmaśānanāmaṭīkā)

The Commentary on the Wrathful Chapter of the Tantra of Glorious Mahākāla, Called the Great Charnel Ground, known in Sanskrit as the Śrī Mahākāla Tantra Rudra Kalpa Mahāśmaśāna Nāma Ṭīkā and in Tibetan as Dpal nag po chen po'i rgyud drag po'i brtag pa dur khrod chen po zhes bya ba'i 'grel pa, is Toh 1753 in the Tengyur, where it belongs to the section of commentaries on the tantras. It is an Indian commentary on a tantra of Mahākāla, the great black protector of the Buddhist teaching, and it opens with a homage to Vajraḍāka and verses praising Mahākāla's body of suchness and his power to protect the teaching in times of decline. Working line by line through its root tantra, it explains the generation of the deity and his retinue, the wrathful rites of suppression and the fire offering, the coded extraction of the deity's mantras from the letters of the alphabet, and the pledges binding those initiated into Mahākāla's maṇḍala. Its closing colophon attributes it to the master Aśvaghoṣa, who presents himself as heir to the instructions of Nāgārjuna.

In the language of India: śrī ma hā kā la tantra ru tra kalpa ma hā sma śā na nā ma ṭī kā. In the language of Tibet: Commentary on the Wrathful Chapter of the Tantra of Glorious Mahākāla, Called the Great Charnel Ground.

Homage to the glorious Vajraḍāka.

Body of suchness that holds every perfection without exception,
Whose faces are in truth the eight liberations, whose sixteen arms are the emptinesses,
Whose four feet are mastery in taming the māras, adorned by the four abodes of Brahmā,
Protector of beings, lord of the eight goddesses of the maṇḍala,
Body of great terror that tames the fierce yakṣa,
Black One whose branching arms adorn the crown: having bowed to you,
If one approaches you in the future, above all in the time of decline,
You especially uphold the teaching of the Victor
And destroy the darkness of the suffering of saṃsāra.
Through the power and strength of having received permission
For the scripture of Heruka from Vajragarbha and others,
And through the power of the holy guru,
I shall compose, if only in some small measure,
A commentary on what is taught in its vajra words.

The passage beginning "Equal to space" is not taught here; it is taught in its proper place.

The vajra words say, "Thus did I hear at one time," and so on. As for its analysis: in order to set out the distinctions by way of their connection with what was heard, the five are spoken with their meaning. Here the Teacher is taught in the passage beginning "From Vajradhara, emanating the body of the Great Terrifier." He dwells in the natural place of emanation called the Great Charnel Ground. "At that time" speaks of the gathering of the retinue. Through the blessing of the samādhi called "Appearing as Nondual," the Blessed One fully taught the Dharma. Further, once the malice of the yakṣa had been removed, the compiler fully compiled the vajra words: this determines the time.

Through the power of these five meanings coming together, "thus" means that it is held without error through the power of the vajra connection, and it is explained to the retinue. "I" means the compiler who is authoritative. "By" means that it was compiled and explained by him. "Heard" means that what was compiled without error, by the very power of its connection with the Teacher's words, is what was heard. "Time" is the time when the wheel of vajra words arises. "At one" shows that it is uncommon.

Alternatively, "Thus did I hear at one time" is connected with this: the Blessed One dwells in just the way that it is fully accomplished in the four syllables e vaṃ ma yā. The meaning is this: the Blessed One abides in the very nature and essence of e vaṃ ma yā. What, then, is the essence of the syllables e vaṃ ma yā? e is the purified earth element and the nature of body, the abode of the method of great loving-kindness, liberated from manifold forms. vaṃ is the purified water element and the nature of speech, which adopts the way of taming the malicious by means of compassion and aspiration. ma is the purified fire element and the nature of mind; from the samādhi that tames the malicious by giving through the power of joy, the Dharma arises. yā is the purified wind element and the nature of the nondual vajra; through the samādhi that conquers all māras, by the union of equanimity and gnosis, the retinue is fully gathered. "Time" is the compiler's compiling and explaining the Dharma.

Alternatively, e is the place, vaṃ is the Teacher, ma is the full gathering of the retinue, and yā is the Dharma compiled in its special way. To explain it more specifically, e is the source of phenomena, the place and the ground; vaṃ is the completion of the deity's body there; ma is being endowed with approach and close accomplishment; and yā is what brings the activities to completion. With such distinctions, the meaning of the introduction is taught in brief.

"The Blessed One" is the Teacher, the glorious Vajradhara. By applying the word bhaga, it has two senses: the meaning of the six qualities such as lordship, and the conquering of the malicious, having taken the assembly of the retinue, the Great Fierce Black One and the rest, under his care. "The vajra of body, speech and mind" means that he abides through the distinctions of the earth element and so on. Alternatively, "body" is the identity of the five purified aggregates, the abode of the nature of the five families, Vairocana and the rest. Their mind is Vajradhara, the sixth family. Moreover, body, the earth element and the nature of loving-kindness, abides in the queen Pukkasī. Speech, by the nature of the water element and compassion, abides in the queen Śabarī. Mind, by the nature of the fire element and joy, abides in the queen Caṇḍālī. Vajra, by the nature of the wind element and equanimity, abides in the queen Ḍombinī. Such is the Blessed One, who abides with the maṇḍala of the vajra of body, speech and mind.

Further, "Emanating as the Fierce Black One in order to tame the fierce yakṣa" means this: the Blessed One entered the nondual samādhi and, through the five awakenings, became fully and perfectly awakened; that is the meaning of "emanated a body as Mahākāla." "Assuming the body of the Great Terrifier" teaches that he matched the Fierce Black One in appearance. "The Fierce Black One" is the yakṣa. "Extremely" means great malice. "Extremely unbearable" means that he arose in bodily form as its antidote. "Doing" means acting to destroy the malice. "Charnel ground" means the outer charnel grounds, the nearby groves and so on. "Supreme place" is the natural place, namely the tent of curved knives. "Supreme place" is also the natural place, the source of phenomena. "Dwells" means that he dwells as the nirmāṇakāya. "At that time" means when he acted to destroy the malice of the black yakṣa.

"The ḍākinī emanated from the vajra mind" has two meanings. Those who became the close retinue of servants are, in identity, the eight goddesses, the non-Buddhist Gaurī and the rest. Further, because the goddesses of the Blessed One's circle, Gaurī and the rest, made the non-Buddhist Gaurī and the rest their seats, those were bound to his command by the power of the samaya. That is why it says, "the ḍākinī emanated from the vajra mind."

"Samaya ḍākinī" means one who does not transgress the samaya of the principal deity, who dwells continually in the circle of the samaya, and who does not transgress the samaya.

They are as follows. The eight beginning with Slayer dwell in the east and are white in color: Slayer, Skull Fragment, Pracaṇḍā, Great Skeleton, Caṇḍākṣī, Laṅkeśvarī, Prabhāvatī, and Bared Fangs.

In the south are Summoner and the rest, yellow in color: Summoner, Mahānāsā, Surābhakṣī, Steadfast Mind, Light Maker, Dwarf, Vajra Messenger, and the Rākṣasī of Laṅkā.

Bewilderer and the rest dwell in the west and are red in color: Bewilderer, Lotus Body, Drumacchāyā, Sprout, Ekajaṭī, the Great Terrifier, and Great Heroine.

The eight beginning with Subjugator dwell in the north and are of various colors: Subjugator, Vāyuvegā, Vajra hūṃ Maker, Blood Drinker, Most Excellent, Śyāmādevī, Vajra Wrathful Frown, and Subhadrā.

All of them have one face and two arms and three eyes, and stand in a dancing posture holding a curved knife and a skull cup. They dwell in the circle of the samaya, and because they do not abandon the samaya they are called samaya ḍākinīs.

"Accomplished from karma" means that they are born from karma in the sacred places and, listening to the commands of the lord of the circle and others, carry out the activities; therefore they are called karma ḍākinīs. They dwell in these places in the manner of body, speech and mind, three ḍākinīs dwelling in each place. The places are Pullīramalaya, Jālandhara, Oḍyana, Arbuda, Godāvarī, Rāmeśvarī, Devīkoṭi, Mālava, Kāmarūpa, Otre, Triśakune, Kosala, Kaliṅga, Lampāka, Kāñci, Himālaya, Pretapuri, Gṛhadeva, Saurāṣṭra, Suvarṇadvīpa, Nāgara, Sindhu, Marothali, and Kulutā. These are the twenty-four special places, and in each of them dwell three karma-born ḍākinīs.

Blood Drinker, Suvīrā and the Great Terrifier: these three perform the rite of making one vomit blood. In Jālandhara are Lady of the Nāgas, Swift Goer, and She with Pendant Snakes: these three hold power over the nāgas and perform the rites of the nāgas. In Oḍyana are Vajra Wrathful One, White One, and Mahābalā: by these three the rite of sowing discord is swiftly accomplished. In Arbuda are Graceful One, Cakravegā, and Lady of the Wind: these three swiftly perform the rite of expulsion. In Godāvarī are Pendulous Belly, Lotus Garland, and Bewilderer: these three perform the rite of bewildering. In Rāmeśvarī are Amitābhā, Hayakarṇā, and Summoner: these three swiftly perform the rite of summoning. In Devīkoṭi are Vajra Blazing Light, Vajra Messenger, and Vajra Subjugator: these three accomplish the rite of subjugation. In Mālava are the ḍākinī called Vajra Body, Mover of Meru, and Lady of Strength: these three accomplish the rite of paralysis. In Kāmarūpa are Sharp Form, Subtle Form, and Vajra Armor: these three everywhere accomplish the rite of messengers in the form of white mustard seeds. In Otre are Cakravartinī, Vajra Fearsome Locks, and Fierce Wrathful Frown: these three accomplish the various rites. In Triśakune are Mind Stealer, Two Forms, and All-Roaming: these three ripen the causal karma that precedes all the rites. In Kosala are Field Protector, hūṃ Maker, and Life Drinker: these three everywhere swiftly accomplish the rite of destroying the bindu. In Kaliṅga are Most Terrifying, Sky-Bodied, and Great Fierce One: these three swiftly complete the rite of joining. In Lampāka are Lady of the Chandoha, Cakravartinī, and Vajra Swift One: these three swiftly accomplish the rite of the universal monarch. In Kāñci are Cakravarmiṇī, Embodied One, and All-Showering: these three perform the rite of bringing down streams of rain. In Himālaya are Virūpākṣī, Crow Form, and Vajra Great Belly: these three everywhere perform the rite of bringing down vajra hail upon the unruly. In Pretapuri are Mistress of Strength, Cakravarmiṇī, and Ākāśagarbhā: these three everywhere perform the rite of protection from vajra hail. In Gṛhadeva are Lady of Glory, Vasudhārā, and Khaṇḍarohā: these three perform the rite of attaining lordship over wealth. In Saurāṣṭra are Utter Destroyer, Yamadūtī, and Lady of Stability: these three complete the rite of stabilizing. In Suvarṇadvīpa are Sky Wheel, Weapon-Formed, and Terrifier: these three complete the rite of offering cakes. In Nāgara are Fire Form, Desiccator, and Wind Wheel: these three complete the practice of incense. In Sindhu are Drier, Action Lady, and Cāmuṇḍī: these three complete the rite of the secret fire offering. In Marothali are Padmanarteśvarī, Mistress of Strength, and Lady of the Charnel Ground: these three complete the rite of the common fire offering. In Kulutā are Vajrasattvā, Mahāvīryā, and Fierce Wheel: these three everywhere perform the rite of guarding the mind-streams of yogins.

All these ḍākinīs dwell in the world-realm. Further, because the Blessed One blessed them into the circle of his command and charged them to perform all activities, they are called karma ḍākinīs.

"Terrifiers, Action Ladies, and the rest": here "Terrifiers" are the eight great yakṣas who dwell in the eight great charnel grounds. In Caṇḍogra dwells the Fierce Lord. In Bhīṣaṇa dwells Vajra Fierce One. In Jvālākula dwells Vajra Vetāla. In Gahvara dwells Vajra Ghala. These dwell in the four directions in order, beginning with the east. Further, in Aṭṭahāsa dwells Gaṇapati. In Lakṣmīvana dwells the yakṣa Ruru. In Ghorāndhakāra dwells the yakṣa Supreme Dancer. In Kilikilārava dwells the yakṣa Iron Mole. These dwell properly in the intermediate directions, beginning with Īśāna's quarter, the northeast.

Further, "female Terrifiers" are Crow-Faced, Sow-Faced, Dog-Faced, Owl-Faced and the rest, who dwell in terrifying forms in the charnel grounds of the directions beginning with the east. Yamadūtī, Yamadāḍhī, Yamadaṃṣṭrī and Yamamathanī also dwell properly in the intermediate directions, beginning with the northeast. Again, the meaning of "Terrifier" is this: because they serve the command of their principal and accomplish their activities without hindrance through the power of great terror, they are called Terrifiers and female Terrifiers.

"Host" means their gathering. "Surrounded" means that, having blessed them, he took them fully into his retinue. "At that time" is said because it means at the time when that retinue too was the retinue of the Black One. "The Blessed One" is the lord of the circle mentioned above; the meaning of "Blessed One" here is "one who has conquered the malice of the Fierce Black One and the others." "Appearing as Nondual" means that, because their malice was conquered, they were gathered into the principal of the circle. "Samādhi" means that the principal too became emptiness. "From" means from emptiness. "Emanated as Mahākāla" means that his body was completed through the five rites, as taught above. "The Blessed One" is the principal, Mahākāla himself. "Heart" means the form of the curved knife and hūṃ. "Light rays" means that they radiated out in the nature of the curved knife and hūṃ. "Directions and intermediate directions" are the divisions of the retinue, beginning with the east. "All" means the whole retinue. "Pervading" means that the light rays entered the hearts of all and pervaded them. "Having removed the malice of the yakṣas": the yakṣas are the retinue, the Fierce Black One and the rest. Malice is perverted conceptual thought. "Removed" means that, having removed their malice with those light rays, he took them as the retinue of the cause. "They became a tent, a fence and a net" means that the retinue are to abide in the samaya of body, speech and mind. Alternatively, it should be understood as the nature of the protection circle. "When they abode" means when they were fully complete as the retinue of the cause. "The ḍākinīs and the host of the Black One paid homage" means that they praised him with the words "equal to space"; the meaning is that they do not transgress the samaya of the principal. "Requested of the Blessed One" means of the principal emanated from Vajradhara. "What did the Fierce Black One explain?" is the request for the extensive introduction concerning the retinue. "The Blessed One said" means that the Fierce Black One, having become the principal, abode in samādhi through his power, and by the power of that samādhi the compiler compiled it fully.

"This is the Tantra of the Fierce Black One": from "Thus did I" up to "he spoke thus," the statement of the meaning found in the tantra that reaches the culmination of the activities is called the tantra. "Great Charnel Ground" is the outer charnel ground, the abode of Mahākāla. "Great" refers to the place of the principal together with the four. "In the place" refers to two places: the place of Meru and the place of Jambudvīpa. "In the place of Jambudvīpa dwelt the yogin called Ratya": the yogin, the yakṣa called Radiant, went from the place of Meru to Jambudvīpa, and because the power of his connection with the fisherman caste was awakened, he became a yogin of Maheśvara (Śiva), endowed with the power of having attained the eight common accomplishments. Further, by the power of his connection with the yakṣas he delighted in flesh and blood, and so he holds power over life and breath.

"On Meru, in the Triple City" means that the yakṣa Mahākāla dwells in the city of the gods on Meru. Its former history is as follows. In the teaching of the Tathāgata Śikhin, a monk called Wheel of Bliss arose from a family of painters. He was dark in complexion and fierce of mind, and through the training of the Four Truths he became a close attendant of the Tathāgata. That monk, in thirty-two instants, came close to the fruit, and since he possessed the higher knowledges, he thought in the presence of the Tathāgata, "I possess the higher knowledges," and so grew conceited. The Tathāgata said: "Monk, you have a false pride. For example, the Maheśvara of the desire realm, out of the pride that thought, 'I am stronger than all the gods,' whirled Mount Meru about his head, drew in the great ocean with his breath, made the great nāgas Nanda, Upananda and the rest his ornaments, bound the sun and moon on his head as a headband, and ate the gods as his servants for food: such was the power he possessed. He too thought, 'I am the one of great power in this world,' yet beside the power of the Tathāgata, equal to the limits of space, his power does not measure up to even a single mote of dust.

"Monk, you too are like that, so do not be proud," he said. At this the monk's spirits sank very low, and thinking, "What need have I to be in the Tathāgata's retinue?" he went off to a forest in the south and stayed there. In that forest there was a shrine of Maheśvara, and for twenty-one days the monk made offerings to it, circumambulated it, and so on, and prayed to it. Then Maheśvara appeared to him in a dream and declared: "Monk, you will become my son. In this world I grant you the accomplishment of great power."

Then the Tathāgata, knowing that the monk had gone for refuge to Maheśvara, took on the form of a monk, went to that monk in the forest, and said, "Monk, what are you doing?" The monk replied, "I am one who, not being included in the Tathāgata's retinue, has gone for refuge to Maheśvara." The Tathāgata said:

"Monk,
To have found the true path
And then set out upon a path of error
Is like having gained clear sight
And then being stricken with the blight of cataract.

Monk, that is what you are like."

Then the monk, filled with earnest resolve, asked, "What should I do?" The Tathāgata in the form of a monk said, "Monk, you are permitted to perform the poṣadha before the Tathāgata and the others," and on the spot he vanished. The monk then left the forest, went into the presence of the Tathāgata, and performed the poṣadha many times. Then he said to the Tathāgata, "I have reached the end of my life. Please make a prophecy for me."

The Tathāgata said, "By the force of your wrong refuge you will pass through many births, and you will be born as the son of Maheśvara called the Fierce Black One." He is black because he turned away from the true refuge, and he is called fierce because he obtained the power of Maheśvara. He is great because he holds sway over the three realms of existence. "After that, in the teaching of the tathāgata called Holder of All Excellences, because you previously generated the resolve to guard the teachings of all the tathāgatas of the three times, you will appear, for the sake of full awakening, as a bodhisattva of the tenth ground called the Black One. Having guarded the teaching of the Tathāgata, you will complete the accumulations, and in the buddha field called Vajra-Adorned you will become fully and perfectly awakened as the Buddha, the Blessed One called Ash-Bearer."

When the Tathāgata had made this prophecy, the monk passed through countless births, and by the power of the Tathāgata's prophecy and of Maheśvara's permission, he was born as the son of Maheśvara called the Fierce Black One, and he dwells on the summit of Meru.

"The Triple City" is said because he holds sway over the gods, the nāgas and the asuras. "Yakṣa" means one who brings harm to the three realms of existence.

"He produces splendor in the body of the yogin" means that, by the power of a link of concordant cause, the Fierce Black One enters the mind stream of the yogin described above.

"Bearing a black form, the vicious one": "black" is the color black. "Bearing a form" means bearing the form of a yakṣa. "Vicious" means that he holds sway over life and breath.

"Holding a fisherman's knife and a skull cup": these are his hand emblems.

"Always eating pills of poison" means that these have become sacred substances in accord with the remedy.

"In the three times he throws the Triple City into turmoil": "the three times" means during the six periods of the day and night. "The Triple City" means the cities of Jambudvīpa, which are like the city on Meru. "Throws into turmoil" means that he takes the lives of the people living there.

From "great flesh" up to "blood" is the food that was taken continually before. It should be understood as having the nature of the five meats and the five nectars. "Blood-drinker" means one who snatches away the breath of beings, or else it is the very destruction of the afflictions such as ignorance. "From the mouth, blood": it is said that blood drips from his mouth in order to send down streams of disease upon sentient beings, to guard the teaching of the Buddha and so on, and to abandon the attachment of saṃsāra.

"Medicine from burnt corpses and" means offering with special things such as lamps of great black butter. "Great burnt incense" means the substance that removes the viciousness of yakṣas, and that is the incense used in the charnel ground.

"Dwelling in the charnel ground, the supreme of scents" refers to the remains of a person endowed with the marks. "Dwelling" and "scent" mean the scent that arises from those remains. "Supreme" means the nectar accomplished in the charnel ground.

"He keeps the vow in the three times" means that, since these substances are concordant sacred substances that sustain life, he is never parted from them.

"Vicious" means the Fierce Black One. "Emanations" means those who belong to his retinue. Because those retinue members are equal in power to the principal, they are called emanations. "Sending forth various" is said because he sends forth the antidote to the lives of those who hate the teaching of the Buddha, and because he guards the teaching with various forms.

"He is taught as the Fierce Black One" shows the setting of the Tantra of the Fierce Black One.

Then, "Mahākāla asked": the compiler asked in detail about the retinue of the result. "Blessed One" is what he said in addressing the principal. From "the Fierce Black One" up to "how is it?", together with "How does he become the retinue of the result?", he asked in detail about the meaning taught above.

"The Blessed One said" means that, through the blessing of the Blessed One having entered the samādhi of body, speech and mind, the compiler gathered it fully and set it forth for the sake of taming beings.

"The vajra of body, speech and mind,
The body of the deity, is completely pure":
here "body" is the form with eight faces and sixteen arms, and so on. "Speech" is the seed syllable that generates it. "Mind" is what has become the hand emblems. "Vajra" is the body in its complete perfection. "Deity" refers to the goddesses who belong to his retinue. "Body" is the supporting maṇḍala.

"Completely pure" is said because it is like the moon reflected in water.

Again, "body" is the form with one face and two arms. "Speech" is the four-armed form. "Mind" is the six-armed form. "Vajra" is the sixteen-armed form. "Deity" is the deity together with the consort. "Body" is the very nature of the maṇḍala circle. "Completely pure" means that its own essence is completely pure: it is called completely pure because it purifies the mind stream of the Fierce Black One.

"Appearing as Nondual" means that the circle of the maṇḍala is gathered into the heart of the principal himself.

"He entered into samādhi": it is called "entered" because the principal too has become emptiness.

"Then" means from emptiness. "Mahākāla" means the yakṣa, the Fierce Black One. "Of matching appearance" is said because his body color and hand emblems match. "He emanated" is said because the bodies of the mirror-like wisdom, the wisdom of equality, the discriminating wisdom, the all-accomplishing wisdom and the wisdom of the dharmadhātu are complete.

From "the light rays from my heart" up to "may it become a net" teaches the retinue of the cause, which was taught above. Now, in order to complete the retinue of the result, two light rays come forth from the heart of the principal and enter the hearts of the retinue, the Fierce Black One and the others. Roused by the light rays, they come to recollect the principal and melt into a ball of light, and the Fierce Black One enters the heart of the principal. The other members of the retinue remain, having taken the nature of seed syllables.

"Became nondual" means nondual with hūṃ. "From my heart" means from the syllable hūṃ at the heart. "Emanated" refers to the intermediate being: from within the ball of light he becomes a form with one face and two arms, a span in height, and he comes forth from one's own mouth and is sent out in front.

Then the principal commanded him:
"Arisen from the three secrets,
You are Mahākāla.
You are the very nature of the three vajras;
He is proclaimed the Great Terrifier."
Here "secret" means what is not shared with others, what is gathered within the mind stream of the principal. "Three" is its division, referring to body, speech and mind themselves. "Arisen" means that, having been accomplished as inseparable from the principal, he arose from hūṃ. "You are Mahākāla" means that he has been accomplished as the intermediate being. "You are the very nature of the three vajras" means the very nature of the vajra of body, the vajra of speech and the vajra of mind. "He is proclaimed the Great Terrifier" is the intermediate meditation, in which he rises from the retinue of the cause into the retinue of the result. "Terrifier" means endowed with the power to crush the vicious. "Great" means inseparable from the power of the principal.

"I shall explain your tantra fully" means: I shall explain the tantra of the setting of the retinue.

"Then" and what follows have already been explained above.

"By the light rays of Vajradhara": Vajradhara is the principal. "By the light rays" means that the light rays of the hūṃ at his heart strike the retinue, Mahākāla and the others. "Having swooned" means that the retinue became the nature of the three syllables. "He entered into samādhi" means that the three syllables too entered, one after another in sequence. It is called "entered" because they became emptiness.

"At that time" refers to the retinue's experience of the result. "By the Blessed One Mahākāla" refers to the principal, who entered samādhi in order to awaken the retinue. The samādhi is called "having taken up the curved knife in the manner of a hook." Here the curved knife is the curved knife that dwells at the heart of the principal. The hook has the nature of the sun and hūṃ; it is called a hook because it awakens the minds of the retinue. "Having taken up in the manner" refers to the principal: the hūṃ is encircled by the mantra beginning with oṃ. "The hook together with its light rays" means the light rays of the hūṃ and of the mantra. As for what is to be recited, it is enough that it be sent forth from the mouth.

"Having been awakened" is explained here. "Roused by those light rays" means that the retinue rises in bodily form through the three rites. "Then Mahākāla" refers to the body of the samayasattva. "Rejoicing, he joined his palms" means: "I belong to the retinue of the result. How wonderful!" The retinue's rejoicing in the principal is the passage beginning "Blessed One."

"I" refers to the time when he abided in the intermediate meditation. "The principal" means by the light rays from the heart of Vajradhara. "Having been struck" means having become the three syllables. "I became exceedingly blissful" means having become emptiness. "The principal, the Blessed One Mahākāla" means roused by the light rays of the principal. "I abide in great bliss" means that the body was completed through the three rites.

"The Blessed One said" means that, by the power of the great samaya, he entered samādhi. "You are the lord of the three secrets" means that he enjoined on that retinue the samaya of the three secrets, as follows:
"Of all the buddhas of the three times,
You are blessed to guard the teaching."
Here "blessed" means blessed as the body, speech and mind of the principal; and because the Tathāgata prophesied it, you are called Glorious Mahākāla.

"Having pledged to guard the teaching": the teaching is that of the three times. To guard it is to tame the vicious and to crush the afflictions such as ignorance. The meaning is: completed as the retinue of the result, and never transgressing the samaya of the principal.

"In the teaching of the Lion of the Śākyas" is said because it is a time when those especially hard to tame appear.

"Abide by the great samaya" means that all the retinue, too, never transgress the samaya of the principal of the retinue. "He said" is used because, by the power of the samaya, he passed from that samādhi into the samādhi of the samaya circle.

The passage runs from "Glorious Black One" up to "Terrifier and the others." These are the names of the retinue, which were taught above.

By the power of the principal's having entered the samādhi of the samaya circle, they too were struck to the very core, and so there comes the passage beginning "they trembled violently." Here "trembled" is said because those members of the retinue had not yet reached the measure of the retinue of the result, and the power of their body was not complete. "Were afraid" is said because the power of their speech was not complete. "Their mindfulness failed most terribly" means that the power of their mind was not complete. With power so incomplete they are unable to guard the teaching of the Buddha.

Then "the Blessed One" refers to the principal, who entered the samādhi called "the completion of the power of body, speech and mind." Its meaning is this: in order to complete the power of body, speech and mind of the retinue, and so that they will not abandon the special samaya, the hūṃ that dwells at the heart of the principal is encircled all around by the mantras of power and samaya. Light rays radiate from the mantra beginning with oṃ, and so it is said, "It is recited by sending it forth from the mouth."

"Then" and what follows means that those light rays pervade the hearts of the whole retinue. "They regained their mindfulness" means that those members of the retinue too regained the power of body, speech and mind, and will not transgress the samaya of the principal.

"The Blessed One" means that he entered the samādhi of the circle of approach of Mahākāla. Because of the power of that samādhi, the retinue became endowed with power, and so it is said, "they offered their own life-essence." Here "their own" means the nature of the body, speech and mind of the whole retinue, the Mahākāla who awaits commands and the others. "Life" is said because it is like life, and because it is the close accomplishment, like life itself. "Essence" is said because, having crushed the vicious, it takes up the essence that protects the mind. "Offered" means offered to the principal by the whole retinue. What they offered is the mantra that begins with oṃ and ends with hūṃ, as it says:
"This king of mantras
Is such as never was and never will be;
It accomplishes every need."

Then "the Blessed One said" means that the principal spoke words of praise to the retinue. "Lords of the retinue" is addressed to the whole retinue, the ḍākinīs emanated from the vajra of mind and the others. "Excellent, excellent!" means "you of the retinue have grasped it well," and it has the nature of approach and close approach.

"Mahākāla" is the retinue addressing the principal. "Excellent, excellent!" means, "You have gathered it well; now explain it well to the assembly of the retinue." Or else the two refer to the accomplishment of the common activity rites and the great accomplishment of the special activity rites. "To you" refers to the compiler. "Of Mahākāla" means having the nature of the excellent setting. "Tantra" means the tantra that has obtained the excellent activities. "The Great Charnel Ground": it is the Great Charnel Ground because in this tantra it was obtained in the great charnel ground. From "I shall explain" up to "he said," the meaning is: "Accomplish well the meaning of the tantra."

From "Then" up to "the assembly of the retinue requested" is the expression for the principal of the retinue making a request to Vajradhara. From "Blessed One" up to "how is it?" means that the compiler asked the principal, "What is the purpose for which the tantra arises?"

"The Blessed One said" means that the principal entered the samādhi of protecting beings, and by the power of that samādhi he summed up the purpose for which the tantra arises. This is the passage beginning "the three realms of existence." Here "the three realms of existence" are the three worlds. "Those who take the wrong side" are those who have become obstructors of the teaching in the three times. "Māras" are the four māras in general, and those who constantly hate the guru. "Tīrthikas" is said because they destroy the teaching of the Buddha and disparage the Great Vehicle. "Rākṣasas" are those who do not enter the Great Vehicle and abandon the awakening mind. "Asuras" are those who cast aside their own special deity and go for refuge to worldly gods, and who abandon the three samayas. "Yakṣas" are those who obstruct the two accomplishments and harm all sentient beings.

Or else, "the three realms of existence" is ignorance itself. "Wrong" is what arises from it. "Side" is the grasped object. "Taking" is the very grasping that arises from it. "Māra" is delusion. "Tīrthika" has the nature of pride. "Rākṣasa" is the rākṣasa of desire. "Asura" has the nature of jealousy. "Yakṣa" has the nature of aversion. "Hosts" refers to the overcoming of these.

"In the teaching of the Lion of the Śākyas" means the last period of the teaching. Because they obstruct the accomplishment of the siddhi of the Great Seal, there is the passage beginning "they cut off and destroy." "He crushes them" means: in order to crush them with the activities and great rites of application that swiftly accomplish approach and close approach, these rituals are explained.

"The first of the tantra" has the nature of the five excellences and is called the setting. It is called the setting because the tantra arises from it. "Second, the application of mantras" refers to deity yoga, which is approach. "Mantra" means the recitation of mantra by way of yoga, which is close approach. "Application" refers to killing, expelling and the like, having the nature of the great rites of application. "Third, the deity" is said because it has the stage of generation from mantra. "Fourth, the maṇḍala" means providing the deity with its support. In order to ripen the practitioner in the maṇḍala and to bring the support to its proper measure, there is what is called consecration. "Explained in the rites of the molded images and so on" refers to special rites, which will be taught in their proper place. From "consisting of ten" up to "he said" is easy to understand. "The Tantra of Mahākāla" means the tantra of the practice of Mahākāla.

"The Great Charnel Ground": since the one who awaits commands and the others dwell in charnel grounds and are the lords of the charnel grounds, it is called the Great Charnel Ground.

"The first chapter, the setting": the setting is the setting in which the retinue, the yakṣa Black One and the others, are fully perfected as the retinue of the result. It is a chapter because it is the chapter that explains at length the nature of the five excellences. It is first because it is the first of the tantra.

This is the first, the explanation of the setting of the Tantra of Glorious Mahākāla.

"These" is said because, arising from the setting, there come the means of accomplishing the supreme and the common aims. "The Glorious Black One" refers to the retinue, the compiler and the others. "Requested of the Blessed One" means they asked Vajradhara.

"First, accomplishing the meaning of the tantra": here "first, the tantra" means that the tantra is the principal, Mahākāla, the tantra whose nature is body, speech and mind. "Meaning" is the mantra; and it is called "accomplishing" because with the root mantra, the heart mantra and the inner heart mantra one accomplishes the siddhis of body, speech and mind.

"And the various rites of activity" refers to the common activity rites. "That which is endowed with the means of accomplishment" refers to the great rites of application of the tantra. "What is the rite of accomplishment?": here "accomplishment" refers to approach and close approach, and "rite" refers to the rites of application and the great rites of application. "What is it?" is a request made in brief.

"The Blessed One" is the Teacher. "He became the Great Terrifier" means that he entered the samādhi of the circle of the Great Terrifier. Then the principal spoke, summing up the meaning of that samādhi.

"One who wishes to practice this tantra" means: one should accomplish the lord of the circle of the tantra. The meaning of "mantra" is the root mantra and the others. From "rite" up to "must be explained" means having the stages of generating the principal and the retinue. "Meditation" has the nature of the curved knife and the hūṃ at the heart of the deity. "The supreme of aims, and the mantra" means: the hūṃ at the heart is encircled by the root mantra. "Will accomplish the siddhis" means that this very mantra is the nature of siddhi; and because it brings about swift accomplishment, it is called "accomplishing."

"The supreme tantra of the Glorious Black One" is called supreme because all supreme aims are accomplished by mantra; it is also because it is the supreme among mantra tantras.

"These roots of mantra": the mantra is the hūṃ at the heart; "these roots" are what encircle the hūṃ. "Explained" means uninterrupted, from mouth to mouth and from heart to heart. This is the mantra beginning with oṃ. "This possesses the meaning of the root" means that it is called the root mantra because it is the root mantra for accomplishing body, speech and mind.

From "activity" up to "explained" is said because it is explained that all activities are accomplished. This is the passage beginning e dam. e dam should be understood, by the force of the context, as the word to be replaced with the specific action.

From "This" up to "he said," the meaning is that the compiler gathered fully the special features of the mantra.

"Then the Glorious Black One" means together with the assembly of the retinue. "Became endowed with splendor" means that the retinue, having understood the meaning of the mantra, became endowed with real power. From "requested of the Blessed One" up to "please explain" is a request summarizing the meaning of approach.

"Then the principal, looking upon the assembly of the retinue" means that he entered the samādhi of the circle of approach. "He spoke thus" is said because he summed it up in these words. And what are they? "One who wishes to accomplish": that is, a hero who has received empowerment in the maṇḍala of Mahākāla, having received permission from the guru, should accomplish what he wishes.

Then, desiring what has the nature of siddhi, one goes to a place such as the great charnel ground and makes a maṇḍala platform of the five nectars. In that place one provides a support such as a maṇḍala or a painted scroll. "Smear the body with the five nectars" means that one makes drops of nectar at the crown, the throat, the heart, the navel and the secret place, and in the places of the drops one sets, upon a sun disk, the syllables hā, kā, la and ya. With the mantra that begins with oṃ and ends with ko'haṃ, one instantly takes on the pride of the deity. The completion of the circle of deities from that should be understood from the chapter on the deity.

The maṇḍala thus completed is to be worshipped with special offerings: one worships with upabakṣe flowers and the like. "Meditation" means that one blesses it with the three syllables as the samaya of body, speech and mind. "Mantra" is the root mantra, a special offering made with the word of replacement. As for the approach, one recites the mantras of the principal and of the ḍākinīs. That is the inner life-essence: for the principal and the one who awaits commands it circles from the mouth to the heart, while for the other members of the retinue it dwells in each one's own heart. It is said that if one does the approach in this way, the approach will be accomplished. That is, one obtains the signs of body, speech and mind. Then one should apply it to the activities; it is not done merely through having obtained the signs.

"The sixth piece": one should take a piece of a black skull of someone who died of a blood disease, or whose family line has died out, or the like. Then one places substances such as nimba inside the skull, upon a platform of poison. Facing toward the direction of the target, one completes the circle of deities, and then joins to those substances the procedure of summoning and absorbing whoever is the object of focus. Then, inside the skull, one should draw with owl's blood a fire maṇḍala enclosed all around by a fence. In its center, it is said, one draws with an owl-feather pen, using the substances mentioned above, the image of the target. One provides it with the seed syllable of fire at the five places, the crown and the rest.

Then the rite is to be performed at the foot of a solitary tree. There one makes a triangular maṇḍala out of charnel-ground earth and shapes it with goat's blood into the form of a fire maṇḍala. On it one sets up hearthstones marked with the seed syllable of fire, and on top of them one places the skull.

Then one generates in full the circle of deities of oneself and of the one who awaits commands, makes offerings, torma and so on, and performs the approach that is the close accomplishment with the inner heart mantra and the mantras of the ḍākinīs. Then, with the light rays from the heart of oneself and of the one who awaits commands, one renders the skull devoid of intrinsic nature, and in that very place one should visualize, arising from khaṃ, a crossed-vajra rock. In its center one should visualize, from raṃ, a fire maṇḍala, and on that, from paṃ, a multicolored lotus. At the three corners of the triangle one should also visualize a lotus and a sun. Upon these, at the heart of the target, one should generate the circle of deities through the three rites. The principal is black in color, with one face and two arms, holding a curved knife and a skull cup in his hands. The three goddesses, Blood Drinker and the others, are black in color and most terrifying, holding a sword and a skull cup in their hands, with the form of heroines and three eyes each.

Having generated them in full in this way, one draws in the maṇḍala of the intrinsic nature with the light from the heart, and confers empowerment. Then, in the belly of the principal, in the center of a fire maṇḍala, one clearly visualizes the image of the target marked with the seed syllable of fire. Then, from the heart of oneself and of the one who awaits commands, one pervades the hearts of the principal and the ḍākinīs with the outer life-essence. One makes offerings, torma and so on to them, and pervades them with the samaya mantra. Then, with the inner heart mantra and the mantras of the ḍākinīs, one sends the approach into the heart of each.

Then one carries out the procedure of summoning and absorbing. That is, by three ḍākinīs emanated from the three ḍākinīs, holding a hook, a noose and a chain, the target is swiftly summoned, drawn in and bound in the center of the fire maṇḍala in the belly of the principal. Then one should kill. Beneath the skull one lights a fire of charnel-ground firebrands, and with charnel-ground ash, earth from a riverbank, and mustard seeds prepared through the rite, one strikes the image in the skull with the killing mantra and the mantras of the ḍākinīs. At that time ḍākinīs emanated from the three ḍākinīs are sent forth; they enter the crown, the throat and the heart of the target who is in the belly of the principal, and one should understand them as eating the brain, snatching away the breath, and drinking the blood. Having carried out the procedure in this way for three days, one then buries it at the foot of a tree. "Immediately he vomits blood" means: as soon as that rite of application is complete, the target immediately vomits blood.

Then one should practice the rite of the nāga lady and the rest. "Whatever buffalo's horn or latrine vessel" is a brief statement of the substances for suppression. "Smear the body with saffron" means that first one should protect oneself. Into a consecrated skull one pours saffron and the five nectars and sets it upon a maṇḍala platform of the five nectars. One completes the circle of deities of oneself and of the one who awaits commands, and with the light rays from the heart one renders the skull and the saffron devoid of intrinsic nature. There one generates Mahākāla in full through the five rites. At his heart, between a moon and a sun joined face to face, there abides, upon a sun, a hūṃ having the nature of saffron and nectar, encircled by the protection mantra. Into him one draws the wisdom deity and confers empowerment. One recites the outer heart mantra and makes offerings and torma. Then one draws the inner heart mantra, together with the protection mantra, into the heart of Mahākāla. Having recited it in this way, at the end of a thousand recitations he melts into light and becomes a substance having the nature of saffron.

"A drop on the forehead" means that one makes drops at the five places, the forehead and the rest. In the places of the drops one should visualize a hūṃ upon a sun disk. "All" is said because the hūṃ is encircled all around by the protection mantra, and its light rays pervade the whole body. "One will be protected" is said because in all the pores of the body there abide three-pronged vajras and hūṃs, which become the rite that removes the viciousness of the nāgas.

Then one makes a maṇḍala platform of nectar and, facing west, there is the passage beginning "a white she-goat." "On the substance, a charnel-ground cloth" means that it is made from the shroud of someone who died of a nāga disease. The meaning from "an owl pen" up to "hūṃ facing outward and inward" is easy to understand. The drawing is done at midday. From "the skin of a black cat" up to "charnel-ground ink" is to be done as the rite: at midnight, facing east or toward the direction of the target, one makes a maṇḍala platform from various poisons and gathers the substances mentioned above upon it. One completes the circle of oneself and of the one who awaits commands, and provides those substances with the procedure of summoning and absorbing the target. "Draw with a crow pen" means that at midnight one draws an image of the target eight finger-widths high. "On the forehead" and what follows is easy to understand. "Likewise ja" means that one writes ja at the five places, the navel and the rest. "The four nāga castes" means: on the right hand of the target the kṣatriya caste, white; on the left the brahmin caste, yellow; on the right foot the vaiśya caste, red; on the left the śūdra caste, green. "At the heart" is easy to understand.

Having drawn it thus, one places it upon the maṇḍala platform of poison, completes the circle of deities of oneself and of the one who awaits commands, and carries out the procedure of summoning and absorbing during the six sessions. Then, facing toward the direction of the target, one inserts mineral poisons such as copper between the image of the nāga and the target, and makes them one. "Place it in front" means place it upon the maṇḍala platform of poison. Then one completes the circle of oneself and of the one who awaits commands, and with the light rays from the heart renders the target and the nāga devoid of intrinsic nature. Then one should visualize, from khaṃ, a vajra rock. Upon it one should visualize, from vaṃ, a water maṇḍala adorned with hanging garlands of snakes. Within it, upon a lotus and a sun, one should generate the nāga lords and the rest, the three ḍākinīs and Mahākāla, through the three rites.

Those ḍākinīs, moreover, are red in color and greatly terrifying, with one face and two arms, holding in their hands a snake noose and a skull cup, all their limbs adorned with venomous snakes. And in front of Mahākāla, one should visualize a black phuḥ at the heart of the target. From its complete transformation one generates a black nāga with three hoods. In the nāga's right hand is a snake noose, and in its left it holds a bag of poison. One should visualize the target dwelling in its belly, upside down, head and tail reversed. Then, from the phuḥ in the target's heart, in its limbs and at its heart, one fully generates the particular classes of nāgas taught above, and one should visualize those nāgas coiling about its limbs and entering through the doors of its senses. Then, with the light rays of the heart, one should dissolve the maṇḍala of such a nature and confer empowerment. Then, at the hearts of oneself and of the one who awaits commands, one pervades everything with the outer life-essence, and one should also make offerings and torma. One should also recite the pledge mantra, and carry out the approach of accomplishment with the respective mantras: the inner essence, together with the mantras of the ḍākinīs and the nāga. Then, urged by oneself and the one who awaits commands, the principal and the retinue ḍākinīs summon the target with snake nooses, and one should dissolve the target into the image dwelling in the nāga's belly.

"Then one should kill: having empowered a peg of nilasura with mantra, one should strike." This means that one should strike with the peg, and with ritually prepared white mustard seed and poison, aiming at the doors of the target's senses. In this way one should perform the rite for seven days. Then, "Putting in any of the substances mentioned above, place it where a nāga dwells": this is in order to accomplish it at a place where a black nāga dwells, and furthermore it should be kept right there. "Like the blossom of the arjaka": this is in order to destroy that target utterly; if the rite does not succeed, that very nāga will be destroyed. The mantras of the nāga and the ḍākinīs should be known as beginning with oṃ and ending with hūṃ phaṭ. That for protecting everything should be known as beginning with oṃ and ending with hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ phaṭ svā.

Then the passage from "Furthermore" up to "requested" means that the members of the retinue, the chief of the retinue and the others, requested the common applications from the Blessed One (the Buddha). The passage beginning "Whatever outsiders" gives those to be tamed and the purpose of the ritual applications. "The Blessed One declared" means that the Lord entered the wheel through the meditative absorption of the six gazes. "Listen!" means that the six gazes were withdrawn. "Power" is the deity that protects the target. "I shall explain the rite of swift separation": because it comes before all the applications, it is called "swift," for by possessing separation, all the rites are swiftly accomplished. The passage beginning "Separation and expulsion" is an explanation in brief. "First I shall explain the rite of separation" is said in order to enter upon the rite of separation.

One performs the rite on the substances, beginning with "earth from the target's left foot." That is, facing in the direction of the target, one places the substances in a skull cup upon the maṇḍala platform of poison, and completes the circle of deities of oneself and of the one who awaits commands. At one's own heart, in the center of a fire maṇḍala, one should visualize the three ḍākinīs, Vajra Wrathful Lady and the others, arisen from hūṃ. She is yellow in color, with one face, two arms and three eyes, the Embodied One with a form of wrathful frowns, holding an iron hook in her right hand and an axe in her left. From the heart of that goddess one emanates three such mātṛkās, who enter the heart of the target, whose body is as though smeared with sesame oil. They draw out the deity who protects its life, which has the same color and shape as the target and is endowed with the three syllables, and one should dissolve it into one's own heart. Then that target, deprived of its life-protecting deity, should be summoned again and again and dissolved into the material of the liṅga effigy. It is completed in this way in six sessions, and this is what "eight finger-widths" refers to.

"On it, around the nape of a man, encircle and insert," and so on: the mantra to be written on a leaf should be written encircling the nape, and inserted. "Its desired deity": in order to assemble the materials of the image, the three ḍākinīs taught above should summon and insert the desired deity, together with the three syllables, into the continuum of the image. Then, "Making the image of correct proportions, insert at its heart the essence or the letters of the name": this means one should interweave the twenty-one-syllable mantra with the essence or the name, and insert it at the heart. "In between" means between the two placed back to back. That is, in the middle of a mantra beginning with oṃ and ending with svāhā, one writes the target's name and the deity's essence, together with de bai de śa ya, on a leaf for the space between them, and inserts it. This should be written with the natural blood of a widow and the brain of a spangti. Then one makes a maṇḍala platform of various poisons, and the rite to be performed on it is the passage beginning "Then," which is easy to understand.

"After that one should burn": this too is done as a rite. One gathers the substances in an inauspicious skull cup, and then, abiding in the yoga of Mahākāla, with the light rays from the hearts of oneself and of the one who awaits commands, one renders the substances devoid of intrinsic nature. Then, upon a sun maṇḍala, one generates hūṃ and encircles it with the separation mantra. Then, with the light rays of the heart, one invites Mahākāla of the charnel ground and requests him to remain in the sky. Then:

To him, the outer recitation,
offerings made, and torma;
the inner recitation completed,
dissolve it into the hūṃ and the mantra.

Then, from the hearts of oneself and of the one who awaits commands, one should dissolve the inner essence and the separation mantra a thousand times into the hūṃ and the mantra in front. Then it melts into light and turns into the substances. Because the substances come to possess potency, it is called "rite." "Then mustard seed," and so on, are the substances for striking, and are easy to understand.

"One should be endowed with meditative concentration" means: having made oneself Mahākāla, endowed with the circle of deities, one should generate the maṇḍala of the retinue in front. That is, upon a vajra rock, in the center of a fire maṇḍala, upon a lotus and a sun, one should generate Mahākāla and the retinue, Vajra Wrathful Lady and the others, through the three rites. Their bodily colors and hand emblems are as taught above. One dissolves the maṇḍala of intrinsic nature into them, confers empowerment, and so on. Then one pervades them with the outer essence, and one should also make offerings and torma. Then one should call them to mind with the pledge mantra, and perform the approach for them a thousand times with the inner essence and the separation mantra. Then one should be endowed with the yoga of summoning and inserting: Vajra Wrathful Lady and the others at the heart summon the desired deity as before, and one should dissolve it. Then, from the heart of the principal of the maṇḍala in front, one emanates emanated ḍākinīs, who summon the target, who remains in an empty hollow, and dissolve the target into the target's image. Having done the summoning and inserting a thousand times in this way, one then emanates, from the hearts of the ḍākinīs, Mahākāla and the rest, emanated forms that are hostile to one another: horses and buffaloes, crows and owls, snakes and mongooses, and so on. Since these enter the hearts of the target and of the deity who protects its life, one should imagine that the target and the life-protecting deity also become many such forms, and fight fiercely for a long time.

"Recite the mantra" means one should recite the inner essence and the separation mantra. That is, one should imagine that from the heart the light rays of the mantra are emanated in the forms of mutually hostile creatures, which enter the target and fight fiercely. "Strike with sand" means one should fumigate with the substances ritually prepared above, and strike with the substances, mustard seed and the rest. From "oṃ" up to the ending in "svāhā," one should know the particulars of separation.

"The desired deity should be pressed down" means it should be pressed down beneath a consecrated stūpa. And if it is a powerful deity, one should do this together with the rite of suppression. "The target should be made into a fire offering" means that, in order to kill the target now deprived of its protector, one makes it into a fire offering. "The power of separation" means "it will be beyond doubt."

"Then another" introduces the rite of expulsion. Here, "a skull in three pieces" is the base of the wheel, since that is what is to be made into the target. The substances are those beginning with "the blood of a black horse." The time for accomplishing it is when the moon is seized by Rāhu, that is, during a lunar eclipse. "Endowed with meditative concentration, the ink": having endowed oneself with the yoga, one gathers the substances of the wheel in front and, with the light rays of the heart, makes them unperceivable. Then, upon a vajra rock, from the complete transformation of yaṃ comes a wind maṇḍala, and in its center, upon a lotus and a sun, one generates Mahākāla and the ḍākinīs, Lāsyā and the rest. Those ḍākinīs are blue in color, with one face and two arms, holding a wind banner in the right hand and in the left a skull cup filled with blood; they have three eyes and abide in an extremely terrifying form. Having generated them in this way, one should draw in the maṇḍala of intrinsic nature and confer empowerment. Then, having also completed the ancillary rites, the outer essence and the rest, one should visualize at the heart of that principal a wind maṇḍala arisen from yaṃ. In its center, within a skull cup arisen from a, one clearly visualizes the substances of the wheel. From the hearts of oneself and of the ḍākinīs one emanates the inner essence together with the expulsion mantra, and one should dissolve them into the completed wheel for as long as Rāhu has not released the moon. Then one should imagine that the principal and the ḍākinīs melt into light and dissolve into the substances. When the ink has been ritually prepared in this way, it will succeed everywhere without obstruction.

Then, endowed with the yoga of the principal, one should begin drawing the wheel. The passage beginning "a child of twelve years" is easy to understand. "Its rite" means: one should first be endowed with the approach. One makes a maṇḍala platform with various poisons, and on it, with charcoal from a charnel ground, one should draw a wind maṇḍala marked with yaṃ. Then, "Having anointed the body with nectar and so on, place the wheel upon the maṇḍala platform": this means that on cloth from a charnel ground one places the image of the target within the center of the letter yaṃ, and at the five places of the target one encloses, between two hūṃ, the letters of its name and clan together with ucchaṭaya. The meaning is that the wheel is placed face down upon that image. "The skull of a weasel" means that one should draw the wheel of expulsion with the substances taught above on cloth from a charnel ground, and insert it into the skull. "The tempered peg" is a consecrated peg. "Likewise" means that one should likewise generate the circle of deities taught above.

"Through the yoga of Glorious Mahākāla" means: having endowed oneself with the yoga of the deity, one then renders the maṇḍala in front devoid of intrinsic nature with the light rays of the heart, and upon a vajra rock arisen from khaṃ one should visualize a wind maṇḍala, moving by nature, arisen from yaṃ. Upon it one completes the circle of deities taught above, and performs the dissolving of the maṇḍala of intrinsic nature and the rest. Then one carries out the approach at each one's heart with the inner essence and the expulsion mantra. Then, in front of the principal of the maṇḍala, upon a wind maṇḍala arisen from yaṃ, from the hearts of the ḍākinīs, Lāsyā and the rest, comes a blue wind camel, and on its back, upon a wind maṇḍala, one should summon and insert the target. One should visualize the wheel of expulsion residing at the heart of that target, in the center of a wind maṇḍala.

Then, from the hearts of the principal and the ḍākinīs, emanated ḍākinīs holding pegs and hammers are emanated. Remaining on the back of the camel, they turn the target to face south. Then, "One should strike with the peg and beat with the hammer" means that one should imagine those ḍākinīs striking those targets and the camel with pegs and beating them with hammers. "The three times" means that at the three times of day one should be endowed with the circle of deities and perform the rite. Then light rays come forth from the hearts of oneself, the chief of the retinue and the rest, and strike the wind maṇḍala at the camel's feet. As the wind is set in motion, one should imagine that the ḍākinīs carry off the camel and the targets toward the south and go, going very far away. "Hide it in the foundation" means that one should press down the ritually prepared wheel with the target's head turned toward the south, so that the wheel weighs it down. "He will be expelled" is said in order to remove doubt.

Then one should enter upon the yoga of bewildering. This is called cuchundara. "Charnel-ground ink" is ink ritually prepared in a charnel ground. "Draw the wheel" means that one should draw the wheel in the shape of fire on the skin of a cuchundara. That is, one makes three tiers in the center and writes the mantra of the wheel in all three tiers. In the center one should place the letters of the target's name and clan between hūṃ syllables. "Earth bearing the target's impurities" means: having taken up the earth of the target's Vairocana and Akṣobhya, "knead it with the juice of the mountain shoma" means kneading it with various poisons. "The target" means that first one should be endowed with the yoga of summoning and inserting into the material of the liṅga effigy. "Image" means it should be made to the measure of six finger-widths. "The letters of the name" means: on charnel-ground cloth one draws the wheel taught above together with the mantra, and since that wheel bewilders, it is said, "insert it at the heart." Then one places it on a fire maṇḍala in front of oneself, and the life protector should be swiftly separated. Then, having completed the yoga of summoning, it is said, "insert it within the wheel." "With the sinew of a monkey" means with both that and the sinew of a weasel. "In front of oneself" means that first one completes the yoga of the deity in oneself, and in front one makes a maṇḍala platform of various poisons. "A clay bowl" means one made from the earth to be explained and nectar. And as for what is to be done: one smears the outside with goat's nectar and smears the inside with frog's blood. In the center of the clay bowl one should place the fire seed raṃ, and so it is said, "upon it place the wheel."

Then, with the light rays from one's own heart, one makes the wheel and the rest unperceivable, and there one should visualize a vajra rock arisen from khaṃ. Upon it one visualizes a blazing fire maṇḍala, and in its center, upon a lotus and a sun, at the heart of a corpse, one generates Mahākāla arisen from hūṃ, and the three ḍākinīs, Lambodarī and the others. They too are generated black in color and greatly terrifying, with one face, two arms and three eyes, holding a trident and a fresh skull, seated in the midst of blazing fire. Then one should dissolve the maṇḍala of intrinsic nature into them and confer empowerment. Then one pervades the heart of each with the outer essence, and also makes offerings, torma and the rest; one calls the pledge to mind, and one should perform the approach at the heart of each with the inner essence and the mantra of the wheel. Then one should visualize a fire maṇḍala in front of the chief of the retinue, and in its center one visualizes the target with faded color and fallen face. Then many emanated forms are sent forth from the heart of the wheel, and with the light rays from one's own heart one should summon the target again and again, a thousand times.

Then one should perform the act of killing with great ash and white mustard seed. That is, from the hearts of the ḍākinīs, the principal of the wheel and the rest, many emanated forms holding curved knives and skull cups are sent forth. Reciting the mantra of the wheel, they chop up the target with their curved knives and devour it. From their hearts, too, are sent forth many emanated embodied forms, hawks, crows, bears, brown bears and the like, who eat the target's flesh, drink its blood, pull out its entrails and so on, and so exhaust its aggregates. "Recite the mantra" refers to oṃ and the rest, which is endowed with a terrifying form. It should have, at the end of suprati, the head and parireutri. And it should be known to end in hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ phaṭ svāhā. "Likewise" means as in the rite taught above. One should press it down at the sleeping place or at the burial ground of the ancestors; that is, one should dissolve the emanated forms into the wheel and press it down.

"Then" means one should enter upon the rite of summoning; this is the passage beginning "Furthermore." "The utpala flower" means within the flower. "Red sandalwood" is the self-arising flower. aparaciti is one's own awakening mind. "Ink" means the fifteenth day of the month. The meaning is that it should be prepared at night within the flower. And abiding in the yoga of Mahākāla, one should recite a thousand times. "Red flower" is easy to understand.

"One should be endowed with meditative concentration" means: facing west, one makes in front a maṇḍala platform of the five nectars shaped like a half moon, and there, with the substances for writing the mantra above, one makes the target's image to the measure of eight finger-widths, and one should write a long jā at its five places and four limbs. Then at the image's heart one places the flower together with the mantra. Then one should endow oneself with the yoga, and with the light rays of the heart render the maṇḍala platform and the rest devoid of intrinsic nature. There, upon a vajra rock, upon a variegated lotus and a sun, one clearly visualizes the target, turned red in color, with the letters set upon it, and at the center of the target's heart one should visualize a source of phenomena shaped like the letter e. In its center one should generate the principal, arisen from hūṃ, and Amitābha and the others. Those ḍākinīs, too, have one face and two arms, are reddish yellow in color, and have three eyes. In the right hand they hold an iron hook and a garland of flowers, and in the left a skull cup filled with nectar.

One should dissolve the maṇḍala of intrinsic nature into such a wheel. Then, having performed the empowerment, the outer essence, the offerings, the torma and the rite of the pledge, one then sends forth from one's own heart reddish yellow light rays together with the inner essence and the summoning mantra. Because they dissolve into the hearts of the principal, Amitābha and the others, it is called "empowered with mantra." Having done a thousand recitations in this way, at the end of it one should make the wheel and the target also turn into the nature of the flower. At that time the mind of that target becomes extremely distracted, and it comes swiftly. The passage beginning "A man or" is easy to understand.

"Then" means one should enter upon the rite of bringing under control; the passage beginning "Another" is easy to understand. "Red flower" is the root of a lotus. "A king or" means the image of the one to be brought under control; or, when a city is drawn on a skull, it is said, "it is looked at." "Endowed with meditative concentration" means: first one completes the yoga in oneself, and clearly visualizing, from heart to heart, the wheel described below, one should begin to draw the wheel at the target's heart. "The wheel" and so on is easy to understand. "hrīḥ" means that the letters of the target's name should be placed between hrīḥ syllables.

"Empowered with mantra through meditative concentration" means: one should complete in oneself the yoga of Mahākāla, the circle of deities, and in front of oneself make a maṇḍala suited to summoning. Upon the maṇḍala one makes the shape of a source of phenomena with the self-arising flower of a prostitute, and there one places the wheel and the flower. Then, with the light rays of one's own heart, one renders them devoid of intrinsic nature, and upon a vajra rock shaped like a half moon, arisen from laṃ, in the center of a red source of phenomena arisen from e, upon a lotus and a sun, one generates the complete circle of deities. The ḍākinīs, Vajra Blazing Light Lady and the others, are red in color, with one face, two arms and three eyes. In the right hand they bear a source of phenomena, and in the left they hold a skull cup filled with the self-arising flower. Then one should perform everything from the dissolving of the maṇḍala of intrinsic nature up to the rite of the pledge. Then, at the heart of the wheel, one clearly visualizes the target, or the target's city, arisen from hrīḥ, and at the target's heart, or in the center of the city, one should visualize the wheel drawn above, red in color.

Then, "Fill the skull cup with red flowers" means one should empower the flowers with mantra and fill the skull cup with them. Here the phrase "empowered with mantra" occurs. "Empowered" means: from the hearts of oneself and the ḍākinīs one sends forth the inner essence together with the mantra of control, which enter the mouth of the principal, and one should dissolve the mantra into the heart of the target residing at his heart. This is done for as long as the signs have not been seen. "Scent and movement" are the signs. "At that time" means at the time the signs are obtained. Then one should insert into one's own heart the target residing at the principal's heart. The wheel of the principal and the rest turns into the nature of the flower, and "merely by giving that flower into the hand" is easy to understand.

"Then" means one should enter upon the rite of all the actions. Here, from "the actions" up to "one should strive" is the brief teaching. The order is reversed in order to bewilder ordinary people. Those who know reality should arrange it in order. Here, "Expel the obstructors through meditative concentration" means: having completed in oneself the yoga of the deity, one clearly visualizes at one's own heart the principal of the circle of deities and the Sharp Embodied One and the others, as will be described below. Then, with mantras such as hūṃ vajra kapaca at the principal's heart and with the light rays of the heart, one expels the obstructors. Then, "Having made the protection wheel, draw it on a skull" means: having washed the skull with nectar, one should draw on the skull a wheel of nine tiers. In all the tiers one should write with charnel-ground ink, beginning with oṃ and ending with hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ phaṭ phaṭ svā. Then it is said, "into that skull, take up earth from a charnel ground." "In the shape of a source of phenomena" means the ground is made in the shape of a source of phenomena, in order to generate the circle of deities within the source of phenomena. And in that same place, on a Thursday, one sows white mustard seed.

Then one should generate the circle of deities. That is, with the light rays of the heart one renders the source of phenomena and the mass of white mustard seed devoid of intrinsic nature, and upon a vajra rock from khaṃ, upon a lotus and a sun, one should visualize hūṃ endowed with light rays of various colors. With the light rays of hūṃ one makes a lattice of curved knives, a tent and so on, and in its center one should visualize a source of phenomena. Then, upon as many variegated lotuses and suns as there are white mustard seeds, one should generate the principal arisen from hūṃ, together with the ḍākinīs. As for the ḍākinīs, one should generate three, the Sharp Embodied One and the others. They have one face and two arms, are black in color with three eyes, are of terrifying form, hold in their hands a skull cup and a curved knife, and stand proudly upon corpses. Having generated them completely in this way, one then, with the light rays of the heart, should complete the ancillary rites: dissolving the maṇḍala of intrinsic nature, empowerment and the rest. "From the seed at the heart" means from the hūṃ at one's own heart. "Light rays" means the inner essence and the utkata mantra together with light rays. "Into the white mustard seed" means into the principal and ḍākinīs generated from the white mustard seed. "One should dissolve" means they dissolve into the hūṃ at the heart of each.

The passage beginning "Seven or" is easy to understand. "Do this for seven times seven days" means: starting from that very time, every seven days one expels the obstructors with the light rays of one's own heart and makes the protection wheel. Then, clearly visualizing in the white mustard seed the circle of deities generated above, one completes the ancillary rites, the dissolving of the wheel of intrinsic nature and the rest, and one should do the recitation as taught above. "At the time of ripening" means: until it ripens, one should act according to the rite taught above.

"Endowed with meditative concentration" means: having completed in oneself the yoga of the deity, one should hold a feast gathering. "Then" means one should gather the white mustard seed. Gathering the white mustard seed means that the ḍākinīs of the circle of deities generated from the white mustard seed are drawn into the principal's heart, and the principal too is made to turn into the nature of the white mustard seed. Then one should begin the accomplishment of the white mustard seed; "a charnel-ground site" is easy to understand. "One should perform the approach for three days" means: having completed in oneself the circle of deities, one then, with the light rays of the heart, renders the white mustard seed and the skull cup devoid of intrinsic nature, and then, as taught above, one should generate everything from the vajra rock up to the source of phenomena. Upon it, in the center of a lotus and a sun, one should generate the principal and the ḍākinīs. The ḍākinīs are Vajra Kumu and the others. They are of various colors, with one face and three eyes, of wrathfully frowning form, holding a crossed vajra in the right hand and a fresh skull in the left; one should visualize them clearly. Then one should perform everything from the dissolving of the maṇḍala of intrinsic nature up to the rite of the pledge. Then one visualizes at the principal's heart a source of phenomena shaped like the letter e, and in its center, upon a lotus and a sun, one should generate from hūṃ forms of Mahākāla, each the size of a finger joint, as many as there are white mustard seeds. Then, from the hearts of oneself and the ḍākinīs, one sends forth the inner essence, and one should dissolve it into the Mahākālas at the heart of the principal of the wheel. When the approach has been completed in this way, the ḍākinīs gather into the principal's heart, and one should imagine that the principal too dissolves into the Mahākālas of the white mustard seed. "Everything will be accomplished" means that all actions will be accomplished, and in particular the actions described below will be accomplished.

"Then Mahākāla" means the principal of the wheel. "The Blessed One" refers to Vajradhara himself. "All the actions" and "How is it to be done?": with these words he made his request, indicating the set of actions in brief. "The Blessed One, having looked upon the assembly of the retinue" means that the great hero entered meditative absorption. "Declared" means that the compiler gathered it up and set it forth. "The armies of the enemy" is easy to understand. What first becomes the rite, beginning with oṃ, is the one ending with hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ phaṭ svā. "Its rite" means: in order to paralyze the armies, upon a vajra rock one generates from suṃ an eight-sided Mount Meru, and upon it one generates oneself as the complete circle of deities. At one's own heart, in the center of a source of phenomena, in the center of a lotus and a sun, one should generate the wheel, the principal and Vajra Kumu and the others; their bodily colors, hand emblems and so on are as taught above. Then the army becomes paralyzed by being looked upon with the vajra gaze; that is, one imagines that in the army's place, from suṃ, within Mount Meru, those figures of the army remain in a paralyzed state. Then, from the principal and the ḍākinīs at the heart, one sends forth emanated ḍākinīs, inseparable from the white mustard seed, who enter through the nostrils of the troops; the outgoing and incoming breath is blocked, and they are paralyzed in a single breath.

Then, further, there is the mantra beginning with oṃ and ending with hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ phaṭ svā. As for "By its rite, packs of lions and the like will become terrified," its rite is this: focusing on any of them, lions or the rest, when one throws the white mustard seed, one sends forth from the hearts of the principal and the ḍākinīs many hosts of emanated ḍākinīs with heads in the likeness of those beasts of prey, and one should imagine them driving those packs of beasts very far away. What further becomes the rite is this: with the one beginning with oṃ and ending with hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ phaṭ svā one terrifies the hosts of obstructors. Its rite is this: one clearly visualizes the hosts of obstructors in their direction and places them in front, in the center of a fire maṇḍala. From one's own heart one sends forth many emanated forms of the principal and the ḍākinīs bearing pegs and hammers, and remaining inseparable from the white mustard seed, they strike those obstructors with pegs and beat them with hammers. It is said that if one pelts again and again with white mustard seed through such a yoga, the hosts of obstructors will be terrified.

"Then, further" is said in order to enter upon another yoga after completing the yoga of white mustard seed alone. Here, "a black cat" and so on is easy to understand. "Mixing these" and so on is the rite to be performed. That is, one makes a maṇḍala platform with poison, pours the substances into an inauspicious skull cup, and sets it down. Then one should complete in oneself the yoga of the deity, and at one's own heart one should generate the principal and Cakravartinī and the others. Those ḍākinīs have one face, two arms and three eyes, are black in color, and hold a curved knife and a skull cup in their hands. Then one performs the dissolving of the maṇḍala of intrinsic nature, the empowerment and the rest. Then, with the light rays of the heart, one sends forth something like a garland of curved knives and should dissolve it into the substances in front. Sending it forth again and again means that, with the inner essence and mantras such as pataya, one sends them forth in the nature of curved knives and should dissolve them into the substances a thousand times. One should burn those substances in front of the one afflicted by a graha.

"One should take pride" is in order to generate the wheel taught above. "Mahākāla in front" is the very nature of the principal and the ḍākinīs at the heart. "Light rays" means light like iron hooks radiating from the heart. "Radiating outward" is in order to summon the obstructors. "Place a curved knife on top" means placing a curved knife that is to be consecrated at the māra door. "White mustard seed" and so on is easy to understand. "It expels" means: one should imagine that, as light rays like a wheel of fire radiate from the heart, the obstructors are driven out of the sick person's body and expelled very far away, and the light rays turn into a tent and so on. Then, by reciting the vajra kavaca mantra, a crossed-vajra armor forms at the five places of the one to be protected, and therefore it is called "expelling." Then, "Also in the middle of the one beginning with oṃ and ending with hūṃ" refers to the name itself.

"Flower summoning" means summoning a consort (vidyā). Because one summons her by means of a flower, it is called "flower summoning." For this, take a flower that has been tied on the head of a prostitute and a red flower struck by the sun. Further, scrape off the dirt of your own right eye, the coating of the tongue, and the dirt of the right thumb; and take the blood of the left ring finger and your own awakening mind. Smear both flowers with these, and consecrate them with mantra as the deity's flowers, as above. Then, having given the prostitute's flower into the body or hand of the woman whom one wishes to summon, if one places the other flower in one's own dwelling, she will be summoned immediately. This rite should be performed on the day of the lunar mansion Bharaṇī.

Then, further, there is the mantra beginning with oṃ and ending with svāhā that turns things into nectar. Here "elixir" means substances such as nectar. "Consecrate" means: having made it endowed with the wheel of the deity Mahākāla taught above, one should consecrate it, by the light rays from the heart, as the nectar of the wisdom beings. "Mantra" means its mantra. The one beginning with oṃ and ending with hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ phaṭ svā is the rite of compounding incense; it is to be learned in a context that is not taught here.

Then, further, the one beginning with oṃ and ending with hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ phaṭ svā is the rite of summoning. For this, from the house of a woman who sells beer, make ink from the beer-seller's menstrual blood and the beer of her house. At the base of the beer jar, using chalk for the wheel, draw a wheel shaped like a jar, with a rim, in the middle. Then, with the ink described above, write vaṃ in the center of the wheel. In its center write ākarṣaya na jaḥ jaḥgañis, so that the name lies enclosed between the two parts. On its rim write the mantra beginning with oṃ. Then make a maṇḍala of the five nectars, and on it set the jar upon a bundle of batri. Fill the inside of the jar with the ingredients of the town's beer. Then, facing toward the town, clearly visualize the wheel of deities described above. Emanating ḍākinīs as emanations from the hearts of the principal deity at the heart and of Cakravartinī and the others, summon whatever town it is where beer is found, and visualize it in the sky in front. Inside the beer jar in the sky, generate a wheel like the one drawn here. Then, by the power of having recited one's own mantra, a stream of beer descends from the wheel of the beer jar in the sky, and beer will flow without interruption into the jar in front.

Then, further, with the one beginning with oṃ and ending with hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ phaṭ svā one should pacify poison. "Removes snake venom" means: having clearly visualized the wheel of deities taught above, one should imagine that white light rays arise from a white syllable taṃ at the crown of oneself and of the deity at the heart, and that through the mantra the poison is pacified.

"Thus he declared" means "the common rites have been taught in the chapter on mantra." This is the second, the explanation of the chapter on mantra.

"Then" means that one is now to enter the wheel of deities. There, the words of the first question run from "Mahākāla" to "he requested." "Whatever applications of mantra" is said because they are both common and great applications. "Become endowed with power" is said because they are endowed with the generation stage. "Please explain" is the request made for that purpose. "The Blessed One said" means that he entered the meditative absorption of Vajradhara and of the wheel of deities. From "By the yoga of the first deity" to "he declared": because the meanings of the mantras and the rites must also be accompanied by the generation of the deity, its purpose is briefly taught so that they may be accomplished endowed with the generation stage. From "Then" up to "How is it?" is the extensive request for that purpose. From "The Blessed One said" to "generate completely" is the brief teaching of the generation stage. "He declared" is the extensive explanation.

"Charnel grounds and so on" means that one should practice in such places as a charnel ground, a solitary tree, an empty house, an island in the ocean, a place with a single landmark, the bank of a fierce river, and a battlefield. "Anoint the body with the five nectars" means that one makes dots with the five nectars at the five places, and by means of the five syllables taught above, in a single instant, one takes on the pride of the deity. "Sit on a comfortable seat" means sitting on a seat that is the corpse of a hero. The one beginning with oṃ and ending with hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ phaṭ svā is the very nature of the vajra armor. "Protect three times" is the meaning of "oneself" and so on. "Immediately" means immediately after anointing the body with nectar and sitting on the comfortable seat. "In the heart" refers to the lotus and the sun disc. "Hūṃ" is the syllable hūṃ endowed with five light rays. "Having dispelled the malignant" means that by the light rays all sentient beings are made into the nature of Mahākāla. "The guru and" and so on is easy to understand. One should make offerings in order to gather the accumulations, namely with scattered ukṣepa flowers and the like. These are consecrated by the three suchnesses and offered with the mantra of idaṃ. "Confess negative deeds": since freedom from negative deeds is itself the supreme accumulation, there is the confession of negative deeds. "Gathered" means confession, the indivisible vow, rejoicing, going for refuge, generating the mind of supreme awakening, and dedicating the roots of virtue. These too become merit for subduing the malignant and for making the teaching of the Buddha endure for a long time. "By the power of Mahākāla" means that one should dissolve the maṇḍala for gathering the accumulations into oneself. "Loving-kindness and so on" means: loving-kindness that wishes beings to attain happiness; compassion that wishes to protect them from suffering; joy at stable happiness and at the attainment of warmth; and, because one regards imagined things evenly, equanimity. Then, "one should look at the suchness of all things" means that all things are unborn from the very beginning; and since this is made clear by śūnyatās, it is called "of clarity."

Moreover, having dispelled the delusion of real things by the light rays from the heart, one makes emptiness clear by the mantra of śūnyatā. "Rest" means resting in the uncontrived state. This is the completion of generation, done without elaboration. "Empty and not empty" is the completion of completion, taught in the context of the great result.

By "From emptiness, the syllable vaṃ" and so on, the protection wheel is taught; this is the purification of the apprehended object. "In the center visualize the source of phenomena" is the purification of the apprehending subject, which holds the nature of the sphere of phenomena. "Corpse seat" means that all things are unborn. From "moon and" up to "arises" is the completion of the body through manifest awakening, which has the nature of the five wisdoms.

"One-faced" is said because he does not stir from the sphere of phenomena. "Two-armed" means abandoning the extremes of permanence and annihilation. "Curved knife" is because it cuts off afflictions such as ignorance and tames the malignant. "Holding a skull cup" means drinking the blood of māras, filled with the taste of great compassion. "Three eyes" means guarding the teaching throughout the three times. "A mass of embers" means burning up obstructors. "Hair coiled upward" is victory over the māras of the three realms. "Blood dripping from the mouth" means that he relishes the blood of the malignant and does not give up guarding the continuum of the yogin. "Tiger-skin skirt" is for taming the various malignant ones.

"His limbs adorned with the eight great nāgas" means: Vāsuki on the crest ornament; Takṣaka as the necklace; Karkoṭaka on the right ear and Padma on the left; Mahāpadma as the right bracelet and Hulunta as the left; Kulika as the right anklet and Śaṅkhapāla as the left. These are because he has the nature of the lord of power and bestows the eight great accomplishments. "With a garland of fifty skulls" means having the nature of the mantra of fifty letters. "Thus generate completely" means generating the commitment being completely.

From "In one's own heart" up to "having made them one with jaḥ hūṃ vaṃ hoḥs" is the summoning and absorbing of the wisdom being, and is easy to understand. "Light rays from the heart" means: emanating light rays like hooks from the heart, one summons the tathāgatas, the knowledge goddesses, the bodhisattvas, the wrathful kings and so on, and makes offerings to them. Visualizing that one makes requests and receives empowerment, there is the vase empowerment at the wheel of great bliss, the secret one at the throat, the wisdom-gnosis one at the heart, and at the navel the fourth, which should be guarded to its culmination. Moreover, one should understand the meaning of the words taught as the great result, "empty and not empty." At the end of that, the retinue gathers into Akṣobhya, and Akṣobhya enters as the head ornament.

Then, here, in order to bewilder ordinary beings, front generation is set far aside; that is, so that it may be thoroughly set aside. Those who know reality should understand the generation here. "The charnel ground stake" means that this support of the deity is to be consecrated. "A body one span tall" is because it is a secret body. From "the earth of the charnel ground" up to "should be absorbed" is easy to understand.

"Then" means entering into the gathering of the accumulations. That is, so as not to transgress the commitment of the principal deity and so that the retinue may obtain the accumulations, a Mahākāla emanated from the principal deity performs the offerings, the confession of negative deeds, and so on. Their detailed divisions were taught above. "Becomes a single concentration" means: that Black One emanated earlier, having completed the seven branches, dissolves into Mahākāla in front, and so it is said that it becomes a single concentration. "The four abodes of Brahmā" are to be cultivated by his continuum, as taught above. "Immediately" is because, immediately after cultivating the four abodes of Brahmā, he is taken as the retinue of the cause. That is, struck by the light rays from the principal deity's heart, Mahākāla is drawn back, enters the mouth, and enters the hūṃ at the principal deity's heart. Then, from the heart, within a ball of light of the servant of the cause, there emerges one whose size is a body one span tall. Then, in order to accomplish him as the retinue of the result, it is said, "visualize him as emptiness." That is, when the light rays of the principal deity's heart strike the servant of the cause, he melts into light and becomes the nature of the three syllables; and because the three syllables too are without intrinsic nature, it is called emptiness. "From emptiness" is because, not transgressing the commitment of the principal deity, and by the power of generating the mind, the body arises from emptiness. "The vajra rock" and so on is easy to understand. "The completed body" is the completed body of the commitment being. "Light rays from the heart": this, too, refers to the empowerment, and is of the principal deity and of that very one. "Invite" means with the four mudrās. "Mahākāla of the charnel ground" is the commitment being. "Nondual with the Black One" means that the Black One by nature is absorbed by the four mudrās above. "Having visualized" means: this too is the empowerment, and one should absorb the outer essence mantra a hundred times from heart to heart. "Offerings and torma" are offered with the root mantra. "Mahākāla the servant" is because the susmata mantra is absorbed a hundred times from heart to heart. "Having recited the mantra" means that one carries out the approach with the inner life-essence. "At the three times" is easy to understand.

Having thus obtained the signs of the approach, one should apply it to the rites; it is not to be done without having obtained the signs, because then one would be scorned by worldly people. "The Blessed One declared" means "since the wheel of deities is complete." "He requested" refers to the principal deity: that is, the meaning is that the rite of the deity is completed once the signs have been found. "How are the applications of the rites?" is the request about the particulars of the ritual applications. "The Blessed One said" means "he entered the meditative absorption of the principal deity's rites."

From "the rites of action" up to "all are accomplished" is the brief teaching, and is easy to understand. "Then Mahākāla requested" is the request concerning the rites of action. If one asks what they are, they are called the place, together with the liṅga, the time, and the application of killing. Their particulars are as follows. "The charnel ground at the water's edge" is one located at the meeting point of three waters. "A solitary tree" means one with no second tree. "The charnel ground where corpses are burned" is so called because it is ground where corpses are burned, or an empty house resembling a hearth. "The empty house of the ḍākinīs" is an empty house where the eight mātṛkās, Brāhmī and the others, dwell. "An island in the ocean" is a charnel ground located on an island. "A mountain peak" is a peak that is higher than the other mountains and is three-cornered. "The liṅga where Mahādeva dwells" is a place with shrines of non-Buddhist gods such as Mahādeva (Śiva). "The forest of salshing trees" is a charnel ground with thorns of the salshing tree and the like. "Those places" means special places, because there accomplishment comes swiftly. "Should be accomplished" means that the rites taught above, too, should be accomplished in those places.

"Then" is said because one now enters upon the rites in those places. First one should remain in the preparation of the materials for the liṅga. The materials for the liṅga run from "the three pungents" up to "dough." "And so on" means the Vairocana of the target, the leftovers of what has been eaten by a crow and by a powerful fanged beast, the five nectars, and the outer three pungents. These are poured into an inauspicious skull cup, a maṇḍala is made with various poisons, and it is placed before the servant.

Then one generates oneself completely as the wheel of deities, and then one should generate the wheel of the servant. That is, by the light rays from the heart one renders the liṅga materials without intrinsic nature. On a vajra rock arisen from khaṃ, in the middle of a lotus and sun, one should generate Mahākāla arisen from hūṃ, together with the three ḍākinīs. The ḍākinīs are the three beginning with Mind-Stealer: each has one face and two arms and so on, is white and yellow in color, three-eyed, of terrifying form, and holds a hook and a noose in her hands. Then, by the light rays from the heart, one absorbs the wheel by nature, confers empowerment, and so on. Then one should pervade the heart of each with the outer life-essence. Then, having also made offerings and torma and performed the rite of the commitments, one should carry out the approach with the inner life-essence.

Then, before the servant, in the middle of a fire maṇḍala, one clearly visualizes the liṅga materials. From the hearts of oneself and the ḍākinīs one emanates many emanated forms holding hooks and nooses, and one should carry out the application of summoning and absorbing the target. Such a rite is the rite of the liṅga materials, and it comes first in every case. "Should be furnished with the letters" means that the letters of the name and clan are inserted between the killing mantra and the two hūṃ.

When the rite of the liṅga has thus been completed, one should then destroy the vital drop; hence it says "at that time." For this, too, of the two fortnights the dark one should come first, and one begins from the first lunar day on which the vital drop rises. That is, first one completes oneself as the wheel of deities and generates the wheel of the servant before the principal deity; the vajra rock and so on are as taught above. Then the wheel of the servant: the retinue, Śaṅkhapāla and the others, are black in color, with one face, two arms and three eyes, baring their fangs, blood dripping from their mouths, holding a skull cup in the right hand and a ritual dagger in the left. Then, from the absorption of the wheel of deities by the light rays from the heart up to the approach, it is as taught above. It is not taught here, because that would be the fault of repetition.

Then, from the hearts of the principal deity and the ḍākinīs, one emanates many emanated ḍākinīs holding hooks and nooses, and performs the application of summoning and absorbing the target, a hundred times. Then, for performing the application of killing, one emanates from the hearts of the principal deity and the ḍākinīs many emanated forms bearing ritual daggers and hammers, and one should hold the thought that, at the place of the vital drop, they recite the killing mantra, stab with the ritual daggers, and pound with the hammers. One should kill a hundred times.

"The thirty-two places of the vital drop": the vital drop is the channel through which the life force moves; it is of the nature of the awakening mind. From the place of great bliss it has the nature of thirty-two channels; "thirty-two" means that, through the distinction of waxing and waning, they lie on the right and the left. They are the left big toe, the instep, the ankle, the calf, the hollow of the knee, the upper knee, the root of the thigh, the genitals, the navel, the armpit, the heart, the small tip, the mouth and nose, the ear, the left eye, and the ūrṇā between the brows. These sixteen are the waxing drops, from the first day of the bright fortnight. The sixteen places from the aperture of Brahmā and so on down to the right big toe are like the above, and since they are the waning drops, they run from the first to the eleventh of the dark fortnight. At those places, at each drop where the approach and the rite are done together, one should kill for one day each, according to the lunar date.

From "the thorns of the salshing tree" up to "should strike with virulent poison" are the particular substances for killing, and are easy to understand. "Concentration and" is said because the hosts emanated from the hearts of the principal deity and the ḍākinīs carry out the act of killing without interruption. "By mantra" means by the killing mantra. "Time" means the common and the uncommon. "Seven or" is the uncommon time: the rite is completed in seven days. That is, in each session the rite of killing is completed at all thirty-two places of the vital drop. At the end of six sessions one should complete it with a killing fire offering. "Thirty-two" refers to the approach and the rite done together, since it is both approach and rite.

"Then, further" means that one should now enter upon the close accomplishment, so that the corresponding sacred substances and the wheel of the servant may be accomplished swiftly. "One should do it in an instant through the concentration of Glorious Mahākāla" means: first, mix various poisons, the five nectars, the five meats and beer together thoroughly and make them into pills. Then place them inside a consecrated skull cup, cover its mouth with charnel ground cloth, and set it on a well-made maṇḍala of the five nectars. Complete oneself as the wheel of deities. Then, by the light rays from one's own heart, visualize the skull cup and the laḍḍu as without intrinsic nature. There one should imagine a vajra rock arisen from khaṃ; on the rock imagine a source of phenomena shaped like the letter e; in its center imagine a lotus and sun; and on that one should generate the wheel of deities arisen from hūṃ. That is, one should generate the three beginning with Sky Wheel: each with one face and two arms, the color of the sky, three-eyed, holding a vajra and a skull cup of blood in her hands. Then, by the light rays from the heart, one absorbs the wheel by nature and so on, up to the rite of the commitments.

Then, in the heart of the wheel, upon a moon and sun, one should imagine, from a repha, sun maṇḍalas equal in number to the laḍḍus. On each sun maṇḍala a hūṃ transforms and becomes a ball of light, and in the center of the ball of light one visualizes, from hūṃ, the inner essence, encircled by the laḍḍu mantra. Then from the hearts of oneself and the ḍākinīs one emanates the inner essence and the laḍḍu mantra, and one should imagine that they enter the mouth of the principal deity and dissolve into the mantra of the ball in the center of the sun. If one does the approach in this way for three days, it will be accomplished. "Instantly" means "is accomplished swiftly." If one asks why, it is because this accomplishes the heart's life force of the principal deity of the retinue that it will be accomplished swiftly. "The laḍḍu torma" means that when the approach has thus been completed, the ḍākinīs gather into the principal deity, and one should imagine that the principal deity melts into light and becomes the nature of the laḍḍus. Making those laḍḍus into a torma is called the supreme torma. The mantra is the one beginning with oṃ and ending with hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ phaṭ svā.

"Then another" means that one should now enter upon accomplishing incense with the sacred substances, both the common accomplishment and the special, supreme accomplishment. First, make a maṇḍala of the five nectars, and on it set up three skulls as a hearth tripod. On top of the skulls make a lamp wick of charnel ground cloth, one span long, and one should pour in the lamp great black butter. In the middle of the wick one should write the life-essence with charnel ground ink. Then light it, and let the light of the lamp pervade the maṇḍala. In the east mustard seed, in the south garlic, in the west menstrual blood, in the north virulent poison, in the northeast great flesh, in the southeast great guggulu, in the southwest Radiant, and in the northwest luti: spread these in equal portions on the maṇḍala. Then complete oneself as the wheel of deities, and by the light rays from the heart make the lamp unapprehended. Then, right there, generate Mahākāla the servant by the three rites, and complete the ancillary rites such as absorbing the wheel by nature. One should imagine the substances, mustard seed and the rest, in the middle of a moon and sun from the āli and so on. Then from the hearts of oneself and the servant one emanates the inner essence together with the mantra for compounding incense, and should absorb it into the mustard seed and the rest twenty-one times. Then, as it says, gather them together, grind them to powder, and mix them with great black butter.

That, too, should be accomplished supremely. Pour it into a consecrated skull cup, place it in the middle of the maṇḍala, and place the lamp on it. Then complete oneself as the wheel of deities, and then by the light rays from the heart make the incense and the rest without intrinsic nature. Then one should generate the wheel of deities: on a vajra rock arisen from vaṃ, in the middle of the protection wheel, one should imagine a source of phenomena. In its center, on a lotus and sun, generate completely the wheel of deities arisen from hūṃ: that is, the ḍākinīs Fire Form and the others, reddish yellow in color, with one face, two arms and three eyes, of wrathful, frowning form, holding a curved knife and a skull cup in their hands. Having generated them completely in this way, then absorb the maṇḍala by nature and so on. In the heart of the principal deity of the wheel, in the middle of a moon and sun generated from āli and kāli, is a lotus vessel formed from ā; in its center is the nature of incense arisen from hūṃ, having the nature of five light rays; and in its center one should make the essence of hūṃ clear. Then imagine it encircled by the inner essence and the incense mantra. Then from the hearts of oneself and the ḍākinīs one emanates the inner essence and the mantra for compounding incense, in the nature of five light rays, and imagines that they radiate to the heart of the principal deity and dissolve there. One should do the approach in this way until signs such as sounds, scents and lights appear. When the approach is complete, the principal deity and the ḍākinīs should be made to become the nature of the incense. "In front" means in front of the servant. "In five days" means that by burning the incense it will be accomplished. Offerings and torma at the three times and so on are easy to understand. "That yogin becomes endowed with every aim" means that he will obtain the common and supreme accomplishments. "He declared" means that the compiler summarized the meaning of the deity and explained it to the assembly. "The chapter on the deity" is the chapter on the rite of generating the deity. This is the third, the explanation of the chapter on the deity.

"Then": in order to do the approach for the generation of the deity, to empower the deity, to ripen the practitioner, and so that it becomes a special support for the approach, therefore he requested. "The Glorious Black One" refers to the retinue, the principal deity of the retinue and the others. "Requested of the Blessed One" means they requested of Vajradhara. "The meaning of the tantra" is said because the meaning of the tantra is the approach and the ritual applications.

"Special features of the rite" means "it is a special maṇḍala." "He declared" is the brief teaching, and is easy to understand. "Then another" means that one should begin drawing the maṇḍala. That is:

having first completed the approach
and obtained the signs of the deity,
so that yogins may gain accomplishment
and in order to ripen disciples,
should examine a genuine disciple:
steady, disciplined and intelligent,
a hero complete in every characteristic,
firm, and desiring the accomplishments of mantra,

should accept the disciple.
Such a disciple, so that
the guru may admit him to the maṇḍala,

join his palms holding flowers,

to command himself in the rite

in the way that will appear below,

These are the places taught above, the charnel ground, the solitary tree and so on. "An empty house" is ground where a maṇḍala was drawn before. In those places one should begin the maṇḍala. In the middle of ground that is very untamed and frightful, sprinkle the five nectars and spread ukṣepa flowers as a carpet.

In its center, oneself the yogin
should generate as will appear below.

golden in color, with a maiden's ornaments,

scatter loose flowers,

of all the buddhas, the protectors,
in the grounds and the perfections,
you, goddess, have gained mastery.
Just as Glorious Mahākāla
crushed the hosts of yakṣas, māras and the rest,

I shall draw the maṇḍala."
Having requested three times,
the earth goddess, with a joyful countenance,
gives her permission and dissolves into the earth.

consecrates the action vajra.

Here "the yogin sits in human form, having bathed" means:

One of fine form, free of conceptualization,
endowed with discipline through a hero's mind:
such a one should be consecrated.
With drops of the five nectars
one makes dots at the five places,
sets him upon a maṇḍala of nectar,
renders him without intrinsic nature by the light,
and generates the yoginī from hūṃ.
At the five places, upon sun disks,
one clearly imagines the syllable hūṃ.
"He is consecrated by all the yoginīs" means:
the Heart, Commitment and Action ḍākinīs,
the terrifying ones and the Terrifiers,
are clearly summoned before one;
having presented offerings and torma,
dissolve them into the five places in order.
Then confer empowerment and seal it,
and give offerings and torma with the root mantra.
The essence, together with the mantra,
arising from my own heart,
should be clearly dissolved into the five places.
The mantra is the one beginning with oṃ
and ending with hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ phaṭ svā.

"Then" means that, having had the action vajra fetch earth and water,

one mixes them with the earth of that place,
and with nectar water and mantra
one should purify the faults of the ground.
The mantra is the one beginning with oṃ
and ending with hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ phaṭ svā.

Then, as for making the maṇḍala:
the square maṇḍala platform
should be pounded level and firm.
With the five kinds of nectar from the cow
one should anoint the maṇḍala well.
Then the lord of yogins,
seated well in the center of the maṇḍala,

Then, from the light rays of the heart,
many emanated Black Ones are sent forth,
all of them one-faced and two-armed,
black in color, of terrifying form,

They are emanated out to the ten directions,

Then the lord of yogins,

the obstructors dwelling in that ground

The wrathful ones melt completely into light

goddess, you are under my sway.
Vajra of body, speech and mind,

and in order to generate the wheel of deities,

With the samaya mantra given from the heart,

Then, with the inner essence mantra,

Hevajra, chief of teachers,
principal, embodiment of the great maṇḍala,
Glorious Mahākāla,
the eight heart ḍākinīs,

"In the palace of the Great Charnel Ground

Having taken refuge and generated the mind of awakening,

Having thus taken the vows,

Then the rite of the tooth stick is performed.
It should be neither crooked nor cracked nor worm-eaten,
and should measure twelve finger-widths.
Anointed with fragrant nectar,
it should be bound with flowers at three places.
Blessing it with the three syllables,

The disciple, placed upon the maṇḍala platform
and facing toward the east,
fixes his mind on the deities of the maṇḍala,
recites the ten-syllable mantra,
and should throw the tooth stick straight down.
Having looked at the tooth stick's signs of accomplishment,
the rest of the maṇḍala rite should be learned elsewhere.
Then, summoning the disciple before him
and consecrating him by name with the root mantra,
the master should place protection at the three places.
Then, for the disciple in front of him,
he pours nectar into a skull cup
and gives it to the disciple to sprinkle and to drink:
oṃ hrīḥ biśodhaya sarba pāpaṃ ca saṃśodhaya bikalpana apanaya hūṃ phaṭ.
With this he should give it three times.
Then, on a red thread,
with the kabaca mantra, the armor mantra,
three knots should be tied,
and the protection cord is fastened on the right upper arm.
Three blades of kuśa grass,
consecrated with the root mantra, should be given.
Then, in order to uplift the disciple's mind,
with the example of the udumbara flower
the Dharma should be well explained.
Then the excellent disciple,
with the generation of the mind of awakening going before,
is given the ten-syllable mantra to recite.
Then, with a joyful mind,
he examines his dreams toward the close of night.
Having thus performed the preparation,
the vajra master, as described,
should present offerings and torma
to the maṇḍala that has been prepared.

"Laying the lines with a thread of human hair" means this:
Then the vajra master

The yoginī blessed earlier

It is colored with charnel-ground pigments,
placed inside five skull cups,
and set in the center of the maṇḍala.

seated upon a corpse seat,
generates the wheel of deities completely.

in the center of moon and sun
arisen from the vowels and consonants,
one should visualize five syllables.
Then, with the light rays of the heart,
one invites the sugatas and places them in the sky.
Then, having made offerings,
one should dissolve them into the line-cords
as the embodiment of the five sugatas:
oṃ samaya sūtra mātikrama hūṃ.
Together with the inner essence mantra,
by the mantra sent forth from the heart

abiding in the center of moon and sun.
Then the line-cords are twisted into one
and placed in the center of the maṇḍala,
and from the line-cord, wholly transformed,
one generates Glorious Mahākāla.
One invites the wisdom wheel and dissolves it in,
confers empowerment, and performs the outer recitation;
one makes offerings and torma and performs the samaya recitation.
Then, with the inner essence mantra,
one performs a thousand recitations
of the mantra wheel at the heart.
Then it melts into light,
and one imagines it becomes the wisdom cord.
Then one should begin to lay the lines
of the maṇḍala of oneself and the attendants.
Then, urging them by means of the line,
one invites the wisdom wheel
and requests it to remain in the sky.
Its samaya is urged in this way:
"In order to tame without remainder
the hosts of māras in all the world realms,
for the sake of beings, the buddhas and all,
this is your purpose": thus one urges.
Then the Brahmā lines should be laid.
On each side of the Brahmā lines,
measuring the intervals with a hardwood rod,
one lays twenty line-strands each.
The gateways follow the general system; the rest should be learned elsewhere.
When the lines have thus been laid,
the maṇḍala invited into the sky
is worshiped with offerings and dissolved into the lines.
Then one begins the rite of the colors.

"Dissolving the maṇḍala arisen from space" means this:
