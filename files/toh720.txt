Dhāraṇī Following the Bodhimaṇḍa, Showing the Tathāgata's Wisdom

Toh 720 in the Kangyur is a short dhāraṇī text concerned with the cultivation of wisdom and the power of retention, commonly known in English as the Dhāraṇī for Retaining What One Has Heard. It belongs to the dhāraṇī class of canonical literature, presenting a sacred mantra formula attributed to the Buddha Śākyamuni together with brief ritual instructions on how its recitation — including a practice involving medicine held in the palm — sharpens the intellect and enables the practitioner to remember whatever has been heard. Translated by the Indian master Vidyākarasiṃha and the great translator-monk Devacandra, who requested and finalized it.

Homage to the Three Jewels. Homage to the Tathāgata, arhat, perfectly and fully awakened buddha Śākyamuni.

The dhāraṇī is as follows:

tadyathā — mati bahulye tathāgatajñānadarśane prajñāptā gambhīre bodhimaṇḍānusāriṇī aviddha tamondha karavidhamani jvala jvala prajñāvīpe medhāpadini sarva adhiṣṭhita suru suru dhuru dhuru medhā ānaya svāhā.

If one performs the ritual inwardly and recites this mantra, one's intelligence will grow sharp. If one places medicine in the palm of the hand, recites the mantra until smoke arises, and then eats it, one will retain what one has heard.

This completes the dhāraṇī for retaining what one has heard.
