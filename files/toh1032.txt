The Dhāraṇī That Causes Displeasure

This brief work, known in English as The Dhāraṇī That Causes Displeasure, is listed as Toh 1032 in the Kangyur, where it belongs to the class of dhāraṇī texts, the collection of incantations and spells preserved within the canon. Its Sanskrit and Tibetan titles are not stated in the opening lines, and the text itself is extremely short. After a homage to the Three Jewels, it gives a single mantra together with a ritual instruction: by reciting the mantra over tooth powder and placing the powder in one's mouth, anyone one looks at will become pleased. The gap between its closing title, which speaks of displeasure, and its stated effect of pleasing others is notable, and it may reflect the way such short practical spells were named and transmitted. Like many works in this section of the Kangyur, it is a compact, practice-oriented formula meant for a specific worldly purpose, here winning the favor of others.

Homage to the Three Jewels.

oṃ namaḥ śparanama svāhā

If one recites this mantra over tooth powder and puts it in the mouth, whoever one looks at will become pleased.

The Dhāraṇī That Causes Displeasure is complete.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The title in the closing line reads literally "the dhāraṇī that makes displeased" (mi dga' bar byed pa). This sits oddly with the rite in the body, which is meant to make others pleased. The title may be corrupt, or it may name the dhāraṇī by the condition it addresses, overcoming others' displeasure. Because neither reading is certain, the title has been translated as it stands. The final syllable after "complete" is a scribal end-mark and is not translated.
