The Dhāraṇī for Learning a Thousand Ślokas is a brief ritual text devoted to the noble bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara, intended to strengthen memory and the capacity for learning. After paying homage to the Three Jewels and to Avalokiteśvara, it gives a short Sanskrit incantation and then a simple rite. The practitioner makes a maṇḍala before an image of the bodhisattva, presents whatever offerings are available, and consecrates water with the mantra. Each morning the mantra is recited seven times over the water, and food taken with milk is blessed with it. The text promises that after seven days one will be able to learn as many as a thousand verses in a single day, with reassuring signs of success. It is Toh 1045 in the Kangyur, where it belongs among the dhāraṇī texts, the short works of protective and beneficial incantations found within the tantra section of the canon.

Homage to the Three Jewels!
Homage to the bodhisattva, the great being, the noble Avalokiteśvara!

tadyathā triśī triśūla śakhi āryāvalokiteśvarāya ājñāpayati barena buhala mṛddhātu svāhā

The rite is as follows. Before the feet of the noble Avalokiteśvara, make a maṇḍala and present offerings with whatever is at hand. Consecrate water with the mantra and set it aside. The next morning, recite the mantra seven times over the water. Then in the forenoon, eat food with milk and the mantra. When seven days have passed, one will learn as many as a thousand ślokas even in a single day. There will be signs that give confidence.

The dhāraṇī for learning a thousand ślokas is complete.

The phrase "eat food with milk and the mantra" is terse in the Tibetan. It most likely means that one eats a milk meal empowered by the mantra, or eats it while reciting the mantra. The final syllable after "complete" appears to be a scribal flourish and is not translated.
