The Noble Great King of Dedications, together with Mantras (Ārya Mahāpariṇāmarāja Samantraka)

The Noble Great King of Dedications, together with Mantras (Sanskrit, Ārya Mahāpariṇāmarāja Samantraka) is a short devotional and liturgical work centered on the dedication of merit, the Buddhist practice of directing the benefit of one's virtuous actions toward the welfare and awakening of all beings. It opens with homage to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas and with verses of going for refuge, confession of non-virtue, rejoicing in the virtue of others, and requesting the buddhas to turn the wheel of Dharma, before turning to its dedications and accompanying mantras. The Tibetan text records that it was translated, revised, and finalized by the Indian preceptor Vidyākaraprabha and the great reviser-translator Palbrtsegs, who were active during the early Tibetan translation period. It appears as Toh 810 in the Kangyur, where it belongs to the tantra section, reflecting its combination of prayer with mantra recitation.

Translated, revised, and finalized by the Indian preceptor Vidyākaraprabha and the great reviser-translator, the venerable Palbrtsegs (Dpal-brtsegs).

In the Indian language: Ārya Mahāpariṇāmarāja Samantraka. In the Tibetan language: The Noble Great King of Dedications, together with Mantras. First volume.

Homage to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas.

I go for refuge, without exception,
to the buddhas, ocean of boundless qualities,
to the noble and sacred Dharma,
and to the Saṅgha who abide throughout the three times.

I confess all non-virtue.
I rejoice in the virtue of beings.
I request the fully awakened ones
to turn the wheel of Dharma.

Whatever small virtue I may have,
seen by the all-knowing eye,
by it may all those who are forever separated
from happiness be completely pacified.

May the samādhi that knows emptiness,
endowed with the supreme of all aspects,
be, throughout my lives until awakening,
always accompanied by happiness.

May I abide constantly in equipoise,
free of concepts and spontaneously accomplished,
and, like the sun, the moon, and the wish-fulfilling jewel,
may I engage in the benefit of sentient beings.

Just as the wise Victors
dedicate through the perfection of wisdom and skillful means,
so too do I dedicate all my virtues
to awakening.

Whatever virtue of my body, speech, and mind
is directed toward the Three Jewels,
and whatever arises from generosity, moral discipline,
patience, diligence, meditative concentration, and wisdom,

by all of these, may I myself
be endowed with pure conduct,
and in the teaching of the Protector of the World
may I be master of delight in going forth.

Having become skilled in the meaning and words
of the scriptures spoken by all the buddhas,
may I uphold the sacred Dharma
and have no attachment to body or life.

When the time of my death comes,
with an undistorted and unconfused mind,
having seen the Buddha Amitāyus,
may I go from here to Sukhāvatī, the Blissful Land.

When the realm of Māra becomes manifest,
may the great sage Amitāyus
constantly be my teacher,
and having attained the ten grounds,

by their stages of complete purification,
may I attain supreme awakening.
Having attained the state of the buddha Samantabhadra,
having uprooted all accumulated faults together with their habitual tendencies,

endowed with unobscured wisdom and compassion,
may I bring healing and happiness to beings.

I pay homage to the Blessed Buddhas of the ten directions, whose minds pervade without exception all that is to be known throughout the boundless ten directions, and to the bodhisattvas, numerous as the ocean.

Having cleared away the stains of mind and the twofold mentation,
first of all I bow my head to Śākyamuni, the sage, king of the Śākyas.
To the Victor Amitābha, who dwells in Sukhāvatī,
and to my teachers as well, I long with joy and reverence.

To those buddhas who now appear in all directions,
who abide with certainty as loving protectors of beings,
and to all the Victors of the past and of the future,
I make perfect offerings with a joyful mind.

To all the fully awakened buddhas of the past, the future, and the present, who possess the nature that is without birth and without cessation, all sentient beings go for refuge, until they reach the essence of awakening.

Likewise, they go for refuge, until reaching the essence of awakening, to the Dharma of those Blessed Ones. That Dharma is free of conceptual elaboration and without letters; it is to be known individually, each by his own awareness, by the noble ones. In it there are no sentient beings, and it is free of the dust of sentient beings. It is without self, free of the dust of attachment, without life-force, free of birth and death. It has no person; it bears the mark of the perfect peace of the earlier extreme and the later extreme. It is free of attachment, of a manner without any object of focus. It is not a letter. All speech is cut off in it; it is inexpressible, free of all waves. It follows after all things and is like space. It is without color, free of sign and shape and all movement. It is without self, free of clinging to a self. It is without conscious intimation, free of mind, mentation, and consciousness. Because it has no equal, it has no likeness. It is free of the mark of a cause, without anything superimposed by conditions. It is equal to the dharmadhātu; it is the meditative equipoise of all phenomena. In the manner of going nowhere, it follows suchness. Because it is utterly unmoving, it abides in the true limit. Because it does not abide in the six sense objects, it is unshaken. Because it has no abode, it comes anywhere and yet there is no going. It is placed in equipoise upon emptiness, distinguished by the signless. It bears the mark of the wishless. It is free of conceptual thought and of refutation, without establishing and without disturbing. It is free of arising and perishing. It has no all-base. It has passed completely beyond eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, and mind. It is without height and without lowness; it has attained abiding without movement. It is free of all activity. In brief, it is without any producing, without arising, without cessation, without ceasing: that unconditioned Dharma. To it, with all their bodies they prostrate, and with every thought of intention and superior intention, with joy from the marrow of the bones and with joy rising from the soles of the feet, they go for refuge, until reaching the essence of awakening.

Having thus gone for refuge to the Buddha, the Dharma, and the Saṅgha, I will generate the awakening mind, with every thought of intention and superior intention, making great compassion manifest, in order to become a physician and a source of happiness for all sentient beings, a protector of those without protectors, a refuge of those without refuge, an ally of those without allies, to bring light to those who have become darkness through attachment, aversion, and delusion, to show the way to those who dwell on evil paths, and so to liberate the sentient beings of the three realms.

Just as the buddhas, the Blessed Ones of the past, the future, and the present, and the bodhisattvas have generated it, are generating it, and will generate it, so I too will generate it. With a mental continuum of intention and superior intention, I will generate it in just that way.

I will generate it in such a way that nothing is obstructed, nothing is accumulated, and it is shown not to be accumulated; there is no accomplishing, no thought, and no conceptual construction.

Where there is no body and no designation of a body; no speech and no designation of speech; no mind, no mental consciousness, and no cognition; it is not suchness, not the dharmadhātu, not the true limit, not buddha, not awakening. Where there is no clinging to any of these, I will generate it in just that way.

Relying on the conventional, I confess all misdeeds. I rejoice in all merit. I request all the Tathāgatas to teach.
