The Noble Unconquerable Jewel Garland (Āryaratnamālānāmāparājita)

The Noble Unconquerable Jewel Garland, known in Sanskrit as the Āryaratnamālā Aparājita and in Tibetan as 'Phags pa gzhan gyis mi thub pa rin po che'i phreng ba, is a short dhāraṇī text, a work centered on the recitation of protective and auspicious formulas. It opens with an extended litany of homage, first to the Three Jewels and the seventy million perfectly awakened buddhas with their communities of hearers, then to the bodhisattvas led by Maitreya, and then to noble practitioners at every stage of the path, from arhats and non-returners to once-returners and stream-enterers. The homage widens further to divine sages, accomplished ascetics, and the many classes of beings in the Buddhist cosmos, including gods, nāgas, yakṣas, gandharvas, mahoragas, and kinnaras, invoking their goodwill as a foundation for the power of the "unconquerable" formula. The text is Toh 929 in the Kangyur, where it belongs among the dhāraṇī works, a class of scriptures valued for their incantatory and protective use.

Then the heart of the Unconquerable Jewel Garland, spoken by the perfectly awakened Buddha and rejoiced in by the gods and nāgas, which accomplishes every activity and is pure, should be recited:

Pure, and likewise auspicious,
merit-making, pure, and glorious,
it cannot be overcome by the gods who crush rākṣasas,
nor by any sentient being.
Utterly destroying all wrongdoing,
accomplishing every aim, glorious,
completely destroying every graha:
this vidyā-mantra should be recited.
It brings victory at all times;
not even Śakra (Indra) can overcome it.
It destroys poison and executioners.
From the fear of fire, enemies and kings,
from executioners, grahas and vetālas,
from bad dreams and bodily corruption,
from gods, asuras and gandharvas,
from yakṣas, rākṣasas and nāgas,
on every side for up to a hundred leagues,
please protect me always!
From fever that comes each day,
every second day and every third,
from fever that comes every fourth day,
from terrible fevers, this protects.
Even poison and venom that are eaten
will be digested like food.
Those in whose womb no child is conceived,
and those whose sons die,
through this jewel garland
are quickly brought to life and thriving.
Those who want wealth obtain wealth;
those who want glory also obtain glory.
In fighting, in quarrel, and on the battlefront,
it swiftly brings victory.
Having looked upon the plight of beings,
the Protector of the World proclaimed
this vidyā-mantra called the Jewel Garland.
It should be recited:

tadyathā oṃ dhanakari vipulakari jayakari yaṣṭikari pridhikari āyurdade puṇyadāri alakṣmāpraśamani dhanadhanya vivartani śrīkari sarva graha nakṣatra pāśavartini medhājanani putrajanani parivarāvardhani āyurdade pritikari jaṃbhani staṃbhani mohani amoghaśakti kañcanamāle grahasaumyakaraṇi sarva kalikalaha vigra vivyādha praśamani paracekranivāraṇi parasainya stambhani cittaniramalīkaraṇī sarva pāpaṃ praśamani sarva kāla viṣṭa nāśani svāhā curu curu svāhā turu turu svāhā muru muru svāhā sarva deva anujhite svāhā sarva nāga saṃrakṣite svāhā oṃ niri niri phaṭ hana hana phaṭ daha daha phaṭ paca paca phaṭ

All those who think with hatred toward me, who intend harm, who perform fire offerings, who perform sorcery, who cause it to be performed, and who rejoice in its being performed, all of them phaṭ phaṭ svāhā

aghiṃ stambhayāmi veṣiśūtrūṃ udakaṃ caurāna sarva duṣṭana sarva pratyarthika pratyamantra staṃbhayāmi jaṃbhayāmi mohayāmi phaṭ phaṭ svāhā

It is called the Unconquerable,
adorned with the jewel garland;
it performs every activity, and likewise
it always gives protection.
Beings, or whoever it may be,
any of them may write it and wear it.
Crusher of rākṣasas, supreme merit,
it destroys the turbid stain of wrongdoing.
In battle and in fighting, too,
it is wholesome, bringing victory everywhere.
Whatever person, having made himself clean,
writes down and wears this vidyā-mantra,
reads it and remembers it, that wise one
will never meet with any fear.
If one reads and wears it according to the rite,
all the guhyakas will always protect him
and will take care of him.
It always brings victory.
Just as a head adorned
with a jewel garland is beautiful,
so one becomes beautiful with every excellence.
Whoever wears this vidyā-mantra,
his grahas are utterly destroyed;
he will not be struck by illness;
his sons and grandsons will flourish;
his retinue will become excellent.
To benefit all sentient beings,
and especially for the sake of what has been done,
all the tathāgatas
blessed this vidyā-mantra.
The lords of the gods
and the bodhisattvas likewise protect him,
and the tathāgatas
of the ten directions watch over him.
Virtue and wealth increase,
and wrongdoings, too, are purified.
From bad dreams and misdeeds committed
he is freed; of this there is no doubt.

tadyathā muni muni muni muni muniri mati mati mati mahāmati Protect me from all fears, protect me! Homage to all the buddhas. From epidemic disease and from all harms may I be safe and well! svāhā

The Noble Unconquerable Jewel Garland is complete.

The verse line rendered "having looked upon the plight of beings" reads gnong gzigs. The word gnong is unusual here, and the sense (compassionate regard for beings' suffering) is inferred from context. In "wrongdoings, too, are purified," the text reads byad par 'gyur, which makes little sense. It has been read as the similar-looking byang bar 'gyur, "are purified." The line "especially for the sake of what has been done" is translated literally, as its intended reference is unclear. "Lords of vidyā" renders rig pa'i bdag po, probably the masters or kings of spell-knowledge.
