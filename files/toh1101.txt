Verses of Well-Being (Svastigāthā)

The Verses of Well-Being, known in Sanskrit as Svastigāthā and in Tibetan as Delek kyi Tsik su Chepa, is a brief verse text of blessing meant to be recited for auspiciousness and protection. It opens with a god approaching the Buddha and asking what words should be spoken so that well-being may arise, and the Buddha answers with a series of verses praising the Three Jewels as incomparable treasures surpassing every precious thing in this world, in other worlds, and in the heavens. Each praise culminates in a solemn declaration of truth, by whose power well-being is invoked, closely echoing the style of the Pali Ratana Sutta and similar protective recitations. The work is Toh 1101 in the Kangyur, where it is found among the short recitation texts of blessing and protection gathered toward the end of the collection.

In the language of India: svasti gāthā. In the language of Tibet: The Verses of Well-Being.

Homage to the Three Jewels.

A certain god drew near
And put a question to the One Worshipped by the Gods:
"Great Buddha, great Sage,
What should one say so that well-being comes about?"

When he had heard these words,
The Kinsman of the World declared:
"Listen to these words of mine:
Whoever recites them constantly, well-being will come.

"Whatever vast and supreme jewels there are
In this world, in other worlds, and in the heavens,
None of them can in any way match the precious perfect Buddha,
Not in number, not in count, not even in the smallest part.
Wonderful, most marvelous, immeasurable
Is the precious perfect Buddha, supreme in the world.
As this of mine is so, supremely true,
By this truth may there be great well-being.

"Cessation, peace, the deathless nectar, most excellent,
Which Śākyamuni realized in meditative equipoise:
There is nothing equal to that Dharma.
Once it is made manifest, from then on there is no sorrow.
Wonderful, most marvelous, immeasurable
Is the precious holy Dharma, supreme in the world.
As this of mine is so, supremely true,
By this truth may there be great well-being.

"The eight persons whom the Victor taught
Are the four pairs of individuals.
Such worthy recipients of offerings are very rare on earth;
What is given to them becomes immeasurable.
Wonderful, most marvelous, immeasurable
Is the precious Saṅgha, supreme in the world.
As this of mine is so, supremely true,
By this truth may there be great well-being.

"Holy one, chief, supreme and excellent giver,
Supreme light, hero, abode of the holy and supreme,
Holy in wisdom, supreme among the holy,
Knower of the supreme and the not supreme: this is the one Blessed One.
Wonderful, most marvelous, immeasurable
Is the precious perfect Buddha, supreme in the world.
As this of mine is so, supremely true,
By this truth may there be great well-being.

"The holy samādhi without interruption,
Taught by the infinite and unsurpassed Tathāgata,
That samādhi is stable, likened to a vajra;
There is no samādhi equal to that samādhi.
Wonderful, most marvelous, immeasurable
Is the precious samādhi, supreme in the world.
As this of mine is so, supremely true,
By this truth may there be great well-being.

"Just as a door bolt, firm and very firm,
Is not shaken by the four mighty winds,
So the wise, who have seen the four truths and abide in them,
Are not moved here by the talkers of the world.
Wonderful, most marvelous, immeasurable
Is the precious Saṅgha, supreme in the world.
As this of mine is so, supremely true,
By this truth may there be great well-being.

"Conqueror of all, all-knowing, all-seeing,
Omniscient, endowed with all the powers,
Holiest of all, free from all grasping,
Knower of every intention: this is the one Blessed One.
Wonderful, most marvelous, immeasurable
Is the precious perfect Buddha, supreme in the world.
As this of mine is so, supremely true,
By this truth may there be great well-being.

"Ending existence, unmoving, without change,
Faultless, fully freed from every fear,
Utter cessation, excellent, the definitive deliverance,
Utterly stainless, peace, not to be carried off by the intellect.
Wonderful, most marvelous, immeasurable
Is the precious holy Dharma, supreme in the world.
As this of mine is so, supremely true,
By this truth may there be great well-being.

"Those who have seen the truths that reveal utter suffering and bestow happiness,
Who are heedful,
And free of doubt about the precious truths,
Will not later take an eighth birth.
Wonderful, most marvelous, immeasurable
Is the precious Saṅgha, supreme in the world.
As this of mine is so, supremely true,
By this truth may there be great well-being.

"Holy among the holy, he knows the holy ultimate;
He knows the supreme and the not supreme, and strives for the ultimate;
Not to be carried off by others, he has realized and understood the holy;
He is the supreme ally of those in great suffering.
Wonderful, most marvelous, immeasurable
Is the precious perfect Buddha, supreme in the world.
As this of mine is so, supremely true,
By this truth may there be great well-being.

"Having generated aspiration, they become endowed with yoga;
Ever diligent in Gautama's teaching, fully liberated and at peace,
Not to be led by others, they touch that peace;
Having touched it here, they no longer fall within reckoning.
Wonderful, most marvelous, immeasurable
Is the precious Saṅgha, supreme in the world.
As this of mine is so, supremely true,
By this truth may there be great well-being.

"Even if one who has seen the truth, through forgetfulness,
Should now and then commit some wrong,
That does not make him one fit to be called immoral,
Because this nature of reality abides unshaken.
Wonderful, most marvelous, immeasurable
Is the precious Saṅgha, supreme in the world.
As this of mine is so, supremely true,
By this truth may there be great well-being.

"In all the world together with its gods
There is none superior or equal to the Buddha,
The unsurpassed teacher of gods and humans,
Supreme among teachers, guides, and physicians.
Wonderful, most marvelous, immeasurable
Is the precious perfect Buddha, supreme in the world.
As this of mine is so, supremely true,
By this truth may there be great well-being.

"In the first month of summer, the Sage
In the forest attained the holy Dharma.
There is nothing superior or equal to that Dharma;
Having touched it here, one no longer falls within reckoning.
Wonderful, most marvelous, immeasurable
Is the precious holy Dharma, supreme in the world.
As this of mine is so, supremely true,
By this truth may there be great well-being.

"Their old evil exhausted, no new evil done,
Free of pestilence, they are fearless and free of sorrow;
Their roots cut off, they produce no further existence;
The teaching will blaze like a lamp.
Wonderful, most marvelous, immeasurable
Is the precious Saṅgha, supreme in the world.
As this of mine is so, supremely true,
By this truth may there be great well-being."

The Verses of Well-Being are complete.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

This text closely parallels the Pāli Ratana Sutta, but it is translated as it stands here. For example, the verse on uninterrupted samādhi closes with "the precious samādhi" rather than the Dharma.

"Kinsman of the World" renders the Tibetan for lokabandhu, an epithet of the Buddha.

The expression rendered "no longer fall within reckoning" is somewhat obscure. It probably means that such persons pass beyond being counted among beings in saṃsāra.
