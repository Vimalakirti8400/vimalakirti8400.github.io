The Noble Dhāraṇī Called the Fourth Essence of Amṛtakuṇḍalin (Āryakuṇḍalyamṛtahṛdayacaturthanāmadhāraṇī)

The Noble Dhāraṇī Called the Fourth Essence of Amṛtakuṇḍalin, known in Sanskrit as the Āryakuṇḍalyamṛtahṛdayacaturthanāmadhāraṇī and in Tibetan as 'Phags pa bdud rtsi thab sbyor gyi snying po bzhi pa zhes bya ba'i gzungs, is a short dhāraṇī text centered on the wrathful deity Amṛtakuṇḍalin. After paying homage to the Three Jewels and to Caṇḍavajrapāṇi, great general of the yakṣas, it presents Amṛtakuṇḍalin surveying the assembly and telling the Blessed One that beings of this age have little merit. He describes them as lacking faith, disrespectful to their parents, and greedy, and as constantly beset by vināyakas, piśācas, and other harmful spirits. He then offers a protective incantation to destroy these obstructors and keep beings safe from harm. The work is Toh 957 in the Kangyur, where it belongs to the class of dhāraṇī texts, the collection of protective and empowering incantations preserved within the tantric portion of the canon.

In the language of Tibet: The Noble Dhāraṇī Called the Fourth Essence of Amṛtakuṇḍalin.

Homage to the Three Jewels. Homage to the great general of the yakṣas, Caṇḍavajrapāṇi.

Then the great wrathful one Amṛtakuṇḍalin looked upon the whole assembled retinue with the gaze of an elephant, and said this to the Blessed One: "Blessed One, the sentient beings here have meager merit. They do not abide in proper conduct, they are disrespectful to their fathers and mothers, they have little merit, they lack faith, they look for openings to do harm, and they are always greedy for material things. They are constantly in the grip of vināyakas, harmful spirits, and piśācas. In order to destroy those obstructors and vināyakas and bring them to their end, I wish to speak secret mantras."

Then the Blessed One said this to the Vajra Wrathful One: "Amṛtakuṇḍalin, that you have made this request for the benefit of all sentient beings is excellent, excellent! The Tathāgata grants you permission. For the sake of all sentient beings, speak the secret mantras!"

Then the wrathful Amṛtakuṇḍalin touched the feet of the Blessed One and of Vajrapāṇi, and spoke the secret mantras:

This is the fourth secret mantra of the wrathful Amṛtakuṇḍalin.

"Next, I wish to explain the meaningful essence of these. When one recites it, repeats it, and practices it, it will be as though one had recited all my secret mantras, repeated them well, and practiced them. This, then, is my essence:

oṃ amṛte hūṃ phaṭ"

This is the essence of Amṛtakuṇḍalin.

As soon as the Vajra Wrathful One Amṛtakuṇḍalin had spoken the secret mantras, this great earth quaked in six ways. The vināyakas, too, were terrified. Left without refuge and without support, they went before the Tathāgata and said, "Blessed One, please be my refuge! Sugata, please be our refuge!" and they bowed their heads down to the ground.

"Hey, hey, you vicious vināyakas, rise and listen to my words! By the vajra that blazes all around, blazes intensely, and blazes everywhere with intensity, your heads will be split apart. Remember how, by the secret mantras of those with tawny matted locks, for a thousand divine years I destroyed, cut down, beat, tormented, and dried up the vināyakas! Remember my power, my vigor, and my great might in vanquishing foes! Do not look for an opening against anyone who holds this secret mantra, recites it, practices it, and makes protection with it. Whether he eats sesame dregs or eats bad food, or even engages in sexual intercourse, do not look for an opening against one who practices all the vidyās!

"When eating food, one should consecrate the first mouthful with the mantra and offer it. If one recites it seven times at sunrise, it will always follow close behind him, and the vināyakas will not look for an opening. Whether it be sesame, lentils, rice, or pentse grain, oil cake, flatbread, or radish, even if one has eaten all of these, as long as one holds my secret mantra, all will be accomplished. Moreover, it should be known that its qualities are immeasurable."

The Noble Essence of Amṛtakuṇḍalin is complete.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The speaker of the closing address to the vināyakas is not named in the Tibetan. From context it is most likely Amṛtakuṇḍalin.

Several food terms in the final list are uncertain. "Sesame dregs" renders a phrase meaning roughly "sifted or pressed sesame." "Pentse" is an unidentified grain, left in phonetic form. "Flatbread" interprets a syllable string that may stand for Sanskrit parpaṭa, a thin cake. These are foods normally avoided by mantra practitioners, which is the point of the passage.

"Sexual intercourse" renders the idiom "village practice."
