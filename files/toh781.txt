The Dhāraṇī for Making the Displeased Pleased

The Dhāraṇī for Making the Displeased Pleased is a very short work of the dhāraṇī type, a brief ritual formula of the kind used for practical, worldly aims. Its Tibetan title lines open with homage to the Three Jewels and give the mantra, and its closing line names it as the dhāraṇī for making the displeased pleased; a Sanskrit title is not stated in the source lines, so none is given here. The text prescribes reciting the mantra over a pinch of dried ginger root, which, once placed in the mouth, is said to make whomever one looks upon become well disposed toward the practitioner. It is Toh 781 in the Kangyur, the Tibetan canon of translated buddhavacana, where it belongs to the Tantra section among the many short dhāraṇīs and protective or ritual formulas.

Homage to the Three Jewels.

Oṃ namaḥ śaparanase svāhā.

Empower a pinch of dried ginger root by reciting this mantra over it. If it is then placed in the mouth, whoever one looks upon will become well disposed toward one.

This concludes the dhāraṇī for making the displeased pleased.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

Two points of uncertainty. The mantra is transcribed exactly as it stands in the Tibetan; its syllables are probably corrupt or abbreviated and I have not emended them. The substance named as the object of the mantra (so rtsi) most likely means dried ginger root, or possibly a similar spice; the identification is a reasonable choice rather than a certain one. The final phrase can also be understood as "the dhāraṇī that causes the unhappy to become happy," and the sense given above, making the displeased pleased, follows the effect described in the body of the text.
