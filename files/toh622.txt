In the language of India: Ārya-sarvarogapraśamanī nāma dhāraṇī. In the language of Tibet: the noble dhāraṇī called "That Which Thoroughly Pacifies All Illness."

Homage to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas.

Thus did I hear. At one time the Blessed One was dwelling at Śrāvastī, in the Jeta Grove, the garden of Anāthapiṇḍada, in the park of Prince Jeta.

Then the Blessed One spoke to the monks: "Monks, take up this enumeration of Dharma, bear it in mind, retain it thoroughly, and teach it fully and correctly to others as well.

Tadyathā: cile mi, cile mi, pana kokili, śrīmati, kuṇḍali, dundubhi, indranīmukhe svāhā.

These are the dhāraṇī foundations for indigestion, bloating, the intermixture of blood and bile, affliction by hemorrhoids, bowel disease, disorders of the digestive juices, phlegm disorders, epidemic illness, headache, pain in the side, and seizure by hungry ghosts. Ānanda, I have not seen, in all the world with its gods, anyone who can counter these dhāraṇī foundations — save only one whose lifespan is exhausted. These dhāraṇī foundations were spoken by the Tathāgata, the arhat, the perfectly and completely awakened buddha. In this regard: the buddha is the highest of all sentient beings; freedom from attachment is the highest of all phenomena; and the Saṅgha is the highest of all assemblies. By these words of truth, by this speech of truth, may all that I have eaten, drunk, taken as food, and tasted be thoroughly and comfortably digested. Svāhā."

When the Blessed One had so declared, those monks rejoiced and praised the words of the Blessed One.

The noble dhāraṇī called "That Which Thoroughly Pacifies All Illness" is complete.
