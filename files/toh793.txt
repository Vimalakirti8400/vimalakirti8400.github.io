The Dhāraṇī of the King of Lunar Days, Constellations, and Moments

This short text, which the Tibetan opening line presents as homage to the Blessed One who is the king of lunar days, constellations, and moments, can be rendered in English as something like "The Dhāraṇī of Auspicious Days and Constellations"; I am not certain of its exact Sanskrit and Tibetan titles, since the source lines given here show only the opening homage rather than a formal title. It is a brief dhāraṇī text, consisting of a homage to the Three Jewels and to a Tathāgata associated with the calendar's lunar days, constellations, and moments, followed by a mantra and simple instructions for its use. The practice is to recite the mantra seven times before setting out in any chosen direction, so that all one's aims will be accomplished, which places it among the protective and auspicious-timing texts concerned with safe and successful travel. It is Toh 793 in the Kangyur, belonging to the Tantra section and, more specifically, to the class of dhāraṇī texts.

Homage to the Three Jewels.

Homage to the Blessed One, the Tathāgata, the arhat, the perfectly complete Buddha, the king of lunar days, of the constellations, and of the moments.

Tadyathā: nakṣatre, sarvatithīmūrte, nakṣatrani, mesamitadhani, bhavantu svāhā.

As for its procedure: whichever direction one wishes to travel in, one recites this seven times and then sets out, and all one's aims will be accomplished.

The dhāraṇī for the accomplishment of all aims is complete.
