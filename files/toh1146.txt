A Praise of the Three Jewels (Triratnastotra)

A Praise of the Three Jewels, known in Sanskrit as the Triratna Stotra and in Tibetan as Könchok Sum gyi Töpa, is a brief devotional hymn attributed in its closing line to the master Vasubandhu. In three short verses, it pays homage in turn to the Buddha, the Dharma, and the Sangha. The Buddha is honored through his three bodies: the dharmakāya beyond all reference, the sambhogakāya of perfect enjoyment, and the nirmāṇakāya that emanates in whatever form is needed. The Dharma is praised as the utterly pure sphere of reality, the noble eightfold path, and the scriptures that teach it, and the Sangha as the bodhisattvas, arhats, and pratyekabuddhas. The work is Toh 1146 in the Tengyur, where it belongs to the collection of praises, or stotras, that make up the opening section of the canonical commentaries.

In the language of India: triratna stotraṃ. In the language of Tibet: A Praise of the Three Jewels.

Homage to the Three Jewels.

To the dharmakāya, beyond all reference,
To the sambhogakāya, the body of perfect enjoyment,
And to the nirmāṇakāya, emanating in whatever form,
I bow down with reverence.

To the utterly pure sphere of reality (dharmadhātu),
To the noble eightfold path of practice,
And to the scriptures that teach it,
I bow down with reverence.

To the bodhisattvas,
To the arhats and the rest,
And to the pratyekabuddhas,
I bow down with reverence.

This completes A Praise of the Three Jewels, composed by the master Vasubandhu.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The three verses praise the Buddha through his three bodies, the Dharma through the dharmadhātu (the truth of cessation), the path, and the scriptures, and the Saṅgha through bodhisattvas, arhats and pratyekabuddhas. "Bodhisattvas" renders the Tibetan "awakening-minds," a metrical abbreviation for bodhisattva. "Beyond all reference" renders "without object of reference" (anālambana), describing the dharmakāya as beyond conceptual apprehension.
