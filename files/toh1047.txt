Avalokiteśvara Dhāraṇī for the Sweet Flag Root Rite

This short work, known in English as The Dhāraṇī for Mastering Three Thousand Ślokas, is a brief ritual and incantatory text, a dhāraṇī, dedicated to the noble bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara, the great being endowed with great compassion. After homages to the Three Jewels and to Avalokiteśvara, it gives a short mantra and instructs the practitioner to make a maṇḍala before the bodhisattva, recite the mantra one hundred and eight times over a thumb-sized piece of sweet flag root, and then eat it. The promised result is a rapidly expanding power of memory: a thousand verses mastered on the first day, three thousand after three days, and after seven days the ability to retain whatever one hears. Its Sanskrit and Tibetan titles are not clearly given in the opening lines, though the closing line names it after this power of mastering three thousand ślokas. The text is Toh 1047 in the Kangyur, where it belongs to the collection of dhāraṇī texts, short incantations prized for their practical and protective benefits.

Homage to the Three Jewels!
Homage to the bodhisattva, the great being endowed with great compassion, the noble Avalokiteśvara!

tadyathā oṃ muni kante muni prabhasvare svāhā

Make a maṇḍala before the Blessed One, the noble Avalokiteśvara. Take a piece of sweet flag root about the size of a thumb and recite the mantra over it one hundred and eight times. If one then eats it, on the first day one will master a thousand ślokas of text merely by reading them. On the day when three days have passed, one will master three thousand ślokas. On the day when seven days have passed, one will retain whatever one hears.

The dhāraṇī for mastering three thousand ślokas is complete.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

"Sweet flag root" renders ཤུ་དག, the aromatic calamus root (Skt. vacā), traditionally used in India to aid memory and eloquence. A śloka is a verse unit of thirty-two syllables, also used as a measure of text length.
