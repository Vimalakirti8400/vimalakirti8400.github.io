The Dhāraṇī Called 'The Noble Goddess Cuṇḍā' (Āryā Cuṇḍā Devī Nāma Dhāraṇī)

The Dhāraṇī Called "The Noble Goddess Cuṇḍā," known in Sanskrit as the Āryā Cuṇḍā Devī Nāma Dhāraṇī and in Tibetan as Phakpa Lhamo Kulgyedma Zhejawa'i Zung, is a short dhāraṇī text devoted to the bodhisattva goddess Cuṇḍā, a multi-armed emanation associated with purification, protection, and the swift removal of obstacles. The text consists primarily of homages and invocations addressed to Cuṇḍā, who is praised as a powerful wrathful deity wielding weapons such as a sword, axe, iron hook, and lasso, and who is extolled by all Tathāgatas for her immeasurable might and miraculous ability. It is catalogued as Toh 613 in the Kangyur, where it belongs to the dhāraṇī section of the canon, a class of texts that preserve sacred ritual formulas regarded as embodiments of enlightened speech and sources of spiritual protection and blessing.

In the language of India: Āryā Cuṇḍā Devī Nāma Dhāraṇī. In the Tibetan language: The Dhāraṇī Called "The Noble Goddess Cuṇḍā."

Homage to the Three Jewels.

Homage to the Blessed One, the Tathāgata, the arhat, the perfectly and fully awakened buddha Vairocana.

Homage to you, O Blessed One, Noble Goddess Cuṇḍā, endowed with immeasurable might and power, accomplished in miraculous ability and the subduing of others, praised and extolled by all Tathāgatas. Make me virtuous in every way and in all things.

Namas saptānāṃ. Samyaksambuddhakoṭīnāṃ. Tadyathā. Oṃ cale cule. Cuṇḍe svāhā.

O you of great diligence, of unobstructed discipline, of great power, of great ability to subdue others — holding in your hands a sword, an axe, an iron hook, and a lasso — O great wrathful one, sovereign lady of the great wrathful ones, one who possesses a fierce form, one whose face is boundless, one who commands a thousand hands, unconquered, not overcome by others, one who accomplishes all aims, difficult to tame, one of a thousand eyes, blessed by all Tathāgatas, thoroughly established as the one worshipped and reverenced by all gods — O you who possess vajra fingernails, who are led by the vajra and who lead with the vajra, who bear vajra weapons, whose body is a vajra, whose fist is a vajra, who stand in the posture with vajra eyes directed outward — although you are one of fierce majesty, you display the appearance of a fierce and majestic form. Your body is adorned with ornaments of vajra and lapis lazuli.

Oṃ bhagavati. Cuṇḍe hūṃ hūṃ. Krūṃ krūṃ. Mrūṃ mrūṃ. Srūṃ srūṃ. Rūṃ rūṃ. Āveśaya. Gṛhṇa gṛhṇa. Gṛhṇāpaya. Gṛhṇāpaya. Hara hara. Bhañja bhañja. Sara sara. Mara mara. Mārayā mārayā. Paca paca. Daha daha. Gṛhṇa gṛhṇa.

All hostile spirits, fevers of one day, fevers of two days, fevers of three days, fevers of four days, fevers of seven days, chronic fevers, intermittent fevers, rising epidemic diseases, risen corpses (vetālas), hungry ghosts (pretas), flesh-eaters, yakṣas, rākṣasas, spirits of the kumbhāṇḍa class, those born from the womb, those born from action, wandering ones, dwelling ones — all those hostile and harmful ones who do me injury and harm:

Saṃdhaya saṃdhaya. Mardaya mardaya. Śoṣaya śoṣaya. Tāpaya tāpaya. Utsādhaya utsādhaya. Hana hana vajreṇa. Sara sara daṇḍena. Mārayā mārayā. Khakkena. Hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ. Cūṃ cūṃ cūṃ. Drūṃ drūṃ drūṃ.

Oṃ cale cule cuṇḍe sarva artha sādhaya svāhā.

By the might and blessing of the Blessed One, the Noble Goddess Cuṇḍā, may I be made virtuous and freed from all harm. Svāhā.

The Dhāraṇī Called "The Noble Goddess Cuṇḍā" is complete.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The epithet "Cuṇḍā" is a tantric goddess associated with the Cundī dhāraṇī cycle, distinct from but related to Tārā. Her name is rendered phonetically as it is a proper divine name with no standard English equivalent.

The phrase rendered "led by the vajra and who lead with the vajra" translates a condensed epithet that could also mean "drawn forward by the vajra" in the sense of being propelled by vajra energy; the dual sense is intentional in the original.

The enumeration of harmful beings includes "gṛhṇa gṛhṇa" appearing twice in the mantra body at different points; this is faithful to the text and is a standard repetition in dhāraṇī literature.
