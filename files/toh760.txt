Then Brahmā, Viṣṇu, the Great God (Śiva), and the Four Great Kings circumambulated the Blessed One three times. Having paid homage, they addressed the Blessed One with these words: "Blessed One, we wish to recite the dhāraṇī called Vajra Iron-Beak — that which, when we all have assembled, tears out the hearts of all nāgas, withers their hearts, destroys the lineages of hostile nāgas, repels all storms of wind and hail, and causes flowers, fruits, and all crops to flourish. Then the Blessed One spoke to Brahmā, Viṣṇu, Śiva, and the Four Great Kings: "Sons of good family, speak it, for I shall bless it by the truth-power of all noble ones and for the benefit of all sentient beings." The moment all hostile nāgas hear this dhāraṇī mantra called Blazing Vajra Iron-Beak, their bodies rot and emit a foul odor. Their bodies break apart and scatter. They then bow down at the feet of the Blessed One and speak these words: "Blessed One, please protect us. We have been reviled by all gods and the inhabitants of the world. We have acted wrongfully."

"Blessed One, from this moment on we will not harm sentient beings in Jambudvīpa. We will not destroy them. We will not even cast a sideways glance at those who are small children — how much less will we seize upon any opportunity to harm them." The nāga king of the ocean, the nāga king who is like an umbrella, the nāga king who is greatly powerful, the greatly resplendent one, the untamed one, and all the rest — nāgas numbering in the hundreds of millions of tens of millions — all touched their heads to the feet of the Blessed One in homage, and every single nāga swore: "In the time to come we will not harm any sentient being in Jambudvīpa. We will protect all blossoms, fruits, crops, leaves, and branches. We will ward off all winds and hail. We will bring rain at the proper seasons. This we vow in the words of our own oath, and we will uphold this commitment in accordance with the pledge of the Tathāgata. We will fulfil the hopes of all sentient beings and satisfy every wish they hold in mind. We will cause no harm. Blessed One, if we act contrary to these particular excellent qualities, may we not attain complete and perfect awakening. The words of our oath are as follows:

tadyathā, oṃ tagi cagi, ghuli ghuli, muli śa śa, gugu, śiti biramati, huyu huyu svāhā.

When there is excessive rain, or when rain fails to fall, or when rain falls out of season, or when there is wind, clouds, hail, cold wind, or falling meteoric iron, let the holder of awareness recite this oath-mantra either on a rooftop, on elevated ground, or on a mountaintop, facing east, seven times aloud. Let him recite it seven times over white mustard seeds and scatter them upward: the moment they are scattered, all nāgas will be cut to pieces, and through diligent application rain will swiftly be brought down. By reciting this very dhāraṇī twenty-one times over a bell, and then reciting it twenty-one times over white mustard seeds, and performing a fierce and forceful torma offering and casting it in the four directions, the boundary will be sealed. By reciting the Blazing Vajra Iron-Beak mantra eight times, the nāgas are summoned. By a single-minded recollection of it, winds and hail are repelled, and even falling meteoric iron is turned to water. If the vajra is waved, hail will be diverted in whichever direction it is waved. Sitting quietly and at ease in a secluded place within the house, reciting this mantra thirty-two times will cause winds, clouds, and hail to be blocked, and nāgas will cause no harm and will be unable to do so thereafter. Reciting it over white mustard seeds and casting them in the four directions will seal the boundary for a full year: winds, clouds, and hail will not stir. They will become obedient, entirely protective, and will do their work. Reciting the mantra twenty-one times and then ringing the bell will cause skulls to split and fall to the ground, and whatever causes harm will be dispersed. Properly performing the offering with praise, reading aloud in a clear voice, and casting white mustard seeds in the four directions, above and below, will bind all hail, wind, and harm caused by nāgas. Bad omens, non-virtue, inauspiciousness, planetary spirits, elementals, flesh-eaters, yakṣas, and rākṣasas will all be driven away. The boundary will be sealed for a distance of one league in every direction all around. All illness, harmful intentions, and hostile enemy obstructors will be repelled. Crops, fruits, grains, and sap will increase, ripen, and grow large. All elementals and every form of harm will be rendered powerless.

The dhāraṇī called Blazing Vajra Iron-Beak is complete.

The mantra syllables are transliterated from Tibetan phonetic transcription of Sanskrit. The Sanskrit underlying these syllables is approximate at several points, as Tibetan phonetic rendering of Sanskrit mantras frequently obscures the original forms. The rendering here follows the Tibetan syllables as closely as possible without conjectural reconstruction. The phrase translated as "tears out the hearts" and "withers their hearts" uses a Tibetan idiom for completely overwhelming or disabling the nāgas — the imagery is of seizing the vital essence.  The phrase rendered "nāga king who is like an umbrella" translates a Tibetan epithet literally meaning "umbrella-like nāga king," which may correspond to a Sanskrit name such as Chattra or similar; no standard identification is certain. The term "meteoric iron" renders the Tibetan gnam lcags, literally "sky-iron," conventionally understood as iron of meteoritic or celestial origin, here appearing in a context of destructive weather phenomena. The torma offering, a ritual cake used in Tibetan Buddhist practice, is implied by the term rendered simply as "offering" in some contexts; where the Tibetan explicitly says "torma offering" it has been retained implicitly in the ritual instructions.
