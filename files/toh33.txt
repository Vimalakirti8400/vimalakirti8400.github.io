The Sūtra of the Palace of Āṭānāṭā (Āṭānāṭiya Sūtra)

The Āṭānāṭiya Sūtra, known in Tibetan as the Sūtra of the Palace of Āṭānāṭā (Lcang lo can gyi pho brang gi mdo), is a protective discourse in which the four great kings — the guardian deities of the cardinal directions — visit the Buddha on Vulture Peak Mountain and recite a powerful dhāraṇī intended to ward off malevolent spirits and safeguard Buddhist practitioners. The text belongs to the genre of rakṣā or protection literature, drawing on the cosmology of yakṣas, gandharvas, kumbhāṇḍas, and nāgas who serve under the four kings and whose names are invoked throughout the sūtra. It is one of the most widely used ritual protection texts in Buddhist traditions across Asia, recited to this day as a safeguard against harm. This sūtra is Toh 33 in the Kangyur, where it is classified among the general sūtra literature.

Thus may it be. In the language of India: Āṭānāṭiya Sūtra. In the Tibetan language: The Sūtra of the Palace of Āṭānāṭā. With reverence I bow to the noble Three Jewels.

Thus did I hear. At one time the Blessed One was dwelling at Rājagṛha on Vulture Peak Mountain. Then the four great kings were abiding there, guarding the four directions together with many troops of yakṣas, many troops of gandharvas, many troops of kumbhāṇḍas, and many troops of nāgas. They abode in groups in each of the four directions. They abode protecting the four directions. Coming in the night, they illuminated only Vulture Peak Mountain with their multicolored radiance, and then went to where the Blessed One was. Having paid homage to the Blessed One, they took their places to one side. Among those yakṣas as well, those who paid homage to the Blessed One took their places to one side. Those who presented their respects to the Blessed One and then inquired of him, as is fitting, about matters of substance and matters without substance — those too took their places to one side. Those who paid homage to the Blessed One with palms joined took their places to one side. Those who exchanged names and examined their lineages took their places to one side. Those who said nothing at all took their places to one side.

Having taken their places to one side, the great king Vaiśravaṇa then spoke to the Blessed One as follows: Venerable One, there are certain wicked yakṣas who have no faith in the Blessed One, and there are certain wicked yakṣas who have come to have faith in the Blessed One. There are certain middling yakṣas who have no faith in the venerable Blessed One, and there are certain middling yakṣas who have come to have faith in the venerable Blessed One. There are certain lesser yakṣas who have no faith in the venerable Blessed One, and there are certain lesser yakṣas who have come to have faith in the venerable Blessed One. What, venerable Blessed One, is the reason why certain yakṣas have no faith in the venerable Blessed One? The venerable Blessed One has taught the Dharma of abandoning the taking of life, the Dharma of abandoning taking what is not given, the Dharma of abandoning sexual misconduct, the Dharma of abandoning false speech, and the Dharma of abandoning the drinking of intoxicating liquor. Now those yakṣas who take no delight in abandoning the taking of life, who take no delight in abandoning taking what is not given, who take no delight in abandoning sexual misconduct, who take no delight in abandoning false speech, and who take no delight in abandoning the drinking of intoxicating liquor — those beings have become displeased and unwilling. Furthermore, the venerable Blessed One has had all the śrāvakas who exist enter the path of remote forests and wilderness, abiding in lying down and sitting in dependence upon solitude. They dwell in seclusion, free from noise and clamor, undisturbed by gatherings. Individual renunciants (śramaṇas) dwell in harmony with one another. And in such places the abodes of the wicked yakṣas are found. Those who have no faith in the word of the Blessed One — in order that they may come to have faith, I request that the venerable Blessed One receive instruction in this matter: that the Palace of Āṭānāṭā be requested to provide protection, so that monks and nuns, male and female lay practitioners may be guarded, sheltered, kept free from harm, at ease, and well established. The Blessed One gave his consent by remaining silent.

Then the great king Vaiśravaṇa, knowing that the Blessed One had given his consent, at that time spoke thus: Let there be protection by the Palace of Āṭānāṭā!

Let the great sages, together with the willow palace, guard this place. May monks, nuns, laymen, and laywomen be protected, concealed from harm, free from injury, and dwell in comfort and well-being.

Whatever great sages are accompanied by monks, or accompanied by nuns, or accompanied by laymen, or accompanied by laywomen — let all of them bear in mind that this willow palace guards and fully protects them here.

The non-humans who are its retinue are as follows: male yakṣas, female yakṣas, yakṣa sons, yakṣa daughters, great yakṣa ministers, yakṣa attendants, and yakṣa servants; male gandharvas (dri za), female gandharvas, gandharva sons, gandharva daughters, great gandharva ministers, gandharva attendants, and gandharva servants; male kumbhāṇḍas (grulbum), female kumbhāṇḍas, kumbhāṇḍa sons, kumbhāṇḍa daughters, great kumbhāṇḍa ministers, kumbhāṇḍa attendants, and kumbhāṇḍa servants; male nāgas, female nāgas, nāga sons, nāga daughters, great nāga ministers, nāga attendants, and nāga servants — those who approach with a mind of intense hostility, who follow along, who dwell together, who abide together, who rest together: these great sages are to be known as non-humans.

In villages and towns, those who show reverence or render honor — the great sage non-humans do not obtain access to such places. The royal palace called the Willow Palace has no ground or abiding place for them. The great sage non-humans and the yakṣas also do not obtain the right to dwell there together.

Furthermore, great sages, one must heed the commands of the non-humans. Furthermore, great sages, when non-humans bring complete benefit to oneself and then speak ill of others and engage in quarreling, and furthermore when non-humans place a blazing vessel upon someone's head, and furthermore when non-humans split someone's head into seven pieces — those great sage non-humans are fierce, violent, and wrathful.

They report in this manner to the great king. If they are unable to report to the great king, they report to his officers. If they are unable to report to the king's officers, they report to those officers' subordinates. Furthermore, great sages, those non-humans speak in opposition to the great king. Great sages, it is just as when a thief arises in the kingdom of Magadha: one reports it immediately to the king. If unable to report to the king, one reports to the king's officers. If unable to report to the officers, one reports to their subordinates — it is exactly like that.

Great sages, they also report to the king of Magadha that a thief has caused harm.

Great sages, such non-human beings as these are fierce, violent, and wrathful. They report at once to the great kings. If unable to report to the great kings, they report to the king's officers. If unable to report to the officers, they report to those officers' subordinates. Great sages, the non-humans report in opposition to the great kings in this way.

Great sages, whatever non-human being — whether a male yakṣa or a female yakṣa, a yakṣa's son or a yakṣa's daughter, a yakṣa's great minister, a yakṣa's retinue, or a yakṣa's attendant retinue; or a male gandharva or a female gandharva, a gandharva's son or a gandharva's daughter, a gandharva's great minister, a gandharva's retinue, or a gandharva's attendant retinue; or a male kumbhāṇḍa or a female kumbhāṇḍa, a kumbhāṇḍa's son or a kumbhāṇḍa's daughter, a kumbhāṇḍa's great minister, a kumbhāṇḍa's retinue, or a kumbhāṇḍa's attendant retinue; or a male nāga or a female nāga, a nāga's son or a nāga's daughter, a nāga's great minister, a nāga's retinue, or a nāga's attendant retinue — with a mind of intense hatred pursues monks or nuns, male lay practitioners or female lay practitioners — following after them, or following along and dwelling with them, staying near them, resting alongside them — these beings petition the yakṣas and great yakṣas, the commanders and great commanders, and raise their voices and cry out loudly, saying: "Here in this place, seized by a yakṣa! This yakṣa takes life! This yakṣa inflicts harm! This yakṣa causes confusion! This yakṣa injures! This yakṣa causes grave injury! This yakṣa will not release them!"

Great sages, by this dhāraṇī let the palace of Jālavana be protected! Let monks and nuns, male lay practitioners and female lay practitioners be guarded, sheltered, kept free from harm, happy, and dwelling in peace.

Great sages, we must now depart.

We have many duties and many tasks to attend to.

The Blessed One declared: Great Kings, you may now depart.

Then the four Great Kings rose, paid homage to the Blessed One, circumambulated him, and vanished from that place. Those yakṣas likewise rose, and those among them who paid homage to the Blessed One and circumambulated him vanished from that very place. Those who had greeted the Blessed One with a respectful salutation and then asked about what is essential and what is not essential vanished from that very place. Those who had joined their palms together toward the Blessed One's presence vanished from that very place. Those who had examined names and lineages vanished from that very place. And those who had remained completely silent vanished from that very place.

This is the first supreme chapter of the Sūtra of the Palace of Jālavana.

Then, when that night had passed, the Blessed One summoned the monks and said: Monks, during this night the four Great Kings, the many great yakṣa commanders, the many hosts of gandharvas, the many hosts of kumbhāṇḍas, the many hosts of nāgas, guarded and dwelt in the four directions. They stayed in groups throughout the four directions. They protected and remained in the four directions. As the night drew on, beings of many colors illuminated Vulture Peak Mountain alone with their radiance, and came to where the Blessed One was; and having come, they paid homage to me and took their place to one side.

Monks, of those yakṣas, some paid homage to me and took their place to one side. Some greeted me with a respectful salutation and then inquired about what is essential and what is not essential, and took their place to one side. Some joined their palms together toward me and took their place to one side. Some examined names and lineages and took their place to one side. And some remained completely silent and took their place to one side.

When all had taken their place to one side, monks, the Great King Vaiśravaṇa addressed me with these words: Reverend Blessed One, there are disreputable yakṣas who have no faith in the Blessed One. There are also disreputable yakṣas who do have faith in the Blessed One. There are middling yakṣas who have no faith in the reverend Blessed One. There are middling yakṣas who have faith in the reverend Blessed One. There are inferior yakṣas who have no faith in the reverend Blessed One. There are inferior yakṣas who have faith in the reverend Blessed One.

What is the reason that certain yakṣas have no faith in the reverend Blessed One? It is because the reverend Blessed One has taught the Dharma of abandoning the taking of life, taught the Dharma of abandoning taking what is not given, taught the Dharma of abandoning sexual misconduct, taught the Dharma of abandoning false speech, and taught the Dharma of abandoning the drinking of intoxicants that lead to heedlessness. Those yakṣas who dislike the reverend Teacher's teachings on abandoning the taking of life, who dislike abandoning taking what is not given, who dislike abandoning sexual misconduct, who dislike abandoning false speech, and who dislike abandoning the drinking of intoxicants that lead to heedlessness — these are the ones who are displeased and unwilling.

Reverend Blessed One, all the śrāvakas that there are have entered into the paths of forest and wilderness retreats, lying down and sitting there. They abide in solitude, free from sound and noise, without the harm of company. The renunciants dwell in harmony among themselves.

There dwell in that place the abodes of malevolent yakṣas. Whosoever has lost faith in the Blessed One's word — in order to bring them to faith, Reverend Blessed One, let this be a training for you: I beg that the palace of Virūḍhaka protect them. Monks, nuns, laymen, and laywomen abide there under protection, in concealment, free from harm, in ease and in good order. As for the monks, I grant this permission without speaking a word of it to them.
