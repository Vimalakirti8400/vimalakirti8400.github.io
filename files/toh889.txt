The Incantation for Remembering Past Births

The Heart of Sukhāvatī, known in Tibetan as Dewachen gyi Nyingpo and likely corresponding to a Sanskrit title such as Sukhāvatīhṛdaya, is a brief incantation text, or dhāraṇī, centered on the noble bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara. It opens with homage to the Buddha, the Dharma, the Saṅgha, and Avalokiteśvara, then presents a short mantra that the reciter asks to be accomplished on their behalf. It promises two benefits to anyone who recites it: they will remember their births for a hundred thousand lifetimes, and after death they will be reborn in Sukhāvatī, the pure land of bliss. The work is Toh 889 in the Kangyur, where it belongs among the dhāraṇī texts, the short spells and incantations preserved alongside the tantric scriptures.

Homage to the Buddha. Homage to the Dharma. Homage to the Saṅgha. Homage to the noble Avalokiteśvara, the bodhisattva, the great being. Having paid homage to them, I take up this incantation: may this incantation be accomplished for me.

tadyathā hana hana svāni navasāni jvala jvalane cala calane purbo yogamanusmara phaṭ mahāhataya svāhā

Whoever recites this incantation will remember their births for a hundred thousand lifetimes. And after dying and passing on from here, they will be born in the world of Sukhāvatī.

This completes the Heart of Sukhāvatī.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The Tibetan here is bde ldan, "possessing bliss," rather than the more usual bde ba can. It is taken as a rendering of Sukhāvatī, the pure land of Amitābha. The phrase rig pa 'di 'dzin gyis is read as the reciter's declaration, "I take up this incantation," which leads into the wish that follows.
