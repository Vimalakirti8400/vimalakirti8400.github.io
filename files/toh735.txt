For black plague, one makes a string, recites over it one hundred and eight times, ties one hundred and eight knots, and, using a vessel on which the heart-essence of the mantra has been written, performs the recitation properly. Afterward, one washes the sick person, or a horse, ox, or other animal, with fragrant water, washes the finest of the herd as well, and ties this around its head — and it will be pacified.

Likewise, for harm that has come to water buffalo, sheep, and goats, one should write it on a slate-board. One fills a vase with clean water, recites the mantra one hundred and eight times over it, and bathes the animal — and it will be pacified.

In the early morning one goes to the deity of the livestock pen, makes an offering of incense, flowers, and a torma cake, fills a vase with water that has not been used for any worldly purpose, recites the mantra one hundred and eight times, and, at the gate of the livestock deity's pen, erects a door-frame. On that door-frame one writes the vidyā mantra in saffron ink on the leaf of an alder tree and affixes it to the top of the door-frame. For human disease and livestock disease, one writes the mantra on birch bark, makes an offering, and affixes it to the top of a victory banner.
