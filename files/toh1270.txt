The Maṇḍala Rite Called The Emergence of Heruka (Herukopodayamaṇḍalavidhi)

The Maṇḍala Rite Called The Emergence of Heruka, whose Sanskrit title appears to be Herukābhyudaya Maṇḍalavidhi and whose Tibetan title is He ru ka nye bar 'byung ba zhes bya ba'i dkyil 'khor gyi cho ga, is a concise ritual manual for constructing and working with the maṇḍala of the tantric deity Heruka. Opening with homage to Vajraḍāka and Hevajra and praising Heruka as the Innate One, the author explains that empowerment, consecration, recitation and accomplishment cannot succeed without the great maṇḍala, and so composes this rite to complete all ritual activity, to realize reality, and above all to ripen practitioners for the path. The work goes on to cover the maṇḍala itself together with meditative absorption, mantra and mudrā, the ritual seat, recitation, fire offering, worship and related practices. It is Toh 1270 in the Tengyur, where it belongs among the commentarial and ritual treatises of the tantra section, closely connected with the Hevajra cycle of practice.

In the language of India: herukopa ududaya maṇḍal bidhi
In the language of Tibet: The Maṇḍala Rite Called The Emergence of Heruka.

Homage to glorious Vajraḍāka.
Homage to glorious Hevajra.

Heruka, glorious one, endowed with the very knowledge of the omniscient,
to that Innate One I bow with the crown of my head.
Free from every fault, endowed with the nature of compassion:
to realize reality, I explain the maṇḍala rite in brief.

Empowerment, consecration, recitation, accomplishment and the rest:
without the great maṇḍala, none of these will succeed.
Therefore, to complete all rites, to realize reality,
and above all to ripen for the path, I compose this maṇḍala rite.

Maṇḍala, meditative absorption, mantra and mudrā,
the seat, recitation, the rite of fire offering,
worship, the application of rites, and withdrawal:
these should be known as the ten principles.

The master is endowed with the ten principles
and knows how to apply mantra and tantra.
He is complete in all the characteristics
and has understood the meaning of all the treatises.
He has received all the empowerments in full
and has gained a vision of the deity of reality.
He is authorized by a qualified guru,
holds the pledges and vows, and is utterly steadfast.
He knows the reality of the three vehicles
and is unstained by worldly concerns.
Compassionate by nature, he is full of loving care.
He has carried his recitation through to completion,
and abides constantly in faith and love.
He is complete in all the rites
and diligent in drawing the maṇḍala.
He wishes to attain supreme merit
and rejoices in reality.

If one who lacks these undertakes a maṇḍala,
deficient in the nature of the rite,
and does it with a conceited mind,
he will meet great suffering in this life
and great terror in the next.
Therefore, when one complete in the characteristics,
who has fully understood the meanings of the tantra,
undertakes the great maṇḍala
without impairing any of the rites,
he attains excellent merit
in this life and in all lives to come.
To attain incomparable merit,
one should strive in worshipping the maṇḍala.

Here, first of all, in order for the disciple to be granted it, supplication should be made three times, preceded by the maṇḍala offering and so on. Whether it is a maṇḍala for one's own accomplishment or one sought for the purpose of conferring it on others, one should enter upon the approach. Then, in a special, secluded place where people do not gather, a place pleasing to the mind, one should dwell there through the power of knowing the reality of the place, and become endowed with all the characteristics of the yoga of Hevajra. Having done a hundred thousand recitations of the mantra of the chosen deity or of the Cakravartin, and ten thousand recitations of each of the mantras of the retinue as well, or else having obtained the deity's permission and received a prophecy from the ḍākinīs, one should then set about undertaking the maṇḍala. These are the stages of approach.

At a lone tree, a fearsome place, a riverbank,
a charnel ground, a solitary forest,
the shore of the ocean, or a battlefield:
practicing there, accomplishment is said to be near.
In lakes and in the middle of cities,
places where people gather, cattle pens,
temples that have not been consecrated,
and ground that lacks the proper marks:
whoever undertakes a maṇḍala there,
all his accomplishments,
say those who know reality, are far away.

This is the stage of examining the site.

At an auspicious time and lunar mansion,
properly, in the morning,
the chief who knows reality
should examine the ground with care.
Shun the bra tree, turmeric, the rno tree and indigo,
black mustard, the skur tree, white mustard and kovidāra,
rough ground, mountain hollows, salty soil and thorny places;
ground shaped like a tortoise's back, avoid completely.
Where in the east there is a mapala tree,
where in the south there is a palāśa tree,
where in the west a nyagrodha tree stands,
where in the north a bakula tree has grown:
all such places should be avoided.
A site in the southeast with a kakubha tree,
in the southwest with a palāśa tree, should be avoided.
Likewise a palabhali tree in the northwest,
and an udumbara tree in the northeast: avoid them.
Aśoka that is fruitless or dry, ground of withered trees,
the tamāla tree, thorns and poisonous trees: avoid them.
The pine and all other harmful trees: avoid them completely.
Ground that is uneven, black, or angular, where water plants grow,
waterlogged, full of rubbish, with ditches, pits and furrows: avoid it.
Dig a neat square pit and fill it with that same earth.
If it is lower than before, examine it well and abandon it.
If it fills level or stands higher, it is declared good ground.

That is the examination of the ground.

Whoever undertakes a maṇḍala
on ground with the bra tree and the like,
quarrels, grief and loss of wealth,
and torment for the patron will arise, without doubt.
If it is low or like a tortoise's back,
death or grave illness will come about.
Therefore, on ground of that kind
the wise do not undertake a maṇḍala.
On ground that has aśoka and the like,
white and soft,
where water flows to the northeast, turning to the right,
there one attains unsurpassed accomplishment.
If the dug pit is not filled by its earth,
the intended purpose will fail.
If the dug pit is filled by its earth with some to spare,
the purpose one has in mind will be accomplished.

This is the examination of the characteristics of the ground.

Then the mantrin sits on a high and comfortable seat in the center of the well-examined ground. Having bathed and anointed himself with fragrant scents, he adorns himself well with garments and the six mudrās. Having gathered all the requisites, he continually recites the protection mantra. The mantrin, in meditative equipoise, holds the vajra and bell, and first makes offerings and torma. In the evening he completes the three meditative absorptions, expels the obstructors through the wrathful fire offering as explained, plants the kīla daggers, and meditates on the vajra fence, the tent and so forth. Then, to protect the disciples, he should perform the peaceful fire offering. Then he blesses a vast torma according to the oral instructions, and with a great sound gives torma to each of the world protectors of all the directions. Then the supplication for blessing should be made.

The next morning, the mantrin, endowed with yoga, consecrates the all-purpose water with the all-purpose mantra and sprinkles it over the ground to purify it. With his own light rays he summons the earth goddess Pṛthivī as described, and he worships her fully, as she stands in the space before him, with offerings and so forth. He makes supplication with the words "Goddess, you are the witness," and so on, and then with the mantra oṃ vajrottiṣṭha she should be placed in the sky.

The karma vajra, the ritual assistant, should dig.
Dig to a depth of about a knee's measure.
First examine the mahoraga thoroughly,
then examine the faults carefully.
During the three months of winter,
beginning as the sun rises,
its head faces toward the south,
and it lies upon its left side.
During the three months of spring,
from the south toward the west where the sun goes,
its upper body, a human form, faces west;
its lower body, like a snake, lies to the east.
During the three months of summer,
its face looks north, and it turns to the west.
During the three months of autumn,
its head is toward the east, and it faces east.
Knowing its belly in this way, one should dig.
Through this the accomplishments people desire
will be attained, without any doubt.
If one digs at its head,
father and mother, dear relatives,
sons and grandsons, together with one's household,
will always be ruined, without any doubt.
If one digs at its back,
one will lose one's land, or else die.
If one digs at its tail,
cows, buffaloes and other
livestock will quickly be lost.
Therefore, examine the mahoraga thoroughly,
circumambulate three times,
and dig on the side of its belly.
The mahoraga, beginning from the east,
according to the times that have been taught,
dwells circling through all directions and intermediate directions.
From within the earth that has been dug,
chaff, stones and gravel,
charcoal, pieces of corpses and the like,
all these should be thoroughly removed.
If they turn up and are not thoroughly removed,
a fault will certainly arise from them.
Through the fault of bones not removed, the body will perish.
Through the fault of charcoal not removed, one is struck by great ruin.
Through ashes wealth is lost; through human bones one will die.
Faults such as snakes should be thoroughly eliminated by mantra;
through the fault of not eliminating them, the punishment of the ḍākinīs will come.
Stones and dust, sand, fingernails, hair, potsherds and clods,
wood and bricks, filth, husks, thorns, stone fragments and the like:
through the fault of not removing them, beating, blows, quarrels and harm arise on every side.
Therefore examine well even the most subtle faults, and eliminate them completely.
If, having dug to the measure of the knee,
the examination of the earth has not turned out well,
one should dig to the measure of the waist.
If gravel and the like are abundant
and one is unable to remove them,
make the place auspicious with water and white mustard seed.
Holding water and white mustard seed in the hand,
scatter them so as to cover the whole ground,
and fiercely recite the mantra, together with light:

oṃ hana hana krodha hūṃ phaṭ

Then the water and earth, blessed with hūṃ, are consecrated many times with the all-purpose mantra and sprinkled so as to cover the ground. The five precious substances and the five medicines are blessed with the seed syllables of the Victors and placed in the heart of the maṇḍala, and then the ground should be pounded very evenly and well. It should not be done by anyone other than oneself or the karma vajra.

Inside a royal palace,
on uneven ground,
in elephant stables and cattle pens,
inside temples, on great roads,
and on the banks of rivers and lakes:
in such places one should not dig.
In order to purify the ground completely,
place the hand upon it and recite the secret mantra.
By purifying the site completely,
the obscurations of the mind will be removed.

oṃ rakṣa rakṣa hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ svāhā

There the maṇḍala is square, adorned with four doors and four porticoes, and pervades the whole of space. It is decorated with a canopy, yak-tail whisks, banners, bells and small bells, hanging silk streamers and hanging garlands of flowers, and adorned with hanging garlands of fragrant flowers and cloths of many colors. On such a site, purified in the five ways, smear the five nectars and fragrant substances, place bowls filled with incense at the corners and in the directions, and there perform the fire offering and so forth. Then summon the earth goddess Pṛthivī and visualize her as the very one who blesses that site. This is the stage of purifying the outer ground.

Then the vajra master sits on a comfortable seat and, in meditative equipoise, on the well-purified ground, with offerings and torma going first, generates the maṇḍala of the special deity and expels the obstructors. He consecrates the all-purpose water seven times with the all-purpose mantra and sprinkles it over the maṇḍala to purify it. Then with the mantra oṃ bhu khaṃ he makes it of the nature of space, and with the mantra hūṃ laṃ hūṃ he visualizes the ground as having the nature of vajra. With the mantra oṃ medinī vajrī bhava vajra bandha hūṃ that very ground should be blessed. This is the stage of purifying the inner ground.

Then the master, holding vajra and bell, sits in the center of the maṇḍala strewn with loose fragrant flowers of many kinds, and endows himself with the three meditative absorptions that are the nature of all the tathāgatas. In the directions, disciples who have entered the yoga of Gaurī and the other goddesses, holding vajra and bell in their hands, should exhort him with the four vajra verses:

Peace of all the tathāgatas,
abode of all the tathāgatas,
supreme selflessness of all phenomena,
please explain the sublime maṇḍala.
You who have wholly abandoned all that is not a mark,
in whom all the marks are perfectly complete,
supreme body of Samantabhadra,
please explain the sublime maṇḍala.
The Dharma of peace arisen from the supreme,
yogin whose conduct is wisdom,
supreme speech of Samantabhadra,
please explain the sublime maṇḍala.
Great mind of all sentient beings,
pure by nature and without stain,
supreme mind of Samantabhadra, the guide,
please explain the sublime maṇḍala.

Having been exhorted and praised in this way, he takes on the pride of all the buddhas and bodhisattvas and speaks thus:

I myself am all the buddhas.
I myself am all the bodhisattvas.
I myself am the vajra sun.
I myself am the unfailing vajra.
I myself am the vajra, all phenomena.
Of all the tathāgatas,
the supreme mind of all, I myself am that.

Then, with the mantra oṃ vajrottiṣṭa, he raises up the maṇḍala described above and places it in the realm of space.

In order to dispel the darkness of the afflictions that shrouds all the realms of sentient beings, one rises up as the very embodiment of compassion.

From ṭa the moon becomes the left eye;
The right, from ma, blazes as the sun.
With a blazing gaze it pervades the whole world,
Looking evenly with the vajra gaze.
Beneath the feet is a blazing vajra;
With the yoga of fierce wrath
One should whirl the vajra vigorously.
Dancing with the gait of the crossed vajra,
One should ring the bell in its place,
And with a wrathful, terrifying frown proclaim the sound hūṃ.
The mantrin, turning clockwise with a wrathful glare,
Fiercely circles the ground of the wheel.
You hosts of obstructors, gods and the rest,
Dwelling in body, speech and mind,
All you hosts of obstructors, listen!
All you great obstructors, gods and the rest,
Who dwell upon the ground of the great maṇḍala, listen!
Since I am raising the palace of the yogic Sugata,
You great obstructors, of whatever class, depart elsewhere!
I am the vajra-holder, the buddha who is the reality of all,
Vajrasattva, arisen from great bliss.
I am the glorious one of great compassion.
By the protective wheel, perfected by me,
By the wrathful form of the blazing vajra,
By the vigorous whirling of the vajra of the three secrets,
You will without doubt be terrified.
If you transgress my command,
You will be utterly destroyed
And without doubt be ground to dust.
Then all the hosts of obstructors flee,
And not the slightest remnant is left.

This is the stage of expelling the obstructors and taking possession of the ground.

Then, in order to pacify the obstructors, the mantrin should imagine Amṛtakuṇḍalin, his lower part in the form of a three-edged peg, his upper part in the guise of a great wrathful one blazing with the light of a hundred thousand suns, and should meditate on him in the center of the maṇḍala.

oṃ gha gha ghātaya ghātaya sarvaduṣḍān phaṭ kīlaya kīlaya sarvapāpaṃ phaṭ hūṃ hūṃ vajradhara ājñāpayati sarvavighāṃ kāyavākacittavajra kīlaya hūṃ phaṭ

These mantras should be struck into the obstructors by the method of emanating them in all directions. In order to destroy those obstructors without remainder, one should visualize, in the quarters of the maṇḍala, the guardians of the directions, Indra and the rest, from their respective directions, together with their sons and daughters, wives, retinues and servants, all those with malicious minds, and with the yoga of Amṛtakuṇḍalin and the mantra one should strike the body, speech and mind of each. Having struck them, one regards them as without intrinsic nature. This is the stage of planting the pegs.

Then, for the ground that has become free of obstructors, one fills the intermediate space with the light rays of sharp forms and makes the outer vajra fence and so forth. For this, one should visualize, in the center of a sun transformed from a red raṃ, a crossed vajra endowed with five lights, transformed from a black hūṃ. Its rays, in the nature of tiny vajras, pervade the entire sphere of space and radiate in the ten directions, and one should fill it with a vajra fence entirely without gaps. By their radiating upward, one should make a vajra tent without gaps. By their emanating downward, one should make the vajra ground firm. From the tent and the vajra fence, light rays emanate in all directions, and one should bind the directions entirely without gaps. The rays of the sun and of the crossed vajra emanate evenly to a very great distance, and one regards them as having the nature of a wheel that seals the boundaries. This is the stage of the protective wheel of the vajra tent and so forth.

Then Pṛthivī, the earth goddess, born from a yellow paṃ, yellow in color, half her body emerging, holding a jeweled vase, fully adorned with every ornament: having visualized her thus on the ground in front of oneself, one should supplicate her with these words:

Goddess who honors wrathful Hevajra,
You have come under his sway.
With powders of various jewels
I shall draw the meaningful maṇḍala.

Having thus supplicated the earth goddess
Three times, and having duly offered
Purificatory tormas and the like,
One should afterwards make all the offerings.
She then dissolves into the ground,
And it comes to abide as the very essence
Of the source of phenomena,
The nature of body, speech and mind.

Then the master, together with the karmavajra, should anoint the maṇḍala with fragrant nectar, scatter various flowers loosely over it, and in the center make nine maṇḍalas with the five nectars. Then one invites the maṇḍala that earlier was placed in the sphere of space, and visualizes it as residing upon the maṇḍala of the five nectars. Then once again, in the proper order, one completes the protective wheel of the vajra fence and so forth, worships with outer, inner, secret and suchness offerings, and performs the praise and the tasting of the nectar.

For taking possession of the ground, the preparatory rites and so forth, the offering of sound at night is this: one should make offering well and very slowly with the sounds of hand drum, cymbals, long drum, drum, half-drum, small rattle, lute and flute; sing the vajra songs well and very slowly in harmony with the dance; recite auspicious verses melodiously; and have adorned dancing women, with various dance movements, make offering with special offerings within the palace of the maṇḍala.

To the east of the maṇḍala, thus well examined and purified, the yogin sits on a comfortable seat. In front, on the left side, upon a stand or the like, a jeweled vessel is filled with fragrant water and flowers, consecrated seven times with the all-purpose action mantra, and purificatory tormas and so forth should be set out. The yogin, endowed with the three samādhis, should set up the vase in preparation.

For this, a vase without black streaks, with a large belly, a drooping lip, a long neck and no footed base is washed well and fumigated with incense. Sesame, wheat, beans, rice and barley are the five grains. bṛhati, kaṇḍakari, śveta aparājita, sahadeva and dṇḍotpala are the five medicines. Saffron, aloeswood, gandhi, sandalwood and satta agha are the five fragrances. Gold, silver, pearl, coral and the royal gem are the five precious substances. Having gathered these properly, one endowed with pure means should pour them into the vase. Water not muddied by horses, elephants, camels, cattle and the like is drawn into a jeweled vessel by a pure person, without mixing it with anything else. By reciting oṃ tapte tapte mahā tapte svāhā, it becomes the purified water of the gods, and with it one should fill the vase completely. The five trees, cuta, jambudha, kapiṅtha, bhilo and madulungga, are fumigated with incense and made the ornament for the mouth of the vase.

Take a new cloth, undamaged and unsoiled by dirt or other faults, and make it into a crest. At the center of the vase, or at its neck, inside the center of a source of phenomena (dharmodaya), draw well, in fragrant substance, the hand emblem of each respective deity. Then imagine that the syllable paṃ transforms into a jeweled vase. It is adorned with parasols, banners and hanging garlands of flowers. In their midst, generate the principal deity and the others according to the rite. Then place a vajra thread, adorned with flower garlands and fitted with a vajra, upon the vases beginning with Vijaya. With the vajra fist of the left hand hold the end of the vajra thread, and with the right hand hold the secret rosary at the heart. Visualize the light rays from the heart summoning the wisdom beings, making them enter, empowering them and so on, and recite the respective mantra together with the three syllables one hundred and eight times.

Then, for the vases of the deities of the circle, the recitation may be somewhat less than for the principal deity. Or else one should recite the root mantra seven times for each. Just as the principal deity was blessed, so too the retinue are blessed. Then, beginning with the cleansing torma, make worship with outer, inner, secret and suchness offerings, and, holding vajra and bell, offer praise.

Then another vase as well, in order to complete the enlightened activity for all sentient beings, should be blessed as above with the wrathful one of all activities, and one should recite the all-activity mantra together with the three syllables one hundred and eight times.

On this, some masters say: "The eight vases, starting from the east, are arranged as follows: Victory, Auspiciousness, Accomplishment, Protection, again Victory, Peace, Miraculous Power and Accomplishment; they abide as the very nature of these. Having blessed them seven times with this mantra, oṃ suṃ khuṃ yuṃ hūṃ svāhā, one should perform the preparation."

This is the rite of the preparation of the vases.

Then the mantra practitioner holds fragrant flowers in joined palms, bows the body, and recites oṃ sarva tathāgata sulalita vilāsanamitairanamāmi bhagavāntaṃ jaḥ hūṃ vaṃ hoḥ pratīccha kusumāñjali nātha hoḥ. Then he scatters the handful of flowers and makes offering to the circle of the maṇḍala. Then, setting the right kneecap on the ground and holding in the hand a vessel of fragrant incense, he imagines a very subtle hūṃ on the tongue, imagines that it transforms into vajra speech, and makes this request:

Blessed wrathful one, holder of the vajra,
king of vidyā-mantras, I pay homage to you.
Out of love for the disciples
and in order to make offering to you as well,
by the grace of you whose nature is compassion,
I wish to draw the maṇḍala.
Blessed One, to me who am devoted,
please grant your permission.
Buddhas striving for the good of those in saṃsāra,
glorious bodhisattvas abiding in the fruit,
and the other deities of secret mantra,
the gods who protect the world,
whatever powerful bhūtas there may be,
and others possessing the divine eye,
beings who delight in the teaching:
please turn your minds to me.
If I, called such-and-such,
for the sake of purifying beings,
draw the maṇḍala of the arising of glorious Hevajra
with whatever requisites I can obtain,
then today, to me together with my disciples,
please turn your loving minds,
and bless the whole assembly
in the maṇḍala.

Having requested thus three times, one should make offerings and praise.

This is the stage of the preparation of the deities.

Then the mantra practitioner comes out from within the maṇḍala, makes the protective circle, and performs the preparation of the disciples.

One's own disciple offers a maṇḍala before the guru,
holding a garland of flowers:
one of keen mind and great enthusiasm,
grateful and without pride,
of high family and endowed with good qualities,
of fine form and complexion, possessed of youth.
If they strive earnestly, seeking to accomplish
the secret of the Sugatas,
even those of ugly form and without good qualities,
and even the lowly, should be given the preparation.
In the rite of the maṇḍala
the four assemblies, too, are permitted.
Those fully endowed with their own training,
whose nature delights in the Great Vehicle,
some of whom seek to accomplish secret mantra:
these one has enter the maṇḍala.
Apart from them are those who desire merit;
others seek the aims of another world.
Those who seek the aims of the world beyond,
by fostering much faith,
the wise one should have enter the maṇḍala,
but they should not desire the fruits of this life.
For one who comes to desire this life,
the aims of the world beyond do not come about;
when seeking the aims of the world beyond arises,
the fruits of this world flourish.

Having spoken thus, mentally draw the disciple
into one's own heart,
send him out along the vajra path,
and place him upon the lotus of the consort.
Gathering him again, summon the mass of light
of the Tathāgatas and dissolve it into him.
Through the union of vajra and lotus,
the disciple born from the drop of wisdom
is placed at the door of the maṇḍala,
and with joined palms should make supplication:

You are the great lord,
truly united with Nairātmyā.
Great hero, I go to you for refuge.
For the sake of all sentient beings,
you, liberated from the ocean of saṃsāra,
are great bliss:
please bestow the same upon me.
Great protector, steadfast in the way of great awakening,
I seek you.
Grant me that pledge;
grant me, too, the mind of awakening.
The Buddha, the Dharma and the Saṅgha,
the three refuges, grant me as well.
Into the palace of the great supreme abode
likewise, please let me enter.

Having supplicated thus three times,
the foremost of the disciples
bows with the crown of his head at the master's feet
and remains bowing with palms joined.
Then the vajra master,
taking the chief disciple into his care,
should speak these verses:

Come, my child! Of the Great Vehicle
you are a vessel of the great way.
This rite, the conduct of secret mantra,
I shall rightly teach to you.
The perfect buddhas who have passed,
likewise those who have not yet come,
and the perfect buddhas who have now appeared,
holders of the vajra of body, speech and mind,
who remain for the benefit of beings:
all of them have known
this excellent rite of secret mantra.
The hero, at the foot of the tree of awakening,
attained signless omniscience.
Through the unequaled practice of secret mantra,
Śākyasiṃha the protector
vanquished the most terrible hosts of Māra
and their great armies too;
and for the sake of beings
he turned the wheel of Dharma.

"Therefore, in order to attain omniscience,
you, my son, must form this resolve."
Thus the vajra master
speaks three times, with the sound of the bell.

Then, once permission has been obtained and the disciple has confessed wrongdoing and so forth, the going for refuge to the Three and the rest should be performed in full.

"The Buddha, the Dharma and the Saṅgha,
the unsurpassed Three Jewels,
from this time forth I shall hold.
The moral discipline of the three trainings,
the gathering of virtuous qualities,
and the moral discipline of working for the benefit of beings,
these likewise I shall firmly hold.
Of the pure great vajra family,
the vajra, the bell and the mudrā too
I shall truly hold;
the masters too I shall hold.
For the delightful samaya
of the great and supreme jewel family,
every day, at the six times,
I shall always give the four kinds of giving.
Of the pure and supreme lotus family,
arisen from great awakening,
the outer, inner and secret three vehicles,
the sacred Dharma, I shall hold without exception.
Of the great and supreme karma family,
being endowed with all the vows,
I shall truly hold;
acts of offering too I shall perform as best I can.
Supreme, unsurpassed awakening,
for the sake of sentient beings,
I shall truly generate.
The vows, just as they have been taught,
likewise I shall hold.
Those not yet ferried across I shall ferry across;
those not yet freed I shall free;
those not yet relieved I shall relieve,
and I shall establish beings in nirvāṇa."

This should be recited three times in this way. If one is unable to take it up in this way, one should recite just this verse:

"I go for refuge to the Three Jewels.
I confess all wrongdoing.
I rejoice in all merit.
I take the awakening of the Buddha to heart."

With these words and so forth, the going for refuge to the Three Jewels and the rest should be conferred.

Then the vajra master should visualize Nairātmyā, Bhūcarī and Khecarī at the heart, throat and crown of the disciples. Touching the vajra held in the right hand to the heart and the other places, he blesses them with the three syllables together with their light rays. Flowers are offered at the crown; the lamp, incense and food offering are offered in front; perfume is offered at the heart.

If the lamp burns clear, the accomplishment
will come about just as intended.
A middling flame is said to mean middling accomplishment.
Burning with other colors, flickering,
or flaring up only moment by moment,
is said to signify obstructors and hindrances.
If it goes out, that person will die.

Then take a stick from a milky-sapped tree such as the vaṭa, the udumbara or the aśvattha, twelve finger-widths in length, free of insects, steady, neither dried out nor cracked, and taken from the tip of the branch. Wash it well with fragrant water, wind a garland of flowers around it at three places, and place it in the disciple's hand. Consecrate it three times with oṃ āḥ jihva saṃ barta ni hūṃ phaṭ and give it.

Then the disciple, facing east or toward the maṇḍala, upon a maṇḍala about the size of a cowhide and well smeared with the five cow products, scrapes away the obscurations of speech with the tooth-stick according to the guru's instructions, and should then cast the tooth-stick in order to request accomplishment from the chosen deity.

When the tooth-stick is cast, if by chance
its tip points upward,
by that one gains supreme accomplishment.
If the tip points to the east,
or likewise toward oneself,
that is said to be middling accomplishment.
If the tip points south or to an intermediate direction,
one obtains worldly accomplishment.
When the tooth-stick is cast, if by chance
the tip points downward,
it is the accomplishment of the underworld;
it will be obtained, of this there is no doubt.
If it lands outside the vajra garland,
for that one there is no accomplishment.

Then, having summoned the seven disciples, one consecrates water with this mantra, oṃ hrīḥ viśuddha dharmā pāpanicāsya viśodhaya sarva vikalpa apanaya hūṃ, and, in order to purify the obscurations of mind, one should pour a sip of water for them to drink three times. Then, having recited the all-purpose mantra, one ties three knots in a protection cord, and while the disciple has not yet been ripened it should be tied on the right upper arm.

Then, in order to purify the obscurations of body and to bring forth signs, one consecrates kuśa grass with the all-purpose mantra seven times for each, and gives one blade of kuśa to serve as a pillow; the others are given for the seat and so forth.

Then, in order to uplift their minds, the Dharma should be taught:

"Omniscient ones in the world
may arise, or else may not arise;
like the udumbara flower,
only now and then, by chance,
they appear, exceedingly rare.
The appearance of the way of secret mantra conduct
is rarer even than that,
for by it one is able, without passing into nirvāṇa,
to accomplish the matchless welfare of beings.
Whatever misdeeds have been committed
over many tens of millions of eons,
if all of them are cleansed
by seeing a maṇḍala such as this,
what need is there to speak of abiding
in the way of mantra conduct of boundless renown?
If one recites the secret mantra of the Protector,
one attains the unsurpassed state.
Those whose minds are utterly unwavering
in this supreme conduct
cut off the stream of the lower realms,
and all suffering and its origin.
Those great beings
today obtain a gain beyond compare.
For all the great-souled Victors
together with their heirs
have taken all of you fully
into this teaching; and as you are now being born,
so tomorrow you will be truly born
into the Great Vehicle.
This supreme path, from which the Great Vehicle
grandly arises, is glorious.
Because you travel upon it,
you self-arisen ones of great fortune
will become tathāgatas
who know the whole world.
It transcends existence and nonexistence,
stainless like the sky,
more profound than all phenomena,
and free of all elaboration.
Yet it is unfolded through elaborations;
if one abides in the Great Vehicle,
this very Dharma will be attained."

Then the assembly of disciples rises with joyful minds. Having instructed them, "Now each of you lie down separately. Tomorrow you will see the maṇḍala, so recall carefully whatever dreams you have," one should protect them with the mantra oṃ rakṣa raṣ hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ svāhā or with the all-purpose mantra. Then they should lie down on beds of kuśa grass. This is the stage of the disciples' preparation.

Then the vajra master, having withdrawn the maṇḍala, should recite the all-purpose mantra for as long as he is able, and should sleep in the lion's posture.

At the time of rising in the morning,
having bathed, ask about the night's dreams.
Reflect on them well, have them recounted, and listen;
examine whether they are wholesome or unwholesome.

Meeting a buddha, and worshipping images and stupas;
entering the Dharma, and meeting monks and the Saṅgha;
temples, buddhas with their offspring, and nuns;
dwelling in flower gardens, on mountains and in hermitages;
crossing streams, rivers, ponds and the ocean;
meeting the lord of beasts, the eṇeya antelope, and oxen;
seeing one marked with a wheel and the like, and good yakṣas;
seeing or meeting in a dream holy persons in meditation;
going upward, riding an elephant, and climbing a fine mountain;
fine canopies and mansions, and resting beneath a wish-fulfilling tree;
seeing laymen, kings, and monks who grant protection;
seeing the special mastery won through mantra, and union with a consort;
father and mother, going through the sky, and meeting one's master;
daughters of the gods, and union with a pure secret consort;
seeing sublime sādhanas, and brahmins endowed with good qualities;
undertaking great offerings, and seeing a celestial palace;
ornaments, clothes, heaps of grain, and holding weapons;
performing a fire offering, and seeing oneself obtain fine fruits and flowers;
royal consecration, and finding stores of grain and jewels;
seeing victory in battle, enemy armies bound, and captives taken;
seeing oneself crossing the great ocean and bathing;
and seeing oneself fly over and cross rough paths hard to travel.

Dreams seen in this way are signs of virtue: one should rejoice in them and carry out the rite on their basis. Dreams other than these are declared to be unwholesome dreams. From the wholesome ones the matter comes to accomplishment; from the unwholesome ones it does not. If one sees such unwholesome signs, the mantrin should perform an auspicious rite with the mantra for all actions, and should resolve that the very nature of the dream is without intrinsic nature, and that the nature of all phenomena is suchness. Then the dream signs are pacified and become a cause of accomplishment.

Then the yogin, endowed with the three samādhis of Hevajra, requests the maṇḍala that was invited earlier, during the preparatory rites, to depart into the expanse of space. Then, with a rite performed by a woman from a charnel ground or a wisdom consort endowed with the proper marks, a thread spun from cotton is dyed white, yellow, red, green and black with charnel-ground pigments, and it should also be mixed with powdered white mustard. The colors are placed in their respective directions and generated as the very nature of Vairocana, Ratnasambhava, Amitābha, Amoghasiddhi and Akṣobhya, who are produced from bhrūṃ āṃ jṝṃ khaṃ hūṃ. They should be generated and set down in stages, divided into twenty-five by way of the nature of the five wisdoms, the mirror-like wisdom and the rest.

Then, from each direction, one visualizes them as the five tathāgata families, Vairocana and the others. At their hearts the syllables bhrūṃ and the rest are transformed, and from them one visualizes the wisdom chalk-lines together with light rays. With oṃ āḥ śaśvata vajra suca me prayaccha mahāmaṇḍala sutrānaya hūṃ one should supplicate Vairocana. Likewise, it is taught that one supplicates Ratnasambhava and the others by knowing to put the syllables of their names in the place within the mantra.

Then the master, endowed with the yoga of Hevajra, should visualize a moon eye arising from ja and a sun eye arising from ma. Gazing with a blazing look that pervades all directions, one should draw in the wisdom chalk-lines with the hook of the eyes and bless them as the Blessed One. The distinct threads of each color are gathered into one and twisted together by a disciple endowed with the yoga of Ghasmarī. Reciting oṃ anyonya gata sarva dharmaḥ parspara anugatā sarva dharmā antana ana prabeśa sarva dharmāḥ hūṃ, one should spin the chalk-line. Then one should meditate as follows: having twisted it into one, one makes it inseparable from the wisdom maṇḍala; its length is made twice the measure of the maṇḍala, and its thickness one twentieth of a door.

Then the mantrin should cleanse it in a vessel of gold or the like with the five nectars and fine fragrances, mark it with the three syllables, make the five kinds of offerings, hold it with the vajra fist, and protect it by reciting hūṃ many times. Then the mantrin should again sprinkle the maṇḍala with the five nectars and fragrant water, scatter flowers, and, with the light rays from the heart, make the maṇḍala of Hevajra clearly present in the sky; and, beginning with purificatory tormas and the like, perform a special offering.

Then the one endowed with the yoga of Hevajra hands the end of the chalk-line to the disciple endowed with the yoga of Ghasmarī, visualizing the two eyes as a moon and sun, yellow in color, arisen from ja. Then the mantrin, standing in the west and facing east, holds the end of the chalk-line with the vajra fist at the place of the navel, hands it to the disciple, and, saying ja ja ja, gives the command. The intelligent disciple, also saying ja ja ja, should take the end of the chalk-line and hold it. With the master facing east and the disciple facing west, joining vajra thumb and forefinger, they should strike the line upon the maṇḍala in space. Likewise they should strike it below. Reciting oṃ vajra samaya sūtra mātikrama hūṃ, one should strike the line with the thumb and forefinger of the right hand, which holds the vajra. Holding it at the navel or the heart and gazing into space, one should strike the Brahmā lines evenly in all directions.

Then, in order to liberate all sentient beings, one urges all the tathāgatas abiding in space by the sound of the line. Then, turning around, the master faces north and the disciple faces south, and they should strike the Brahmā line. Then, with the master facing north and the disciple facing south, they should strike it on the east. Then, turning around and facing south and north, they should strike it on the western side. Turning around again, one from the northwest, Vāyu's quarter, facing east, and the other from the northeast, Īśāna's quarter, facing west, they should strike it on the northern side. One from the southeast, Agni's quarter, facing west, and the other from the southwest, the rākṣasa quarter, facing east, they should strike it on the southern side. From that very spot, facing northwest, they should strike the diagonal line.

Facing from the southwest, Nairṛti's quarter, toward the northeast (Īśāna's quarter), one should lay down the other diagonal line. Then, from the Brahma lines, one should lay down the lines of the doors at the measure of the eight-spoked wheel. Beginning from the root lines, one should lay down the lines of the colored bands of the square, at the measure of a door unit. Outside that, at half a door unit, one should lay down the line of the band of the sense pleasures. Outside that, one should lay down the lines of the jewel wall, equal to the color unit. Outside that, one should lay down the lines, equal to the band of the sense pleasures, together with the half-nets and the vajra crescents. Outside that, one should lay down a line equal to the color unit. Then, at the sides of the four doors, one should lay down the lines of the deity bands, equal to the color unit. Then one should lay down the lines of the pillars, equal to the door unit. Then, at three times the door unit, one should lay down the lines of the porticoes above the doors, in whatever way is beautiful.

Then, folding the measuring cord double, one should plant a peg of khadira wood firmly in the center and, beginning from the northeast corner, turn it clockwise and draw the lines of the three outer fences of the maṇḍala. Folding that cord double, one should draw in the center of the maṇḍala the line encircled by the vajra fence. Then, outside the maṇḍala, one should lay down the lines of the eight charnel grounds, so that the corner lines are contained within the vajra wall.

For the maṇḍala of Nairātmyā, the lines of a two-tiered palace are laid down, and the lines of the eight pillars and so forth are laid down in the same way. The wheel of the east is laid down at the pillar unit, facing from south toward north. The lotus of the west is laid down at the pillar unit, facing from north toward south. The sword of the north is laid down at the pillar unit, facing from west toward east. The jewel of the south is laid down at the pillar unit, facing from east toward west.

Then, if a yogin who wishes to apply himself to laying down the lines has no disciple, he should tie the cord to a peg and lay it down that way. At the time of the lines, omens should be examined:

If the string breaks, the master will die.
If it is pulled too long or too short, illness will come.
If the direction is confused, the disciple will go mad.

These signs should be examined at the time of laying down the Brahma lines. Or else they should be examined at the time of the Brahma lines and the diagonal lines. Or else they should be examined at the midpoint of all the lines, the Brahma lines and the rest.

Then the Brahma line of each door should be erased. Moreover, all the lines that arose in the course of marking out should also be erased. Further, the lines of the deity bands and so forth at the sides of each door should be erased for about one foot-measure. Then at the junctions of the nets and half-nets, too, the lines should be erased for about one foot-measure. At the junctions of the jewel wall, too, the lines should be erased for about one foot-measure. The seven lines at the inner end of its doors should also be erased for about one foot-measure.

The maṇḍala whose lines have been laid down in this way should be drawn well and perfectly even. The palace of the maṇḍala should be drawn with great care.

Four-cornered, with four doors,
beautified by four porticoes,
furnished with the four lines,
adorned with hanging silks and garlands of flowers,
beautified by the five colors,
the supreme maṇḍala, three cubits across,
exceeding that by three color units.
On all the sides of the corners
and on the sides of the door recesses
it is finely ornamented with vajras and jewels.
The maṇḍala of Nairātmyā
is the same in its outer features.
Its measure is half the outer maṇḍala;
in the center one should view it evenly.
The pillars of the wheel and so forth
are well encircled by garlands of vajras.
Adorn it with yak-tail fans and mirrors;
with parasols, banners and ornaments
one should arrange it within the wheel.

Having thus completed the laying of lines on the maṇḍala whose nature is the Dharma body, one should request the colored measuring cord to depart with the three syllables ending in muḥ, and place it in a clean vessel. Then the vajra master sits in the center of the maṇḍala and views his right and left eyes, arising from hi, as having the nature of moon and sun. Gazing evenly in all directions with a wrathful look, he imagines that he mentally opens the four doors, and goes out. This is the procedure for laying down the lines.

Then the colors are made from substances that come from the jewel family, pigments belonging to the jewel class, fruits turned into colors, bricks from a charnel ground, charcoal, bone, and thief's leaves. They are white, yellow, red, green and black. They should be ground finely, sifted through a cloth strainer, well fumigated with incense, poured into clean vessels, and set in their respective directions.

Then, with the light rays from one's heart, one makes them devoid of intrinsic nature, and fully generates them from bhrūṃ jrāṃ khaṃ hūṃ. In this way one visualizes them in front. Just as was taught before, with a gaze blazing with the light rays of the wisdom of all the Tathāgatas, Vairocana and the rest, one summons them by the application of the hook, and they become one with the seed syllables. From that, one views the blazing light rays as the nature of wisdom, and one should bless them with this secret mantra: oṃ vajra citra samaya hūṃ oṃ vajra bega. Reciting this mantra, one mentally removes the wisdom cord and enters the maṇḍala. Saying hūṃ, one pulls out the peg at the navel of the maṇḍala and imagines that it dissolves into the vajra in one's hand. One fills the holes completely with the five colors, and, in order to remove any inauspicious signs that may occur, one should sing the vajra song accompanied by the sound of the bell:

This is the pure dharmadhātu,
which liberates the realm of sentient beings.
The king Hevajra himself,
the abode of all the Tathāgatas,
fully liberated from all faults,
abides perfectly within the wheel,
lord of the yoga of wisdom and means.

Holding the color in the left fist and beginning from the northeast corner, one should draw the white, yellow, red, green and black colors clockwise, in order from the outside inward, evenly and without a break.

It is said:

White has the nature of the diamond's point;
The second is conceived as gold;
Then comes the nature of the ruby;
Likewise the other is like emerald;
Black is to be known as the innermost.
This is taught as the order of the colors.

They should be laid down as follows: evenly, without wavering, without breaks, each occupying one-twentieth part of a door, and each separately, without mixing, with a gap the size of a barleycorn between them.

Then the mantra practitioner, in meditative equipoise, should examine the omens:

If laid down thick, one is struck by illness;
If thin, one's wealth is destroyed;
If crooked or broken,
The master and the disciple die.
If laid down other than clockwise,
One is struck by the stake of the colors,
And for both master and disciple
Great anxiety arises.

Then, taking the measure from the lines of the central palace, white should be placed on the door sections in the east. Yellow should be placed on the southern section. Red should be placed on the western section. Green should be placed in the north. Black should be placed in the center.

Then in the center, upon an eight-petaled red lotus with its golden anthers, a sun disk is placed. Upon it, at the hub of a crossed vajra, a white skull cup, red inside and marked with a crossed vajra, should be drawn. For the one-faced, two-armed or the six-armed Hevajra, one draws the lord of one's own family, black in color and blazing, and on the petals of the east and the other directions one should draw, in order, white skull cups without the crossed vajra.

Alternatively, at the east door, upon a moon disk on a lotus, a black curved knife is drawn. At the south door a red ḍamaru drum is drawn. At the west door a yellow tortoise is drawn. At the north door a snake like emerald is drawn. In the northeast a śarabha like sapphire is drawn. In the southeast a monk like moonstone is drawn. In the southwest a wheel the color of the sky is drawn. In the northwest a multicolored vajra is drawn. These too should be drawn upon a moon disk at the hub of an eight-petaled red lotus. For these as well, the four māras, Brahmā and the others, may serve as seats, or they may be without them; either is acceptable.

Then, further, one who wishes to draw the maṇḍala of Nairātmyā should draw an eight-petaled red lotus at the central hub. At its hub, upon a moon disk, a black curved knife should be drawn. On the petals of the directions, east and so on, four curved knives should be drawn. On the outer petals, in the intermediate directions, northeast and so on, four curved knives are drawn upon moons. At the doors, east and so on, four curved knives are drawn on moon and lotus. Beside the east and west doors, two curved knives are drawn upon lotus and moon.

At the time of laying down the colors of the maṇḍala, when entering and exiting, one lifts the vajra lines into the sky with the mind, recites the bajrabe mantra, and lays them down without departing from the form of the deity. It is taught that if this is not done, the samaya is broken. As it is said in the Compendium of Principles:

When exiting and entering the maṇḍala,
If one acts having lifted with the mind
The vajra lines and the colors,
One is not stained by a breach of samaya.

Then, in the outer ring of the maṇḍala, in the directions and intermediate directions, following the order of clockwise and counterclockwise, one draws corpses, vetālas of terrifying form, bhūtas and the like, embodying the eight charnel grounds. In the east, one draws the charnel ground called Fierce and Savage, of the class of the burned. In the south, one draws the charnel ground called Terrifying, of the class of those cut into pieces. In the west, one draws the charnel ground called Blazing Skeletons, of the class of the discarded. In the north, one draws the charnel ground called Filled with Roaring Flames, of the class of those impaled on stakes. In the northeast, one draws the charnel ground called Laughing Ha Ha, of the class of those hung from trees. In the southeast, one draws Excellent Forest, of the class of the half-burned. In the southwest, one draws the charnel ground called Unbearable Darkness, of the class of the half-cut. In the northwest, one draws Resounding with Cries of Kili Kili, of the class of those buried underground.

For these, too, the trees are drawn in their respective order: śirīṣa, cūta, kaṅkeli, aśvattha, bhatha, karañja, lataṭa and prakari, growing from the earth, are drawn in the order of east and so on. Within the trees one draws the lords of the trees, holding curved knife and skull cup, together with their retinues. Of these, the elephant-faced one is surrounded by a host of elephants. The buffalo-faced one is surrounded by a host of buffaloes. The makara-faced one is surrounded by a host of fish. The yakṣa-faced one is surrounded by a host of humans. The bull-faced one is surrounded by a host of bulls. The goat-faced one is surrounded by a host of goats. The rākṣasa-faced one is surrounded by a host of pretas. The śarabha-faced one is surrounded by a host of śarabhas.

At the roots of these trees the guardians of the directions, as taught, dwell with their retinues, embracing their own consorts. Of these, one draws Indra, yellow in color, with a jeweled crown, adorned with various ornaments, holding a vajra in his hand, seated in a majestic manner on a white elephant. One draws Yama, black in color, his eyes like heaps of glowing embers, his brows and forehead knit in a wrathful frown, his yellow hair blazing upward, holding a staff and a trident in his hands, seated wrathfully on a buffalo. Varuṇa, the lord of waters, is white in color, with seven serpent hoods, holding a snake noose and a conch, seated on a makara. Kubera, the lord of wealth, is yellow, the color of gold, with a jeweled crown, holding an iron hook and a staff, seated on a yakṣa. Īśāna is white in color, with his matted locks in a topknot and a crown of the moon, three-eyed, with a garland of skulls, four-armed, holding a ḍamaru drum, a skull cup, a trident and a khaṭvāṅga, seated upon a bull. Agni, the fire god, is red in color, with his matted locks bound in a topknot, wearing the brahmin's sacred thread, four-armed, holding a rosary, making the gesture of granting protection, and holding a water pot and a staff, seated upon a goat.

Nairṛti is blue in color, with red hair blazing upward. He wears a garland of skulls, holds a curved knife and a skull cup, and is seated upon a corpse. Vāyu is smoke-colored, holds a banner of the wind, wears a jeweled crown, and is seated upon a deer. Draw them so.

In their places too, draw them surrounded by gods, pretas, nāgas, yakṣas, bhūtas, sages, rākṣasas, lords of the wind, and their hosts.

To the left of this, draw the eight great nāgas with their hoods. Vāsuki is the color of the moon, marked on the crown of his head with a blue utpala, and has a hood. Padma is the color of a conch, with a hood marked by the śrīvatsa. Karkoṭa is blue like dūrvā grass, marked at the throat with three jewels. Takṣaka is the color of the nyerden flower, marked with a blazing jewel. Mahāpadma is the color of jasmine, marked with a trident. Hulu Hulu is deep black, marked on the crown of his head with a sign. Kulika is smoke-colored, his throat marked with a moon jewel. Śaṅkhapāla is golden, his throat marked with a moon disk. Draw them so.

Behind them draw the clouds. The Thundering is black. The Coiling is blue. The Murky is saffron-colored. The Darkness is multicolored. The Full is reddish yellow. The Raining is bluish red. The Fierce is the color of lac. The Massing is white. Beyond this, the maṇḍala should be beautifully adorned with every ornament proper to it.

This is the stage of laying out the colors.

At the time of drawing the maṇḍala, in order to avert whatever obstructors may attempt and whatever arises as a hindrance of its own nature, one should, following the earlier sequence, stake with kīlas, in order, the guardians of the directions beginning with Indra, and so forth.

If rain should fall on the site of the maṇḍala, one who possesses the yoga of Hevajra should sit on a cloth from a charnel ground and, with a fierce mind, recite this mantra:
oṃ ārya ārya smaśan priyāya hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ svāhā
Gazing upward with a blazing look, one should hold the conviction that from one's heart a host of blazing wrathful ones fills the whole sphere of space, and that their light rays, like the fire of wisdom, utterly scatter the masses of clouds.

If the danger of fire arises, then above the fire, from a transformed BAṂ, there is a white water maṇḍala. Upon it, at the center of an eight-petaled lotus, visualize an eight-spoked wheel transformed from BHRŪṂ. At its hub, from BHRŪṂ, there is an autumn moon, and at its center, blessed by BHRŪṂ, are the four oceans and the four great rivers, like the waters of Anavatapta. From the seed syllable at the heart, light rays like masses of clouds come forth and pervade the whole sphere of space, and streams of water pour down and fill the ten directions. Visualize oneself as Vairocana.

If wind causes hindrance, in order to pacify it, enter into the yoga of one's special deity or of Vajrahūṃkāra and recite the mantra of HŪṂ a hundred thousand times. Then, from the five products of the cow that have not fallen to the ground, or from the earth of an anthill, make an image of Vāyu. Visualize in the sky the form of Vāyu arising from YAṂ, summon him, and dissolve him into the image made of the substance. Having bound it, recite over a kīla of raṃśu wood four fingers long one hundred and eight times, and drive it into the ground. In the same way, to remove harm from water, stake Varuṇa with a kīla.

Again, as before, having meditated in sequence on the vajra fence and so forth down to the vajra ground, gaze in all directions with a wrathful look and recite the root mantra:
oṃ aṣṭa nanāya piṅgordhva keśa bartmane caturbiṃśati netrāya ṣoḍaśa bhujāya kṛṣṇa jimūta vapuṣe kapāla mālānekaṃ dhāriṇe āddma krūra cittāya ardhandu daṃṣṭṛṇe oṃ māraya māraya kāraya kāraya garjaya garjaya tarjaya tarjaya śoṣaya śoṣaya sapta sāgarān bandha bandha nāgāṣṭikān gṛhṇa gṛhṇa sarba śatrūṃ ha hā hi hī hu hū he hai ho hau haṃ haḥ phaṭ svāhā

Or else, to pacify hindrances, write the name of the obstructor with the juices of arka leaves, mustard seed, aconite, and citraka, and recite this mantra ten thousand times:
oṃ he bajra jrara jvar śatrāṃ nana bhrūṃ hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ svāhā
Then, in fierce wrath, perform a fire offering with powdered rice husks.

Then the mantrin faces the maṇḍala, gathers all the requisites of the maṇḍala, and makes the maṇḍala offerings. With a thoroughly pure mind, he should then carry out the maṇḍala rite exactly as prescribed. The sequence is as follows. Together with an outer consort (vidyā) who is thirteen years of age, or else with the inner consort, one becomes endowed with the three samādhis. With the sound produced by vajra and lotus, one summons all the tathāgatas, draws them into the heart, and melts them into light. One sends them out along the vajra path and lets them descend into the place of the mother's lotus. As the nature of all the tathāgatas they are emanated into the ten directions, and they accomplish the benefit of beings without exception. Then the wise one draws them back. As they abide in the sky beside the maṇḍala, the obstructors who have come along with them are driven off completely with the sound aṣṭānanā or hūṃ, and the maṇḍala should be protected.

Then give the cleansing offering, the torma, and so forth. With jaḥ hūṃ baṃ hoḥ, summon them in due order, and with the emblems of the maṇḍala, perform the summoning, the drawing in, the binding, and the delighting. Then the eyes, the body, and so forth should be blessed. Again, preceded by the cleansing offering, the torma, and so forth, emanate the eight goddesses from the seed syllable in one's heart, and make outer, secret, and suchness offerings. Lengths of cloth, garlands of flowers, and the like, equal in number to the deities, should be offered to the maṇḍala. One should praise the wheel:

You, surrounded by the yoginīs,
Accomplish the purpose of your own mind.
You accomplish all of saṃsāra, the moving and the unmoving;
With a mind of compassion you bring beings to awakening.
Mind that accomplishes the fruit through conventional illusion,

The maṇḍala, and the one who embodies the maṇḍala, should be satisfied by that nectar and abide as the embodiment of great bliss. The master of the maṇḍala should then undertake the recitation of the secret mantras of all the deities, each in its proper order.

Then the mantrins who are not the appointed action vajra are dismissed from the maṇḍala palace. The master comes out, surrounded by a white parasol, banners, fans, victory banners, and music with song, dance, and a mass of sound, and sits on a comfortable seat. In front of himself, in a large copper vessel or one made of bamboo or the like, he fills it completely with a variety of foods: fish, meat, barley meal, yogurt, milk, honey, beer, water, cooked rice, roasted rice, sesame, little balls, broth, and so forth, along with the special fare of a feast, kinds of drinks, things to lick, and kinds of things to suck, and with all those substances. It should also be adorned with ornaments such as the five lamps, nectar, parasols, victory banners, and flower garlands. Moreover, gather whatever array of food is available. If none is available, make it from nothing more than water and barley meal, and visualize everything as assembled.

Having viewed it in this way, purify it through the sequence of the five wisdoms, beginning with the blazing. The sequence is as follows. In the sky in front, visualize a vajra arising from the transformation of a blue hūṃ. Below it, visualize a skull cup, red inside, transformed from a red āḥ. In its center, visualize the embodiment of the five nectars, blessed by a white oṃ. Then the kindling of fire through the union of vajra and lotus is the mirror-like wisdom. The expansion of the substances by that fire is the nature of the wisdom of equality. Their being heated, melting into liquid, and radiating light is the discriminating wisdom. Their becoming like the disk of the sun of wisdom is the wisdom that accomplishes all actions. Its rays spread into the ten directions and become the forms of all the tathāgatas without exception. By the rays from the wisdom seed in their hearts, the nectar of wisdom is summoned and absorbed, and the two are made nondual; this is the nature of the wisdom of the utterly pure dharmadhātu. Blessed in this way as the embodiment of the five wisdoms, it becomes the supreme nectar, and it should be blessed with the three syllables.

Then, in the sky before oneself, visualize a red eight-petaled lotus in the center of a source of phenomena (dharmodaya). On its four petals beginning with the east are Indra, Yama, Varuṇa, and Kubera. On the petals beginning with the northeast are Īśāna, Agni, Nairṛti, and Vāyu. They should be meditated on as born from hūṃ, blue in color, with one face and two arms, holding vajra and bell as they embrace their own consorts, bearing the six seals, standing in a dance with one leg extended and one bent, their heads adorned with Akṣobhya.

Above that, contained within the source of phenomena, are the moon, the sun, Brahmā, and the others; the Four Great Kings, the Heaven of the Thirty-Three, Yāma, Tuṣita, Nirmāṇarati, and Paranirmitavaśavartin. These are the gods of the desire realm. Brahmakāyika, Brahmapurohita, and Mahābrahmā are the first dhyāna. Parīttābha, Apramāṇābha, and Ābhāsvara are the second dhyāna. Parīttaśubha, Apramāṇaśubha, and Śubhakṛtsna are the third dhyāna. Anabhraka, Puṇyaprasava, Bṛhatphala, Abṛha, Atapa, Sudṛśa, Sudarśana, and Akaniṣṭha, these abodes are the fourth dhyāna. With rays of light one summons those who dwell in the Sukhāvatī world-system and all who dwell in the other world-systems, and they melt together. They arise from oṃ, white in color, their hands holding vajra and bell as they embrace their own consorts, bearing the six seals, standing with one leg extended and one bent, their heads adorned with Vairocana.

At the base of the lotus of the source of phenomena are the nāga kings Vāsuki, Takṣaka, Karkoṭa, Padma, Mahāpadma, Kulika, Śaṅkhapāla and the others; the great asuras Vemacitra, the Scatterer, Virocana and the others; the earth goddess Pṛthivī with her retinue; and those who dwell in the hot hells: the Ephemeral, the Black Line, the Crushing, the Terrifying, the Greatly Terrifying, the Hot, the Intensely Hot, and the Incessant. Then there are the cold hells: Blisters, Bursting Blisters, Chattering Teeth, Haha, Huhu, Like a Blue Lotus, Lotus, and Great Lotus. One summons all the many kinds of hells there are, those in water, those on dry land, those among families and the rest, together with the hungry ghosts and the animals, and performs the rite of purification. Then one should visualize the nāgas, who arise from a red āḥ, as having one face and two arms, holding vajra and bell in the mudrā of embracing their own consorts, standing in the posture with one leg extended and one bent, wearing the six seals, and crowned by Amitābha.

Then the vajra master, wearing varied garments, ties his yellow hair up in a topknot, makes a crest from a garland of flowers, and is to be richly adorned with every ornament: blue lotuses at the ears, wheels, armlets, bracelets, necklaces and necklaces, and ornaments of gold, silver and the like.

Next, smear the ground with the five kinds of clear perfume, and draw an eight-petaled lotus with its rim so that it fits within the source of phenomena. In the east and the northeast, at the upward-facing point of the source of phenomena, is the place where the deities are arranged. In the west and the southwest, at the foot of the source of phenomena, is the place where the nāgas are arranged. On the west side one regards oneself as having the nature of the guardians of the directions. Then, through the application of Gaurī and the others, they are summoned, made to enter, bound, and brought under control. Preceded by the cleansing libation and the rest, one blesses the torma with the torma mantra and offers it.

The deities and the others, having received the supreme nectar, become utterly satisfied. Then, to make up for whatever in the rite was deficient or excessive, one should begin the fire offering for increase in the morning. It is preceded by prostrations and the rest to the maṇḍala of the deity to whom one is especially devoted, and it is performed either in whichever hearth one prefers or in the hearth for all activities. It should be made to the south or to the east of the maṇḍala. In yellow-colored ground, dig a round hearth one cubit across and twelve finger-widths deep. Inside a vajra garland, arrange the hand emblem of the deity. The emblem of the principal deity, matching his body color and eight finger-widths in size, is to be seen at the center. Then, leaving a margin of four finger-widths, raise a rim four finger-widths high. Outside that, give it the shape of an open lotus. In the cardinal directions arrange five-pronged vajras, and in the intermediate directions three-pronged ones. On the rim a wheel is arranged. It should be made round all the way around.

Smear the hearth all over with the five products of the cow. Over that, smear it well with yellow pigment mixed with perfume and nectar. Then scatter various flowers of excellent color and fragrance, or else strew loose yellow flowers at the three times of day. Cover the hearth evenly with branches bearing fragrant yellow fruit, or with utpala branches, or with plantain leaves, or with perfumed yellow cloth. These are to remain in place until one's own meditative absorption is complete.

Then bathe, anoint the limbs well with sweet fragrance, and adorn yourself fully with various garments, ornaments and flower garlands. Make a crest of yellow cotton cloth, and in a state of great joy, protect yourself with mantra. With graceful bearing, face north or east, and seated in the auspicious posture, the mantra practitioner should perform the fire offering single-pointedly until the activity of increase is complete.

Then, with water containing the five nectars, make a maṇḍala in front and arrange heaps of flowers on it. Wash them well and fumigate them with incense. Then fill them well with water containing yogurt, sesame, yellow perfume and rice water. The water of the first vessel is consecrated seven times with the all-activity mantra or with oṃ. The water of the second vessel is consecrated seven times with the mantra of one's own deity joined with the three syllables, or else with seven oṃs. Then, in front of yourself, set the yellow arghya water on a stand as before.

Then, to the right, on a spot that has been cleaned, set out the substances to be burned: firewood; food mixed with the three sweet substances; black sesame mixed with yogurt, butter and honey; curd rice; special offerings with butter; bilva fruit; unbroken śāli rice; rice grains; barley; wheat; rājamāṣa beans; lotuses; nāgakesara; karṇika flowers, and so on. Recite seven times the mantra of the deity to whom you are especially devoted, and with a ladle of butter make the fire blaze with splendor.

The wisdom being is to be summoned into the sky. With this mantra, oṃ aṅye mahātejaḥ sarva karma prasādhaka kārunyā kṛtvā satvārthan a sminsanni hitva bhavā, the wisdom fire god should be invited. This is done with the mantra beginning oṃ nama. Then, with the fire mantra given above alone, one should offer from one to three or seven pourings of ghee with the ladle.

Fresh wood of palāśa, udumbara, āmra (mango), kadamba, aśvattha (the sacred fig), plakṣa, śamī, nyagrodha (banyan) and other milky trees, with a pair of leaves, cut to equal length, taken from the tip, straight and free of insects, and anointed to a yellow color with fragrant nectar, should be held two at a time, evenly between the ring finger and the thumb, and offered with both ends dipped in ghee, yogurt and milk. Further, yellow woods such as khadira, arka, apāmārga, nimba and bhekaṅka should be cut together with their leaves and flowers, fresh, free of insects, and untouched by faults such as blight, and offered as above with their respective mantras. One should avoid wood that is crooked in shape, foul-smelling, hollow, rotten, overly long, without bark, short, dry, broken, split at the tip, old, bearing traces of worms, and the like.

Then, when the radiance of the fire has grown strong from the firewood, one should offer ghee, sesame, unbroken rice, yogurt, choice food, kuśa grass, dūrvā grass and other things, each with its own mantra. At the end of each mantra one should urge with oṃ amukaṃsya puṣṭiṃ kuru oṃ and then offer.

The sequence of mantras is as follows. oṃ vajra yajñāya svāhā is for udumbara. oṃ vajra bodhi pṛkṣāya svāhā is for aśvattha. oṃ vajra tāya svāhā is for plakṣa. oṃ vajra kuberāya svāhā is for nyagrodha and the other milky trees. oṃ vajras ha kārāya svāhā is for āmra. The others should be understood in the same way. oṃ sarva pāpaṃ dahana vajre ye svāhā is for sesame. oṃ vajra puṣṭa ye svāhā is for unbroken rice. oṃ sarva saṃpade svāhā is for yogurt-rice and choice food. oṃ vajra āyuṣe svāhā is for dūrvā grass. oṃ aprati hate svāhā is for kuśa grass. oṃ vajra vijāya svāhā is for paddy rice. oṃ vajra mahā bagāya svāhā is for barley. oṃ mahā bala ye svāhā is for beans. oṃ vajra ghasma ri ye svāhā is for wheat.

All the substances and their essences, arisen from hūṃ endowed with the three realities, should be offered in their proper order into the mouth of the fire god (Agni). Firewood, kuśa grass and the like should be offered into the blazing light. Mouth-rinsing water, food and the like should be offered into his hands. Flowers should be offered onto his head. Incense should be offered into the blazing light. Scent is offered at his heart. The purifying sprinkling is offered so that it pervades his body. Water for the feet is offered onto the flower seat beneath his feet. Welcoming water, lamps and food offerings are offered in front of him. If the filling ladle and pouring ladle are merely visualized, the fine substances for burning should be offered with the thumb and ring finger. Then, having blessed the hollow of the palm of the five fingers with the five syllables, one should offer the ladlefuls. The coarse substances for burning should be offered by joining the middle finger, ring finger and thumb.

Then, having completed for the fire god the sequence of foot water, purifying sprinkling and so on, because that very fire god is of the nature of the sphere of reality (dharmadhātu), the wisdom of the mind of all the Tathāgatas, and is therefore endowed with the characteristic of exceedingly profound wisdom, and in order to bring his special activity to completion, one should visualize the supramundane fire god in his heart. On a multicolored lotus, upon a sun seat, one imagines the respective seats, such as the four māras. Upon them, from the seed syllables of the respective deities, arise the hand emblems, and from the transformation of the seed syllables and emblems, the maṇḍala of glorious Hevajra, the nature of all the Tathāgatas, together with its circle, is fully generated. Then the wisdom maṇḍala should be summoned. By means of the light rays from the heart acting as hooks, one visualizes it in the sky in front:

oṃ ehyehi bhagavāna iha bhagatya samayena ja arghaṃ dhikan cha pratīccha da bucan carya prasidha me

With this mantra one expels the obstructors and performs the purifying sprinkling, the foot water and all the rest. Then with the mantra jaḥ hūṃ baṃ hoḥ the wisdom circle is summoned, made to enter, bound and brought under control, and the wisdom beings and the commitment beings become inseparable, like water and milk mixed together. Then, having performed the purification of the eyes and so on, the blessing of the body and so on, the empowerment and so on, and further having completed worship preceded by purifying sprinkling, foot water and so on, along with praises and the rest of the sequence, one visualizes on the tongue of each deity of the maṇḍala a five-pronged vajra transformed from hūṃ, and with each deity's own mantra one should offer the ladlefuls and the substances for burning in order, as before.

Then, again, at the beginning and at the end of the offering, one should make mundane and supramundane aspirations. The substances to be burned are purified by sprinkling. Then one imagines that, from the seed syllable at one's heart, light rays suited to the activity turn them into the nectar of primordial wisdom, in the color suited to the activity, and one offers them: once, or three times, or seven, or twenty-one, or a hundred, or a hundred and eight, or a thousand, or a thousand and eight, or a hundred thousand, or as many as one's means allow. One should make the fire offering as before.

To accomplish what is to be accomplished, one makes full offering of the special inner substances with the root mantra to the wisdom maṇḍala alone, and so accomplishes it. By this, the four activities and the rest are accomplished in a special way. The substances offered dissolve into the wisdom being at the heart of each maṇḍala, and so one imagines that everything has the nature of great bliss.

At the time of empowerment, in order to burn away the two obscurations of body, speech, and mind of the disciple who gives the supreme offering fee, and to complete the two accumulations, one takes hold of the disciple's left ring finger, and with the left hand one should give from three up to seven ladled oblations. Or else one should do it with the action vajra. Further, so that the accumulations of merit and wisdom of the ruler of the realm, of the Saṅgha and the rest, of the guardians of the directions and the rest, and of oneself may increase, one should offer oblations in the same numbers as before.

Having in this way made the fire offering of whatever substances have been gathered, one should give seven or twenty-one ladlefuls of butter blessed by the seed syllable at the heart.

Then, when the burnt offering is complete, preceded by the purifying sprinkling, the water for the feet, and the rest, one makes the outer, inner, secret, and suchness offerings. Further, one should make offerings of tooth-sticks free of dust, of garments such as cloth, and of ornaments, as many as one's means allow, and then praise.

Then all the deities of the maṇḍala, satisfied by the sun and moon, and satisfied by the burnt substances such as butter, become greatly delighted. By the light rays from the hearts of these deities, one visualizes bodies filling the whole expanse of space. One visualizes them holding jewel vases filled with the nectar of primordial wisdom and conferring empowerment on the practitioner and the others for the sake of accomplishment. Having made the practitioners into the nature of the vajra body, one visualizes the practitioner's palace filled with all manner of things, and the accomplishments such as the sword are also brought to completion. Such an assembly of bodies should then be gathered into the heart of the maṇḍala of deities.

Then the mantra practitioner, in meditative equipoise, fills the cupped hands with flowers and bows deeply in body, speech, and mind. In order to pacify the faults of mistakes, of what was lacking and what was excessive, and to bring the desired accomplishments to completion, one should ask for forbearance and make aspirations:

Whatever was not gathered as it should be,
what I did not know through my lowliness,
what I could not do according to the sense of your command,
and whatever fell short or went too far,
O you great beings,
please grant me your great forbearance.

Then one should recite vajra tva and the rest three times and circumambulate.

oṃ You have accomplished every aim of beings;
grant the accomplishments that accord with them.
Though you depart to the buddha-realm,
because you do not forsake the samaya and the mudrā,
please come again.

oṃ āḥ hūṃ. With the mantra vajra muḥ one should request the superior maṇḍala of deities to depart.

Then, to the earlier fire god, who is the very nature of the hearth, one should make the purifying sprinkling, the water for the feet, and the offerings as before. Then, with the oblations and the substances to be burned, in the earlier order, one should give the offering of the remainder. Then again one should make worldly aspirations over the oblation and offer it. Having made the water for the feet and the offerings, one gives tooth-sticks, cloth, and the rest, and, holding vajra and bell, one should praise. Then, having made aspirations for what one wishes and asked for forbearance, and having urged three times with oṃ satva and the rest, one should request the fire god to depart with this mantra: svaḥ arthan cā bā parārtha cā sādhitaṃ gaccha ha bya bhu ka āgamiṣya pi yathā kāle sarva siddhi kuru ṣa me.

Then, at the fire in front, in a well-washed vessel, one combines the remainder of the oblations of the maṇḍala deities with butter, milk, honey, sugar, choice food, and the rest. Having prepared it well through the yoga of the wheel of deities, one makes full offering of it oneself to the root maṇḍala in the manner of the secret offering. Then, further, one should give a little at a time to those who possess the yoga of the deity.

Then one recites the verses of auspiciousness beginning "Perfect," recites the hundred-syllable mantra three times, and should dedicate the root of virtue entirely to perfect and complete awakening. Then one rises and, reciting the hundred-syllable mantra, should circumambulate. It is this: oṃ vajrasatva samaya manupālaya vajrasatva tveno patiṣṭha dṛḍho me bhava sutoṣyo me bhava supoṣyo me bhava anurkto me bhava sarva siddhiṃ me prayaccha sarva karma su ca me cittaṃ śreyaḥ kuru hūṃ ha ha ha ha hoḥ bhagavāna sarva tathāgata vajra mā me muñja vajrī bhava mahā samaya satva āḥ.

When one performs the pacifying fire offering oneself, then, in order to pacify illness, harm, hindrances, obstructing spirits, mental afflictions, and the other hosts of Māra, for the sake of oneself and others, and in order to attain the higher knowledges and the supreme accomplishment, one should undertake the pacifying fire offering.

Then, to the east of the great maṇḍala, on white cloth, one marks out a circle one cubit across. It is dug to a depth of twelve finger-widths. In its center one carves an eight-petaled lotus of sixteen finger-widths, and on it one sets the emblem of the deity. From its central rim one extends four finger-widths outward, and one should build up a rim four finger-widths high. It should be surrounded by a border of five-pronged vajras eight finger-widths wide. Outside that, it should be surrounded by the pacifying offerings, such as white-colored ground. It is plastered well with white color, and over that one should smear the five products of the cow.

Over that, one should apply a coating of white fragrances, such as white sandalwood, together with the five nectars. The hearth should be strewn all over with loose white, sweet-smelling flowers. Alternatively, the maṇḍala, built on firmly tamped ground, should be drawn with colored powders. Then a vessel of gold or the like should be filled to the brim with white-colored scent and with water into which milk and barley have been poured; it should be consecrated as before with the mantra ending in svāhā and set in place. The fire offering should be performed with firewood from milky trees and dung fuel of the white-colored kind, and with curd, butter, honey, milk, barley, parched rice, priyaṅgu, sandalwood, the choicest śāli rice, fresh kuśa grass, dūrvā grass, white flowers, saffron, mango leaves, white and fragrant tree resin, and the like.

The kindling sticks, of palāśa or similar wood, should be twelve fingers long and about as thick as the little finger. They should be taken from the upper part of the tree, be fresh and free of insects, and be soaked in the five nectars and in fragrant water; both ends are then dipped in honey and milk, and the collection of offerings is presented as before. Fire produced by churning with a fire drill of bodhi-tree wood, or else fire brought from a brahmin's house, should be kindled by the rite described before. The fire god should be visualized as white in color. His ornaments and garments are white, and all his characteristics are the same as above.

The mantrin, adorned with white garments and ornaments, his hair bound up on top of his head and crowned with flowers and white cloth, endowed with loving-kindness and compassion, sits on a lotus seat in the auspicious posture, facing east. With a peaceful mind, he should begin, intent solely on white and wholesome conduct. The peaceful fire offering should be performed with mantras that have svāhā at the end of each, and with the mantra containing the words of urging, "So-and-so śātniṃ kuru svāhā."

Then the deities, satisfied with the butter, the nectars and the rest, are delighted. From the heart of each of them, a maṇḍala of wisdom beings, white in color by nature, is emanated into space, and these deities, holding jewel vases filled with the nectar of wisdom, are visualized conferring empowerment on the practitioner and the others, and on the patron together with his retinue. Having removed every harm, such as disease, one should bring one's own desired accomplishment to completion as before. Then the maṇḍala of wisdom beings should be gathered in and dissolved into one's heart. One should imagine that all who have entered into this are endowed with happiness. This is the procedure of the peaceful fire offering.

Then further, in order to accomplish the sword, the wheel, the arrow, the vajra, the jewel, the lotus and the great treasure, to accomplish elixirs and special elixirs, and to obtain the qualities of a Cakravartin king, one should undertake the fire offering of increase on the fifteenth day of the month, for one's own sake or for that of others. For this, to the south of the great maṇḍala, on yellow earth, a pit two cubits wide and one cubit deep should be dug. A rim eight fingers wide is made to extend outward and is built up eight fingers high. In the middle, on the hub of an eight-spoked wheel, the emblem of the deity is placed, and there a vajra, a jewel, a lotus and a sword are also set out. The rim is surrounded on all sides by blazing jewels six fingers in size, and the hearth is yellow and square, or else made eight-cornered in the shape of a blazing jewel. It is smeared with yellow earth, and over that it is smeared well with the five nectars and yellow scent. Over that, it should be strewn all over with loose yellow, sweet-smelling flowers, or with flowers of various kinds. The fuel should be made of purified dung of wild animals and of the trees mentioned before, together with their fruit. The kindling sticks, of palāśa or similar wood, are taken from the middle part of the tree; they are one finger-joint thick and one cubit long, with squared tips. The rest should be understood as in the fire offering for all activities. This is the procedure of the fire offering of increase.

Then, in order to accomplish such activities as killing, drying up, expelling, dividing, paralyzing, stupefying and cutting the body into pieces, one should undertake the fire offering of the fierce rite. For this, to the north of the great maṇḍala, on extremely rough ground where no sprouts grow, black as water crystal or as a rain cloud, a pit twenty fingers across and ten fingers deep is dug, and in its center a wrathful vajra eight fingers in size is made. Then a rim three fingers wide is set outward and built up three fingers high, and the pit is surrounded on all sides with this three-finger band. Its point faces south. The hearth is made black, and it should be smeared with black earth, bone powder from a charnel ground, the dung of donkeys, camels and the like, and every kind of poison. In the center, the deity's emblem is set out, the rim is surrounded with swords or three-pronged vajras, and the hearth should be spread as a floor with husks and black flowers.

Then the vessel described before is filled to the brim with the urine of hawks, humans, oxen, donkeys and camels, and with blood. The syllable phaṭ is recited over it as before, and it should be placed on the right, upon a fire maṇḍala. White mustard seed, pungent oil, blood, salt, kétsé mustard seed, human bone powder, earth from footprints, neem bark, the dung of donkeys, camels and the like, and hair: these should be pounded together and made into an effigy. It should be smeared well with the feathers of crows and similar birds, excrement and urine, thorns, potsherds, bone powder, the great fat, the great flesh and the like, blood, poison, kétsé mustard seed, and the juice of an intoxicating plant. Then the all-activities water is consecrated and, after sprinkling and purifying, one visualizes all of these as the target. His bodily vital points are smeared all over with poison, kétsé mustard seed and the like, and he is offered into the fire. A lamp of great black oil should be placed on the left. Pungent, thorny wood, bitter and foul-smelling wood, wood from a charnel ground, and the dry dung of donkeys, camels and the like should be used as fuel.

Then take wood of nimba, khadira, kovidāra, the bse tree, the madana tree, pungent trees, thorny trees, bitter trees, or else wood used for burning corpses. Taken in the proper portion, ten finger-widths in length, and with their tips made three-cornered, these should be smeared well with poison, mustard seed and blood, and made into kindling sticks.

Then fire from a charnel ground, fire from charnel-ground bones, fire from a fire-drill base and drill made of wood used for burning corpses, fire from stone, and fire taken from the house of an outcaste (caṇḍāla): these fires should be kindled. There the fire god is black in color, his hair blazing upward, his eyes like heaps of glowing embers. His mouth gapes, baring his fangs, a terrifying face with wrathful frowns. He is exceedingly fierce, of greatly terrifying form, with a large belly and short, thick limbs. He wears black cloth slung across his body as a sash, is adorned all over with black ornaments, and blazes like the fire at the time of the world's destruction. His mouth is identical with the hearth.

Then the mantra practitioner, his mind exceedingly fierce, his upper body covered with black charnel-ground cloth, adorned with blue ornaments, wearing a garland of blue flowers slung across his body, his lower body wrapped in cloth smeared with charnel-ground blood, his eyes blazing fiercely and his face exceedingly wrathful, sitting in the manner of the left foot pressing down the right, gnashing his teeth, should sit with a fierce mind upon the effigy of the target. Or else he should sit in the manner described before upon a black charnel-ground brick, pressing down on the heart of the target. The target is visualized as having lost his color, his countenance changed, utterly ruined. Or else, at the heart of the target, with a dance of stretching and bending, facing directly toward the direction of the rite: one who acts as an enemy of the Three Jewels, reviles the master, causes division in the mantra tantras, and is malicious toward all sentient beings, such a one should be subjected to the fierce rite with a mind of great compassion in wrathful form. Moreover, it is taught solely for the sake of the practitioner.

Here, at any time during the dark fortnight, at noon or at midnight, the violent action should be begun. Visualize hosts of wrathful ones emerging from one's own heart, holding various kinds of weapons, by means such as cutting the target to pieces and devouring him. With the threatening forefinger of the left hand, and especially with the blazing left eye, one should gaze, and reciting phaṭ amukasya abhicarya kuru phaṭ, one should perform the fire offering of the fierce rite.

The particular actions are distinguished by the application of urging and by the application of mantra. For causing division: oṃ ji svāhā hūṃ amukaṃ bedveṣaya hūṃ. For expelling: oṃ khaṃ svāhā hūṃ amukaṃ ucchaṭaya hūṃ. For paralysis and stupefaction: oṃ hūṃ svāhā boṣatta amukaṃ staṃbhaya mohaya biśodha. For killing: oṃ ghuḥ svāhā phaṭ amukaṃ maraya phaṭ. Elsewhere too it should be understood in the same way.

Then, from the hearts of the deities who have been satisfied by the fire offering, countless hosts of wrathful ones are emanated, and these wrathful ones rain vajra thunderbolts without interruption upon the body of the target, and rain various kinds of weapons upon the target's body. Having swiftly liberated that very target, one should send him, by the application of the three syllables, to the realm of Sukhāvatī. The emanated wrathful ones are gathered back into the heart right there. For accomplishment alone, one should perform the approach on the wisdom maṇḍala with the root mantra. This is the sequence of the violent fire offering.

In order to bring under control or to summon gods, nāgas, yakṣas, gandharvas, garuḍas, asuras, kinnaras and the like, together with their ranks, kings together with their retinues, and whatever object one is acting upon, one should begin the fire offering of control or of summoning.

Here, to the east or to the west of the great maṇḍala, one makes the hearth for summoning. With red earth it is made in the shape of a bhaga, or like a half moon, with a lotus and vajra blessed in the center, its rim encircled with a garland of lotuses, and in the center the emblem of the deity is drawn with red sandalwood. For summoning, it is marked in the center with a single-pointed vajra and a vajra hook, and its rim is encircled with hooks. The rim, depth and so forth of that hearth are the same as for the peaceful hearth.

Having made the hearth in this way, one plasters it with red earth, smears it well over that with red scent, and there scatters loose red flowers. Having cleansed it well with branches of the plantain and the like, one fills the vessel described earlier with water containing red scent together with red flowers, blesses it with a mantra ending in hoḥ, and places it on the right for summoning and on the left for bringing under control.

From priyaṅgu, campaka, punnāga, nāgakesara, lakuca and aśoka, from a lone tree standing in the middle of a forest, having flowers and red fruit, kindling sticks are made from its western branch, five finger-widths in length, possessing all the qualities explained above and untainted by faults. Or else they should be made from the leaves of a tree of sour taste and red color that grows red flowers and fruit. For bringing under control, they are placed on the right side, upon the ground of the half moon. For summoning, thorny wood shaped like hooks should be placed on the left side, upon the ground of the hook.

For firewood one should use nimba, emblic myrobalan, sour-tasting trees, and trees with red flowers together with fruit. These are equal to the size of the hearth. For summoning, it is done with thorny wood. The fire should be taken from the house of a prostitute and the house of a woman dyer. For summoning, it should be taken from the house of an oil-presser or from a merchants' inn.

The fire god's body is red in color, adorned with an upper garment, a lower wrap and all red ornaments, wearing a garland of red flowers slung across his body, and seated on a red lotus seat. His other characteristics are the same, as in the rite explained above.

For this, the mantra practitioner wears red clothing, a garland of red flowers, and a crest made of red cloth. He anoints his body with red scents such as saffron and sandalwood. He gives up thoughts of anger and pride, and takes on a red complexion, endowed with the application of magnetizing. With the self-arisen fluid (svayambhū) of a courtesan he draws the form of a source of phenomena at his own heart. Then, facing west, he should sit in the vajra posture. For summoning, one should gaze slightly upward.

The mantra for magnetizing is this: oṃ ā svāhā hoḥ amukaṃ vaśaṃ kuru hoḥ. For summoning it is this: oṃ hūṃ svāhā jaḥ amukaṃ ākarṣaya jaḥ. Reciting these mantras in full, one should perform the fire offering for magnetizing or for summoning at midnight. From the hearts of the deities, satisfied and delighted by the fire offering, one emanates countless hosts of deities whose color matches the activity. They hold bows made of garlands of red flowers and arrows made of red utpala stems. Drawing them all the way back to the right side of the nose, they shoot them into the heart of whoever is to be brought under control, draw that person in, and bring him under control. For summoning, they bind whoever one wishes to summon at the neck with a noose of red utpala stems, seize him at the heart with a hook of red flowers, swiftly draw him in, and place him beneath the practitioner's feet. One then gathers back the emanated deities and meditates that they dissolve into one's own heart. This is the sequence of the fire offering for magnetizing and summoning.

To complete the activities as they were taught earlier, one should perform the fire offering of each activity in the way known from the fire offering for all activities. If one asks why: to please the deities and to lengthen life, one should offer dūrvā grass dipped in ghee. Further, for protection, one should dip kuśa grass in ghee and make offerings to the remaining deities. At the end of the mantra, one should perform the fire offering knowing the sequence as it has been taught, according to the distinctions of the urging syllables, the substances, the visualization, the mantra, and the generating seed syllables. One should begin the fire offerings of the four activities with an intention guided by the particular days, times, and circumstances suited to pacifying and the rest. It is further stated:

With joy, perform the fire offering for enriching;
When all is complete, perform pacifying;
To slay the vicious, perform slaying;
For magnetizing and other aims, make supplication.

The mantra practitioner performing the outer fire offering, endowed with deity yoga, should purify the seat and all the substances by contemplating everything from loving-kindness up to emptiness. Here, by setting out the ultimate truth and the conventional truth, one should understand the substances and the forms of the deities as having the nature of the two truths. Here, with a prior intention to bring śrāvakas, pratyekabuddhas, and others to complete the path of the Great Vehicle, and so that persons of lesser, middling, and supreme capacity may attain the fruit of complete liberation, one leads them onto this path of the fruit and so leads them into perfect and complete awakening. On this it is said:

The fire offering has three characteristics,
And it possesses three kinds of circumstance.
When it has the three acts of the rite,
The fire offering is the Tathāgata.
Whoever undertakes it lacking the characteristics,
Performing the fire offering without knowing the circumstances,
Has abandoned mantra and wisdom;
For that great fool there is no fruit.

If one undertakes the rite endowed with all the characteristics taught above, all its accomplishments come swiftly. Further, to make the accomplishments come very swiftly, one should remain one-pointed in mind until each fire offering is complete. The hearth, the kuśa grass, the flowers, the offering water and the rest; the filling ladle and the pouring ladle; the wood, the fire god (Agni), the maṇḍala deities, the offerings and the rest; oneself and everything that arises in its various forms: in order to purify the afflictive obscurations and the obscurations to knowledge, one generates discriminating wisdom, and in order to realize the expanse of phenomena, one makes the mind one-pointed.

Next, what remains to be explained:
I set out the fruits of each burnt substance.
Whatever I have not set out
Should be learned from the guru's instruction.
To enter a pure buddha-field,
One takes possession of the ground.
By the hearth one gains the celestial palace;
Ghee ripens the fruit;
Kindling sticks give rise to splendor;
The firewood is the great hero;
Kuśa grass protects all;
White mustard seed pacifies;
Unbroken rice brings increase;
Sesame burns away all evil;
Millet grants the accomplishment one aims at;
Black gram produces great strength;
Barley gives the accomplishment of swift feet;
Dūrvā grass lengthens life;
Wheat pacifies illnesses;
Milk with honey increases wisdom;
Curd and rice grant everything;
Fire grants the eight accomplishments
And whatever accomplishment one desires;
Through the maṇḍala of the chosen deity
The supreme accomplishment is attained;
By the rest one knows the particular activities:
They are the accomplishments of pacifying and the others.
The filling ladle has the nature of wisdom;
The pouring ladle is rightly declared to be means;
Their union is nondual gnosis;
The nondual essence that drips from it
Is held to be the nectar of great gnosis.
Through that union one honors the fire god;
I too have explained it through that union.
If one undertakes the fire offering in this way,
By it the supreme accomplishment comes about.

This is the sequence of the fire offering rite.

When the worship of the fire offering is thus complete, one should begin the disciples' entry into the maṇḍala. For this, the mantra practitioner looks directly upon the wheel of deities, worships it properly with offerings in the earlier sequence, praises it, and then performs the tasting of the nectar and so forth.

Then, in order to take the disciples into care through empowerment and the other rites, the master himself enters the maṇḍala by the procedure for introducing a disciple into the maṇḍala, and takes up the vows in their proper order. The master should thus obtain permission for the ritual act. Then, in order to ripen the disciple in the maṇḍala, he should call to mind, just as it is, the body of primordial wisdom: the nondual suchness of all phenomena, the essence of Vajradhara whose nature is great undefiled bliss, the very nature of the wheel of the maṇḍala. Then he should make supplication with these words:

Arisen from space, endowed with the marks,
Supreme one without beginning or end,
Whose nature is the great vajra being,
O Hevajra, grant me accomplishment.

Supreme of all, the great accomplishment,
Great lord Maheśvara, the surpassing deity,
Vajra-holder, king of all,
Grant me accomplishment in the supreme and unchanging.

You are without fault, and you are permanent too,
Attached with the attachment of all desire,
Great passion, great joy,
O Blessed One, by that very nature grant accomplishment.

Utmost joy, highest of all,
The Tathāgata who goes from the very beginning,
Samantabhadra, the self of all,
Grant me accomplishment as a bodhisattva.

Great accomplishment, king of all,
Greatly praised as the accomplished vajra
By the supreme king of great lords,
O lord of vajra pride, take me as your own.

You pervade the minds of all beings,
You dwell in the hearts of all beings,
You are the very father of all beings.
By that truth of yours, who have become the supreme pledge of passion,
Whereby supreme primordial wisdom,
Wisdom and skillful means, are the maṇḍala,
By that truth, O protector,
Fulfill completely all that I desire.

Phenomena are like reflections,
Pure and clear, without turbidity,
Ungraspable and inexpressible,
Arising entirely from causes and from karma,
Born with certainty from suchness.
By this truth, may the disciples
All see clearly, free of stain,
The reflection in the maṇḍala.

Then the master comes out beyond the curtain. The excellent disciple, preceded by a maṇḍala offering, presents a supreme fee, bows his head at the master's feet, and, wishing to obtain the master empowerment, should make supplication:

Bestow on me the empowerment of the wheel
Of no turning back, and all that goes with it.
Bestow on me, a master of the rites,
The wheel of the purity of the deities.
The pledges of all the buddhas
And the perfect secret vow
I shall hold, and for the sake of all beings
I myself shall always act as master.

He should make this supplication three times. Then the master who has been so supplicated should supplicate the deities of the maṇḍala and obtain their permission in his mind:

I, who am called such-and-such,
Shall take up the supreme mind of awakening,
And, fully possessed of the secret vow,
I wish to enter the great maṇḍala.

When the master has said this, he says:

O great beings, you who are here,
Into the palace that is supreme,
Of the pure family of the great secret,
Do you wish to enter today?

And the disciple replies, "I ask for your great kindness."

Then the master bathes the disciple, anoints his limbs with perfume, and makes his mouth fragrant with tooth powder and the like. He makes him a crest of flowers and red cloth, blesses a red cloth and covers his face with it. Together with a supreme fee, he has him hold a garland of flowers blessed with the mantra a khaṃ bīra hūṃ, and with that same mantra sprinkles him with purifying water from the vase of all activities. He regards him as his own chosen deity, blesses his body, eyes and so forth, and then, in order to introduce him into the maṇḍala, he should question him outside the curtain. He asks, "What happiness do you desire?" The disciple answers thus: "Praising you, Great Joy, I ask for the great protector." Having said this, he honors the guru's feet with flowers and the like. Then, drawn in with hūṃ and having circumambulated three times, he is placed at the eastern gate of the maṇḍala, and a vajra is placed at his heart. With oṃ vajra citta utpādayāmi the mind of yoga is fully generated. With oṃ surata samaya staṃ hoḥ siddha vajra yathā sukhaṃ the vajra together with the flower is placed at his heart. Then one should say the following: "Today, by the blessing of all the tathāgatas, do not speak of this supreme secret of all the tathāgatas to those who have not seen the maṇḍala, nor to those who lack faith."

Then the disciple is summoned with the mantra of all activities or with the mantra of Ṭakki, and with the mantra of a khaṃ bī he is brought inside the curtain. The disciple bows to the master and to the maṇḍala and should say this: "oṃ If I offer my body in service to all the tathāgatas, what need is there to speak of anything else? oṃ If I offer my body in service to the vajra master, what need is there to speak of anything else? oṃ If I offer my body for the sake of protecting all sentient beings, what need is there to speak of anything else?"

When the disciple has made this supplication, the master should then proclaim the three pledges: "Today, since you have become one of the family of all the tathāgatas, today I shall generate in you vajra-like primordial wisdom, and nothing else. Since through that primordial wisdom one attains the accomplishment of all the tathāgatas, what need is there to speak of other accomplishments? Because I shall generate such primordial wisdom, do not speak of it to those who have not seen the maṇḍala, or your pledge will be broken. ta re hūṃ"

Then he places a vajra on the crown of the disciple's head:

This is the vajra of the pledge,
Now placed upon the crown of your head.
If you should speak of this way,
Your head will split into a hundred pieces
And be destroyed, without a doubt.

Then he places the vajra at the disciple's heart:

oṃ Today Vajrasattva
Has been properly placed within your heart.
If you should speak of this way,
He will at once tear it apart and depart.

Having thus proclaimed the pledges,
water endowed with the five nectars,
blessed with the three syllables,
is placed in a lotus vessel,

and is consecrated seven times with oṃ vajrodaka ṭhaḥ ṭhaḥ ṭhaḥ. Then:

Drink this water of hell!
If you transgress the pledge, it will burn you.
If you keep the pledge, accomplishment
is won through this water of vajra nectar.

So saying, one recites oṃ vajrodaka ṭhaḥ ṭhaḥ ṭhaḥ.

The master should teach the pledge,
and to make the commitment firm he says this:

"From this day on I am your Vajrapāṇi, so whatever I tell you to do, you must do. Do not hold me in contempt. Otherwise, with misfortune not averted, you will die of a strange disease and fall into hell."

Then the disciple offers the master the supreme gift and makes this request: "May all the tathāgatas bless me! Glorious Vajrasattva, please descend upon me!"

When he has said this, one meditates on a square earth maṇḍala at his heart, arisen from laṃ. It is yellow and square, marked with a three-pronged vajra, and blessed at its center by hūṃ. At his throat one meditates on a water maṇḍala generated from vaṃ. It is white and round, marked with a vase, and blessed by aḥ. Then at the crown of his head one should imagine a wind maṇḍala generated from yaṃ. It is blue and shaped like a bow, marked with a banner, and blessed at its center by ha. Below his feet is the syllable jhaiḥ, blazing in nature, and on it a wind maṇḍala. On that one imagines a fire maṇḍala arisen from a red raṃ, triangular and blazing in nature. Applying its blazing rays and sounding the bell, one should make the wisdom descend:

oṃ āveśaya stambhayar ra calaya calaya hūṃ ha a jhaiḥ

Urging it with this mantra, one should make it descend, continuing until it has arrived. Alternatively, it is taught that one makes it descend by urging it into the heart with the wind maṇḍala located below the disciple's feet, or that one makes it descend by focusing on the special syllables. At that very time, with the strength of intense divine pride, one performs dance and music accompanied by the sound of bells and so forth, and reciting oṃ lole lole hūṃ ā, one should make it descend, continuing until it has arrived.

When one sees that it has arrived, one should visualize the disciple in the form of Rāgavajra and meditate on a red a on his tongue. Then:

"O Rāgavajra, abiding here,
speak, vajra, of good and ill!"

After that one asks him about his experience and performs the rite for stabilizing the wisdom that has descended:

oṃ vajra tiṣṭha vajra samaya pratipālite hūṃ hūṃ ho ho

When the wisdom has been stabilized with this mantra, the being should be blessed.

For entering the maṇḍala:

Today, by the blessing of truth,
I have summoned the disciple.
As his merit is, so is his deity
in this sequence of families.
Whichever family within is best for him,
may it strike that in this maṇḍala.

Then one should ask about the appearance of colors in the disciple's field of vision. According to whichever appearance of white, yellow, red, or black light arises in his mind, one should indicate the rite of pacifying, increasing, subjugating, or fierce action. Then, with the power of hūṃ, saying pratīccha vajra hoḥ, he should cast the flower garland he was given to hold earlier.

By the way the flower falls
one should examine superior and middling accomplishment.
By which particular deity
it falls upon, examine the particular accomplishment.
If it falls on the crown protuberance of a buddha,
the accomplishment of the great seal (mahāmudrā)
will be won; of this there is no doubt.
If it falls straight on the eyes or face,
the accomplishment of secret mantra recitation is won.
Falling on the upper body
is taught as the supreme accomplishment of that deity.
One whose flower falls on the lower body
has middling accomplishment, said the Sage.
By the supreme, buddhahood is attained;
by the middling, lordship and the like,
or else the accomplishment of treasure, is won.
In this same sequence, accomplishments
are examined likewise for Nairātmyā too.
For all the deities of the circle
it should be known through direct perception.
If, of a hundred flower casts,
one lands far from the figure,
accomplishment comes in its own time.
If the flower is near, accomplishment too
is said to come near in time.
One whose flower falls upon a family of deities
is the fortunate lord of that deity's mantra practice;
therefore a name in keeping with the family should be given.
If the cast falls on the border between deities,
whichever one it lies nearer to
should be known as his lord deity.
If, of a hundred flower casts,
one falls outside the circle,
he has no fortune for accomplishment
from the deities of the maṇḍala.
If it falls on the border between the garlands,
then first performing a pacifying fire offering,
the rite of burning away sins,
the flower cast should be repeated.
If he doubts and has no faith,
he should not be allowed to cast the flower,
because he has no fortune for accomplishment.
If it falls squarely on a border,
or bounces up and falls
outside the maṇḍala,
the wise one should first perform an auspicious rite
and then have it repeated.
In all flower casts,
when it strikes within the palace,
since he is fortunate,
the distinctions of superior and middling
are determined by the divisions of the lines.
If, by such a sequence,
it has not fallen properly,
and after three repetitions it still does not strike,
he has no fortune to be admitted.

This is the procedure for casting the flower.

Then, having learned the identity of his deity from where the flower fell, one takes the flower garland, recites this mantra, and ties it on his crown:

prati sri hna sva mi maṃ satva mahā bala

That is the empowerment of the flower garland.

Then one meditates on a radiant oṃ on the disciple's eyes and says:

oṃ Vajrasattva today
strives to open your eyes.
He opens all eyes:
the unsurpassed vajra eye.

So saying, one unties the blindfold and shows him the maṇḍala of Hevajra, saying: "So-and-so, look well at the maṇḍala of Hevajra!" Then one should show it to him in sequence, with the deities' names, body colors, and so forth.

Then the master, delighted, says this to the disciple:

O, look upon this excellent maṇḍala!
Now give rise to intense faith.
Through the blessing of mantra and mudrā,
you too are born into the family of the buddhas.
Because you have seen this unprecedented distinction,
be devoted to the guru,
guard all the pledges (samayas),
and strive in the secret mantra.
And so you are born as a son of the heart.

Then the disciple bows with the crown of his head at the guru's feet and says this:

oṃ In the supreme maṇḍala
of the great vajra maṇḍala I abide.
oṃ The maṇḍala of great yoga,
the great maṇḍala, I behold today.
oṃ In the great secret maṇḍala,
in the maṇḍala, I am empowered.

This is the stage of introducing the disciple into the maṇḍala.

Then, so that the disciple may be blessed with good fortune, the master places him on a maṇḍala platform at the eastern gate of the maṇḍala and should recite this song of auspiciousness:

Endowed with every excellence, like a mountain of gold,
protector of the three worlds, who has cast off the three stains,
the Buddha, whose eyes are like the petals of a blossoming lotus:
this is the first auspiciousness, which brings peace to beings.
The supreme and unmoving, taught by him,
renowned in the three worlds and worshipped by gods and humans,
the sublime Dharma, which brings peace to beings:
this is the second auspiciousness, the virtue of the world.
The Saṅgha, endowed with the sublime Dharma, rich in the auspiciousness of learning,
the object of worship of gods, humans and asuras,
supreme among assemblies, the ground of modesty and glory:
this is the third auspiciousness, the virtue of the world.

Then the disciple, holding flowers, should make this request:

Just as Bodhivajra made
the great offering and request to the buddhas,
so that I too may be fully protected,
O Space Vajra, bestow it on me!

Having made this request, the disciple is placed upon an eight-petaled lotus. Focusing on Akṣobhya, one visualizes that all the tathāgatas, having taken the form of Hevajra, and the hosts of consorts, holding jewel vases filled with the nectar of wisdom, confer empowerment on the disciple:

In order to empower you as the great vajra,
arising from the three secrets
of all the tathāgatas,
to whom the three realms bow down,
today I confer empowerment on you.

Holding the vase with the vajra fist, one confers the empowerment, saying oṃ vajra abhiṣiñca, and empowers him as a universal monarch (cakravartin). In this sequence, this is the victory vase. Second, one should confer empowerment with the vases of the boundaries, those of Gaurī and the others. Having the nature of mirror-like wisdom, this is the water empowerment.

Then, visualizing the disciple as the Jewel King, one places on the crown of his head a crown ornament of gold or the like, visualizes it as the deities of that family, and confers empowerment. Conferring the empowerment with the mantra and words given above, one summons all the tathāgatas of the ten directions and dissolves them into the crown ornament. This, having the nature of the wisdom of equality, is the crown empowerment.

Then, visualizing the disciple in the form of Amitābha, one generates Amitābha in a five-pronged vajra and says:

The vajra empowerment of the buddhas,
the indivisible vajra, clear light,
the gathering of all the buddhas,
having become the gateway, the source of the pledges:
take hold of this vajra!

Touching it to his crown, throat, heart and back, one places the vajra in his hand and confers empowerment as before. This, having the nature of discriminating wisdom, is the vajra empowerment.

Then, visualizing the disciple in the form of Amoghasiddhi, one generates the bell as Amoghasiddhi and places it in his left hand. Embracing him completely with the hands holding vajra and bell, one says a vajra adhipati tvaṃ abhiṣiñcāmi tiṣṭha vajra samaya stvaṃ, and confers empowerment as before. This is the wisdom of accomplished activity, the bell empowerment.

Having released the vajra and bell, one confers the lordship empowerment. Visualizing the disciple in the form of Vairocana, one holds the vajra and bell on the crown of his head, says oṃ vajrasatva stvaṃ abhiṣiñca vajranāma abhiṣeka ta, and confers empowerment: "O glorious vajra so-and-so, because you have received the name empowerment in this great maṇḍala, in the future you will become the Vajradhara named such-and-such." This is the empowerment having the nature of the wisdom of the dharmadhātu.

Through these empowerments one is permitted to hear the mantras and tantras and to perform all the rites of practice, and permitted to explain all the sādhanas and the rest. These are the empowerments of the practitioner, the five knowledge empowerments. "Knowledge" means that because ignorance is abandoned and the five wisdoms are attained, they are the knowledge empowerments.

Whatever appears
has as its nature the chief, Heruka.
Phenomena are each pure in themselves;
view them thus as the wisdom of the buddhas.
The knowledge empowerments are common awakening;
the special awakening
of the perfectly complete buddhas is not thereby attained.

Therefore one who wishes to attain perfect and complete awakening in the future should receive the master empowerment. One who possesses the common vows, preceded by the special vows of a master, should be given the vajra discipline; prophecy and authorization are not given. Why is that? Because it has the nature of the irreversible stage. Moreover, some masters say that prophecy and encouragement are given according to the particular inclination of the individual, and that, just as at the time of conferring the vajra discipline empowerment, only prophecy and encouragement are given, not authorization. Furthermore, other masters say that, taking inclination as primary, the authorization too is given with the five verses beginning "For the sake of all sentient beings."

Then, further, one trains the disciple in the mudrā of his own deity, recites the root mantra or the heart mantra one hundred and eight times, and gives the disciple the authorization.

Into this supreme path, which all buddhas approach,
I shall rightly bring you, my son.
The whole sequence of stages, just as it was taught,
I grant to you as authorization.

Then the disciple says this:
"The heart of the Blessed One Vajradhara,
this knowledge, I shall uphold."

When this has been said, the master recites the authorization mantra one hundred and eight times and then instructs the disciple: "Do not teach this to those who do not know the mantras, the mudrās, primordial wisdom and the suchness of the self, who do not know the suchness of the maṇḍala, and who have not seen the maṇḍala. Because both you and the one to whom it is taught would then have entered into it and taught it out of attachment and aversion, you would certainly fall into hell." This is how the disciple should be instructed. This is the rite of the knowledge empowerment, a branch of the empowerment.

Then, when the disciple has obtained the knowledge empowerment and has taken up the vow of the master, he should bow at the feet of the guru, offer the supreme fee, and make this supplication:

"Just as Bodhivajra
petitioned the Buddha for the stūpa,
so that I too may be fully protected,
grant me the vajra of space."

Having made this supplication three times, he is to be brought into the maṇḍala as follows. At the eastern gate of the great maṇḍala, upon a drawn eight-petaled lotus, a special seat is arranged and the disciple is placed there. Oneself, endowed with the yoga of the deity, sends rays of light from one's heart out into the expanse of space, visualizes all the buddhas of the ten directions in the form of Heruka, and invites them with special offerings, parasols, victory banners, pennants, all kinds of music, and the singing of all kinds of auspicious songs. One visualizes them holding jeweled vases filled with the nectar of primordial wisdom and conferring empowerment, and one should confer the empowerment with these words:

"The very nature of the mind of the buddhas,
empowered by the great vajra,
to which the three realms pay homage,
this supreme empowerment endowed with the three secrets,
in which all buddhas are gathered,
I shall now confer on you as well."

Having said this, one should raise the lotus vessel and confer the empowerment.

Then one should teach the disciple the suchness of the maṇḍala, the suchness of the deities, and the tasks and activities of the master, as follows.

It is a maṇḍala because it takes up the pure essence, because it fully holds meditative absorption, and because it shows the characteristics of the enjoyment body. It is a celestial palace because the appearances of the mind are pure by nature. It is four-sided because the mind rests in meditative equipoise. The four lines are through the purity of loving-kindness, compassion, joy and equanimity. The line is the recollection of the Dharma. The subsidiary lines are because the wrong view of a real body, the inferior wrong view, the wrong view of engaging in moral discipline, and the wrong view that departs from what is correct have been abandoned.

Vows, acting for the benefit of sentient beings, and gathering virtuous qualities: the purity of the collection of these is the various ornaments. The minds of faith, diligence, mindfulness, meditative absorption and wisdom are the five colors.

Abandoning nonvirtuous qualities that have arisen is the eastern portico. Not giving rise to nonvirtuous qualities that have not arisen is the southern portico. Giving rise to virtuous qualities that have not arisen is the western portico. Guarding virtuous qualities that have arisen is the northern portico.

The purity of the close placements of mindfulness on the body, feelings, mind and phenomena is the ledge of sense pleasures. Through the purity of the retentions of patience, of the Dharma, of meaning and of mantra, there are the hosts of goddesses abiding with the deities, the singers and the dancers.

The liberation of emptiness is the eastern gate. The liberation of signlessness is the southern gate. The liberation of wishlessness is the western gate. The liberation of the uncompounded is the northern gate.

Investigation as a basis of miraculous power is the pair of jeweled pillars in the east. Diligence as a basis of miraculous power is the pair of jeweled pillars in the south. Mind as a basis of miraculous power is the pair of jeweled pillars in the west. Aspiration as a basis of miraculous power is the pair of jeweled pillars in the north.

The full vases are because of impartial love for all and the completion of the accumulations of merit and primordial wisdom. Mindfulness, discernment of phenomena, pliancy, diligence, joy, meditative absorption and equanimity are the seven factors of awakening; they are the garlands of flowers. The purity of the abandonment of all obscurations, of meditative absorption, of the complete realization of wisdom, and of the primordial wisdom of the path of the Tathāgatas is the fly-whisks. The nets are because the seeds of the afflictions have been destroyed and one is free of the afflictions. The half-nets are the destruction of and freedom from the secondary afflictions. The purity of mirror-like primordial wisdom and the others is the mirrors. The lotus is because all beings are indivisible in the nature of the Dharma and because it is unstained by the faults of saṃsāra. The purity of the awakening mind, which comes forth from all sentient beings yet does not truly arise individually, is the moon. The wisdom and primordial wisdom born of vajra-like meditative absorption is the sun disk. The ledge of sense pleasures is because the five sense pleasures are properly taken onto the path and because fixation on form and the rest is abandoned. The special offerings are because they are the concordant result of the primordial wisdom of the utterly pure sphere of phenomena. It is adorned with various swords because of conscientiousness and a sense of shame.

The Dharma distinguished by the Vinaya, the class of discourses, the class of verses sung in prose and verse, the class of expositions, the class of verses, the class of inspired utterances, the class of narratives of deeds, the class of birth stories, the class of extensive teachings: the purity of these twelve branches of the scriptures is the bells ringing on the pennants stirred by the wind.

The purity of the flesh eye, the divine eye, the wisdom eye, the Dharma eye and the buddha eye is the five lines (pañcarekhā).

In the same way, the suchness of the maṇḍala of Nairātmyā should also be understood.

Moreover, there is this further distinction. The eight pillars are the purity of the noble eightfold path: right view, right thought, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right effort, right mindfulness, and right samādhi. The vajra garland above the eight pillars is there so that one may be completely freed from the five bonds in every respect, and so that the wheel of Dharma of the vajra-like path of all the perfect buddhas may be turned. The eight great charnel grounds are there to show that all world-systems are selfless. That is the suchness of the maṇḍala.

The purity of the sense base of form, and of the nature of body in its own particular aspect, is Gaurī. The purity of the sense base of sound, and of the nature of mind in its own particular aspect, is Caurī. The purity of the sense base of smell, and of the nature of speech in its own particular aspect, is Vetālī. The purity of the sense base of taste, and of the nature of wisdom in its own particular aspect, is Ghasmarī. The purity of the earth element and of the wisdom of the suchness of body is Pukkasī. The purity of the water element and of the wisdom of the suchness of speech is Śabarī. The purity of the fire element and of the wisdom of the suchness of mind is Caṇḍālī. The purity of the wind element and of the wisdom of suchness is Ḍombinī. Glorious Hevajra is the fruit, the purity of wisdom just described, and he is the very nature of unsurpassed awakening, the state of the great Vajradhara.

In the maṇḍala of Nairātmyā, the nature of the sense base of touch and of the faculty of wisdom and gnosis is Bhūcarī. The purity of the sense base of mental objects and of the powers of faith, diligence, mindfulness, samādhi, and wisdom is Khecarī. The nature of the faculty of faith is Gaurī. The nature of the faculty of diligence is Caurī. The nature of the faculty of mindfulness is Vetālī. The nature of the faculty of samādhi is Ghasmarī. The nature of the grounds Utterly Joyful, Stainless, and Luminous is Pukkasī. The nature of Radiant, Difficult to Conquer, and Manifest is Śabarī. The nature of Far Gone and Immovable is Caṇḍālī. The nature of Excellent Intelligence and Cloud of Dharma is Ḍombinī.

The aggregate of form, the sense base of the eye, and great delusion are purified, and the nature of the mirror-like wisdom of the buddhas is Vajrā. The aggregate of feeling, the sense base of the nose, and the wisdom of equality of the buddhas in whom great pride is purified, are the Secret Gaurī. The aggregate of perception, the faculty of the tongue, and the nature of the discerning wisdom of the buddhas in whom great attachment is purified, are Vāriyoginī. The aggregate of formations, the sense base of the body, and the nature of the all-accomplishing wisdom of the buddhas in whom great envy is purified, are Vajraḍākinī. The aggregate of consciousness, the sense base of the mind, and the nature of the wisdom of the dharmadhātu, the ground of the buddhas in whom great aversion is purified, are Nairātmyā. These are the suchness of the deities.

Purifying the outer and inner ground and the rest are the acts and tasks of the master. Then one who possesses the three pledges is a fit vessel for the vajra vow. Having consecrated the vajra, the master says:

The vajra endowed with the five wisdoms,
Praised by all the buddhas,
I shall rightly give to you.
Hold it rightly at the heart with hūṃ.

Speech is like an echo;
Being free of conceptual thought, its many sounds
Pass beyond the domain of expression.
The bell is the nature of speech;
You must hold it.
Since this is the pledge of speech,
Hold it rightly with the syllable āḥ.

The continuum whose nature is method and wisdom,
The essence of the indivisible vajra,
The embrace, is the great seal;
This is the pledge of body.
Hold it rightly with the syllable oṃ.
When one thus possesses the three pledges,
The vajra vow should be bestowed.

Born from the syllable hūṃ,
The nature of vajra mind, cause and result:
When the rite is complete, give it to the disciple.
This, belonging to all the buddhas,
Rests in the hand of Vajrasattva.
You too must hold it always.
Steadfast in the vow of Vajrasattva,
The being without beginning or end,
The great joy of Vajrasattva,
Samantabhadra, the self of all,
The vajra of pride, lord of lords:
You must hold it always.

Having recited these, one should place it in the disciple's hand: oṃ sarba tathāgata siddhi bajra samaya eṣa tvaṃ dhārayami hi hi hi hi hūṃ. With this one has him take up the vajra, and he is steadfast in the vajra vow.

This is declared to follow
The sound of the wisdom of all the buddhas.
You too must hold it always;
The Victors deem it supreme awakening.
You shall proclaim the sound of Dharma.
Since existence is pure by its own nature,
Without essence, rendered insubstantial,
May those who contemplate this natural purity
Bring about a sublime existence.

With these the vajra vow is bestowed; one has him take hold of the suchness of the bell, and he should ring it.

Then, having consecrated him as the great seal with the mudrā of the special deity:

Through the pledge of body, form and the rest,
Recite the secret mantra without omission.
It is the essence of one's own awareness,
Of those who abide in the ten directions and three times;
You must hold it with oṃ.

Thus the three suchnesses have been taught. Then he is empowered with the six mudrās, and the prediction is given.

Taking on the pride of being the principal deity oneself, and making the fields of all the buddhas directly present, one holds the end of the Dharma robe at the heart with the left fist, makes the right hand into the gesture of granting refuge, and should give the prediction in order, beginning with the east:

oṃ Of the great and supreme vajra family,
Together with all the tathāgatas,
With one voice I give the prediction.
Having been thus predicted by me,
Vajrasattva, Tathāgata,
In order to accomplish the very limit of existence,
Free beings from existence and the lower realms.
O great Tathāgata So-and-so!

That is: tathāgata siddha bhur bhuva svaḥ. In the same way, the prediction is given in the four families, as the nature of jewel, lotus, and action as well.

"This mantrin who has received its empowerment,
lord of all beings,
the very embodiment of the great seal:
may the Sugatas too prophesy him
to unsurpassed awakening!"

This is what is taught here. Because through mantra and mudrā he has come to the state of Vajradhara, it is the prophecy that is to be declared.

Next, the one who possesses the three samayas should be given encouragement:

"Since you have seen and entered
the sublime maṇḍala of the supreme secret,
you are surely freed from every evil;
you abide well in reality itself.
Through this vehicle of great bliss,
for you there is no more death and the rest.
Unshakable, the supreme fruit
can by no means be shaken: rejoice!
You have passed beyond the suffering of existence.
To accomplish a sublime existence,
you have become supreme among teachers.
This perfected samaya-vow
you must always guard.
All the buddhas have spoken it in unison;
it is the sublime and eternal command.
The mind of awakening, by merely generating which
buddhahood is beyond doubt,
must never be given up.
Whatever is mudrā and vajra,
the sublime Dharma, do not abandon,
and never cast it aside.
To those deluded by ignorance
the great way should not be taught.
Do not forsake yourself
and torment yourself with austerities;
take things up at your ease.
This one is a perfect buddha yet to come.
Never cast away
the vajra, the bell, and the mudrās,
and never revile the master,
for he is equal to all the buddhas.
Whoever reviles the master,
the guru whose way is equal to the buddhas',
reviles all the buddhas,
and so will always meet with suffering:
plague, poison, venom, disease,
the punishment of the ḍākinīs;
slain by harmful spirits and fierce vināyakas,
that being falls into hell.
Therefore, with every effort,
never revile
the vajra master of great intelligence,
whose virtue is never overcome.
With reverence you should offer
a fitting fee to the guru.
Through that, plague and the other harms
will not arise again.
Always guard your own samaya,
always worship the Tathāgatas,
always make offerings to the guru as well,
for he is equal to all the buddhas.
To offer to him
is always to offer to all the buddhas.
Offering to him is the accumulation of merit,
and from the accumulation the supreme accomplishment is won.
Because this is well established,
today you abide close to the fruit.
There is no doubt that today
you become equal to all the samaya deities.
Since all the buddhas, together with the vajra holders,
have empowered you today,
you are proclaimed the great victor of the three realms,
the lord of kings.
Today, victorious over Māra,
you dwell in the supreme city.
There is no doubt that you
will attain buddhahood itself.
So make your mind clear and vajra-like;
show forth your own samaya, the inexhaustible great bliss.
Here and now, swiftly, you will come to realize
that you are fully equal to Vajrasattva and eternal."

Further, the fourteen samayas are taught.

Then the disciple, pleased and gentle, bows at the guru's feet and says:
"Lord, just as you have commanded in your words,
so shall I carry out your command."
This is the giving of encouragement.

Next, the authorization. Then, endowed with the yoga of one's own deity, one consecrates a golden spatula with mantra and, as with the light rays from one's own heart, one should begin to open the eyes with the eye ointment of wisdom:

"Son, the faults of the cataract
of ignorance in your eyes,
just as a skilled eye doctor would,
so Vajrasattva
shall remove that cataract by this application.
oṃ vajra netraya apahara paṭalaṃ hrīḥ"

Visualizing the supreme syllable on the disciple's two eyes, one should remove the cataract with the spatula and the light rays.

Then, with oṃ vajrasatva, one shows the mirror and teaches the nature of all phenomena:

"Phenomena are like reflections,
pure, clear, and without turbidity,
ungraspable and inexpressible,
arisen entirely from causes and karma,
born definitively from suchness,
without essential nature, without location.
Knowing all phenomena in this way,
as without intrinsic nature and without basis,
accomplish the matchless benefit of sentient beings:
you will become supreme among protectors."

Then, ringing the bell and showing the reflection in the mirror, one commands, "Know the reality of all phenomena in this way!" and one should recite these words:

"All things have the characteristic of space,
yet space has no characteristic.
Because of their union equal to space,
sameness, the supreme of all, is made clear.
Vajrasattva, like a mirror,
is pure, clear, and stainless.
The very self of all the buddhas
dwells, my son, in your heart.
Know that this is the bodhisattva who is lord of all the Tathāgatas!"

Saying this, one touches the mirror to the heart.

Then one places in the hand the bow arisen from ho and, saying, "Follow all the Tathāgatas! Subdue the four māras!", one should draw four bows above the head toward the four directions, and also above and below.

Then with oṃ vajra hetu maṃ one places a wheel between the two feet, and with oṃ vajra bhaṣa raṃ one gives a conch into the right hand. Into the left hand one gives the book arisen from the first vowel letter and the bell arisen from the last vowel letter, and says the following:

"From now on, merely by generating the mind of awakening, turn the wheel of Dharma!"

From this day forth, for the sake of the world,
turn the wheel of the protectors.
With the unsurpassed conch of Dharma,
fill every place, leaving none.
Do not be hesitant or of two minds;
with a mind free of doubt,
proclaim far and wide in this world
the supreme way of conduct of secret mantra.
Doing so is called
repaying the kindness of the buddhas who have helped you.
All those vajra holders, too,
will protect you everywhere.

Further, having joined the disciple with the Cakravartin application in its proper sequence, say:

Out of the wish to benefit sentient beings,
in all the world systems,
in whatever way each is to be tamed,
in that way turn the wheel of Dharma.
In the places of the Dharma, vajra,
jewel, and lotus,
and the wheel of activity, in their order.
In the sequence of this explanation in verse,
by means of the five ślokas,
the permission is given.

Some say that everything up to the giving of the permissions for the vows and so on is given after the fourth empowerment has been completed. Here, however, it is said to precede the stage of the fourth.

Then, having performed purification, the torma offering and so on for the disciple:

The master, having completed
all the qualifications of a vajra master,
ties on the disciple's hand
a bracelet as a protection of mantra.
An umbrella should be held above.
Holding vajra and bell,
he circumambulates the maṇḍala three times
and empowers him with the six mudrās.
Today, in this way, the disciple
becomes a holder of the meaning of the tantra.
Having removed the umbrella,
in order to exalt the disciple, say this:
Now you have become a master of the maṇḍala,
and you will hold the tantras of secret mantra.
The buddhas and bodhisattvas
and all the gods will honor you.
Out of love for sentient beings,
you should diligently generate and draw
maṇḍalas according to the rite,
and introduce practitioners to the tantras.
Then, having recited his own mantra one hundred and eight times,
he says: "I give you this mantra.
This mantra is close to all the Victors,"
and so it should be given.

The disciple, for his part, says:

Blessed One, I have received it well.
The accomplishment of mantra draws near.
Having received the secret mantra, I shall recite it.

Then, in order to show the disciple the four mudrās, say this. "From this time on, you must not show even a single mudrā to anyone who does not know the four mudrās, or who does not know even one mudrā." Thus he is commanded. If one asks why: because such beings have not seen the maṇḍala and have not entered into the maṇḍala and the mudrās, they are in doubt about the result to be attained; therefore they should not be shown. If they are shown, then because this turns into the nature of superimposition and denigration, they fall into the hell of Avīci. Therefore he is commanded, "Do not teach to such people." These constitute the common master empowerment.

The special one is conferred with the hands holding vajra and bell:

That very bell of wisdom
is visualized as a sixteen-year-old wisdom consort;
embracing her well with the hands,
complete with all the characteristics:
this is explained as the master empowerment.

That is the transcendent empowerment. This master empowerment is also called the irreversible empowerment, and also the vajra master empowerment. It is called the mundane and transcendent master empowerment. Here, the knowledge empowerments together with the master empowerment are called the vase empowerment: because at the time of all the empowerments one confers empowerment with the special and the common vases, it is called the vase empowerment. Through it the obscurations of the body are purified and the empowerment of the enlightened body is obtained; and because it has the nature of the emanation body, it is called the vase empowerment.

The lord of supreme masters,
having completed the master empowerment,
to whoever is fortunate enough for the secret empowerment,
to him the pledges should be given,
and he should be made to take up the supreme vow.
If one relies on the five meats, human and the rest,
and on the five nectars,
one attains the accomplishment of Hevajra.
You should take the lives of others,
you should speak false words,
you should take what is not given,
and always resort to the women of others.
Making the mind one-pointed and stable,
slay the mind's delusion born of ignorance.
That all things lack intrinsic nature,
and, to liberate the world, one speaks of untruth.
Not abandoning one's own bliss,
in order to attain the wisdom hard to realize,
that itself is taking what is not given.
Realizing the queen who is free of elaboration and unborn,
one resorts to the women of others.
The perfectly complete Buddha
declared these to be the pledges.
The excellent one possessing the characteristics
should take up these pledges well.
Then, with the five kinds of offering,
making them complete with every special feature,
so that he may be endowed with accomplishment and vow,
the master honors the disciple.
That itself is the giving of the pledges.
Then the disciple, to the master,
having offered a feast gathering,
the disciple offers these eight knowledge consorts:
mother and sister,
daughter and niece,
likewise the maternal uncle's wife,
the mother's sister, the mother-in-law,
and likewise the father's sister.
These are renowned as the eight knowledge consorts.
They are of the nature of the eight goddesses;
if practiced with, they bestow accomplishment.

Then, if one does not have those eight knowledge consorts, one should offer whichever knowledge consort one does have, such as Vajrā and the others, who are of the nature of the five families.

The dancer, the washerwoman, the ḍombī woman,
The caṇḍālī, and the brahmin woman,
Born from the Tathāgata families,
Are taught to be the five consorts.
The yogin should worship them.
Embracing and kissing at length,
He should engage in amorous play with her
Through the union of vajra and lotus.
There liquor is to be drunk,
And meat and the great meat eaten.
Give her a feast of drink,
And then make offerings once more.
Then, in the second watch,
The disciple should be brought inside.
His eyes are covered with a cloth,
And afterwards the maṇḍala is shown.
With prepared offerings of rice
And with liquor, worship the yoginīs.
A single piece of great bliss
Is filled with liquor along with nectar.
The greatly fortunate one offered it
To the master and to the Teacher himself,
And I too, in great delight,
Bowed down and then drank.
The mudrā of giving and receiving
Is made opened out like a lotus;
Again and again it is a prostration.
If one worships mother, niece,
Mother-in-law, and maternal uncle's wife,
Accomplishment is swiftly gained,
Because the mind is free of conceptual thought.

Then the vajra master enjoys the assembly of the five qualities of sense pleasure, and the whole assembly of yogins and yoginīs fully enjoys them with the play of desire. Having completed, with every kind of special offering, all the pleasing conduct of vajra songs and the like, the feast gathering is drawn to a close. Then the place is made secluded with curtains and the like, the four directions are adorned with canopies and fans, a comfortable and soft seat is spread, and the place is filled with fragrant incense and with lamps in the four directions.

In this way the master and the action seal take their seats. She is the foremost of those consorts, or one who has faith in the master: her hair soft and curling, her face beautiful and anointed with fragrance, her eyes long, her complexion a clear dark blue, her breasts close together, her upper body full and her waist slender, adorned with beauty and youth, of high family and endowed with compassion, steady and possessed of power, never apart from the nature of samādhi, adorned with various garments and ornaments, and anointed with storax and camphor. Or else a consort is found as circumstances allow, one twelve years old or sixteen years old, and the disciple presents her to the master. The master takes such a consort, adorns her with special cloth and garlands of flowers, anoints her body with special, captivating unguents, and embraces her in order to bring about bliss.

Then, seeing the master united with the consort, the disciple makes offerings with a special array of various offerings such as these: the fivefold offerings, a parasol, a victory banner, a banner, a bell, various cloths, various ornaments, liquor, meat and so forth. Then he should offer praise:

O Lord, Blessed One,
Embodiment of great peace,
You have become the supreme vajra yoga,
Nondual with the Great Seal,
Born from the vajra yoga itself.
You who are the supreme true meaning,
By the Lord's kindness, O Guide,
Please rescue me too
From the mire of saṃsāra's suffering.

So he supplicates. Then the master presses the consort's lower lip with his teeth, takes her two breasts in his hands, touches the inside of the lotus with his hand, and places her two feet above his shoulders. He engages in desire to its culmination and increases the bliss. Until the bliss has increased, he should practice with the bliss of kunduru. Having realized the nature of suchness, at the moment bliss arises he praises with the verses beginning "O Blessed One," and makes the deity yoga manifest. Having blindfolded the wisdom consort and the disciple, the drop filled with the essence of all the Tathāgatas, swollen like the moon, which through the union of kunduru has reached the inside of the vajra jewel, is taken with the thumb and ring finger and placed in the disciple's mouth. Then he should say:

Just as the Tathāgatas confer
Empowerment for the ultimate aim
On the bodhisattvas and their heirs,
An empowerment that perfects all bliss,
So too, just as I confer it,
Receive well this secret empowerment.

Then the disciple, having examined it well and received it, should say, "aho sukhaṃ aho sukhaṃ." Then he should receive it by way of the tongue, just as it is and without error. The consort too, remaining naked, should give to the disciple, born from vajra and lotus, by the sequence of giving through kunduru. By this the faults of speech are purified and the secret empowerment is obtained, which is the nature of the enjoyment body. This is the stage of the secret empowerment.

Then the vajra master should confer the wisdom knowledge empowerment on the disciple. Taking that same consort or another, and joining in the union of kunduru as before, the disciple who possesses the true characteristics should make supplication. Then the master rises and, seeing that the disciple possesses the characteristics of a great disciple and has abandoned anger and jealousy, he should give the consort:

The supreme vajra holder
Grants permission to the disciple:
You possess compassion and love;
Take this one of great mind.
Through the union of kunduru
You will become a vajra holder.
Goddess who grants the supreme and the great state,
Of varied form, captivating one,
Take him, take him, great-minded lady;
Having taken him, join in that very offering.

Then the master places the consort in the disciple's hand, and joy arises in him:

One should distinguish by the division of moments;
By knowing the moments, bliss is known.
It abides in the form of evaṃ.
First there is simple joy;
Supreme joy is the second;
Joy of cessation is known as the third;
The fourth, likewise, is the innate.
Slight joy is bliss;
Supreme joy surpasses it;
Joy of cessation is freedom from passion;
The innate is free from all.
The first is the wish for touch;
The second is the wish for bliss;
The third is the very conquest of passion;
Then the fourth is to be meditated upon.
The first joy is said to be existence;
Being free of passion, it is nirvāṇa;
The middle is simply joy;
Having left these behind, the innate.
Variety and maturation,
Likewise consummation and freedom from characteristics:
Relying on these four moments,
Thus should the yogin know them.
Variety is said to be of many kinds:
Embracing, kissing, and so forth.

Ripening is other than that:
it is the wisdom of enjoying bliss.
Consummation is reflection,
drawing forth one's own bliss.
Free of characteristics is other than these:
it is free of both passion and dispassion.
The first, joy, is variety;
supreme joy is ripening;
the joy of cessation is consummation;
innate joy is free of characteristics.
It is not passion, nor is it dispassion,
nor is it found in the middle either.
That which is the nature free of these three
is explained to be the innate.
When the joy of cessation and the rest are examined well,
even within the nature of the three joys,
in the middle between the joy of cessation and supreme joy,
there the innate abides.
Empty and not empty is Heruka.
This is the order in which these are counted.

In the manner of the generation stage, one should examine the innate in the midst of the joy of cessation. Then the special consort, looking at the practitioner, should say this:

Are you able, my son,
to eat this excrement and urine,
and likewise semen and blood,
to honor women always,
and to lick my lotus?
Speak, my son, as you please.

The practitioner, too, should say this:

O goddess, what could I not do?
I shall eat semen, blood and the rest,
I shall always honor women,
and I shall kiss the bhaga.

Then, having attained great joy, she is free of clothing, anointed with sweet fragrances such as saffron, musk and sandalwood, and crowned with a garland of fragrant flowers. Just as a lotus is unsullied by stains, she shows her inner lotus as if opened, and the wisdom consort says this:

How wonderful!
At my marvelous lotus,
endowed with every bliss,
whoever brings it delight by the rite,
before him I remain.
To please the perfect Buddha,
serve the lotus as is fitting.
This very one is the king of great bliss;
he dwells always in its abode.
Practice the approach of the red drop of great bliss
with the lotus of your mouth.
bhañja mokṣa ho

Then the practitioner, following the instructions, should imagine the form of Nairātmyā (the goddess of selflessness) through the application of her seed syllable and hand emblem. At her heart, throat and crown he should imagine hūṃ āḥ oṃ respectively. Then the vajra and the lotus should be purified:

oṃ padma sukhadhara mahārāga sukhaṃda catur ānanda bhaga viśva hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ karyaṃ kuruṣa me oṃ vajra maha dvekha catur ānanda dayakaḥ khaga mukhe kārasonāthaṃ hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ karyaṃ kuruṣa me

Having purified them with these, in order to signify and to generate intense bliss, one should engage in the practices of gazing, laughing, holding hands and embracing. Then, in the center of Nairātmyā's lotus, one should seek out the point that is the nature of the three channels by means of the tongue or the hand, and practice until the bliss expands. That gazing and the rest correspond to the paths of accumulation, preparation, seeing, meditation and the special path of culmination, so that one becomes a perfectly complete buddha without interruption, is to be learned from the succession of the lineage of the guru's instructions.

That awakening mind which has reached the tip of the jewel has the nature of innate great bliss. Because it is liberated from all the senses and has a focus without an object, it signifies the ultimate innate; it is the form of the exemplary innate. Through the guru's instructions, through familiarization, through the practice of pressing, through mastery over the winds, and by uniting the winds in the outer consort, once this has been shown, one comes to see the ultimate innate. If one does not have a consort, the fourth empowerment should be conferred by means of the waves of complete enjoyment.

Moreover, it is said:

As long as the yogins'
awakening mind is not emitted,
its attainment is uninterrupted.
What joy this is, what great bliss!
When the awakening mind,
the ground of all accomplishments, is emitted,
the aggregates and consciousness decline,
and its accomplishment is suffering.
What has arisen from that yoga
should not be discarded at all.
It is not to be taken up
with the hand, a conch or mother-of-pearl;
take it up with the vajra, as the five nectars.
That generates strength and bliss.
Then the wise one rises
and bows at the guru's feet.
As the Teacher has said, the great being,
to attain the true and supreme joy,
relying on and then relinquishing the moment of variety,
should hold to great bliss.
Until awakening is finally reached,
for the sake of beings, the vajra holder
will succeed through pacifying, enriching and the rest;
of this there is no doubt.
From the union of the two organs, five arise;
they are the nature of the five elements.
The single great joy
becomes fivefold in its divisions.
Through the union of vajra in lotus,
touch is the latency of solidity.
Because solidity is the property of delusion,
delusion is Vairocana.
Because that awakening mind
is held to be moisture, the water element,
and because water is by nature unshakable,
aversion has Akṣobhya as its nature.
Through the perfect union of the two,
warmth always arises.
From warmth arises desire;
desire is Vajra Amitābha.
Whatever mind abides in the lotus,
that is the mode of wind.
Jealousy is Amoghasiddhi;
Amogha truly arises from wind.
Bliss, desire, is not blood;
blood has the characteristic of space;
space has the characteristic of avarice;
avarice is Vajra Space itself.
The sentient being, though only one,
is conceived in five forms.
Of what arises from the five families,
there are many thousands.
Therefore this is of a single nature,
always the supreme great bliss.

This itself is great wisdom;
It abides throughout all bodies.
Nondual, and yet abiding as two,
It is the sovereign whose nature is both entity and non-entity.
Method and wisdom without duality,
Passion and dispassion become mingled.
This itself is the life of living beings;
That itself is the supreme syllable.
Having attained with certainty the moving and the unmoving,
It appears alike in illusory forms.
By the method of the maṇḍala circle
It always comes to certainty.
Or else, it is the self of all;
Or else, it has abandoned all.
It is imagined as the absence of joy and so on.
Having abandoned the three joys,
This great wisdom is subtle,
The vajra essence, like the sky,
Stainless, liberation, peace itself.
You are the father, the progenitor.

By these the obscurations of the mind are purified and the empowerment of the enlightened mind is obtained. This is the empowerment of wisdom and gnosis, whose nature is the Dharma body.

What, then, has been taught by these?

Master, secret, and wisdom,
And the fourth likewise.
By the count of the four empowerments
The joys and the rest should be known in order.

How, then, is the fourth empowerment taught? In the Hevajra Tantra only three empowerments are taught. Where it says, "The fourth, again, is suchness itself," the teaching of the fourth as suchness, in connection with the water, the flower garland and the rest, is the teaching on laughter in the Tattvasaṃgraha Tantra. How, then, is the fourth that is taught in the chapter on the vajra family to be understood? Apart from that, it is said, the fourth should be understood as solely the empowerment of wisdom and gnosis. It is taught thus:

In laughter, the master;
In gazing, likewise, the secret;
In holding hands, wisdom;
And in the embrace of the pair, the tantra.

Its meaning is this. Laughing and showing affection toward the wisdom consort, in the form of an offering to all the Tathāgatas, is the defining mark of the master empowerment. That is the master empowerment.

Gazing at the lotus with close affection toward the wisdom consort is the secret empowerment, because it is close to the secret. Closely touching the body of the wisdom consort is the wisdom empowerment, because it is closely joined with wisdom. The embrace of the pair, since it points to the suchness of the ultimate meaning that comes from the tantra, is nondual gnosis. It is called the fourth empowerment, the tantra of the fruit.

Some say that the fourth empowerment is distinct from the third: "Because in the first chapter all four empowerments are taught separately, the fourth exists as something distinct." Even if one were to accept that, since it would not be settled which is the principal one, it would not be bound up with meditation. Therefore the fourth should be understood through a twofold distinction within the empowerment of the enlightened mind. Of the two, the first: "When joined with the body of the wisdom consort, it should be taught as coemergent great bliss." This defining mark arises when the mind is closely joined with the body of the wisdom consort; therefore it is the empowerment of wisdom and gnosis. Then, having made that very thing stable, the coemergent great bliss: just as, by the power of meditative equipoise, bliss pervades the whole body part by part, and pervading the various things, makes them indivisible as the nature of great bliss; nondual, like wisdom and gnosis, free of all conceptual thought, devoted to great bliss alone, and having abandoned clinging even to that: this is the fourth, the empowerment of suchness. For this reason too it is said, "The fourth, again, is suchness itself."

Through these, the nature of the body of great bliss, whose defining mark is the purity of body, speech and mind, is the fourth empowerment.

Consecration is a following after that is equal to the bounds of space.
One should consecrate the Dharma, and likewise the Sugata, the vajra and the bell.
The ultimate of the protectors, which worldly people do not fully know:
Out of love for the world, how should one perform consecration?

Cast images, stūpas, temples,
Painted images of the Dharma, and books:
Having fixed their number and measure,
Complete in proportions and marks, beyond doubt,
One whose intellect knows suchness
Should undertake the consecration.
Whatever is made for the increase of merit
Should be made a support for later worship.
Even when images and the like are well made,
If one has not long performed the approach,
And so is not endowed with its accumulation,
The consecration will not be accomplished.
And when a person
Lacks the qualities and defining marks,
Beings do not worship there,
And its form becomes debased.
Whatever has not been consecrated,
To make offerings and prostrations to it
Is like performing a fire offering without mantra:
The burning merely turns it to ash,
And no result will come from it.
As was said above, if there is no mantra,
Even though one practices Dharma and makes offerings and the rest,
Through the ripening of that karma
In all one's births there is no share of offerings.

Moreover, on a good day, when the planets and lunar mansions are auspicious, the vajra master should bathe, anoint himself with fragrant scents, put on clean clothes, and furnish himself with the action vajra. Having assembled the offerings and so forth and taken up the vajra and bell, he should sit on a high, comfortable seat and, endowed with meditative absorption, perform the worship of the maṇḍala.

Then, in order to consecrate cast images, painted scrolls, books and the like, one regards all things as being by nature primordially unborn, and then regards them in a corresponding form: one fully generates the supporting and supported maṇḍala from seed syllables and hand emblems. Regarding them as adorned with crowns corresponding to their family, one completes the meditative absorption of the samaya being, and visualizes the wisdom being in the element of space. Having offered purifying incense, torma and so on, one worships with the eight goddesses emanated from the seed syllable at one's own heart, praises, and has them taste nectar. Then the mantra practitioner, holding fragrant flowers in his cupped palms and bowing his body, recites oṃ sarba tathāgata salalita bilasyana mitena namī bhigabantaṃ jaḥ hūṃ baṃ hoḥ pratīccha kusuma añjali nātha ho samayastvaṃ samayastvaṃ samayastvaṃ. Having said this, he scatters the flowers from his cupped hands, sets his right knee down, holds in his hand a vessel of fragrant incense, and should consecrate the principal deity or the others:

Blessed wrathful one, holder of the vajra,
King of vidyā-mantras, I pay homage to you.

Out of love for me,
for the sake of the consecration to be performed,
out of love for the disciples,
and so that offerings may be made to you as well,
you whose very nature is compassion,
please grant your permission.

Just as the perfect buddhas
dwelt in the Tuṣita heaven
and then entered the womb of Queen Māyā,
so too enter this image,
and remain here always.
To hold us close with love,
and so that the mind of awakening may grow,
please consecrate it.

After making this request three times, one should make offerings of flowers, incense and so forth. Then one goes outside and gives torma to the guardians of the directions and the others, and comes back in. One recites the mantra of all activities, makes a protection cord with three knots, and ties it on the disciple's right upper arm. One then consecrates the water of all activities with mantra and gives protection by sprinkling it in purification. Every rite should be carried out together with the disciple or the vajra assistant.

One should instruct the disciple or the patron in this way:

Whatever little the mantra practitioner instructs,
the rites, the offerings and so forth,
carry them out without deceit;
by this you will gain a great result.

Having instructed them thus, one recites the root mantra in order to pacify all obstacles, and then lies down to sleep. These make up the preparatory rite of the deity.

Then in the morning, the mantra practitioner, having every quality just as described, casts the pacifying torma. With the yoga of the deity, one makes a square maṇḍala of the proper measure, raised two cubits high. One installs the image or other object on it facing east, and clothes it in yellow garments. Following the yoga described earlier, one generates it in the manner of arising from the state in which all things are unarisen, and blesses it at the heart, the throat and the crown with oṃ āḥ hūṃ.

Starting with the lustration (nimañca), one performs the auspicious rite. Holding white mustard seeds in the cupped palms of the right and left hands, one circles them clockwise and counterclockwise, removes faults with light rays from the heart, and burns the seeds in the fire three times with oṃ sarva pāpaṃ dahana vajrāya svāhā. Then, with that same mantra, one casts three handfuls of water in the same way as before.

oṃ sarva tathāgata kāya viśodhani svāhā

Reciting this mantra three times, one anoints the image well with fragrant melted butter, using a bundle of dūrvā grass, and places it in a bronze basin. Then, to remove impurities and the butter, one blesses the bark of five trees, vaṭa, udumbara, śiri, aśvattha and palāśa, with the five seed syllables, grinds them, mixes them into a cleansing paste, and bathes the image.

The arrangement is that of a royal consecration. A white parasol is held aloft, praises are offered from the auspicious verses, conches, copper horns, great drums and other instruments sound, a troupe of offering goddesses dances, and vajra songs are sung very slowly. Then, in the manner in which the tathāgatas were bathed, one holds countless vases and recites the bathing verses, and:

The consecration of a revered stupa
is generated as a celestial mansion;
one should meditate on it as the abode and support
of buddhas and bodhisattvas.
All the buddhas and bodhisattvas without exception,
the deities of knowledge and secret mantra,

are summoned, made to enter, bound and delighted. One gives them the purifying torma and so forth, makes offerings and praises, and has them taste the nectar. With the mantra oṃ supratiṣṭha vajre svāhā one makes it stable and seals it with the respective buddha family. The rest is as before.

A book is consecrated in the same way. One sees the book, arising from the transformation of jṝṃ, as having the nature of Amitābha. Having completed the parts of the rite there, one imagines that it transforms into the very nature of the collection of letters of the scriptures. With oṃ hūṃ hrīḥ dhīṃ one seals it as having the nature of speech. Imagining that it is blessed by all the tathāgatas and all the vajra holders, one does the rest as before.

A temple is the support of buddhas, bodhisattvas, śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas, and therefore it should be generated through samādhi. Focusing on it as having the essence of the sphere of reality, one blesses it with oṃ hūṃ hriḥ. Preceded by the preparatory rite, one makes offerings, gives torma and so forth, and consecrates it with the mantras oṃ supratiṣṭha vajra svāhā and oṃ hūṃ hrīḥ bhu khaṃ vajri bhava dṛḍha tiṣṭha bhu khaṃ. Meditating that it is blessed by the buddhas, the bodhisattvas, Vajradhara and the others, and abides with a nature like the vajra, one sprinkles water from all the vases over the whole temple and recites oṃ vajrodaka abhiṣiñca.

Then the wise one should summon Mahākāla. Performing the rite, one summons him, makes him enter, binds him and brings him under control. Then, with the yoga of Hevajra with one face and two arms, one should give him this command: "For as long as this temple stands, you must guard it in keeping with this command of the Tathāgata. Furthermore, you must accomplish whatever is needed, just as the Saṅgha commands you. Keep the rite firm, just as it has been blessed."

Then the guru tells the disciple or the patron, "Come here and listen," and says:

Just as you requested,
the consecration that removes evil
has been completed properly according to the rite.
You must always make offerings.

Then one gives the caru that has been purified with mantra. Once it has been offered to the Tathāgata, the remainder should be given out in very small portions, so that all evil may be cast off.

Then the disciple should perform the bathing:

Just as, at the very moment of his birth,
the Tathāgata was bathed
with the pure water of the gods,
so too I perform this bathing.

oṃ sarva tathāgata abhiṣeka samaya śriye āḥ hūṃ

Painted scrolls, books and the like are bathed through a mirror, using the rite given above.

At that time one should recite the auspicious verses called "The Excellence of Oneself or Others." When the image has been bathed in this way, one should dry off the water with an extremely soft, spotless cloth, or else with the hands.

Clothe the image in many lengths of cloth the color of saffron, using the mantra oṃ divavastraye svāhā. Recite the mantra oṃ vajra gandhe āḥ hūṃ, and anoint the whole body with fragrances such as camphor, musk, saffron and sandalwood. With the mantra oṃ vajra avaraṇa viśuddhane svāhā, offer and fasten ornaments such as earrings, a necklace and a girdle. With the mantra oṃ vajra ratna trāṃ, set the crown upon the head. With the mantra oṃ vajra satva āḥ, show it a mirror. Then, with a golden probe and a silver dish, apply the supreme medicine, and with the mantra oṃ cakṣu samanta cakṣu viśodhani svāhā anoint the eyes and open them.

Next, make a mark with gorocanā at the place of the ūrṇā between the brows, and worship with flowers, incense, lamps, tooth-sticks and so forth. Protect it with the all-purpose action mantra. Then lift the image and the rest with hūṃ, bring them into the maṇḍala with the mantra of Ṭakkirāja or with the five syllables, and set them in the center. Then, by the rite for accomplishing the maṇḍala, accomplish the wisdom maṇḍala. Having offered the purificatory offerings, tormas and so on, expel with aṣṭānanā or with the sound of hūṃ the obstructors that came along with it. With the mantra jaḥ hūṃ vaṃ hoḥ, summon the circle of wisdom deities into the image and the rest, make them enter, bind them and delight them. Then bless the eyes, the body and so forth, and confer empowerment.

After that, again offer the purificatory offerings, tormas and so on, and worship with offerings of flowers and the rest, each with its own mantra. Then the mantra practitioner, settled in meditative equipoise and holding flowers in cupped hands, recites these words of suchness, beginning "Because it arises from space and bears the marks." Recite the hundred-syllable mantra seven times as well, and circumambulate.

Having thus made it stable, one makes this supplication for the sake of all sentient beings:

"Thus, O Sugata, protector,
for the sake of beings everywhere
taking on a variety of forms,
in the world realms of the ten directions
please appear as the Vajra Holder."

Then go out as before, set out a great torma, satisfy the guardians of the directions with nectar, worship them, and request them to depart to their own abodes. Then enter the maṇḍala house, circumambulate three times and prostrate. In order to make good any deficiency or excess in the rite, begin a fire offering for increase. At the time of the fire offering, in order to increase the accumulations of merit and wisdom, take hold of the disciple's right thumb and offer three ladlefuls and so forth.

Then the guru should ask the disciple for the offering fee sanctioned by the Tathāgata. The disciple, with a joyful mind and in keeping with his own means, should offer whatever he has: a wife adorned with every ornament; possessions such as elephants, horses, chariots, oxen and buffaloes; gold, silver, copper, jewels, pearls and other choice gems; estates with their grain, lands, fine orchards, houses and so forth. When the master is pleased, the Tathāgata is pleased, and by pleasing them one attains the supreme accomplishment.

Then prepare the caru. Wash the vessel well, and bless the remainder of the ladle offerings, together with honey, milk, sugar and the like, into supreme nectar, and recite the mantra of the principal deity as well. With the mantra oṃ dibyani samādhi dhyana praṇini svā, pour it into a vessel of gold or other precious material, and offer it to the Tathāgata.

Then, in the temple and the courtyard, the cast images and the rest are to be seated upon a well-made throne or upon a pedestal built of earth. Without letting them tilt, bend or lean, recite oṃ supratiṣṭha vajre svāhā and set them in place. If this is not done, it becomes a fault. Then, as before, give the purificatory offerings, tormas and so on, worship fully, praise, offer nectar to taste, recite the hundred-syllable mantra one hundred times, and ask for forbearance:

"O buddhas, perfect in patience,
whatever I did not know and whatever was incomplete
in the rites as they were taught
for the gods, the buddhas and the vidyādharas,
and for Brahmā and the other gods,
please bear with it in your great forbearance.
Gather all peace and auspiciousness
upon this patron,
and though you depart to the realm of the buddhas,
please come again.
As long as this form body stands consecrated,
for many hundreds of eons
may all the gods protect it.
May all the gods of the elements,
fire, water, wind and earth,
guard well the aggregates of the inner elements,
and act according to the rite.
The master together with the disciples,
the sons and grandchildren with their retinue,
please protect them."

Further, these particulars are taught:

"Images drawn in painting and images in relief
are consecrated by the sequence above."

With offerings of many kinds, worship the deities as well; then circumambulate three times accompanied by an ensemble of music, and make vast aspiration prayers.

Then go to one's own residence and hold a great feast for the monks, nuns, laymen, laywomen and others, and in addition hold great feasts as one's wealth allows. Invite the master with his retinue, the abbess of the vihāra, the stewards and others, hold a feast and make generous gifts; having pleased them, ask for their forbearance. At night, hold a feast gathering and vast offerings.

Then the mantra practitioner enters the maṇḍala house and worships fully. Holding incense in the right hand and ringing the bell with the left, he offers praise, and requests the deities to depart with the three syllables ending in muḥ. Then gather up the colored powders and put them in a clean vessel. Give a very little to whoever wants some, and cast the remainder into a great river. Then smear the ground of the maṇḍala with the five products of the cow and recite this mantra: oṃ akāro mukhaṃ sarva dharmaṇāṃ ādyanutpanna tvāta oṃ āḥ hūṃ muḥ. There, too, give a great torma to the guardians of the directions.

Thus, through the great maṇḍala,
all accomplishments are attained;
so, to achieve the aims of this life and the next,
one should always undertake the rite.

As it is said:
Those of base caste, bamboo workers,
butchers, fishermen, those of low caste,
if they meditate on Hevajra,
will gain accomplishment, without doubt.

Even one who has committed the five deeds of immediate retribution will certainly gain accomplishment, provided he has not done such things as reviling his master.

The consecration, the building of the maṇḍala,
and the substances used for the fire offering and the wheel diagram,
all are to be offered to the master himself:
so spoke the Vajra Holder (Vajradhara) himself.

Through this rite of the maṇḍala of the Emergence of Glorious Heruka that I have composed,
may beings cast off the faults of error, gain the true path,
and wholly destroy, without remainder, the suffering of saṃsāra;
and, the Innate having become clear,
may they swiftly attain
the supreme state of glorious Heruka.

This completes the maṇḍala rite of glorious Hevajra called The Emergence of Heruka, composed by the great master Śūnyatāvajra. It was translated, edited and finalized by the Indian preceptor, the monk Pūrṇakīrti, and the Tibetan translator Nanam Dorjé Lama.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

Several plant names in the ground-examination verses are hard to identify. The bra tree, the rno tree, the skur tree, the mapala tree and the palabhali tree are given as the Tibetan names them, because their botanical or Sanskrit identity is uncertain. Mapala may be a corruption of the name of another tree. Turmeric, indigo, black mustard, white mustard and pine follow the usual senses of the Tibetan words.

In the verse on the dug pit, the Tibetan says the pit is filled "with earth and water." It is taken here as referring to refilling the pit with the excavated earth, as in the earlier verse.

"Ten principles" renders the ten tattvas of the master.  The mahoraga here is the earth serpent whose seasonal position governs where one may dig; the house rendering is kept. The line "through this the accomplishments people desire will be attained" renders མི་འདོད་པའི་དངོས་གྲུབ, reading མི as "people"; read as a negation it would mean "undesired accomplishments," which contradicts the context of digging correctly at the belly. "Karma vajra" (ལས་ཀྱི་རྡོ་རྗེ) is taken as the ritual assistant, since the text later says the work must be done by oneself or by the karma vajra. "In the five ways" (རྣམ་པ་ལྔ) is not specified further in this passage.  The fifth of the five precious substances is given simply as "king" (rgyal po); its exact identity (perhaps a particular gem such as sapphire) is uncertain, so it is rendered "the royal gem." The instrument called rdeu chung in the list of musical instruments is uncertain and is rendered "small rattle." The verse line addressed to the earth goddess is ambiguous in the Tibetan. It is read here as "Goddess who honors wrathful Hevajra, you have come under his sway." The karmavajra is the master's ritual assistant.  The phrase rendered "one hundred and eight times" reads literally "one hundred and eight, eight," apparently a dittography or possibly "one hundred and eight or eight times." In the disciple's supplication, the line "grant me, too, the mind of awakening" has gsol where the parallel lines have stsol ("grant"); it has been read in that sense. "Preparation" renders sta gon (adhivāsana), the preliminary rite on the day before the main maṇḍala rite.  "The seven disciples" renders སློབ་མ་བདུན literally; it may be a scribal variant for a plural marker ("the disciples"), but as written it reads "seven". "Steady" renders མི་གཡོ་བ, here probably meaning a firm, straight stick.  "Powdered white mustard" renders don grub kyi phye ma, taking don grub as the usual ritual shorthand for siddhārthaka, white mustard seed. "Lord of beasts" is taken as the lion. In the verse, sgom ba is read as sgom pa ("in meditation"), and brtson bzung as btson bzung ("captives taken"). In the prose, spangs is read as sbangs ("cleanse, soak"), and brten pa'i chog as brten pa'i cho ga ("the rite on their basis").  Several measuring terms are technical and not fully certain: "at the measure of the eight-spoked wheel" (literally "at the part of eight wheels"), "foot-measure" (a small unit of length used for the gaps erased in the lines), and "exceeding that by three color units" (the verse line may instead mean "set off especially by three colors"). "Thief's leaves" translates the Tibetan literally; the plant intended could not be identified with confidence.  In the description of the western charnel ground, the text reads 'dod pa'i rigs, "of the class of desire," which makes no sense in this series of corpse types; the similar-looking 'dor ba'i rigs, "of the discarded," has been read. The names of the charnel grounds are translated by meaning, since their Sanskrit forms cannot be identified with confidence apart from Laughing Ha Ha, which corresponds to Aṭṭahāsa. Several tree names (bhatha, lataṭa, prakari) are given as spelled because their Sanskrit identities are uncertain. Agni's attribute gundhe is understood as a water pot (kuṇḍī), but this is uncertain.  Nyerden flower: the Tibetan flower name for Takṣaka's color could not be securely identified; it is given in phonetics. Raṃśu wood and khaṇḍhabindha incense are likewise uncertain plant or substance names, kept as written. The eight cloud names are translated by meaning. The "special deity" renders lhag pa'i lha, probably one's chosen or principal deity.  In the verse, the third line reads "tantra and the unmoving." This is taken as a slip for the near-identical word for "moving," which gives the standard pair "the moving and the unmoving," that is, the animate and inanimate world. "In front of oneself" follows the evident reading of a word that is written as "seven" in the Tibetan. In the last sentence, "six seals" renders what is literally "six hands," taken as short for the six seals (bone ornaments) named in the parallel description of the protectors just before. The "five lamps" may be the coded term for the five meats, as in Hevajra literature. The "action vajra" is the ritual assistant.  "The Scatterer" translates འཐོར་བྱེད, an asura name I could not identify with confidence. The incense verses contain several substance names in transliteration whose identities are uncertain, especially ཤེལ་པངྐ, ཀ་ལ་ལོ་དྷ་པ་བྷི, and བོ་ལ་ས་དྷ་ར་ས. The line ཨུན་དུ་རུ་དང་པོ་ལ་ཀཾ could also be read as "unduru first, then lakaṃ". The line ས་འདི་དྲི་དང་ས་འདི་ཉིད is obscure and has been translated literally. The recipients "Eternal One", "Lord of Jewels", "Lord of the World" and "Amoghavajra" seem to correspond to the families of the maṇḍala, but the text does not make this explicit. The hell names follow this text's own list, which differs from the standard list of the eight hot hells. The "six seals" are the bone ornaments.  "The three realities" here most likely refers to the three syllables oṃ āḥ hūṃ, between which the Ṭakki mantra is inserted. The phrase rendered "rice water" (water in which rice has been rinsed or steeped) is somewhat uncertain. The hearth's measure is given in the Tibetan only as "one full [measure]", taken here as one cubit.  "Words on the intrinsic nature of existence" renders an obscure phrase; it may simply mean a praise describing the fire god's nature. The description of the two ladles is compressed in the Tibetan and it is not fully clear which features belong to which ladle; it is translated as it stands. "Jasmine" renders a plant name written as "mali upi," whose identification is uncertain.  The text reads "smell of khā and snakes"; ཁཱ makes no sense here and is taken as ཁྭ, "crow". The wood written bhe kaṅka could not be identified with confidence and is given as spelled. The grain called "bras so ba" is rendered "paddy rice" tentatively. "The all-purpose mantra" and "all-purpose water" render the Tibetan for the mantra and vase water used for all activities (sarvakarma).  "The superior maṇḍala of deities" renders lhag pa'i lha'i dkyil 'khor; the sense of lhag pa here (superior, or additionally invited) is not certain. "On white cloth" for the hearth translates ras as written, though the context suggests a prepared ground. "Satisfied by the sun and moon" is literal; the intended reference is unclear.  "Great fat" and "great flesh" are the standard tantric code terms for human fat and human flesh. The measure rendered "one finger-joint" (Tibetan chak gang) is uncertain. "Water crystal" translates chu shel literally; its use as a comparison for blackness is unclear. The text reads "dad" in the list of effigy ingredients, evidently a slip for "dang" ("and"), and is translated accordingly. The first item in the list of urines, khra, is taken as "hawk"; this is uncertain.  The bse tree (bse shing) is an unidentified tree name and is left untranslated. The heading for the killing mantra reads "gsang pa" ("secret"), which makes no sense here; it has been translated as the evident "gsad pa" ("killing"). The phrase "their ranks" renders the uncertain "rim ma".  "Self-arisen fluid of a courtesan" renders the tantric code term for menstrual blood (svayambhūkusuma), used here as a red drawing substance. In "to lengthen life" the text reads སྲིད, evidently for སྲིང ("lengthen"), as in the later verse on dūrvā grass. The grain called སོ་བ is not securely identified; it is rendered "millet."  In the supplication verses, "great lord Maheśvara" and "king of great lords" are epithets of Hevajra/Vajradhara, not references to Śiva. In "Grant me accomplishment as a bodhisattva" the text reads སེམས་དབར, taken as a slip for སེམས་དཔར. The Tibetan ཊཀྐི is rendered "Ṭakki", presumably Ṭakkirāja.  The line rendered "O Rāgavajra, abiding here" reads literally "Rāgavajra re abiding"; the syllable "re" is unclear and may be a scribal slip for the vocative "kye". The phrase "with misfortune not averted, you will die of a strange disease" follows the Tibetan, which appears to reflect a standard Sanskrit formula about dying without having avoided misfortune. "So-and-so" renders the placeholder for the disciple's name.  "The Jewel King" renders rin po che rgyal po, here evidently the Ratnasambhava-family form in which the disciple is visualized for the crown empowerment; the exact Sanskrit name is not certain, so it is translated literally. "Space Vajra" (Gaganavajra) and Bodhivajra are rendered as they stand in the petition verse.  In Bodhivajra's verse the Tibetan reads "stūpa" (mchod rten); parallel versions of this supplication read "offering" (mchod pa), and the line may reflect that. "Investigation" as the first basis of miraculous power renders spyod pa, read as dpyod pa, the standard term in this list. "Fixation on form and the rest" renders mngon par shes pa ("higher knowledge"), read as mngon par zhen pa ("fixation"), which the sense of "abandoning" requires. The text lists only nine named classes before "these twelve branches of the scriptures"; this is translated as it stands.  The Tibetan names Gaurī twice in the first series, for sound as well as form; since this series otherwise follows the standard Hevajra sequence and the text itself later gives Caurī for diligence, the second occurrence has been read as Caurī, an evident copyist's slip. The "Secret Gaurī" renders a literal Tibetan phrase; it stands where Hevajra lists of Nairātmyā's inner goddesses usually have Gaurī.  The verse line "do not torment yourself with austerities" reads ཤིད, evidently a slip for the prohibitive ཤིག, and is translated so. The line describing the vajra master, "whose virtue is never overcome," could also be read as a purpose clause, "so as not to destroy your virtue." "Harmful spirits" renders གནོད, which may abbreviate yakṣa.  The verses on killing, lying, stealing and resorting to others' women follow the Hevajra tradition: the literal-sounding injunctions are immediately reinterpreted in the following lines as slaying deluded thought, teaching the emptiness of things, seizing nondual bliss and wisdom, and union with the unborn "queen" of emptiness. The lines on the Dharma places "vajra, jewel, lotus, and wheel of activity" are terse; they appear to assign the four family emblems in sequence as part of the permission rite.  "Vajra songs" renders the reading rdo rje glu; the text as written has rdo rje klu ("vajra nāga"), which makes little sense here and is very likely a slip for the similar-sounding glu. "Kunduru" is the tantric technical term for sexual union, left untranslated. Rgan po and ming med are taken in their ritual sense of thumb and ring finger. The "piece of great bliss" is probably a skull-cup fragment, but the text does not say so explicitly. The ages of the consort given here are as stated in the source.  "The waves of complete enjoyment" renders a terse phrase whose exact technical sense is unclear. It may refer to the saṃbhogakāya or to a visualized consort. The line rendered "Bliss, desire, is not blood" is syntactically ambiguous in the Tibetan.  "The teaching on laughter in the Tattvasaṃgraha Tantra": the Tibetan dgod pa can mean "laughter" or "arrangement"; laughter is chosen because the verse quoted just after correlates laughing, gazing, holding hands and embracing with the four empowerments. The phrase "well made" renders the unclear legs par char ba, read as a slip for a word meaning "fashioned". "Sets his right knee down" renders the obscure lha ngas la btsugs, understood as the standard kneeling posture.  "Nimañca" appears to be a form of the Sanskrit nirmañchana, the lustration rite of waving substances around an object to remove faults. This fits the mustard seed procedure that follows. The tree name "śiri" may stand for śirīṣa, but this is not certain, so it is kept as spelled. The command "Keep the rite firm, just as it has been blessed" may instead be an instruction addressed to the officiant. It is taken here as the end of the command to Mahākāla.  "Purificatory offerings" renders བསང་གཏོར, taken as purificatory (fumigation or cleansing) offerings together with tormas. The verse "Because it arises from space and bears the marks" is cited only by its opening; the full words of suchness are not given here. ཨཥྚཱ་ན་ནཱ is kept as a mantra; it may refer to a specific deity's mantra not identified in this passage.
