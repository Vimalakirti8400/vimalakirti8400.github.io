The Means of Accomplishing Kurukullā through Self-Blessing, from the Stages of the Glorious Hevajra Tantra (Śrīhevajratantrakrameṇa Svādhiṣṭhāna Kurukullāsādhana)

The Means of Accomplishing Kurukullā through Self-Blessing, drawn from the stages of the Glorious Hevajra Tantra, bears the Sanskrit title Śrī Hevajratantrakrameṇa Svādhiṣṭhāna Kurukullā Sādhana, and in Tibetan Dpal kye'i rdo rje'i rgyud kyi rim pa las bdag byin gyis brlab pa ku ru kul le'i sgrub thabs. It is a short sādhana, a manual of tantric meditation practice, devoted to Kurukullā, the red goddess who brings the three worlds under her power and who wields a bow and arrow of flowers, a noose and a hook. The practitioner rises early, takes refuge, cultivates compassion and meditates on emptiness, then visualizes himself as the sixteen-year-old dancing goddess, receives empowerment, makes offerings and recites her eight-syllable mantra, before the text sets out a lineage instruction for magnetizing beings, brief magical applications, and a dedication of merit; its colophon credits the master Sahajavilāsa. It is Toh 1314 in the Tengyur, where it belongs among the tantra commentaries associated with the Hevajra cycle.

In the language of India: śrī te vajra tandra mena svātiṣṭhana kurukulle sādhanaṃ. In the language of Tibet: From the Stages of the Glorious Hevajra Tantra, the Means of Accomplishing Kurukullā through Self-Blessing.

Bowing to Kurukullā,
she who is linked with the Hevajra Tantra
and brings the three worlds under her power,
I shall clearly explain her means of accomplishment.

A mantra practitioner who has the good fortune to meditate in this tantra should rise from bed in the early morning. At the heart, from the complete transformation of the syllable raṃ, one should visualize a sun disk, and on it a red syllable trīḥ. By its light rays one should visualize in front the Blessed Lady Kurukullā, who will be described below, and worship her perfectly with cloud masses of the five offerings. Then, in her presence, one should recite three times the verse beginning, "I go for refuge to the Three Jewels."

Then one should hold in mind the sentient beings who suffer from the three kinds of suffering, and cultivate great compassion, whose characteristic is the wish that they be turned back from suffering. Then one should meditate on emptiness, whose characteristic is the knowledge that the two, the grasped and the grasper, which are the very being of phenomena and of persons, are without intrinsic nature. This should be blessed with the mantra, namely: oṃ śūnyatā jñāna vajra svabhāva ātmako'haṃ.

Then, by that very nondual wisdom, one should visualize a sun disk in the sky. In its center is a red syllable hrīḥ, from which comes a red utpala lotus, and at its center, on a sun, is that same seed. From that hrīḥ one emanates and gathers back light, and dissolves it fully into the syllable. From the complete transformation of all of that, one should visualize oneself as Kurukullā.

In the center of a red eight-petaled lotus, on the heart of a corpse, in the center of a sun, she stands in the dance posture of half-crossed legs. She has a halo of sunlight, and is surrounded in the cardinal and intermediate directions by the eight charnel grounds. Her body is red in color, and she is sixteen years old. Her tawny hair bristles upward. On her head she bears five skulls, which have the nature of the five Victors. She wears a necklace of freshly severed human heads. Her face bares its fangs, and her tongue is curled. Her three round red eyes roll to the left. She has the mood of wrath and of amorous grace. She is adorned with a wheel of human bone, earrings, a necklace, bracelets, a girdle, armlets and anklets. With her two principal hands she draws to her ear a bow of red flowers, together with an arrow tipped with an utpala bud. With her remaining hands she holds a noose of red utpala and a hook. So should one imagine her.

At her crown, throat and heart one should visualize oṃ āḥ hūṃ. Then one should visualize a red hrīḥ on the sun at the center of the red utpala that rests on the sun at one's own heart. By its light rays all the Tathāgatas are urged and invited. When one supplicates them, they confer empowerment with jeweled vases filled with the five nectars, and the head is adorned with the Blessed One Amitābha: this is the sealing.

Then, from the seed at one's own heart, one should emanate the eight goddesses, worship perfectly with their respective offerings, the awakening mind and the rest, and gather them back into that same seed. One should praise with this, namely:

akṣara manta vajri a o
na u so bindu na cita
e so vara mahāsukha he o
na u pheṭi a na u kirata

Then, by the rite of tasting the nectar, one should prepare the five nectars and the lamps. One should meditate on the tongue as having the nature of a vajra arisen from a white syllable hūṃ, and bless them with the three syllables. In this way one should delight oneself, who is in essence Kurukullā.

Without parting from meditative concentration, one should recite the mantra. One should vividly imagine in the mind the garland of the eight-syllable mantra, oṃ kurukulle hrīḥ svāhā, which stands circling around the syllable hrīḥ, like a blazing garland of lamps; and, free of faults such as going too fast or too slow, one should count on a rosary of red sandalwood. By reciting it one million times, the sentient beings belonging to the wheel of the six classes come under one's power.

In the other sessions, one should imagine oneself as Kurukullā and make offerings, praises and so forth, and one should perform every activity, such as listening and explaining, with the pride of the Blessed Lady. Then one should make aspirations in accordance with one's wishes.

Here I shall also write down the instruction that has come down through the lineage of gurus. The seed at one's own heart, as pure nectar fluid, rises from the heart, circles round from the left of the head, and comes out through the opening of the right ear. Through the hollow opening of the arrow set on the string of the bow in the right hand, it enters the center of the utpala flower. By its light rays it is made to expand, and as it radiates, it enters the hearts of the sentient beings of the three realms of existence, rouses their lotuses, draws them in, and places them again in that same flower. Then, closing, it passes from the arrow through the opening of the ear, circles round to the right of the head, and abides at the center of the forehead. By the path of the avadhūtī, through the throat and the heart, it should be fully inserted, as the instruction teaches, at the end of the navel, the place of great bliss.

Then one should visualize one's own body and the three worlds filled with the supreme bliss that arises from the stainless meditative absorption of wisdom and means, and in that instant one should call to mind the verse:

That which has no beginning, end or middle,
which is neither existence nor nirvāṇa,
this is the supreme great bliss;
there is no self, nor is there any other.

Again, in that way one should bring sentient beings under one's power, up to the point where one is able to establish them directly in the Great Seal.

Likewise, having meditated on the man or woman to be accomplished as having the nature of Kurukullā, the seed, as before, goes to the practitioner's arrow; it is made to expand by the light rays of the flower that rests in the hand of the one to be accomplished, and it enters the opening of the right ear. Having gone thus, it remains there; and having fully entered in exactly the same way, it reaches that place and should be made to remain there likewise.

On the form of Kurukullā, too, by placing the syllable hrīḥ on the parasol that intoxicates, women are captured. If that same syllable is written on the tongue with the substances, intelligence is produced. And for one seized by a snake, by meditating on it thoroughly in the center of the heart, the poison is consumed by the syllable of the Dharma. Through the heart mantra of Tārā, those who possess gold should know victory over the three worlds. Then, without doubt, the one to be accomplished comes under the practitioner's power and carries out all his aims.

By whatever merit there is
in having composed this means of accomplishing Kurukullā,
may there come the power
to establish all beings in awakening.

The Means of Accomplishing Kurukullā through Self-Blessing, composed by the master Sahajavilāsa, is complete.
