The Sādhana of the Innate Two-Armed Glorious Cakrasaṃvara (Śrīdvibhujasaṃvarasādhana)

The Means of Accomplishment of the Innate Two-Armed Glorious Cakrasaṃvara, known in Sanskrit as the Śrī Dvibhuja Saṃvara Sādhana and in Tibetan as dpal bde mchog phyag gnyis pa'i lhan cig skyes pa'i sgrub thabs, is a short tantric meditation manual devoted to the innate, or sahaja, form of Cakrasaṃvara with two arms. It opens with homage to the glorious innate Cakrasaṃvara and then guides the practitioner to generate the mind of awakening, aspiring to buddhahood in order to free all beings of the three realms from suffering and establish them in perfect bliss. The text then names three kinds of yoga to be understood: blessing, thorough examination, and the yoga of the inconceivable perfected form. It goes on to describe an instantaneous self-generation in which one arises at once as the Vajraḍāka, like a being born miraculously, without the gradual visualization of seed syllables. This work is Toh 1438 in the Tengyur, where it belongs to the tantra commentaries as one of the many sādhanas of the Cakrasaṃvara cycle.

In the language of India: śrī dvibhuja saṃbara sādhanaṃ. In the language of Tibet: The Means of Accomplishment of the Innate Two-Armed Glorious Cakrasaṃvara.

Homage to the glorious innate Cakrasaṃvara.

"Through my virtue, so as to clear away every suffering of the three realms, to the very end of existence,
And to establish all beings in complete and perfect bliss,
I shall attain buddhahood": having generated this aspiration,
One should also fully develop the engaging mind, for the sake of others.

Three kinds of yoga should be known:
Blessing, thorough examination,
And the yoga of the inconceivable perfected form,
Which the buddhas have praised.

Through instantaneous union
One becomes oneself the Vajraḍāka.
Like beings who are born miraculously,
One should meditate without seed syllables.

With a garland of skulls set upon the forehead,
Bearing a half moon upon the crown,
With the six mudrās and a garland of heads,
Three eyes, and a crossed vajra,
Standing with the right leg extended,
Upon a sun on a lotus of many colors,
Pressing down Bhairava and Kālarātri,
And wearing a tiger skin;
Black, with Akṣobhya as crest ornament,
With vajra and bell, and with matted locks.

The Hero's consort, Vajravārāhī,
Holds a vajra and a skull cup filled with blood.
She is red, her girdle marked with skull fragments,
With three eyes and a garland of heads,
Bearing the five mudrās, her hair hanging loose,
Clothed in the directions, her head adorned with buddhas.

In this way, complete in an instant,
I should meditate upon my body.
Clear, with the nature of the five wisdoms,
It radiates the light rays of the buddhas.
Making these the single domain of the mind,
Contemplate them alone for a long time.
Having established beings in that state,
Then begin the joy of the buddhas.

From the banner of the dharmadhātu, all passion,
The excellent, not inferior, flowings,
The host of joys of perfect awareness,
And that which has flowing yet does not emerge:
This is the vast mental attention.
Upon the radiating streams of the conditioned
Meditate constantly, and in just this way
Recollect the clear light.

The many-colored lotus and the sun, Bhairava and Kālarātri,
The Beautiful Lady, the Lord, the sky jewel, the five tathāgatas,
The body and the head, the white light, the bindu and the nāda:
These one should gradually dissolve into the state free of obscuration.

Just as breath upon a mirror
Fades away completely,
So, in the true culmination of yoga,
One should dissolve again and again.

All-pervading, without appearance,
With a mind of one taste with compassion,
The Lord, just as he is, embraces emptiness
In a single instant.

Embraced by that Blessed Lady, Wisdom, with aspects and without aspects;
Free of arising and perishing, immutable bliss, who has left behind the blisses of laughing and the rest;
Begetter of the buddhas, endowed with the three bodies, the one who truly knows the three realms;
Omniscient, supreme primordial Buddha: to that very Blessed One I bow.

Immutable bliss, wisdom, is time,
The very nature of method and compassion;
The wheel that moves in the aspect of knowables
Is wisdom, the very nature of emptiness.

kaṃ is that bliss, the mind without a cause,
Compassion, immutable wisdom;
Emptiness, like pratisena,
Is the seeing of all things.

With hands and feet on every side,
With eyes and heads and faces on every side,
Mindful on every side in the world,
It abides, pervading all things everywhere.

This is the essential body,
The nonduality of emptiness and compassion,
Renowned by the name "neuter,"
And in some places called "union."
The aggregates and the rest, phenomena free of obscuration,
Abide here within it.
This characteristic of support and supported
Is entirely the maṇḍala itself.

The Means of Accomplishment of the Innate Two-Armed Glorious Cakrasaṃvara, composed by the great paṇḍita who attained accomplishment, the glorious Vajraghaṇṭāpa, is complete.

It was translated, edited and finalized by the Indian preceptor, the great paṇḍita learned in the five sciences who attained accomplishment, the venerable Ratnarakṣita, and by Chak Lotsāwa, the Śākya monk Chöjé Pal.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

"The blisses of laughing and the rest" refers to the graded blisses of laughing, gazing, holding hands and embracing, associated with the four classes of tantra. In the verse beginning "Immutable bliss, wisdom, is time," "time" and "wheel" gloss the two halves of the name Kālacakra. The verse on the "banner of the dharmadhātu" is highly compressed, and its rendering is tentative. "Sky jewel" is kept literal, since its referent in this dissolution list is uncertain; the term often denotes the sun. "Pratisena" (prasenā) is a form of divination in which images appear in a mirror or similar medium, here used as a simile for emptiness in which all things are seen. "The Hero's consort" renders dpa' bo rdo rje phag mo, which may also be read as "the Hero, with Vajravārāhī."
