The Dhāraṇī Called 'The Noble Immovable One' (Ārya-Acalā-nāma-dhāraṇī)

Toh 631 in the Kangyur is a dhāraṇī text known in Sanskrit as the Ārya-Acalā-nāma-dhāraṇī and in Tibetan as Pakpa Miyowa Zhejawa'i Zung, meaning "The Noble Immovable One." It belongs to the dhāraṇī section of the Kangyur, a class of canonical works centered on sacred ritual formulas understood to carry protective and purifying power. The text opens in the heaven of the Thirty-Three Gods, where the Buddha is surrounded by an assembly of great bodhisattvas, including Maitreya, Mañjuśrī, Avalokiteśvara, and Vajrapāṇi, all of whom have attained the tenth bodhisattva ground. The bodhisattva Maitreya initiates a dialogue concerning the harms inflicted on human beings in Jambudvīpa by malevolent spirits, gods, and nāgas, providing the narrative occasion for the Buddha to impart the dhāraṇī and its associated benefits.

In the language of India: Ārya-Acalā-nāma-dhāraṇī. In the language of Tibet: The dhāraṇī called "The Noble Immovable One."

Homage to the Three Jewels.

Thus did I hear. At one time the Blessed One was dwelling in the Palace of the Thirty-Three Gods. At that time those gathered in the Blessed One's retinue were exclusively bodhisattvas who had reached the tenth ground: Samantabhadra, Ākāśagarbha, Kṣitigarbha, Sarvanivaraṇaviṣkambhin, Mañjuśrī, Avalokiteśvara, Vajrapāṇi, Maitreya, and others.

Then the bodhisattva Maitreya rose from his seat, arranged his upper robe over one shoulder, placed his right knee on the ground, and addressed the Blessed One in these words: "Blessed One, the gods, nāgas, yakṣas, and malevolent flesh-eating spirits dwelling in Jambudvīpa bring forth human disease, livestock disease, epidemic disease, harm, untimely death, hatred, bad dreams, unseasonable winds, hail, earthquakes, and the like. Please, Blessed One, pacify all of these."

When this request was made, the Blessed One, without speaking a word, entered into meditative absorption in the samādhi called "The Gate of Perfect Purity." From the pores of his body there blazed forth flaming rays of light, and they became a single mass of blazing fire reaching throughout the desire realm, the form realm, and the formless realm. At that time even the light of the sun, the moon, and the rest was dimmed and extinguished, and the light of the gods too was dimmed and extinguished. The nāgas, yakṣas, flesh-eating spirits, and the like were as trees shaken by a fierce wind.

Then the Blessed One entered into meditative absorption in the samādhi called "The Non-Conceptual Basic Space of Phenomena." Breaking the mudrā of equipoise, he struck the earth with the knee of his foot. From this there arose wrathful ones of the stable body of the essential nature of mind, seated with one leg extended and one drawn in. Together with immeasurable emanations of wrathful ones born from skillful means, they filled all of space with light. Within maṇḍalas of light there dwelled Brahmā, Viṣṇu, the Great God (Śiva), and the four great fierce ones — emanated ones who terrify with the cry "Ha! Hūṃ! Hrīḥ!" and who destroy attachment, aversion, delusion, and miserliness. And emanated ones who terrify nāgas, yakṣas, flesh-eating spirits, hungry ghosts, and the like with the cry "Haṃ! Maṃ!" were also sent forth. There were also emanated wrathful ones of the stable body of the essential nature of speech, seated with one knee and one thumb placed upon the ground, one leg raised with the knee set down — the stable-bodied wrathful ones; and those of the essential nature of wisdom, filling space with light from a sword blazing with fiery radiance — these too were emanated.

Then the Blessed One spoke the mantra of the wrathful ones born from his blessing:

Namaḥ samanta-vajrāṇāṃ. Acala-kaṇa-caṇḍa-mahāroṣaṇa-sphoṭaya hūṃ vajra-dhṛk khaḍga. Hūṃ maṃ hūṃ maṃ.

The mudrā is this: the right hand forms a fist, with the ring finger and little finger extended upward — this is the sword mudrā; one traces a boundary in a clockwise direction.

Namaḥ samanta-vajrāṇāṃ. Acala-kaṇa-caṇḍa-mahāroṣaṇa. Sphoṭaya hūṃ. Amṛta hūṃ hūṃ.

The mudrā for this: the fingers are extended and the little finger is pressed down by the thumb — this is the three-pointed vajra mudrā; one circles clockwise and dispels obstacles.

Then the Blessed One spoke the protection through the samādhi of perfect purity: Visualize the red syllable Raṃ at the crown of the head. Having been stabilized by this syllable of perfect purity —

Visualize the body as a vajra and sword, together with blazing fire and light.

Namaḥ samanta-vajrāṇāṃ. Acala-kaṇa-caṇḍa-mahāroṣaṇa-katebhyaḥ. Sarva-vigatebhyo. Viśvamukhe-bhyaḥ. Sarva-tathāgata hāṃ māṃ rakṣa.

The mudrā for this: join the palms in the vajra gesture, place the two index fingers crossed behind one another at the middle knuckle, and position them at the center of the brow.

Then the Blessed One spoke the protection through the great awareness of the awakening mind. Look with the form of the syllable Hūṃ as ground.

Namaḥ samanta-vajrāṇāṃ. Acala-kaṇa-caṇḍa-mahāroṣaṇa-katebhyaḥ. Sarva-vigatebhyo. Viśvamukhe-bhyaḥ. Sarva-tathāgata hāṃ māṃ rakṣa mahābale. Sarva-tathāgata-prameni-ragardha hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ tratha tratha. Apratihate svāhā.

The mudrā for this: join the palms in the vajra gesture, curve the two index fingers inward, touch the tips of the two middle fingers together, extend the two ring fingers straight out, bend the two little fingers slightly inward, and hold this at the heart. This is the protection of the mother. Any obstacle-maker, misleading spirit, or harmful force whatsoever will be scattered merely by remembering it.

Then the Blessed One entered into meditative absorption in the samādhi of loving-kindness, and blessed the various foods — those eaten in mouthfuls, those taken as meals, those licked, those drunk, those tasted, and so on — as well as forms, sounds, smells, and tastes of all kinds:

Oṃ sarva-dharmatā-sita hūṃ — by this they were purified.

Oṃ amṛta oṃ hūṃ hrīḥ — this is for the fulfillment of desire.

Oṃ namaḥ sarva-tathāgata-avalokite. Saṃbhara saṃbhara hūṃ — this is recited seven times seven times, and having dedicated the merit, all will be satisfied.

The mudrā is the mudrā of supreme giving.

Then the Blessed One spoke again: If one wishes to rise, one should recite this mantra.

Namo ratnatrayāya. Nama Ścanḍa-vajrapāṇaye. Namo vajra-krodhāya. Daṃṣṭrodkaṭa-bhairavāya. Asi-musala-paraśu-pāśa-hastāya. Tadyathā: Oṃ amṛta-kuṇḍali. Kha kha khāhi khāhi. Tiṣṭha tiṣṭha. Bandha bandha. Hana hana. Gṛhṇa gṛhṇa. Gardha gardha. Visphoṭaya visphoṭaya. Sarva-vighnaṃ vināyakānāṃ mahā-gaṇa-pati jīvita-antakaraṇa svāhā.

Clasp both hands into fists with the palms facing inward, extend the index fingers, cross them at the chest, and bind them in this mudrā. This same mudrā is also used when casting a torma offering and when departing.

For the touching and the bathing: into fragrant water placed in a precious vase, recite seven times seven times:

Namaḥ samanta-vajrāṇāṃ. Acala-kana-ya amṛta.

Then, holding the vase with the left hand, bend the middle finger and ring finger of the right hand slightly inward, visualize the syllable Aḥ inside the vessel, pour in the water, and bathe with:

Oṃ śuddha-dunte śodhanaye svāhā.

Thus did the Blessed One declare — and this was addressed to the bodhisattva Maitreya.

Then the four great kings, together with their retinues, and the gods of the Heaven of the Thirty-Three, the gods of the Heaven Free from Strife, the gods of Tuṣita, the gods of Nirmāṇarati, and the gods of Paranirmitavaśavartin, bearing flowers, drums, incense, divine foods, and divine sons and daughters adorned with jeweled ornaments, along with immeasurable chariots and immeasurable articles of offering, made vast offerings and circumambulations to the Blessed One, and then took their seats alongside the bodhisattva Maitreya.

Then the bodhisattva Maitreya, filled with joy, looked to the Blessed One. The Blessed One, knowing Maitreya's intention, entered into meditative equipoise in the first dhyāna, having relinquished the pleasures of desire, remaining only in joyful discursive thought. Then, from the first dhyāna, he entered into meditative equipoise in the second dhyāna, having relinquished the discursive engagement of one-pointed focus, remaining only in joy and bliss.

From the second dhyāna he entered into meditative equipoise in the third dhyāna, having relinquished discursive thought and examination, remaining only in bliss. Then from the third dhyāna he entered into meditative equipoise in the fourth dhyāna, remaining only in the bliss of feelings drawing near to stillness.

Then the Blessed One declared: "When one is endowed with these meditative absorptions, there is perfect purification. Through loving-kindness, compassion, joy, and equanimity, the continuum of the awakening mind is cultivated. Likewise, all phenomena are, like space, freed from the four extremes.

Namaḥ samanta-vajrāṇāṃ. Dharmadhātu-svabhāva-ātmako'ham."

Having said these words, the mudrā is as follows: each hand separately forms a fist, the two thumbs are tucked inside, and the two forefingers are raised upright.

This concludes the first chapter, the chapter of introduction, from the noble dhāraṇī of Acala.

Then the Blessed One declared: "Sitting in the vajra cross-legged posture, visualize at the heart a solar maṇḍala bearing the mind-seed, its light filling all of space. From that seed, all phenomena radiate and gather back with light-rays expanding and contracting, accompanied by the sound of the words: 'beyond the path of speech.' From lotus flowers of various colors, rising stainless and fully bloomed upon the seat of the lord of the assembly, together with seven-horsed sun-discs radiating light in all directions from which they spring, through a mind of sky-blue color, identical in essence with existence itself, filling the entire expanse of space with light — there arise blue wisdom-swords. From these, as the great wrathful one who conquers all māras and all that is malign, who utterly vanquishes all conceptual thought, there is a form stainless and youthful.

This form is youthful and of full and rounded body, like a vigorous young man in the flush of youth. His body color is deep dark blue, like the color of a dark rain-cloud in summer. He fills all of space without remainder with light, like the sun with its seven horses illuminating every continent. His hair is bound together in a single topknot and stands upright, like the fruit of the āmalaka tree. His body is slightly swaying and moving, like a wishing-fulfillment tree bent by the wind at a meeting-place of all. He stands with one leg extended and one drawn up, like the gait of a great athlete. His three eyes are very red, like the flower of the palāśa tree. His gaze directed upward and downward burns away the obstructions above and below, like an asura who does not eat the scattered food-offerings. His fangs are bared, like a half-moon. His tongue moves with great swiftness, like lightning across the sky. His brows are drawn into a fierce frown, like the leaves of the palākṣa tree. In his right hand he holds a sword, like the falling light of a great planet. In his left hand he holds a serpent-lasso, like the gall-bladder of the ocean.

With such a form he brings under his power Brahmā (Brahmā, lord of the highest heavens), Śakra (Indra, king of the gods), the four great kings, the guardians of the directions, and all the rest. He subdues malignant gods, nāgas, rākṣasas (flesh-eating spirits), piśācas (carnivorous demons), kumbhāṇḍas, serpents, and all the like.

His mantra, as proclaimed by the Blessed One, is to be recited thus: Namaḥ samanta-vajrāṇāṃ traṭ. Amogha-caṇḍa-mahāroṣaṇa. Sphoṭaya hūṃ. Tramaya tramaya hāṃ. Tratahaṃmaṃ.

By this mantra, all injury and violent harm, hatred and fierce enmity, evil possession and perverse leading astray, rākṣasas, gods, nāgas, kumbhāṇḍas, piśācas, and all the rest shall be pacified. Disorders arising from wind, disorders arising from bile, disorders arising from phlegm, tumors, swellings,

itching, leprosy, pox, small pox, fever lasting one day, fever lasting two days, fever lasting half a month, fever of exhaustion, intermittent fever, and chronic fever — from all of these one shall be freed. From harm caused by lions, tigers, bears, brown bears, wolves, venomous snakes, insects, mosquitoes, and bees, one shall be freed. From royal punishment and the terror of armies, one shall be freed. From thieves, slaves, and those who quarrel and dispute over food, one shall be freed. From the terror of water, the terror of fire, the terror of earthquakes, and the terror of lightning strikes, one shall be freed. From the terror of swords, śaktis (throwing spears), battle-axes, lances, arrows, great arrows, wheels, and weapons, one shall be freed. From death and untimely death, one shall be freed. All sins, including the five acts of immediate retribution and the like, shall be purified. One shall not be reborn in the hells, among hungry ghosts, in the realm of animals, in border regions, lacking the sense faculties, among gods with very long lives, holding wrong views, or in places where no buddha has appeared.

This dhāraṇī mantra was taught by all the buddhas of the three times, and even reciting it once causes all sins to be purified. The person who recites it shall swiftly attain the vajra-like path of the nature of reality (dharmatā).

Then, upon the syllables arrayed in pure rows within a seven-horse chariot, visualize the letters filling the entire sky with light. To look with a gaze of loving-kindness, visualize the yellow syllable Hāṃ at the eyes. At the channel of vitality, visualize the black syllable Maṃ. At the crown, visualize the syllables of pure reality, and recite this mantra: Namaḥ samanta-vajrapāṇi caṇḍa-mahāroṣaṇa hūṃ. Then join the palms together. Raise the middle fingers upright with the remaining fingers curved slightly inward at the back, and do likewise with the little fingers — this is the vajra mudrā. By its blessing, ignorance is vanquished and one is consecrated into the very body of primordial wisdom.

Into a vessel of gold or the like, pour a variety of foods — barley, rice, wheat, barley flour, sesame, and the like, along with yogurt, milk, honey, molasses, gold, silver, copper, pearls, and coral. Consecrate them with the mantra of the three grains, and recite this mantra: Namaḥ samanta-tathāgatebhyaḥ. Viśvamukhe­bhyaḥ. Sarva-thā kham utgate. Spharaṇa imaṃ gagana kham. The mudrā for this is the mudrā of supreme giving.

Reciting Namaḥ sarva-tathāgata-avalokite. Oṃ sambhāra sambhāra hūṃ, make offerings to all spirits.

Then recite this secret mantra: Namaḥ samanta-buddhānāṃ. Caṇḍa-mahāroṣaṇa asame trisame mahāsamaya. Sarva-pāpaṃ svāhā. Join the palms together in supplication and restore the sacred commitments (samaya).

This is the second chapter of the noble dhāraṇī of Acala.

Then these are the sacred commitments: The buddha, the Dharma, and the Saṅgha are distinguished as fruit, path, and practice. Beginning from whence one starts, one shall honor them with distinction and never be separated from them. One shall continuously cultivate the stream of immeasurable awakening mind and make vast aspirations. One shall continuously uphold and never be parted from the mantras, mudrās, and their supports. One shall abandon from the heart any agitated mind toward the gods, the lama, and companions on the path to awakening. Preceded by loving-kindness and compassion toward all sentient beings with breath, one shall abandon harm and

harm and never abandon it. One shall abandon miserliness and practice generosity toward sentient beings. One shall uphold the ethical conduct that abandons harm and injury to sentient beings. One shall be patient toward those who harm oneself. One shall never abandon diligence in accomplishing the benefit of oneself and others. Abandoning distraction, one shall enter into meditative concentration. In sleeping and rising, at all times, one shall not be distracted from the meaning of naturally purified awareness. To those without sacred commitments (samaya) and who do not abide by them, one shall not teach or expound meditative concentration, meditative absorption, mantras, mudrās, and the like. Killing, taking what is not given, sexual misconduct, and false speech are to be abandoned by one who wishes to practice and live the holy life of celibacy. One shall be respectful toward monks, toward those who have generated awakening mind, and toward those who hold their sacred commitments, and one shall not despise them. One shall be endowed with the preparatory practices at the session intervals.

Anger, offensive speech, belching and passing wind, foul odors from bodily excretions, and the unclean things such as unwashed hair and nails — all of these, together with foul and unpleasant odors, stench and malodor — all of these shall be abandoned entirely. As for one's dwelling, it should be a pleasing incense-perfumed house, clean and unstained, its auspicious door facing east — a house with a skylight, or a brahmin's home, or the pleasure grove of the Saṅgha, or a temple, or a mountain peak, or a place where birds call, or a hermitage, or a forest edge, or a pleasant meadow — there, having cleaned and sprinkled it with fragrant water, one shall spread a grass seat and dwell. One shall avoid sitting on a high throne. With fasting, one shall eat curds, milk, butter, molasses, flatbread, sesame oil, barley flour, rice, and rice dishes and the like. Meat, alcohol, garlic, and all pungent foods shall be entirely abandoned. With regard to months, stars, the count of days, and so forth, during the bright fortnights one shall make prayers to the Three Jewels and accomplish the practice. What is not appropriate is to proceed with hope, cunning, or roughness. There is no contradiction in proceeding gently and with dark earnestness for the sake of desire's aim. Always, in order to generate faith in the meditational deity (yidam), one shall accomplish the practice while maintaining a loving intention.

The emanated wrathful one Vajra-Power (Vajraugha) is deep black in color, his very nature being the ego and ego-clinging, bearing a meteor-iron wheel. Vajra-Great-Passion (Vajramahārāga) is red in color, his face a bow that attracts and causes attraction. Vajra-Sharp (Vajratīkṣṇa) is green, his nature being grasping and utter grasping, holding an iron hook. Vajra-Blazing (Vajrajvāla) is red in color, his nature being ignorance and the destroyer of ignorance, holding a vajra-cliff. Vajra-Wrathful (Vajrakrodha) is yellow, bearing a flaming sword, his nature being wrath and the wrathful. Vajra-Great-Fierce (Vajracaṇḍamahā) is dark green in color, his nature being equanimity and the state of equanimity, holding a cloud-lasso. Vajra-Hearth-Lord (Vajrāgnikuṇḍa) is smoke-colored, his nature being action and the agent of action, holding a clay drum. Vajra-Great-Power (Vajramahābala) is dark blue, holding a meteor-iron wheel, his nature being the inferior and the nature of the inferior. Vajra-Causal-Power (Vajrahetu-bala) is dark red, his nature being dependent origination and arising through dependent origination, holding a trident. Vajra-Great-Discerner (Vajravibhāga) is of variegated activity-color, his nature being freedom from extremes and liberation from extremes, holding a single-pointed vajra. These ten, of the nature of ten, radiate forth brilliantly from a body ablaze with fire.

This is the third chapter of the dhāraṇī known as "The Noble Immovable One" (Ārya Acala).

Then the Blessed One, in order to demonstrate this in a manner held in common, declared to the bodhisattva Maitreya the ritual procedure for it.

On ground that is pleasant and fragrant, one should make a maṇḍala the width of an arrow's span, perform the protection ritual of the five aspects, and scatter blue flowers.

Then one should take a vase made of gold, silver, or copper, fashioned to the measure of a monk's bowl. Into it one should place five kinds of precious substances: gold, silver, copper, coral, and pearl. One should also place inside it honey, molasses, raw sugar, yogurt, milk, and butter. One should place inside it barley, rice, wheat, sesame, wheat grain, and kuśa grass. One should place inside it long pepper, saffron, nutmeg, cardamom, kakola, and cloves. One should also place inside it fragrances such as sandalwood, aloeswood, bdellium resin, musk, benzoin, and camphor. One should also pour in water drawn from an ocean, a lake, a marsh, a flowing stream, and unspoiled, clear springs. Having then bound the neck of the vase with a silk or cotton cloth purified with scented water, one should set it in place. A tree adorned at its crown with fragrant substances, flowers, and fruits should be prepared, and one should also arrange perfume, incense, flowers, lamps, unguents, food offerings for the gods, and the sounds of music. For the torma of all spirits, one should offer seven portions, or five, or however many are appropriate.

To bind the boundaries one should recite: Namaḥ samanta vajrāṇāṃ, acala caṇḍa mahāroṣaṇa, sarva vighnan sphoṭaya, vajra dhṛk khaḍga hūṃ.

Likewise, one should complete the protective ritual procedure. Having fastened a cord of dhāraṇī to the vase and transformed it with pure syllables, from the seed syllable Bhruṃ there arises a vase endowed with proper color and measure. From the seed syllable of the mind, the Blessed One — dwelling in meditative equipoise in the very reality of suchness, accompanied by bodhisattvas, pratyekabuddhas, and noble persons — is envisioned as seated upon a lion throne, upon a variegated lotus and moon disk seat. One should meditate upon the Blessed One by way of six aspects possessing the nature of reality: the nature of reality, mind, the mental aspect, sound, echo, form, and reflected image — in this form seated upon a lotus and moon disk, and atop Gaṇapati the elephant-faced one. From the seed syllable of the mind, a sword wreathed in flames of fire fills all of space.

From that, one should visualize the form of the Blessed One as the Tamer of Māras, and make offering to the Blessed One with offerings.

One should recite: Namaḥ samanta buddhānāṃ, oṃ āḥ hūṃ, pravarṣad kara pratīccha hūṃ.

One should recite: Namaḥ samanta buddhānāṃ, oṃ āḥ hūṃ, sarva devātā acintyāmṛtadhārāya svāhā.

One should recite: Namaḥ samanta buddhānāṃ, āhara bhagapāna, sarva vidyādhara pūjite svāhā jite svāhā.

One should recite: Namaḥ samanta buddhānāṃ, oṃ āḥ hūṃ, dharmadhātu anugate dhūpe hūṃ svāhā.

One should recite: Namaḥ samanta buddhānāṃ, oṃ āḥ hūṃ, mahāmaitribhyo gate puṣpe hūṃ.

One should recite: Namaḥ samanta buddhānāṃ, oṃ āḥ hūṃ, sarva tathāgata aciprana abhāva gagana bhyānirya āloke hūṃ svāhā.

One should recite: Namaḥ samanta buddhānāṃ, oṃ āḥ hūṃ, gagana samaya gandhe hūṃ svāhā.

One should recite: Namaḥ samanta buddhānāṃ, āhara sarva tathāgate naivedye pūjana spharaṇaye hūṃ svāhā.

One should recite: Namaḥ samanta buddhānāṃ, abhismāraye, spharaṇa himaṃ gagana khaṃ, dharmadhātu, ākāśāya, samantraya, sarva tathāgata pariśuddha maṇḍale, mama praṇidhi pūjana ma belena dharmadhātu, adhiṣṭhāna pāca hūṃ svāhā.

One should then ring the vajra and bell together.

One joins the palms, bends both index fingers slightly back behind the middle fingers, interlaces the ring fingers and little fingers, and bends the thumbs slightly inward: this is the mudrā of water for offering. With the fingers resting on the middle fingers and bent slightly inward, and the middle fingers raised upright: this is the mudrā of incense. The fingers are interlaced and tucked inward, and the index fingers are pressed down by the thumbs: this is the mudrā of flowers. The tips of the thumbs and middle fingers are brought together, and the remaining fingers are held in a fist: this is the mudrā of lamps. The fearlessness mudrā, with the left hand lifting at the wrist: this is the mudrā of perfume. The palms are joined at the edges, the two little fingers are crossed and raised upright: this is the mudrā of food offerings. One circles the hands above the crown of the head, joins the palms hollow, and then releases them: this is the great offering, by which the offering substances are purified by the goddesses of intrinsic purity.

One recites: Namaḥ samanta buddhānāṃ, abhāva svabhāva mudgatānāṃ, namaḥ pratyeka buddha ārya śrāvakānāṃ, namo bodhisattvānāṃ daśabhūmi pratiṣṭhita śaraṇānāṃ, bodhisattvāya mahāsattvānāṃ, tadyathā: oṃ kha kha khāhi khāhi, duṣṭa svāhā, kāmakra asi musa la paraśu pāśa hastāya, catur bhujā catur mukha ṣaṭ caraṇa, gaccha ya gaccha ya, sarva vighnān ghāta vikṛtīnānāṃ, sarva bhūta bhayaṃkara aṭṭahāsa ninīte byāghra carma nivāsani, kuru kuru sarva dharmāṇāṃ, chinda chinda, sarva mantra, bhinda bhinda, para mantra, ākarṣaya ākarṣaya, sarva bhūta, niramatha niramatha, sarva duṣṭaṃ, praveśaya praveśaya, maṇḍala madhye bhaivas tvājī vitinta, kara kara, kuru kuru, mama sarva kāryaṃ, daha daha, paca paca, mā vilamba, samayaṃ anusmara, hūṃ hūṃ, sphoṭaya sphoṭaya, sarva ṣaṭ paripūraka, he bhagavān, kañci rāyasi, mama sarvārthaṃ sādhaya, amogha, caṇḍa mahāroṣaṇa sphoṭaya hūṃ, trama trama haṃ maṃ.

This is to be recited seven or twenty-one times. One visualizes paying homage to all buddhas and bodhisattvas, and recites it while visualizing the pacification of karma and karmic maturation, destruction, harm, injury, illness, and the like.

Then, when one wishes to perform the torma offering, at the time of recitation one should present it in accordance with the instructed manner.

This is the fourth chapter of the noble dhāraṇī of Acala.

Then the Blessed One taught the ritual of the painted cloth. One should take extremely pure cotton and have a brahmin or someone of pure conduct wash it, then pray to the Three Jewels and examine it. Cloth from low castes and common people and the like is to be rejected. The cloth should be washed clean with a solution of saffron, musk, and aloeswood and the like. One then writes this mantra on birch bark or on the cloth and affixes it.

A ā, i ī, u ū, ṛ ṝ, ḷ ḹ, e ai, o au, aṃ aḥ, ka kha ga gha ṅa, ca cha ja jha ña, ṭa ṭha ḍa ḍha ṇa, ta tha da dha na, pa pha ba bha ma, ya ra la va, śa ṣa sa ha kṣa.

Having written these, one places them.
