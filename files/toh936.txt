The Six Heart Mantras of the Perfections

This short Kangyur work, which might be called in English something like The Heart Mantras of the Perfections, opens with homage to the Three Jewels and then sets out a series of brief Sanskrit mantras, each linked to one of the perfections, the core virtues cultivated on the bodhisattva path. Each mantra is introduced with the promise that hearing it just once brings the quality it embodies, beginning with generosity and continuing through moral discipline, patience, and diligence, and presumably on to the remaining perfections. Its source title lines do not give a distinct Sanskrit or Tibetan title, so the work is best identified by its content: an invocation that pairs devotion to awakened beings with the aspiration to embody these virtues. It is Toh 936 in the Kangyur, where it belongs among the dhāraṇī and mantra texts, compact ritual formulas valued for the blessing and protection they are believed to confer through recitation and hearing.

Homage to the Three Jewels.

By hearing this heart mantra of generosity just once, one will attain generosity.
oṃ namastvai bhagavati dānādhipataye svāhā

By hearing this heart mantra of moral discipline just once, one will attain moral discipline.
namaḥ sarva tathāgatānāṃ mahākanādamakānāṃ namo bhagavatyai śīlaparipūrṇaśarīyai śīlaṃ me sādhaya sādhaya kinatiṣṭa saṃbuṃddho bhagavana dharmasya samājñānāpayati svāhā

By hearing this heart mantra of patience just once, one will attain patience.
namaḥ sarva tathāgatāya acinta gocarya namo kṣānatiyai bahubhiḥ tthāgatanabhutakoṭibhivarivaratitayai rakṣa kuru turu turu samaya manusmara śākyamuni sarva tathāgatānāṃ aparimitā jñāna gocaro jñānāpayati svāhā

By hearing this heart mantra of diligence just once, one will attain diligence.
namaḥ sarva tathāgatānāṃ macinatya viśāyanā namastute bhagavate vīryapāramitā vīryana kuru vīrye vīrye mahāvīrya tathāgata sarva jñānāpayati svāhā

By hearing this heart mantra of meditative concentration just once, one will attain meditative concentration.
namaḥ sarva tathāgatānāṃ oṃ vimale niramale dhyāne dhyāne dhyānapriye hūṃ phaṭ

By hearing this heart mantra of wisdom just once, one will attain wisdom.
namaḥ sarva tathāgatāya aparimita jñāna gocarāya namo bhagavate jñāna pāramitāyai jñānāṃ ne kuru kuru vigāna tathāgatasya samaya manusmara hi hi hi hi jñā jñā jñā jñā hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ

Here ends the dhāraṇī through which the six perfections are retained.
