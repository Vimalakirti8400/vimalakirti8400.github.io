The Great Sūtra of the Great Assembly (Mahāsamāja Sūtra Nāma Mahāsūtra)

Toh 653 in the Kangyur is a sūtra known in Sanskrit as the Mahāsamāja Sūtra and in Tibetan as the Mdo chen po 'dus pa chen po'i mdo, rendered into English as the Great Sūtra of the Great Assembly. It is a Mahāyāna sūtra recounting an occasion when the Buddha Śākyamuni was dwelling at Kapilavastu among five hundred fully liberated arhats, and depicts a vast gathering of beings — human and divine — assembled to hear his teaching. As a canonical discourse attributed directly to the Buddha, it belongs to the sūtra class of texts within the Kangyur, the collection of translated Buddhist scriptures considered to be the word of the Buddha.

Buddha. Adhiṣṭhāna ni svāhā. Here ends the dhāraṇī of the noble Guru of the Victors.

In the language of India: Mahāsamāja Sūtra Nāma Mahāsūtra. In the language of Tibet: The Great Sūtra, called the Sūtra of the Great Assembly.

Homage to the Three Jewels.

Thus did I hear. At one time the Blessed One, Śākyamuni, was dwelling in the forest of the Śākyas at Kapilavastu, together with a full assembly of five hundred monks, every one of whom was an arhat — with defilements exhausted, who had accomplished what was to be done, had done what had to be done, had laid down the burden, had attained their own aim, had utterly exhausted all bonds to continued existence, and was completely liberated in mind through right knowledge. At that time the Blessed One was teaching the monks of the Saṅgha a discourse on Dharma connected with nirvāṇa. Then, from the world-systems of all ten directions, gods renowned for their great power arrived and paid honor to the Blessed One and the monastic Saṅgha.

Then four gods of the Brahmā realm dwelling in the Brahma-world thought to themselves: "The Blessed One, the Buddha, is dwelling in the forest of the Śākyas at Kapilavastu, together with five hundred monks, every one of them an arhat — with defilements exhausted, who had accomplished what was to be done, had done what had to be done, had laid down the burden, had attained their own aim, had utterly exhausted all bonds to continued existence, and completely liberated in mind through right knowledge. The Blessed One is teaching the monks of the Saṅgha a discourse on Dharma connected with nirvāṇa. Even now, from the world-systems of all ten directions, gods renowned for their great power are arriving and paying honor to the Blessed One and the monastic Saṅgha. Let us too go, lest we miss the opportunity to praise the Blessed One each in turn with a verse."

Then those four gods of the Brahmā realm — just as a strong man might extend a bent arm or bend an extended arm — vanished from the Brahma-world in an instant and appeared before the Blessed One. They took their place to one side, and each god of the Brahmā realm addressed him in verse.

The first god of the Brahmā realm spoke this verse:

Drawn to this great assembly,
all the host of gods has come from every side.
We have come to behold this Saṅgha
that none other can surpass.

The second god of the Brahmā realm spoke this verse:

With minds made straight and true,
the ascetics here strive earnestly.
Like a charioteer they have taken up the reins
and rightly restrained all their faculties.

The third god of the Brahmā realm spoke this verse:

Like a stake so soaked in water
that it cannot be split by the sharpest wedge,
they dwell in conduct free of craving,
tamed by the Guide like young elephants.

The fourth god of the Brahmā realm spoke this verse:

Those who go for refuge to the Buddha
will not go to the lower realms.
Having laid down the human body,
they will attain the body of a god.

Then the gods of the Pure Abode, renowned as greatly powerful, having rejoiced in the teaching of the sūtra, at that time recited this great assembly sūtra with these words: "Blessed One, we too, with these words, will rightly proclaim and rightly speak this sūtra."

"And the words of the secret mantra are these: tadyathā — teje tejabati citrecitrabati late latabati khame khambati kṣeme kṣamapati bhadre bhadrabati yaśe yaśabati dūte dūtabati capale capalabati khace khacabati."

"Blessed One, these are the words of the secret mantra of the gods of the Pure Abode. Blessed One, whoever retains this sūtra and these mantra words without confusion — for that person, for as long as they live, all spirits will afford protection, and all spirits will be pleased. No disease of any kind will be able to affect them. Wherever it is read aloud, that place will become auspicious, and for seven years those there will be protected. To whatever extent rain is desired, to that extent the rain will fall from the sky — and it will cease when wished, and hail will be driven away."

"It will also cease. Blessed One, if one sprinkles all those afflicted with fever, or recites the mantra over water and gives it to them to drink, all their fever diseases will be thoroughly pacified."

Then Brahmā addressed the Blessed One: "Blessed One, apart from the gods of the Pure Abode, these are also the knowledge mantras of the higher classes of gods who move in the form realm, and these are their subtle secret mantra words. This is what retains this sūtra here. Thus: bhale bhala male, dur dule, hime, phubpise, kācākṣi kuce si, mamāṇi ṣere thoraṇī, bambipampoṭi steke, tarake, gurugaro ṭe, guggumiti, tāre rakṣa, sūtra manupālaya thā. This is the subtle ritual of our knowledge mantra. Whoever retains this sūtra and these secret mantra words without fragmenting them, or reads them aloud — no humans or non-humans will come near that person with hostile intent, nor will anything harmful come, nor serpents, nor poison. Harmful things will be unable to affect that person's body. All spirits will become joyful. For as long as they live, they will be protected from all spirits. Whoever reads it aloud and whoever listens — they will be protected for seven years. If the mantra is recited over water and that water is sprinkled or given to drink, all fever diseases of that person will be swiftly pacified."

Then all the lords of the desire realm addressed the Blessed One: "Blessed One, this is also our domain, and this is the knowledge mantra for retaining this sūtra. Thus: liṇṭe li pe, lo pe, ba pe, pad ke, ka me, kāce kāce kṣi, kāca pi gale, du me du me, taṭe traṭe, ta da tra, ṭe tra ṭākṣi, golā, koli, kika koli ke sādhā, sūtra manupālaya thā, udgṛhṇaya ceti. Blessed One, whoever retains without fragmentation these secret mantra words and this well-spoken sūtra, holding them together, will be protected from all things. All spirits will become joyful. To whatever extent rain is desired from the sky, to that extent it will fall — and it will cease when wished. Hail too will fall and will cease when wished. One will be fully liberated from spirits and harmful influences. Poison too will be expelled. If the mantra is recited seven times over water and that water is sprinkled or given to drink, all fever diseases will be swiftly pacified."

Then Śakra, lord of the gods (Indra), addressed the Blessed One: "Blessed One, this knowledge mantra of ours likewise retains this sūtra. The higher gods who move in the desire realm have rejoiced in it, and the lower ones too have rejoiced in it well. Thus: vīre vīre vati, toṭe toṭa vati, bhadre bhadra vati, gone gona vati, meghe megha vati, jvalane jvalana vati, bhūti bhūta vati, khorate khorata pati, idan sūtra manugrihṇ ṭha, anupālaya tha ceti. Blessed One, whoever retains this sūtra and these secret mantra words without fragmenting them — for as long as they live, they will be protected from all spirits. No human or non-human whatsoever will harm them. Wherever it is read aloud, that place will be protected for as long as those there live."

All sentient beings, too, will rejoice. No one whatsoever will overcome them with power. Diseases of contagion will be unable to afflict their bodies. It will also bring benefit to all sentient beings. Wherever it is read aloud, protection will be given there for seven years. They will also be freed from spirits and malignant forces. Poisons will be overcome. All diseases of contagion will be dispelled. If the mantra words are recited over a thread or over water, and it is then tied on or sprinkled, one will be freed from all diseases of contagion.

"Blessed One, whoever retains and guards these four subtle rites of the vidyā mantras spoken by the four great kings, together with this sūtra, without fragmenting it and without misusing it — for as long as they live they will be protected. All spirits will not return to them. All diseases of contagion will not recur. They will be freed from the grip of malevolent forces and from all poisons. If the mantra words are recited over a thread or over water, and it is then tied on or sprinkled and bestowed as a gift, the rite will be accomplished for that person."

tadyathā — gale, ga ga gale, gale gani, khini khini, ghuṣe ghuṣe ghughuṣya, khana khakhane, khana khani, lupe lupi, lupa lupiti.

"Blessed One, if these secret mantra words and this sūtra are held without being fragmented and without being misused, protection will be given for as long as one lives. All diseases of contagion will be utterly pacified. Poisons will be unable to afflict the body."
