Toh 382 in the Kangyur is a tantric scripture belonging to the class of highest

Toh 382 in the Kangyur is a tantric scripture belonging to the class of highest yoga tantra, known in Tibetan as a section treating the profound esoteric doctrine of bindus — subtle drops or essential points — within the context of the self-arisen deity and the great elements. The text opens with the bodhisattva Vajragarbha and other great beings making offerings and prostrating before the Blessed One, then posing questions in verse about the nature of the self-arisen, its relation to the supreme deity, and the inner meaning of union with all buddhas, situating it within the genre of dialogic tantric revelation. While a distinct Sanskrit title is not clearly determinable from the available material, the scripture is framed as a teaching on the deepest secret of nondual bliss and the central element understood through inference. The colophon notes that the translation was written after Kongba examined and made revisions, indicating it passed through a phase of editorial refinement characteristic of later canonical transmission in Tibet.

Written after Kongba examined and made revisions.

Then the bodhisattvas, the great beings, headed by Vajragarbha, made further offerings, prostrated, and spoke these words:

If you would explain, settling the matter clearly,
the meaning of bindus within bindus,
then since the self-arisen dwells at my center,
let that self-arisen be spoken of.

The word "self-arisen" — so called — pertains to the great elements,
and is to be understood by inference.
This self-arisen Blessed One
is the singular supreme deity.

As for "supreme deity" —
those are the elements spoken of above.
Among them the central one is supremely excellent,
as expressed through the form of the primary lord.

In the supreme secret delight,
the self of all forever abides.

In what way, O Blessed One, does
union with all buddhas,
the binding of the ḍākinī net —
in what way does this form of vow
serve as a supremely beneficial vow?

The word "secret" is itself endowed with qualities;
"secret" too is proclaimed to be a quality.
Vajra-possessing is endowed with qualities.
It is praised by those who take supreme delight in the world, and blame is abandoned.
The word "secret" is a language of the outcast peoples;
even when sought externally,
this is to be known by direct perception,
by inference, and by the word of the qualified.

When mind cannot be seen —
this very thing called "mind"
is said to be both the qualified and the qualities.

In the supreme secret delight,
the self of all forever abides.

In this secret, the nature of reality abides.
With respect to that, the self of all —
that is where all things fully reside.

Whatever is the empty vajra-holder
rejoices together with bliss.

When, through the form of mind,
blue and other colors are not apprehended by the eye,
and when sounds and such are not heard by the ear —
then what is mind?
Then what is inference?

If this is not apprehended by the nose,
does fragrance dwell at the center or not?
Mind does not apprehend externally,
nor is there likewise any proximate enjoyment.
Taste is neither sweet nor pungent.
Likewise too with what is tangible.

In the supreme secret delight,
the self of all forever abides.

How, then, is this secret
to be contemplated through inference?

Just as wisdom itself
has gone into the form of letters —
in what other way is the word "Blessed One"?
What is meant by the word "deity"?
What is the definitive meaning of all buddhas?
What is to be called "union"?
What name belongs to the ḍākinī?
What sort of thing is the net?
What is called the vow?
And likewise, what is difficult to practice?
What did you mean when you spoke of no attachment?
And equally, what of the one without desire?
What name does "the Middle Way" belong to?
What is the illusion of all women?
Emptiness, the form realm and so forth —
what is explained and how is equality applied?

The first: six-fold transformation;
the varied lotus-painted body;
the great mudrā of Āli and Kāli;
the yoga of mind free of suchness;
and how is suchness good?
Meditative equipoise in yoga itself,
and the outer, though fierce, is rightly explained.
Moon, sun, perfect buddha —
what sort of arising is this?
Drawn and undrawn —
how are maṇḍalas to be established?
Likewise mantra, tantra itself,
the secret place — I do not understand —
and the four lotuses in particular:
how are self and what belongs to self?
Likewise mind and mental factors;
the essential nature of mind is said to be gnosis.
Likewise consciousness itself,
the things that are causes and results,
and likewise phenomena and their possessors —
what is called the stream of a river?
The ocean, too, is distinguished.
If you teach variety,
how will the many not be obtained?
You have spoken of consciousness and gnosis —

Likewise, what is the nature of taking the essence, the stretching out to the left and so forth? Likewise, what is the victory-bow and what else belongs to it? What is the sound of "principal" and "non-principal"? And what is said to be examination? The Dharma factors of great awakening — the thirty-seven inner ones and the bodies and so forth and what else is like them — what does your teaching of them to śrāvakas intend, and what is the purpose of that? What does the yoga of the form of great bliss of Vajrasattva accomplish? Brahmā, Īśvara, and Viṣṇu — likewise, what is the meaning of the petition to them? The god of desire together with his consort, the goddess Durgatipraśamanī who possesses the dhāraṇī — she who formerly went for refuge — the union of the two together, the maker of pleasure: what is this union-combat, and what is the secret of it? The Vajra-Bearer spoke these things as falsehood, yet afterward expressed them in words. Buddha, Vajrasattva, Brahmā, Viṣṇu, Īśvara, and the Moon — previously taught differently — all of these dwell forever as a single self. How is it that the Vajra-Bearer is peaceful, and how is it that he has a wrathful form? What is the dharmakāya? What is the saṃbhogakāya? How does the emanation arise as vajra itself, and how is Heruka explained? How is Padma the Lord of Dance? How does he take the form of Yama? Likewise, what of the supreme horse itself — how is it spoken of by rite, and what is said? Likewise the goddess and the wrathful one — what you have explained of the outcaste — what is the purpose of that? For now, how were the words of the goddesses collected? O Vajrasattva, you who speak of mantra — explain all of these correctly and completely.

The Blessed One declared: beginning with "Thus did I hear" and so forth, composed first, it recurs here as well, and it is to be spoken, O son of the lineage. The word "secret" has seven aspects; "seventh" means the seventh. The eighth is characterized by the inexpressible. By the word "secret" is meant the non-duality of mind and mental factors — this is said to be the Tathāgata itself. Because it has one taste and one essential nature in suchness, it is hidden there — hence "secret." Pervading all of space is the measure of the Blessed One; dwelling therein is the gnosis-mudrā of glorious Vajrasattva and so forth, together with the vajra-goddesses. O Blessed One, what is the supremely distinguished abode?

The Blessed One declared: "Supreme" means supremely distinguished — it is the domain of sacred primordial wisdom. "Where there is delight" means where there is experiential joy, free from all analogy. Through perfect union with the eyes and so forth, one makes joy arise — therefore it is called "where there is delight." Through the union of glorious Vajrasattva and going together with all the goddesses, extending to the ends of the expanse of space, in the great bliss of all the goddesses — the power arising from this union possessing the bliss of all unions is immeasurable. Rejoicing mutually and giving rise to joy: the joy of men and their delight is "where there is delight." "The self of all" means the self of all things. O son of the lineage, in this way the Blessed One has taught the abode as stated before. Therefore it is taught as the self of all Tathāgatas — "the self of all" is that which was taught. Thus being so, "the self of all" expresses the excellence of the abode. As declared, by the nature of generating each in the abode as spoken, he dwells forever in the midst of however many retinues of queens. The buddhas and all these buddhas — all buddhas —

equal to the number of the finest particles of dust in all world-systems. The quality of permanence and so forth — because it is made by those Tathāgatas, or because it is their intrinsic nature, the abode is called "the very substance of all buddhas" — substance here meaning the illusory or intrinsic nature. "Hero" — because of the capacity to act for the benefit of sentient beings. "Vajra" — because it is of the intrinsic nature of the wisdom of skillful means and wisdom-discernment. "Hero" — because it is unmoved by all conceptual thought. "Bliss" — because, from the union of joy spoken of before, it is greatly endowed with the nature of a mind that is utterly at peace. "Supreme" means especially exalted. This is said to express the ultimate excellence of these heroes dwelling in these stations of bliss.

Nevertheless, the line "the union of all buddhas and the vow of the ḍākinī web" is not desired in that sense. Rather, in order to speak of it in a different way, taking up what might be doubted, the Blessed One declared: the words "this, then" — that is to say, O son of the lineage, what was spoken there is precisely that same thing — "the union of all buddhas and the vow of the ḍākinī web" — that is the meaning. Surrounded by all the goddesses and, with the yoga of the identity of the glorious Vajrasattva complete, that is good fortune — to be supremely attached, endowed with supreme desire, in the accomplishment of the very practice of one's own identity. Being free of male dancers, dwelling within the retinue of one's own queens, with the mind utterly undistracted from what is meaningful, one should experience the bliss of true union with the goddesses. "True union" — that is yoga, it is said.

Steadiness, permanence, and ultimate contentment of mind. Or: "steadiness" — because one is not shaken by the winds of conceptual thought. Furthermore, "permanence" — because one abides to the very boundary of all extremes. "Ultimate contentment of mind" — because one directly experiences the bliss of all the goddesses. Steadiness, permanence, and ultimate contentment of mind — these are steadiness, permanence, and ultimate contentment of mind.

"All buddhas" refers to form, sound, smell, taste, and touch — the intrinsic natures of sense-objects. When one's own merit accumulation is in harmony as cause, what does one do? One makes use of what is one's own enjoyment and one consumes these. Otherwise, in a separate thing, it would not be experience that is one's own. The primordially born intrinsic nature of wisdom is not abandoned even by the Victors. Whatever within the intrinsic nature of the graduated union of various stages of practice — the conduct of desire and so forth — whatever is desired from the conduct of desire, those are desired as the ritual of Vajrasattva. Those whose intrinsic nature is excellent desire the siddhi of Vairocana. Similarly, those who conduct themselves in aversion, desire, miserliness, and laziness come to desire the rituals of Heruka, the Lord of the Lotus Dance, Vajrasūrya, and the Supreme Horse.

O son of the lineage, in just such a way, having generated how adverse conditions such as conduct in desire and so forth generate the glorious Vajrasattva and others, and having produced them from the yoga of the feast-maṇḍala, one tames disciples with the discourse of yoga. Discourse means declaration.

"What kind of discourse, O Blessed One?" The Blessed One declared: it is the discourse called "secret" and so forth — expressed in the manner of conventional truth.

Because it accords with the cause of the dharmakāya of the glorious Vajrasattva, and in order to engage in proximity to the intrinsic nature of the essential-nature body (svabhāvakāya), the glorious Vajrasattva and the others are generated. The intrinsic nature is to be declared.

"Firm" means firm like the Dharma-Mount Meru. By way of example, Mount Meru is difficult to turn back by the wind, and even the most violent storm cannot move it. In just the same way, this Blessed One, the glorious Vajrasattva, cannot be moved or displaced by the mental afflictions such as attachment and so forth, by the secondary afflictions, or by the ferocious and crushing assaults of malevolent non-Buddhists. Though dwelling in the charnel ground, he courses through the mindstreams of all sentient beings; the purified mind follows him through many gateways of the Dharma; and together with this, the Blessed One dwells within saṃsāra.

"Firm" refers to the aspect of stability. "Moving" is to be understood as grasped from within the mindstreams of sentient beings — these are to be known by the Blessed One. In the way that those who are the very selves of purification are of one form with others in their essential nature, for that very reason this Blessed One dwells within the minds of all the Tathāgatas. Sentient beings, too, are all Tathāgatas; because they abide in the ultimate reality of things, they are called Tathāgatas. "Moving" is like a monkey, and because one who stabilizes the minds of all sentient beings is called a bodhisattva, and because of that holding, it is samādhi (meditative absorption), and through discernment it becomes vajra itself. Therefore, mind alone is declared to be the very nature of movement, and not exterior phenomena and the like, for those are without substantial existence by way of either unity or multiplicity.

"All" means without exception. "Thing" means the meaning of abiding. "Existence" means birth. "The firm and the moving" means the firm and the moving. "Things and all" means all things; and that encompasses all the firm and the moving — things and existence. The intrinsic nature of all things both firm and moving is this: whatever is the intrinsic nature of the self of all is the self of all things both firm and moving. For that very reason, the Blessed One who holds the vajra spoke this vajra-word:

By the nature of reality (dharmatā) of the mind of Vajrasattva, which is the self of all things both firm and moving, that which will express these matters — the utterly pure dharmadhātu, the basic space of phenomena — is gathered together through the mirror-like wisdom and the rest, and all the splendors of the five buddhas — Akṣobhya and the others, the permanent and the immovable — are fully gathered.

The word "all" is to be taken as meaning "completely." "Union" means coming together. Where? In the secret — as the very self, the source of all phenomena, and nowhere else. Together with whom? With the Blessed One Vajrasattva — together with him through the nine aspects: his own true essential nature, emanation, and the rest. Yet through the single body that he manifests as the perfect saṃbhogakāya, everything from every direction converges on the self of that, like water and milk, and is fully held. Then, through the yoga of the great maṇḍala, within all outer and inner things, in that very instant, just as all buddhas have performed it, one realizes and knows it. Therefore the union together with all the buddhas and with those other than them is the union with all the buddhas. The time of union is the time of indivisibility.

Through the place of union, by entering into meditative equipoise in mind-only for all of that, things that have become unified mutually gather one another in; and since, through the intrinsic nature of all things, one proceeds everywhere, this is called "union with all buddhas." Therefore, through union with all buddhas, this — the perfect seeing of beings throughout the expanse of the ten directions — is declared to be the ḍākinī of union with all buddhas.

The word "net" is to be understood as a synonym for illusion. Just as illusory conjured elephants and the like arise from wood and other materials, so the Tathāgatas — Akṣobhya and the rest — do not abandon sentient beings who are to be tamed without form, and this is why.

Through contact with the form of Vajradevī and the other goddesses — the white one and the rest — one becomes that very reality, and by the illusory characteristic of union with all buddhas, whatever Tathāgata is stilled in mind through undefiled bliss becomes the many Tathāgatas.

Then one should recite by means of the word of the vow. The syllable "sam" means bliss. The word "bar" means "supremely excellent." "All women" has been explained above. "Illusion" is as it has been declared — meaning: "by illusion, through that very nature of illusion." This too is established by its intrinsic nature, and what is established is what is complete. How does one's own essential nature undergo full transformation? One's own form, and the form belonging to them — these are one's own form: the all-base consciousness (ālayavijñāna), the afflicted consciousness (kliṣṭamanas), the mentation (manas), and the consciousnesses of the eye, ear, nose, tongue, and body — the full transformation of all these is the full transformation through one's own essential nature. Through cultivation in the meditative absorption of selflessness, resting in the intrinsic nature of mirror-like wisdom, the wisdom of equality, discerning wisdom, and all-accomplishing wisdom, one undergoes transformation — that is the meaning. Since through the full transformation of one's own essential nature one obtains the illusory-natured attainment of all women, therefore:

Through the blissful delight of all that is gained,
all women come to be accomplished.

"Variegated" and "illusion" together make "variegated illusion" — forms of various colors and shapes, such as the forms of all the goddesses and the rest. Having taken up this mudrā of variegated illusion and fixed it in the mind, the following was declared: O ḍākinī, that which proceeds throughout the expanse of the ten directions, having entered into the retinue of queens — that is the Blessed One Vajrasattva himself — this is the meaning of the phrase "the mudrā of variegated illusion." What is widely renowned in all the treatises of the world and beyond the world yet is expressed in an obscure language through its own coded signs — that too is the meaning. "Ḍākinī" and the rest: all this has been stated in summary.

Since all women are the Blessed One's vajra goddesses, abiding in the eight aspects of the all-base consciousness and the rest — the consciousnesses that perpetuate saṃsāra — and undergoing transformation, they are accomplished through the illusory nature of the four wisdoms: mirror-like wisdom, the wisdom of equality, discerning wisdom, and all-accomplishing wisdom, by reason of that characteristic of transformation. The mirror-like wisdom and the rest belonging to these are the essential natures of variegated illusion; and their activity through illusion is the moving through space, which manifests as variegated form. Therefore this mudrā of variegated illusion is to be spoken of as "ḍākinī." The barbarians' speaking of it as ḍākinī merely on account of their moving through the sky alone is not what is meant. As it is said:

Accomplished in ranging through all of space —
that is why she is celebrated as ḍākinī.

That, then, is the explanation.

"From all" refers to the ten directions. "Variegated" and its mudrā is the variegated mudrā — the mudrā of the eyes and so forth. Because through the yoga of reflections one seals the constitutions of sentient beings, it is therefore spoken of as "mudrā." "Variegated" means the intrinsic nature of all things, or the glorious Vajrasattva together with all things, and the mudrā is the variegated mudrā. "By the variegated vow gathered from all" means of variegated aspects: she whose nature is to move through all of space by supreme bliss is the ḍākinī.

"The expanse of buddhas' speech" — abiding in the glorious buddha Vairocana and being reflected back therefrom are the natural constituent elements: the natural constituent elements of the body, speech, and mind of all Tathāgatas are expressed by the term "expanse of buddhas' speech." Just as the causes of being reflected back while abiding in the glorious Vajrasattva are the natural constituent elements of that one, so by the term "ḍākinī" the nature of moving through space is indicated. In the same way, this is to be understood with respect to the constituent elements of Vairocana, Akṣobhya, Ratnādhipa, Amitābha, and Amoghasiddhi.

"The ḍākinī of all buddhas" — all those who move through space surrounding the Blessed One Vajrasattva with eyes and so forth are ḍākinīs of the nature of Vajrasattva. Likewise, all those who move through space surrounding the glorious Heruka together with the white one and so forth are ḍākinīs of the nature of Heruka. Likewise, all those who move through space surrounding the one endowed with the yoga of Nairātmyā together with Vajradevī and so forth, along with the ḍākinīs of the nature of selflessness. Likewise, all those who move through space surrounding the one endowed with primordial wisdom together with Vajraḍākinī and so forth are ḍākinīs endowed with the nature of primordial wisdom. In the same way, together with Tārā: those together with Padmāvatī, the Lotus Wheel, the King of Yama, likewise Hayagrīva, and likewise the very Victor over the three worlds — and moreover, whatever else is wished for in mind, differentiated by classification of lineage, is said to be surrounded in the same way by ḍākinīs.

Having thus set forth the ḍākinīs of all buddhas, and in order to explain the vow, "bliss" means: that which, through union with the wisdom-mudrā of the Blessed One, the glorious Vajrasattva, generates in one something possessing supreme bliss. What arises from that? "Supreme" means especially distinguished, because it is the most excellent means of all bliss. For that very reason it is the engagement with all illusion. This also means that it is not through union with śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas in any other way. That which generates bliss through union in the very way of the network of ḍākinīs — the union with all buddhas — is expressed by the syllable "saṃ." Therefore, that which, through that cause, generates supreme, especially distinguished bliss, by realizing all those illusory phenomena that are the purified aspect of the nirvāṇa of śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas, great compassion arises.

"Not empty" — because the three realms and all objects of desire are entities falsely imagined in their falseness, they are not empty of falsely imagined entities that are falsely imagined therein, since they are indeed entities.

"Not empty" — and "not" means that ultimately there are no entities of conceptual thought that apprehend and grasp as subject and object. The wisdom by which the absence of those entities of apprehended and apprehender is made directly perceived — that wisdom itself also does not become an object of focus, because it is not within the domain of conceptual thought. For that very reason, the meaning of what is said is: "It is to be understood as the Middle Way of authentic wisdom." And it is said, "Joined with the Perfection of Wisdom" — the skillful means for that very reason is great compassion.

The object of focus that is the very nature of compassion belonging to the awakened buddhas, the Blessed Ones, is the intrinsic nature of compassion. Then "the beautiful compassion-method" means compassion that is exceedingly beautiful — the great compassion that focuses on nothing, through equanimity. That very skillful means — this method endowed with exceedingly beautiful compassion — is the nature of non-abiding nirvāṇa (Skt. apratiṣṭhitanirvāṇa): this is the glorious Vairocana. When asked what it becomes, one connects it with the statement "the arising of pure phenomena."

When asked how the Perfection of Wisdom illumines authentically: "Wisdom" means one should investigate through discriminating analysis that divides phenomena as if they were illusions — that itself is the excellence of the self. "Perfection" means it teaches the crossing over — thus it is declared: "the illumination of the Perfection of Wisdom."

By whom? It is "through aspiring faith free of conceptual thought." What does it do? The intention is expressed in the words "previously, from the capacity of the master." What was analyzed? "All that was analyzed" means the essential nature of form and so forth as being illusory, in their own independent manner. From what characteristics does one cling? "In phenomena free of conceptual thought, the aim of sentient beings is thoroughly analyzed" — this means: analyzed through the force of compassion, with the benefit of sentient beings as its cause.

When asked what becomes of that analysis: as for the word "echo" in "equanimity, like an echo" — an echo is the sound of a valley. By that, it is equal to the most excellent sound, following along in equal resonance, and the nature of conceptually elaborated sound; and through the Perfection of Wisdom one conceives of that as being like an echo, having become just so.

When asked what that is: "Then from that, this Dharma-speech arose" — meaning, from that, on account of that, from the Dharma, the distinctive words of certainty regarding the meaning of the Dharma appearing individually and known individually are the nature of what has been analyzed. For that very reason, this illumines the three pledges (Skt. samaya) of the glorious Vairocana and others.

The Dharma of speech means the melody of speech. That it has become so means it opens the voice, and this speech of voice is expressed in just that way accordingly. And that which is established from what is not authentic is established from the Dharma-speech. "Vehicle" and "great" together form the Great Vehicle — the path of bodhisattvas together with its fruit. It is established as great through being connected with the seven aspects of greatness in the sūtras of the Perfection of Wisdom and so forth.

Therefore, the especially distinguished vajra vehicle, which is the Great Vehicle, is for the purpose of realizing in this very life buddhahood and so forth — and so its qualities have become supreme: powers, fearlessness, and the like. The accumulation of these is vast, precisely because those are qualities become supreme. The three times are: the past, the future, and the present. The three pledges means everything of the nature of body, speech, and mind. All Tathāgatas are Vairocana and so forth. When asked how the praise is expressed: through the reasoning of reflections and so forth, free of conceptual thought and liberated, all Tathāgatas are praised.

Because emptiness by its very nature pervades all things without exception — the stable and the moving alike — and because that stability and movement and so forth are pervaded by it, the three realms are nothing but mere consciousness (vijñaptimātra). Therefore vows and the like are taught as divine yoga.

The Blessed One was then asked: "Blessed One, for what reason can worldly beings not realize the nature of that — that is, everything just as it is, in its own aspect?"

The Blessed One declared: "The word 'because' is the reason. 'Everything, as its very self' means: by the actuality of everything. 'As its own self' means: as belonging to itself. 'Dwelling in the aggregate of consciousness' means: in that which is the body of consciousness. 'The abiding' is the essential nature of self-knowing mind. Therefore 'confusion' is delusion. 'The lowest of persons' refers to those with awareness and so forth. 'Unable to realize' means: not knowing, because of being accompanied by confusion."

The question was then put: "Blessed One, if that is not sought, how can the suchness of those beings be sought?"

The Blessed One declared: "Mind, mental factors, and the nature of mind — self." This phrase means: the essential nature of the mind of sentient beings. "Mind" here means: the living principle that has the character of cognizing, in all things, the nature of phenomena such as virtuous aggregates and the like — it is the entity possessed of that character. "Having the nature of gnosis as its self" means: that which pervades everywhere; in that, the person is the self whose nature is the gnosis of Viṣṇu that pervades all things. "Having the nature of what is to be known" means: seed, born of seed, the one who feels, the one who acts, the object understood as place, that which has its own intrinsic form, and agenthood itself. Those who speak of a Lord of Power (Īśvara) say that the Lord of Power is the agent in all phenomena. "The very existence of the qualified subject as its essential nature" means: from the extreme of establishing that very self-possessing entity, the Great God (Śiva) is posited as the agent of phenomena such as merit and the like, or as the essential nature of phenomena — a Great Lord of Power, a Lord of Power.

In order to demonstrate that fully, the Blessed One taught: the consequence and the repudiation, the singular and the many, what is to be proved, the subjects of proof, the reasons, and the examples. Among these, the statement "of phenomena" and so forth — and "by many": if one were to argue for multiplicity as the cause of what is to be proved, this would not produce multiplicity itself. Since this is stated in order to prove singularity, the example of the river and the ocean is given for both. This is to be cited as an example because it is proved by both the singular and the many. As to this: first, the things such as mind, stated at the end of the Dharma of multiplicity — the many phenomena — are indeed numerous, since they turn away from the one in a sequence of various forms. "By many, phenomena" means: non-Buddhists, by way of cause, posit mind and so forth in many different forms. "Blessed One, is this the object of thought?"

The Blessed One declared: "The phrase 'stream of a river' refers to the śrāvakas. 'Ocean' means: just as outside the inner sea, the streams of rivers — the Sītā, the Sindhū, the Gaṅgā, and the like — flow and circulate, and one does not come to see them as multiple — thinking 'this is the river Sindhū, this is the river Gaṅgā' — so too, outside the profound and unfathomable dharmadhātu, non-Buddhists posit as multiple what they call self, the living principle, person, and individual. Blessed One, is the suchness here of a multiple character?" The Blessed One declared: "It is not. Whoever posits suchness here as singular is apprehending the nature of that suchness as being one alone."

As for the phrase "singular and multiple" and what follows — the term "multiple" refers to those manifold forms of mind and so forth, which are not apprehended by the noble ones and are not understood by reasoning. Because multiplicity enters into unity — just as the multiplicity of rivers enters into the ocean — it is not understood by reasoning that mind, self, and so forth exist. And even when brought into accord with reasoning, when mind and so forth have entered into the selflessness of all phenomena, their multiplicity is still not accepted. Just as, when the multiplicity of rivers has entered into the ocean, there is no separately appearing thing there — so too, because there is no thing that is multiplicity, suchness is understood as being of the character of unity. By such a character as this, from where is suchness to be found?

The Blessed One declared: "What is called 'from the mouth of the master' — the master is one who possesses mantra, tantra, and oral instruction, and it is through words that the oral instruction is to be found." The Blessed One then declared the definitive oral instruction of that master:

"What is called 'inner' refers to dwelling in such a form at the crown of the beautifully veined channels, adorned with the five spaces. The secret lotus is said to mean the secret place. It is the place of the crown's space, beautified by the five channels at the navel of the lotus that hold blood, semen, and urine in the direction of the crown of beautifully veined channels. Visualising oneself in the form of a wrathful deity, intensely red like the radiance of the sun's colour, with a blazing Hūṃ upon one's tongue, as vast as the sky, splitting and completely filling space — by this, one inhales the clouds through the hollow of the heart and, exhaling, scatters them like tufts of cotton. Alternatively, by releasing the blazing host of one's own mudrā, splitting and cutting, one supremely suppresses them with Hūṃ. The Blessed One then declared the purpose of destroying that. As for "the maṇḍala of fire" and so forth: one who is endowed with perpetual yoga draws forth from one's own forehead the noble Acala, the Immovable Lord, arisen from RAṂ, abiding in the maṇḍala of fire, six-armed — from the left KṢIṂ, the pointing finger of threat; from HŪṂ, placed within a noose; from DHAṂ, a bow. From the right, from KHAṂ, a sword; from HO, an arrow; and one visualises holding a vajra from HŪṂ.

By the rays of light from that very deity, together with the wrathful ones, all obstructers are made to resound with the cry of Hūṃ, and they are pierced with blazing swords and the like, struck down by arrows that have been shot — thus are the obstructers born of men destroyed.

As for "the great blood of men" and so forth: one makes an image to be accomplished using the dust of footprints and the blood of men, then with twenty mantras endowed with light-rays one gloriously consecrates the stake-pegs made of human bone, drives a stake-peg into the eye, and inserts it into the mouth of the lord of the assembly.

For the syllable Oṃ Sumbha, vajra pestle and hammer — one should visualize first striking the face with the hammer, then striking the back with the pestle.

For the purpose of destroying the blazing one, "the colour of eye-medicine" and so forth: into the space of the sky one inserts Hūṃ Gaḥ Hūṃ inwardly.

From the syllable Phuḥ there arises a black nāga; one commands the garuḍa dwelling in the sky, encircled by a thousand black nāgas. Having meditated exactly as it is, "Oṃ Vajranārāyaṇa" and so forth: first one summons, and by that summons one also stirs Śakra (Indra) with the mind. This too — one commands the rain-cloud for the purpose of rain; and even if the command is heard and rain falls, if one wishes in mind to turn back the cloud, the garuḍa turns it back with its wings and, by piercing it with its beak, makes a boundary for the river. The meaning of the vajra-turning-back is hereby declared.

As for "fire offering" and so forth: for the purpose of performing virtuous acts, for the characteristics of pacification, enrichment, and subjugation, one uses fire produced by the fire-drill. For wrathful activity, one uses the fire of the charnel ground.

The fire-pit is round, square, half-moon shaped, or triangular. Along the rim of the fire-pit, accordingly, one should also place lotuses in white, yellow, red, and black. Then, in accordance with the corresponding colour, the uṣṇīṣa, the water-born lotus, the precious jewel, and the blazing vajra are to be made — that is the meaning.

"Recitation accomplishes all activities" — this means that they are eminently consecrated by Hūṃ.

"With the left-hand vajra" means that the left hand is formed into the three-pronged vajra.

"Adorned with the light of activity" means ornamented with light in the manner appropriate to the activity — pacification and so forth.

"Having caused it to be fully absorbed in the blazing form" — here "blazing" means either a deity or a blazing scorching form. In the same way, one meditates on the ritual procedure of the nature of primordial wisdom. From Oṃ at the beginning and Svāhā at the end, abiding supreme, one applies it to pacification, enrichment, and subjugation. For wrathful activity, one recites the mantra with wrathful words combined with Hūṃ and Phaṭ. At the beginning and the end one offers oblation in full measure. The phrase "filling the mouth" means one performs the fire offering at the lotus of the deity's mouth.

As for the oblation materials such as excrement, urine, and so forth, one should have no apprehension of sin or the like, because ultimately all things are pure by nature — everything is mere conceptual imputation.

Then also, entering into the union of the two faculties, one places the divine pride foremost, makes the union of skillful means and wisdom manifest, and generates undefiled primordial wisdom.

The Blessed One declared that the body and so forth are to be correctly explained. "From meditation" means through intelligence that is free from conceptual elaboration.

"As before, through complete transformation" — this refers to performing offerings and so forth. Just as the wheel of the deity has been drawn out in the manner described, so too that very wheel of the deity is to be visualized inwardly in the same way — this is the full extent of the meaning.

Furthermore, first, endowed with the yoga of conceptual construction, one draws out Akṣobhya and the rest from the heart center and makes them identical in nature with the five cords of blue and the remaining colors. Turning gently with hūṃ, one recites that hūṃ one hundred and eight times.

In this way it is taught that the colored powder too is to be drawn in accordance with this very yoga. Consecrated by the five buddhas, and drawing the lines in the proper directions of the maṇḍala, first the master draws the colored powder design, and afterward the disciples draw the five kinds of colored powder — blue, red, white, green, and yellow — on the outside of the maṇḍala. "The wheel's hub blazing with diversity" — great Vairocana is to be placed there, residing on a diverse lotus.

Then the vase that stands at the gate — having been purified by means of all-action recitation as a fumigation offering — these vases, adorned with garments and the like, and the master, being endowed with the yoga of lordship, through excellent yoga meditates the wheel of all the gods within his own being and abides in the pride thereof.

Reciting throughout the service-use of the water offering, the gate is opened. The vases and the rest are placed in their proper positions, the deities of the maṇḍala are drawn forth as before, and the wheel is to be introduced into their proper abodes. In order that the mudrās of one's own deity may confer blessing, one displays one's own mudrā and, having visualized oneself abiding in the meditative absorption of self-empowerment through one's own authority, one makes offering with the earlier offerings such as the gesture of the alluring one and the rest, and, the vidyās who have been blessed by the distinctive offerings of one's own pleasing offerings — drums, flutes, and so forth — with palms joined, recite three times the well-composed vajra song, and prostrate. Then, the fire offering taught for the purpose of bringing satisfaction having been performed, one thereafter requests forgiveness.

For the purpose of all attainments (siddhi), having prayed oneself, one generates the first and subsequent ones, and adorns the disciples with various ornaments. With oṃ and the rest, one blesses the heart and the other places with the mudrā of blessing of the body and so forth. With palms joined and index fingers interlocked in the form of a wheel, reciting hūṃ, one performs this at all five places. Having covered the face with a cloth, one takes a flower held in the mudrā of the Vajra Goddess and the others, introduces it with hūṃ itself, and performs all the rites of the crown. Having been blessed through the yoga of glorious Vajrasattva, one adorns with incense, flowers, and the like, and in the proper manner, empowerment is conferred by means of all the vases.

The Tathāgatas offer the water offering, and one is to regard those holding vases filled with excellent nectar as conferring empowerment. Furthermore, adorned with garments and the like, having been empowered by the vajra and the rest, and the many forms of Akṣobhya and others having fully manifested, one performs the proximate rites of the skull bowl, the rosary, the vajra, the lord, and the vajra name empowerment; the veil of ignorance is cleared away with a stylus of gold and the like; and a mirror is taken up. Then the defining characteristics are to be proclaimed by means of the verse beginning "It is the very nature of glorious Vajrasattva" and so forth.

With the first vowel, the book is given; with the vowel following the first, the vajra bell; and for the purpose of granting permission to teach, the feet are blessed and a sword is given into the right hand. From this day forth, turn the wheel of Dharma for the world — this and what follows, beginning with "O Protector," is to be properly visualized.

The phrase "from this day forth" and what follows sets forth the pledges in close detail. The preceptor then accepts the fee and performs the pacifying fire offering. After performing the offerings, praises, and so forth, the syllable hūṃ is to be recited. With the words "O Vajrasattva of the awakening mind — muḥ," the deity's maṇḍala wheel is invited to depart. Then, drawing out the ritual dagger, and in the same way with the secret maṇḍala described above, the departure is invited using both hands as previously described. Reciting jaḥ hūṃ baṃ hoḥ, one meditates inwardly that the supremely exalted one abides in his own proper seat. By means of the syllable hūṃ alone, the colored sand is swept away, gathered up, and poured into water.

Since the wrathful one holds the quality of wrath, it is declared that glorious Heruka arises — and the passage beginning with the words "That very one is the Blessed One who is the yogin" and so forth is a proclamation made to the son of the family under the heading "The First Yoga of Glorious Vajrasattva." There it is stated that those who engage in the ten non-virtues such as taking life, and those whose pledges have degenerated, are purified — and the word "purified" here means cleansed. He possesses the flavor conveyed by the phrase "resplendent and alluring" and so forth.

The Blessed One declared: eight faces, four feet, and adorned with sixteen hands, with twenty-four eyes.

Furthermore, by this gradual sequence, the Blessed One is arrayed with four faces and four arms. Of these, the first face is wrathful. The second, on the right, is radiant with bliss and awe-inspiring. The third face, at the back, partakes of wondrous food. On the left, the face is alluring and grants nectar. Likewise, the topmost face, called "the glaring one," is the supreme expression of all activity. The three faces convey the primordial wisdom of emptiness, signlessness, and wishlessness. The vajra-bearer of Akṣobhya's form similarly has six arms.

In the elaborated form, two arms with a single face are also to be depicted, and at the left shoulder, for the purpose of offerings, the ritual connection holds the vajra and bell in the pure joining gesture, along with the khaṭvāṅga staff. In his right hands are a three-pronged banner tip, the vajra hook, and a blazing wheel. In his left hand he holds a skull-cup filled with great compassion.

The phrase "seated in the half-cross-legged posture of the Great Seal" means seated upon a high throne — the left foot is placed upon the ground, and the right foot is raised onto the left thigh, in the joining posture of the awakening-mind's cross-legged seat. By pressing with the sole of that foot, the left thigh is brought into union through the joining posture.

O son of the family, one who practices the maṇḍala of the great seal in the cross-legged posture — the posture of the Blessed One seated upon the wrathful throne — will fully attain its accomplishment. By the joining of the raised foot upon the left thigh, bringing it to powder and extending it fully, the wrathful body is to be visualized above the face, and from that position the substance of accomplishment is to be drunk. That very one is to be meditated upon above the face of the Blessed One, and by means of the right foot in the joining posture of the vajra cross-legged seat, all women are taken as one wishes, and union is accomplished by moving the left thigh.

Alternatively, the face mudrās are to be proclaimed. In the eastern cell: a white, peaceful face. To the right: the fierce face of the robber woman. At the back: the utterly stupefied one — the face of her who is bewildered by the intoxication of bliss. To the left: the face of the risen corpse woman, rigid and erect. In the fire cell: Pukkasī with the dancing face. In the cell of the non-true: the caṇḍāla woman, with a face full of reverence for all, through the union of stealing the life of another.

In the wind cell: Ghasmarī with the face of one who consumes the dead, engaged in union. In the cell of power: the form of the Blessed One in the likeness of Heruka. The white one has the gaze of peacefulness. The robber woman has the fierce gaze. The utterly stupefied one has the gaze of utter stupefaction. The risen corpse woman has the gaze that rouses the dead. Pukkasī has the dancing gaze. The caṇḍāla woman gazes upward. Ghasmarī gazes downward upon the seat.

When one visualizes the white woman as present in a flower and gives that flower to someone, that person is sealed with the mudrā. In poison, the form is that of the robber woman. In ale, the form is that of the utterly stupefied one. In water, the form is that of the risen corpse woman. In a wooden staff, it is Pukkasī. In cloth, it is the fierce one. In fire, it is Ghasmarī — and through the meditative absorption of one-pointed union, she abides everywhere.

Thereafter, sealing with the mudrā is also to be performed through the meditative absorption of the white one, as it is stated. Acting in this way: through the meditative absorption that the robber woman draws forth from the old abode, she enters the body of the one to be accomplished and draws from the heart. Through the union of the utterly stupefied one, one bewildered by bliss imagines the one to be accomplished as stupefied — thus, by bestowing bliss exceedingly, she causes utter stupefaction. Through the meditative absorption of the risen corpse woman, by scattering nectar-water she rouses the dead. Through the yoga of Pukkasī, by striking with a skull she causes dancing. By the extremely upward breath in the meditative absorption, becoming one together with the caṇḍāla woman, she causes killing. Through that meditative absorption of Ghasmarī, one is to consume by eating what is placed upon the seat.

The white one, through her leftward gaze upon the one to be accomplished, performs the sealing with the mudrā. With the mind she enters into the heart of the one to be accomplished, and with the left hand she draws forth and hooks the lotus of his heart. The robber woman draws from the heart. The utterly stupefied one — whether mentally or directly, acting through bliss-induced stupefaction — causes utter stupefaction. The risen corpse woman — whether mentally or by physically embracing — rouses death. The yoga of Pukkasī that causes dancing: by becoming one together with the one to be accomplished in the mind, she herself dances. By becoming one together with the one to be accomplished in the mind, bewildered by bliss and employing the upward gaze, the yoga of the fierce one causes killing. Through the yoga of Ghasmarī, having placed the one to be accomplished in the mouth, one is to partake and consume. "To consume" means that all enemies are utterly and perpetually devoured — that is the meaning.

The one who performs the vajra Hūṃ obtains the name "Liberator of the Three Worlds," and he is the Vajrasattva who has thoroughly conquered the three worlds.

Then, "elsewhere" means: adhering to all other wrathful ones — such as the beautiful one, the one with the power of compassion, and so forth — as being of a single taste as wrathful ones.

The Blessed One was asked: "What are the characteristics of the form of the wrathful one, and for what purpose were they taught?" Those who assert a self in the body, in the life-force, in the person, and so forth — the one who puts an end to all such conceptual constructs is the Lord of the Dead. The one who conquers, through the wheel of their constructs, these three agents is the supreme lord of accomplishment over all conceptual constructs. The king of wrathful ones, called Vajrabhairava, is to be explained: because from Viṣṇu (the All-Pervader) and the Great Lord (Śiva) onward — all of them — Vajrabhairava reduces to ash.

As for "great attachment" (rāga): great attachment is the vajra of desire, and what possesses its essential nature is expressed as such. Furthermore, having become Vajrasattva and then becoming the vajra of one who possesses desire — just as in spring, a garland of sweetly fragrant flowers —

— adorned with the heart and so forth, and because of possessing a flower-bow and arrows, I am desire.

The arrow of the three bodies, called "the arrangement of the arrow": in the middle of the flat surface of the fletchings, the syllables YUṂ, KHAṂ, and OṂ, together with the other two, are to be arranged as a set of three, and these substances are to be visualized at the crown of the consort's head together with the arrow, and for the purpose of the dripping they are taught at the edge of the lotus. If desire is melted, it is like the melting of desire. The word "lotus" means the essence; through having come under the power of the EVAṂ, it drips. "This teaching, boundless and supreme, the supreme vow of all buddhas" — thus the Blessed One, the Tathāgata, declared the vajra of body, speech, and mind of all Tathāgatas.

What is called 'perfectly and excellently taught' means 'caused to arise.' What is called 'the vessel of the lotus' is to be understood as a human skull. What is to be accomplished by the effort of this skull-seat? The accomplishment of intrinsic nature itself, through going-forth from that nature — by the power of going forth from intrinsic nature, conceptual thought regarding apprehended and apprehender is without occasion, and by reason of that essential nature one becomes suchness itself through going forth. That is accomplished by its own intrinsic nature.

"From the intrinsic nature of form of the skull-garland crown ornament" means: it is taught thus from non-dual primordial wisdom. One writes HŪṂ with the mind on the moon within one's own heart, and recites it, meditating that one's very self is equal to space. The moon-maṇḍala of that suchness is itself the moon-maṇḍala. "Some" means those who have not seen. Since it individually discerns one's own mind, it is the awakening mind. A being who has planted mind in awakening is a bodhisattva. Their conduct is the conduct of awakening, and since one who has rightly found the heroic mind follows after it, one goes accordingly.

Accomplishing victory, producing honey, and accomplishing every purpose — the bodhisattva acts for the benefit of sentient beings in the manner of remaining in the form of three stamens, transforming variously as dust, darkness, and heartwood for the sake of sentient beings as well. Possessing the conquest of the four māras and having become victorious over the māras, having become Śākyamuni in one account, he became perfectly and fully awakened. For this purpose the Blessed One Vairocana turned the wheel of Dharma. The sixty-two views of self, attachment in all its forms, and their purification — because no self is perceived in suchness, there is no inherent existence. The great seal (mahāmudrā) like the sun is the seal of the sun. What is called "the essence of space" — because it generates the essence from the wisdom of equality with space, it is called just so, for what it is. The lord of all attainments who fulfills all wishes: those who pray to become wheel-turners and the like for all sentient beings in all their forms — because it thoroughly grants those wishes, it fulfills the wishes of all. Therefore it is declared: "Raining down from the sky all the precious things — elephants and the rest — the shower of all the seven precious gems" means the shower of the seven precious gems. For this purpose Tārā arises as the one victorious over the three worlds.

"Extremely faint in diligence" means laziness and the like. The yoga of power is the yoga of renown. Through the application of diligence: for this purpose, through the purification of the yoga of power, the supreme horse arises — meaning Hayagrīva.

Now, how is the meaning of that emptiness to be sought? The Blessed One declared: for the purpose of attaining the two virtues — the conditioned (Skt. saṃskṛta) virtuous and the unconditioned (Skt. asaṃskṛta) — and for the sake of constantly benefiting sentient beings, and for the sake of supremely benefiting sentient beings, by not abandoning saṃsāra, the emptiness of that is the emptiness of beginning- and end-lessness. Now, what is emptiness in this context? Emptiness is the absence of any ultimate entity in phenomena and in persons. Therefore, the ultimate entity that arises from that absence — the emptiness of the absence of an ultimate entity in those very phenomena and persons — is one kind. And the ultimate entity arising from the emptiness of the absence of an ultimate entity in phenomena and persons on the other side is another kind.

As has been said, in order to explain the defining marks of the sixteen emptinesses — because things such as depth and so forth are empty — two systems set forth emptiness. These are the emptiness of absence of entity and the emptiness of the intrinsic nature of the absence of entity. In order to comprehensively abandon all the forms of emptiness that arise in engaging with phenomena and persons in a correct manner, one should understand the subdivisions of emptiness according to their sequential order.

How is this to be established and known? If this were not defiled throughout, then all embodied beings would be liberated. If this were not purified, then any effort undertaken would be fruitless. If the emptiness of phenomena were not defiled — because adventitious defilement has not arisen — in the sense that emptiness does not become corrupted through defilement by adverse conditions, then, since defilement itself would have no entity, all sentient beings would be liberated without any effort. But then, since that which is unarisen is also not purified by adverse conditions, any effort undertaken for the sake of liberation would be fruitless.

Proceeding in this way, because the intrinsic nature of mind is luminosity, it is neither afflicted nor unafflicted, neither pure nor impure. How then is it that it is neither afflicted nor defiled? Because affliction is adventitious. Similarly, how is it that it is neither unafflicted nor pure? Because of that very adventitious nature of affliction. This is what is expressed through the subdivisions of emptiness. By that, the condensed meaning of emptiness has been established.

It is further said: as the antidote to attachment, there is meditation on unattractiveness and the like; as the antidote to aversion, there is loving-kindness; and as the antidote to delusion, there is dependent origination.

How does one reverse attachment? If it is by means of what contradicts attachment, that is partly so. Yet one who takes the very nature of attachment as its own essence becomes thereby free from attachment. Just as it is said: the Blessed One declared, "How wondrous, son of good family! The gnosis of desire in all buddhas is without turbidity. Through desire itself, desire is overcome, and all happiness is bestowed. Attachment and the like are by nature free from any intrinsic fault."

Otherwise, if one who has found attachment while abiding on the path of a stream-enterer continues to act without completely abandoning that attachment and the like, a difference in qualities will nonetheless arise from the distinction in levels of purification. As an example: when the fragrant ketaka flower is consumed by a superior elephant, it becomes a substance like musk and the like; whereas when consumed by an ordinary elephant, it becomes a substance like dung. Therefore the fault lies not with the ketaka flower itself.

In the same way, attachment and the like, when one abides on a level of purified progression and applies them through the distinctive engagement of superior intention, ripen the fruit of a supremely distinguished result. As an example: when milk is drunk by a snake or the like, it becomes a poisonous substance; but when drunk by a human being, it arises as a nectarous substance.

In this way, son of good family, because attachment and the like are without fault by virtue of the distinctive nature of intention, applying them produces the fruit of a supremely distinguished result. In whatever way one is endowed with the qualities of a stream that has become especially elevated, the fruit of that particular stream arises as especially elevated. For example, the intrinsic nature of attachment and the like, when one abides on the especially elevated progression of those such as the ketaka flower and so on, is perfectly pure.
