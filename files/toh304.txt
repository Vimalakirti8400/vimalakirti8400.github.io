Sūtra Examining the Virtuous and Non-Virtuous Results of the Five Classes of Transgressions (Pañcapātanikāyaśubhāśubhaphalapārīkṣāsūtra)

The Sūtra Examining the Virtuous and Non-Virtuous Results of the Five Classes of Transgressions, known in Sanskrit as the Pañcapātanikāyaśubhāśubhaphalapārīkṣāsūtra and in Tibetan as the Ltung ba sde lnga'i dge ba dang mi dge ba'i 'bras bu brtag pa'i mdo, is a canonical Buddhist sūtra in which the Buddha, speaking to an assembly of monks at Śrāvastī, examines the karmic fruits — virtuous and non-virtuous — associated with the five classes of transgressions recognized in Buddhist ethical and vinaya thought. The text proceeds by elaborating the cosmological framework of divine realms and their lifespans, situating the consequences of moral conduct within a vast vision of cyclic existence. Homage to all buddhas and bodhisattvas.

Thus did I hear. At one time the Blessed One was dwelling at Śrāvastī, in the Jeta Grove, the garden of Anāthapiṇḍada (the benefactor of the destitute), in the pleasure grove of Prince Jeta. There the Blessed One declared to the monks:

Monks, fifty years of the humans of Jambudvīpa constitute a single day and night for the gods of the realm of the Four Great Kings (Cāturmahārājakāyika). Thirty such days and nights make one month, and twelve such months make one year. Five hundred years reckoned in this way constitute the full lifespan of the gods of the realm of the Four Great Kings.

Monks, those who guard, preserve, fully maintain, and do not transgress the trainings (śikṣā), and who reveal and fully proclaim the transgressions committed against them — such persons, upon the dissolution of this body, will be reborn in a happy destiny among the gods of the realm of the Four Great Kings. There too they will enjoy a divine lifespan, divine complexion, divine strength, divine bliss, and divine sovereignty, and they will be endowed with divine forms, sounds, fragrances, tastes, tactile objects, and divine attendants.

Monks, five hundred years of the gods of the realm of the Four Great Kings constitute a single day and night in the great hell Sañjīva (the Reviving Hell). Thirty such days and nights make one month, and twelve such months make one year. Five hundred years reckoned in this way constitute the full lifespan in the great hell Sañjīva.

Monks, those who do not guard, do not preserve, do not fully maintain the trainings, who transgress them, and who do not reveal or fully proclaim such transgressions — such persons, upon the dissolution of this body, will after death be reborn in the great hell Sañjīva. There too they will experience suffering that is fierce, rough, burning, and deeply unpleasant.

This concludes the examination of transgressions involving harmful conduct.

Monks, therefore, those who have heard this treatise, out of wishing to benefit me, out of love, out of compassion, out of desiring my welfare, and out of seeking my benefit — through this loving concern, you should act in this way. Monks, dwelling in forest retreats, at the foot of trees, in empty dwelling places, in mountain wilderness, in mountain caves, in charnel grounds, in forest clearings, and in lodgings at the edge of the open air — you should most assuredly reflect upon these matters. Monks, be heedful. Do not act in such a way that you will later come to regret it. This is my instruction to you.

When the Blessed One had thus declared this, those monks rejoiced and praised what the Blessed One had spoken.
