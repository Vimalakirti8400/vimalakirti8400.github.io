In the language of India: Krodhabhura Kuṃkūṭarāja Stotramantra. In the Tibetan language: The Mantra of Praise to Kumeratsegs (Kuṃkūṭarāja), King of Wrathful Ones.

Homage to the Three Jewels.

Oṃ bhur kuṃ mahāprāṇāya, bhura cihura kimānāse, ucuṣma krodha hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ phaṭ svāhā.

Homage, praise, and salutation to the victorious mudrā!

Wrathful against the wrathful, you are the Great Wrathful One.
A buddha, a buddha, a buddha from before —
dharmakāya, saṃbhogakāya, and nirmāṇakāya —
to you called Kumeratsegs, I pay homage.

Blazing at the center like the fire of a cosmic age,
you are seated upon a lotus, sun, and moon throne.
Deep dark green, marked with spots,
with three faces and six arms,
right face white, left face red, central face green,
with three eyes you bare terrifying fangs.
You drink impure nectar like medicine.

In your right hands you hold a vajra and a sword.
In your left hands a lasso and an iron hook.
The last pair crosses index fingers at your heart.
You strive in the activity of those who know no purity.
Adorned on head and body with fierce serpents,
you practice your discipline without fear and without shame.
King of Wrath, to you I pay homage.

You make the impure pure —
King of Wrath, to you I bow in praise.
You protect from all that is terrifying —
King of Wrath, to you I bow in praise.
You guard against illness and epidemic —
King of Wrath, to you I bow in praise.

Supreme among those who dispel all impure food and impure clothing,
you bring delight to gods, nāgas, yakṣas, and spirits — every one of them without exception.
You perform the activity of guarding against illness, epidemic, and plague —
to you I bow in praise.
I beseech you: please protect me.

You make the impure pure.
You make the spotted spotless.
You make the heavy light.
You make the narrow wide —
to you I offer praise.

Kumeratsegs, King of Wrathful Ones, you yourself
are blessed by all the buddhas.
Those who place their faith and trust in you:
their body is well, their mind is well, their senses are well.
To the deity of wellbeing I pay homage.
Their body is pure, their mind is pure, their senses are pure —
to the deity of purity I pay homage.
Their body is clean, their mind is clean, their senses are clean —
to the deity of cleanliness I pay homage.
Body, mind, and senses are clear —
to the deity of clarity I pay homage.
Their body is radiant, their mind is radiant, their senses are radiant —
to the deity of radiance I pay homage.

If gods, nāgas, hungry ghosts, demons, flesh-eaters,
yakṣas, and spirits hear this,
their suffering will be pacified and they will come to be at ease.
They will become white like a snow-range,
clean like pure crystal,
clear like a mirror,
transparent like the open sky.

All the eight classes — gods, nāgas, and the rest —
who dwell upon the earth and in the sky:
let them be gathered in wholesome intention
and, causing no harm to others, be at peace.

The phrase rendered "those who know no purity" (སྨེ་གཙང་མེད་པའི་ལས་དག་བརྩོན) is ambiguous: it could mean the deity strives in tasks involving impurity on behalf of beings, or that he acts for those who lack purity. The rendering chosen reflects the purificatory function that is the text's central theme.

The mantra syllables are rendered phonetically from Tibetan transcription of Sanskrit, which is itself irregular; no claim is made that this reconstructs the original Sanskrit form precisely.
