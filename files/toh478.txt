The King of Tantras Called the Glorious Red Yamāri (Śrīraktayamāritantrarājanāma)

The King of Tantras Called the Glorious Red Yamāri, known in Sanskrit as the Śrī Raktayamāri Tantrarāja and in Tibetan as Palden Shinjé Shepa Marpö Gyü kyi Gyalpo, is a Buddhist tantra devoted to Red Yamāri, the red, wrathful form of Yamāntaka, the conqueror of death. Framed as a dialogue in which the Blessed One, embraced by his consort, answers her requests for the sake of beings, it presents itself as a practical complement to the Hevajra, Saṃvara and Guhyasamāja tantras, and sets out the mandala and sādhana of the deity together with a wide range of ritual applications, including peaceful, enriching and fierce rites, yantras and mantras, medicinal and magical preparations, signs of approaching death, and the sexual yoga of the completion stage. It is Toh 478 in the Kangyur, where it belongs to the Tantra section among the highest yoga tantras of the Yamāri cycle. Its Tibetan translation is credited to Butön Rinchen Drup, and it was later revised against a Sanskrit manuscript obtained from Nepal.

In the language of India: śri maṃ rakta yamāri tantra raja nāma. In the language of Tibet: The King of Tantras Called the Glorious Red Yamāri.

Homage to Red Yamāntaka.

Thus did I hear at one time. The Blessed One, in whom all the tathāgatas are blended into one, was dwelling in the bhaga of selflessness.

Embracing him tightly about the neck,
She herself made a request for the sake of beings.
Though it was taught in the Hevajra,
And likewise in the Saṃvara for liberation,
And in the Samāja and other tantras,
And has been taught again very extensively,
What fulfills the wishes of beings completely
I shall now explain.

Some desire kingship,
And it grants the fruits of long life and health;
The peaceful rites and the rest, the increasing rites,
Whatever fierce rites are performed,
Destroying the troops of enemies,
Expelling, summoning, and killing as well:
Though these have been taught in various tantras,
The yantra not spoken of in the various tantras
Is hard to find in the three worlds.
As a gift to benefit you
I shall explain it; goddess, listen with respect.

By the power of this yantra
One always attains kingship;
And if one wishes to live
As long as the sun and the moon,
Or for stiffening of semen, summoning of troops,
Sky-going itself, and supreme swiftness,
Here and now, within the range of words,
I shall explain its remedy.

First, one should meditate on loving-kindness;
Second, likewise on compassion;
Third, one should meditate on joy;
Fourth, one should meditate on equanimity.
The eight worldly concerns
One should give up completely and contemplate as empty.
Again, emptiness is awakening;
In the second, gather the seed;
In the fourth, set out the syllables.
From the syllable rā arises a sun;
On it, from hūṃ, arises a crossed vajra.
By that very means there is what is called
The vajra fence, and likewise
The vajra binding of the ground.
First one should meditate on emptiness.

Then, from the transformation of a red syllable aṃ, there is a sun disk. Upon it is a red syllable hūṃ. Above that, at the center of a staff adorned with a moist head, is a red syllable huṃ. There, again, on a sun, is the syllable hūṃ. By the rays of light that stream forth from it, all three worlds are gathered, in the expanse of space, into that form of the hero, and are then drawn back into it once more. One should then visualize oneself in the form of the glorious Red Yamāri.

His body is red. He stands with his left leg extended. His wrathful face bares its great fangs, and his tongue is curled back, terrifying. He is adorned with the eight nāgas. His reddish-yellow hair blazes upward. About his waist he is adorned with a dripping tiger skin. He is dwarfish, with a hanging belly, and terrifying. His three eyes are utterly terrifying and red, with an inexhaustible stream of flame and rays of light of every kind swirling all around. In his left hand he holds a human skull cup filled with the blood of Nārāyaṇa and the others, and in his right a white staff that destroys the four māras; he is ornamented with ornaments of nāgas and the like. Vajravetālī embraces him at the shoulder. He is wholly expanded in great passion. Just as is the nature of the Lord, so too is the Lady, distinguished by an ornament of dry heads. The ornament of the Blessed One, with whom she is joined, is of dripping heads, and her girdle and skirt are loosened. He is seated upon a red buffalo, with his left leg extended.

Afterward, at the center of the white three-pointed staff that has arisen from the Blessed One, one should again visualize the seed syllable, red in color. Then the vajra of the Blessed One, too, arises from the syllable hūṃ. Again, the secret lotus of the Blessed Lady, eight-petaled, with its small frog's-eye opening, is fully marked by the syllable aḥ. After that comes the mantra whose nature is the Victor:

oṃ sarba tathāgata anurāgaṇa bajra svabhāba ātmako'haṃ

Recited below the navel, it becomes an eight-petaled lotus, red in color. A red syllable hriḥ goes there. One should again visualize a white line reaching the crown of the head, and it should be inserted into the head of the Blessed One. Again, the one of good understanding should visualize it reaching that vajra path. Through their mutual union, the two together, it takes the form of a seed of equal taste.

The arousing from that seed, from the nature the goddess has taken, is as follows. One should see all the goddesses dwelling in the four directions, east and the rest, looking at their own lord with urgent minds and making an offering of song in order to arouse him:

"Wrathful one of compassion, Lord, arise!
Dispel the delusion of all the three worlds!"

So sings Vajracarcikā.

"Master, victor over the four māras,
My mind is distracted; Lord, arise!"

So sings Vārāhī.

"Host of the world, do you remain in emptiness?
Lord, arise for the very merit of the world!"

So sings Sarasvatī.

"You whose nature is awakening, host of the world,
Why do you remain, contemplating emptiness?"

So sings Gaurī.

Having been fully awakened, he again remains there. Then, from that semen, oneself having the nature of the deity: seated on a red buffalo, endowed with the nine moods of the dance, like a bandhūka flower, fierce, with one face and two arms, unbearable with fangs bared, with three round red eyes, a terrifying reddish-yellow beard, dwarfish, with a hanging belly, terrifying, his reddish-yellow hair standing on end, his tongue curled and dripping blood, his brows knit, laughing fiercely; in his right hand he holds a white staff marked with a head, and in his left a skull cup filled with liquor and blood; his splendor equals that of ten million suns; he stands with his left leg extended, and from the back of the buffalo he terrifies and threatens Yama; he has the appearance of one sixteen years old; he is adorned with the eight nāgas, a garland of heads bound on his head and a garland of heads hanging down; he is the deity who wears a skirt of tiger skin. One should meditate with a diligent mind on Vajravārāhī, who embraces him, alike in form and color to the Blessed One, holding a curved knife and a skull cup, her body adorned with the five mudrās. Then, if one maintains the pride of the deity, there is no doubt that one will be accomplished.

Then one should recite the mantra bajra svabhāva ātmako'hāṃ. Then the Blessed Lady, with great passion, gazes over all his limbs, greater and lesser, and with her lotus arouses the vajra; and one should visualize the tiger-skin garments of both of them being lifted up. Then, thinking, "These arouse passion," one gives the vajra and the lotus to the mind, and one should meditate intently on the vajra arising from the transformation of a red syllable hūṃ, its tip blessed by a yellow syllable oṃ; and on the lotus arising from a red syllable āḥ, its anthers blessed by the syllable āḥ.

Then, having recited oṃ sarba tathāgata anurāgaṇa bajra svabhāva atmako'haṃ, one should meditate on the couple themselves as rising up from the equal union of vajra and lotus, just as with an outer couple. Then, at the moment of emission, one should meditate that the two elements, dripping at the same moment, mingle within the lotus of the goddess, whom one has meditated upon as oneself. Through the transformation of that white and red, one imagines Delusion Yamāri and the others, each just like oneself, at the center of the lotus, and one should meditate that they are born from the Blessed Lady. After that, looking upon them before one, one should imagine them each dwelling in their own places.

Then, on a sun on the eastern petal, is Delusion Yamāri, embraced by black Carcikā. On a sun on the southern petal is Slander Yamāri, yellow, embraced by yellow Vārāhī, who has the face of a sow. On a sun on the western petal is Passion Yamāri, red, united with white Sarasvatī. On a sun on the northern petal is Jealousy Yamāri, pale blue, in union with Gaurī. Or else one may meditate on all of them as alike to Red Yamāri and Vetālī.

In the southeast, the quarter of Agni, is a sapphire vase filled with the fourfold mixture of perfumes. In the southwest, the quarter of Nairṛti, is a golden vase filled with musk. Likewise in the northwest, the quarter of Vāyu, is a coral vase filled with sihlaka. In the northeast, the quarter of Īśāna, is a silver vase filled with camphor, each just as appropriate. One looks upon the filled skull cups, upon the sun and the moon in their completeness. This itself is nothing other than the supreme union with great bliss.

Then, with the rays of light from the syllable hūṃ that has arisen upon a lotus and a sun at one's own heart, one worships the wheel of the maṇḍala with every kind of offering, and with jaḥ hūṃ baṃ hoḥ one should truly summon, insert, and so on, and make offerings. With the words, "All sentient beings, according to the guru's instruction," there are the two yogas. Therefore the intelligent one, having summoned as before the hosts of yogins and yoginīs dwelling in the ten directions, makes offerings to them, confers empowerment, and so forth. While the tathāgatas and Locanā and the others, with minds wholly delighted, with songs of various kinds and with dance, confer empowerment with vases filled with the five nectars, they say:

"The great and precious empowerment,
To which the three realms pay homage,
Arising from the place of the three secrets
Of all the buddhas, I shall bestow."

As they speak and praise in this way, one imagines that, from the semen that has risen up onto the heads of all those in the maṇḍala, they are sealed by Vajrasattva. This is the rite of complete generation.

The supplication made by Delusion, Slander, Passion, and Jealousy:

"O, O Red Yamāri,
Guru whose nature is a rākṣasa's form,
Seeing you, I am terrified;
Guru, give up your wrathful nature!
When you dance, the face of the earth,
Above the earth and below the earth all tremble.
Wrathful one adorned with passion,
You who dance are a vetāla.
Your body is stout, short, and red,
Emanating forms of every kind;
Vajrasarasvatī bows to you;
You who dance are great bliss.
By the mantras hrīḥ ṣṭriḥ
You dispel the confusion of the three worlds.
Lord, you are the wrathful one of compassion;
You who dance, look upon beings!"

oṃ sarba tathāgata pūja bajra svabhāba atmako'haṃ

Then, as before, above a wind maṇḍala is a fire maṇḍala; above that, upon the three secret maṇḍalas arisen from the transformation of the syllable oṃ, one should visualize a skull cup arising from the transformation of the syllable āḥ. In its center, the five nectars, the five lamps, curd, milk, meat, fish, and so on are stirred with a staff until they are of equal taste; they are cooked, and one sees them as like the juice of lac. Having cooled them with oṃ āḥ hūṃ, one imagines a tongue with a tube arising from the transformation of the syllable hūṃ, and one should drink. With one's own mouth one should give it into the mouth of the goddess. Those in the maṇḍala, too, should imagine this and partake fully.

Then, having tasted the nectar and become truly delighted, one should afterward recite the mantra. Having shown the seed syllable and the emblem dwelling at the heart, one should recite the mantra:

oṃ hrīḥ ṣṭrīḥ bikṛtānana hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ phaṭ svāhā

One should recite it, contemplating, for as long as one wishes. When one wishes to rise, one acts for the benefit of beings with the light rays of one's own body, draws them all back into oneself, and should then remain at ease, mentally, in the yoga of the innate Yamāri. The state of the yoga of the innate Yamāri is described as holding the pride of being Yamāri, established by nature, in a form like one's own: "I am indeed Yamāri, naturally established, with one face and two arms." This is the great yoga. With these four yogas, in two sessions, or taking more sessions, at whatever time one now wishes, one continues constantly until it is complete.

Then, at the end of a hundred thousand recitations, one should recite throughout the whole night; in this way the preliminary approach will have been done. Then, with the hearth rite for all activities, one should perform ten thousand fire offerings of butter, honey, and red flowers, reciting the mantra with each one:

oṃ hrīḥ ṣṭrīḥ bikṛtānanamantra siddhimme dehīti hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ phaṭ svāhā

Then the mantra for accomplishing all activities will be accomplished.

This is the first chapter, on the deity and so forth of the Red One.

Then the Blessed Lady asked:

"How does the accomplishment
Of the Blessed One's great seal come about,
And by what means is it to be accomplished?
Blessed One, please tell this too."

Kissing mouth to mouth,
Embracing each other tightly,
In a form of supreme delight:
"I shall explain Ratī; listen!"

Into whatever form the yogins themselves assume in the manner of dancing, the Blessed One descends supremely and without interruption, just as the current of a river flows on. Here, whenever one grows weary of meditation, one should recite the mantra. In that case the form of the Blessed One will be quickly accomplished, and not otherwise. As long as this lasts, the accomplishment here is none other than the accomplishment of the great seal (mahāmudrā). All these too are accomplished solely through familiarization with yoga, and not otherwise. Through a person's practice of familiarization, the aims of liberation and the aims of desire, those that have arisen, those that have not arisen, and those already present, in all their varieties, and even the accomplishment of buddhahood itself, will come about in this very life.

Having completely abandoned all activities, with the mind having arrived at that state, one recites until he himself comes and speaks: "O, O practitioner, I shall grant whatever is supreme." Then, having given him a welcome offering and bowed, one asks to receive the accomplishment, and he grants it.

oṃ hrīḥ ṣṭrīḥ pikṛtānana āgaccha āgaccha hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ phaṭ svāhā

Then, if one wishes to accomplish Vajravetālī, one should make an image of Vajravetālī; it should be made on cloth or on a fruit. Having properly set before oneself the color and shape of the noble lady, and having made offerings as one's means allow, one should recite until one sees them all, each in her own distinct form, unmixed. One recites until she comes, embraces the practitioner, and says, "O practitioner, what is your command?" The practitioner should give her a welcome offering and say, "Become my wife," and at that she becomes his wife. By enjoying her, he becomes accomplished in the form of Yamāri.

oṃ dveṣarati āgaccha āgaccha nuda nuda truṭa truṭa hūṃ jaḥ svāhā

Or else:

oṃ vajravettali āgaccha āgaccha hūṃ jaḥ svāhā

Having taken as a bride, or having properly taken up, a young woman sixteen years old with beautiful limbs, one should generate her and make her a fortunate one. Then one should properly worship her as the empowered yoginī by way of the meditation of the mantra, embrace her with the left arm, imagine her in the form of the yoginī, and recite until she blazes. Then, holding her, one becomes accomplished in the form of Yamāri. One should recite:

oṃ vajravettali jvala hūṃ jaḥ svāhā

Then, if one wishes to accomplish Delusion Yamāri, one should take on the guise of Delusion Yamāri, press down a lotus with the soles of the right foot and the other, and recite until he calls out, "O practitioner!" and says, "What is your command?" and says, "What shall I do?" From then on he grants even the kingship of the three worlds, so what need is there to mention other accomplishments? Likewise, if one wishes to accomplish Slander Yamāri, Passion Yamāri, and Jealousy Yamāri, it is done in just the same way. The secret mantras that churn out the result are these; one should recite:

oṃ mohāyamāntakṛt agaccha āgaccha hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ phaṭ
oṃ piśunyamāntakṛt agaccha āgaccha hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ phaṭ
oṃ rāgayamāntakṛt āgaccha āgaccha hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ phaṭ
oṃ īrṣayamāntakṛt āgaccha āgaccha hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ phaṭ svāhā

Then, if one wishes to accomplish Delusion Ratī, one should make the form of the goddess described earlier, as Delusion Ratī, riding a buffalo together with oneself, press her down as before, and recite until she comes and says, "What shall I do?" From then on the practitioner's wishes will be perfectly fulfilled. In the same way one should accomplish Slander Ratī, Passion Ratī, and Jealousy Ratī. Then the mantras; one should recite:

oṃ mohārati āgaccha āgaccha hūṃ jaḥ
oṃ piśunārati āgaccha āgaccha hūṃ jaḥ
oṃ rāgārati āgaccha āgaccha hūṃ jaḥ
oṃ ījyarati āgaccha āgaccha hūṃ jaḥ

Or else:

oṃ vajracarcike agaccha āgaccha hūṃ jaḥ

and likewise one should recite vajravārāhī, vajrasvarasvasti, and vajragaurī.

Then, if one wishes to accomplish a yakṣa, one should at that time, with the intention of gaining a worldly accomplishment, make an image of a yakṣa, press it down, and accomplish it. All one's commands will be perfectly carried out. The same applies to a yakṣiṇī, a nāga, a nāginī, a preta, a female preta, a piśāca, a piśācī, a god, a goddess, a man, and a woman. Others too one should accomplish directly, reciting:

oṃ hrīḥ ṣṭriḥ bikṛtānana so-and-so sādhaya sadhaya hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ phaṭ svāhā

Then, if one wishes to accomplish the vajra staff, under the constellation Puṣya one should make a vajra staff of wood, a staff thirty finger-widths long, smear it with the five products of the cow, and adorn it beautifully with sandalwood and garlands of flowers; or else, holding whichever one with its tip pointed in the manner of a sword, one should recite until it blazes. Then one becomes a staff-bearer and, like Yamāri, overpowers adversaries. One should recite:

oṃ hrīḥ ṣṭriḥ bikṛtānana vajradaṇḍa jvālaya jvālaya hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ phaṭ svāhā

Then, if one wishes to accomplish the skull cup, one should make a skull cup thirty-two finger-widths in size, in the form of the crusher of Māra, place it in the left hand as before, cover it with the right hand, and recite until it blazes. The result is exactly the same. In the mantra, the difference is kapālaṃ jvālaya.

Then one should accomplish the staff mudrā. At that time, one makes a fist of the right hand and extends the index finger. Binding that mudrā, one should recite until it blazes. Then one gains the accomplishment of the form of Yamāri. In the mantra, the difference is daṇḍamudrāṃ jvālaya.

This means that one should accomplish everything precisely by fully enjoying all five sense pleasures. Whatever substances of the commitment one obtains should be eaten and drunk for the sake of completely fulfilling one's intention.

Again and again, whatever it may be,
All of it, without any doubt,
All that is to be eaten, one should drink;
Offer all first portions to Yamāri.

This was the second chapter, on the rite for accomplishing the great accomplishment.

Then, if one wishes to accomplish the minor accomplishments, one should accomplish pacifying and the rest. On birch bark, with milk enchanted a hundred and eight times, one should draw a twelve-spoked wheel with separate rims. Beginning from its eastern gate, one should write the twelve syllables clockwise:

oṃ hrīḥ ṣṭriḥ bikṛtānana hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ phaṭ

In the center one should write the syllable baṃ, the seed of water; joining two syllables raṃ face to face, one should write the name of the target in their midst. On the outside it should be encircled with the syllables. Then, having put the wheels face to face, one wraps them with white thread, binds them with an intoxicant, consecrates them a hundred and eight times, places them in a vase filled with milk, and recites the mantra. Having made offerings as one's means allow, one recites:

oṃ hrīḥ ṣṭriḥ bikṛtānana ba debadattasya śāntiṃ kuru hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ phaṭ svāhā

and there will be pacification.

When worldly people are afflicted by evil, disease, and the like, then in the ten directions of the village one should consecrate the torma seven times with this:

oṃ hrīḥ ṣṭriḥ pikṛtānana amukagrumasya sarbopadrava śāntiṃ kuru kuru hehe baliṃ gṛhṇa gṛhṇa phaṭ phaṭ svāhā

and give the torma according to the rite. Beginning with the outskirts, with this placed before one, if one performs a fire offering soaked in milk in the middle of the village for as long as needed, all afflictions will be pacified.

Then, if one wishes to enrich, one should draw two vajras as before, and in the center of the twelve syllables draw the syllable bhruṃ, the vajra of the wheel of the maṇḍala. In its center one should write the target's two pauṣaṭa joined face to face. On the outside it should be encircled with three square lines in saffron. Wrapping it with yellow thread, one consecrates it a hundred and eight times, places it in a vase filled with butter and honey, and recites the mantra. Having made offerings as one's means allow, one recites the mantra:

oṃ hrīḥ ṣṭriḥ bikṛtānana bhruṃ pauṣaṭ Devadatta's puṣṭiṃ kuru pauṣaṭ hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ phaṭ svāhā

One recites this until wealth grows and increases. Throughout the recitation, without interruption, one should perform fire offerings with curd and barley flour.

Then, if one wishes to bring someone under control, as before one should write the twelve syllables on two wheels with the juice of sena, and in the center the syllable hriḥ, the seed of Kurukumbha. In the center one writes two syllables hoḥ joined face to face. On the outside it should be encircled with a triangular line. Then, wrapping it all round with red thread, one consecrates it a hundred and eight times, writes with lac juice on a bowl filled with butter and honey, places it inside, makes offerings as one's means allow, and recites the mantra:

oṃ hrīḥ ṣṭriḥ bikṛtānana hrīḥ hoḥ Devadatta, to Devadatta, vaśamanaya hoḥ hūṃ hūṃ phaṭ phaṭ svāhā

One recites this until he comes under control. At the end of the recitation one should perform a fire offering of red flowers together with salt.

Then, if one intends to summon a person's body, one places the mantra and that person's name at the heart, in the middle of an image made of charnel-ground charcoal and earth, presses it down with the big toe, and recites, thinking, "This is the very body of the target," reciting until the body is summoned. In the mantra, the difference is ākarṣaya hūṃ in place of vaśamānaya, and jaḥ in place of the syllable hoḥ.

oṃ marme marmā abighāte oṃ carcike hara hara pukkaṃ māraya māraya for the sake of so-and-so jaḥ
oṃ gaṃbhire carcike padma hūṃ śoṇitaprīye gaṃbhīre to so-and-so jaḥ

At a solitary tree, in a charnel ground, or on a mountain, facing south and having gone naked, one should recite this a hundred and eight times. Then there is no doubt that it will succeed.

"Blessed One, what is the rite of the mantra?"

The Blessed One said: "Being alone in a secluded place, and having made directly present a very clear painted image of the noble lord, together with the gurus, buddhas, and bodhisattvas abiding in the ten directions, one writes, with a cut human bone as pen and with the great blood and crow's blood, the seed of wind in the center of a wheel twelve finger-widths across in the form of twelve spokes. Within it, joining face to face the enemy's name together with the visarga, one ties it to the neck of a crow; if it is not untied, he is then destroyed. If he is not destroyed then, one should write the twelve seeds on it:

phaṭ phaṭ hūṃ hūṃ na na ta kṛ bi ṣṭrīḥ hrīḥ oṃ

Having made offerings as one's means allow and fumigated it with great strength, one should send it off on a stream, if one can. If not, one enchants a crow's wing seven times; if one then speaks his name at the door of his house, he is at once driven out and destroyed.

Likewise, in the center of a twelve-spoked wheel, with the three hot spices, poison, mustard seed, the great bone, and firewood, one joins face to face two syllables ya with the visarga in the center of the seed of fire, encircles them on the outside with two syllables ṭha, and heats it in a fire of that firewood. In that very instant he will die, together with his son and wife, and together with his retinue. If not, one enchants a human bone four finger-widths long seven times and hides it at the enemy's door; this destroys his lineage.

In just the same way, with the twelve seeds transformed, in the center of a twelve-spoked wheel, using wood that lives on sandalwood or a creeper that others cannot overcome, one writes in the center of the seed of water. Joining those two face to face, one should encircle them on the outside with two syllables ṭha."

With milk over which a hundred and eight recitations have been made, as before, with a hundred thousand red flowers one causes a king to die; and as for a king who would not otherwise come into the condition of a servant, at that time one would oneself be committing the five acts of immediate retribution; this is certain. Or else it is done with śephālikā wood or with red flowers like it. In the same way, one should place it in a bowl fully filled with the twelve seeds and recite. Gods, asuras, kinnaras, great mahoragas, venomous snakes, ḍākas, ḍākinīs, yakṣas, rākṣasas, people hard to tame, lords of men, kings, and the rest: one makes them enter the condition of male and female subjects.

This was the third chapter, on the rites of the common pacifying and the rest, in the Tantra of Red Yamāri.

Then, as the Lord embraced her tightly
And pressed her lower lip with his teeth,
For the sake of beings, a matter of benefit
Was asked by Nairātmyā:
"Blessed One, how do beings,
How do they become kings?"

The Blessed One said, "Since I cannot refuse you, goddess, I shall explain it to you. Apart from the syllables a and ba, one should make the four flowers into pills and accomplish the three earths under the constellation Puṣya. In five households of the royal caste one should recite the secret mantra a thousand times. The smoke purification and torma by the vajra sun of the yoginī with her own mantra, and the accomplishment of one's own secret mantra, come from the practice of ten thousand recitations done beforehand. Having performed a hundred thousand approach recitations upon gold, silver, coral, beryl, and mālika, one should likewise perform fire offerings amounting to a tenth of that.

"Again, through effort on the vajra ground, one should set out rtaṃ paṃ caṃ paṃ raṃ in the directions. In the center the yogin, adorned with all ornaments, devoted to the secret mantra and in meditative equipoise, honors those who embody the deities of the mantra with offerings of the five enjoyments. Having taken up the burden of the whole realm,
The lord of men, like the captivating moon,
Through which all the buddhas and all the accomplishments
Swiftly come about:
Now, god, bring that about,
And, sovereign, grant me the supreme."
"So be it: truly, truly."
Bowing with gentleness and mildness,
And asking forbearance, he should go home.
Likewise with various songs
And music and the like,
Joyfully one should perform the fire offering.
Each day with the offerings of enjoyment,
And at night performing the gaṇacakra,
Together with the consort and with gifts:
In this sequence, for twenty days.
In six months the sacrifice is complete.
Likewise the lord of men comes properly,
And having given gifts of elephants, horses and the rest,
He requests the supreme.
Again and again the lords of men,
With whatever it may be and so forth,
Elsewhere he appoints as envoys:
"This kingdom is held by me.
If not, placing you under punishment,"
He gives. Various jewels and the like,
And swift steeds, are offered.
If he wishes to set the kingdom
Upon one of good fortune who benefits himself,
He gives the kingdom to him.
Whoever does not come,
With his name, the yantra
Should be heated in a khadira fire.
In that instant he is bewildered
And goes away to another land.
Whatever ash there is from the principal's hearth,
When given to anyone for illness and the like,
Brings those things to peace.
If, having kept the poṣadha,
One performs in this way the fourfold fire offering,
One attains the very rank of Indra.

"O Blessed Lady, because you were not content,
It has been explained to you.
How hard to find in the three worlds
Is this supreme among tantras!
O glorious goddess, in the Hevajra
The supreme liberation is taught.
In the tantra of glorious Cakrasaṃvara too,
The wheel of the yoginīs is set out at length.
Goddess, in the glorious Samāja
And in the yoga tantras too it is set out at length.
Ratī, in the tantra of Red Yamāri
The accomplishments are very many,
As many as the sands of the river Gaṅgā.
Goddess, listen with respect.
Goddess, when I have passed into nirvāṇa,
Unseen by Indra and the rest,
Virūpākṣa, lord of yogins,
Will appear in a brahmin family,
Possessed of great diligence and great power,
A teacher of my Dharma.
Some, having obtained this hidden tantra,
Will keep it well concealed,
And having recited the secret mantra a hundred thousand times each,
They will accomplish me.
In Jambudvīpa they will bestow
Accomplishments of many kinds.
kṛśu bījaṃ māskaṃ:
These the yogins of the seven
Will come to perceive.
Again, in the age of strife,
In the line of Bāla, found by the earth-dwellers,
Servants and the like
Will appear as yogins.
Kaliṅga and Baṅga,
Goḍa and Magadha,
Suraka, King Ubikṛ,
The king of Bijikala,
Godarama, Kaliṅga,
The one called Bimoji, and others:
Kings will become yogins
Of glorious Red Yamāri.
Some will be possessed of splendor,
Some possessed of diligence,
Some great in overcoming others.
Some here are at first worldly,
But then, intent on mantra as supreme,
Will again become kings, Indra and the like,
And universal monarchs.
The great secret mantra, well concealed,
Is not to be shown to childish yogins.
It should be taught to one who truly desires buddhahood
And enters into the tantra.
For this tantra that I have explained,
Empowerment should be conferred first,
And afterward this should be taught.
The king of tantras, the supreme of tantras,
Saying little of mantra and meditation,
Is accomplished merely by reading it.
Those who delight in sinful deeds,
And those of meager faith, will not accomplish it.
Yet even one who commits sin,
If he holds to the trainings,
Will accomplish the accomplishments without effort;
Of this there is no doubt.
Beginning from the ten virtues,
Afterward the Dharma should be made clear.
Likewise its activity
Will come about just as it is."

This is the eighth chapter, on the training in kingship.

"O Blessed One, if one who has become a king
Becomes righteous and fares well,
Please tell me that Dharma too.
For those who do not know the reality of mantra,
And for people here without meditation,
In order to liberate all sentient beings,
Make clear the reality of mantra."

"Without meditation there is no accomplishment.
If there is no meditation, then, apart from that reality,
It becomes a cause of something else,
And the childish grow weary.
First, by means of the deity's form,
The body should be purified.
Second is the recitation of secret mantra:
Reciting the vowels and consonants,
And, divided into body, speech and mind,
The wise one should recite the three syllables.
Likewise the wise one should recite
Twenty-one thousand six hundred times.
By the count of day and night
The secret mantra recitation is performed.
Then, at the very tip of the nose,
One should imagine the sequence of seed and sign
In the form of a mustard seed.
Through the vajra cross-legged posture,
By making the mind steady,
One will also bless oneself.

Just like an illusion, just like a dream,
And likewise again the moon in water,
Just so, from the guru's instruction,
Meditate on a nature such as this.
Just as water meets with water,
Just as water and water merge as one,
By joining illusion and emptiness as one,
Through the union of these two
One fully accomplishes union.
The yogin should not think of anything at all;
This becomes the supreme equanimity.
From not thinking of anything at all,
One's own reality becomes clear.
That which has nothing to be thought of and no thinking
Does not exist anywhere at all;
Here that is called the inconceivable.
It is the axe for the tree of existence.
At times laughing, speaking and staying,
At times one should fully engage.
The mind that cannot meditate
Does not know the distinctions as they are.
Accomplishing the various accomplishments
Likewise comes about through the stages of yoga.
Worldly people, through the wavering of their own minds,
Are born in the higher realms through desire.
He delights all the consorts,
The supreme sun of protection and the rest.
At times, with many ornaments and with camphor
One anoints the body; some anoint it with mud.
At times one says, "What do many ornaments accomplish?"
Those who abide in this have supreme joy,
Those who doubt have suffering,
And through bliss there is no harm.
If the yogin desires these things,
He should do them without deliberating.
Enjoying the five desirables,
Possessed of the five qualities of desire,
One should always play:
Liberated within saṃsāra, and liberated.
Liberated from things and non-things,
Wholly abandoning acts of shame,
Gone beyond the workings of the intellect,
Supreme in knowing all actions.

This is the ninth chapter, on reality.

"O Blessed One, what is the consecration like?
Sovereign, tell me of it."
"Ratī, listen to the four kinds of empowerment
That liberate sentient beings.
Because sprinkling makes one stainless,
One binds the vajra cross-legged posture.
That is what is truly desired:
Filled with the bliss of non-emission and the rest.
As long as these have become stainless,
The yogin should not cast off
The mind of awakening.
Having reached the place of birth, one discerns
For as long as the supreme of all joys lasts.
The form of bliss itself
Is the form of the perfect buddha.
Until the semen has become stable,
One should always meditate on oneself.
That is known as worldly truth;
It is not the ultimate meaning.
Why? Because the fourth is given again.
Here, the wisdom-gnosis,
That very one, as the fourth empowerment,
Childish people will disparage,
Saying, 'That is again the same.'
The fourth itself, in the third,
Would not be performed in the same way;
Likewise, to mention it again
From the first limb would be a fault.
For the childish, what is the third, the cause,
Is the fourth, the result;
Because cause and result are not different,
The fourth too is established in the third, it is said.
It is the very ultimate truth spoken of before,
From which, by means of the three mudrās,
In order to accomplish the three results,
In this way of secret mantra, the result of the fire offering,
Free of the two mudrās, is wondrous.
For accomplishing the result of the two mudrās,
The action mudrā and the wisdom mudrā
Do not become the cause, it is said.
Having wholly given up meditation
On the action mudrā and the wisdom mudrā,
One meditates with certainty on the great mudrā.
This is the bliss of the union of the two organs,
Called the bliss of enjoying every desire.
With the characteristics of emission and non-emission,
The enjoyment of the essence does not become bliss.
The emission of the family produced by the third, the cause,
Is the result of the third;
It does not become the ultimate fourth."
Thus it should be understood.

This is the tenth chapter, on the rite of empowerment.

"Blessed One, please teach the rite of the painted image."

The Blessed One declared: "One should diligently have a woman of red and white complexion spin the thread. One should begin under Puṣya, and she too should be consecrated. Then one should give her diligently the yarn and the materials for weaving, with thread, a narrow cloth of red and white. Each day, relying on the samaya, together with the consort, it is woven; and that cloth should be given a sprinkling of impurity by the woman of red and white complexion at the time when she has her menses. After three days, one should call for a painter who possesses the samaya, and whatever he names as the price of a hero should be given to him. Having made ink with the great honey, one should paint with a pen of human bone. For as long as the painting is being done, having drunk a little of what intoxicates, each day at the four junctures of the sessions one should offer torma and recite the secret mantra one hundred times. The families should, through the samaya, be made one without fail. By the sequence of the heroes, the consecration too is to be done in great secrecy, it is said. Moreover, the yogin of Yamāri who possesses the samaya should make a book of twelve finger-widths on birch bark, it is said."

This is the eleventh chapter, on the rite of the painted image and the book.

"Sovereign, teach me the yoga of the winds."
The Blessed One declared:
"At the heart, the life wind and the downward-clearing wind are taught;
In the region of the navel, the equally abiding wind;
In the region of the throat, the upward-moving wind;
That which reaches the whole body is the pervading wind.
Of these, the chief, in the center,
Is the life wind abiding at the heart.
Through the distinction of breathing out and breathing in,
The transitions of life are sixteen.
In practice, for each one individually
There are four maṇḍalas, and for each one,
Through each daṇḍa as it flows,
Twenty-one thousand six hundred.
Flowing and touching from the right,
It should be called the maṇḍala of fire.
From touching the left and the right,
It becomes the wheel of Mahendra,
And becomes the steady, slow maṇḍala.
Lalanā and rasanā
Abide on the left and on the right.

Avadhūtī, in the central region,
Follows the innate.
It comes to enter, to be expelled,
And to abide in its motionless nature.
The wind of the thread, called the Destroyer,
Enters for as long as life lasts.
Entering is called kumbhaka;
From holding it, pūraka;
Expelling should be known as recaka;
The motionless becomes kumbhaka.
The mass of winds that fully enters
Is counted in hundreds of thousands and more.
Just as the speech of the Buddha, the protector, says,
From that instant it becomes accomplished.
Whatever mass of winds has become one,
By tightly embracing the consort,
Is accomplished in about half a month.
Through union with one's own chosen deity,
One is fully endowed with the gnosis of a buddha
And comes to know the five superknowledges.
Through union with one's own chosen deity,
One tightly embraces one's own woman:
Heart to heart,
The secret joined fully with the secret,
And mouth placed to mouth.
Devoted to non-action,
One should meditate on the four seeds abiding at the heart
As completely filled.
Whoever is stable there, by that very means
That person will come to know everything.
By merely turning his attention,
He knows past, future and present,
And knows the minds of others too.
He speaks the truth truly.
Likewise, by that very union,
One should meditate on the ears.
Just like a sound close by,
One hears all the directions and places.
Likewise, having meditated on the eyes,
One surely sees even the three worlds.
Likewise, having contemplated the nose,
One knows all smells.
Likewise, having contemplated the tongue,
One fully perceives tastes from far away.
Likewise contemplate the tip of one's own organ,
And one knows all tangible things.
Likewise, having contemplated the center of the head,
One greatly increases all powers.
Wherever the wind abides,
By making it of one taste,
It ceases in that very place,
And one realizes each of them individually.
Pacifying, increasing, subjugating,
Likewise summoning, killing,
Expelling, and all the rest,
One accomplishes fully by meditation alone.
Through the practice of kumbhaka and the rest,
The four gazes should be applied with certainty.
The gaze that looks to the left
Subjugates through kumbhaka.
Summoning from the right should be known;
It is applied with certainty through pūraka.
The gaze that abides at the forehead
Kills through recaka.
The gaze that abides at the tip of the nose
Paralyzes through kumbhaka.
The moving flower with kumbhaka;
On the snuhī tree, too, with recaka;
Eating evenly, with recaka;
With movement, at the root, with kumbhaka.
First applying the gazes with certainty,
One should contemplate for six months.
Possession of every power
Is accomplished from the cessation of mind.
From the cessation of mind, the wind ceases;
From that cessation, the mind too ceases.
It is said, "Seeing together with the gaze";
It is thinking of one another.
When the vajra of wisdom and means
And the lotus meet as one,
By enjoying the bliss of cessation,
All is held to be perfectly accomplished.
Brahmā, Viṣṇu and the other gods
Become that mantra practitioner's companions;
What then of all worldly beings?
What could they do?
What the Omniscient One taught
In the yoga and yoginī tantras
Is fully practiced in the age of strife.
Do not regard it as without accomplishment.
The accomplishments that arose
In the Kṛta, the Tretā and the Dvāpara ages,
Again, in the age of strife,
Goddess, regard as the supreme accomplishment.
It is hidden, the domain of the Omniscient;
For that purpose I have explained it to you.
Whoever does not know glorious Yamāri,
His toil is without fruit.

So it is said. This is the twelfth chapter, called the reality of wind, in the Tantra of Red Yamāri.

"Blessed One, in the name of Yamāri,
The one whose name says 'Red,'
What does the syllable ya express?
Likewise, what of the syllable ma?
Again, its syllable a,
And what does the syllable ri express?
Of what is rakta the name?
Sovereign, please tell me truly."
"Excellent, excellent! What you have asked
I shall explain, so listen.
The syllable ya is the wind;
It is to be called Red Yamāri.
Then the syllable ya is supreme joy;
The syllable ma should be called the second;
Āri is the third joy;
Rakta bears the name of the fourth.
Or else, for the one possessed of wind,
Its means is the syllable a;
The syllable i is the faculty of touch;
Rakta is nondual union.
Or else, rakta is the king of passion;
The wind impels the arrow, and the mind reaches there;
Āri is emptiness and compassion as one.
Thus it is called Raktayamāri.
The five names of the four joys
Should be known as the four moments."
"Then, sovereign, tell me
The meaning of delusion and the rest."
"Delusion Yamāntaka
Is Vairocana; the Yamāntaka of slander
Is Ratnasambhava;
Passion Yamāntaka
Is Amitābha; envy Yamāntaka
Is Amoghasiddhi; in the center Akṣobhya
Is Red Yamāntaka."
"What is the nature of Carcikā and the rest?"
"The earth element is Locanā, Carcikā;
The water element is Māmakī, Vārāhī;
The fire element is Pāṇḍarā, Sarasvatī;
The wind element is Tārā, Gaurī;
The space element is Vajravetālī,
Who embraces the Blessed One.
From the passion that follows great passion,
Through the taste of great bliss,
The hero of nondual nature
Is like the moon full in its sixteen parts."

This is the thirteenth chapter, the brief teaching of the names, in the Tantra of Red Yamāri.

Vetālī tightly embraced him
And pressed his lower lip with her teeth.
For the sake of sentient beings,
That goddess asked him about the great torma.

The Blessed One declared: "Having taken on the pride of being glorious Red Yamāri, with a canopy above and white yak-tail whisks, and outside beautified with dance and song, adorned with every ornament, with the left hand and the other, filled entirely with the sound of bell and vajra, one should cast the torma from a vessel that is blazing, clear and abundant; two vessels should be set out. One vessel should be filled completely with go, ku, da, ha and na, with flesh together with the five nectars, bi, ra, śu, mā and mu, with curd, and with fish meat.

Elsewhere, having set up the gods of the house and so on, with this mantra, oṃ hrīḥ ṣṭrīḥ bikṛtānan hūṃ sarba bighāgaṇāṃ saparivārāna ākarṣaya jaḥ, one gives command to those of the eastern quarter. Then one should imagine going there oneself, binding the throats of the hosts of obstructors with a noose, piercing their hearts with a hook, and drawing them in.

Indra is white and thousand-eyed, holding a vajra in his hand and riding a white elephant. In the south is Yama, black, with a staff in his hand, riding a buffalo. In the west is Varuṇa, white, riding the sea monster makara, with a white water lily in his hand. On the northern petal is the yakṣa, yellow, riding a man and holding a pomegranate in his hand. All of these are adorned with every ornament and wear jewel crowns. Yama has a sun seat and the light of the sun; the rest have moon seats and the light of the moon.

On the petal of Īśāna is the lord of bhūtas, white and three-eyed, with a crown of matted locks, riding a bull. He holds a spear in his hand, wears a garment of tiger skin, his body is smeared with ash, and he is adorned with every ornament of bone; he has a moon seat and the light of the moon. On the petal of fire is Agni, the god of fire, yellow, short of stature and pot-bellied, riding a goat, holding a rosary and a water pot, with a crown of matted locks, a sun seat and the light of the sun, and wearing the garments of a hero. On the petal of Nairṛti is a rākṣasa with loosened hair, holding a garland of skulls in his hand, wrathful, his brows knitted in a scowl, black, upon a corpse, possessing a sun seat and the light of the sun. On the petal of the wind's quarter is Vāyu, god of the wind, green and red, holding a banner in his hand, with a jewel crown, riding a deer, with a moon seat and the light of the moon, wearing every ornament. At the center is Pṛthivī, the earth goddess, yellow, adorned with divine garments and every ornament, with a moon seat and the light of the moon, abiding entirely surrounded by pretas and earth gods. Indra and the others hold nāga nooses in their left hands, and all of them stand with the left leg extended.

Above, on an eight-petaled lotus resting on a source of phenomena: on the eastern petal is the Moon, white, riding a horse, with a white jewel crown and the light of the moon. On the southern petal is the Sun, red and two-armed, holding lotuses, riding a green horse, with a jewel crown and every ornament, and with a deep blue light. On the western petal is Mercury, yellow, riding a bull, with a sun seat and the light of the sun, holding a trident. On the petal of the wind are Rāhu and Ketu, with sun seats and the light of the sun, deep black and smoke-colored: Rāhu in the manner of seizing and devouring the wheel of saṃsāra, and Ketu holding his palms joined. At the center of it, on the hub, is Brahmā, four-faced and four-armed, holding a triple staff and making the gesture of fearlessness, with his remaining hands holding a rosary and a water pot. He is yellow, riding a goose, with a moon seat and the light of the moon, wearing the sacred thread, with yellow matted locks, and surrounded by the short-statured sons of Brahmā: those named the gods of Brahmakāyika, Brahmapurohita, Yāma, Tuṣita, Nirmāṇarati, Akaniṣṭha and so on.

On the petals in the directions of the lower source of phenomena are the son of Vāsuki, yellow; the nāga Padma, white; the nāga Karkoṭaka, red; the nāga Takṣaka, black; and the nāga Śaṅkhapāla. They have human faces, wear every ornament, are marked by serpent hoods upon their heads, and are together with asuras and hell beings. At the center is Śeṣa, white, with a jewel crown and a human face.

Having thus made these hooded ones firm, and they being transformed in turn, one completes them with the form of glorious Yamāntaka. Then, through the full union of body, speech and mind, those abiding above are of the aspect of body; those abiding in the middle are, again, of the aspect of Akṣobhya; and those abiding below should be seen as of the aspect of vajra speech. For the sake of all rites, again, one should take on the pride of their arms and faces, whatever they are, as is fitting. Or else, in every case, they are just like one's own form.

After that, with oṃ arghaṃ pratīccha svāhā, having offered water for the feet to their feet with the fang mudrā, and tasting the nectars spoken of before, one should carry out the conduct of the torma.

"bhagabān! Blessed One, please speak of the four bodies."

The Blessed One declared:
"Listen! In the bhaga, beyond the world,
Are the four bodies of the buddhas;
For the benefit of the worlds
I, Heruka, have taught them.
Those who lack the instructions of the holy guru,
Who have utterly cast aside the body of the Buddha,
Who are wholly deficient in the Vajrayāna,
Turn their faces away from this tantra.
Whoever does not know the syllable ho,
The yogin gathered into existence,
Following the explanatory tantra,
The yogin who is himself Yamāri,
The great tantra of the Red One, of few words:
Whoever does not know it is utterly wearied.
Why is its bliss great?
Because it gathers all bliss together.
On the lotus of sixty-four petals,
Together with the cause, for the sake of the yoginī,
Bliss is attained from complete enjoyment;
Enjoying and enjoying, one sports in it.
In a single instant there is perfect, complete awakening;
One enjoys through the conduct of the three existences.
These are the four bodies;
They are not the domain even of the buddhas.
With the conventions of what transcends the world
They are taught in the manner of the holy yoga.
In the form of the four cakras
I taught it in the Hevajra.
The application of meditation, in the Samāja;
The channels, by all the ḍākinīs;
The sādhana of the result, in the Hevajra;
And where the sādhana of the wind,
From the division of day and night,
Is the sixty-four daṇḍa periods on the petals:
These things, through a shared analysis,
Should now be stated.

Having the nature of the deity's form,
Again, by division into body, speech and mind,
With a mind set in equipoise on the three syllables,
One should realize it inwardly.
With the syllable oṃ, entering;
With the syllable āḥ, likewise abiding;
With the syllable hūṃ, expelling as well:
Do this according to the yoga instructions.
By this, called oṃ āḥ hūṃ,
What moves and stirs becomes stable.
Then, again, the seed and its sign
One should contemplate intently at the tip of the nose.
Make it the nature of luminosity,
And likewise the equality of self and other.
When the form without an agent is fully apprehended,
Again and again, instant by instant,
Having risen once more from the one,
The mantrin should contemplate throughout
With the discernment that it is like a shadow, an illusion.
Whatever comes through this pure body,
One should meditate on with diligence in wisdom.
Through that, for all sentient beings,
It acts as the nature of reality.
This very yoga of the deity
Should be realized from the guru's instructions.
Again, the mantrin, for the benefit of the world,
Should once more meditate on the syllables.
Continuously, at all times,
One should bind at the place of complete enjoyment.
Joined firmly with the hands, from the upper
Yantra of the stable syllable,
Into the middle of the lalanā and rasanā
Make it enter without moving.
Having joined it firmly into body, speech and mind,
One should contemplate the supreme mudrā.
From that instant the innate arises;
Of what is to be meditated here there is no doubt.
Whoever comes to know this meaning,
The one of excellent understanding, his mind in equipoise,
By it comes to know all phenomena:
This is the instruction of the path of the buddhas.
Washed by it at the great ford,
He is consecrated by the tathāgatas.
Moreover, wherever he goes,
There the buddhas come.
Being a bodhisattva at all times,
He benefits sentient beings of every kind.
Proclaiming the syllables hā hā,
And crying 'what?' again and again,
He makes the māras who dwell in the ten directions
All take flight.
Joining their palms,
All bow down at his feet.
Women bearing various forms,
Seeking their chance out of various desires,
Rambhā, Tilottamā and the others:
Having summoned them, he enjoys them fully."

This was the fourteenth chapter, on the reality of the wind, in the Tantra of Red Yamāri.

Then the Blessed One was asked to teach the enumeration of the winds.
"Listen, and I shall explain, analyzing it fully.
The thirty-two daṇḍas of the family
Become the four watches;
By two sixteens they are differentiated.
In one watch, with the daṇḍas in excess,
A half watch is four daṇḍas;
Thus it proceeds in sequence.
By day and by night, in sequence,
Sixteen transitions are declared.
Fire, wind, water and
Mahendra, these four maṇḍalas,
Through their flowing, are known as four daṇḍas.
The winds, from the increase of the four,
Are declared to be four cakras.
The four, the various and the rest,
From the division into instant, moment and so on,
Are declared to be of four kinds.
Likewise there are the four joys;
The places too are declared to be four.
The three bodies, and among them a fourth body;
Likewise everything is fourfold,
And the outer and the rest follow the same order.
The four yogas, and the fourth,
The bodies, are rightly declared to be four.
By this they become four daṇḍas;
By full division, sixty-four.
As taught in the Hevajra and here,
So too it is definitively taught: at the base
Of the body there are four cakras,
And these are the four bodies.
The joy that comes through the four joys
Is also declared to be the four bodies."

This was the concise teaching of the Tantra of Glorious Red Yamāri, the fifteenth chapter.

The Blessed One declared:
"What is to be said of the wheel of existence?
You have asked what is more secret than the secret of the gods.
For the deluded, failing beings of the three existences,
Listen to this essence!
By it the māras are surely conquered, it is said,
And buddhahood itself is ever accomplished.
Thus the yogin wanders in saṃsāra,
Sporting supremely in saṃsāra;
Like a lotus petal in the water,
He is not stained by faults at all.
This wheel of twelve aspects
Is established as twelve meanings;
This very wheel alone
Is renowned as the twelve links.
Whoever comes to know this wheel
Knows without remainder the teachings
Proclaimed by all the buddhas,
And turns the wheel of Dharma.
Therefore the turning of the wheel of Dharma
Of the twelve links
I shall explain: listen!"

Ignorance, formations, consciousness, name and form, the six sense sources, contact, feeling, craving, grasping, becoming, birth, and aging and death: these are the twelve links. Ignorance is the afflictions of the past. Formations are the actions of the past. Consciousness is the individual conceptualization of the past. Name and form is the five stages: the gelatinous, the viscous, the lump-like, the firming, and the stage in which the hands and feet move.

The six sense sources are the completion of the six faculties, the eye and so on, that have definitely emerged from the womb. Contact comes from the coming together of three: object, faculty and consciousness. Feeling is divided into three kinds by its characteristics of pleasure, pain and equanimity. Craving is the desire of sexual union. Grasping is the full seizing of desired objects. Becoming is the result of action, the becoming that is to come. Birth is the linking of rebirth once again in the aggregates. Aging and death are the four links beginning with name and form; so it is said.

From the distinctions of the twelve links
The Sage taught dependent origination.
Insofar as there are afflictions, actions
And sufferings, they are gathered into these three.
The first, the eighth and the ninth are afflictions;
The second and the tenth are actions;
The remaining seven are suffering.
Because the twelve are gathered into three,
The three phenomena are taught.
From the pair and the pair it arises,
And it arises in sevens and sevens.
That very thing turns again and again,
And because it turns, it is the wheel of existence.
Beings are mutually cause and effect;
Here there is no one at all; sentient beings are empty.
Thus from the empty, phenomena arise,
And there is no transference from phenomena either.
Recitation, a lamp, a seal,
A mirror, a sound, a sunstone,
And a seed: so this aggregate
Links into rebirth, and yet there is no transference.
What the learned are to take up,
Having rightly understood the discipline,
Is whatever the distinctions of the twelve links are:
This the Sage taught in brief, and it is explained.
How things arise in dependence
Should be understood in condensed form.
Wishing to hear each one definitively taught,
"That is the domain of the buddhas":
The master said this in that heart of the matter.
"Afflictions, actions and sufferings":
By this, "insofar as" is gathered up.

Here, in teaching the ten plus two one by one, "links" means the distinctions of the parts of these, just as with the countries of Aṅga and Baṅga. "Three distinctions" refers to the distinctions of the links. A sage is one who is a sage through the restraint of body, speech and mind; it was taught and spoken by that Sage. "Well known" is a synonym. These, moreover, are not established as the Principal, Nature, the Lord, the guardians of the directions, an essential nature or the like; they are whatever the distinctions of the twelve links are. Even so, into afflictions, actions and sufferings, supporting one another in the manner of three sticks propped together, they proceed, gathered into three for as long as they last, like water.

Again, if one asks how, when afflictions, actions and suffering are gathered into these three just as they are, they proceed, it should be stated: "The first, the eighth and the ninth are afflictions." Among the distinctions of these twelve links, these three, the first, ignorance, the eighth, craving, and the ninth, grasping, should be understood as the conditions that are the afflictions. What is meant by "The second and the tenth are actions"? Formations are the second, and becoming is the tenth. These should be known as gathered into action. "The remaining seven are suffering": the seven distinctions of the links that remain of those gathered into affliction and action should be known as gathered into suffering, namely, consciousness, name and form, the six sense sources, contact, feeling, birth, and also aging and death. The use of the word "also" is the proper use of gathering: it properly gathers ignorance and the rest into suffering. Likewise, all these twelve phenomena are gathered into the three called affliction, action and suffering; this is so as to make known that there are neither fewer nor more. The word "are" shows definitively that, for the present, these are what the sūtra teaches; this is said because there is no enumeration here declaring that there are others besides these.

If one asks why, and into what, these turn by way of actions and afflictions, and by way of actions and sufferings, it should be stated: the three become a pair, and from the pair they become seven. From the three called afflictions arises the pair; what is called action is the pair. From the pair the seven called suffering arise, and, as definitively taught before, from the seven the three arise. Because that very pair called affliction keeps turning within what is called suffering, it is the wheel of existence. There, those existences proclaimed in the form of action become a wheel bearing the names of the three, and in the seven they make the world revolve fully. The word "is" has the sense of certainty. For example, just as the moon revolves in rising in the east, it is the wheel of existence. Likewise, it does not arise as complete; even so, there is no fixed certainty. So much for the word "is".

Then, if one now asks what sentient being is the lord of all bodies, and by whom it was made, it should be stated: all beings are mutually cause and effect, and here there is no sentient being at all; the term comes from imputation upon something else. And the cause, too, is merely imputed as a cause to this collection. Therefore, when one speaks of a sentient being as existing, it is with reference to another substance.

Then, if one now asks what it is that goes from the present world to the world beyond, it should be stated: not even the mere object of the world beyond transfers as an entity. Rather, from the empty alone empty phenomena arise, and there is no transference. From the five phenomena devoid of anything belonging to a self, named affliction and action, arise the seven phenomena named result, empty of self and of what belongs to a self, and there is no transference. That is to say, from those things that abide as a self, with a nature grasped in that way, arise the seven empty phenomena devoid of intrinsic nature, and there is no transference.

If one asks what illustrates these, it is stated here:
"Recitation, a lamp, a seal,
A mirror, a sound, a sunstone,
And a seed: by these it is definitively taught."
That is, in reality there is no going, and by intrinsic nature there is no self, yet conventionally the world beyond exists. For example, in reciting after a teacher there is no transference: that entity is not the teacher's recitation, nor is it the student's recitation either.

Nor is it anything other than the student's recitation. Likewise, if it were to arise entirely anew, it would follow that it had the fault of being causeless. Likewise, the last mind of one who is dying does not go to the world beyond, for it would follow that it had the fault of permanence. Nor does the world beyond arise from something wholly other, for the fault of causelessness would follow. For example, the student's recitation has as its reason the recitation from the teacher. One can declare that it is not other than that, and likewise that it is that. In the same way, the mind that has the portion of birth, arising in dependence on the mind at the very end of dying, is not something other; likewise one can also declare that it is that. For example, there is a lamp from a lamp; an impression from a seal; a reflection in a mirror from a face; an echo from a sound; fire from the sun, from the light of a gem, and from firewood; and a sprout from a seed. For example, when, because one person eats a sour fruit, another's mouth waters, it should be well understood that the one does not come into contact with the other. Likewise, the learned have ascertained that aggregates do not transfer from aggregates; so it is said.

Here, the aggregates are those named form, feeling, perception, formations and consciousness. These aggregates that link into rebirth should be definitively examined as the five aggregates: they are neither causeless nor do they arise from something other. It is not that this world becomes the world beyond, and it is not that that becomes this one either; there is no transference of an entity. This, for the present, is the sequence of the wheel of saṃsāra.

The word "also" indicates reversal as well; it should be understood as the inverse of that. Because it is an entity whose nature is always empty of suffering, one does not become deluded about the entity of individual conceptualization. A mind without delusion does not continually give rise to it. Without craving, there is no fault. Without malice, there is never the action of wrongdoing. One does not grasp at entities that have become action. Without grasping there is no conditioning. There is no imputation of entity or non-entity. With respect to unborn aggregates, minds do not turn into sufferings. Likewise here, from following the cause of these five kinds, the seven kinds of result are obtained here; they do not arise from what is nonexistent other than that. That should be called liberation. Likewise, the bad and excessive views of self, permanence, annihilation, transference and the like do not arise. Here the Blessed One also spoke this verse:

"One who does not see what arises from conditions,
And in whom nothing is done by the object itself,
Who has meditated even on the subtle,
Examines by what it arises.
In this there is nothing at all to remove,
And not the slightest thing to be added.
Look correctly at the correct itself;
When the correct is seen, one is liberated."

This was the chapter on dependent origination, the sixteenth.

Those people of the world of Jambudvīpa who abide even in the manner of union, having the bliss of emission, have lifespans of a hundred years.

"Blessed One, apart from women, how does the semen become firm? Without the semen being made firm, one does not go on to a long life."

"Goddess, therefore, by binding the movement of the wind below and above, when the life force is made stable, aging and death do not occur in the body. First, once the body has been purified, one should accomplish the dharma mudrā. One should meditate on it in the same way as the outer joy. Likewise, the yogin should then again undertake the joy of the action mudrā.

"Pressing the thread of Brahmā firmly with the left foot and turning the face upward, one should visualize the syllable hā. Again, having made the syllable sīta, the syllable hūṃ abides at the heart. One should again meditate on the syllable āḥ. At the center of an eight-petaled lotus with its anthers is a blazing syllable hrīḥ, together with rays of light. One should imagine a white line that emerges and goes forth from it, comes back again, and reaches the center of a lotus of sixty-four petals. Again one should visualize the syllable aḥ, and the yogin should again bind the wind of the life force. In that region, the knot of Brahmā, opened by the goddess, comes forth, and one should imagine it joined to the bhaga and to the heart as well. On that eight-petaled lotus one should imagine red in the directions. In the corner of fire, fire, and likewise again in that of wind; in Īśāna's, blue as well; and likewise in the quarter of the rākṣasa. At its center is a syllable hūṃ that has arrived there, black in color, with rays of light. One should visualize a line emerging from it and reaching the crown of the head. Below and above, one should imagine a black syllable hūṃ with rays of light arriving there. One makes the line blaze, again and again in the same way. At that time the meditation to be done by the husband is from the guru's instructions.

"At that time, by the blazing of caṇḍālī, all the tathāgatas are burned, and the moon, the hare-marked one, melts and drips, becoming the bliss of great joy in the bhaga below. Again, so that it goes upward, pair by pair, one stops the life wind and the downward-clearing wind, joins the mind firmly to emptiness, and becomes intoxicated. Again, for as long as the mind is intoxicated, this is through the practice of binding the coil. At that time, one makes the moving essence turn to the right with effort, and again it settles near by means of the sound kheṭa. In the cakra of Dharma, by the nāda of the syllable hūṃ, one draws it back to its place, and having made the two channels stir in the cakra of complete enjoyment, one should again bind it firmly. When those two on the left have melted, then it will come. There it will be thoroughly purified. Again, churning and kissing in the cakra of wisdom are from the guru's instructions. Again, one should purify by the practice of intoxicating the three cakras. When it has gone upward, great bliss becomes stable.

"These are the supreme path; they are not the domain even of the chief gods. Whoever knows this possesses merit and will be worshiped by the three worlds together with their beings.

By practicing for one year, one's body becomes extremely subtle, one speaks forth with the nature of a great voice, and likewise one's mind becomes subtle in nature. One delivers at will, and at times one goes even across the ocean and through the sky. One does whatever one wishes. The one of excellent understanding sports and rejoices.

This was the chapter on practice in the Tantra of Red Yamāri, the seventeenth.
