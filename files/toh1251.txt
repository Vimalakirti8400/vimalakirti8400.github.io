The Sādhana Called Blazing Jewel (Ratnajvālasādhananāma)

The Sādhana Called Blazing Jewel, known in Sanskrit as the Ratnajvāla Sādhana and in Tibetan as Rinchen Barwa, is Toh 1251 in the Tengyur, where it belongs among the commentaries and ritual works on the tantras. It is a means of accomplishment, or sādhana, for the meditational deity Hevajra, opening with homage to the goddess Vajraḍākinī, and its author presents it as a guide of moderate length for practitioners daunted by works that are either too extensive or too condensed. Organized as a sequence of chapters on the stages of ritual activity, it describes the qualities of the qualified tantric master and leads the practitioner through the rites of Hevajra practice, including detailed instructions on the offerings made to the deities. It closes with verses insisting that conventional cause and effect be honored together with the realization of emptiness, and its colophon attributes it to the great Indian scholar Prajñendraruci, who translated it into Tibetan together with the monk Shākya Yeshé.

In the language of India: ratna jāla sādharna nāma. In the language of Tibet: The Sādhana Called Blazing Jewel.

Homage to glorious Vajraḍākinī.

Pervading all the places and objects that thought imagines, and gathering them in;
wisdom and method, the two; the four joys, the four drops;
through the emanation of the four bodies you act for the good of beings: to you I bow.
For myself and for others like me, daunted by what is too extensive or too condensed,
I compose the path of the stages of activity of Hevajra,
the sādhana called Blazing Jewel.

The yogin who is devoted in turn
to the conduct of the śrāvakas, of awakening and of mantra,
calm, profound and skilled in every science,
who brings about bliss through the union of mantra and tantra,
and who knows the ten realities:
the wisdom of vajra and bell,
the deity, the maṇḍala, the fire offering,
the mantra, the colored powders, the torma,
and the empowerment,
in a charnel ground, on a riverbank, by a solitary tree,
on a mountain, in a thicket, in a rock shelter and the like,
in a place adorned with flowers, fruit and so forth,
having anointed it with the five nectars and with perfumes,
should perform the approach before the accomplishment.
Without it there will be no accomplishment.
Whatever ritual actions there may be,
they are accomplished through close approach.
On Hevajra and his retinue,
the essence of all, of entity and free of entity,
meditate in four sessions, with offerings and torma.
Between sessions, recite a hundred thousand or ten thousand times,
or until excellent signs are seen.
Then the accumulations increase, obstructors are pacified, and the aim is accomplished.

In order to draw this supreme maṇḍala,
the wise one, in a village or a town,
on a mountain or in a delightful region,
in a flower garden or a solitary place,

Ground with udumbara, nyagrodha,

or shaped like a tortoise's back, is to be avoided.

Obstructors who dwell here,

Beginning with the winter months and so on,
for three months at a time the head of the mahoraga
looks to the south, the west and the east,

To the depth of knee, navel or waist,

Throw out husks, gravel, stones,
sticks, charcoal and the like.

With offerings and torma,

Then, with the wheel of protection,
cut down obstructors and enemies and give protection.

The vajra wrathful ones, great beings,
all have one face and two arms;
the left hand at the heart is a fist with the index finger raised,
and the right holds each one's own weapon.
The slayer of Yama has a club;
Aparājita, a vajra staff;

Ṭakkirāja has a hook;

Acala the protector, a vajra.
Visualize the wheel of Dharma
of the king with the white parasol.
The one beneath the feet has a pestle vajra.
They stand with the left leg extended.
Like the color of blue sapphire,
the Antaka is to be visualized.
Aparājita
is terrifying, like a moonstone.
The wrathful one of the lotus is a deep red;
Vajrāmṛta is black by nature.
The wrathful ones of the intermediate directions are very black,
and bear Vajradhara on the crown of the head.
Yama is sealed by Vairocana himself;
the one with the staff by Vajrasattva;
the lotus wrathful ones by the Seer;
the one with joined palms by Vajrasattva.
In the center of the vajra maṇḍala
one should visualize a vajra sun.
Imagining a lotus seat beneath,
visualize the ten wrathful ones.
The wheel of protection, of great splendor,
blazing like the fire at the end of an eon,
spinning yet utterly unmoving,
measures a full hundred leagues.

From The Sequence of Rites of Glorious Hevajra, the third: performing the preparation.

On ground that is level and pleasing,
well smeared with the five products of the cow,
the five nectars, and fragrant water,
the wise one, with an outer extent of seven cubits
and an inner one of three cubits and three thumb-widths,

A new and beautiful line-cord,
arisen from the seeds of the five families and of their colors,

divided into five groups of five,
is drawn in with hooks from the blazing eyes of sun and moon.

Sending it out with jja, and reciting oneself as well,
holding it at the center with the left fist,
from west and east the intelligent one

oṃ vajra samaya sūtra mātikrama hūṃ
"Sugatas who fill the whole of space,

Roused by the sound of the line,

If the line-cord breaks, the master dies.

Through mistaking the directions, harm is declared

The maṇḍala is said to be a lotus.
The supreme maṇḍala of the mind
is what is called the celestial palace:
the mind is the palace, tiered and lofty.
Its outside is square,

The minds of loving-kindness and the rest

What is rightly called the laying of the lines
is the yoga of mindfulness of phenomena.
Liberation from all views
is said to be the line-cords of wisdom.
What are rightly called the ornaments

The four pillars of the archways

The four parapets
are the four applications of mindfulness.

The adornment with flower garlands

The turning of the wheel
is the yoga of the two accumulations.

it is called the half-net.
This is the suchness of the maṇḍala.

The five aggregates, in brief,
are well known as the five buddhas.
The vajra sense sources, assuredly,

Earth is everywhere called Locanā;
the water element is Māmakī;

Through hatred enjoying hatred,
hatred is exhausted: this is Prajñāntaka;
he is therefore the ender of mind,
and therefore the ender of the afflictions.
Through passion enjoying passion,
passion is exhausted: this is Padmāntaka;
he is therefore the ender of speech,
and likewise the ender of union.
The exhaustion of all afflictions,
likewise the exhaustion of all karma,
and the exhaustion of all obscurations, the wisdom,
is called Vighnāntaka.
This is the suchness of the deities.
This is the irreversible master empowerment.

From The Path of the Sequence of Activities of Glorious Hevajra, the activity of offering: the thirteenth chapter.

Since whatever belongs to the three realms is stained,
having set the mind firmly on the sacred that transcends them,
there is the single path of the Victors, endowed with wisdom and means,
profound and vast, not the domain of logicians.
Therefore, through the succession of the lineage of the guru's instructions,
the Lord of Victors' treasury of the essence of nectar,
the path of the ocean of the mantra way, is here condensed:
the Path of the Sequence of Activities of Hevajra.
Those who see that these dependently arisen phenomena,
distinct results from a gathering of causes and conditions,
by their very nature have a capacity to produce beyond thought,
who do not let such conventional truth go to waste,
and who realize that, since they arise thus from causes and conditions,
in the ultimate sense they lack intrinsic nature:
for those, undefeated, rich in the wealth of wisdom,
adornment with this Blazing Jewel becomes beautiful.
But those who let conventional cause and effect go to waste,
who are unskilled in the ultimate way and have become deluded,
paupers without the wealth of wisdom:
for them it does not become beautiful, like ornaments on a corpse.
The wise, having untied the knot of clinging to self-grasping,
in order to bring self and others across to unsurpassed awakening,
wearing the armor of compassion, endowed with wisdom and means,
have nothing they may not do with body, speech, and mind.
Therefore, at the request of noble friends,
when the path of the learned had fallen into decline,
with the eye of wisdom and the feet of reasoning
I have examined it well and traversed it, free from fear of error.
By whatever merit I have gained in composing this,
through this path, the sādhana of the Blazing Jewel,
may all beings swiftly cross the ocean of saṃsāra
and swiftly attain the state of Hevajra.

The sādhana called The Blazing Jewel, the Path of the Sequence of Activities of Glorious Hevajra, is complete. It was composed by the master, the great scholar Prajñendraruci, and is complete. It was translated, edited, and finalized by that same Indian preceptor and the monk Shākya Yeshé.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

ཁྲེ in the line on recitation is read as ཁྲི, "ten thousand". The lines naming the attributes and crown seals of the ten wrathful ones are very terse. Here the "slayer of Yama" is Yamāntaka, Aparājita is Prajñāntaka, and Amṛta is Vighnāntaka (Amṛtakuṇḍalin). ཐབ་སྦྱོར is read as ཐལ་སྦྱོར, "joined palms". གཟིགས, "the Seer", probably refers to Avalokita, but this is uncertain. The trees listed in the site test are given under their Indian names as the Tibetan spells them.  The lists of dance stances, hand gestures, sentiments and the parts of song are technical terms of Indian performance. They are translated literally and some are uncertain. The seven songs correspond to the seven notes of the Indian scale: ṣaḍja, ṛṣabha, gāndhāra, madhyama, pañcama, dhaivata and niṣāda. The line "With the syllables ma ṭa, for eyes of sun and moon" is terse, and its sense is uncertain.  The five medicines are named in Sanskrit transliteration, and their spelling in the Tibetan is irregular. The line "Buddhas, who grant the aims to be done for beings and their retinues" is compressed, and its construction is uncertain. In "the master, the gods of the elders", the phrase རྒན་པའི་ལྷ is taken literally. The stick-casting lines are terse: བཅས་ནས is read as བཅའ་ནས "having chewed it", and བ་ཀོ as "cowhide".  Two evident copyists' slips are translated by sense: རབ་ཏུ་འཆང ("hold") is read as འཆད ("cut off") in "cut off the stream of the lower realms," and རིན་ཆེན་ལྔ་ཕྱེད ("five and a half jewels") as ལྔ་ཕྱེ ("powder of the five precious substances"). ཐེག་ཅན is taken as the Great Vehicle (ཐེག་ཆེན), as the next verse confirms. The line-by-line instructions for the maṇḍala's eight subdivisions (door recess, wall, garlands, ledge, cornice, parapet, archways) are terse technical shorthand; they are rendered literally and the exact architectural reference of some phrases is uncertain.  Some evident copyists' slips are translated by sense: རང་ལྟ ("own view") as རང་ལྷ ("own deity"); in the vajra family verse, སྒོམ་པ ("cultivation") as སྡོམ་པ ("vow"), matching the parallel lotus family line; མི་ནུས་བྱ ("do what one cannot") as ཅི་ནུས་བྱ ("as far as one is able"); and the instrumental in འབྱུང་པོས་གཏོར་མ་སྦྱིན as a dative ("to the bhūtas"). In the proclamation of truth, Maheśvara ("Great Lord") is used as an epithet of Hevajra, not of Śiva.  The line རྗེས་མཐུན་སྔོན་པ is obscure; it is taken as referring to the ancillary (literally "concordant") pledges. The verse beginning "In both cases, whether it was received or not" is terse, and its sense (that the eye-opening proceeds whatever the outcome of the flower-casting and the descent) is a probable interpretation. "The fifth of the deathless substances" renders མི་འཆི་ལྔ་པ literally; it refers to one of the five nectars.  Some evident copyists' slips are translated by sense: སྟོང་བྱེད ("empty") in the Kapilavastu verse as བསྟོད་བྱེད ("praise"); བདུད་ཀྱི་བདེ ("Māra's bliss") as བདུད་ཀྱི་སྡེ ("Māra's hosts"); and ལྟ་མོ as ལྷ་མོ ("the goddess"). ཆེ་གེ་མོ is the standard placeholder "so-and-so" for the initiate's name. The phrase རང་གི་རིག་མ་སྐྱེས་བུའི་ཆུས ("with the water of one's own consort and of the male") is terse, and its precise ritual reference is uncertain.  Some terse lines are uncertain. ཉེས་པས་ཉེས་པ is rendered "hatred enjoying hatred," as the parallel with delusion and passion requires. ཉ་ཕྱིས་ལ་སོགས་ཞེན་པ་སྤངས is taken to mean giving up reliance on a mother-of-pearl vessel and the like, so that the substance is taken directly with the tongue. "Twofold in the mind" (སེམས་གཉིས) probably refers to the five faculties and the five powers together forming the five walls. The meaning of "the flowing family" (འཛག་པའི་རིགས) introducing the empowerment verse is unclear.  The opening verses and the dialogue that follows correspond to the wisdom-empowerment passages of the Hevajra Tantra. "Delivering" renders bsgral ba in its sense of rescuing (Skt. trāṇa), not the wrathful sense. In "why should I not be able?" the text reads mi thig, taken as a slip for mi thub. In "it appears like an illusory form" the text reads sdang, taken as a slip for snang. "The illuminator" (gsal byed / snang byed) is understood as the sun disk. The line "Here there is constant vanquishing" is terse and its exact sense uncertain.  The deities' quarters are named after their guardians: Indra (east), Yama (south), Varuṇa (west), Kubera (north), Īśāna (northeast), Agni (southeast), Nairṛti (southwest) and the wind (northwest). In Pukkasī's description, "sapphire blue and yellow on the right" follows the Tibetan, but the hand emblem on the right seems to be missing. "What is ripened by water" (chu smin) among the offerings, "you who bear the lordly fragrance" in Caṇḍālī's verse, and "he gathers the illnesses" for the right hands are literal renderings of obscure phrases. In the sequence of eight faces, four legs and sixteen arms, these are the eight liberations, the four means of attraction and the sixteen emptinesses.  "In the place of the egg-born" renders sgong skyes literally; it seems to name a site in the body, but which one is unclear. The lines "Then, on the sun above, the sun itself; its rays as the tip of a hair" are compressed, and their sense is uncertain. In the inner-elements verses, "bliss therefore is not reality itself" follows the Tibetan de nyid min. Two items in the lists have not been identified: "barkata" among the five barks (perhaps the parkaṭī fig) and "lagu and drantika" among the five fragrances, so they are kept as spelled. In "cleanse the stain of aversion" the text reads spyod, taken as a slip for sbyong. In "for the five products of the cow" the text reads pha'i, taken as a slip for ba'i. "Cow's water" stands for the cow's urine of the five cow products.  "By the one who benefits both" renders phan pa gnyis kyis literally; given the deity names in the lines that follow, it may stand for a deity presiding over pacifying, but it has not been identified. "The half-border" (phyed ma) for the colors of the hearth is uncertain. "Sacrificial wood" reads mchod sbyin zhing as mchod sbyin shing. In "the vajra expanse" verse, rdo rje dbyings may be meant as the name Vajradhātu. "Plakṣa" takes ba la kṣa as the Indian fig of that name. The line "one sets a hearth smaller than the hearth" is obscure in the Tibetan.
