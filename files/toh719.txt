The Dhāraṇī Called That Which Cultivates Wisdom (Prajñāvardhanī nāma dhāraṇī)

Toh 719 in the Kangyur is a short dhāraṇī text known in English as "That Which Cultivates Wisdom," in Sanskrit as the Prajñāvardhanī nāma dhāraṇī, and in Tibetan as the Shes rab skyed pa zhes bya ba'i gzungs. It belongs to the dhāraṇī class of canonical literature, presenting a brief but potent mantra accompanied by a simple ritual instruction: the mantra is to be recited three times over warm water, which is then drunk, with the promise that within seven days the practitioner will gain the ability to retain whatever they have heard. The text is closely associated with the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara, to whom homage is paid at the outset, and its single mantra — Galate galate manite svāhā — forms the heart of the entire work. It was translated into Tibetan by the Indian master Jñānagarbha together with the Tibetan monk-translator Nāgadbang.

In the Indian language: Prajñāvardhanī nāma dhāraṇī. In the Tibetan language: The dhāraṇī called "That Which Cultivates Wisdom."

Homage to the Three Jewels. Homage to the noble Avalokiteśvara, bodhisattva, great being.

Thus: Galate galate manite svāhā.

The ritual application of this knowledge mantra is as follows. Recite it three times over warm water and drink it, and within seven days one will come to retain what one has heard.

The dhāraṇī called "That Which Cultivates Wisdom" is complete.
