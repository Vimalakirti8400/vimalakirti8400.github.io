The Heart of Secrets: The Definitive Great Lama of Suchness (Guhyagarbhatattvaviniścaya)

This is a tantra in the Kangyur, Toh 837, belonging to the Nyingma-oriented collection of Mahāyoga tantras preserved in the Kangyur's tantra section. The surviving title lines point to a work known as "Dispelling Ignorance," described as a great magical display (mahāmāyā) of the Blessed One, and identified in the Tibetan colophon as the heart of secrets and the certainty of reality (tattva), a great teacher or lama of primordial wisdom. A Sanskrit title cannot be determined from the lines given, so none is offered here. The text is presented as the speech of the Blessed One, the glorious Samantabhadra, who is shown dwelling in a secret abode and revealing the nature of the lama as the embodiment of the body, speech, mind, qualities, and activities of all the tathāgatas. Its central claim is that all who accomplish this secret practice are Samantabhadra himself, so that it concerns the recognition of the teacher and of enlightened reality as the essence of the path.

All those who accomplish this secret practice are Samantabhadra himself. Thus ends the great magical display called "Dispelling Ignorance" of the Blessed One.

In Tibetan: this is the great lama, the very heart of secrets, the certainty of reality (tattva). Homage to the Blessed One, glorious Samantabhadra, the great lama of primordial wisdom.

At one time this was spoken. The great lama of magical display is the lama of all the buddhas, the supreme secret bliss of all yogis. Dwelling in the secret abode, the Heart-Essence, the lama, spoke thus.

The Tathāgata, the perfectly complete Buddha, the Blessed One, the great enjoyment of the secret lama, whose very nature is the vajra of body, speech, mind, qualities, and activities of all the tathāgatas of the ten directions and the four times. Everything without exception, whether remaining or not remaining, has the nature of being neither separate nor different, inseparable. In the dharmadhātu of the utterly secret great lama, in Akaniṣṭha, the abode without edge or center, there is a wheel of boundless primordial wisdom, luminous. In it stands a palace blazing with precious wisdom, boundless in all ten directions like the expanse of space. Because it is expanded by countless great qualities of the lama, it is square in form. It is adorned with four projecting balconies of supreme precious wisdom, the lama. At its peaks, all the maṇḍalas of the buddhas of the ten directions and four times, not separate and not different, are encircled entirely by one single essence, the wisdom lama. It is the inconceivable expanse of the wisdom lama, and it has distinctions and varieties such as different colors. It is adorned with immeasurable garlands of precious wisdom, the lama, with hanging tassels and with fringed ornaments. Forms, sounds, smells, tastes, and tactile sensations arise by themselves, clear and unobstructed. It is covered with special luminous ornaments, the lama. It has gateways entered through the four immeasurables, and is furnished with the staircases of the eight liberations. There is neither outside nor inside, for everything has become the interior of the great lama. In the pervasive and expansive palace of the great lama, the most secret, there are thrones of the five perfections: the throne of the lion, which is fearlessness; the throne of the elephant, which is power; the throne of the horse, which is miraculous power; the throne of the peacock, which is mastery; and the throne of the garuḍa soaring through space, which is unobstructedness. Upon these are stacked the sun and moon discs, which are naturally luminous, and the lotus seat of precious jewels, which is unstained.

Because from the beginning the secret is the lama,
the Mother's palace blazes in the ten directions.
Because from the beginning there are the perfections,
the throne's legs are firm and unobstructed.
Because by nature it is luminous from the beginning,
the seat of stacked sun and moon blazes.
Because it is stainless from the very first,
the lotus seat is utterly pure.
Because it fulfills wishes like a wish-granting jewel,
the seat is a treasure of wish-fulfilling jewels.
Because it is spontaneously complete without accumulation,
it is the ornament of the great, varied maṇḍala.

Upon the seat of great qualities, with no front or back to the body, the lama's faces appear everywhere, vivid and clear, endowed with the major and minor marks, and everywhere there appear the inconceivable varied body, speech, mind, qualities, and activities. He sits in the full lotus posture of the ascetic discipline, with the two feet of skillful means and wisdom even. His hands are the six wisdoms, with the mudrā of blazing precious wisdom. He has three heads, inconceivable as body, speech, and mind.

He is the Blessed One, the Tathāgata, the king of consciousness, the lama, together with the supreme consort, the lama of the expanse of solidity. The Tathāgata, the king of form, the lama, together with the supreme consort, the lama of the expanse of appearance. The Tathāgata, the king of feeling, the lama, together with the supreme consort, the lama of the expanse of softness.

The Tathāgata, the king of perception, the lama, together with the supreme consort, the lama of the expanse of warmth. The Tathāgata, the king of formations, the lama, together with the supreme consort, the lama of the expanse of motion. All these kings of wisdom, beginning with these, radiate as the lama in the colors of dark blue, white, yellow, red, and green. Since they are joined without duality, the expanse of phenomena is pervaded on every side, without limit, by the conduct of the great lama. It is like a lump of sesame or a casket of sweetness: they dwell pervaded everywhere by great bliss.

Then the great awakened one, Vajra Sight-Power, the lama, together with the consort, the lama of what is to be seen. The great awakened one, Vajra Hearing-Power, the lama, together with the consort, the lama of what is to be heard. The great awakened one, Vajra Smell-Power, the lama, together with the consort, the lama of what is to be smelled. The great awakened one, Vajra Taste-Power, the lama, together with the consort, the lama of what is to be tasted. The great awakened one, Vajra Knowing-of-Union, the lama, together with the consort, the lama of the lamp. The great awakened one, Vajra Knowing-of-Severing, the lama, together with the consort, the lama of the fierce. The great awakened one, Vajra Knowing-of-Entering, the lama, together with the consort, the lama who sends down rain. The great awakened one, Vajra Gathering, the lama, together with the consort, the lama of increase.

The great subduer, Vajra Right Touch, the lama, together with the consort, the lama of non-permanence. The great subduer, Vajra Left Touch, the lama, together with the consort, the lama of non-annihilation. The great subduer, Vajra Secret Touch, the lama, together with the consort, the lama of non-self. The great subduer, Vajra Gathered Touch, the lama, together with the consort, the lama of non-characteristic. Such a gathering cannot be spoken of in words. Without duality, they dwell in the lama, the expanse of the delight of secrecy.

Then the Tathāgata, the gathering of consorts, and the nondual maṇḍala, the lama himself, the secret lama: from his body, speech, mind, qualities, and activity, the vajra arose.

Emaho! The expanse of suchness itself, in the state of the lama, the maṇḍala of sovereign power.
That very state is perfectly pure, the secret expanse of supreme bliss.
That very expanse is the treasure of precious supreme bliss in equal union,
the lama of the secret nectar, holder of the inexhaustible treasure.
The unsurpassed awakening, the drop, the maṇḍala of the name, appears.
Its own appearing, the reflection of meditative absorption, appears perfectly pure:
body, speech, mind, qualities, and activity, unobstructed, wish-fulfilling, possessing inexhaustible jewels,
the wheel of ornaments, the lama, the very abode of the supreme vajra.
This is the definitive meaning of the secret vajra lama.

This is the first chapter, the chapter of the introduction, on the lama who is the definitive reality of the essence of the secret, the lama of the secret bliss of expanse and wisdom.

Then the Blessed One, the lama who attends to all, Vajra Samantabhadra, in the manner of all without remainder, entered through the vajra the lama of the consort, the one who acts, Samantabhadrī. He uttered these words of the secret lama.

Oṃ! The expanse of the nature of phenomena, the expanse of action,
Samantabhadrī, the mother of bliss,
the unmoving natural maṇḍala,
is myself, and is my own domain.
The various mudrās shine forth, each distinct.
In the western direction, in the supreme abode of Akaniṣṭha,
the unattached lotus seat is spread out,
the place where the victors of the three times dwell.
The four doors of liberation
are perfectly joined with the four mudrās.
To remove the bad fortune of those without power,
the inner is firm and the outer is layered, endowed with iron.
The secret Dharma of all the victors
is difficult for anyone to fathom.
Samantabhadra himself has seen it.

Hoḥ. Then the queen, Samantabhadrī, the one who acts, spoke these words of the secret guru on purpose.

Oṃ. Chief of all that acts as the very nature of mind,
Samantabhadra, the vajra mind,
the nature of the eight wisdoms,
from the eastern direction, Abhirati, the realm of inconceivable splendor,
you draw forth a treasure like a sapphire.
With the key of the Secret Assembly's magical display
you open the door of complete liberation,
and release what lies within supreme awakening.
You abide upon the unattached lotus seat.
For the benefit of beings you give rise to the awakening mind,
and gather the five realms of beings and the four modes of birth under your power.
From body, speech, and mind you bestow empowerment.
So she declared.

Then the queen, Samantabhadrī, the one who acts, made this offering to the secret guru on purpose.

Oṃ. This precious lotus maṇḍala
I offer with the radiance of a smile.
It is the key of the unsurpassed magical display.
Having delighted the supreme vajra of joy,
I pray that you let fall a great rain of nectar.
So she declared on purpose.

Then again, the vajra Samantabhadra, the one who makes the mind, spoke these words of the secret guru on purpose, in order to satisfy the Jambudvīpa of vessel and contents with the rain of the guru's nectar.

Oṃ. This supreme delight of the vajra guru,
having delighted him, that the rain of nectar may fall,
I delight him in the place of smiling and gathering.
This objectless bliss and rapture,
Samantabhadrī gathers into the basic space.
So she declared on purpose.

From the state of nonduality, the whole range of activity of karma arose as the secret maṇḍala of the guru, extraordinarily adorned, with as many particles as there are atoms, and performed great magical displays of illusion beyond all thought. Some dwelt in the maṇḍala of the secret guru and blessed all their limbs as vajras. Some, in order to tame the six classes of beings, dwelt as the six great sages, as follows.

In the realm of the gods, Indra, the protector.
Among the asuras, Thagzang Ri.
Among humans, Śākyamuni, tamer of Māra.
In the realm of hungry ghosts, Mouth-Aflame, the blissful.
Among animals, the one who speaks as a lion.
In the hells, Yama, the protector.
They show their bodies and manner of dress,
and some enter the womb and are born as bodies.
Some guard the discipline and go forth.
Some take full ordination and practice austerities.
Some take vows and liberate beings.
Some urge beings toward the sixteen teachings.
Some blaze in fire and thunder in water.
Some blaze as sound in the sky.
Some are firm as a vajra rock.
Some nourish and destroy, defeat and conquer.
Some bind and release, some summon and release.
Some tame the māras and purify the lower realms.
Some gather clouds of maṇḍala offerings.
All of them turn the wheel of Dharma.

The way they constantly turn the wheel of Dharma is as follows.

The first vehicle protects with the sixteen.
The second guards the view and conduct of the four.
The third teaches definitively the twelve.
The fourth trains in the view and conduct of the two.
If the fourth vehicle is gathered in brief,
it is led into the basic space of the unsurpassed vehicle.
For the definitive emergence through the four vehicles,
one vehicle abides as the fruit.
The arising maṇḍala is a great magical display.
Because it is connected with the many, it has the meaning of a net.
Because it is uncommon, it has the meaning of the secret.
The essence of the vehicles is the basic space of the heart essence.
So she declared on purpose.

The world of vessel and contents was transformed into the secret abode of the maṇḍala of the Victors. All the Tathāgatas of the ten directions and four times were pleased by the gaze of the secret guru's samādhi. Then, the whole world of vessel and contents, without remainder, became fully awakened in the maṇḍala of the secret guru, and uttered this solemn utterance.

Emaho. The limbs of the vajra aggregates
are known as the five perfect buddhas.
The sense sources and elements, all the many,
are themselves the maṇḍala of the bodhisattvas.

The earth-water one, and Māmakī, the one with the mirror, the white-robed one, and Tārā, the sovereign lady of the expanse of space: the three realms of existence are buddha fields from the very beginning. All phenomena, without remainder, are none other than buddhahood itself. A dharma other than buddhahood itself cannot be found even by a buddha. Through this declaration, all the Tathāgatas were pleased.

Then all the assemblies of the Tathāgata consorts spoke this solemn utterance.

Emaho. The realms of the ten directions are secluded from the very beginning. The three existences are themselves a pure field. The five degenerations are themselves an abode endowed with bliss. The five aggregates are themselves manifest buddhahood. Since all is the supreme essence, the Victors do not seek the Dharma elsewhere. Even if one sought a dharma called "other than this very nature," the Victors would not find it.

Through this declaration, the Tathāgata himself knew that all phenomena are buddhas from the very beginning.

Then, from the state in which expanse and wisdom are not two, this solemn utterance was spoken.

Emaho. The Dharma is secret from the very beginning. The various appearances are secret in their nature. The supreme secret, the essence, is unsurpassed. All is definitively ascertained as the secret expanse. From the supreme essence of the guru, buddhas and sentient beings are not two.

Through this declaration, he knew that everything is definitively the expanse.

Even so, in order to arouse great compassion and the vast wisdom of a buddha for the inconceivable classes of beings who are ripened through the conceptual thinking of beings, this word of the vajra guru was solemnly uttered.

Emaho. From the essence of the Sugata, through their own conceptual thought and karma, they emanate manifold bodies, enjoyments, abodes, sufferings, and the rest. They cling to each as "self" and "mine." Yet no one has bound them; there is no binding, and nothing that could be bound. Through conceptual clinging to a self, one forcibly ties knots in the sky. The buddha-dharma, spontaneously present from the beginning, without bondage and without liberation, is manifold in its play, displayed for the sake of teaching. Yet though it displays, the expanse neither increases nor diminishes.

Even so, for those who have not realized it so, in order to arouse the essential nature of mind in the conventional aggregates, in the maṇḍala of the guru who is the awakening mind, this vajra secret word was solemnly uttered.

The very nature of mind, empty and secluded, has no family, color, or shape. Though nothing at all, it appears as anything. Unmoving, it brings forth various miracles. The manifold maṇḍalas of the Victors too are the great magical display of mind. Not apprehended as one or many, it accomplishes everything through the six superknowledges. Through the great samādhi of the guru, the making of the great maṇḍala is taught. Gathered by the five causes and four conditions, one meditates on the great and small maṇḍalas of the Victors. One performs the four stages properly. All the ornaments and modes of the mudrās are made. Within a sesame seed, Mount Meru is generated. The maṇḍala of a thousand buddhas, and the mudrās of the two, the display and the cast-down, are adorned and arrayed with the minor marks and ornaments, just as with Meru in the sesame seed. Definitively, one meditates the forty-five. Expanse and wisdom, the display and the cast-down, are the doers of all maṇḍalas.

Perception is Amitābha; in the object, she is the white-robed one. Hoḥ. Formations are Amoghasiddhi; in the object, she is Samaya Tārā. Hoḥ. Consciousness is Vajrasattva; in the object, she is Locanā, the broad-eyed one. Hoḥ. In the eye is Kṣitigarbha; in the object, she is the white one of great elegance. Hoḥ. In the nose is Ākāśagarbha; in the object, she is the one with the garland of flowers. Hoḥ. In the tongue is Avalokiteśvara; in the object, she is the one with the melody of Dharma. Hoḥ. In the ear is Vajrapāṇi; in the object, she is the dancer of action.

the one who clears away all obscurations in the object, she is the one who rains down flowers. Hoḥ. The all-knowing is Samantabhadra; in the object, she is the lamp of clarity. Hoḥ. The cutting consciousness is the very self of Maitreya; in the object, she is the one who sends forth clouds of the fierce. Hoḥ. The entering consciousness is Mañjuśrī, the youthful; in the object, she is the one who rains down sandalwood. The two on the right are the wrathful ones of great power; in the object, they are the terrifying-faced women. Hoḥ. The two on the left are the executioners of Yama, the Lord of Death; in the object, they are those who send forth swords and strike terror. Hoḥ. The secret place is the Horse-Necked hero, Hayagrīva; in the object, he is the mistress of the charnel ground. Hoḥ. At the navel is the swirling nectar; in the object, she is the fierce Caṇḍālī, inspiring dread. Hoḥ.

In the six continua, in the six places of cyclic existence, are the six Sages, the great seals. The body of all, the speech of all, and likewise the mind seal of all, abide thus in body, speech, and mind. Hoḥ.

The nature of mind is the net of magical illusion. From the great miraculous display of illusion arise the forty-five maṇḍalas of the net. From the certainty of the secret maṇḍala, the essence maṇḍala is the guru.

By this utterance, all sentient beings became fully and completely awakened buddhas, and in joy, inseparable from all the Tathāgatas, they dwelt in the expanse of equality.

This is the second chapter, "The Nature of Mind Accomplishes Whatever Appears," from the Guru of the Certainty of Reality, the Essence of the Secret, in the state where expanse and wisdom are not two.

Then all the Tathāgatas, whose nature is both one and many yet inseparable, desiring to draw forth this guru, the great maṇḍala of their own being, into all the ten directions of the six worlds, spoke this inspired utterance.

Oṃ. Through the play of the wisdom guru, if one envisions the four directions and the center as five, the maṇḍala, immeasurable and spontaneously accomplished, is the realm in which the yogin who realizes the Great Perfection engages the great maṇḍala of the origin of all. The wheel has four spokes with a rim, adorned with four cut corners, a vajra enclosure. It is square on every side with doors and encircling borders, like a mass of clouds of manifold play, adorned with forty-two maṇḍalas. Upon the great thrones of lion, elephant, horse, and garuda, in the sky, upon a seat of sun, moon, and precious lotus, they appear in the manner of the fully accomplished ones and the bodhisattvas. The king of the wheel with his queen, and from the right, the seeing and the hearing, the absorption, the experiencing, are accompanied by their company of consorts. On the cut corners dwell the cutting one, the illuminating one, and the other agents, the engaging ones, together with their companies of consorts. In the intermediate spaces dwell the six Sages, in the form of the seals that purify the six continua. Likewise the great drops, those who act before and behind, are shown to abide as the doers of action. At the four doorways dwell the subduers, together with their companies of consorts.

They bear the emblems of the vajra, the wheel, the jewel, the lotus, the sword, the blazing gem, the utpala, the kalpa-tree, and so forth, holding implements of beauty and delight. Their colors are manifold: dark blue, brilliant white, yellow, green, and others, with supple, slender, graceful forms, youthful in bearing, radiant, dense and luminous, blazing with splendor. A great host of rays of light streams forth, garlands of blazing fire densely massed, pervading without center or edge. The maṇḍala, immeasurable and spontaneously accomplished. Hoḥ.

By this utterance, throughout all the boundless, limitless ten directions of the six worlds, the great guru maṇḍala, beyond expression, became vividly present as many as the dust motes of the realms.

Then the Tathāgatas, inseparable from their companies of consorts, drew forth this secret guru of those very maṇḍalas, these qualities of body, speech, and mind and these enlightened activities.

Wisdom, self-aware, the maṇḍala of the guru, beyond thought.
The expanse of the secret, pervading all, equal and unequal.
The wisdom luminous in all things, the maṇḍala free of elaboration, the state of the guru.

Then the King of Wisdom became inseparable from the consort, the expanse of the guru. Mounting the summit of the intent called "the Great Bliss, the Awakening Mind Gradually Clarified," and wishing that the aspects of absorption be needed throughout the ten directions and the four times, he drew forth these from the expanse of the guru, the body, speech, and mind.

First, the guru's prophetic declaration:

The awakening mind that cannot be indicated,
the luminous essence of wisdom,
and the three kinds of meditation are completed at one time.
Since the bindu of the awakening mind
is spontaneously complete, unobstructed,
that is the meaning of the great bindu.
That is the expanse of suchness.
That is the all-appearing,
and it possesses the stages of the cause. Hoḥ.
These are the words of the secret guru.

From the definitive meaning of the secret guru,
the secret maṇḍala of speech is proclaimed.

By this utterance, the maṇḍala of speech, the great guru, resounded throughout all the ten directions of the six worlds; it resounded supremely; it resounded everywhere. Through this resounding, the maṇḍala of all the worlds, vessel and contents, became pervaded by the great guru.

Then, once more, the King of Wisdom, playing in the inseparability of the two, the fierce and the gentle, dissolved into the guru. Then this, called "the withdrawal of the blessing," together with that same King of Wisdom, arose from the vajra of body, speech, and mind.

Oṃ. From the great equality of the dharmadhātu, pure and luminous wisdom arises. By the wisdom of the dharmadhātu, may I gain mastery over the unborn Dharma. Oṃ mahā guhya mahā śūnyatā dharma jñāna svabhāva ātmako 'ham.

Hūṃ. Pure and luminous in nature, the flawless mind itself is a precious jewel. By the mirror-like wisdom, may I gain mastery over the three realms of existence. Oṃ mahā guhya mahā ādarśa jñāna vajra svabhāva ātmako 'ham.

Svāṃ. The king who fulfills the hopes of the three realms, the supreme treasure of wish-fulfilling jewels. By the wisdom of equality, may all beings have their hopes fulfilled by a king. Oṃ mahā guhya mahā samanta āveśa jñāna vajra svabhāva ātmako 'ham.

Aṃ. Free of the fault of error, without stain, the accomplishment that is without arising or cessation. By the wisdom of discernment, may the faults of the three realms be dispelled. Oṃ mahā guhya mahā pratyaveśa jñāna vajra svabhāva ātmako 'ham.

Hā. The maṇḍala of the dharmadhātu is brought to completion, and from the diverse kinds of accomplished activity, by the wisdom of all-accomplishing action, may beings be liberated into the dharmadhātu. Oṃ mahā guhya mahā kṛta anuṣṭhāna jñāna vajra svabhāva ātmako 'ham.

Ani. Both emptiness and non-emptiness do not abide even in the middle. By the wisdom of great emptiness, may I gain mastery over the unchanging expanse. Oṃ mahā guhya mahā āveśa śūnyatā jñāna vajra svabhāva ātmako 'ham.

From the expanse of non-duality, on the upper rim of the spokes of the wheel in the east, upon a throne with sun and moon, there was Mahādeva, and upon the desire-realm gods, laid out and subdued, was the very mass of his own form aggregate, the Blessed One Buddha Heruka. His body was dark yellow, with three heads, six arms, and four feet, adorned with the ornaments of the charnel ground. In his hands he held many kinds of weapons. The Sugata dwelt in a great embrace with the wrathful queen.

On the upper rim of the spokes of the wheel in the south, upon a throne with sun and moon, upon a seat where Yama was laid out and crushed, there arose from his own feeling aggregate the Blessed One Ratna Heruka, his body dark blue, with three heads, six arms, and four feet, holding many kinds of weapons and adorned with the ornaments of the charnel ground.

He dwelt in the manner of a great embrace with the wrathful queen. On the upper rim of the spokes of the wheel in the west, upon a throne with sun and moon, upon a seat where the Lord of Waters (Varuṇa) and the black wheel-wielder Viṣṇu were laid out and crushed, there arose from his own perception aggregate the Blessed One Padma Heruka, his body dark red, with three heads, six arms, and four feet, holding many kinds of weapons and adorned with the ornaments of the charnel ground. He dwelt in union with the wrathful queen Padma.

On the upper rim of the spokes of the wheel in the north, upon a throne with sun and moon, upon a seat where the yakṣa Kubera was laid out and crushed, there arose from his own formations aggregate the Blessed One Karma Heruka, his body dark green, with three heads, six arms, and four feet in a wide, striding stance, holding many kinds of weapons and adorned with the ornaments of the charnel ground. He dwelt in the manner of a great embrace with the wrathful queen Supreme Action.

Then the great arrogant ones and the rest, with malicious minds, displayed many kinds of fierce miraculous powers. With one voice, in a fierce roar, they cried out harsh words: "Look, look! Lord of compassion, why do you act like this?" And they bellowed most savagely.

Then the Blessed One, with great delight, became the very radiance of the Wisdom King, a body of immense majesty. With great compassion, by a means of taming, he released these great cries, the roaring tones of the supreme guru, the secret mantra of the net of illusion: Hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ. Ha ha ha. Khāhi khāhi khāhi.

By this utterance, he drew out the hearts and all the sense faculties of those malevolent hosts, the Great Lord and the others. He pulled out all their entrails, severed and cut all their limbs, ate their flesh, drank their blood, and made ornaments of all their bones.

Then that Great Terrifier himself uttered: Hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ, bhyo e a ra li jā jā. By this, he gathered the realms of the worlds of the ten directions, together with space, into the size of a single mustard seed.

Then he gathered in all the malevolent spirits, the lord of lords of them all, and the consorts of the great arrogant ones and the rest, the female spirits, queens of the queens of them all. He gathered the great ogresses, Mi Srun Mo, Brahmī, Krodhā, Īśvarī, Viṣṇuvī, Kumārī, the Red One, Amṛtā, Śāntā, the Great Pestilence Woman, the Ogress, the Eater, the Joyful One, the Corpse Blood-Drinker, the Solitary Wanderer, the Mind-Stealer, the Accomplished One, the Wind Woman, the Slayer, the Fire Woman, the Sow, the Aging One, the Great-Nosed One, the Water Goddess, the Great Black One, the Great Red-Yellow Goat-Headed One, the Great Blue-Black Jar-Nosed One, the Great Blue-Black Belly-Holder, and the rest, as well as servants, attendants, and attendants' attendants. Even the dust of the whole field of their retinue he gathered in.

Then once more the Wisdom King, the Great Delighted One himself, absorbed himself in the meditative absorption called Cloud Arising, the great secret of the delighted guru, and from his body, speech, and mind, the guru, he released these utterances: Oṃ āḥ hūṃ. Vajra Padma praveśa padma hūṃ a la la ho.

By this utterance, the minds of all the malevolent spirits were stirred with intense desire. Their secrets were laid open, and as iron filings cling to a magnet, eight, beginning with Mi Srun Mo, clung to the body of the Blessed One, the Wisdom King. Five, beginning with Brahmī, clung to the body of the Blessed One, the Great Yellow-Black One. Five, beginning with Amṛtā, clung to the body of the Blessed One, the Great Blue-Black One. Five, beginning with the Blood-Intoxicated One, clung to the body of the Blessed One, the Great Red-Black One.

Five, beginning with Me Mo, clung to the body of the Blessed One, the Great Green-Black One.

Then, filled with great delight, those deities uttered hūṃ, and the gathering of lotuses was drawn together and became utterly crushed. Then, delighted, they uttered ha ha, and from the eight consciousnesses of their own awareness there came forth eight, beginning with the White One, each wondrously arrayed with his own implements. Having come forth, they took their places on seats outside the blazing wheel, circling from the eastern direction, in wrathful form, with a compassionate yet terrifying bearing.

Then, delighted, they uttered he, and from the eight objects of their own awareness there came forth eight, beginning with the Great Lion-Faced One, each wondrously arrayed with his own implements. They too took their places on seats in the space within the blazing wheel, with a compassionate yet terrifying bearing and a fiercely valiant demeanor.

Then, delighted, they uttered phaṭ, proclaiming it to the buddha-fields of the ten directions without exception. Thereupon the six Great Sages, the six Blessed Ones, who accomplish the aims of all beings in the buddha-fields of the ten directions without remainder, worked for the benefit of beings and tamed the six classes of beings. Moreover, from the expanse of the dharmadhātu the mind took delight and became eight, beginning with the one who sends forth, the one with the rays of the sun, and the one with the noose. In the third blazing ring of space, upon seats that bring dryness and moisture to the fields, they took their places with their own implements and a fiercely valiant demeanor, circling from the eastern direction.

Then, delighted, they uttered phaṭ, and from the four inversions of their own awareness, four, beginning with Vajra Sriṅgdrogma, each wondrously arrayed with her own implements, took their places at the four gates of the great blazing maṇḍala. They stood upon seats of compassionate yet terrifying bearing, in fiercely valiant form, in the manner of skillful means and wisdom.

Then, from the cloud of great delight, forty-two mudrās of the buddhas' play uttered phaṭ at one time. All became wrathful, and in an instant all the female spirits were sent off, each to her own abode.

Then the Blessed One, the Great Blood-Drinker himself, through great compassion entered at once into the contemplation called Arising of Nectar, and drew forth these from the vajra of body, speech, and mind. When he uttered oṃ vajra amṛta mahākrodhi aṃ aṃ, Maheśvara and all the others came forth from the underside of the Blessed One, the Great Blood-Drinker. He poured the mire-water over them, and they regained their memory.

Then all those great Blood-Drinkers were seen performing great and marvelous miracles. At that time, all the great arrogant ones dwelling in the six worlds were seen to be subdued at once. Utterly cowed and trembling, they spoke with one voice:

We come as subjects, we come as subjects.
If we do not serve as subjects with diligence,
may our heads and bodies split into a hundred pieces,
may our hearts burst and be hacked apart,
may we rot, burn, and be destroyed,
may we fall into hell and cry out.

Thus each took an oath of becoming subjects. Then all of them were set upon the seats of the maṇḍala. And again they all spoke with one voice, offering to the maṇḍala their own wives, daughters, mothers, and sisters:

Our consorts, our mothers,
our sisters, and our daughters,
we beg you to accept in the great maṇḍala.
O heroes, O divine lords, we beg you to accept them.
Who among all our retinues
could merely hold the name of this great blazing maṇḍala?
Like a blazing wish-fulfilling jewel,
it fulfills every need.

Thus they swore. Then their mothers, sisters, wives, and daughters came in an instant, and the accomplishment of karmic activity was bestowed. Then the Great Blood-Drinker of Wisdom gave them names, placed a vajra in their hands, and conferred empowerment upon them as vajra servants and messengers.

Then, in order to make the meaning of the great blazing maṇḍala clear and radiant to the Tathāgatas of the ten directions and the four times, the Queen of Wisdom became nondual with her own secret consort, the Queen of Secrets, and these were drawn forth from the body, speech, and mind of the vajra guru.

Emaho! The secret Dharma, primordially pure,
arises from the nonduality of space and the expanse.
In space the maṇḍala of the guru appears.
Set up a throne, a sun-disc seat,
and spread the assembly of deities as its cushion.

Hūṃ! In the center of the blazing wheel,
a throne is arranged, a sun-disc, subduing demons and spirits.
Upon it, visualize this:
a body arisen from awakening mind,
the glorious great Supreme Heruka,
with nine heads and eighteen arms,
and twenty-seven blazing eyes.
Upon the heads he wears the crowns of the five families.
His feet, too, are set in eight postures.
Dark blue, with the wings of the vajra garuḍa,
he covers the world-realms entirely.
Fearsome black serpents
and the ornaments of the charnel ground adorn him.

The three heads at the front:
right white, left red, the center dark blue.
These three heads of complete liberation
are the faces that overturn the sorrow of the three realms.
The three heads in the middle, wrathful:
right blue, left green, the center dark blue.
These are the heads of means, wisdom, and mind,
the faces that perfect the three existences within the expanse.
The three heads at the edge, wrathful:
right dark blue, left dark blue, the center dark blue.
These are the heads of scriptural transmission and pith instruction,
fulfilling the hopes of the three realms,
the unchanging dark blue faces
whose fangs cut the root of birth and death.
From the blessing of the nine heads' play,
the three on the right emit a thousand lightning flashes,
the three on the left roar with a thousand thunders,
and the three above rain down great golden vajras.

The nine eyes gaze as follows.
The first set gaze unchangingly
into the expanse of Dharma, of Samantabhadra
and Samantabhadrī.
The middle set gaze with the wisdom of complete liberation,
looking upon all beings of existence
with eyes of loving-kindness, protection, and equanimity.
The outer set: the three on the right are eyes wrathful with aversion,
whose gaze liberates māras and non-Buddhists;
the three on the left are eyes of loving-kindness, protection, and equanimity,
whose gaze establishes beings in boundless bliss;
the three above are eyes playing with wisdom,
whose gaze empowers beings in their abodes.

The nine heads bear nine crowns of the five families,
five times nine, forty-five in all,
adorned with forty-five buddhas.
With the wisdom of great emptiness
he carries them as the sign of great pervasive expanse.
With eighteen hands at play
he joins the eighteen emptinesses to the expanse.
His eight feet crush pride and arrogance.
Endowed with means and wisdom,
he spreads the dark blue vajra wings.
In the eighteen hands he holds:
at right, the vajra, the lung-heart, the small vessel;
at left, the bhaṇḍha, the great vessel, the large vessel;
the inner noose and the sun, the great garuḍa at right;
the great vessel and the moon, the owl at left;
fire and ocean, the gau at right;
wind and jewel, Mount Meru at left.
The eighteen qualities are perfected in the expanse.

The first, the great vajra conch,
gathers the three realms under his power
and subdues all māras and non-Buddhists.
The second, the great vessel and lung-heart,
liberates all beings of existence
into the expanse of the samaya.
The third, the small vessel and pill,
is the sign of fully liberating the three poisons.
The fourth, the great vessel and inner noose,
draws beings of the five lineages into the expanse.
The fifth holds sun and moon together,
unmoved from awakening mind.
The sixth, the garuḍa and the owl,
is the sign of subjugating all ḍākinīs.
The seventh, a mass of fire scattered by wind,
reduces the habitual tendencies of existence to ash.
The eighth, Mount Meru and the oceans,
set all things without exception in motion.

This is the attainment of mastery over the five elements. The ninth, the precious vessel, is the sign that accomplishments and emanations will arise. From the two nines joined as a pair, the eighteen thousand are completed in the expanse.

The adornment of the charnel ground, the garland of blazing dry skulls, is the pacified, one-sided sorrow, repeated: the attainment of the perfectly complete body. This garland of blazing fresh skulls, having gathered the three realms under its power, is the attainment of mastery over the emanation body. The garland of half-skull bowls transforms the five poisons into nectar: the attainment of mastery over the enjoyment body. The garment of the great moist field is the great equality of the dharmadhātu: the attainment of mastery over the truth body. The tiger-skin loincloth, a place of abyss and foundation, is the very nature of the four enlightened activities: the attainment of mastery over the vajra body. The many black snakes hanging down are the sign that all wisdoms will arise: the attainment of mastery over the body of the great sign. The trampling of the eight gods beneath the eight feet is the subjugation of the arrogant.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The passage is a colophon followed by the opening of a tantra. Here bla ma is rendered "lama" throughout, as the text uses it as a quasi-technical, near-synonym for "supreme" or "ultimate" (as in the Great Perfection tantras, where it functions adjectivally); "supreme" would also be defensible. "Heart-Essence" for snying po in the second verse is tentative. The passage breaks off mid-list of the aggregate buddhas and consorts. The "six wisdoms" of the hands is rendered literally.  The lines on the lamas of sight, hearing, smell, and taste, and the vajra names beginning "Vajra Knowing-of-Union," rest on a corrupt or unusual text.
