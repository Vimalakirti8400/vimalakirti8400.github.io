The Purification of Offerings (Dakṣiṇī Pariśodhanī Nāma)

The Purification of Offerings (Sanskrit Dakṣiṇī Pariśodhanī Nāma, Tibetan Yon yongs su sbyong ba, though the Tibetan title line is only partly legible in the source) is a short dhāraṇī text concerned with purifying the gifts and offerings made to the saṅgha and other recipients, so that the giver's merit is made complete and unobstructed. It opens with homage to all buddhas and bodhisattvas, to the Tathāgata King of Light Shining Everywhere, and to Mañjuśrī in his form as a youthful prince, and then presents the mantra itself, whose invocation addresses the one who is free of all support and appearance, victorious and wise. The text promises that whoever recites it will be freed from all illnesses and harmful spirits and will not give an opening to hostile forces, benefits typical of the protective dhāraṇī genre. It is Toh 777 in the Kangyur, where it belongs to the Tantra section, among the many brief dhāraṇīs and ritual protection texts preserved there.

Homage to all buddhas and bodhisattvas. Homage to the Tathāgata, the arhat, the perfectly complete buddha, the King of Light Shining Everywhere. Homage to the bodhisattva, the great being, endowed with great compassion and endowed with intelligence, Mañjuśrī who has become a youthful prince.

Namaḥ Samanta Prabha Rājāya, Tathāgatāya, Arhate Bhyaḥ, Samyaksaṃbuddhāya. Namo Mañjuśriye, Kumārabhūtāya, Bodhisattvāya, Mahāsattvāya, Mahākāruṇikāya. Tadyathā: Oṃ Nirālambe, Nirābhāse, Jaye Jayalabdhe, Mahāmate Dakṣe Dikṣaṇī Pariśodhāya Svāhā.

By reciting this just once, all negative deeds are purified. Even offerings that have been misused or squandered are purified. The benefactor's offerings will increase, and all accumulations will be completed. By reciting this, no obstacles of any kind will arise.
