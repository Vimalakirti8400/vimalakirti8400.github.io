The Dhāraṇī-Mantra Called Healing Wounds

Healing Wounds, known in Tibetan as Rma ’byor bar byed pa zhes bya ba’i gzungs sngags, is a very brief dhāraṇī-mantra, a short incantation recited for a specific practical benefit; no Sanskrit title is given in the source. After an opening homage to the noble Mañjuśrī, it presents a single mantra and explains that reciting it three times and smearing spittle on the wound will heal many kinds of injury, including wounds from a thrown stone or a weapon, severed blood vessels, and even injuries that have cut into the brain. Texts of this kind reflect the practical, healing side of Buddhist ritual practice, in which spoken formulas are used for protection and recovery. The work is catalogued as Toh 1054 in the Kangyur, where it is among the collection of dhāraṇī texts.

The dhāraṇī-mantra called Healing Wounds.

Homage to the noble Mañjuśrī.

oṃ ubasa ubas suma suma sarba nanrbad svāhā hūṃ phaṭ mama śāntiṃ kuru svāhā

If one recites this three times and smears spittle on a wound, it heals injuries from being struck by a stone, wounds from a weapon, severed blood vessels, and injuries that have cut into the brain.

The dhāraṇī-mantra Healing Wounds is complete.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

Here ཆད་པ has its everyday sense of "cut" or "severed", not the doctrinal sense of annihilation. The term རྩ, "vessel" or "channel", is taken here as a blood vessel, since the context is wound healing.
