Homage to the Lotus-Bearer (Avalokiteśvara), who holds the lotus in his hand. Homage to the arising intelligence, who perfectly rouses those sentient beings who have sunk into the dense thicket of manifold sufferings. I shall now set forth the supreme secret mantra of the Tathāgata — the arising of the pinnacle of great compassion — the siddhi of unobstructed and purposeful activity that has come forth from the mouth of the Tathāgata itself, which pacifies the karmic obscurations of the entire maṇḍala of sentient beings and the manifold sufferings of the lower states of existence, which utterly pacifies all inauspiciousness, bad omens, evil dreams, ill portents, the many kinds of disease, and the many kinds of untimely death, and which thoroughly crushes the great thousandfold world-system of negativity. This dhāraṇī, called "The Supreme Compassion of the Blessed One," issued from the uṣṇīṣa of the Blessed One. It is attended by an immeasurable thousandfold retinue of dhāraṇīs. It destroys all misdeeds, karma, and afflictions. It pacifies all inauspiciousness, evil dreams, ill portents, and all forms of untimely death. It grants long life, merit, good form, strength, happiness, confidence, and freedom from illness. Merely by holding, hearing, or uttering the name of this dhāraṇī, the karmic obscurations accumulated over a hundred thousand billion lifetimes will be purified. All afflictions will be burned away. That person will be embraced and upheld by all buddhas and bodhisattvas. They will come to be endowed with the virtues of a bodhisattva. They will come to possess qualities that never decline. They will never fall away from compassion. All sentient beings will come to rejoice in them.

If one recites this seven times in each of the three periods of the day and seven times in each of the three periods of the night, one will be freed from all wrongdoing, and one will come to obtain immeasurable and countless thousands of millions of bodhisattva qualities.

At dawn, having seated oneself comfortably in the cross-legged posture and formed the triangular mudrā with the hands, one directs one's attention to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas. Bringing to mind with clarity the Tathāgata as the great embodiment of all qualities, one says, "I prostrate to the Tathāgata," and having made mental prostration, if one then recites this twenty-one times, every wish and intention one holds in mind will be completely fulfilled, and one will attain accomplishment exactly as desired.

On the eighth, fourteenth, and fifteenth days of the month, one makes offerings to the Blessed One, takes the eight-fold precepts of the purification fast, dresses in white robes, makes acts of generosity, takes up the great awakening mind, and, with hands extended in an unimpeded gesture, draws a maṇḍala with one's own hands. One makes offerings of flowers of three different colors and burns white sandalwood incense. Then, mentally contemplating the various offerings and great honors fitting for the Tathāgatas throughout boundless thousands of world-realms, one presents them in thought to all the buddhas and bodhisattvas.
