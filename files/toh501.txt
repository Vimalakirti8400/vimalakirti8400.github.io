Homage to the Three Jewels.

Thus did I hear. At one time, the Blessed One was dwelling in the northern direction from Mount Meru, in the Palace of the Grove of Willows. He declared to an assembly of gods, humans, and asuras and other worldly beings beyond reckoning, and to the bodhisattva Vajrapāṇi and the others: "Sons of good family, do not be afraid. Do not be frightened. Do not be terrified."

Then the Blessed One entered meditative absorption into the samādhi called "Taming the Three Worlds," and from the auspicious maṇḍala at his heart there shone rays of light composed of three colors. From those three-colored rays, many hundreds of thousands of myriads of light-beams were scattered throughout the three worlds, gathering everything together and illuminating all once more with that radiance, and he declared:

Oṃ vajra caṇḍa paca duṣṭānta ka hana daha paca hūṃ phaṭ. Nāga-vidāraṇāya phaṭ. Tiryag-vidāraṇāya phaṭ. Preta-vidāraṇāya phaṭ. Nara-vidāraṇāya phaṭ. Naraka-vidāraṇāya phaṭ. Deva-vidāraṇāya phaṭ.

Upon these words, the bodies of all beings throughout the three worlds were burst open, and their minds came to rest like crystal spheres. The minds then abided in the likeness of three syllables:

Oṃ is three faces and six hands.
Āḥ is one face and two hands.
Hūṃ is stripped of all ornament.
These are the three families arisen.

Then, having become the three families, this was proclaimed as the proclamation of purpose:

How wondrous — most difficult to find!
The one whose very nature is great aversion,
Vajrapāṇi, so hard to encounter —
we shall attain him now.

Then the king who holds the vajra declared this mantra to arouse the heart of Vajrapāṇi, the chief of the retinue: Vajrapāṇi hṛdayaṃ da.

Then the bodhisattva Vajrapāṇi offered this praise to the Blessed One:

Tadyathā: bho bho vānāga adhipataye, idaṃ nīlāmbara-dhara-śarīraṃ, vajrapāṇi hṛdayaṃ, yasmāti kramath'uttsādanaṃ bhaviṣyati.

When this praise was offered, twelve transformations and eighteen omens occurred. All the gods, humans, and other beings arriving from other world-realms were struck with awe and cried out: "We take refuge in the Three Jewels!" The Blessed Ones dwelling in those other world-realms spoke to their own respective disciples: "Do not be afraid. This is an omen of praise to the World-Knowing One, for the bodhisattva Vajrapāṇi is dwelling in the world-realm called Endurance."

Then those of the various retinues who were endowed with faith besought their own World-Knowing Ones: "World-Knowing One, we pray that you would grant permission for us to go and see that bodhisattva." And then the various retinues, each grasping hold of the light of their own Teacher, came to the Palace of the Grove of Willows atop Mount Meru, each accompanied by their own assemblies.

Then, having made every offering to the bodhisattva Vajrapāṇi and offered praise, they declared:

Having come here at the wondrous omen
of praise to the bodhisattva —
we pray to be told of what is rare to arise in the world,
setting it forth fully and following it through to the end.

Then the Blessed One entered meditative absorption into the samādhi called "Vajra of Samaya" and performed the empowerment (abhiṣeka), declaring: "Abhiṣiñca" — and thereby rendered all present into proper vessels.

First, the approach practice is to be performed. A blue stūpa is to be constructed, and a cloth painting and similar images are to be drawn. In order to purify the obscurations of the tongue, confession is to be made with the Hundred-Syllable mantra. Then the four divine abodes (brahmavihāras) are to be cultivated. From emptiness, these three — having been thoroughly transformed at their extremities — become vajra. That is designated as Vajrapāṇi. Deities are to be emanated and drawn back upon the mantra at the navel-center. Having done so, one becomes Vajrapāṇi. The deity's body-color, ornaments, and so forth are to be known from the root tantra.

From the śrīvatsa at one's own heart-center, rays of light are emitted, gathering the Blessed One. From the tip of the second ray of light, the ten forms of offering are emanated, and the assembly of the Blessed One is to be offered to. Then, having made proper praise, the third ray of light is directed into the heart-center. The emblem at the heart — the lunar disc — dissolves. In the center abides the twenty-one-fold vajra. Around this, the king of mantras is to be encircled.

The mantra is explained in four aspects: name, meaning, extension, and recitation — these are the four aspects. The recitation is celebrated in this tantra as being of sixteen kinds. The recitation of vajra, the recitation of jewel and lotus, and the recitations of action — these four — each have the nature of the four activities. There is the recitation of the intrinsic nature of vajra, the recitation of fierce action and the cord tantra, the recitation of the fire-sickle wheel, the recitation of arrow and serpent, the recitation of cessation, the recitation of body-protection and command-obedience, the recitation of the all-pervading lance and the bow of Brahmā, and the recitation resembling a lamp.

The characteristic mark of recitation by its very nature is this: constantly, when the gods perform empowerment with a vase, or when one who has reached eight years of age appears, or when a blue flower appears — if none of these occur, the approach practice is to be performed in accordance with the appropriate time. Then the accomplishment practice is to be commenced.

The Sanskrit title Ārya-Vajrapāṇi-nīlāmbara-dhara-triloka-vinaya is rendered literally: "Noble Vajrapāṇi, Wearer of the Blue Garment, Taming the Three Worlds." The term "nīlāmbara-dhara" specifically identifies Vajrapāṇi in his fierce blue-garbed form associated with this tantra.

The mantra strings are left in their Sanskrit phonetic form as is standard for liturgical mantras; the "vidāraṇā" sequences mean "the splitting apart of" nāgas, animals, pretas, humans, hell-beings, and gods respectively.

The phrase "great aversion" in the verse of proclamation renders "zheshdang chenpo" (aversion/anger, Skt. dveṣa) intentionally: Vajrapāṇi in his wrathful form embodies the transformation of great anger into wrathful wisdom, which is the doctrinal point of the verse.

"Endurance" renders Mi-mjed (Skt. Sahā), the standard name for our world-system, literally "the world that is to be endured."

The verse section describing the three syllables and three families presents the seed-syllables Oṃ, Āḥ, and Hūṃ as corresponding to the three buddha families (Body, Speech, and Mind, or alternatively Buddha, Lotus, and Vajra families) — a standard Vajrayāna doctrinal schema.  The phrase rendered "the one whose chief aspect is aversion" translates a verse identifying the deity's wrathful, aversion-natured form as principal. This is doctrinally standard for the wrathful Vajrapāṇi tantra context, where aversion transmuted is the chief energy, but the phrasing is unusual and worth noting.

The term "Kīrti" appears as a proper name or technical label for the ground or surface of the ritual diagram to be "rolled up." Its exact referent in this ritual context is not fully transparent; it may denote a place-name or a specific material substrate. This is a genuine ambiguity.

The mantra syllables are transliterated from the Tibetan phonetic rendering of Sanskrit and are presented as best reconstruction; minor variations from canonical Sanskrit recensions are possible.
