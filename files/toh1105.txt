Auspicious Verses of the Five Tathāgatas (Pañcatathāgatamaṅgalagāthā)

The Auspicious Verses of the Five Tathāgatas, known in Sanskrit as the Pañcatathāgatamaṅgalagāthā and in Tibetan as De bzhin gshegs pa lnga'i bkra shis tshigs su bcad pa, is a short devotional poem of blessing addressed to the five buddhas of the tantric tradition. After an opening homage to the Three Jewels, it praises each tathāgata in turn, beginning with Vairocana and Akṣobhya and continuing through the others. Each stanza celebrates that buddha's wisdom, purity, and qualities, then closes with a refrain asking that, by the auspiciousness of those qualities, peace be brought to all beings. Verses of this kind were typically recited to invoke good fortune and to dedicate merit at the close of rituals and teachings. The work is Toh 1105 in the Kangyur, where it belongs among the brief auspicious verse and prayer texts collected alongside the tantras and dhāraṇīs.

In the language of India: pañcatathāgatamaṃgalagāthā
In the language of Tibet: The Auspicious Verses of the Five Tathāgatas

Homage to the Three Jewels.

Vairocana, endowed with a great ocean of sublime wisdom,
Whose body is like the full moon, who has gone to the far end of existence:
Whatever auspiciousness there is in his intentions, perfected without remainder,
By that auspiciousness may peace be brought to all beings.

King Akṣobhya, who utterly purifies existence and delusion,
The protector, auspicious in perfecting every quality:
Whatever auspiciousness there is in his mind, which dwells on virtue and evenness,
By that auspiciousness may peace be brought to all beings.

He who is adorned with the ornament of all the qualities of the buddhas,
Who bestows the riches of genuine truth and never deceives:
Whatever auspiciousness there is of the one called Ratnasambhava,
By that auspiciousness may peace be brought to all beings.

Abode of the perfection of meditative concentration, samādhi and sublime wisdom,
Seated upon the lotus of wisdom, endowed with immeasurable life:
Whatever auspiciousness there is in his blessing of life beyond count,
By that auspiciousness may peace be brought to all beings.

He who applies his heart to the work of benefiting sentient beings,
Who frees all migrating beings from the bonds that bind them:
Whatever auspiciousness there is of the supreme sage Amoghasiddhi,
By that auspiciousness may peace be brought to all beings.

This completes the Auspicious Verses of the Five Tathāgatas.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

The phrase in the second verse rendered "his mind, which dwells on virtue and evenness" is terse and could also be read as "endowed with virtue, evenness, contemplation and mind." The fourth verse does not name its buddha; the epithet "endowed with immeasurable life" and the lotus seat point to Amitābha / Amitāyus.
