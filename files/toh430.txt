The Glorious King of Tantras Called the Explanation of the Four Seats (Śrī Caturvīṭhābhikhyā Tantra Rāja Nāma)

Toh 430 in the Kangyur is a tantric scripture known in English as The Glorious King of Tantras Called the Explanation of the Four Seats, in Sanskrit as the Śrī Caturvīṭhābhikhyā Tantra Rāja, and in Tibetan as the Dpal gdan bzhi pa'i rnam par bshad pa'i rgyud kyi rgyal po. It belongs to the class of Yoginī or Highest Yoga Tantras and centers on teachings delivered by an omniscient buddha figure — identified with the Yogi of Space and associated with Vajrayoga and the Vajrayoginīs — in response to questions posed by Vajrapāṇi among a vast assembly of yoga lords. The text opens with homages to key figures of the Vajrayāna pantheon, including Avalokiteśvara and Vajrasattva, before presenting the tantra's doctrinal and ritual content concerning the "four seats," a framework of sacred bodily or geographical locations that plays an important role in certain tantric systems. As Toh 430 in the Kangyur, it is preserved among the canonical scriptures of Tibetan Buddhism and represents the type of esoteric tantra concerned with advanced yogic practice, subtle-body teachings, and the transformative methods associated with the Vajrayoginī cycle.

In the language of India: Śrī Caturvīṭhābhikhyā Tantra Rāja Nāma. In the language of Tibet: The Glorious King of Tantras Called the Explanation of the Four Seats.

I bow with reverence to the Blessed One, the Yogi of Space, to Vajrayoga, and to all the assemblies of Vajrayoginīs — equal in number to the grains of sand of the Ganges River.

I bow with reverence to the great bodhisattva, endowed with great compassion, the noble Avalokiteśvara, Lord of Vision.

I bow to the glorious Vajrasattva.

The sovereign of yoga,
likewise the supreme among yogis,
the lord of gnosis of the sages,
the supreme bliss-web of the yoginīs —
dwelling in the pure abode,
the omniscient one spoke thus.

From among eighty million lords of yoga, Vajrapāṇi pressed his palms together and addressed the Blessed One in these words:

The particular union of the secret,
how I myself am the deity in that reality,
what water is and what fire is,
how the sky is supremely beautiful,
how gnosis is to be sought —
I wish to hear the lord of gnosis.
The defining marks of liberation from saṃsāra:
hear them, O glorious Vajradhara.

First is the defining mark of empowerment,
second is the single syllable,
third is the eightfold seed,
fourth is the deity of the channel itself.

The fire of gnosis of one's own body —
so it is to be known by the intelligent yogi.
In the fire-pit of the belly's fire,
into that fire the burnt offering is to be made.
Above, below, and in the middle,
the channel of consciousness is to be contemplated.
At the very beginning, within one's own belly,
the deity of the channel is to be fully visualized.
When the fire of the belly blazes forth,
the particular colors to be visualized are as follows:
the fire of the navel is white;
the fire on the left is likewise yellow;
the south bears the name of blue;
the remainder is to be purified as red;
the center there is renowned
as the color sky-blue, as the smoke of fire.

In the midst of the fire is a lotus,
adorned by the disk of the sun.
In its center is the deity of gnosis,
who liberates from all of saṃsāra —
seated on a throne of the union of two hands,
beautifully adorned with vajra and bell.
In the right hand a vajra,
with ornaments reaching to the crown of the head,
laughing and wrathful, proclaiming Hūṃ —
this is the deity renowned as the sovereign lord of gnosis.

In its center:
to the east, the goddess Tārā;
to the north, likewise White-Robed Lady (Pāṇḍaravāsinī);
to the west, the goddess Māmakī;
and Buddhalocanā is in the south.

The form is only a thumb's measure in size —
oneself is the very deity of deities.
On the tongue the lips are to be joined.
Space is for the ears and the nose.
For the eyes, this earth is consciousness;
likewise for Brahmā, blazing fire;
at the throat is the element of delusion;
at the heart dwells the element of movement.
All afflictions are at the chest,
and the two limbs root at the navel.
Arranged in eight members,
this itself is to be known as the deity.

From the channel of consciousness thus drawn forth,
likewise the nine nectars:
from the channel of the two upper fangs,
all flow down like dissolving salt.
Accordingly, water bears the name of milk.
Resting upon the surface of the tongue,
with the blazing solar drop,
it is like a wheel ground to dust.

Above, space into space
dissolves into the nature of burning.
The left breath is the feminine,
the right is likewise the masculine.
The lower and upper are the channels of this;
the lower is renowned as what comes first.
With breath and fragrance evenly drawn forth,
upon the five of the full expansion,
draw from the root of the two consciousnesses,
and into that nectar it fully dissolves.
Upon that sovereign lord of gnosis,
that one is to be bathed with nectar.
Having thus swallowed everything throughout,
meditate with the breath unmoving.

In the palm, meditate on the moon and the sun.
Through the gnosis syllable, that is the centre.
By the mudrā of the deer's head,
the five elements are themselves burned up as oblation.

Previously, having made the seed syllable Hūṃ,
likewise the second; and with the third,
by patience it is consumed.
Likewise not-breath is the fourth,
and by the fifth, the innate seed syllable —
the wise one performs the fire-offering of the five elements.
My own suchness into that fire:
oneself is burned by the union of burning.
Just as that fire of gnosis
is famed as the very identity of burning —
the chapter called "The Practice of the Fire Offering" is hereby completed.

By virtue possessed of the identity of emptiness,
through the utter destruction of the net of birth,
one who does not abide in saṃsāra —
to the Blessed One I wish to listen.

The suchness of emptiness by its intrinsic nature —
hear this, O King Vajradhara.
Meditate throughout on the other continents, and likewise Mount Meru,
and the earth, spread into space.
The ocean has the shape of water,
adorned with the arising of a beautiful lotus.
With the characteristic of emptiness as the preliminary,
meditate on the identity of fire.

In the east is the goddess Tārā;
likewise in the north is White-Robed One.
Māmakī is in the western direction;
in the south is the goddess Buddhalocanā.

Water dissolves into Tārā altogether.
Fire dissolves into White-Robed One.
Māmakī from the west dissolves into earth.
Wind dissolves into Buddhalocanā.

This water-born flower, this lotus —
it is the throne of egg-born gnosis.
The gnosis queen sovereign of that,
she who opens below the egg-born,
has the form of a formless coil —
she dissolves into the king of gnosis.

Thus the self-identity of emptiness,
suchness itself, suchness of emptiness —
free from fear, free from harm,
meditate on entity by means of entity.

Having thus meditated on that emptiness,
cleanse the body's impurities thoroughly.
Then meditate in the form of water —
red, with eight petals,
adorned at the centre by a lotus,
with stamens and a hub —
on the eight petals, adorned
by the eight seed syllables of the goddesses.

In the east is what is called Sikā,
in the north, the seed syllable of patience likewise.
In the west, it is said, the seed syllable of wind;
in the south, the lord of Hūṃ.

In the region of lordship, the vowel of the sense powers;
in the direction of fire, sound and consciousness.
The eighth vowel letter is without truth,
and the fifth vowel letter belongs to wind.

In the centre, this king of gnosis —
the egg-born gnosis lord,
encircled by a solar maṇḍala —
blazes as the seed syllable of nectar.

This fire of the seventh hue
is to be meditated upon with supreme excellence.
The bodhisattva whose breath is unmoving,
having meditated on entity by means of entity,
then makes the channels themselves the divine throne.

From the eye, the seed syllable of Yi;
space belongs to nose and ear.
On the tongue, this seed syllable of nectar;
at the heart, what is named Hūṃ.
By the seed syllable that holds the six limbs,
the body of the yogin is fully protected.

Having known this body of gnosis —
why then is there the name of a common person?
Gnosis without form, yet possessed of form;
without colour, yet fully manifest as colour;
without abiding, yet possessed of the form of abiding;
without caste, yet suchness is reason itself.

The chapter on emptiness, the second, is hereby completed.

I wish to listen to the Blessed One
regarding the distinctions of the five self-identities.
Hear this, O King Vajradhara.

Relying on duality and non-duality,
one hundred excellent aeons ago,
dwelling entirely in a pure abode —
on Mount Meru, the goddess Buddhalocanā;
Māmakī abides in the southern direction;
the goddess Tārā is on the western continent;
likewise in the north is White-Robed One;
in the east is this goddess called the Pleasant One.
These are the abodes of the five goddesses.

Thus the heart is Mount Meru,
and likewise the head is Māmakī.

Tārā is the abode of the south,
and likewise to the left is White-Robed One;
this Pleasant One is my own abode.
May the five goddesses bestow their blessing.

In the center of the lotus is the vajra,
and in the center of the vajra is the lotus.
The vajra-nectar pervades everything,
encircling the entire body throughout.

At the very root of the eight limbs,
the waters of nectar fully permeate.
The abode of the deity is space itself —
know this perfectly as the seed of the vajra.

This, cultivated as the self-identity of the body,
is renowned as the vajra-channel.
The blood of that body
dwells together with the body as one.

Through the union of blood and vital fluid,
it flows from the eight limbs for the wise.
Just as I contemplate the five seeds,
so too must one cultivate the lotus.

Just as this body of mine
is that which the space-vajra pervades —

feet entwining feet,
lips pressing lips,
the hands of both forming a mudrā —
contemplate the three-pointed vajra.

By nature, through the cultivation
of teeth, firmness, and the consciousness of the heart,
this body bearing the form of wisdom's king —
when the practitioner utters Hūṃ with wrathful force —

thus this vajra-lotus
is to be moved through the gathered yoga.
With the delight and satisfaction of both,
all the deities are to be honored.

From the arising out of blood and vital fluid,
the seed-sprout goes forth to beings;
the bodies of the gods arise perfectly.
Yet it is not for the sake of desire and pleasure alone,
nor should one remain there for the sake of enjoyment.
It is for the sake of sentient beings and out of desire
that one abides in the union of non-duality,
offering the torma as a preliminary —
the perfectly complete, delightful body.

The third chapter, called The Chapter on Union, is hereby completed.

Hear this, O King Vajradhara.
As for the very distinctions of the ten realities,
one should prefix the ten syllables
and understand them as the five self-identities.

The fifth — the self-identity of the five —
is the five that fully protect the body.
What are the ten syllables?
How are they to be applied to the self-identity?
How does one purify and cultivate?
What are the seed-sprouts?
And how does one abide in liberation?
I wish to hear from the wondrous deity.

The Secret Secret — hear this, O King, exactly as it is.

First, in that which is empty — suchness itself —
one is to conceive of water as empty.
The lotus of seven colors,
lovely with its anthers,
with a spread of about one hundred thousand,
its form as the lotus abides with its inner essence.
The lotus, vast and supremely beautiful,
is adorned with the seven precious substances.
Garlands of hanging bell-nets,
silks and garments hang visibly.
Visualize the lion throne,
and above it the solar disc.

This very nature of the lotus-vajra —
with rays of ten million suns,
through the seeds of the five buddhas —
clearly visualize the vajra-tip.

In the center, Vairocana in bliss;
in the east, likewise Akṣobhya;
the jewel-seed in the south;
likewise in the west, Amitābha;
in the north, Amoghasiddhi.
Clearly cultivate the five seeds.

The lotus of awakening with its eight limbs —
examine it precisely on the ground.
In the center, into the egg of the inner essence,
the self-identity of consciousness dissolves.
At the tip of the eighth lotus,
the sovereign lord, Vairocana likewise.
In the east, the goddess Tārā;
in the north, the goddess Buddhalocanā;
in the west, contemplate White-Robed One;
likewise in the south, Māmakī.

With blazing rays in manifold forms,
one should cultivate this fearlessly.
From the vajra, the body of the deity
is to be conceived as dwelling there.
Three eyes, six hands,
supremely beautiful and deep blue in color,
wearing a vajra crown,
bound with silk and with streamers flowing.
Fierce, wrathful, and alluring,
seated in the heroic cross-legged posture.

On the upper right hand, a brandished sword;
on the second, likewise a bow;
on the third, a vajra held aloft and poised.
On the upper left hand, a khaṭvāṅga;
with the second, squeezing the goddess's breast;
with the third, sounding the ringing of a bell.
Clothed in supremely beautiful garments,
wearing a skirt of tiger's skin,
adorned with all ornaments complete,
and excellently decorated with garlands of flowers.

To the left, the goddess Tārā:
with one hand she embraces,
and in the other hand she holds a vessel
fully filled with the water of ambrosia.
Māmakī likewise:
with one hand she clearly embraces,
and in her other hand, in a skull cup,
is what is filled with blood and flesh.

Behind, contemplate Paṇḍaravāsinī in every direction.
Her hand is adorned with a rosary,
and her second hand scatters offerings;
facing forward, she gazes into the vessel.

Thus the five-natured body
abides completely throughout the body.
In the east, the goddess of flowers;
in the south, she who is endowed with incense;
in the west, she of the lamp-bearing hands;
in the north, the goddess of perfume.
In the powerful direction, the coquette herself;
likewise in the direction of fire, the garland-bearer;
in the direction of the void, Vāsinī;
and in the direction of wind, the eighth, the dancer.

With the hue of rays of light in manifold forms,
like hundreds of thousands of millions of suns,
radiating like clouds of buddhas,
most earnestly striving to liberate sentient beings.

As for oneself as the deity and other deities,
oneself as the deity is supreme.
Since one cannot be rendered in physical form,
one should meditate upon a reflected image and so forth.
Afterward, directly before it,
the skilled one should make offerings.
That very lotus is to be contemplated,
and that very celestial palace is to be contemplated.
Upon the seat described previously,
one covers it with the sun disc.
Various jewel offerings,
and various silks are to be spread out.
Joining the two palms together,
opening them in the manner of a lotus:
Oṃ kamala udbhave śrī svāhā.
This is the mantra and mudrā of the lotus.

Drawing it forth upon one's own breath,
one vividly contemplates it before the vajra.
Holding both hands in fists,
one coils the left little finger around the index finger —
this is the form of the iron hook.
Oṃ vajra aṃkuśa jaḥ.
This is the mantra and mudrā of the vajra iron hook.

Making both hands into vajra fists,
with both index fingers coiled —
this is the form of the lasso.
Oṃ vajra pāśa hūṃ.
This is the mantra and mudrā of the lasso.

Just as one is oneself like a reflected image,
so too should one vividly contemplate the same before oneself.
The eight goddesses being entirely complete,
this is the fourth: the kings of the wrathful ones.

In the east, Yamāntaka himself;
in the south, likewise the obstacle-slayer;
in the west, the Lotus-Terminator;
in the north, the Wrathful Protector.

Both hands are bound in vajra fists
and are to be joined back to back.
Oṃ vajra sphoṭa bhaṃ.
This is the mantra and mudrā of the vajra iron chain.

Both hands are held in vajra fists
and both little fingers are to be joined.
Oṃ vajra ghaṇṭe hoḥ.
This is the mantra and mudrā of the vajra bell.

From the mudrā of the iron chain,
extending the middle fingers outward:
Namo ratnatrayāya. Oṃ vairocana samādhi viśvaye ghuyapaja bhiṣai svāhā.
Oṃ hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ. Ho ho ho ho ho ho. Hrī hrī hrī hrī svāhā.
This is the mantra and mudrā of the Blessed One, the yoga of space.

Oṃ tāre tuttāre sa takṣitā hūṃ svāhā.
This is the mantra of the goddess Tārā.

Oṃ hili hili bali svāhā.
This is the mantra of the White-Robed One.

Oṃ tara sarva tana hūṃ phaṭ.
This is the mantra of Māmakī.

Oṃ buddha vibuddhaya hūṃ.
This is the mantra of Buddhalocanā.

The two index fingers and the middle fingers
are to be joined center to center,
and placed touching on the sides of the thumbs —
this is the mudrā of the four goddesses.

The hermaphrodite's four syllables
pertain to the flower goddess and the rest.

This itself is the mantra of the Alluring One. Standing with the right leg extended in the proper stance, one places the index finger at the heart, then extends the forearm forward, and combines this with the appearance of a bell and the syllable Hūṃ together — this is the mudrā of the wrathful one.

Oṃ āyuḥpāla pratīrakṣa dukṣa bandhaya hūṃ phaṭ.
This is the pacifying one at the eastern gate.

Oṃ ākarṣaya sattvadhātunāṃ kro svāhā.
This is the enriching one at the northern gate.

Oṃ mohaya mohaya sarvasattvānāṃ śrī svāhā.
This is the magnetizing one at the western gate.

Oṃ śakṣa sarvaduṣṭa kḍātaya hrī phaṭ.
This is the wrathful activity at the southern gate.

Oṃ suganadha hūṃ haṃ svāhā.
The right hand extended — this is the mudrā of the incense.

Oṃ susobhid puṣpebhyaḥ svāhā.
The index finger and thumb joined — this is the mantra and mudrā of the flower.

Oṃ dhūpamālakula svāhā.
The middle finger raised upright — this is the mantra and mudrā of the incense offering.

Oṃ tīkṣṇadīpe pratigṛhṇa svāhā.
The fist clenched with the thumb raised — this is the mantra and mudrā of the lamp.

Oṃ kuru kuru vijaya svāhā.
Both hands extended and joined in the center — this is the mantra and mudrā of the torma.

As taught by the way they are combined,
the wise one is to make offerings.
With the appearance of the song of Hūṃ,
all buddhas are to be delighted.

With both hands formed into vajra fists,
held at the sides of the waist,
one plays in the manner of a lute,
and blows in the manner of a flute,
and sounds in the manner of a pleasant cymbal,
and strikes in the manner of a drum,
and moves in the manner of a dance,
and fans in the manner of a fan,
and raises in the manner of a victory banner,
and sprinkles in the manner of a ladle,
and waves in the manner of a pennant,
and purifies in the manner of an umbrella,
and jingles in the manner of ankle bells,
and beats in the manner of a hand drum,
and smiles in the manner of laughter,
and moves the limbs in the wrathful posture.

Thus the yogin, in just this way,
performs the distinctive offering of song.
Hūṃ hūṃ hūṃ.

Having meditated on the yoga of selflessness,
one holds the seed of emanation.
This reveals the general yoga.

The great offering of the supreme sense faculty,
the great place of great liberation,
secret and the secret of suchness —
the lord of secrets, great bliss —
to Vairocana I bow and pay homage.

From the five channels of the body,
gods and asuras are directly emanated.
Called "the accomplishment of bliss,"
to the body of union I bow and pay homage.

Having made close offerings with song and praise,
just as one delights in the activity,
one recites upon the rosary
with the syllables of one's own mantra.
Having made offerings to the best of one's ability,
one again requests the departure.

Filling one's own body with breath,
one is to conceive that all dissolves together.
Just as the yoga is the very nature of space,
so Vairocana is oneself.
Just as the deity Vairocana
is so, just so is the channel of the back.
Just as from the channel at the front,
so arises this gnosis.

Water is explained as Tārā.
Fire is truly endowed with the White-Robed One.
Wind is the goddess buddha — likewise the supremely beautiful Māmakī.
The white channel is that of water,
and smoke is the station of fire.
This conch channel is that of wind.
Likewise the lotus
and the channel that draws forth gnosis —
the distinctive identity of the five
cannot be heard by the four thrones.
What is taught here is the brief summary.

This concludes the fourth chapter, called the Chapter on the Yoga of Reflected Images.

How is the vajra water? How does the nectar water arise? What is its distinction, and how is it tasted? How does one seek it and what manner does one seek it in? What need is there for this? I humbly request to hear this from the wondrous god.

Hear this, O King, O Vajradhara —

Having abandoned duality and non-duality,
secret and supremely concealed —
the very suchness that is the fruit for all beings,
through ten trillion aeons,
urged on by the wisdom of buddhas,
is the path of buddhas that is seen.
The illuminations of wisdom are those seven themselves.

Just as fire burns in perfect equanimity, demonstrating that manner of activity; just as the sun teaches the manner of perceiving in perfect equanimity; just as the wind receives in perfect equanimity — so too the yogin who gathers the ocean of conceptuality and delusion:

The lord of lotuses, his body the color of gold,
the essential nature of fragrant water, supremely luminous,
and the taste — Vajradhara, lord of vajras with the vajra hand:
one must come to know the rite of these three deities.

With the syllable HA, color is seized.
With the syllable HO, one becomes stainless.
With the syllable HRĪḤ, the hero is vanquished.
These should be understood in sequence.

Always meditate on the lotus throne in equanimity.
With thumb, vajra finger, and both remaining fingers fully present,
with the three mantras and three syllables, maintain and guard the rite.
Whoever tastes this taste is the wise yoginī.

Now hear the distinction of the vajra water,
O Blessed Vajradhara (Vajradhara — the Vajra-Holder, a form of Vajrapāṇi in union):

The water of the body, which flows as a drip,
is consecrated by the fivefold selfhood.
The warm nature is to be seen as fire,
the peaceful is to be thoroughly examined as water,
the wind is wind, and wind is smoke,
and earth appears as form.

Whoever tastes that very suchness —
a defiled body becomes stainless.
O noble joyful yogin, Ratnasundara,
of Vajrabhairava who has been purified through training:
pure and impure are equally substance —
this is the defining characteristic of the first yoga.

Affliction — that darkness itself —
appears alongside consciousness.
Having struck it with the vajra of wisdom,
it appears to the yogin in that.

One who desires to rely upon the nectar, embodied,
should join in non-dual union.

The great practitioner of mahāyoga
should first contemplate ālī and kālī.
What is called ālī is declared to be the male bees.
Kālī itself is the Tathāgatī.
By the sound of wisdom in union,
both are to be contemplated as embracing.

Just as holding the third syllable
of the third group of letters,
with merely a vajra enclosure,
one should encircle Amitābha.
The third goddess herself,
with merely a staff, holds the vajra.
In the center, with the lasso of sacred commitment,
one should strive supremely at the head of the kunda flower.

Thus the mantra-reciting yogin
is stirred by the lotus vajra bearer.
Naturally, with a hissing sound,
mixed with the nectar water —
tasting that very suchness here,
the yogin is abundantly auspicious in the Dharma.
The Dharma of truth is supremely true.
The Dharma of the yogin is thoroughly praised.

Yet with a mind of inferior conceptuality,
how will the union be accomplished?

The eighty million yoginīs
of this very tantra of the fourth throne —
a learned one should examine it with discernment.
With the eighty yoginīs of union,
one offers the torma cake through the Dharma of union.

This concludes the fifth chapter, called the Brief Summary of the Five Nectars.


Now, the doubt concerning the breath of the elements — one should ask the fully awakened buddha directly. How does one seek the signs of death, and having come to know them, how does one engage with the practice? The Blessed One declared:

For the benefit of embodied beings and sentient beings throughout all destinies,
the complete deception of the lord of death —
hear this, O King, O Vajra-Self.

Whoever, through mere consciousness,
dwells in the body of the lord of death —
first, at the time of the early morning,
the manner in which the breath is distributed is explained.
That an embodied being dies within six months:
this is taught with certainty, beyond all doubt.

If the time has come,
the breath will be cut and will cease.
Likewise, when one sees a slight fright,
the joints of the jaw will tremble.
Having recognized the signs of the body,
the yogin should bring it to peace.

If the inner cavity of the ear is severed,
one will not last beyond five hours.

If the vessel-vein of the eye is severed, likewise five days.
If the nasal channel is cut, seven days is certain beyond doubt.
If the upper and lower teeth lock together, three days without question.
If the tongue bears a black streak, in two days the embodied one will die.
If the outer vein of the throat and lungs fails, one will not last beyond half a month.
If the lower joint beneath the chest collapses, a physician may attempt a cure within half a month.
If there is no blood under the fingernails, one will not last beyond five hours.

These are the death omens of the eight messengers — setting aside death-wounds and the like.

The essential nature of the sound arising in the ear: attend to it through the distinction of yoga.
If the vessel of the eye's sphere is severed, the wise one should recite the seed-syllable of Ghaṇṭā.
At the nasal channel, restrain the breath — it is the smoke-channel of the human being.
At the vein of the locked teeth, wrest it away by reciting the conch-bone syllable.
Upon the black streak of the tongue, press upon it by engaging one's own nature.
At the outer vein of the throat and lungs, stimulate it with the lotus seed-syllable.
At the collapsed joint of the chest, meditate upon the gnosis born from the egg.
Where there is no blood under the fingernails, gather the hands together and recite.
At the nine gates of the nine openings, stimulate with nine seed-syllables.
At the nine channels, nine seed-syllables; bind the nine gates with nine seed-syllables.
By mere binding in this way, consciousness will not depart.

Take two unburnt vessels; with saffron and musk, correctly inscribe twelve sections. Encircle them with the vowel seed-syllables. These seed-syllables of the eight goddesses — at the borders, the seed of the neutral one (māning), and at the center, the seed of gnosis — let the wise one encircle them. Examining the three directions, it is like a reflection:
the face, limbs, and extremities of that form,
the body hair and fingers undergo transformation.
At three months, the feet and ears.
At six months, half the body takes shape.
The five consciousnesses and the mind arise.
The ten stages are fully explained —
the one who is born, born from the womb.
Through the application of yogic awareness,
the stages of the womb are fully expounded.

Hair, body hair, and nails; teeth;
dust and likewise impure flesh;
bone, the head, and the abdomen;
urine, excrement, and sweat;
saliva, mucus, and vomit;
and whatever forms of oozing filth —
these arise through the rite of formations (saṃskāra).
The yogin, knowing all of this,
draws forth the karma of former lives.

Through the condition of ignorance, formations arise.
Through the condition of formations, consciousness.
Through the condition of consciousness, name and form.
Through the condition of name and form, the six sense bases.
Through the condition of the six sense bases, contact.
Through the condition of contact, feeling.
Through the condition of feeling, craving.
Through the condition of craving, grasping.
Through the condition of grasping, existence.
Through the condition of existence, birth.
Through the condition of birth, aging and death,
grief, lamentation, suffering,
unhappiness, and disturbance —
nothing but this entire mass of suffering arises and comes to be.

This completes the seventh chapter, called the full exposition of abiding in the womb.

Then, furthermore, let there be a full explanation. From virtue, virtue is born. The essential nature of consciousness and form is the radiance of seven colors — the rays of the five sense faculties. From the eye, the ear, the nose, and the tongue, these emerge from the nine channels. Form and the sense faculties assemble together, emitting and drawing in rays of light. Breath too is to be applied in union with this. Just as a mere thumb's width, form moves centrally. The nine nāgas always abide on the lotus. In a single day, eightfold — form and color become qualities. That form always moves onward. In the midst of the second application, peace is to be simultaneously undertaken. Birth into sixty-four is to be grasped at the tip of the application. The eighth, the one abiding in equanimity in the sense of mastery, is equal to the application of the central channel. In the eighth, one engages fully in the dark. The application is to be taken up beforehand. Pressing down the seat of the nine channels entirely with the coils of the nāga, wrapped and encircled by the channel, one draws all out and is decisively victorious. What is called "the white one in the north" — its smoke similarly to the south — breath, in brief, is explained. The left breath, the three stainless ones. By the substances of day and night, transference is explained as eightfold. Through that application, transference is to be grasped by the application of transference. Drawing out is even as the form of the lotus. The wheel of channels abides in its arrangement. If a person should die in the right manner, it is not to be released. Thus the continuum and assemblage of winds abides entirely and correctly as smoke. Even as the form of the lotus, the wheel of channels abides in its arrangement. The white ones are said to be the five sense faculties, and likewise the bones. Worms and insects are the lotus. Residing at the joints of the bones is what becomes the smoke. Rocanā is likened to wind. The three stainless ones are at the tips of the hands. The distinction is between the knee and the throat. With the earth-parasol bound at the waist, it draws out that which has no inherent nature. Whoever abides at the time of death with nothing but bare consciousness — when the due time merely arrives, the breath will cease and be cut off. Likewise, when one sees the terrifying water, the cheeks will tremble at the seam. In twelve days, ten thousand — the breath is cut off moment by hour. Whatever is the characteristic of the night-sign is correctly described as journeying southward. What is described as journeying northward is like the luminous appearance of day.

At just the measure of six finger-widths, the inner consciousness transfers. It is secret, the secret great mystery — the practice is equal to the secret. The yogin who knows yoga therefore rests in equal union with the secret. Thus the chapter called "The Inner Chapter," the eighth, is complete.

Then, furthermore, is excellently taught the characteristic mark of the vajra-union: grasping the very form of the lotus, bringing the supreme and the vajra-supreme into equality, and equalizing the lotus, the coupling is the union of nectar. The intrinsic nature of the seminal essence — the yogin's seminal essence is secret. In the measure of seven tips of kusha grass, one offers this to the eight yoginīs. Taking up the churning of the nectar, one offers it to the lungs like a seminal essence. Afflictions arise at every moment, like the poison of arsenic. Having consulted the yogin's treatise, this Vinaya, by constant recitation, beings attain passage beyond the world. The mire of defilement from evil conduct — whoever is intelligent, having cleansed it with this virtue, this defilement — by the path along which one travels, through what has been done, one becomes the Sugata. Thus the chapter called "The Wheel of Channels," the ninth, is complete.

With the form of a substantial thing that is emptiness, one should meditate upon it together in the center. Washed by the very suchness of emptiness — like the seed of emptiness within emptiness, described as the water of the empty direction — there the lotus has a form. In the center of the lotus, that lotus arises together, delightfully. Consciousness is as a single seat. That suchness, together with the seat, and likewise the seminal essence above is taught — as consciousness abides together in the center. The ground, as having the form of light-rays, is to be meditated upon at the measure of half a cubit. Consciousness is to be meditated upon as white. Whatever appears, knowing the practice is luminous. Thus the chapter called "The Chapter of Luminous Appearance," the tenth, is complete.

Then, furthermore, is to be taught: the gnosis of another being, moving supreme and white, is described as being in front. Smoke is outside, to the west. Having grasped its left side, consciousness together in the center — consciousness is to be meditated upon as yellow. Its name is called Rohita. The name is likewise described as empty. By rotating it outside a conch shell, one also meditates upon that mind. The intelligent one, through knowing another's mind, sets the mind of another constantly as a syllable and, merely by resting in equipoise and meditating, comes to know the mind of another. Thus the chapter called "The Chapter on the Mind of Another," the eleventh, is complete.

Then, furthermore, is to be taught: by equalizing together with the inhalation, the nine channels are to be sealed. By the identity of inhaling and filling, one thoroughly purifies oneself through the union. The movement that causes the breath to go out — the intelligent one who seals the door should apply the syllable of movement. By applying it at the end of the breath, the arising elements are not reversed. By the arising elements at the end of the breath, the intelligent one fills and empties. Upon filling, there are the syllables of the nine channels of the divine. The intelligent one, by inhaling together, draws in form as a single act. By inhaling, exhaling, and filling, the union for consciousness-transference abides together. When by reason the due time arrives, one begins the practice of consciousness-transference together. Thus the chapter called "The Chapter on the Training of Consciousness-Transference," the twelfth, is complete.

Then, furthermore, is excellently taught: one extracts from the nine joints. Likewise the nine joints, as described above — one should rely on the transference together. Being stirred by the seed of smoke, one likewise proceeds to omniscience. Through the meditation of the dark union, one fully emerges from the Brahmā-aperture. This is called the true union of consciousness. As the seat, the maṇḍala of wind and the seed of wind are described as equal.

From it, like a crown, one transfers. Likewise, from above the nine joints, it is equal to the Brahmā-aperture. From the Brahmā-aperture arising, it is the very nature of instantaneous transference. Thus the chapter called "The Chapter on Instantaneous Transference," the thirteenth, is complete.

Then, furthermore, is excellently taught: that very suchness, endowed with true characteristics, through meditation on that suchness together as one, the seat becomes perfectly white. This is taught under the name "the name of emptiness." By applying everything at the time of pacification, one should begin the practice of peace together. Thus the chapter called "The Chapter on Pacification," the fourteenth, is complete.

Then, furthermore, is excellently taught: just as one purifies the sign of selfhood, one purifies, and purifies into great selfhood. One should purify together with emptiness. In the center of the maṇḍala of wind is the equal maṇḍala of fire. In the center of the maṇḍala of fire is the equal maṇḍala of the sun. In the center of the maṇḍala of the sun is the equal maṇḍala of the moon. What is taught to be in the center of that is the characteristic of the co-emergent sprout. The great secret of what is secret within the secret — through the great essence, the very essence — one should purify together the consciousness of all those who do not know suchness. Thus the chapter called "The Seventh of the Seventh of the Fifteens" is complete.

Then, furthermore, is excellently to be taught: one sees the eighth white seed. The nine-edged seed is like the seed of smoke. The conch seed has six limbs. The first is the lotus-seed of the syllable of sound. Its head likewise has the vowel-syllable at the end. The first portion is the three stainless ones. The category of distinction is the last. The syllabic seed at the outer edge is the canopy of earth. The seed of the syllable is to be known as the ge. From what is dear arise sorrow and from what is dear arises fear. Being wholly separated from what is dear, there is no sorrow and no fear. From longing arises sorrow and from longing arises fear. Being wholly separated from longing, there is no sorrow and no fear. Endowed with the previously described characteristics, on the seat of suchness together, endowed with that great selfhood, that suchness is the seventh ray of light. In the center of the maṇḍala of wind is the likeness of the maṇḍala of the moon. The lotus nature and the palace together give rise to consciousness. Even the yellow rephā in front, together with the color category, is like the mind. What is to be meditated at the boundary of the earth, together with the seed of one's own body — the name of the bodhisattva's cross-legged posture, and in the intellect the two secret Merus. The eyes should look directly at the tip of the nose, and the breath is directed to just before the navel. Through the nature of the union of meditative equipoise, one mounts evenly upon the secret seat. Through attachment one is liberated from sinful action. By the mere act of mounting the seat entirely, a king comes under one's power. Or else through the recitation of mantra, this is to be begun as an empowerment. Or else through the possession of medicine, the mantra is the seat itself of another. This is the first chapter.

Then, furthermore, is excellently taught: the one who extracts from the nine joints is described as equal to extracting from emptiness. The joint itself is the very waist, and the joint together with the navel is white. Wrapped in white around the trunk, the hollow of the heart is of the nature of smoke. Even after death one becomes smoke. At the heart too is the very conch, and it resides at the joint of the conch. Above the heart is the lotus nature; the lotus is described at the chest. Likewise at the throat is the gi-wang, and the gi-wang is described at the five sense faculties. The three stainless ones are at the border of the teeth. The canopy of earth is near the messenger. The dark and the left side is the opening — at the root of the canopy of earth, the root of the messenger. The extraction is the Brahmā-aperture. If one seals all the gates with this, what is consciousness to be called? This is the second chapter.

Then, furthermore, is to be taught: by beginning with this rite, by applying this syllable, and by the mere meditation on this — what yoga is brought into connection as a valid cognition, and what valid cognition of the syllable, and what valid cognition endowed with the divine —

The fever of wandering beings, whichever it may be —
by meditating just once upon it,
one goes to the end of the enemy.
Likewise, the name of the enemy is to be recited;
it is to be applied together with the black one.
This is the third chapter.

Then, furthermore, is to be excellently taught: the words of the supreme rite. By the essence of the deity itself together, one summons with the seed of smoke: Om dhūma dhūma piṭa yakṣa ra dhuma bhyo mantra bālayam svāhā. Make joyful the supreme syllable endowed with the syllables of the boundary. By meditating on the yellow samaya (sacred pledge-seal): Om samaya ākarṣaya ākarṣaya dhuma bīja svāhā. This is the fourth chapter.

Then, furthermore, is to be taught: the distinguishing characteristics of the four types of birth. The conch-woman and the lotus-woman, likewise the one marked with the form of an elephant — the lotus-woman moves with the gait of an intoxicated elephant, together with a good fragrance; she always speaks pleasantly, and is capable for the lesser and middling. All her limbs are praised: her eyes are like lotus petals, her hair curls in just that way as long as it grows; her face and teeth are refined, her fingers are slender and her limbs are fine, her fingers are red and small. She is said to be of the middle type, bearing a form of peace. By means of the lotus-woman, one accomplishes splendor and is well-suited to the enjoyments of peace. This is the fifth chapter.

Then, furthermore, is to be taught: the elephant-woman has the smell of fermented drink, with effort suited to the lower and middle; her lips hang, her limbs are thick, and she moves with the gait of an intoxicated elephant. Her body is tall, her nails are long, her breasts are pendulous and her hair is coarse. Her desire is small and her limbs are thick; likewise her shoulders are raised. Her eyes and nails are slender; she delights in honey and speaks pleasantly. Her face is very much inclined to laugh; she is addicted to pleasant sounds and desirable things. Her secret place is wide and spreading, and the soles of her feet rest evenly on the ground. She is perpetually fond of drink; the elephant-woman, too, is intent on her object. She is to be applied to the rite of expansion. This is the sixth chapter.

Then, furthermore, is to be excellently taught: the conch-woman delights in conch-like things; her face and complexion are like that of a conch, her limbs are long. She has a crow's cry and a crow's smell; her fingers are thick and long. Her hair is coarse yet her limbs are soft; she moves with the swift gait of a great donkey or a mountain deer. Her neck is long and she has a gap between her teeth; her nose is high, her armpits are hollow, and her lips are like the shape of a parasol. A woman with such characteristics is to be abandoned by one of intelligence together; she is a woman applied to the rite of subjugation. This is the seventh chapter.

Then, furthermore, is to be excellently taught: the spotted-woman delights in spotted things; her form and complexion accord with spots. Her breasts are small and her fingers slender; her voice is small and her limbs small; her body is small, her hair too is small. Her nature is like a crow and her cry is like a crow's; she delights in quarreling and speaks harshly. These are the characteristics of the boundary-type woman. She is to be applied to the black woman's purpose. With her left eye and her lower eye, a body resplendent like a jackal's face — her hair and teeth are always standing up; her desire is fierce and she falls upon it fiercely. Her complexion is gray, she is stooped and short; her breasts are high and her shoulders are high. Her eyebrows are merely slightly frowning. This one devours and destroys all servants, or else kills the master. A woman of this kind is to be entirely avoided.

The lotus-woman is of the lineage of gods; the elephant-woman is of the lineage of yakṣas (gnod sbyin); the conch-woman is of the lineage of humans; the spotted-woman partakes of the nature of mountain animals. This is the eighth chapter.

As for those born human: by means of all applications, first the share of the lineage — the bāḍavā is described as the elephant type; the preta-woman (hungry ghost type) is of the root lineage. The pārāpa is of the divine lineage. With shoulders like a parasol and a long neck, with sharp teeth, and moist lips — or else she is of the lotus or red lineage. Her forehead and eyebrows are broad and wide; her hair is soft and golden-pale. Her form is harmonious and slightly frowning; her nose is high, the bridge of her nose is arched; her limbs are soft and her shoulders are long. Her gait is like a swan's, her voice is like a swan's; she delights in fragrances and flowers. She delights in milk, she gives gifts, and is endowed with great merit and elegance.

She who is endowed with the glory of sovereignty gives abundantly; her mind is steady, and she lives on little food.

This is called the First Chapter.

Then, furthermore, it is well explained: she of the elephant type has the legs of an elephant; her arms are long and her face is broad; her eyes are narrow, her ears hang down; her hair is coarse, and she has prominent shoulders; her lips hang down, and her limbs are fully developed. She eats with great voracity. She of the fine-faced type has hanging ears; her desires are few, and she delights in gatherings; her shoulder-tops have drooping folds, and her limbs are slender; her hair is long, her teeth are wide-set; her nails are small and her feet are soft; her nose and belly are long; her voice is like a bee's, and her body is rough. She delights in desire and is genuinely deluded. She is wealthy and mistress of all.

This is called the Second Chapter.

Then, furthermore, it is well explained: she of the deer type has the legs of a deer; her gaze is long, and her body is large; her hair is fine, her teeth are small; her lips are thin, and her body is beautiful; her complexion is like sesame, and her skin is delicate. She delights in crowds and speaks at length. Her voice is like a crow's, and her gait is unsteady. She is perpetually poor and unstable, and by nature she bears a malicious mind. She is one who commits misdeeds and utters harsh words. She is of the animal or hungry ghost lineage.

This is called the Third Chapter.

Then, furthermore, it is well explained: she is learned, her diligence is steady; her wisdom and her discipline are firm; her courage is stable, and she is like an empty vessel in her stillness; her neck is like a pot; her body is proportionate and her limbs are slender; her skin is firm and her nails are red; she speaks pleasingly and is greatly compassionate. She takes constant delight in the nature of giving; she delights in garlands of flowers; she delights in yogic union and in wisdom; her mind is by nature inclined toward compassion. She is distinctly superior to ordinary women.

This is called the Fourth Chapter.

She who takes open delight in fermented drink, who constantly delights in craving — she who constantly engages in low conduct upon the ground — such a one is to be avoided.

This is called the Fifth Chapter.

Then, furthermore, it is well explained: fire-pit by fire-pit is to be understood as equivalent. The kurume fire is to be examined in every respect. The root cakra (saṃskāra) is to be understood as equivalent to a lotus. Smoke and fire are to be thoroughly examined. With white seeds, wood; like conch seeds, flowers. The torma and incense are said to correspond to the lotus. The five elements are to be employed in close conjunction. The three aspects of civet fragrance are performed. Just as ground-seeds extract the toxin from the ground — joining the ring-finger, thumb, and middle finger together into a mudrā, one performs the fire-offering to oneself, the worship and oblation, by means of the character of the nectar syllable. The substance burned in the fire is milk to drink. On the fifth and fourteenth days, and especially on the eighth day, relinquishing distinctions through full union, one performs the fire-offering to the guru just as to oneself. Likewise — just as in the subjugation of the gods, or the destroying of a secret, or the pleasing of the guru — similarly, upon taking a guru, and upon receiving the rite in its entirety, one unites oneself with the fire. By this, the yogin will undoubtedly destroy misdeeds; of this there is no doubt.

This is called the Sixth Chapter.

Then, furthermore, it is well explained: the supreme rite of the mudrā — pressing down with the right outstretched foot, one fully extends the index finger to the heart. By this, the yogin will undoubtedly destroy misdeeds; of this there is no doubt. Having combined the form of the syllable hūṃ with a brass hook together, it causes the nāgas to tremble with fear, makes misguiding spirits shake, opens locked doors, slays elemental beings, tames flesh-eaters, and drives away hungry ghosts. This is the mantra and mudrā of the rite of Tsam-ngam. Furthermore, with the mantra and mudrā of the vajra Tsam-ngam, so as not to let things be destroyed, and for hurling upward and for subjugating elemental beings, smoke-seeds are to be applied. To invite a bodhisattva who has transcended the world, one should use the mudrā of the vajra iron hook. One offers a lotus throne.

This is called the Sixth Chapter.

For worldly dwelling — the mere Tsam — one invites with the conch iron hook, binds with the vajra lasso, gladdens with the vajra iron chain, and makes offering with the vajra bell. With the finger-mudrā of flowers, the finger-mudrā of perfume, the finger-mudrā of incense, the finger-mudrā of butter-lamps, and the finger-mudrā of torma, one equalizes the five offerings and makes offering. One seals with the mudrā of the enclosure, the mudrā of the net, the mudrā of the phurba, the mudrā of the utpala, the mudrā of the śakaṭi, and the mudrā of the lotus. One performs the mudrā of the water fire-offering, the mudrā of the lotus in fire, cutting with the vajra curved knife, slaying with the reversed curved knife, driving away with the sow mudrā, gathering into the heart, and requesting departure. This is called the Chapter on Mudrās, the Seventh Chapter.

Alas, all formations are impermanent,
bearing the nature of arising and ceasing.
Therefore abandon, in just the same way, all desires.
Desires are boundless in their harm, like poison —
they cause one to fall into the hells and to be born in the realm of hungry ghosts.
The qualities of desire, which do not remain even for an instant,
are like a mirage, without essence, like a bubble on water.
Clinging to the finest form of the prime of youth —
lovely and pleasing — belongs to the time of childhood.
A beautiful form becomes an unlovely form.
Without a companion, it comes to resemble death.
The beings of this world are impoverished, going about begging.
Therefore, with effort, the yogin
should restrain the body in all circumstances
and should restrain food and drink.
At the time of conduct, let this be the restraint.

This is the Eighth Chapter. The Fourth Treatise is complete.

Again, the examination of the fire:
When it burns high, it is pacification.
When it burns low, it is increase.
When it burns in circles, it has power.
When it burns with crackling, it destroys the self.

For pacification with power, it moves together with glory in the east.

to the south, wealth is destroyed.

In the wind direction, wealth declines;
to the north, the lineage fully flourishes.

The tongue is the adept of the supreme, unendurable white light blazing forth, the secret play. The fluid produced by the two upper fangs is called the water of milk. The two fangs, whose color resembles the color of the flowing water, are like roots. The water that dispels the torment of the tongue is the five nectars of the white nectar. Applied to the five expansions in the manner of the taste of nectar, it is not known to the unskilled. Likewise, on the tongue, that white seed is drawn by the iron hook of smoke. The emptiness of everything, and beings too, is emptiness — the fifth. The five sense objects too are emptiness, and likewise the sense faculties and so forth: one abides truly without examining them as empty. Therefore the mode of emptiness is without dust.

Just as a farmer skillfully purifies his field again and again, and by releasing the plow turns everything completely over, thereby clearing away on a vast scale the grass and trees that harm the field — and so it is thus called pure and clean —

In just the same way, the master of supreme union too should be established as the second. Likewise, in that same way, the outer is also washed. Therefore the wise should purify by the mode of the three through emptiness.

Just as form and emptiness are so, in the center is the seed of consciousness. Seed, seed, and sprout — form, the sense faculties, and the root ground. The characteristic of each part, in the manner of intrinsic nature, abides truly in the flawless vajra-nature. From the six sense faculties six rites arise truly. The twelve divine abodes, the ground sarva, and the supreme syllables of mantra are to be accomplished through the magical display of mind. Alas, how bewildered are sentient beings! The mantra ugu ugu, and the contemplation of the equal image of deteriorated form — these are abandoned entirely by the very essence. Therefore the mind follows the supreme syllables of mantra. The mantra that arouses and stirs and generates all sentient beings is to be understood with certainty by one possessed of intelligence. Those who accept that mantras without suchness can lead beings are fools. The image and mind of those who speak wrongly, and by the ultimate supreme syllable that is the very identity of mantra — the four activities and their rites are fully renowned.

This is called the chapter on emptiness, the fourth.

Then, moreover, what is to be taught: faults and qualities are abandoned. The position of the Middle Way is free from fault. The body arising from mind, pure by intrinsic nature, is to be engaged with the mind of the single yoga of selflessness. Just as water in an unfired vessel destroys, so too, you destroy many inner yogas. Just as a potter fills everything fully with fire, bringing the many to exhaustion in the center, it becomes like one's own form. By the blazing fire of the sovereign inner gnosis, through that destruction, form here is liberated. By the purity of intrinsic nature, the phenomena of purity — the great path of the pure melody — abandon the ocean of impure bewilderment along with conceptuality. The phenomena of purity are supremely peaceful. The phenomena of purity are without fault. In order to attain awakening through purity, the phenomena of purity are supremely taught.

Then, moreover, what is to be explained: those not yet liberated are to be liberated; those not yet freed, I myself shall free. With such compassionate intent, one should practice the meditative concentration of union. The body has three aspects; speech likewise has four aspects; the mind has three aspects — guard these through the body and thoroughly restrain them. The ground of duality is to be fully abandoned. One who possesses gnosis should resort to liberation. Momentary enjoyment is likewise suffering; the non-dual is to be shown together. For sentient beings in this very life, in one moment excellence, in the next moment ruin — likewise, the enjoyment of the deluded is not to be regarded by the yogin. Therefore the place of fault, the fault of beings, the reality of conditioned existence — the desire for the fruit of a single place of superior enjoyment is reversed. The place of enjoyment is called the ground of the craving that beings have in giving. Therefore, having abandoned the path of duality, the yogin in the same way centers himself. Through the practice of union with equal-minded focus, the Dharma of conduct is peaceful and without flaw. The selfless body is authentically established; one rests in equipoise upon selflessness.
