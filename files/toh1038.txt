The Dhāraṇī Called Increasing Wisdom (Prajñāvardhanī nāma Dhāraṇī)

The Dhāraṇī Called Increasing Wisdom, known in Sanskrit as the Prajñāvardhanī Dhāraṇī and in Tibetan as Sherab Kyepa, is a very short incantation text addressed to the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara. After paying homage to the Three Jewels and to Avalokiteśvara, it gives a brief mantra and a simple accompanying rite: one recites the mantra three times over a palmful of water and drinks it, and after seven days one is said to retain whatever one hears, reflecting its concern with strengthening memory and understanding. According to its colophon, it was translated into Tibetan by the Indian preceptor Jñānagarbha and the monk-translator Lui Wangpo, and later revised by the Indian preceptor Vidyākarasiṃha and the editor-translator Devacandra. It is Toh 1038 in the Kangyur, where it belongs among the collection of dhāraṇī texts.

In the language of India: prajñābardhanī nāma dhāraṇī

In the language of Tibet: The Dhāraṇī Called Increasing Wisdom.

namo ratnatrayāya nama āryābalokiteśvarāya bodhisatrāya mahāsatvāya tadyathā skhalate slalate manite svāhā

This is the rite for this vidyā-mantra: if one recites it three times over a palmful of water and drinks it, then after seven days one will retain whatever one hears.

The Dhāraṇī Called Increasing Wisdom is complete.

It was translated by the Indian preceptor Jñānagarbha and the translator, the monk Lui Wangpo. It was revised and finalized by the Indian preceptor Vidyākarasiṃha and the chief editor-translator, the monk Devacandra.
