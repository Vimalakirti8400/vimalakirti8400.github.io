Dhāraṇī for Attaining Loving-Kindness, Compassion, Joy, and Equanimity

The Dhāraṇī for Attaining the Four Immeasurables is a brief ritual text made up of four short mantras, each paired with the promise that reciting it just once will bring about one of the four immeasurable states of mind: loving-kindness, compassion, joy, and equanimity. Each mantra opens with homage to all the tathāgatas, and the work closes simply by naming itself, with no narrative frame or teaching setting. Its Sanskrit and Tibetan titles are not given in the opening lines, so it is best known by this descriptive English title. The text is Toh 938 in the Kangyur, where it belongs to the class of dhāraṇī texts, the collection of incantations and protective formulas preserved in the Tibetan canon.

By reciting this once, one will attain loving-kindness.

namaḥ sarva tathāgatānāṃ curu curu muru muru kāruṇe kāruṇya cittamutpādaya bhagavati sarva bhayaṃ me nāśaya svāhā

By reciting this once, one will attain compassion.

namaḥ sarva tathāgatānāṃ namo bhagavati sarva satra priye hūṃ mudite svāhā

By reciting this once, one will attain joy.

namaḥ sarva tathāgatānāṃ acintya viśānī oṃ turu turu traṃ hulu hulu mulu mulu svāhā

By reciting this once, one will attain equanimity.

namaḥ sarva tathāgatā hṛdaya anugate oṃ kuruṃ gini svāhā

The Dhāraṇī for Attaining the Four Immeasurables is complete.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

Each statement "By reciting this once..." follows the dhāraṇī it refers to. The first statement, about loving-kindness, therefore belongs to a dhāraṇī that comes just before this piece. The four immeasurables (apramāṇa) are loving-kindness, compassion, joy, and equanimity. The final syllable of the colophon, written སྷོ, is read as the usual closing so, "is complete."
