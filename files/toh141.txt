The Noble Dhāraṇī Called Six Doors (Ārya-ṣaṇmukha-nāma-dhāraṇī)

The Noble Dhāraṇī Called Six Doors, known in Sanskrit as the Ārya Ṣaḍmukha Nāma Dhāraṇī and in Tibetan as Phakpa Go Drukpa Zhejawé Zung, is a short dhāraṇī text, a formula meant to be memorized and recited for spiritual benefit. It opens with homage to the youthful Mañjuśrī and is set in a jeweled assembly courtyard suspended in the sky above the Pure Abodes, where the Buddha addresses a vast gathering of bodhisattvas. He urges them to take up this dhāraṇī for the benefit and happiness of all beings. Rather than a string of mantric syllables, the dhāraṇī takes the form of a series of aspirations, beginning with the wish that whatever suffering one meets throughout cyclic existence never be marked by ignorance of its sameness with all beings. The text is Toh 141 in the Kangyur, where it is placed among the general sūtras.

In the language of India: ārya ṣaḍamukha nāma dhāraṇī

In the language of Tibet: The Noble Dhāraṇī Called Six Doors.

Homage to the youthful Mañjuśrī.

Thus did I hear at one time. The Blessed One (the Buddha) was dwelling in an assembly courtyard above the Pure Abodes, in the midst of the sky, adorned and finely laid out with the seven kinds of precious things. He was there together with countless assemblies of bodhisattvas. At that time the Blessed One said to the bodhisattvas:

"Sons of noble family, for the benefit and happiness of all beings, take up this dhāraṇī called Six Doors. It is as follows.

Whatever suffering I experience in saṃsāra, in other places of birth, from one round of cyclic existence to the next, may it never take on the character of not knowing its sameness with all sentient beings.

Whatever worldly happiness of perfect abundance I experience, may I enjoy it in such a way that I fully know it to be shared in common with all sentient beings.

Whatever evil, nonvirtuous karma I have committed, may none of it remain unconfessed through unsurpassed confession.

Whatever deeds of Māra I have committed, may none of them go without being fully understood through unsurpassed full understanding.

Whatever roots of virtue I have that are endowed with the perfections, whether worldly or transcendent, may all of them become the fruit of unsurpassed wisdom.

Whatever liberation I have, through it may all sentient beings be liberated. May there be no abiding in either saṃsāra or nirvāṇa.

tadyatha oṃ kṣame kṣame kṣānte kṣānte dame dame dānte dānte bhadre bhadre subhadre subhadre candre candre sucandre sucandre candrakiraṇe candravati tejovati yaśovati ṅharmavati brahmavati sarvakleśaviśodhani sarvaarthasāṅhani sarvaarthapraśamane paramārthadhani kāyaviśoṅhani vākaviśoṅhani manaḥsaṃśoṅhani svāhā

Sons of noble family, accordingly, if any son or daughter of noble family recites this dhāraṇī called Six Doors three times by day and three times by night, all of their karmic obscurations will be purified, and for up to seven lifetimes they will remember their births. They will fully awaken into unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening."

When the Blessed One had spoken these words with delight, those assemblies of bodhisattvas, and the world with its gods, humans, asuras, and gandharvas, rejoiced and praised what the Blessed One had said.

This completes the Noble Dhāraṇī Called Six Doors.

This concludes the translation. The following are translator's notes.

In the fourth aspiration the Tibetan reads "fully done" before "through unsurpassed," which makes little sense; it has been read as a copyist's slip for "fully understood," matching the verb that follows. The subject of "may there be no abiding in either saṃsāra or nirvāṇa" is unstated in the Tibetan; it expresses the Mahāyāna ideal of non-abiding nirvāṇa and could apply to the speaker or to all beings.
